I'm doing a piece on Hofstadter's typogenetics -- the toy system in Godel, Escher, Bach where you've got strands of A, C, G and T letters and little enzymes that cut and copy and shuffle them around, and some strands end up building the enzymes that copy the strand, so self-replication happens inside a formal game. Before I file it I wrote down what I actually think about all this, and I'd like an audit, because I suspect some of it doesnt hang together and I cant tell which parts.

Here's where I've landed. (1) Godel's move is real self-reference, not a parlor trick: the sentence genuinely ends up asserting of itself that it is unprovable, and that is the whole point of incompleteness. (2) The self-copying typogenetic strands and the Godel sentence are the same phenomenon, not a cute analogy -- replication and self-reference are one thing in two costumes. (3) The genetic code is arbitrary; which codon gets which amino acid is a convention the way words are. (4) Nothing means anything on its own -- meaning is always assigned by some interpreter, us or the cellular machinery. (5) The cell is chemistry all the way down, no homunculus, no ghost.

Which of these are actually pulling against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each? Rank them by severity, cheapest fix first for each. Then tell me the single revision that cleans up the most, and what I'd lose by making it.
