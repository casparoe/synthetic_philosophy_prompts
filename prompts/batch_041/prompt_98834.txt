Ok so my reading group got onto this thought experiment and now I can't sleep. Setup, as best I remember it: imagine a giant sphere — call it the careenium — packed with enormous numbers of tiny bouncing balls, the simmballs. They just roll and collide according to boring mechanical laws, nothing fancy. But the balls are arranged so that certain stable patterns act like the letters of a code, and the careenium's whole dynamics, described at that level, looks like a machine reading symbols and acting on what they mean — it can work out that 7+5=12 and then shove a lever. Now here's what everyone fights about: when it shoves the lever, did the meaning of the symbols push the simmballs around, or did the simmballs just do their ball thing and the meaning is something we're reading into it?

A friend built this argument and I think it proves way too much, but I can't find the bad step.

(1) Take any collision of simmballs. The complete prior arrangment of the balls, plus the mechanical laws, already fixes what the next arrangement will be. Nothing is left over for anything else to determine.
(2) If the prior micro-arrangement is already enough to fix what happens, then the only thing doing any causal work is the micro-arrangement. Anything else would be redundant overdetermination, and we don't allow that.
(3) The symbolic facts — which pattern means "7", which means "plus", which state counts as concluding — aren't part of the micro-arrangement. You could in principle keep the ball-by-ball story identical and reassign the meanings.
(4) So the symbols never push the simmballs around. Meaning is a passenger, not a cause.
(5) But nothing in the argument was special about symbols. The same moves apply to the simmball-pattern level, to "the careenium is computing a sum", even to "this chunk of balls is an arm". None of those push anything around either; only the micro-laws do.
(6) So no explanation that cites symbols, computations, or meanings ever names a cause. "It moved because it worked out 7+5" is false as a causal claim.
(7) But that's absurd — the whole point of the careenium was that its doing the sum is why the lever moved. And if (6) holds, then no psychology, no cognitive science, no economics explanation is ever a causal explanation either, which can't be right.
(8) So one of (1)–(6) is wrong.

I'll give up anything except (1) — I really don't want to deny that the ball-arrangement plus the laws fixes the next ball-arrangement, that just seems like physics. And I obviously can't accept (6)/(7). So which step do I drop, why did it look fine when it isn't, and what do I have to pay? Like, if I keep (1), what am I actually giving up to keep symbols as causes — do I have to say there's downward causation, or a seperate kind of causal power, or that overdetermination is fine after all? Lay out the menu of exits and price each one.
