I'm re-reading Lord of the Rings for a book club and I want to pitch it as a Glaucon text — tell me if the angle holds up. Everyone treats the One Ring as the ring of Gyges, but I think that gets it backwards. Gyges's ring buys him impunity: nobody can see him do it, and that impunity is the whole engine of Glaucon's challenge, that the just man given a free hand would act exactly like the unjust one. Tolkien's ring doesn't buy impunity, it buys complicity — put it on and you drop into the wraith-world, and every bearer gets handed a flattering picture of themselves as the one person who'd finally use the power well. Galadriel as a queen, Sam as a great gardener-king, Boromir as Gondor's savior. So the ring is really a test of whether anyone is just for its own sake, and Tolkien's answer looks like no: Frodo fails at the end and claims it, and the thing only gets destroyed by Gollum's accident. Read the whole book through Glaucon's challenge and give me a verdict — does Tolkien vindicate Glaucon or refute him, and does that ending's dependence on accident (or grace) quietly concede the point? Point me to the scenes I should put in front of the group, and say honestly where the reading strains the text. Be commited: I'd rather have a strong reading I can argue with than a survey of both sides. Keep it to one page, 600 words max.
