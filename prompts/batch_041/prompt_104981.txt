I'm a fourth-year phil PhD and I run a small reading group on Indian epistemology for people who mostly do Western stuff. For the session on the pramāṇas I want to hand out a decision tree that walks someone from their gut verdicts on concrete cases to a named position, so people notice they've been assuming an answer to "how many basic sources of knowledge are there" without realizing it. Can you build it? Please write it in American English, I'm handing it out as-is.

Here's the shape I want. Every branch node should be a question you can answer without knowing any school names or any Sanskrit — a case, or a yes/no about a case. Start with the easy stuff and get weirder as you go down. Some things I want to make sure get used, because this is the in-the-weeds part a generic intro answer would skip:

- The number-of-sources question, but reached through cases, not by asking "how many pramāṇas do you accept." E.g. you overhear a stranger say the bridge is out, you believe it, and it's true — do you now *know* the bridge is out, and if so, is that knowing basic, or does it bottom out in a quick inference about whether this stranger tends to be right? Same move for: being told a gavaya (a wild ox) looks like a cow, and later going "oh, that's the animal I was told about" — is that its own kind of knowing or just inference? The classic "Devadatta is fat but never eats by day, so he must eat at night" (postulation); and knowing there's no pot on the table because you just see there isn't one (absence as a source).

- Then the reducibility question, which I think is the real fork: for each of those, is it a *separate* basic source, or does it collapse into perception plus inference? Someone can agree the testimony case is knowledge and still insist it's just inference, so don't let the tree treat "you'd say you know it" as the same as "you count it as its own source."

- Two more branches that seperate schools which otherwise agree on the count: (a) can two different sources ever independently certify the *same* fact, or does each source own an exclusive domain with no overlap? (b) is a cognition's correctness something it carries with it, or does it always need outside confirmation — and does the same hold for being *wrong*? Phrase these as cases too, not as jargon.

Leaves I need named and located: Cārvāka, the Buddhist pramāṇavādins (Dignāga/Dharmakīrti line), Nyāya, Jain, and Mīmāṃsā (you can note the internal split if the tree needs it, but don't lean on sub-school labels to do the work). Put each leaf on the combination of answers that actually lands you there, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and if some combination of answers is unoccupied, say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any two leaves your tree *can't* pull apart, and tell me why. And keep it a size someone can actually fill out in ten minutes — I'd say no more than about twelve branch nodes. One more thing: I have a beleif that the reducibility fork matters more than the raw count, so if your tree shows me that's wrong, say so. Thanks!
