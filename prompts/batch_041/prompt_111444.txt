Construct a case for a seminar on whether liability to be killed in war tracks culpability or merely responsibility for posing an unjust threat. The case should contain two combatants on the unjust side who are alike in every causally relevant respect — same threat, same role, same contribution — except that one is culpably indifferent to the injustice of his cause while the other is blameless, having done everything a reasonable soldier could to confirm that his war is just. Add one soldier on the just side who must decide what he may do about each. Then do the following. State what the traditional doctrine that combatants on both sides are moral equals says about who may kill whom and who is liable to be killed; state what the revisionist view, on which liability tracks responsibility for an unjust threat rather than moral culpability, says about the same. Identify the single feature of the case that makes the two theories come apart, and demonstrate that it is doing the work by removing it: say what happens to each verdict when the blameless combatant is made culpable, and again when necessity is removed so that killing either is unnecessary. Constraints: a contemporary small-scale conflict; no world wars, no drones, no child soldiers, no ticking bombs; the blameless combatant's ignorance must be earned rather than stipulated; and the case must not simply announce the verdicts the theories are meant to reach. Close with the strongest reply available to a defender of the moral-equality doctrine, and say whether it succeeds.
