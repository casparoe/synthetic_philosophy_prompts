I'm a third-year student in a moral philosophy course and my seminar group has split pretty much down the middle over Taurek's line that the numbers don't count — half of us think that if you can save five people or save one, you save the five, full stop, and anyone who says otherwise is being clever for the sake of it; the other half (me, mostly) think there's something real in the idea that you can't just add people up and that treating the one as a means to the five is the thing that needs justifying, not the thing that gets assumed.

We've been going round in circles for three weeks and our tutor finally said we should stop arguing and actually write the kind of document an adversarial collaboration would produce. Can you draft it for us? I want it to be something both sides in my seminar could genuinely put their names to, not a compromise that quietly gives the game away to one side.

What I want in it: first, a numbered list of claims both sides can accept — about what a rescue case is even asking, about what counts as a reason, about what we're allowed to assume about the people involved, about whether this is a question about what's right or about what you could be blamed for. Then the actual points of disagreement, each written in wording that the side holding it would sign off on, not a caricature. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where, if you settled one, at least one side would have to move. And for each crux, a case or argument that both sides agree is relevant, with each side's prediction about how it comes out written down in advance.

Here's the thing I really want it hung on. We came up with two cases ourselves and I suspect they're where the real disagreement is hiding. The first one: you can save one person for certain, or press a button that gives a one-in-three chance of saving six people and otherwise saves nobody. The numbers side seems to get its answer by just multiplying, which feels like it's doing aggregation through the back door; the Taurek side seems to have trouble saying anything at all, because there's no determinate group of six you're failing to save. The second one, which I think is nastier: the single person you can save is also one of the people you'd be saving in the larger group — so the bigger option doesn't leave anyone out, it just adds four more on top of the same person. Does the Taurek side have to admit the numbers count here, or does it have a way out? Does the numbers side have to admit there's a case where they don't?

Also please flag anything you think is just a verbal dispute — where the two sides are using a word like "reason" or "claim" differently and there's no substantive disagreement left once that's fixed. And tell me honestly if one of the two cases is doing no work, because I'd rather cut it than pad the document. Don't worry about citations, I'll add those myself.
