Next month I present to a reading group my argument that iterated amplification and distillation cannot, even in principle, preserve alignment across rounds: distillation trains a student to match the amplified teacher's outputs, but a system's alignment-relevant dispositions are not identifiable from its outputs alone, so the student is underdetermined with respect to alignment and the training signal will select for whichever imitation-optimal shortcuts happen to be cheapest — with the result that each round of distillation launders the previous round's alignment rather than transmitting it. I need to be ready for the objections this particular audience will actually raise, not the ones my own camp would raise.
