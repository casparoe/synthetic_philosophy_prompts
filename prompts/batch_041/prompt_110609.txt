I'm a backend dev, no real philosophy background, but I fell down a Wikipedia hole about George Berkeley — the 18th century Irish bishop, the one the college in California is named after — and it turns out that besides the whole idealism thing he had this whole second career as an economics crank. In the 1730s he wrote a book of questions called The Querist where the basic idea is that money isn't actual wealth, it's just a ticket or a counter that lets a society command and circulate labor and goods, and that what really matters is the industry of the people and keeping stuff moving. He wanted Ireland to start its own bank and issue paper currency and thought hoarding gold/silver was basically a superstition. There's even a line of thought in there that credit rests on opinion and trust more than on metal.

Anyway I rewatched It's a Wonderful Life over the holidays and it hit me that Bedford Falls is like a dramatization of this. The run on the Building and Loan, George telling the panicked crowd their money isn't sitting in the vault, it's out there in the houses and the town and the people's mortgages — that is the ticket/counter view almost beat for beat. And Potter is the exact opposite: money as a scarce thing you own and hoard and use to control people, the miser view.

Point me to specific scenes, not a plot summary.
