My mate at work has gone deep down the many-worlds rabbit hole and he's built an argument that I'm pretty sure proves way too much, but I can't find the bad step. He does infra now but he did a physics degree, so he's definately not talking nonsense, which is the annoying part. Here's the argument, numbered the way he wrote it on the whiteboard:

(1) In many-worlds, every outcome of a quantum measurement actually happens: one copy of me sees spin up, another copy sees spin down.
(2) A probability is a fact about which of a few possible outcomes will happen. When I say a die has a 1/6 chance of landing six, I mean it might land six and it might not, and one of those is what happens.
(3) In many-worlds nothing "might not" happen. Everything happens.
(4) So there are no probabilities in many-worlds. All a probability statement can mean there is "I don't know which branch I'll end up in."
(5) But if it's only me not knowing which branch I end up in, then how much I should bet on each outcome should just go by how many branches there are, split evenly. Two branches, 50/50. If a thousand branches are roughly "up" and one is roughly "down", that's 999 to 1.
(6) That's not the Born rule (probability = amplitude squared). The Born rule weights by amplitude squared, not by counting branches, and every experiment we've ever done comes out matching it.
(7) So many-worlds either hits the Born rule by coincidence or doesn't hit it at all, and either way it doesn't predict the numbers we actually see.
(8) He says this also shows there's no real probability in any deterministic theory. In classical mechanics the coin flip is already settled before it lands, so "50%" there is just ignorance, and all of statistical mechanics is us not knowing things.

I don't buy (8) — stat mech predictions are real predictions, they're the ones that make engines work. And I'd rather not throw out (1) either. So which step do I reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost me? If the fix is that something in the middle is doing sneaky work, tell me what's supposed to be there instead, because "the amplitudes just are the probabilities" sounds like assuming the thing he's meant to be getting out.
