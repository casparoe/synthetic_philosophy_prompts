I am preparing a graduate seminar on the Vienna Circle and have received a summary of Otto Neurath's views on picture languages from a museum-studies textbook. The summary runs roughly as follows: Neurath held that ordinary verbal language is irredeemably vague, metaphysical, and easily abused by political propaganda, so he sought to replace it with ISOTYPE, a universal picture language. Because pictures resemble what they represent, they are self-explanatory and require no conventions or translation; once the Vienna Method had standardized its symbols, social and scientific facts could be communicated directly to anyone, regardless of language or education. This picture language was to be the basis of Neurath's physicalism, which holds that only physical objects exist, and of his protocol sentences, which are foundational reports of immediate experience that picture language could record without distortion. The summary concludes that Neurath's project was a radical break with verbal philosophy and that it promised to dissolve many disputes in the unity of science by replacing argument with visual representation.

Please audit this summary sentence by sentence. Separate what Neurath would accept, what is a common but misleading simplification, and what is simply mistaken. In particular, address how his physicalism and his treatment of protocol sentences relate to his anti-foundationalism and coherentism, what role the transformer and verbal captions played in ISOTYPE, and whether he regarded picture language as a replacement for or an auxiliary to verbal language. Finally, assess whether the summary's conclusion about dissolving philosophical disputes follows from anything Neurath actually maintained, or whether it overstates the ambitions of visual education.
