I would like a consistency audit of my position on the self before I commit it to writing. Here is the profile.

1. Physicalism. Every fact about the mind is a fact about the physical organisation and activity of the system that has it. There is no additional non-physical ingredient, and no mental fact that floats free of the physical.

2. The strange-loop account of the self. The self is not a substance or a homunculus but a self-referential symbol. A system complex enough to model itself generates a representation of itself as a unified agent, and because that representation is tangled with the very processes it represents, the loop closes on itself. What the first-person pronoun picks out is this pattern, not a thing standing beside the processing.

3. Realism about the self. I take this to be fully compatible with (2). There really are selves; ordinary talk of beliefs, intentions and persons is literally true rather than a useful fiction.

4. Privileged access. I have a distinctive first-person epistemic standing with respect to my own mental states. When I sincerely report that I want or believe something, I am better placed than anyone else to be right about it, even if I am not infallible. I also hold that the self-model is transparent — I do not experience it as a model — which is precisely why the self strikes me as simply given rather than constructed.

5. Psychological continuity. What makes me the same person over time is the continuity of this self-model — memory, character, and the ongoing narrative — rather than the persistence of any particular matter.

6. Responsibility. Selves are genuine agents. They are reasons-responsive, and this is sufficient for them to be appropriate targets of blame and praise. I am not a sceptic about moral responsibility.

I suspect I assembled this from writers I admire without checking whether the parts actually fit. Tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which one is doing the most unacknowledged work. Rank the real tensions by severity, state for each what would have to be given up and what that would cost, and identify the single revision that would resolve the most at once.
