I'm a PhD student in the philosophy of mind, and a colleague in my reading group has produced an argument that I cannot shake but also cannot accept. I'd like you to act as an expert philosopher of mind with a background in cognitive neuroscience and help me diagnose it. Please think carefully before you commit to a verdict, and begin by reconstructing what each step is actually committed to and what would have to be true for it to hold; only then tell me where, if anywhere, it fails.

The argument runs as follows:

(1) In patients who have undergone commissurotomy, each hemisphere can independently process stimuli, retain information, and issue reports that the other hemisphere cannot access.
(2) Any system that processes information, retains it, and issues coherent, integrated reports is a subject of experience.
(3) Therefore a commissurotomy patient is at least two subjects of experience.
(4) The only relevant difference between such a patient and an intact person is the bandwidth of information transfer between the hemispheres.
(5) Bandwidth varies continuously, and there is no principled, non-arbitrary threshold at which two partially connected streams of experience merge into a single one.
(6) Therefore an intact person is also, strictly speaking, two or more subjects, the ordinary sense of a single 'I' being a convenient fiction.
(7) So the unity of consciousness is an illusion.

I am content to grant (1), and I am inclined to grant (3) as well; what I cannot accept is (7). I would rather keep (1)–(3) than (7), but I want to know what I am paying for that. Tell me which step to reject, explain why it looked compelling in the first place, and set out what I have to give up in order to reject it. If you think the argument is in fact valid and that one of (1)–(3) has to go instead, then say so and price that option too. One further thing: a friend insisted the argument trades on an ambiguity in 'unity' and that this is the whole of the trouble, but I refuse to be fobbed off with a label — if there is such a shift, show me exactly where it occurs and what it costs.

Please write your answer in British English.
