Write a short joke — a dialogue of roughly 200–300 words — in which a causal decision theorist and an evidential decision theorist are both approached by the same stranger, who flips a coin and makes each of them an offer. Unbeknownst to them, the stranger is running a counterfactual mugging on one of them and a case of XOR blackmail on the other, and neither can tell which problem he is in. The joke should turn on the actual content of the theories: in counterfactual mugging, functional (or updateless) decision theory pays because the decision is subjunctively linked to the counterfactual branch where the coin landed the other way, whereas in XOR blackmail, evidential decision theory wrongly pays because it treats the correlation between payment and the blackmailer's threat as evidence, while functional decision theory refuses. The punchline should be that each theorist, applying his own theory, gets the right answer for the wrong problem — the causal theorist refuses in the mugging (where he should pay) and the evidential theorist pays in the blackmail (where he should refuse). The humor should come from the theories' actual failure modes, not from generic nerd jokes.
