I am writing a novel in which a presentist and an eternalist, both philosophers of time, argue through the night after a funeral. The presentist insists that only the present is real, so the dead are gone; the eternalist insists that all times are equally real, so the dead are still there in their own times. I want their dialogue to be a genuine contest of equals, not a strawman for either side. Let it turn on the truthmaker objection -- what makes it true that Caesar crossed the Rubicon if the past is not real? -- and on the special theory of relativity, which seems to make simultaneity frame-dependent. I would also like them to address the problem of temporary intrinsics: how can a person be both sitting and standing if all times are equally real? Introduce one concrete case: a photograph of the dead child on the table, and let each character use it differently. The dialogue should include real back-and-forth, with each responding to the other's actual points. Please write the dialogue.
