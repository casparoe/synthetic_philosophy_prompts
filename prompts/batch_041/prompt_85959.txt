Here's my opening move, the one that keeps wrecking me. A friend gave me a thought experiment I can't shake: imagine a pill — call it the grey pill — that leaves every brain process exactly as it was but switches off the qualia. Same neurons firing in the same order, same behavior, same verbal reports. My zombie twin takes it, I don't, and we sit side by side, indistinguishable to any neuroscientist in the room. We both say ouch when pricked, we both write papers about the redness of red, we both insist we're in pain right now. By your own account, the only difference between us — that mine actually hurts and his doesn't — is doing no work at all. So which one of us is telling the truth when we say we're in pain? If it's both of us, then the word pain seems to be picking out the brain state and not the feeling, and your precious qualia turn out to be irrelevant to what we mean and what we know. If it's only me, then you owe me a causal story about how the hurting gets into my report, and you've just spent the last hour telling me it can't. And notice the pill doesn't just rob my twin of his experience — it seems to rob you of any account of why anybody's report about their own inner life is evidence of anything.
