I'm a novelist — the book I'm wrestling with right now is about a municipal engineer in a mid-sized city, so I've been reading around the idea that technology isn't neutral, that the things we build can carry a politics inside them rather than just reflecting the politics of whoever paid for them. A friend who actually studied this stuff sat me down and gave me the clean version of the case, and I'm pretty sure it prooves way too much, but I cant find the loose joint. Here's how she put it: (1) An artifact counts as political when its design settles some question about who gets to do what — who gets access, who holds the power, whose way of life the thing quietly assumes. (2) But you can say that about almost anything: a door that only swings inward, a curb with no ramp, a chair bolted to the floor, a highway on-ramp that assumes everyone has a car. (3) So almost every artifact is political. (4) And once everything down to a soup spoon is political, the claim that artifacts have politics stops doing any work — it can't sort the loaded cases from the innocent ones, so it's either true and empty or it's just false. (5) The examples everyone trots out — the bridge built just low enough that the buses can't get under it, the harvesting machine that only pays off if you already farm at industrial scale, the machinery brought into the factory specifically to break the union — look special only because we already know the backstory, not because there's anything in the object itself. (6) So the thesis is trivial or it's wrong.

Which of those steps is doing the damage, and what does it cost me to give it up? I'd rather keep (1) and the bridge example than keep the whole thing tidy, but I don't want to cheat to save it. And honestly — if the setup itself is confused, like 'political' is just sliding between two different senses and that's the entire answer, then please say so plainly instead of walking me through the numbered list as if it deserved respect. I'd rather be told my friend's argument is a mess than get a polite tour of it.
