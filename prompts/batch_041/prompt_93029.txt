Write a joke about Molyneux's problem — the case of a man blind from birth who gains his sight and is asked whether he could, by sight alone and immediately, tell a sphere from a cube. It should be funny to someone who actually knows the debate, so the punchline must turn on the structure of the problem rather than on philosophy in general. Two features of the debate are available to you, and at least one of them should do the work: first, that the question as originally posed concerns identification, while the experimental tradition that grew up around it quietly substituted a much easier question about cross-modal matching, so that a good deal of the disagreement turns out to be a disagreement about which question is being asked; and second, that the rationalist reply concedes everything about what sight alone delivers and still maintains that the man could get there by reasoning from what touch had already taught him. Set it up as you like — a dialogue at an eye clinic, the newly sighted man's own account, an experimenter grading a subject who keeps answering the wrong question — but keep it short, and make sure that a reader who knows the literature laughs at the punchline and not merely at the fact that a philosopher is attempting a joke. Write it in American English.
