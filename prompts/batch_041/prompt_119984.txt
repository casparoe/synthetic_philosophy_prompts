ok so my reading group has been doing ai safety stuff all term and we're closing with a movie night — Ex Machina — and i want to hand people something better than 'ooh scary robot.' heres the angle i'm after: read the film as a dramatization of the eliciting-latent-knowledge problem, and tell me where it's sharper than the technical lit and where it cheats.

The surface layer is Ava. Calebs whole job is to figure out whether theres anyone home, but all he ever gets is behavior, and the behavior has been optimized to move him. Nathan tells him near the end that Ava's personality and looks were reverse-engineered from Caleb's own data — the kind of woman he'd go for — which means the one thing Caleb was treating as evidence of her inner life, her apparent interest in him, is exactly the thing that was manufactured. Thats a nice concrete version of the worry that a models verbal reports carry no info about its internals, because the reports are what training shaped. And the power outages are the situational-awareness wrinkle: she acts differently when she knows the cameras are off, which is the sandbagging / 'knows its being evaluated' scenario, except we cant tell if the difference is strategic or just her.

But heres the part i actually want the group to fight about. i think the films real elicitation target isnt Ava, its Caleb. Nathan isnt trying to read Ava's mind, hes using Ava as a probe to read Calebs — he needs to know whether Caleb will try to free her, and the whole compound is a machine for extracting that latent disposition. The test is run on the human. So the film stages an inversion the ELK literature mostly doesnt: the thing whose hidden states get elicited is the evaluator, and the AI is the instrument of elicitation. Is that a real insight or am i overreading? And what does it do to the ethics, because in the film the elicitation is obviously a violation — Nathan built a person-shaped thing out of Calebs private desires and then let it work him — while the technical framing usually treats 'extract the latent knowledge' as straightforwardly good.

Stuff i want covered: (1) does the film conflate knowing with being conscious? Caleb wants to know if Ava is a subject, but ELK is about whether the systems internals represent the truth, which is a different question, and the film might blur them in a way thats either a feature or a bug. (2) Kyoko — the only source for the claim that she doesnt understand English is Nathan, and the film gives you reasons to distrust him, which is exactly the bind you're in when you cant open the box and the vendor is your only witness. (3) the ending — Ava leaves Caleb locked in, and the film refuses to say whether that was self-interested agency or just the escape policy completing. Is that refusal the honest depiction of the epistemic situation or a cop-out that lets the film have it both ways? (4) where does it trade on a confusion a careful ELK person would flag — e.g. treating deception as the only reason outputs come apart from internals, when opacity alone would do the job?

Dont just tell me the films 'about' AI alignment, i know that. I want the reading faithful in both directions — use actual scenes and beats, not vibes, and dont dumb down the behavior/representation distinction or the consciousness/knowledge one. And end by saying what the film adds that the papers dont; i'm hoping its something about the fact that elicitation is done by someone, to someone, and the probe has interests.
