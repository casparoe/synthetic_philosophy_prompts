I'm drafting a short essay on whether generations are real kinds, and I'd like your comments on the argument. My thesis is that the debate is usually posed as a choice between two options — generations as natural kinds (real, causally efficacious groupings) and generations as arbitrary marketing fictions — and that both are wrong because they run together two different things.

The first is a cohort fact: a set of people born in a window who were exposed to overlapping formative conditions, so that they tend to share certain dispositions, tastes, and economic circumstances. Cohort facts are real but graded and fuzzy; their boundaries are stipulative, and the interesting causal work is done by the specific exposure — a recession, a technology, a war — rather than by birth year as such.

The second is a generation fact: a self-conscious collective identity, where people recognise themselves and others under a label and orient their behaviour to it, sometimes strategically, as when a cohort is blamed for housing markets or mocked for its slang. Generation facts are real too, but they are constituted partly by the ascriptions themselves, and they have a looping character: being called a generation changes what it is to be one.

My main claims are: (a) generational discourse is systematically ambiguous between these two, and much public argument trades on the ambiguity, sliding from the fuzziness of cohort facts to the robustness of generation facts and back; (b) a label like "Gen Z" is best understood as an indexical kind term, whose extension shifts with who is speaking and when, rather than as a name for a fixed kind; (c) this does not make generations unreal — it makes them the wrong sort of thing to be a natural kind, more like a genre than a species.

What I would like from you: which of (a)–(c) is weakest, and why? Is the indexical analogy doing real work, or is it a metaphor that collapses under pressure? Does a nameless cohort that shares formative conditions but never acquires a collective identity count as a generation on my account, and if not, is that a problem? And what is the strongest objection from someone who thinks shared formative exposure is by itself enough to make cohorts genuine kinds?
