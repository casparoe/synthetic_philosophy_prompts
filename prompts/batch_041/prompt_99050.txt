I am writing an entry for my school's philosophy prize, and I need a thought experiment built to order. I want a single case that drives a wedge between hedonistic utilitarianism and preference utilitarianism: the hedonistic version must imply that a handful of ordinary people should be sacrificed for the sake of one being with an enormously greater capacity for enjoyment, while the preference version must imply that they should not. Neither verdict may be reached by quietly attributing a bad version of the theory to it, and neither may be asserted rather than derived. Constraints: an ordinary setting such as a hospital or a care home; the being in question should be a human with an unusual condition, not a fantastical creature, and its moral status should be uncontroversial, so that nothing turns on infants or animals; one paragraph; no infinite or unbounded utility; and the divergence must turn on a concrete feature of the case rather than on an ambiguity in the word utility. After the scenario, set out what each theory says and why, identify the feature doing the work and what becomes of both verdicts if it is removed, and explain how a hedonistic utilitarian would argue that the case is rigged. Write in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables, and in British English.
