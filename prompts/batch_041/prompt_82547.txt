I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I want to defend a version of pattern-realism about the intentional stance. The pragmatist version Dennett made famous ties the reality of a pattern to what is useful for describers like us to notice; mine says instead that a pattern is real just in case it would be the best compression of a system's behavior for an ideal compressor, actual describers' limits aside. Tell me what should count against this, ranked by how decisive each consideration is — in particular, whether the ideal-compressor move collapses back into observer-relative anti-realism, or instead smuggles in the stance-independent, intrinsic facts the original view was meant to avoid. Which of these objections would a sympathetic pattern-realist concede, and which would they simply dismiss? Can my version still do the work the three-stance framework needs — sorting real patterns from gerrymandered ones — and does it predict or forbid anything its rivals don't, or has it been insulated by stipulation?
