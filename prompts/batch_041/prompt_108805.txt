I moved across the world for work about four months ago — new country, smallish city — and most of my energy has gone into figuring out the local rules, and the thing that keeps triping me up is that the rules don't hang together. A handful of things here are absolutely rigid. You do not show up late to a family thing. You do not wear the wrong thing to a religious event. You do not eat certain things in front of certain people. Get any of that wrong and it's not a scolding, you're just quietly out — enforced hard, by everyone watching everyone, no officials involved. But in the very same place, traffic is a free-for-all, queues are suggestions, half the permits get "sorted out" with a phone call, and nobody seems to care at all. My old city was the mirror image: nobody gave a damn what you wore, but everything was written down and enforced by forms and inspectors.

I keep hearing the phrase "tight cultures" and "loose cultures," and I get the gist, but it seems to want to sort a whole place into one bucket, whereas what I'm looking at is a place that's both at once, just about different things. Some domains are sacred and rigid, others are totally negotiable, and the rigid ones don't seem to be the important ones in any obvious sense — they're just the ones that got marked.

Is there a name for this? And I don't mean I just want a label — I want to know how what I'm describing differs from the tight/loose idea, from whatever the "sacred values" people are talking about, and from honor-culture stuff, and which of those, if any, actually fits. Is "tightness is domain-specific" already a known claim, or am I reinventing something? And is the fact that the rigid norms are enforced informally — by shunning rather than by any authority — a separate axis, or is that part of the same thing?

Smaller second version, in case it helps: my company is like this too. Career-ending, obsessive rules about how you log your hours and about not sharing certain documents, and then literally everything else is chaos — no dress code, no set hours, no process for anything. Same shape.

Where I'd like you to be careful: keep what's actually established separate from what's just a plausible-sounding story. If the tight/loose framework is contested, or leans on shaky cross-cultural data, say that instead of presenting it as settled, and don't hand me one researcher's pet theory dressed up as the consensus.
