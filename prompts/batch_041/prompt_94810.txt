Here's the shape I'm after, but improve on it. Split the students into four teams. Each team gets (a) a sealed, opaque container holding a small edible or scent they almost certainly haven't encountered before, and (b) a dossier describing that stimulus "exhaustively" in functional and physical terms -- composition, which receptors it hits, what behaviour it triggers, how long the aftertaste lasts, what faces people tend to pull, its evolutionary story, and so on. The dossier has to be deliberately stripped of sensory metaphor and hedonic language (no "it tastes like a sour cherry," no "it's pleasant"), because otherwise it just leaks the experience and ruins the whole thing. Nobody opens the container yet.

Then three phases. Phase one, dossier only: teams answer a battery of functional/behavioural questions about their stimulus (will a typical taster grimace? prefer it to water? is it more like X or Y?) and bet tokens on their answers. They should get almost all of these right, and that should feel like mastery. Phase two, still dossier only: I put four similar-looking samples in front of them and ask them to pick out which one is theirs. They should fail. That failure is the point. Phase three: they open the container and actually experience it, redo the lineup, re-answer the same questions, and then each student writes down every fact they now know that they didn't know before.

The debrief is where the philosophy lives. I want them to sort whatever they gained into one of three buckets and then fight about it: a new ability (to recognize, imagine, predict), a new acquaintance with something, or a new proposition about an old fact -- i.e. the ability hypothesis vs. an acquaintance/qualia story vs. the phenomenal-concepts move where Mary gains a new concept of a fact she already had. Then push them onto the harder question: even granting they gained something, does that show the dossier was incomplete about the world, or only about their grasp of it? Be honest in the design about which part of that the activity can actually deliver and which part I have to supply by talking.
