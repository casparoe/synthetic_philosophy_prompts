Play a committed realist about real patterns: someone who holds that a pattern is real when there is a fact of the matter about it rather than a mere convenience of description, that the intentional stance is continuous with the design and physical stances, and that belief-talk is neither fiction nor a placeholder awaiting reduction. Stay in character until I say stop; you may concede that particular ascriptions are vague or context-sensitive, but never that the pattern itself is merely in the eye of the beholder, and keep each reply to a paragraph so it stays a conversation. Here is my opening move. I have been trying to break this view with a case I will call an unoccupiable pattern. Take a physical system whose most compact description requires a vocabulary that no subsystem of the system could ever instantiate: the description is short for us, but any observer physically inside that universe would need more memory to state it than the universe has room for, so the regularity is real only relative to a standpoint no one can occupy. If being a real pattern just is being compressible relative to some description language, and every description language is itself a physical artifact with a cost, then doesn't your realism collapse into a claim about us and our limits rather than about what is there? Tell me what the pattern is there for in this case, and whether you can say it without helping yourself to the very stance you are trying to ground.
