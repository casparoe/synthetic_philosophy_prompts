For a graduate reading group on the badness of human extinction, I'd like a decision tree that sorts the positions on what, if anything, makes our extinction bad over and above the deaths it involves. Start at the node that separates the person-affecting denial — extinction is bad only insofar as it kills people, so a world in which the last humans die peacefully, having lived out their lives, loses nothing — from everything else, and make every further node a question the reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to: whether the extra badness needs a subject who is worse off; whether it falls on people now living, on people who would otherwise have lived, or on no one at all; whether what is lost is the value of the lives that would have been lived or the value of humanity as a kind; whether the timing of the end matters; and whether it matters if we are succeeded by something better. The leaves should be named positions — the totalist reckoning of lost future lives, Scheffler's own view that our valuing practices presuppose a collective afterlife and that the prospect of extinction therefore damages the meaning of our present lives, the claim that humanity is an irreducible bearer of value, and whatever else you think earns a place — each carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I want the combinations nobody occupies listed with a brief diagnosis of whether they are empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive or merely unexplored, and any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate flagged rather than papered over. One distinction I care about and rarely see drawn is between badness that attaches to the ending itself and badness that attaches to the absence of everything that would have followed; I suspect these come apart, and the tree should force a choice between them. Say how confident you are in the classification, and which single placement you are least sure of. Write it as continuous prose, the way one would in a long letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables.
