I'm assembling materials for a working group on the naturalization of mental content, and I need a document that two of the leading camps in that area have never actually produced between them: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the teleosemantic camp and the indicator-semantic camp. By the teleosemantic camp I mean those who hold that a state's representational content is fixed by the teleological proper function it was selected to serve — a state represents what it is supposed to indicate, and misrepresentation is a failure of that function. By the indicator-semantic camp I mean those who hold that content is fixed by an actual or idealized causal-informational relation between the state and what it tracks — a state represents what it reliably carries information about — and who regard teleology as either unnecessary or not yet shown to be doing independent explanatory work. Take it that both camps agree mental content is real and that a naturalistic account of it is both wanted and possible; what they dispute is what the account is.

Draft the joint statement. It should contain, in order: first, a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, formulated so that neither is quietly conceding its position — covering what they jointly take the explananda to be (successful behavior, misrepresentation, the possibility of content in creatures unlike us) and which formulations of the problem they jointly reject as ill-posed; second, a table of the disagreements that remain, each row giving the crux in neutral wording, the teleosemantic position phrased as that camp would accept it, and the indicator-semantic position phrased as that camp would accept it; third, for each crux, a diagnostic case or argument both camps agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Construct those cases rather than reusing the stock ones, and make sure the set includes at least one case in which a system has a selection history but no current reliable correlation with what it is supposed to track, and at least one in which it has a current reliable correlation but no selection history. End by telling me which of the listed disagreements you judge to be merely verbal — disputes about what "representation" or "content" is to mean rather than about any fact — and which are substantive, and why.

No citations, bibliography, or reading list; I will supply those myself. Take a definite stand throughout: commit to particular formulations, and to a specific verdict on which disagreements dissolve and which do not, rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me.
