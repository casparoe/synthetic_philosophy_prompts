Right, I've got a pile of gut verdicts about when it's OK to free-ride on something shared, and I want to know what rule, if any, they add up to. Please actually commit to a principle rather than laying out the options and letting me choose.

Ground rules: I'm one household, one person, basically negligible on my own. Here's the list.

- I never donate to Wikipedia despite using it every day. Fine.
- Not voting in a seat that's a dead cert for one party. Fine.
- Chucking the odd bit of plastic in the general bin instead of rinsing it for recycling. Fine, more or less.
- Not putting a fiver in for Dave's leaving present when there's twenty of us and someone's already bought it. Not fine, actually.
- Skipping my week on the allotment weeding rota because I know Priya will do it if I don't. Not fine.
- Reading the shared group-project doc and never adding a word, while the others grind away. Not fine.
- Bailing on a massive march that'll happen with or without me. Fine. But bailing on a tiny one where the organisers need bodies to keep the permit. Not fine.
- One boat quietly cheating its quota while the fishery collapses anyway. Still wrong, I reckon, even though the collapse happens regardless.
- Leaving the communal kitchen a tip once. Not fine.
- Crowdfunding a mate's surgery that'll hit its target with or without my tenner. I feel fine not giving.
- The office coffee fund: I drink it, I don't pay in, and I feel like a scrounger even though it's pennies a week.

The two I can't square. First, Wikipedia (fine) versus the coffee fund (not fine), even though the coffee costs me less and the website is a far bigger thing. Second, the big march (fine) versus the small one (not fine), when the nudge I make is equally tiny in both.

My half-baked stab: it's fine to free-ride when my contribution makes no difference to whether the good comes off, and not fine when it does. But that can't be it, because 'no difference' covers the present, the rota and the doc, and I only excuse the first of those. Then I wondered if the real split is rivalrous versus non-rivalrous — me catching fish leaves fewer for you, me reading Wikipedia doesn't. But the rota isn't rivalrous either and I still say skipping it is wrong, so that's not it either, definately not on its own.

So: is there one principle here, or a small set of candidates, and what does it actually say? I want it tight enough that I could apply it to a case I haven't thought of. Please make it pronounce on at least these: (a) an anonymous rota where nobody can tell I skipped; (b) a shared thing where I'm the only person who can fix it and it's a right faff; (c) whether it matters that some goods need a certain number of people to tip them over the line while others just take whatever slivers they can get. And be straight with me about which of the verdicts above your principle has to overturn — I'd rather you told me plainly than fudged it to save them all. Pick a side and give me your reasons.
