I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on virtue ethics and the situationist challenge, and I want it to be an actual decision tree, not a list of the usual four responses everyone gives. So: a tree where every branch node is a question a student can answer without already knowing which leaf its going to land them on, and every leaf is a named position -- or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it -- with one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment. Say 8-10 leaves, 6-8 branch nodes, and I'd rather it be built from the logical space first and have the actual positions dropped into it than the reverse.

Use these as the diagnostic cases, since they're the ones my students will have heard of: the seminary students who were running late to give a talk and stepped over the guy slumped in the doorway; the one where someone finds a coin in the phone's return slot and then helps a stranger who dropped their papers; Milgram; Asch; the interwar character-education studies where the same kid cheats in one classroom and not in another; and the delay-of-gratification stuff with kids and a bell. No new cases needed.

The distinctions I actually care about, and want the branches to turn on: whether the challenge is read as normative (it tells us something about what to do or admire) or empirical (it tells us something about what people are like); whether the position treats virtue as a disposition to act or as something more like a commitment or a way of caring that doesn't predict behaviour situation-by-situation; whether the position is globalist or localist about traits; whether it survives if the classic studies turn out not to replicate; and whether the position needs eudaimonism at all.

Please flag any leaves the tree can't really seperate -- I suspect the local-traits view and the if-then person-profile view collapse into each other, but tell me if that's wrong. And note the combinations nobody occupies, with a short diagnosis of whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

One more thing: if I've set this up badly -- if the challenge splits before the tree even starts, or if some of the branches I named are really the same branch wearing two hats -- just say so and tell me what you'd change instead of building the thing anyway. And mark which leaf descriptions are genuinely standard and which are your gloss, since I'll be reading this out loud.
