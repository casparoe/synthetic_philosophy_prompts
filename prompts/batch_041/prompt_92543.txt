Before a reading group next month I've been asked to write up where I actually stand on consequentialism so the others can attack it, and I'd rather find the weak joints myself than have them found for me in public. Here is the package as I'd state it. (1) The rightness of an action is fixed by the consequences it actually has, not by what the agent foresaw or had reason to believe. (2) For nearly any decision I face, the downstream consequences of the options ramify so far, and through causal chains so long and so entangled with other people's choices, that I have no real basis for assigning credences to the relevant outcomes; my honest state is cluelessness rather than uncertainty with well-defined probabilities. (3) A moral theory has to be action-guiding: it cannot be that I ought to do something when no one, not even an ideal reasoner in my position, could have worked out that I ought to. (4) All the same, I am confident that giving a serious fraction of my income to the most effective global-health charities is required, and that spending the same money on a nicer flat instead is at least mildly wrong; I don't think that verdict is hostage to philosophy. (5) Where options are comparable at all, I maximize expected welfare on my credences, and I don't think moral uncertainty licenses any special hedging beyond folding it into that calculation. (6) I won't accept the usual rescue, which says: stop calculating and simply follow the rules or institutions that tend to go well, because whether a rule tends to go well is itself exactly the kind of cluelessness-laden empirical question at issue.

What I want is an audit rather than a verdict on consequentialism as such: which of these pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and for each real conflict which commitment has to give and what that costs. Rank the conflicts by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say which single revision would clean up the most, since I suspect some of these are cheaper to fix than they look and others much more expensive. I also don't know this literature as well as I should, so please separate what is settled among people who work on it from what is genuinely contested — in particular I don't want a contested assessment of how damaging the cluelessness problem really is handed to me as though it were the consensus.
