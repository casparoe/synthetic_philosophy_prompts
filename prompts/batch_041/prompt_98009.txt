My sister's getting married next month and she's asked me to give the toast. The wrinkle: she's finishing a PhD in philosophy and her whole dissertation is on L.A. Paul's thing about transformative experience -- the idea that some big life choices (having a kid, becoming a vampire, uprooting to another country) change you so much that you literally can't know in advance what they'll be like from the inside, so the usual "list the pros and cons and pick the best option" model of rational choice kind of falls apart. She's got her own spin on it too, which she's defaintely told me about at least four times: that getting married is promising something on behalf of a person who doesn't exist yet, because future-you -- the one who'll actually be doing the being-married -- gets made by the very choice you're making now. Half the room at the reception will be her department, who'll catch every nod; the other half is our very warm, very emotional, non-academic family who just want to hear something lovely about the two of them. She told me beforehand, only half joking, that if I stand up and say "you two are perfect for each other and you'll be happy forever," she'll disown me, because on her view nobody can honestly know that going in. So write me a four-to-five minute toast. It needs to be genuinely moving and funny and land as love rather than as a seminar, and it can't smuggle in any claim she'd have to object to. And it has to actually do something with the philosophy -- not just name-drop it once and move on -- so the idea shapes what I'm allowed to promise on their behalf and what I have to leave open.
