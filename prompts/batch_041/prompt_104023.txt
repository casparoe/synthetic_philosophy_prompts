OK so I'm a science-and-ideas reporter, and I've been rereading that late Simone Weil essay where she wants political parties abolished altogether. My editor wants somthing lighter for the year-end issue, and I've got a running bet with a colleague that you can actually make a joke out of Weil's anti-party argument that a person who's read her would laugh at — not just nerd snark.

Here's the raw material as I understand it (tell me if I've got her wrong): a party, for her, is less a vehicle for opinions than a machine for manufacturing them. It needs its members to share a collective passion, and to get that it asks them to stop thinking for themselves about anything the party has a line on; parties also corner the market in expressing political opinion, so citizens end up picking between pre-packaged positions instead of forming any of their own; and because a party sits closer and more intimately to you than the state does, its grip on your mind is actually worse than the state's. Hence: abolish them.

So give me a joke, or a short comic scene, that turns on that actual content. Not "parties, am I right." The punchline should be something only someone who knows her argument gets — it could exploit the way the party form is supposed to be self-undermining, or the demand for unanimity of thought, or the strange position of a party of one. Then, briefly, tell me where the joke takes liberties with her ideas.

One more thing, and I mean it: in that explanation, keep what's actually settled about her view seperate from what's genuinely contested among people who know her work — there's real disagreement about how literally she meant the abolition, about how democratic she was being, about how much she's just Rousseau with better prose — so don't hand me a contested reading as if it were the consensus. I'd rather have a thin joke than a confident wrong gloss.
