Two of the group are dyed-in-the-wool adaptationists who think the critics just dislike where the conclusions lead, two think the whole research programme is unfalsifiable story-telling, and after six sessions nobody's budged an inch. I want to make them write the document they should have produced months ago: the joint statement of an adverserial collaboration.

So: the claims both sides can actually sign — about what the evidence base really looks like, about what the environment of evolutionary adaptedness was and wasn't, about what would even count as a psychological adaptation, about which findings are solid enough to build on; then the disagreements that remain, each phrased the way the side holding it would want it phrased; then the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move somebody; and for each crux a test or case that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two bits I want done properly rather than waved at. First, modularity: how much domain-specific machinery does the programme actually need, and what should each side say the cheater-detection work and the deontic/permission versions of the selection task really show? Second, the falsifiablity charge itself — have each side spell out concretely what a failed prediction would look like for a flagship case (language, religion, moral judgment, whatever they pick), and whether they'd count it as evidence against their own view. I suspect at least one of the rows in the table is really a fight about what counts as a prediction rather than about the mind, so if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.

Numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table with the cruxes and predictions in it. No references needed, I care about the substance. British English please.
