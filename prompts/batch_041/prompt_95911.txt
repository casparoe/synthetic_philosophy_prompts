So this started in my group chat because someone claimed you can't forecast AGI timelines "because of Popper," and it turned into a whole fight, and now I need an adult. Context: I've been lurking on rationalist blogs since the SSC days, so I've absorbed a lot of Popper by osmosis, but I've never actually read The Poverty of Historicism. My friend — smart guy, read all teh Sequences, says "not even wrong" unironically — sent me his summary of Popper's case against laws of history, and I have a nagging feeling he's flattened it. Here's what he wrote, roughly verbatim:

"Popper basically proved there can't be laws of history. The argument is airtight: any real theory of history would have to predict the future course of society, but the future depends on future knowledge, and you can't predict future knowledge, because to predict a discovery you'd already have to have made it. So a science of historical development is a contradiction in terms. He's also got the Oedipus effect — predictions change the behavior they predict, so acting on a law of history would break it, which is a second, independent reason the whole thing is a nonstarter. And since Popper's whole thing is falsifiability, and historicist doctrines can't be falsified, they're not just unscientific, they're meaningless; that's why he lumps Hegel, Marx and Spengler together as pseudoscience and blames that whole tradition for the 20th century's body count. Takeaway: history is basically random, one thing after another, there are no patterns, sociology and econ can only ever be descriptive, and the deep reason is that the future isn't determined in the first place — Popper was an indeterminist, and if it were all determined you could just compute it from the initial conditions, which is exactly the fantasy the historicists were selling. Oh and this is the same historicism people complain about in architecture and lit crit, where everything's relative to its period and there's no objective standard."

For what it's worth I mostly buy the conclusion — I dont think there's a science of history either — I just don't trust the reasoning, and I can't tell which parts of that paragraph are load-bearing Popper and which are vibes.
