I'm setting the final exam for an upper-level seminar on the intentional stance, and I need one essay question that a competent summary of the physical/design/intentional trichotomy cannot answer. Build it on this case. Two systems, Index and Net, have identical input–output profiles over the same fixed battery of questions: same answers, same reported confidence, same response times, and both are artifacts whose internals we could in principle read off in full. Index answers by a giant stored lookup; Net answers by a compact generalizing model whose internal states compress the battery rather than store it. On Dennett's own account of what makes a pattern real — a pattern is real in some data when there is a description of the data more efficient than the bit map — Index's behaviour exhibits no real pattern at the intentional level while Net's does, despite the behavioural identity. The question should force students to (a) say whether belief attribution is warranted for Net and unwarranted for Index on identical behavioural evidence, and whether that verdict is a consequence of the stance or merely of our descriptive convenience; (b) say whether the stance was ever motivated by our ignorance of mechanism, and what becomes of it once the mechanism is fully inspectable; and (c) say whether, for Net, there is a fact of the matter about which content it believes when several incompatible content schemes predict its outputs equally well, or whether content is as indeterminate as the Quinean tradition the stance grows out of would suggest. It should be answerable in about ninety minutes.

Give me the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 built around five must-hit points, at least one of which is the move that separates an A from a B, plus a one-line note for each point on how candidates typically lose the mark.
