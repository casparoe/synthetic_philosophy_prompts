I was rereading Turing's 1950 Mind paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence and got stuck on the bit near the end where he brings up telepathy. He doesn't dismiss it — he says the statistical evidence for telepathy is strong enough that he can't just wave it away — and so he suggests the imitation game be run in a room that would screen out telepathy, so the interrogator can't pick out the machine by mind-reading rather than by what's actually in the answers.

Now, the thing that got me going: psi researchers who actually run these experiments do basically the same thing. The reciever sits in a Faraday cage, the room is sound-proofed, the sender and receiver are isolated, the target is randomly selected, guesses are recorded before anyone compares notes. So there's a real, ongoing experimental practice of shielding and isolation, and Turing's proposal is a cousin of it.

What I want from you: what philosophical commitments does that shielding practice presuppose? It looks like it presupposes that telepathy is a signal that travels through space and can be blocked — which is already a pretty specific theory of what telepathy would be, and not the only one on offer. Does it also presuppose that the mind stops at the skin, or that every relevant causal channel is physical? Turing's own version seems to presuppose something slightly different again: that the imitation game is measuring something that could be contaminated by an extra channel, so the test only counts as valid if every channel is closed. Does that quietly commit him to a behavioral criterion of intelligence in a way that sits awkwardly with him taking ESP seriously at all?

And how underdetermined is the practice? Could a dualist, or someone who thinks telepathy is real but not a spatially transmitted signal, run the exact same experiments and just get null results for an entirely different reason? If the shielding does work, does that show the channel is physical, or only that whatever it is happens to be sensitive to Faraday cages and distance?

Then tell me what would have to change if we swapped the presupposed view for a rival. If telepathy were real but wasn't a signal — say it's non-local, or depends on some kind of resonance between minds rather than transmission — would the whole shielding methodology be the wrong shape, and would Turing's test need a different kind of fix? Separate out what the practice strictly needs from what it just happens to fit well with.
