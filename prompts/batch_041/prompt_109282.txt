I have drafted the following short essay for a collection on conceptual problems in cognitive science, and I would value your comments before I revise it. I am less interested in whether I have cited the right literature than in whether the argument holds up and whether the central distinction is worth making.

Essay: The Frame Problem Is Not About Frames

The frame problem is usually introduced as the difficulty of representing what does not change when an action is performed. But this description lumps together three different difficulties. The first is the update problem: after an agent learns that it will rain, how does it know that the cat is still on the mat, that the treaty is still signed, and that its shoes are still by the door? The second is the relevance problem: how does it know which of its countless beliefs bear on whether to take an umbrella? The third is the salience problem: how does it know which changes matter for its goals? I argue that the first two are engineering problems, soluble in principle by default rules, caching, and attention mechanisms, while the third is not a problem about computation at all. It is the problem of specifying what matters, and no finite set of update rules can solve it without a prior specification of the agent's project.

Consider a museum guard robot whose task is to protect the paintings. When a fire starts, the robot must treat the fire as relevant. But it must also treat the color of its own uniform as irrelevant, even though the uniform is represented and even though its color may change if the robot passes under a colored light. The point is not that the robot cannot infer that the uniform's color is unchanged. The point is that no formal rule tells it that uniform color is irrelevant to painting protection while fire is relevant, unless the task has already been described in a way that marks the difference. I conclude that the frame problem, properly understood, is a special case of the problem of relevance, and that AI has misdescribed it as a problem about non-monotonic logic.

I would be grateful for comments on the following: (1) Is the three-way distinction between update, relevance, and salience stable, or does the salience problem collapse into the relevance problem? (2) Does the museum example actually separate the update problem from the relevance problem, or does it illicitly assume a solution to the former? (3) Is the claim that the third problem is not an AI problem defensible, or is it just a restatement of the AI problem at a higher level? (4) Is the conclusion too strong? I am also happy to hear about places where I have conflated the frame problem with the qualification problem or the ramification problem, but please keep the comments conceptual and do not supply reading lists or citations.
