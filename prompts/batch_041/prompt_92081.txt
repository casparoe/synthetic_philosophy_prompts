OK so I'm teaching a seminar on Hume this term -- we've been through the moral philosophy and now I'm doing a couple of weeks on the economic essays, the money and trade stuff, where he's trying to convince you that a nation's wealth isn't its stock of gold and that the balance of trade more or less takes care of itself through price movements. Fine. The problem is that my students have all converged on "money is a veil" as the One Big Takeaway, and they're now using it to win arguments with. Last week one of them informed me that worrying about inflation is basically a category error, because money is just a veil over the real economy; a different one announced that trade deficits therefore can't possibly matter; and a third seemed to mean that only "real" things are worth caring about at all. I'm fairly sure the phrase is being asked to carry alot of incompatible jobs at once and nobody's noticing the switches.

What I'd like from you is a proper disambiguation, laid out as a menu rather than a lecture. Take "money is a veil" and give me the distinct precise claims it could be making, roughly from the trivial to the radical -- e.g. that money is merely a unit of account and adds nothing to the goods it measures; that changes in the money supply have no causal effect on output or employment, at least in the long run; that monetary phenomena have no bearing on welfare; that money is a means and never an end; whatever else is hiding in there. For each reading I want a verdict -- true, false, or genuinely open -- and, crucially, whether it would actually license the moves my students are making with it. If some of these readings collapse into each other or turn out to be trivial once stated precisely, say so; if I've missed a reading and that's the one really doing the work, tell me. Then finish by telling me which reading (if any) is closest to what Hume is actually up to in those essays, and which reading my students almost certainly have in mind when they use the line to shut a discussion down. Assume I know the essays but not the secondary literature, and please don't send me to anything -- I just want the menu and your verdicts on it.
