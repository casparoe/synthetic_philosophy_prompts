I'm a philosopher of science, and I'm helping design a small three-year research programme — about four full-time researchers, half philosophers and half interpretability engineers — on a question that keeps slipping away from us: in mechanistic anomaly detection, when is a newly flagged mechanism the same mechanism as one we already understand? The working framework I've been developing treats mechanism identity as distribution-relative: two components can be counterfactually equivalent across the training distribution yet diverge off it, so sameness may only hold relative to a regime, a task, or a specified family of interventions. Alongside that, we distinguish three candidate criteria for identity — sameness of causal role (inputs, outputs, and intervention profile), sameness of computational procedure, and sameness of representational content — and we suspect they come apart in cases that matter. What I want from you is a prioritisation. Which open questions in this area most deserve the programme's effort, and in what order? For each, explain why it is upstream of the others, whether it is tractable for people doing conceptual work rather than running experiments, and how neglected it currently is. I am at least as interested in what to drop: which candidate questions look important but would not change how anyone builds, audits, or trusts an anomaly detector even if they were settled. Please also say what developments over the next two or three years would move a question up or down your ranking. Give me a defended agenda, not a survey.
