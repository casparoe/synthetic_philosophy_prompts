I've read On Liberty and some of the Principles, so I have a rough sense of Mill, but the socialism stuff is new to me and I dont want to nod along to something wrong. Here's what he wrote:

"Mill's late views on socialism are usually understated. By the end of his life he had basically become a socialist. In the posthumously published Chapters on Socialism he accepts that the socialist critique of private property is correct and that the existing distribution of wealth is indefensible. He says that if he had to choose between communism and the present state of society, he would choose communism. That's a pretty clear break from the liberalism of On Liberty. His one serious worry was incentives — he thought people might not work hard under socialism. He also held that while the laws of production are fixed, the laws of distribution are a matter of human choice, which means we are free to redistribute or socialize property however we like. So his late position is a kind of liberal socialism: he keeps the liberty stuff but drops the property stuff."
