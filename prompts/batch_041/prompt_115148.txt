I host a small interview series on how people form expectations about the future — the audience has read the popular forecasting literature but not the academic version — and next week's guest is a forecaster-turned-philosopher whose position I would like help interrogating. She spent a decade producing long-horizon cost and schedule estimates for large public infrastructure projects, and has since argued that the received distinction between the inside view and the outside view does less work than its friends suppose. Her thesis, roughly, is that the outside view is never independent of the inside view: a reference class is constituted by a prior judgment about which features of the present case matter, so what we call the outside view is really the inside view of a slower and larger agent — and that most appeals to epistemic modesty in forecasting practice function as a ritual for distributing blame rather than as a corrective to overconfidence. She is careful to add that she still takes modesty to be a genuine virtue; she simply locates it somewhere unusual, in the disposition to keep the choice of reference class open to revision, rather than in any lowering of one's stated credences. She is also very good at closing off challenges by saying "that's an empirical question" and then declining to say what the empirical question is.

Please write me exactly five numbered questions for the interview, each followed by a single follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, with no preamble, framing, or closing remarks. One question should force her to explain how a reference class gets selected without an inside-view judgment having already smuggled in the answer, and what stops that from regressing; one should press whether her deflationary account is distinguishable from the flat claim that base rates are irrelevant, given how convenient her critique would be to someone insisting that this case is different; one should take up the social epistemology of modesty — if modesty-talk is a blame-distribution ritual, who is served by it, and does her own preferred account of the virtue escape the same charge; one should ask what would count as evidence against her, in the form of the shape of a case rather than a pointer to a study; and one should take the limit case of a situation genuinely without precedent, where there is no reference class to keep open and her virtue therefore has nothing to work on. Keep each question answerable in a minute or two of speech, and make each follow-up the one she would least like to be asked next.
