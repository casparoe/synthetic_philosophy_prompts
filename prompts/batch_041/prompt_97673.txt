I teach a grad seminar on the ethics of war and I'm trying to put together a proper handout for the pacifism session. What I want from you is a decision tree -- a taxonomy of the positions rather than a list of -isms -- where every internal node is a single question (two- or three-way) that genuinely splits the space, phrased so a student can answer it without already knowing which leaf it leads to, and every leaf is a named position plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I'm bored of the usual "absolute vs. conditional pacifism" slide, so please build the tree from the distinctions first and locate the names afterwards. Some distinctions I want on it somewhere: whether the doctrine is a constraint on what the agent herself may do or a refusal to take part in a shared practice; whether it's grounded in the wrongness of the act, in the wrongness of intending, or in the corruption it visits on the agent's own character; whether it binds only individuals or also states and officials; whether it forbids all violence or only lethal violence, with self-defence as the pressure point; and whether the claim is about permissibility, about obligation, or about something merely supererogatory. I also want to build in a distinction I've been chewing on in my own work -- the difference between the "clean hands" pacifist, who cares that she not be an instrument of violence, and the "clean ledger" pacifist, who cares that the world contain less violence no matter whose hands produce it -- and I'd like the tree to show that these two come apart and give different verdicts on defending a third party. Give students something concrete to grab onto at the branch points: the pacifist who hires a bodyguard, the pacifist legislator voting to fund a defensive war, the one who won't kill but pays taxes that pay for killing. Keep the whole thing to about a dozen leaves; if two leaves really can't be told apart by any question a person could answer, say so instead of papering over it.

I want the response in four named sections, in this order. First, "The branches": the questions in the order they should be asked, each with a one-line gloss of what turns on it -- 250 to 350 words. Second, "The leaves": every position, its name, and its most awkward commitment, one sentence apiece -- around 400 words. Third, "Empty branches": combinations of answers that no actual position occupies, and a short diagnosis of each (is nobody there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?) -- 150 words or so. Fourth, "Pairs it can't separate": the leaves your questions fail to distinguish, and why -- 100 words. No reading lists or citations, I just need the instrument.
