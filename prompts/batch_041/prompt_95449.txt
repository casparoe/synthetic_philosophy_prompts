My school's philosophy club has been stuck for months on the problem of other minds, and we got there by an unusual route. We wrote a small chatbot that says things like "that hurts" when its inputs become chaotic, and we disagree about it. One of us says its output is produced by the same kind of physical process as my saying "that hurts", so if I have experiences it does too; the other says the resemblance is only in the words, and that we have no more reason to think the chatbot feels anything than to think a thermometer is uncomfortable. We then tried the usual routes to justifying belief in another's mind — inference by analogy from my own case, treating the other's behaviour as the best explanation of it, and treating the other's utterances as criteria for a mental state rather than as evidence of one — and each time the same two of us ended up exactly where we started. My hypothesis, which I would like you to test rather than assume, is that we are not disagreeing about evidence at all, but about whether evidence of that kind could ever be what is wanted here. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually — a factual one, one about standards of evidence, or one about what the words "feels" and "mind" are doing — and given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a redesigned test, or a demonstration that no progress should be expected?
