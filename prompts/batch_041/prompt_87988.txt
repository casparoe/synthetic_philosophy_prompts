I am a professor of philosophy of science, and I have been asked to give the closing toast at a small interdisciplinary workshop on formal modeling in the social sciences. The room will contain modelers who defend the F-twist—roughly, the view that the unrealisticness of a model's assumptions is no defect if its predictions are good—and historians and ethnographers who regard that view as a scandal. I want the toast to do real philosophical work: it should distinguish between assumptions that are false yet load-bearing in a model's explanatory structure and assumptions that are false but inert, and use that distinction to explain why each side is half right. It should be affectionate rather than a lecture, and it should be intelligible to the non-modelers without condescending to the modelers. Please write a five-minute toast.
