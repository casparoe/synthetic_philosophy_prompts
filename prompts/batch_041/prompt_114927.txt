I'm in a graduate epistemology seminar that has spent three weeks on the closure debate, and I have started to suspect the two sides are not really disagreeing about the same proposition. The closure defenders treat it as close to a datum that knowledge is closed under known entailment: if I know I have hands, and I know that having hands rules out my being a handless brain in a vat being fed a simulation, then I know I am not such a brain. They think any theory that licenses the opposite is refuted by the absurdity of the conjunctions it forces on us — knowing I have hands while not knowing I am not a brain in a vat. The tracking theorists start elsewhere: on their view knowledge requires a counterfactual sensitivity, so that if the belief were false you would not hold it. On the skeptic's hypothesis that condition fails (in the nearest worlds where you are a brain in a vat you would still believe you were not), so they accept that you do not know you are not a brain in a vat while insisting you do know you have hands. They treat the loss of closure as a discovery about knowledge rather than a reductio, and they point out that their account also delivers the verdict most of us want in the fair-lottery case (you do not know your ticket will lose, since you would have believed it lost even if it had won), even as it has to be patched for necessary truths, where the relevant counterfactual has no false case to test.

What I cannot settle is what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it substantive — a real dispute about whether knowledge is a closed, non-modal relation or a modally fragile, tracking one? Is it verbal, with the two camps trading on different senses of 'knows' or exploiting a context-sensitive term? Are they answering different questions under a single heading, one camp doing the descriptive semantics of ordinary knowledge talk and the other pursuing a theoretical relation engineered to defuse the skeptic? Or is it at bottom a methodological clash, over whether intuitions about cases are data a theory must save or defeasible evidence a theory may overturn in the name of explanatory power? A classmate keeps insisting the whole thing is an artifact of asking whether we "know" in the skeptic's context rather than in the kitchen, and I cannot tell whether that dissolves the dispute or merely relabels it.

I want a diagnosis rather than a survey. Say which of these it is, or what combination, and ground it in what each side actually says — being specific about where the real hinge lies: do the tracking theorist and the closure defender disagree about a case, about a principle, or about what would count as a refutation of the tracking theory? Then tell me what, if anything, would move the debate: a formal result showing which closure principles are consistent with which tracking conditions, a distinction between knowledge being closed and warrant being transmitted from premise to conclusion, an account of how the word 'knows' is actually used, or nothing at all. If you think no progress should be expected because the two sides have simply set themselves different questions, say so, and explain what makes the question ill-posed rather than merely hard.
