Two friends of mine got into an arguement at a party and I've been chewing on it ever since. The setup they were fighting about: a giant book that encodes the state transitions of a human brain. Each page is one state, and which page you turn to next depends on the page you're on plus the input coming in. There's no index of answers -- you can't look up "what does it say to this question," you have to step the thing forward page by page. You ask it something, a technician flips to the next page, reads the output off it, that becomes the next input, and so on. Run the transitions long enough and you get fluent, thoughtful replies -- physics, jokes, all of it. Nobody alive can read the code the pages are printed in, including the guy who compiled the book off a brain scan.

Dan: the book is a mind, full stop. Consciousness is a pattern, not a substance -- swap a neuron for a chip doing the same job and nothing changes, so if the page-to-page transitions mirror the brain's, the book is as conscious as you are. Paper and slowness don't matter.

Priya: that's three claims and none of them survive. (1) It only thinks while someone's flipping it; a closed book thinks nothing, so it's a disposition to think, and dispositions don't have experiences. (2) The technician doesn't understand a word of what she's flipping through, so the understanding isn't in the room anywhere. (3) The page order is just a lookup table, and any object can be described as computing any table, so "the book implements a brain" has no content.

Dan: (1) you're not conscious in deep sleep and you're still you. (2) The technician is a hand, not a mind -- the mind is the whole loop. (3) Wire the transitions up with magnetic tags, a scanner, an arm that moves to the next page, and the book is running a computation just like a laptop; your point would rule out laptops too.

Priya: then it's not the book that's conscious, it's the book plus scanner plus arm plus a power supply, and what you've added is a clock -- so the book is a component of a mind, not a mind. Dan: nobody says a brain is conscious without its blood supply either.
