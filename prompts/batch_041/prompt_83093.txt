In a graduate seminar on Otto Neurath and the epistemology of visual representation, I am designing an exercise that asks students to extend Neurath's picture-language program rather than summarize it. In my exposition, the program treats picture language as an international, anti-metaphysical auxiliary to scientific language: its elements should be readable without specialized verbal training, its statements anchored in observable or measurable features of the world, and its syntax built from repeatable iconic units rather than from perspective or decorative variation. Suppose we now want to extend such a language to represent three matters Neurath's own examples handle only unevenly: causal claims, uncertainty, and institutional predicates such as ownership or legal authority. Rank the five candidate extension rules below by overall Neurathian adequacy, meaning fidelity to the program's core commitments together with promise as a working design. Give a definite ordering, not a survey of considerations; do not qualify the ranking by saying that different orderings are defensible. Return exactly five numbered items, one per rule, ordered from your first to fifth choice; each item should name the rule and give one or two sentences of justification. Do not add an introduction or conclusion.

A. Add a small stock of conventional operators for negation, conjunction, and existential quantification, so that pictorial statements can be embedded in explicit logical relations.

B. Require every icon for an institutional predicate to be decomposable, in principle, into picturable relations among human bodies, documents, and physical spaces; where no such decomposition is available, forbid the icon.

C. Permit culturally specific visual metaphors, such as scales for justice or chains for obligation, provided they are taught as arbitrary notation and used with a fixed syntax.

D. Represent quantities only by repeated identical icons at a fixed unit, and prohibit size, area, perspective, or color intensity from encoding quantity or causal strength.

E. Reserve pictorial representation for the display of already-established conclusions, and require all inference, qualification, and uncertainty to be carried by accompanying verbal or mathematical text.
