Context: I run a 300-level seminar on philosophy of mind / cognitive science, 18 students, one 80-minute meeting. I want to do the three-stances / real-patterns material (Dennett: physical, design, intentional) as a game instead of me drawing three columns on the board. I've got a rough shape in mind — please take it, fix it, and hand me back the whole runnable thing.

The shape. I build two little deterministic machines, A and B, that live on a 6x6 grid (wrap-around) and move one cell per tick. Both have a dead-simple behaviour: there are two marked cells, F1 and F2; the machine heads for whichever is nearer, if its a tie it goes for F1, and when it lands on one it pauses a tick then heads for the other. The catch: A tracks the markers — move a marker and A re-routes. B has the two coordinates hardcoded — move a marker and B keeps trundling toward the old spot. For the first ~50 ticks with the markers where they start, A and B emit identical tapes. Nobody can seperate them by watching.

What the students do: teams of 3-4. Round 1, they only get the tape (I print the last 60 outputs; they can ask me for more, but only forward in time, no poking). They submit (a) a written description of what the thing is doing and (b) a prediction for the next 10 moves. They pay for the description — every word costs a point off, correct predictions are points on — so the lazy move is something like "it wants the corner and then bounces around", which is cheap and nails the tape. Round 2, they may intervene: move a marker, drop a wall, teleport the machine. Now A keeps its story and B's falls apart. Round 3 (if time): teams have to invent the intervention that separates two descriptions that both fit the tape and both survive the obvious pokes — i.e., make them feel the underdetermination themselves.

What I want from you: the exact build spec for A and B (precise enough that I can do it in a 20-line script or by hand off a printed transition table), the tape to print for round 1, the scoring sheet, the round-by-round timeline inside the 80 minutes, and the debrief that maps what happened onto the physical/design/intentional stances and onto the compression + projectibility criterion for a pattern being real. Then — this matters — a frank note on what the activity can't show. My hunch is it can't settle whether there's a mind-independent fact about which patterns are real, and it can't reproduce the rationality/idealisation assumptions baked into the intentional stance; tell me if that's right.

Also tell me what predictibly goes wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it — my guess is teams treat the intervention round as cheating, somebody anthropomorphises B, and a team wins with a short rule that only fits the tape (overfit) and I have to decide whether to let them.

One more thing: at the end, say how confident you are that this actually produces the "aha" rather than just being a puzzle, and name the single part of the design you're least sure about.
