Write a joke whose punchline turns on Good's theorem — the result that the expected value of information is non-negative, so a Bayesian expected-utility maximizer should never pay to avoid free evidence. To keep it from collapsing into the standard "ignorance is bliss" gag, build it on the theorem's fine print: the guarantee is ex ante, and it holds only because the agent can, in effect, ignore what she learns and act as if she never received it; the value actually realized once the news arrives can be negative. So the joke should depict someone who acquires true information that is genuinely bad for her — say, because she cannot unlearn it and it makes her abandon her own best policy, or because the information concerns her own future choice and thereby feeds back into that choice. The punchline should be intelligible to someone who knows what the theorem actually claims, rather than resting on generic jokes about knowing too much.
