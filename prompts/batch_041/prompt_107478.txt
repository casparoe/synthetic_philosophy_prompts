A book on the philosophy of physics that I am reading summarizes the hole argument like this: "The hole argument shows that general relativity is indeterministic. Fix everything outside some region -- the hole -- and the field equations still leave the metric inside it undetermined, because you can redistribute the metric within the hole without changing anything outside. The culprit is substantivalism, the view that spacetime points are real over and above the fields; the only way to restore determinism is to give up substantivalism and treat spacetime as a system of relations among material events." I suspect this is too compressed to be fair, but I cannot see where the distortion lies. Please audit the summary claim by claim: what a philosopher of physics working on spacetime would accept, what is a loose simplification, and what is a mistake; whether the conclusion that only relationalism saves determinism survives once the summary is corrected; and how the indeterminism at issue differs from the ordinary question whether a theory's equations have a well-posed initial-value problem. I would also like to know what the argument presupposes about when two models represent the same physical situation, and whether a substantivalist has a defensible reply.
