I am preparing a two-page proposal for a six-month fellowship and want help turning a half-formed idea into it. The idea: the debate over shared resources has been stuck for decades between the view that depletion is inevitable without privatization or external policing and the view that communities often govern their own fisheries and forests successfully, so depletion is a design failure rather than a law of nature. Both sides treat the governing rules as given once they exist, but those rules are themselves a shared resource — making them, monitoring compliance, and sanctioning defectors are individually costly, benefit the whole group, and cannot be withheld from those who free-ride — so there appears to be a dilemma one level above the one everyone argues about. My hunch is that the standard remedies for this second-order problem (graduated sanctions, reputation, cheap talk, monitoring by users) do not dissolve it but work by making enforcement privately rational, which would mean the difference between the two levels is one of cost rather than of kind. Write the proposal: state the question precisely enough to have a determinate answer — I would put it as whether there is any principled, non-cost-based difference between a first-order commons dilemma and the dilemma of supplying the institution that resolves it — explain why the existing positions leave it open, set out a method concrete enough that a reviewer can see what I would actually do and in what order, say what would count as the project failing, and be honest about whether it fits in six months. Take a definite position on how the question should be framed and on which method to use, with your reasons, rather than laying out options for me to pick from. I will add citations myself.
