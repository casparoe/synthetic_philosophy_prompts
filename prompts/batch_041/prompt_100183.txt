Write an essay on the following controversy. Suppose a penal device — call it the Ledger — could, at the moment of conviction, force an offender to undergo in an instant the full measure of the suffering their crime inflicted on its victims, and in doing so permanently remove any inclination to reoffend. The ordeal is agonizing but leaves no lasting injury, and the offender emerges genuinely reformed and remorseful. A retributivist may deny that the Ledger punishes at all, or may object that it punishes in the wrong currency — pleasure and pain rather than the considered deprivation of liberty and rights; a consequentialist may applaud it as an instrument that both reforms the offender and, through its reputation, deters others. Write an essay that adjudicates this dispute and defends a verdict: does the Ledger punish, and ought a just state to employ it? Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides, and make explicit the conception of punishment — of desert, or of deterrence — on which your verdict depends.
