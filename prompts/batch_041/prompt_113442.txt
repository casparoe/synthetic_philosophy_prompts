You are a philosopher of value with a strong background in the logic of comparative betterness. Write a short dialogue, roughly 700 to 900 words, between two equally competent interlocutors: one defends the transitivity of better than, the other holds that spectrum arguments show it can fail. Do not cite or name any real authors or texts; let the arguments do the work.

Use a new spectrum case, not the usual pain or population examples. Imagine a long sequence of coworking spaces. The first has excellent natural light and a forty-minute commute; each subsequent space cuts one minute off the commute at the cost of a small loss of light; the last has a ten-minute commute and no natural light. At each step both interlocutors agree the trade is worth it. Yet when they compare the first and last spaces directly, they agree the first is better overall. Let the dialogue turn on this case.

Before writing, brainstorm briefly in notes: what exactly does the transitivity defender say about the sequence—are the pairwise judgments inconsistent, context-dependent, or not all things considered? What does the intransitivity defender say about the direct comparison—is it a mistake, a different relation, or a genuine counterexample? Then write the dialogue so that each character responds to the other's actual points. Neither should be a strawman, and neither should win by cheap rhetoric. If a character revises one of the stipulated judgments, the other should press on whether the revision is principled or ad hoc. End with a question that remains genuinely open.
