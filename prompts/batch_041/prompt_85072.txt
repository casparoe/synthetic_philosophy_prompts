I'm a software engineer at a logistics company, and I'd like your advice on a disagreement with my manager. I have no background in philosophy, so if there's a standard way of thinking about this, I'd like it in plain terms.

For two years we have run an assistant that answers questions about what is physically in our warehouses: which pallets are where, which were damaged in transit, which were repacked, and so on. It is a language model reading a retrieval index of floor reports. It has never been wired to a camera, a scale, or a scanner. What it reads are short reports typed by the people on the floor, who walk up to a pallet, look at it, and write a few lines. The system works well, and we all treat its vocabulary as dependable: when it reports that a pallet is crushed, we know what that means, and we assume it does too.

Next quarter we open a second warehouse, and the plan is that nobody there will write reports. Cameras and a vision model will generate them from the feed, in the same format, into the same index. My manager's position is that nothing important changes — same words, same schema, same accuracy on our evaluation set — and that the assistant already knows what a crushed pallet is, so it shouldn't matter where the sentences come from. A data engineer on the team put the point more sharply: our ground truth here is a text file.

My instinct is that something is being waved through, but I can't articulate it. Two things bother me. First, at the first warehouse the assistant's words were learned from sentences written by people standing in front of the thing, and at the second they will be learned from sentences produced by a model looking at pixels; I don't know whether that difference survives translation into the same tokens. Second, and this may be the same worry wearing different clothes, our evaluation set was written by the same floor workers whose reports trained the system, so any score we compute at the second site is graded against a vocabulary that only the first site's floor has ever produced. I can't see how our tests could distinguish the two situations, which makes me suspect they can't.

What I want is advice on how to think this through and what to do next: whether the two warehouses really differ in a way that matters, what would show it, and how to raise it with my manager without sounding like I am just uneasy about automation. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, in American English.
