I am writing the final essay question for an upper-level seminar on value theory and rational choice (twelve students, 45 minutes, closed book, answer worth 20 marks, roughly 800 words). The students have read the spectrum argument against the transitivity of 'better than' — the chain in which each outcome is slightly better than its neighbour, running from something clearly good to something intuitively worse — and most of them will answer by reciting the shape of that argument unless the question forces them to work.

So I want the item to turn on a construction stipulated in the question itself, and on nothing they could have memorized. Stipulate: every outcome has a real-valued score; t is a fixed positive threshold; A is better than B iff A's score exceeds B's by more than t; A and B are on a par iff their scores differ by at most t; A is at least as good as B iff A is not worse than B. Ask students (a) to determine, with reasons, which of these three relations is transitive in the stipulated model and which is not, and (b) to say what the spectrum argument must therefore establish if it is to deliver the conclusion that 'better than' itself is non-transitive — in particular whether the intuition that adjacent items are merely 'roughly equal' can carry the argument, and what the standard exploitation or 'money pump' objection presupposes about the relation it targets. State the spectrum in a sentence or two inside the question so the item stands alone, and make it unanswerable by a summary of the literature.

Please give me the question, a model answer of about 650 words, and a marking scheme of five must-hit points worth four marks each (total 20), each with a one-line note on the mistake that typically costs candidates that point. Make explicit in the scheme which two or three moves separate an A from a B.
