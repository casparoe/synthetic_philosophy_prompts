My friends been running this argument at me for a couple weeks and now it's living in my head, and I think somethings wrong with it but I can't find where. Here it is, in his numbering:

(1) The ant colony behind my building relocates when its nest gets wrecked. The scouts that favour one candidate site never visit the other one, and no ant ever sees both. Yet the colony ends up at the better site, and it ends up there more reliably the bigger the gap in quality between the two.
(2) The only thing that predicts where the colony ends up is a model that treats the colony as carrying a representation of the sites and a preference ranking over them. Nothing in any individual ant's state does the predicting.
(3) So the colony really does represent the sites and prefer one to the other.
(4) A system that represents options and acts on a preference has beliefs and desires.
(5) So the colony has beliefs and desires, and none of them is any ant's.
(6) Note that nothing in (1)-(5) ever required an ant to know anything. Every ant is just following local rules that represent nothing much. So the same style of argument, turned on the ant, gives you: no ant represents anything, no ant has beliefs.
(7) Keep going and it's my neurons, and then me. Or go the other way and the market represents prices, the traffic system represents congestion, the forest represents the drought.

I can live with (5) if I have to — I'm fine saying the colony has a preference in the thin sense that a thermostat has one. It's (6) and (7) I can't take, in either direction. I'd sooner give up (4) than give up (1) or (2), but I suspect that costs me more than it looks like. Which step is cheating, and if none of them is, what's the price of each thing I could drop?
