ok so I need a poem for the last session of a grad seminar on the norms of assertion — we've done the knowledge norm, the truth norm, the belief norm, and I want to close on something we can fight about instead of another handout. 18-22 lines, no fixed form, but I do want a refrain, and the refrain should change.

Heres the trick. The speaker makes the same boring claim four or five times ('the 8:15 is running late', 'the keys are on the hook', whatever) and each stanza closes on a different tag — 'I know it', 'that's just true', 'I believe it', 'I'm pretty sure'. I want the reader to feel those tags arent interchangeable: that one of them is what actually licenses the saying and the others are just things that happen to be true of you when you're licensed to say it.

Then the turn: the speaker finds out she only had a hunch that panned out. She never knew. She still believes it, and it's still true, and she has to take the assertion back anyway. The form should make that retraction feel forced by the knowledge tag alone. Also work in one moment where someone asks 'how do you know?' and the poem makes clear that's a challenge to entitlement, not a request for more evidence.

But dont let it read as a clean win for the knowledge norm. Include a case where the speaker is fully justified and sincere and it's false, and we still dont want to say she did anything epistemically wrong — and a stanza where the claim is true and sincerely believed and the saying still seems out of order, the 'my ticket isn't going to win' kind of case, so truth and belief each get their moment. No philosopher names in the poem, and no closing couplet that settles it — I want the seminar to have something left to argue about.
