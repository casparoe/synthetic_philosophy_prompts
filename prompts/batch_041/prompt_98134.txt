Leibniz used it against Newton: if space is a real thing over and above the bodies in it, then you can slide the entire universe three feet to the left and get a different situation that's indistinguishable from the original, so absolute space is unverifiable. Einstein ran the same argument in reverse to show his field equations couldn't be generally covariant, and then changed his mind. In its modern form it goes like this. Take a solution of general relativity, pick out any region of spacetime -- the 'hole', which has to be empty, otherwise nothing interesting happens -- and then smoothly shuffle the fields around inside the hole while leaving everything outside exactly as it was. You get a second solution that agrees with the first on every observation anyone could make outside the hole but disagrees about what's going on inside. If you think the points of spacetime are real individuals, then these are two genuinely different worlds, so the theory fails to determine what happens inside the hole from what happens outside it. The theory is therefore indeterministic, and this is why physicists gave up on the idea that spacetime points exist: they're just labels, like coordinates, with no identity of their own."
