I'm a novelist — three books, none of them any good at endings — and I've been circling the same idea for a year and a half now: the donkey in the middle of two identical hay bales, starving because it can't find a reason to go left rather than right. Every draft I write turns it into a fable and fables are dead on arrival. So write the story for me instead, and make it a real one: real person, one afternoon, no talking animals (unless you can genuinely make an actual donkey work, in which case god bless).

Two things I want it to actually be about, and they're the parts I keep failing:

First, the donkey in the picture is supposed to be indifferent, and that's the boring case. I want the person who is not indifferent at all — who cares enormously about both things, and there's no scale to put them on. Not equal. Not one better. Nothing to add up. I know there's philosophy on this (hard choices, values that don't rank on a single scale, the difference between a tie and a failure of comparability) and I don't need it named, I need it dramatized.

Second, and this is the one that keeps eating my drafts: why the escape hatches don't get her out. She can flip a coin — but the coin has to be picked, and the moment picked, and she has to agree in advance to be bound by it, and being bound isn't something she can get out of the values at stake. She can stop deliberating and just commit — but then what was the committing a decision about? My own hunch, and you can tell me if it's wrong: her problem isn't the tie, it's that she thinks acting requires a sufficient reason, and the demand for one is the trap, not the symmetry. Run with that or overrule me.

Keep it tight, keep it domestic, let the stakes be big but small-scale, and don't end with a narrator explaining what it meant. If a line has to tell the reader what the story is about, cut the line.

Then, after the story, one paragraph on where you took liberties with the ideas or leaned on a reading that's contested, and which reading you committed to and why. Don't hedge it — I'd rather have a story that takes a definite position on what her paralysis is than one that keeps all the doors open.
