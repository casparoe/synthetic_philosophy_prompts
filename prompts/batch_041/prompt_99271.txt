I chair the Public Memory and Art Commission of a mid-sized city, and I need the written opinion I will circulate before Monday's vote on a contested monument. Our commission adopted a rubric last year, and I want the opinion to apply it rather than reinvent it: the rubric sorts contested works into three cases -- (a) the honoree's wrongdoing is the very ground of the honor; (b) the wrongdoing is incidental to an honor conferred for other reasons; (c) the honor was unobjectionable when conferred but now functions to celebrate the wrongdoing, whatever the original intent. One commissioner argues the rubric is too retrospective and proposes a prospective test instead: ask only what continued display will teach the next generation, regardless of what anyone originally meant.

The case before us: a bronze statue erected in 1911 to Elias Wren, who donated the land and most of the cost for the city's first public library and a vocational school for orphaned children; his fortune, however, came from a shipping firm, and he spent his later years as an officer of the state Board of Charities, lobbying to expand a residential institution where children were separated from their families and, in some cases, placed without consent. A bas-relief on the pedestal shows Wren leading a line of children toward the school. Some residents see charity; others see the removal of children from their families. An artist collective has offered, at no cost, a counter-monument: a dark figure placed so that at noon its shadow falls across the statue. The commission's options are to remove the statue, retain it as is, retain it with an added interpretive plaque, relocate it to the city museum, or accept the counter-monument in place of the plaque.

Draft the opinion: a clear recommendation among those five, the rubric category you assign and why, a serious engagement with the prospective test and with the strongest argument for the outcome you reject, the conditions attached to your recommendation, and a concrete time frame. After the prose opinion, append a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "recommendation" (one of the five options), "rubric_category" ("a", "b", or "c"), "decisive_reason" (one sentence), "strongest_counterargument" (one sentence), "reply_to_counterargument" (one sentence), "conditions" (array of strings), "implementation_deadline" (string), and "dissent_note" (one sentence naming the view of the commissioner who rejects the rubric).
