For a foundations-of-statistical-mechanics reading group I'm running, I want to build a decision tree that sorts someone from a deliberately thin starting point into a named position on how Maxwell's demon is supposed to be exorcised. The starting commitment is only this: the microdynamics is time-reversal invariant, and the second law is a statistical regularity rather than a fundamental law. The branch nodes should be the assumptions on which the competing exorcisms actually divide, each phrased as a question a reader can answer without already knowing which view it leads to — whether the demon's operation must be paid for by a compensating entropy increase somewhere; whether that cost falls at the measurement step or at the erasure of the demon's memory; whether the argument depends on the demon being finite and forgetful, so that a demon with a perfect, never-erased record is a distinct case; and whether any information-theoretic principle is needed at all, or whether ordinary statistical mechanics applied to the demon as a physical system already does the work. I want leaves for the Szilárd–Brillouin measurement-cost view, the Landauer–Bennett view that measurement can be made reversible and the cost falls entirely on erasure, the Smoluchowski trapdoor / purely mechanical demon, the deflationary view on which no Landauer-style erasure principle is required and the demon is ruled out by its own thermodynamics alone, and a fluctuation leaf on which the demon succeeds in principle but not systematically; add one further leaf for a combination nobody actually defends and mark it as unoccupied. Finally, tell me which combinations of answers are left empty and whether that is because they are incoherent or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate — my suspicion is that the Landauer–Bennett and deflationary leaves will collapse into one another unless the demon's finitude is made a separate node.
