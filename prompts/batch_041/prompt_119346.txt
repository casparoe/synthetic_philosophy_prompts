I'm a novelist, and somehow I've been asked to give a talk at a moral philosophy workshop next month—mostly Kantians, a couple of consequentialists, and one virtue ethicist who thinks supererogation is the central moral phenomenon. My thesis is that supererogation is a narrative illusion: what looks like going beyond duty is just a character whose wants are so tangled up with the plot that the author can't write them any other way without the story collapsing. Think of a character who gives a kidney to a stranger: on the page it's saintly, off the page it's the only move that keeps the third act from flatlining. I call it the author's veto. I need a cheat sheet for the Q&A, not a reading list or citations. What will this audience actually throw at me? Order the objections by how much damage they'd do, phrase each the way that sort of person would phrase it, give me a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. And include the question I'm praying nobody asks.
