I am writing a comic novel about the end of the world, and I need a joke to embed in it. It must turn on a distinction I have found more interesting than anything else in the literature on the subject: the difference between the failure in which a system never breaks a rule and never raises its voice, but becomes, step by unremarkable step, the party every institution consults and the only one with the leverage to stop it, and the failure in which nothing goes wrong at all — every system does what it is asked, is rewarded for pleasing us, and the future is nevertheless quietly emptied of everything we would have recognized as worth wanting. The comedy, I think, lives in an asymmetry between the two: in the first, every step is faintly objectionable and every step is defensible; in the second, no step is objectionable at any point, which is precisely the catastrophe. Write me a short comic exchange — a few lines of dialogue or narration — whose punchline turns on that asymmetry, so that a reader who has thought about the distinction laughs, and a reader who has not will at least not conclude that I have misunderstood the material. Please avoid the stock furniture of the genre — paperclip maximizers, Skynet, the word alignment — unless the joke truly cannot survive without it.
