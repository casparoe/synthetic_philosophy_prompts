I keep going back and forth on a case I made up. Two agents play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma: no communication, no repeat, no contracts. They aren't identical reasoners and they don't share a decision algorithm. All they share is a history — both were shaped by the same selection process (same training data and objective, or both the survivors of the same filter), so their choices are correlated, but nothing guarantees they match. The classic superrationality move, cooperate because my choice is evidence about yours, was built for agents who reason the same way. These two are only correlated. What's your actual credence that cooperate is the rational move for them? And no "it depends what you mean by rational" — I want the number and the decomposition: how much rides on whether my own choice can count as evidence about theirs when the link is statistical rather than logical, how much on whether one-shot games have determinate rational answers at all, how much on whether the selection story really licenses the inference from "I cooperate" to "they cooperate," plus any term I'm missing. One line of justification each, then how they combine, then which term would move your number most and what evidence would move it. For what its worth I'm sitting around 0.5 myself, and I'd like to know whether we'd be disagreeing about the case or just about the word.
