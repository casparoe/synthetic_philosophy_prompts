I work on mechanistic interpretability for large language models, and my training is in machine learning rather than biology or philosophy. I use sparse autoencoders to decompose a model's activations into features and treat a feature as functional when causal interventions on it reliably change downstream behavior. A colleague from philosophy of biology tells me that this is a causal-role account of function and that the etiological account — the one that ties a function to a history of selection — is the more fundamental one, so my usage is at best incomplete and at worst confused. I want you to restate the debate between etiological and causal-role accounts of biological function in the vocabulary I actually use: training distributions, selection pressures, loss landscapes, gradient descent, causal mediation, intervention, ablation, circuits, features, generalization, and out-of-distribution behavior. Be as faithful as you can to both accounts, not just to the one that flatters my usage.

Take a concrete case: a feature that seems to encode a model's disposition to agree with a user, such that ablating it makes the model less agreeable. My colleague says its function is to produce agreement; the philosopher says its function is whatever the training process selected it for, which may be something else entirely. Can my vocabulary even state that difference, or does it collapse into a distinction between what the feature does now and what it did during training?

Then give me the residue report. Which claims in the biological debate translate cleanly into that vocabulary? Which translate only with distortion, and what exactly gets distorted? Which have no counterpart at all — for example, if there is a distinction between what a trait was selected for and what it currently does, or between function and accident, or between proper function and mere effect, can my vocabulary mark it, or does it collapse? I especially want to know whether the notion of malfunction or dysfunction survives translation: in my world a feature can fail out of distribution, but is that the same as a biological trait malfunctioning? And does it matter that the selection in my case is gradient descent on a loss function rather than differential reproduction, or is that just a difference in the selection mechanism that the etiological account can absorb?

Finally, tell me whether the residue is a defect of my framework — something it cannot say that it ought to say — or a sign that the biological debate is carrying baggage that the ML case does not need. I am not asking for a reading list or citations; I want the translation and the residue report.
