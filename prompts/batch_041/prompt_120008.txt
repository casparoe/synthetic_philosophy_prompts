A friend in my reading group keeps dropping this on me and I can't get out from under it. You know the old Carroll bit — Achilles and the tortoise, where Achilles hands over premise after premise and the tortoise keeps saying sure, but now write down that if those are true then the conclusion is, and so never has to accept the conclusion. My friend says the tortoise is the only honest one in the story, and that the whole thing isn't a gag about stubbornness, it's a proof that there is no such thing as a justified deductive inference. Here's how he writes it up:

(1) To be justified in believing a conclusion on the strength of some premises, you have to be justified in the move from them to it — the transition itself has to be in good order, not just the starting points. He calls this the taking requirement.
(2) But "these premises support that conclusion" is itself a further claim, call it R. So being justified in making the move requires being justified in R.
(3) If R needs backing, it needs another claim in the same style — "the premises together with R support the conclusion" — and then another one for that, and so on. Nothing blocks the demand, since every answer is just more material the demand applies to.
(4) So either the chain runs forever, or it stops at some step that is simply taken and not backed by anything further.
(5) If it stops, the stopping point is groundless, and a groundless step can't do what a justification is supposed to do — a rule that licenses itself would license anything.
(6) So no deductive transition is ever justified: infinite regress, or else no way to tell a good move from a bad one. Either way the tortoise wins, and "valid inference" is a flattering story we tell about moves we were already inclined to make.

I want to keep (1). Its basically what it means for a belief to be based on reasons rather than just to happen to line up with them, and if I drop it I don't know whats left of the idea of inferring at all. But (6) is absurd, so something has to give. Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? I can see three doors — deny that the thing needing justification in (2) is a further claim in the first place, deny (5) and say some transitions are basic and that basic isn't the same as arbitrary, or accept (6) and give up on justified inference. If you take one of the first two, tell me why it isn't just a relabeling of exactly the regress-stopping that (5) already discredited, and whether there's a principled line between a legitimate regress-stopper and a question-begging one, because thats the part I cant see. And say whether the tortoise is being irrational or merely unmoved, and whether those come to the same thing.
