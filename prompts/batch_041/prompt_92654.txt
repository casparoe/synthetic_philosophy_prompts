I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I'm trying to think through a design fight on my team. We're changing the default in our code review tool: by default, reviewers will no longer see the author's name or photo, because we found people leave harsher comments when they know who wrote the code. Product says this is just removing bias, but I think it's a nudge. What philosophical comitments does that default presuppose about the reviewer's autonomy and about the difference between manipulation and persuasion? Does it presuppose that reviewers can't be trusted to correct their own bias, or only that the environment should be neutral? And is it consistent with the fact that we also default to auto-assigning reviewers based on their past activity, which seems to assume they do need help choosing? I want the implicit theory reconstructed, what the practice strictly needs vs. what it just fits with, and what would have to change if we treated reviewers as fully sovereign over how they review.
