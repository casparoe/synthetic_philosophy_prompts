I am a software engineer working on formal verification, and next month I am presenting a short paper at an internal logic-and-computation seminar. My thesis is that Fitch's paradox of knowability is best diagnosed as an artifact of treating knowable as a bare monadic possibility operator, and that the knowability principle should instead be read as saying that for every true proposition there exists a verification procedure — a method in the constructive-computation sense — whose successful execution would yield knowledge of that proposition. On my view, when knowability is indexed to methods in this way, the standard proof that all truths are known fails, because the proof requires substituting a proposition that mentions knowledge itself into the scope of the method-indexed operator, and no method can be specified for that substituted proposition without circularity. The audience will be about twenty people: mostly engineers who work on proof assistants, type theory, and program verification, plus two philosophers of mathematics who know the modal-logic literature on Fitch and are sympathetic to anti-realism. The philosophers will press me on which modal or structural principle I am giving up and whether my move is just a restriction to Cartesian propositions under another name; the engineers will press me on whether the method-indexed operator is decidable, whether the relevant methods can be represented as proof objects, and whether my circularity claim is a theorem or a hand-wave. I have no formal philosophy training, so I need the objections in their vocabulary but translated enough that I can answer from the podium. Prepare a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they would do, each phrased the way that audience would phrase it, with a short reply I can give in two minutes, a note on where the reply is weakest, and what I should concede up front versus what I should hold. Include the one objection I am hoping nobody asks. Do not give me citations, reading lists, or suggestions for further reading.
