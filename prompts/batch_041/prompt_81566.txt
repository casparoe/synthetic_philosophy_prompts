For several years I have been party to a recurring dispute about the lexical-threshold version of negative utilitarianism — the view that some quantity of suffering cannot be outweighed by any quantity of pleasure, so that the two are not exchangeable at any rate — and about the more general claim that pleasure and pain are commensurable. The dispute is entirely stalled, and I want a diagnosis of it rather than a verdict on it.

Both sides know all the moves. One side presses the intuitive case: offer someone an arbitrarily long life of mild contentment conditional on an initial episode of extreme agony, and ask whether any amount of contentment would make the exchange worth it; if none would, the values are lexically ordered. The other side presses the additive case: any finite suffering is outweighed by a sufficiently large finite quantity of small pleasures, so a threshold would make a finite disvalue infinitely exceed a finite value, which looks like a defect in the arithmetic rather than a discovery about value. Each side treats its own case as the datum and the other's as an artefact, and neither has shifted in years.

I have three hypotheses about what is really going on, and I want you to test them rather than choose among them by taste. First, that the disagreement is about continuity: one side treats the Archimedean condition — that no value is infinitely greater than another — as a constraint on rational preference, while the other treats it as a mere artefact of representing value by real numbers, so that they disagree about whether a lexical order is a finding about value or a formal impossibility. Second, that 'commensurable' is being used in two senses, a common unit of measurement versus mere comparability, and that the parties are not in fact disagreeing. Third, that the two sides are answering different questions under one heading: the intuition pumps concern what a single person may be asked to trade within one life, the additive argument concerns aggregation across lives or repetitions, and the threshold view is ambiguous between the two.

Diagnose the dispute. Say which hypothesis, or which combination, or what else, is correct; identify what each side is committed to that it has not noticed; say whether the disagreement is verbal, substantive, formal, or two questions under one name; and state what would count as progress — a distinction, a representation result, a change of question — or why no progress should be expected.

After the diagnosis, give me a JSON object with these fields: 'diagnosis' (one sentence naming the kind of disagreement), 'kind' (one of: verbal, substantive-empirical, substantive-evaluative, formal-representational, two-questions-under-one-name, ill-posed), 'hidden_question' (the question doing the real work that neither side has stated, or null), 'threshold_side_commitment' and 'additive_side_commitment' (one sentence each), 'progress_condition' (what would count as progress), 'no_progress_reason' (why none should be expected, or null), and 'confidence' (high, medium, or low).
