I'm a fourth-year PhD student in cog sci — my dissertation is on analogy-making, so I spend alot of time in the Hofstadter-adjacent corner of things — and I run the weekly reading group in our lab. This week it's on the careenium and simmballs, the thought experiment where you imagine a giant arena full of marbles ricocheting off little pegs, and the only real goings-on are marbles hitting pegs and each other, but from far enough above you can see these stable clusters that seem to behave like symbols, and the question is whether those symbol-level patterns actually push the marbles around or whether they're just a fancy redescription of where the marbles happened to end up. I've got 14 people, a mix of philosophy and neuro grad students plus two undergrads who keep showing up, 80 minutes, one seminar room, no laptops. What I want is an activity where they feel the force of that question before anyone says the word epiphenomenal. So I need a low level that runs on fixed, boring, purely mechanical rules — people following instructions with no discretion, no judgment calls — and a level above it that's supposed to be the symbol level, and I want the two to interact in a way where it's genuinely hard to tell from the inside whether messing with the symbol level did anything to the substrate or whether the substrate would have done exactly the same thing regardless. At some point I want them to have to place a bet (points, candy, whatever's cheap) on whether a particular symbol-level intervention will move the marbles, and I want them to be right sometimes and wrong sometimes and to not be able to tell which was which until the debrief. Materials need to be cheap and printable — index cards, tokens, a big sheet of butcher paper, dice only if you really can't avoid them — and one facilitator who's also playing along has to be able to run the whole thing. No lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the words careenium or simmball until afterwards.

Give me the rules, the roles, the scoring, and a minute-by-minute timeline for the 80 minutes. Then the debrief: which specific moment in the activity maps onto which part of the idea, and how to handle it when someone says "well obviously the symbol level is real, we changed it and the marbles moved." Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it. My worry is that people will just play along and make the symbol-level moves work by fiat, which would wreck the whole point, but you might see a failure mode I'm not thinking of. And be honest about which feature of the idea the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply in the debrief. My own guess is that it can't reproduce the fact that in the real version the symbols aren't a separate layer sitting above the marbles — they're made out of the marbles — so any setup with two rooms or two sets of people will bake in a dualism I don't want. Tell me if you think that's the right thing to worry about.
