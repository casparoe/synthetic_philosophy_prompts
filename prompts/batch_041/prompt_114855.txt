So: which open questions in this area actually matter most, and in what order should we hit them? I have my own list and I'm not sure it's any good:

- Is the Allais-style stuff a failure of the axiom or a failure of the way we individuate prospects in the first place? I keep thinking the interesting move is to deny that a lottery's risk structure is the right unit of normative assessment at all, but I can't tell if that's a real position or just me being bored with expected utility.
- Sequential vs one-shot: how much of the paradox survives when the choices are small, repeated, or when you can precommit / delegate to a machine that just does the math?
- The "in the small" variants, where the stakes are pennies and the intuition pumps go quiet. Do those argue the violation is a mistake rather than a preference?
- What independence is actually for: how much of the machinery we care about -- representation theorems, value of information, dynamic consistency, Dutch-book style arguments -- depends on it, and which of those we'd have to give up if we dropped it.
- Whether the axiom should be stated at the level of acts, lotteries, or events, and whether that choice changes anything downstream.

Rank these, tell me what's upstream of what, add what I've missed, and flag which ones are secretly the same question or don't deserve three years.
