Heres my gut, case by case. Not going to pretty it up.

- Surgeon could kill one healthy guy in the waiting room and use his organs to save five dying patients. Absolutely not — and not just squeamishness. It feels like a totally different kind of act from the trolley, even though the arithmetic is the same.
- A pileup where the five people who need rescuing are the drunk drivers who caused it, and the one person is an innocent bystander who just happened to be standing there. I save the bystander. The five can wait.
- Big charity appeal. I can pay to save one specific kid I've seen on the news, or give the same money to a program that will statistically save five kids I'll never see. I give it to the one I can see. I know thats probably irrational but there it is.

So what I want to know is whether theres actually a principle underneath all this, or whether I'm just making it up as I go. And I want the real principle, not a label — don't just tell me I'm a consequentialist with a sentimental streak.
