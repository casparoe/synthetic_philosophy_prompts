ok, sanity check time. I'm writing up my take on the special composition question for my reading group and I want to know whether the package hangs together before I present it, because I suspect one bit is doing something the others won't let it do.

Here's where I've landed:

(1) Restricted composition: universalism (any things whatsoever compose something) is false, and nihilism (nothing composite exists) is false.

(2) Composition isn't identity. When some things compose a whole, the whole is a further thing; the parts aren't it, and it isn't just "nothing over and above" them.

(3) Which things compose which isn't up to us -- not our concepts, not our language, not what's useful to say. It's a mind-independent fact.

(4) The answer is the organicist one: some things compose something exactly when they're caught up in a single life, i.e. a self-maintaining, self-directing process. So the only composite objects are organisms. No chairs, no statues, no lumps of clay, no brains as such; in those cases there are just simples arranged chairwise, statuewise, and so on.

(5) I'm a physicalist and I don't believe in brute facts: fix the fundamental physical facts and the laws, and you've fixed every fact there is, including which composite objects there are.

(6) The argument that vague identity is incoherent convinces me. But I've got no problem saying that in some cases there's no fact of the matter about whether some things compose something.

Where does this pull apart? Rank the tensions by how bad they are, tell me the cheapest fix for each and what it costs me, and flag any that only look like conflicts. Don't assume I know which one to drop -- I genuinely don't.
