I'm not a philosopher or anything, just someone who fell down a Wikipedia hole about Fitch's paradox of knowabilty and now I can't stop thinking about it. The gist, as I understand it: there's this anti-realist slogan that all truths are knowable — that truth is basically what we could in principle come to know. Fitch's proof supposedly shows that if every truth can be known, then every truth actually is known, which is obviously false, so the slogan is in trouble. The step I find wild is the one where you take an unknown truth, p and nobody knows p, and ask whether that conjunction is knowable; if it is, then you'd be able to know p and also know that you don't know p, which is incoherent. So the only way to keep the slogan is to say there are no unknown truths, which is nuts.

Anyway, I want you to write a satirical piece — maybe an internal memo from a tech company that has decided to implement the knowability principle as a corporate policy. Like, the CEO has read some pop philosophy and announced that henceforth all truths must be knowable, and then the legal/compliance team has to deal with the fact that this logically collapses into all truths are already known, so they start shutting down the unknown unknowns department and issuing deadlines for employees to know everything by Q4. The jokes should come from getting the proof right: the unknown truth, the conjunction, the factivity of knowledge (if you know something, it's true), the distribution of knowledge over conjunction (if you know A and B, you know A and you know B), the difference between possibly known and actually known, and the desperate move of saying the unknown truth just isn't knowable after all, which seems to give up the whole slogan. It should be deadpan, around 600-700 words, formatted like a real memo with subject line, bullet points, maybe a line from a confused employee.
