My friend and I spent last night arguing about laws of nature, and I want to predict where she will come down on a case we did not discuss. Her verdicts so far: she counts the Pauli exclusion principle, the inverse-square law of gravitation, and the law that like electric charges repel as laws; she does not count the fact that all gold spheres are less than a mile across, that all planets in the solar system orbit in the same direction, or that all particles in a particular laboratory have spin up as laws. Now suppose we discover a fundamental relation of necessitation between two properties, a relation that has been instantiated exactly once in cosmic history, and that adding the corresponding generalization to our best systematization of the world would make the system considerably more complex without adding any predictive strength. Would she call it a law? What rule is she implicitly running? What other rules fit her earlier verdicts just as well? And which further case would best tell those rules apart?
