I've ended up as the person who writes the rules and does the bans for a pretty big online community (like 40k people, forum plus a chat), and I keep running into the same question over and over: when is it actually ok to punish somebody for something that hurt no one. A friend told me there was a famous old fight about this between two British judges, Hart and Devlin. The way it was told to me, Devlin said a shared morality is part of what holds a society together, so the law can step in to protect it even when nobody's been harmed, and you can kind of tell a practice is a threat because ordinary people respond to it with disgust and outrage. Hart said no, that's not enough, you need actual harm to other people before you get to coerce anyone. I've read maybe two summaries and I might have both of them wrong.

So what I want: restate the whole thing, Devlin's side and Hart's reply, in the vocabulary I actually think in. Written rules vs unwritten norms, reports, warnings and timeouts and bans vs just letting somebody leave, mod teams, shadowbans, the diffrence between something being invisible and something being private, churn, lurkers who never post but vote with their feet, the fact that a fork or a rival server is always one click away, the vibe of the place being a load bearing asset. Be as faithful as you can and don't just relabel their words with mine, if you have to bend one of them to make it fit my vocabulary then say so out loud.

Then the part I actually care about: what doesn't survive the translation. Which bits of their argument have no counterpart in how a community like mine actually works, and for each one, tell me whether that's because my vocabulary is missing something real or because the philosophers were papering over something. Two hunches I'd like you to either back up or shoot down: first, law comes with cops and I don't have any, everybody here can walk out whenever they want, so society might not map onto community at all. Second, almost nothing here is really private, everything gets logged and screenshotted and quoted out of context, so their carve out for private conduct between consenting adults might be empty in my world. And tell me where you're least sure about any of this, what would have to be true about a community like mine for your verdict on the residue to flip.
