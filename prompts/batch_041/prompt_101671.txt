My seminar has spent three weeks on the regress of justification, and I still can't tell whether foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism actually disagree about any ordinary case, or only about which description of the case is allowed to stand. So I want a worked case instead of another round of abstract argument. Build me a single scenario in which one person's belief comes out justified on foundationalism, justified on coherentism, and unjustified on infinitism — the reverse of the usual dialectical situation, in which infinitism is the view that has to strain to accommodate ordinary justified belief. Keep the subject and the belief ordinary: an acquired competence like a beekeeper reading a hive or a nurse reading a patient, not a mathematician contemplating axiom systems, and no idealized mind that can actually traverse infinitely many steps. The belief should be one she holds and acts on, and the case must not simply stipulate a self-justifying belief or a circular chain of support, since either stipulation would hand the verdicts to one theory by fiat instead of letting them fall out of the theories themselves. My friend Dana suspects the whole dispute is verbal, so the case should be one where that suspicion has to be met rather than dodged.

Give the scenario in a single paragraph, then in no more than 500 further words: (a) state each verdict and trace how it follows from the theory, and for foundationalism say which variety you need and whether a modest version that admits only perceptual or experiential states as basic would still return the same verdict; (b) name the one feature of the case that is doing the real work — coin a short label for it and use that label throughout — and say what happens to each verdict when you remove it; (c) tell me whether any single, independently describable addition to the case flips the infinitist's verdict to justified while leaving the other two verdicts where they were, and if none does, what that shows about the disagreement; and (d) give the strongest line an infinitist would take against your case as built, that is, the charge that you have described the terminus into existence rather than shown one. Total response under 800 words.
