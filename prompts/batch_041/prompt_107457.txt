Two people in my metaphysics reading group have been circling each other for about a year and I want to force the issue. One of them is an endurantist: an object is wholly present at every moment it exists, so you persist by being numerically one and the same thing at each of those moments. The other is a perdurantist: you're a four-dimensional worm made of temporal parts, and what's here right now is one slice of it. They've stopped hearing each other. Before the next meeting I want the document an adverserial collaboration between them would produce — the thing they could both actually put their names to.

Do it in this order. (1) A numbered list of claims both of them can sign without crossing their fingers: what a theory of persistence is even being asked to explain; which cases we all agree any story has to handle; what "change" and "the same object" are supposed to mean here; and what the fight is not about — I'm fairly sure neither of them thinks you're annihilated and rebuilt every instant, but make that kind of thing explicit rather than assumed. (2) The remaining disagreements, each one written in wording its own side would sign off on, so the other side can't wave it away as a strawman. A table is fine. (3) The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. My guesses: the temporary-intrinsics problem (a poker that's cold at noon and hot at one — can a thing just have a property, or does the property have to be relativized, to a time or to a part?); whether a chunk of this dispute is really verbal, i.e. whether they even mean the same thing by "object", "exists", "part", "identical"; and whether special relativity decides anything, given that simultaneity isn't absolute. Tell me if I've missed a crux, and say which one is doing the real work. (4) For each crux, a case or argument both of them agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

And please build in these three cases, which keep derailing our meetings and which I don't think either of them has a clean answer to: (a) a thing exists, stops existing, and later something is assembled that matches it perfectly in every respect — same thing or a new thing? (b) a single-celled organism splits in two, both halves survive, and later the two merge back into one; (c) an object whose spatial parts get swapped around over time, so the left half ends up where the right half was. For each, what does the endurantist predict and what does the perdurantist predict.

Last thing, and I care about this one most: one of them read about stage theory — the idea that the thing that persists is the momentary stage itself, and that the earlier and later stages are counterparts of it rather than parts of one worm — and now insists it's the best of both worlds. Say whether that's a genuine third option or the perdurantist view with extra steps, and what the endurantist would say back to it. No citations needed, I don't care who said what first, and keep the tone like something a person would actually write, not a journal article.
