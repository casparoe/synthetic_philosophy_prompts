Some background before the request. I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have spent the past few months reading about the emotions on my own time — the James–Lange picture, the appraisal tradition, cognitivist accounts, the basic-emotion and constructionist programs — because a side project of mine tags customer support messages by the emotion behind them, and I keep reaching the point where I cannot tell whether I am disagreeing with an author or simply failing to grasp what the author is asking a theory of emotion to do. So before I read anything else, I want the specification. Please write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of emotion has to deliver. I mean the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not be allowed to collapse, the observations it has to be consistent with, and the explanatory jobs it has to perform — each one accompanied by the case or consideration that motivates it, so that I can see why it belongs on the list instead of taking your word for it.

The things that keep sending me back to the drawing board are these. Someone who is afraid of a spider while sincerely believing it is harmless. Someone whose palms sweat and heart races when a door slams, who insists — plausibly — that they were startled but not afraid. Grief that shows up in behavior long after the person says they have stopped feeling it. A dog that appears jealous. The fact that I can be furious at a colleague and know, while I am furious, that the anger is unwarranted. I cannot tell whether those are five separate problems or one problem wearing five coats, or whether some of them are genuine requirements and others are merely the prejudices of whichever camp I happened to read last. So for each requirement, give me the case behind it and tell me how firm it is — which ones are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp. Then tell me which requirements you believe cannot be met together; if the list contains a subset that is jointly unsatisfiable, I would rather learn that now, because that looks to me like the actual result rather than a preliminary to one.

Finish by naming the single requirement that does the most work in ruling candidates out, and then give me a short, definite verdict on the three-way choice I keep encountering in the literature: whether an emotion is best understood as a judgment, a perception, or a feeling. I want you to commit to a position and defend it rather than laying the options out for me to choose between; if you think the question as I have posed it is malformed, say so and explain why, but do not hedge your way out of answering.
