I am a member of a small food-buying club — some forty households that pool orders of grain, oil, and vegetables directly from farms up the valley. It has always run on volunteers; nobody in the club has ever been paid for anything. Our annual meeting is on Thursday and I have to vote on a proposal I cannot think through clearly, so I am asking for help in working out what is genuinely at stake.

The proposal has two parts. First, one of our members, who has by far the best relationships with the farmers, would become our paid buyer: he would purchase the goods himself and resell them to the club at a markup of about fifteen per cent on what he pays. Second, to cover his fee, the club would lend its surplus — a little under four thousand pounds — to a neighbouring club at three per cent a year, which is what that club has offered to pay for the use of the money.

What troubles me is that I cannot articulate why either part should trouble me. When I objected that the buyer does not make anything, he pointed out that our van driver does not make anything either and we pay him; and he is right that we would be paying for a real service, since the ordering is a shambles. When I objected to the loan, one member said that money is a tool and that renting out a tool for a fee is ordinary, and another asked why it is acceptable for the club to keep its surplus in a bank account that pays interest if it is not acceptable to lend to the neighbouring club. I had no answer to either.

So: are these two parts the same kind of thing or different kinds? Is the markup simply payment for work, or is something else going on that the word 'work' conceals? And if there is a genuine difference between growing a sack of wheat and trading it, or between hiring out a van and hiring out money, I would like to be able to state it without hand-waving about 'real value'. I have a nagging sense that this is an old argument about what money is for, and about whether some ways of getting it have a natural limit while others do not — but every version I try to write comes out either as nostalgia for barter or as an accusation I cannot defend. Please tell me which parts of my unease are tracking something and which are confusion; if the position I am groping toward has a name and a standard set of objections, I would like both; and if you can, tell me how I should vote on Thursday.
