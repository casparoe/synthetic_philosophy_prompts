A foundation will support one philosopher for three years on original versus derived intentionality, on the condition that the result be useful to people who build and audit AI systems. The obvious anchor is Dennett's two-bitser, the coin-operated device whose state of having taken a quarter holds only relative to designers, users, and a history, so that its intentionality is inherited rather than its own; Searle's insistence that derived intentionality is not intentionality at all marks the other pole. Now add a wrinkle the two-bitser does not cover. Systems are increasingly trained on text that is itself the output of earlier such systems, and their behavior is then redescribed in intentional terms by users, deployers, and auditors. Call this derivation laundering: the chain from any original intention to a given output becomes practically untraceable, even though nothing new has been added at the far end of it.

Which open questions in this area should the postdoc take up first, and in what order? I am less interested in the list than in the reasoning: for each question, why it is upstream of the others, whether one philosopher can make real progress on it in three years, and what the answer would change for practitioners. Close by saying what argument or evidence in the first year would move a question up or down your ranking.
