For a small philosophy of physics reading group I run, we have spent the past three sessions on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument — the style of reasoning that runs from the relativity of simultaneity to the conclusion that all events are equally real and that the passage of time is an illusion — and I want to close with a one-page decision tree that lets each participant discover which position their own commitments actually commit them to, rather than which label they find congenial. Please build the tree so that it starts from a premise nearly everyone in the room already grants (that special relativity is correct and simultaneity is frame-relative) and branches only at nodes that are themselves the contested steps of the argument, never at the names of positions. Concretely, I would like the branch questions to include: whether the frame-relativity of simultaneity is a fact about the world or an artifact of clock-synchronisation convention; whether any frame-independent present exists; whether, if none does, the whole of spacetime is equally real or only part of it is; and whether the inference from "simultaneous with" to "equally real" survives once reality is indexed to a frame — that is, whether the transitivity step that lets reality propagate along chains of simultaneity is legitimate. Phrase every node as a question a participant could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to, and let the leaves be the actual positions: presentism defended by positing a privileged foliation (neo-Lorentzian style), presentism defended by denying that relativity bears on ontology at all, the growing block, the moving spotlight, the block/eternalist conclusion the argument itself is meant to establish, a fragmentalist view on which reality consists of a plurality of mutually incompatible perspectives, and a deflationary view that treats the whole dispute as verbal or conventional. Give each leaf one sentence naming its most awkward commitment, and mark any combination of answers that no one occupies, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Please also keep the tree honest about its limits: flag explicitly any leaves the tree cannot separate (I suspect, for instance, that an eternalist who accepts the argument and an eternalist who arrives at the block through Minkowski's geometric picture of spacetime will answer every node identically, and that the growing block and the moving spotlight may come apart at only a single question), and say in each case why the instrument fails rather than papering over it. Keep it to roughly six to eight decision nodes and seven to nine leaves so it fits on one page, and arrange it so the order of the questions is itself defensible — a participant who reaches the wrong leaf because of an early fork should be able to see exactly where they left the path. The intended reader is a graduate student who knows special relativity well enough to be suspicious of the argument but has not read much of the metaphysics of time, so the nodes should be intelligible to them while still being precise enough that someone who has thought hard about the debate would not find them caricatures.
