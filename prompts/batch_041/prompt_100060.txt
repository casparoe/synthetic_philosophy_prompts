I am helping to design a teaching module on relational conceptions of personhood — the family of views on which a person is not merely an individual bearer of capacities but is constituted, or at least completed, by standing in relations of mutual recognition with others. To keep the discussion manageable we have reduced the family to five positions, set out below. These are our own reconstructions rather than summaries of any particular author, so we are content to be told that one of them is a position nobody actually holds.

1. Constitutive: to be a person just is to stand in the relevant relations; there is no further fact about one's personhood over and above one's place in those relations.
2. Capacities-acquisition: the capacities constitutive of personhood — reason, self-consciousness, moral agency — can only be acquired and sustained through social interaction, but once acquired they belong to the individual and do not depend on continued recognition.
3. Recognition-dependent standing: personhood is intrinsic, but the rights and dignity one actually holds are conferred by the community's recognition, so that an unrecognised person is a person who cannot be wronged.
4. Dialogical: personhood is realised in the exchange of address and response, so that one is a person in the second person rather than the third.
5. Narrative-embedded: a person is the protagonist of a life story that only makes sense within a community's shared narrative; outside that narrative the life is not yet a life.

We want these five ranked by how well each survives the following four stress cases, which we intend to use in class:

(a) the castaway, raised in a community but marooned alone for decades, capacities intact and no relations remaining;
(b) the scapegoat, expelled from the community and declared outside its moral order, still alive and still interacting with others instrumentally;
(c) the person in advanced dementia, whose capacities for recognition have largely gone but who remains embedded in relations of care;
(d) the newborn, whose capacities are not yet present but who is already held within relations.

Please sort the five into tiers, from the account you regard as most defensible overall to the least, where each tier contains accounts you take to be roughly on a par. For each account, say in a sentence or two which case damages it most and why, and whether the damage can be repaired by a modest amendment or is fatal. Then give a single number between 0 and 1 for each account, representing how confident you would be in presenting it as a serious contender rather than a historical curiosity, and explain what drives the spread between the highest and the lowest.
