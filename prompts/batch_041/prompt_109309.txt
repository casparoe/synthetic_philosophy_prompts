Longtime lurker on the rationlist blogs and forums, first time actually asking something. There's a move I keep running into in arguments about why some regions stay poor, or why certain conflicts never seem to end, and I want to understand it instead of just being annoyed by it.

The move is the one-liner 'geography is destiny.' Latest sighting: a comment thread where I was arguing that institutions and the decisions of particular people do a lot of work, and a guy basically ended the exchange by telling me to look at the map. (I'm not asking you to adjudicate any real country, and please no reading list or citations -- I can't check them and you can't either.)

What I want is the slogan taken apart into the distinct precise claims it could be making, laid out as a menu from the boring to the radical, with a verdict on each: trivially true, substantive and defensible, false, or mush. Is it just that initial conditions constrain what's possible? That the constraint binds, i.e. some outcomes are off the table? That it's permanent, or only permanent conditional on a tech level? Is it a claim about explanatory weight, that when you decompose the variance geography dominates? Or a claim about which explanations are even legitimate, so appeals to culture or individual choice are always epicycles? And is there a temporal version of it -- the Braudel / longue duree thought that the slow structures outlast the events and the personalities, so the real causal action is all at the slow scale?

Two things I'd especially like sorted out. A friend of mine keeps talking about the moving constraint: the terrain doesn't change, but what the terrain means changes with technology, since a coastline or a coal seam only pays off given ships or engines. Is that a genuine escape hatch from the strong readings, or does it just relocate the determinism? Another friend says the interesting question is whether geography fixes the mean or the spread -- where places end up on average versus how far apart they can get -- and I honestly can't tell which one people are asserting when they say destiny.

Then the part I care about most: which of these readings actually does the work the guy in the thread needed it to do? He was shutting down a conversation about institutions, and I suspect only one or two of the strong readings defintely get him there. Which ones, and are they true?
