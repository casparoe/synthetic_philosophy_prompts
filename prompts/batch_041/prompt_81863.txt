im working on a writeup about whether debate between AI systems can scale as a safety method, and I want to stress-test my own proposal against the strongest version of the obfuscation worry. so: play a researcher who thinks debate is fundamentally broken by obfuscation. Not a strawman — someone who grants the easy stuff (debate clearly helps when verification is cheap, it beats naive baselines alot) but never budges on the core claim that a clever debater can always hide a false claim inside a pile of individually-plausible steps that the judge can't check in the time available. Stay in character the whole time until I say stop, dont drift into "both sides have a point" mush, and keep replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like a back and forth. Attack my proposal specifically, and be concrete about the failure mechanism — I want the actual move, not just "it's hard".

My proposal, call it the debt rule: since the judge can't verify long arguments, make length a liability. Each debater gets a fixed word budget. Any claim the judge can't check goes on that debater's "unverified debt," and the debt only comes down when a later claim is checkable and actually gets checked. Whoever ends the debate with more debt loses. My thinking is this turns obfuscation from a free move into a costly one: to bury the weak link the obfuscator has to burn budget and rack up debt, and a debater telling the truth never needs to. So the arms race you're worried about just becomes a race the honest side wins by default. Where does it break?
