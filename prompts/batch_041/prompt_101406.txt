I'm hosting the end-of-term dinner for our MA seminar next week and, through a moment of pure hubris, I've volunteered to provide the one joke for the toast. The running theme this term was consequentialist cluelessness, and I'd like a joke that actually turns on it rather than just gesturing at it. The lazy version — "consequentialists can never decide what to order" — misses the point entirely; the point is that even when you're perfectly decisive about the immediate act, the total consequences run off into the long-run future in ways you simply can't survey, so the whole evaluative exercise gets stuck. I want the humour to come out of that structure: the gap between confident short-run effects and an utterly unsurveyable long-run upshot, ideally so that the joke can't even cleanly resolve itself because the cluelessness keeps biting. Can you write me one? Feel free to invent the specifics — a lightbulb, a parking space, a wedding present, whatever gives the best traction.

It'll be told to a mixed crowd: a handful who'll get the reference straight away, and plenty who've never heard the term, so it needs to land as a joke on its own for someone who knows nothing about it, while still being, to those who do, genuinely about the problem and not just a generic "philosophers are indecisive" gag. If my framing here is muddled, or if this is one of those cases where the theory can't sustain a joke without my bending it into something it isn't, please say so plainly instead of fobbing me off with something that sounds knowing but doesn't turn on the content — I'd much rather rethink the toast then deliver a dud. British English throughout, ta.
