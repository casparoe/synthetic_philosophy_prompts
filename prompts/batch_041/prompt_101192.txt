I am putting together a position paper for a graduate seminar in population ethics, and I want to run a consistency check on the package before I commit to it. A colleague insists that I am trying to have it both ways but cannot put her finger on where, so I would rather find out now than be caught out in the seminar. Here, as plainly as I can state them, are the commitments I hold. First, I am an axiological totalist: the value of an outcome is the sum of the welfare of every individual in it, and I understand welfare as the satisfaction of preferences rather than as felt pleasure. Second, I take the utility monster seriously and do not treat it as a reductio; if there really were a being who converted resources into welfare at a vastly higher rate than anyone else, the sum would favour giving it everything, and I am willing to say that this is what we ought to do. Third, I hold that the separateness of persons is a genuine moral fact: there is a real difference between harming someone and merely failing to benefit them, and persons are not to be treated as so many vessels into which welfare may be poured. Fourth, I regard the Repugnant Conclusion as a genuine reductio and believe that any acceptable theory must avoid it. Fifth, I do not think the utility monster is a real possibility at all; I regard it as an artefact of the thought experiment, and I mention it chiefly to show that I am not frightened by its conclusion.

What I would like from you is an audit rather than a defence of any one of these. Which of my commitments actually pull against one another, which merely appear to, and in each genuine case what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me elsewhere? Please order the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious, and for each say explicitly which commitment would have to be weakened or abandoned. Where a tension dissolves on inspection, explain why it is only apparent. Then tell me which single revision would resolve the greatest number of the genuine tensions at once, and whether the position that survives is still recognisably the one I set out to hold, or whether it has quietly become a different view. Please write your answer in British English.
