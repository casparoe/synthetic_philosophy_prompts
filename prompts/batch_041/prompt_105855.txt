ok so parfit and the teletransporter. you step in, the machine reads every detail of you, vaporises you, and a replica wakes up on mars with your memories, your handwritting, your fear of the dentist. parfit says the boring question is whether the replica counts as me, and the interesting thing is that identity isnt what matters anyway — the connectedness is, and it hardly matters what causes it. fine. but the version of the case i cant stop chewing on is the slow one: what if the machine shipped you over a bit at a time, replacing you piece by piece with new stuff that works just as well, so that at some point youd want to say the original is gone but there isnt a moment you could point to. i want a poem built that way. six stanzas, four lines each, five words a line. each stanza is the one before it with exactly one word swapped out in each line — one at a time, a different word each time, never the same one twice — so that by the sixth stanza not a single word from the first stanza is still there. with one exception: the word i never changes. it stays put the whole way through, doing the same job at the end as it was doing at the start, which is to say no job at all, and i want the reader to feel that without being told it. keep it first person, keep the rhythm and the shape identical stanza to stanza, dont explain the trick, dont tell me where the person stopped being the person, and dont let it collapse into a word puzzle — it still has to be a poem someone could read knowing none of the above. rhyme or dont. im reading it out at the end of a seminar so it has to land out loud.
