I chair the grantmaking committee at a small foundation — we hand out about $4M a year across global health, animal welfare, and a bit of longtermism — and we meet quarterly to rank roughly thirty proposals. The problem is we keep deadlocking: one member is a straight-up utilitarian, one gives priority to the worst off, one thinks there are side-constraints you can't cross whatever the consequences, and one thinks the whole thing is bankrupt because it ignores relationships and community. I don't want to fake a consensus and I don't want to just average our vibes. What I want is a procedure we can actually run on each proposal that takes our moral uncertainty seriously instead of hiding it.

So write me that procedure. The steps in order, what counts as a good enough answer at each step, where it stops with a clear no, and where it has to hand the call back to the committee because the disagreement is about values and not facts. Ground it in whatever the moral-uncertainty literature has come up with that actually helps here: the problem of comparing amounts of value across theories that don't share a currency, the idea of maximizing expected choiceworthiness, the alternatives to plain expected value (weighting each theory by how much we'd loose if it's wrong, or letting the theories "vote"), and the worry that a tiny probability of some fanatically huge payoff swamps everything else. Be honest about which steps are just judgment calls dressed up as math, and say what to do when we genuinely can't get numbers.

Then run it on two proposals: (a) a deworming program with good evidence and a large expected health benefit but nothing for animals or the far future, and (b) a moonshot research bet on cultured meat that most of our theories say does almost nothing but one says could be enormous. Flag it if the procedure spits out an answer I won't like.

Keep the whole thing under a couple pages. End with exactly one final line, in this form: Verdict: <the single decision rule you'd actually have us adopt, in one sentence>.

American English please.
