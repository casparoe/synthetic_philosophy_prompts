I'm a philosophy professor (phil of science, mostly) and I'm applying for a semester of teaching relief from my faculty's internal research fund. Deadline's Friday, I've got about two pages to fill, and I have a half-formed idea I keep failing to turn into something with a shape. Draft it for me.

The gist: I've taught Lakatos's methodology of research programmes for years and I still can't tell my students what a scientist is supposed to do with it. Calling a programme progressive or degenerating always seems to happen after the fact, once we know who won; at the point where the choice actually bites — where do I put the next three years, this student, this grant — the framework goes quiet. I dont think that's a bug in my exposition. I think it comes from the criterion doing two jobs at once, and I think the equivocation is hiding in the requirement that progressive programmes predict novel facts. Sometimes novelty seems to mean facts nobody knew when the theory was proposed; sometimes it means facts that are novel relative to the programme's own content, the ones the programme gives you reason to expect and its rivals don't. Only one of those can plausibly be used prospectively, and I think it's the second, but I can't yet say whether the prospective version amounts to more than a fancy way of telling people to bet on whichever programme's heuristic is still generating questions.

Second itch: if I generalize the criterion so it covers programmes that predict no empirical facts at all — competing programmes in philosophy, say — what plays the role of the novel fact? If the answer is just that new problems keep getting solved, the criterion starts to look vacuous, and I want to know whether that's a real collapse or a bad way of stating the generalization.

So write the proposal. Pin the question down so there's a determinate answer to it; explain why the existing positions on Lakatos's criterion leave it open (positions and arguments, not citations); say what I'd actually do month by month for a semester; what a finished result looks like; and what would count as the project failing or proving too big for the time. No bibliography or reading list please, I'll add the references myself.
