The topic is path dependence — the claim that some historical outcome (pick your favorite: which side of the industrial revolution a given country ended up on, which institutions got locked in, QWERTY, whatever, and yeah I know QWERTY is a contested example, don't get hung up on it) was contingent and could've gone otherwise, versus the view that it was overdetermined and some functionally similar version was going to arrive no matter what.

Here's the proposition I want a number on. Fix a case C and a time T before the outcome crystallized. The claim is: there exists a counterfactual perturbation of the world at T, small relative to some reasonable metric (I know, that clause is doing a lot of work, deal with it), such that the outcome at T+n is materially different. Whats your credence in that for a mid-sized case, not the maximally dramatic one. Then a second number: given that, your credence that the coarse-grained outcome — some society in that region ends up industrial, some keyboard layout ends up standard — comes out the same across the relevant perturbations anyway.

Don't give me a bare number, give me the decomposition:
- how much rides on whether the mechanism is genuinely self-reinforcing (increasing returns, network effects, coalitions with sunk costs in the status quo) versus just sensitive dependence with no lock-in, so a random walk that could've gone either way but wouldn't have stayed put once it went
- how much rides on the counterfactual-measure problem: whether the claim is even well posed without a distribution over perturbations, and whether under most natural distributions it's trivially true (almost any perturbation changes something), which would make path-dependence claims near-unfalsifiable and therefore not the claim anyone actually means
- how much rides on whether the process has a unique absorbing state or attractor. my sense is that in a lot of these models the randomness is in the path but not the destination, and people slide between those two without noticing
- how much rides on the level of description, since fine-grained outcomes basically never converge and coarse-grained ones often do, so the truth of "it could have gone otherwise" flips depending on how you carve the outcome
- and whatever other term you think belongs

Then give me the combination, tell me which term you're least confident in, and what would actually move it.
