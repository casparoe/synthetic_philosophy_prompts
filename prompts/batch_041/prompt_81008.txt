so I've been thinking way too much about the word "intentionally" and I've got a pile of gut reactions that I can't get to fit under one rule. I was hoping you could either find the rule or tell me which of my reactions is the one that has to go.

It started with this setup I came across somewhere. A company's thinking about starting a new program. The program will make them alot of money. It will also, as a side effect, pollute a river. The boss says he doesn't care one bit about the river, he just wants the money, so go ahead. The program runs, the river gets polluted. Did he intentionally pollute the river? Yeah, I say yes, obviously.

Flip it. Same program, same boss, same speech about not caring about the river — except this time the side effect is that the river gets cleaner. It runs, river's cleaner. Did he intentionally clean the river? I want to say no. And that's what's bugging me, because the two setups are the same in every way except whether the side effect is good or bad.

So I tried to pin it down with more:

- Boss never looked into it at all, has no idea it'll pollute. It pollutes. Intentional? No.
- Boss knows it'll pollute, hates it, feels awful about it, would stop it if he could, but does it anyway for the money. Intentional? Ugh, I definately still say yes. Which seems wrong, since he'd undo it in a second.
- The program also changes the office paint to a slightly different shade of blue. Boss knows, doesn't care. Was the repaint intentional? No. (So it's not just "anything he knew about and didn't stop.")
- Now make the cleanup the means instead: the program only makes money because the cleanup brings good publicity. Did he intentionally clean the river now? Yes, I think so. So helping is intentional when it's a means but not when it's a side effect — while harming seems intentional either way.

The pattern I'm stuck on is: bad side effect = intentional, good side effect = not, boring side effect = not, and means = always intentional whatever the valence. Is there one principle that gets all of that? If not, which of my gut reactions should I ditch?

And whatever principle you land on, tell me what it says about these, which I haven't thought through:

- Boss knows the program will hurt a rival company as a side effect (bad, but not a means). Intentional?
- Boss knows the program will, as a side effect, make one employee rich and another employee poor, both of them, and he doesn't care about either. Which one did he do intentionally, if either?
- A regular guy knows that driving to the store wears down his tires. Did he intentionally wear them down?

Last thing: if the honest answer is that "intentionally" isn't a plain fact about what someone did but partly a way of handing out blame, then just say that, and tell me how to talk about it without it sounding like I'm making the facts up as I go.
