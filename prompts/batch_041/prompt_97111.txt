I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy preparing an application for a nine-month dissertation fellowship, and I need a two-page research proposal. My half-formed idea is this. The familiar debate over Wittgenstein's late remarks on certainty tends to ask whether hinges are epistemic propositions with a special status or non-epistemic commitments, ways of acting, or rules of a practice. I am less interested in that taxonomy than in what happens when a hinge is deliberately made explicit. I have been calling the phenomenon hinge conscription: the process by which a proposition that previously operated as an unspoken background certainty is dragged into the space of reasons by an actor who wants a community either to affirm it or to abandon it. The interesting cases are not the ones where a hinge is simply doubted by a Cartesian skeptic, but the ones where making it explicit is itself a move in a social struggle—say, when a workplace requires an employee to state in writing that she trusts the company's safety procedures, or when a political movement forces a public avowal of a proposition that was previously just part of the scenery. My question is whether hinge conscription is always a form of epistemic damage, or whether it can sometimes be a necessary repair, and what distinguishes the two. I suspect the answer turns on whether the practice that contains the hinge has the resources to treat the newly explicit proposition as a genuine object of reasons, or whether making it explicit only transfers the certainty to a higher-level hinge that remains equally unavailable for criticism.

I would like the proposal to state this question precisely, explain why the existing positions—epistemic and non-epistemic readings of hinges, and the usual contrast between doubt and ungrounded commitment—leave it open, and set out the method by which I would actually make progress on it. The method should be concrete: what distinctions I would draw, what cases I would analyze, what objections I would consider, and how I would test the resulting account against the cases that motivated it. I would also like a realistic month-by-month plan for nine months, a statement of what a successful result would look like, and an honest account of what would count as the project failing or being too large for the fellowship. I do not need citations, a bibliography, or a literature review; I will fill those in myself.
