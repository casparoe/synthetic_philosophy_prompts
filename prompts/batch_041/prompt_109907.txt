I'm a novelist, and I've got a scene I can't finish because I don't know what the thing inside it is called, or even whether it's a thing. Bear with me, I'll describe it badly.

A group of people are all in the same position, and all know it. Each of them, separately, has to decide whether to do one small thing. The thing only works if exactly one of them does it. If none of them do it, nothing happens for anybody. If two or more do it, the whole thing is spoiled -- not split smaller, spoiled. And the reason it isn't just an ordinary muddle is that they're all equally well placed and all equally sharp, so they run the same reasoning, and whatever one of them concludes, all of them conclude. Which means "somebody ought to do it" and "I'd better be the one, because if everyone thinks like me then nobody does it" are the same sentence said by six people at once. And the second one, the one that sounds like the responsible move, is exactly what wrecks the evening.

Two times I've watched this happen for real. (1) Our writers' group -- six of us -- got offered a single slot on a festival panel. One of us had to reply by Friday. Nobody replying and the slot goes to another group; two of us replying and the organisers get embarrassed and give it away. All six of us, being decent and equally hungry, thought "better not leave it to the others," and all six of us replied, and the slot was gone. The person who did the responsible thing destroyed it. (2) Same group got a small grant, £900, with the rule that whoever put their name down got an equal share. One name, £900. Six names, £150 each. Every one of us put our name down, obviously, because a share beats nothing -- and so the thing we'd actually wanted the money for never happened.

Then there's a case I'm not sure belongs with those two: in the house I share, somebody has to put the bins out. If two of us do it, it's mildly silly and nothing is lost. Is that the same shape or a different one? I keep wanting to say same and feeling it isn't.

So. Is there a name for the first thing, if it exists? And separately, I suspect the *reasoning move* -- pick the option that would be best if everyone in your exact position picked it too -- has its own name, and that it isn't the same name as the situation. Is that right? Because in the scene my character has to be able to say what she's doing and why, and at the moment she'd need a paragraph.

And please don't just hand me labels. I want the edges. How is my first shape different from the plain "everybody assumes somebody else will do it" problem, from the commons thing, from a straightforward coordination problem? Does the trap even survive if the people aren't identical -- because the moment one of them is visibly keener than the rest, it seems to me the whole thing dissolves, and I want to know if that's the crux or a side issue. Does everyone talking it out first fix it, or does it only hold if the agreement can actually be enforced? And is the reasoning move a good rule or a trap? I've a feeling clever people have argued about that for a long time and I need to know which side my character lands on.

One more thing and then I'll shut up. I half remember a lottery where you send in a postcard and the prize money gets divided between however many people enter, and a friend told me the answer to that one is stranger than "everybody hold back" -- something about each person deciding at random so that probably only one of them enters. Is that right? And if it is, why does one case want a volunteer and the other want a die roll? Thats basically the whole book, so no pressure.
