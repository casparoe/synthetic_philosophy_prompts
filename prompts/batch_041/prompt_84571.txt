Here is roughly what he said, as faithfully as I can reproduce it: "Everett's move was to take the Schrödinger equation completely seriously and drop collapse, so the universal state is a superposition of branches and every outcome of every measurement happens in some branch. Since every outcome happens, there's no fact of the matter about what will happen to you, so there's nothing for probabilities to be about; the Born rule, on this reading, is just a bookkeeping convention telling you how to count branches. What makes the branches separate and irreversible is decoherence: you trace out the environment, the reduced density matrix comes out diagonal with the familiar squared amplitudes sitting on the diagonal, and those numbers are the probabilities — decoherence is what supplies the weights. The last gap was closed when decision theorists proved that any rational agent in a branching universe has to bet in accordance with the Born rule, so the probability problem is solved. The upshot is that many-worlds is empirically equivalent to collapse theories, which is why it can't be refuted; the whole dispute is therefore metaphysical."
