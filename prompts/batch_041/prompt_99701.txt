I've spent the last couple of years reporting on climate inaction, and more recently on the whole "nobody is really responsible for AI risk" genre of argument, and somewhere in there I drifted into having actual views about causation, which was not the plan. A friend with a phil PhD has been poking holes in them over beers and I'd like a second opinion before any of this ends up in a piece. Roughly where I've landed: (1) causation is just difference-making — c caused e iff, had c not happend, e wouldn't have; no extra "mechanism" or "productive force" needed; (2) omissions count as causes — if the inspector never checks the valve and the plant blows, her not-checking caused the explosion as much as a wrench in the gears would have; (3) there's no such thing as "the" cause of anything, since which condition we single out — the spark, the oxygen, the building code, the inspector — depends on what we happen to care about, and there's no further fact about which one is really it; (4) there are no omissions floating around in the world, though — the world contains stuff that happens, and a failure to act isn't an entity, it's an absence; (5) you're only responsible for outcomes you caused, so if we caused the warming by not acting we're on the hook and if we didn't we aren't, and no amount of "we should have" changes that; (6) but I also don't think I'm responsible for the people who die because I didn't give my salary to Oxfam — I'm not a monster, I just don't buy it.

Which of these are actually pulling against each other, and how badly? I have a hunch that (1) and (2) together make (5) and (6) impossible to hold at once, but I can't tell whether the repair belongs in (2), in (5), or in (6), and I have a nagging feeling (3) is either doing a lot of quiet work here or is completely irrelevant — I genuinely can't see which. Order the tensions by how much damage each one does, tell me the cheapest fix for each and what that fix costs me elsewhere, and say which single commitment I should drop if I want the rest to stand as a package. Please flag how confident you are in each verdict, and single out the one you're least sure about — I'd rather know where you're guessing than get a tidy answer.
