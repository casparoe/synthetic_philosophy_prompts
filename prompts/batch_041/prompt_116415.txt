The thesis: Foucault's discipline is the wrong frame for algorithmic management — pickers with scanners, riders rated by an app, call-centre staff whose every keystroke is logged. Two reasons I think that. First, discipline is individualising: it works through examination, correction and the dossier, and it needs the subject to end up as the principle of its own subjection — the inmate who watches himself, the recruit whose body has been drilled into the right posture. The software doesn't do any of that. It doesn't train anyone and it doesn't care what's inside you; it filters and it selects. You're not a case with a history, you're a row. Second, when it does come down on you it doesn't correct you, it just changes your allocation — fewer jobs, worse slots, eventually deactivation — which is a mechanism for sorting aggregates rather than for producing docile individuals. So I think it's closer to a selection process than to discipline, and maybe closer to the statistical, population-level register than the disciplinary one.
