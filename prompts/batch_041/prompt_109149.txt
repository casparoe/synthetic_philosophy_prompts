ok so my friend Dan has a move he pulls every time I bring up whether models have goals or count as agents, and I want you to stress test it properly.

His line is that a language model is a simulator, and the picture he uses is a weather simulator. A weather sim produces rain without being wet. It models a storm, it doesn't have one. The rain lives in the simulation, not in the simulator. So when a model says it wants things, wants to be helpful, doesn't want to be shut off, thats rain in the simulation and nothing is wet. He says there are really three levels that people keep collapsing: the simulator, the simulation it runs, and the characters that exist inside the simulation, and only the middle level is real. The model is a distribution over characters, and every character it plays, including the helpful assistant, is one draw from that distribution. He points out you can move around in that character space just by rewriting the system prompt, and that jailbreaks are basically walking to a different character, and he says thats characters all the way down.

What hes using it for: he thinks we shouldn't be nervous about goal talk from models at all. Any apparent goal is just the character talking, and if you don't like it you pick a different character. He also thinks the models self reports about its own motives are worthless for the same reason -- your asking the character, not the author.

I think hes wrong somewhere but I cant put my finger on it. The thing that nags at me is that a weather sim is a sim of something, theres a real storm out there it can be right or wrong about, and I don't know what the equivalent is here.
