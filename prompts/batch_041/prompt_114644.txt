I'm putting together an anthology of short fiction that actually does philosophy rather than just name-dropping it, and I need one more piece: a short story (800-1200 words) on the substantivalism vs. relationism fight over space, but I don't want the standard Newton's-bucket retelling where everyone just recites the bucket, Leibniz, and Mach at each other.

Here's the setup I'd like you to use. A sealed habitat, generations deep, drifting in a void. Nobody inside has ever seen a star, and as far as anyone knows the habitat is the only matter there is. They've got an ocean and a long pendulum. The pendulum precesses, the ocean bulges, and a schism opens: one faction says this proves the habitat is really turning, that there's a space out there telling it so; the other says rotation is only ever rotation relative to something, and the only something here is the habitat itself.

The part I actually want dramatized is that the dispute should turn on a test, not a lecture. The relational/Machian line is supposed to mean the local standard of what counts as not spinning is set by the matter around you — so if the shell of the habitat is what defines it, then changing how much mass sits in that shell should change what the pendulum does, a heavier rotating shell dragging things a little more. The substantivalist line says the shell's mass is irrelevant, absolute space doesn't care. Let the characters work out that this is the experiment that could seperate the two views, then run it, or fail to run it and understand exactly why they can't.

Please dont give anyone a tidy answer at the end — let the argument live in what they measure, build, and refuse to admit, not in monologues.

After the story, add a short note (clearly outside the story) on where it takes liberties with the physics and the philosophy, which side of the dispute it leans on and why, and what experimental result or argument would make you lean the other way.
