I'm running a session on Dennett's four grades of creature — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — and I'd rather hang it on a case than just re-explain the ladder. The case: AlphaGo vs Lee Sedol, Seoul, March 2016, game 2, move 37, the shoulder hit on the fifth line that everyone first took for a glitch and that turned out to be the move of the match. I know the game and I know the Go, so skip the play-by-play and the Go theory — I want the mapping, and I want it to run in both directions.

Direction one: which grade is AlphaGo actually occupying, and at which moment? My own hunch, which you should feel free to shoot down, is that the training pipeline runs the grades in sequence and that this is the interesting bit. The supervised phase that soaked up human game records is Gregorian — it's inheriting human thinking tools, joseki and all. The self-play reinforcement phase is Skinnerian — consequences doing the selecting on a single system, no understanding required. And the tree search at play time is Popperian — candidate lines die in its stead instead of in the game. Is that defensible, or am I just pattern-matching? And where does the Darwinian grade live, if anywhere — the architecture search, the population of self-play opponents, or nowhere?

Direction two: what does the case do to the theory? Dennett's grades are supposed to be a ladder of progressively cheaper ways of coping, with the Popperian trick as the big one, because you can kill off bad ideas internally. But if a Skinnerian-style system plus a good search can produce move 37 without anything like testing a hypothesis in thought, does that undercut the ladder, or does it just relocate the Popperian element into the machinery? And the Gregorian grade is defined by culture and borrowed tools — does it even make sense to call a system Gregorian when it absorbs the products of human culture but doesn't itself traffic in them? Use AlphaGo Zero (the 2017 version, trained from self-play only, no human games) as the control case here.

Two things to be careful about. Keep what's settled seperate from what's genuinely contested — the four-grade scheme is a useful piece of philosophy, not a received scientific taxonomy, and I dont want you presenting it as consensus, or presenting your own extension of it as if Dennett said it. And where the theory is silent or just doesn't fit, say so instead of forcing it to speak.
