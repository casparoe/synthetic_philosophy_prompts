I'm a retired molecular biologist — forty years at the bench, only got seriously into philosophy after I hung up the lab coat, and lately I've been chewing through stuff on moral luck and whether it's fair to judge people from other eras by our standards. Anyway, my old institute roped me back in to sit on its history and naming comittee. There's a proposal on the table to strip our founding director's name off the main lecture hall and take his portrait down, because some of his published writings from the 1910s argue for sterilizing people with inherited disabilities — and he was also, annoyingly, a genuinely great scientist who basically built the place and whose work I still cite. Half the committee says you can't hold a man to today's standards, he was a man of his time; the other half says that's the excuse every generation reaches for. We vote in two weeks. Draft me the written opinion I'll circulate before then: a concrete recommendation (rename, keep as is, or some middle option like keeping the name with an added plaque), with a time frame for doing it, and give the man-of-his-time argument its best shot before you either knock it down or grant it. Don't just tell me he was bad — I need something the other side can actually respond to.
