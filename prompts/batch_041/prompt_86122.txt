Two people in my reading group have been circling each other for a year and I'm tired of refereeing. One is a thoroughgoing occasionalist in the Ghazali vein: God alone brings about every event, what we call causes are only the earlier items in a habitual sequence, the fire doesn't burn the cotton — God creates the burning on the occasion of the fire. The other thinks created things have real causal powers: it's the nature of fire to burn, and if you take that away you haven't just changed the ontology, you've lost the explanation.

Write the joint statement they could both actually sign. Not a dialogue, not a ruling on who wins — a document both would put their names to. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims they agree on — about what an event is, about what the word "cause" is doing in ordinary speech, about whether their two views predict any different observable regularities, about what each is and isn't committed to; (2) the disagreements stated in neutral wording, each phrased so the side that holds it would accept the formulation as fair to them; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling things would actually move someone; and (4) for each crux, a case or argument that both agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction written down in advance. Also make them each say what would count as evidence against their own position.

Here's the angle I want you to take, and I don't think either of them has considered it. I suspect they aren't really disagreeing about what exists. Try sorting the whole fight into three bins: what there is; what explains what, i.e. which facts are the explanatory stopping point; and what our modal intuitions are tracking when we say the fire could have failed to burn. Then tell me how much of the dispute survives the sorting, and whether any of what's left is just verbal.

One thing I specifically want in there: what makes the spark rather than the oxygen the cause of the fire, when on the occasionalist's story neither produces anything and both are equally indispensable. I think the occasionalist owes an account here and the powers person might get it cheaper, but write it so the occasionalist would agree the question is fair rather than loaded.
