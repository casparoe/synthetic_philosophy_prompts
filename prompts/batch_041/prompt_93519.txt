Something I keep running into and don't have a word for. Say I'm sure enough of something to say I know it -- my partner isn't cheating, say. A friend tells me she just saw her out with someone. My honest reaction isn't "better look into that", it's "then my friend must have seen something else", and I can even give the reason: if I know she's faithful, then any apparent evidence to the contrary has to be misleading, so there's nothing here to investigate. Same shape with a diagnosis I'm confident in -- the conflicting test result is a false positive, and not getting spooked by it is what being good at this looks like. The reasoning seems airtight and it also seems like a machine for never learning anything. And from the inside I can't tell it apart from being a stubborn person who happens to be wrong; the difference is supposed to be whether I actually know, which is no help in the moment. Is there an established name for this? I don't mean confirmation bias or motivated reasoning -- those are flaws in the person, and this looks like a flaw, if it is one, in a principle that's true. I'd also like to know how it differs from the thing people bring up when two experts disagree and one refuses to budge, which I suspect is a different animal with an unfortunately similar name. And what's the standard fix supposed to be -- stop saying we know things, or reject the step from "I know it" to "that evidence is misleading"? Does it even need knowledge, or does plain belief do the same work? A bare label won't help me much; I want the boundaries.
