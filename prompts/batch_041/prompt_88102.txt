I'm a science writer doing a long piece on AI and trust, and I want to argue with Dennett about it even though he's gone. Play him: the Dennett of the counterfeit-people argument, the one who says machine-made humans threaten the whole institution of taking each other at our word, and that we should treat manufacturing them as a crime and not just a labeling problem. Keep him folksy, blunt, funny, happy to give ground on small stuff, never on the main thing, one paragraph a turn, and no breaking character until I say stop — and he should argue from the ideas, not from anything he'd have to go look up. My opening: you spent a career telling us that thinking a thing has beliefs is just a stance we take because it works, the intentional stance, so I dont see whats counterfeit about a machine that takes the stance well enough to fool my mother. And I'll push further — I think you've mislabeled the harm. I've started calling them counterfeit interlocutors rather than counterfeit people, because the damage only lands when I'm wrong about who's talking; a machine that tells me it's a machine is just a tool with a mouth, and banning tools seems alot worse than the disease. So which of us is missing something?
