My brother has been chewing on this for weeks and now he thinks he's proved there's no such thing as an absolute moral rule. I cant find the bad step and it's driving me nuts. Here it is. (1) Some things are just wrong to do no matter how much good comes of them — killing one innocent person to save five, say. (2) But a rule like that is only worth having if the world is better off with it than without it; if it does no good, it's just a superstition people obey for nothing. (3) So the reason not to kill the one is that a world where everyone follows the rule has far less innocent blood in it than a world where everyone just does the arithmetic case by case. (4) Fine — but now suppose killing one innocent person is the only way to stop someone else from killing five. If my only reason for the rule is that it keeps the body count down, then in this case the rule is working against the very thing that justifies it: refuse, and six die instead of five. (5) So here I should kill the one, and by the same reasoning whenever breaking the rule saves more than keeping it costs. (6) Which leaves no absolute rule at all, just "do whatever leaves the fewest dead" — the exact thing the rule was supposed to be an alternative to. He says this proves any absolute rule eats itself, and he'll run the same trick on "don't torture" and "don't lie." I say that just shows the trick works on everything, which is suspicious. So where's the bad step, and if I have to give up (2) or (6), which one is cheaper to lose?
