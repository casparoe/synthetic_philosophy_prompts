Write a short story about a city of reasoners that must price a sentence whose truth depends on the price they give it.

The city is inhabited by patient, unsentimental people who are good at arithmetic and have little else to their name. Their one great institution is the Exchange, which keeps a single long ledger. Every day, each citizen must post a number between zero and one for every question on that day's list, and the Exchange's official price for a question is the median of the numbers posted for it that day. Any citizen may add a question to the list by writing it there. Three rules have never been broken. First, no question may go unpriced: a citizen may price badly, but never decline. Second, prices are scored against the truth, by a rule the citizens know to be strictly proper, so that a citizen's best possible score on any question comes from posting exactly their honest degree of belief; the citizens have always taken this to mean that the ledger is a record of what the city thinks, and they prize coherence — it is a scandal when two questions that must have the same answer are priced differently. Third, the ledger is auditable: the city's verifier can check any arithmetic claim that has a proof, and can read the ledger's own past entries, so any claim about what was posted on an earlier day is settled at once, though the entries of the day in progress are not final until the day closes. Long ago, for reasons of audit, the charter was amended to admit questions about the Exchange's own records. No citizen has ever had cause to regret the amendment.

This is the morning on which the following sentence appears on the list:

"The Exchange's official price for this sentence on the day it is first posted is less than one half."

The citizens see quickly what they have been handed. Let the median that day be m. If m is less than one half, the sentence is true, and the price of a true sentence should be one; the city will have priced a truth at m. If m is one half or more, the sentence is false, and the price of a falsehood should be zero; the city will have priced a falsehood at m. No value of m is self-consistent: the correct price always lies on the opposite side of one half from the price the city posts. Nor can anyone be excused from pricing it. The sentence is admissible under the charter, it is not malformed, and the verifier has nothing to check until the day is over. And there is no fact waiting to be discovered: the price does not track the sentence's truth, the price makes it.

Requirements:

- Show the day and its aftermath. Include at least three citizens with different responses: one who tries to compute the median by simulating everyone else's reasoning, one who argues from the charter and the scoring rule about what is owed, and one who posts quickly and honestly without seeing the trap. A keeper of the ledger should be present, but not omniscient.
- Let the difficulty surface through action, argument, and the shape of the ledger rather than through exposition. No character may deliver a lecture on the paradox, and no one may name a famous theorem as a key; the citizens must find the structure from their own rules.
- Do not let the difficulty be dissolved by fiat. The citizens may not declare the sentence meaningless or strike it from the ledger, and no outsider may arrive with a proof. They may change the rules, but only after the change has been argued for and its cost counted.
- The story may end in reform, stalemate, or ruin, but it must end in a settled practice rather than a settled answer: something the citizens will henceforth do, and a changed understanding of what a posted number is. Make the last state of things concrete — an amended rule, a new column in the ledger, a habit.
- The subject is logical uncertainty in its sharpest form: reasoning and acting when the question at hand is not independent of one's own answer to it. Let the citizens feel that, not merely diagnose it.
- Third person, roughly 1500 words. Give the city and at least two of its citizens names. No epilogue or afterword explaining the idea.
