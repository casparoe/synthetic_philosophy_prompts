Every few months I come across a new explanation for why we have never heard from anyone else, and each one sounds airtight while I am reading it and flimsy a week later. Before I collect any more of them, I would like the specification first: what does an adequate explanation of the silence have to deliver? Please give me the requirements, with the case or consideration behind each.

Here are the things I would want it to handle, though you should tell me which of them are genuine requirements and which are merely the habits of the people who write about this:

The timescale. It is not only that we hear nothing now. Nobody has left anything we can find, and there is no sign the solar system was ever visited, in a galaxy old enough that a civilization expanding at a leisurely fraction of the speed of light should have crossed it in a few million years, many times over. An explanation that accounts for the present quiet but not for the past seems to be doing half the job.

Whose silence it is. The explanation has to say whether the quiet is a fact about civilizations (they destroy themselves, they hide, they lose interest), a fact about the universe (intelligence is vanishingly rare, expansion is much harder than it looks), or a fact about us (we have swept a sliver of the sky for a sliver of a century). I suspect the third is not an explanation but a description of our ignorance, and I would like that suspicion tested.

Self-application. Whatever it says about detectable civilizations has to hold for us, since we have been leaking radio signals for about a century and are not obviously different in kind from anyone else. If the answer is that being noticed is fatal, the account must either predict our end or say what exempts us — and if we are exempt, it owes an explanation of what makes us special.

Consequences. It should forbid something: there should be a fact about what we would find if we listened harder, looked further, or lasted longer. A story that cannot be wrong in any way is not an explanation of anything.

Parsimony, and the price of giving it up. One cause is more satisfying than a pile of them, but there may be several distinct silences — no signals, no visitors, no traces — and a single mechanism may not cover all three.

I am most interested in any pair of requirements that cannot both be satisfied. My own suspicion is that the silence is total, that we are nothing special, and that we are still here, and that these three cannot all be true at once if the reason for the silence is that anyone who shows themselves is destroyed. I would like to know whether that is right, and if so, which of the three has to give.

Finally, which single requirement does the most to rule candidates out? Then, briefly, how the dark forest hypothesis — everyone hiding because everyone is a potential threat, and striking first upon detection being the only safe move — fares against the list.
