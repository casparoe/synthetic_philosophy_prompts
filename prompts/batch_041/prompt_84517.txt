Consider four modes of holding someone responsible: blaming (forming a negative judgment), resenting (having a reactive attitude), demanding (addressing a claim or reproach), and sanctioning (imposing a consequence). For each of the following agents, rate their standing in each mode on a 0–5 scale, then rank the agents overall by how much standing they have to blame, and identify which agents have standing in one mode but not another. (a) A person who has never committed the wrong but whose condemnation is motivated purely by competitive advantage. (b) A person who committed the same wrong, has sincerely reformed, but has never apologized or made amends. (c) A person who is currently disposed to commit the same wrong but has had no occasion to do so. (d) A person who is complicit in a structurally similar wrong but not in this token wrong, and who benefits from it. (e) A person who is subject to the same coercive institution they condemn, and whose blame functions as protest. (f) A person who is not complicit, but whose blame is epistemically careless: they have no reliable evidence, though their judgment happens to be true. Then give a brief verdict on whether the hypocrite’s loss of standing is best modeled as a single threshold or as a mode-specific and degree-sensitive relation.
