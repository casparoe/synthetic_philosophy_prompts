I run a monthly "ethics lab" for a local effective-altruism-adjacent meetup — mostly twentysomethings who've read all the standard EA material but have never had to actually choose anything under constraints — usually 16-18 people, and I've got the room for 90 minutes next month. I want to build them a trading floor: a market where people buy and sell moral favours, so that they feel, in their bodies rather than in a lecture, the odd result that two people who disagree about what matters can both be better off by doing each other's thing — and then feel exactly where that stops being true. In my own notes I've been calling it the Favour Exchange, but dont let that name leak into whatever instructions I hand them; they should just be told it's a market with a score. Here's the skeleton I have, and I'd like you to build it out into something I can actually run. Each person gets a card with a private value profile: the same four causes for everyone (I've been using animal welfare, global poverty, AI risk, and something small and local, like your own neighbourhood or your own family), with weights summing to 100, deliberately including at least three people whose weights are near-mirror images of each other, because that's where the trade gains live. Then a menu of about six action tokens, all costing the actor the same small amount of time or money but each tagged to one cause — a week of meat-free eating, two hours of volunteering, a small donation, showing up to a protest, a public post, a shift at a phone bank. Everyone must spend every token. The important bit: your score counts every token anyone spends on a cause you care about, not just your own, so the only way to do well is to get other people spending on your causes, and the only thing you have to give them is your own labour on theirs. I want an exchange-rate board where people post offers ("three vegan-weeks for one phone-bank shift"), two or three bargaining rounds on a hard clock, and a clearing house where I record and enforce the deals.

Then I want three frictions layered on top, because I'm fairly sure the interesting part of this idea is the friction and not the free lunch. First, two or three of the cards carry a constraint in small print: this player regards one particular cause as impermissible to promote even instrumentally, so any trade requiring them to do that action is off, no matter how good the rate. Second, the last ten minutes of trading happen with no enforcement at all — deals are just promises and I stop recording them — and I want the group to discover for themselves what that does to a market. Third, one card belongs to someone who refuses to trade on principle and gets five minutes at the end to make their case to the room; I want to see how the traders answer them. I want a debrief that maps the moments back onto the underlying idea: the comparative-advantage logic (gains from trade exist even when one person is better at everything, as long as relative valuations differ), the gap between "more moral value got produced" and "everyone did better by their own lights", the enforcement and free-riding problems, and the integrity objection. Give me the timeline, what to print, how to score it without calculators, what will predictably go wrong the first time and how to patch it live. And tell me straight what the game can't reproduce — my suspicion is that it can't touch the question of whether the players' scores are commensurable at all, i.e. there's no fact of the matter about whether the world after trading is better, only that each player prefers it, and I'd rather the debrief say that plainly than pretend the exchange rate is a real exchange rate. If a smarter version has them trading something other than actions — attention, endorsements, votes — say so too, because I might want that for a second session.
