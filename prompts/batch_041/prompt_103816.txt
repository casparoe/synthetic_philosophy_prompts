So I'm a novelist, about a third of the way into a book, and I've written myself into a problem I actually want to keep. Premise: a woman with a fast-moving neurodegenerative disease enrolls in a trial where her biological neurons get swaped out for silicon ones, a couple percent a week, over roughly a year. She's awake and lucid the whole time. It's not the "scan and copy" version — it's continuous, one-in-one-out replacement, and the whole story is told in her close third person from start to finish.

The thing the book is about (or one of the things) is: when does she stop being her? I don't mean mystically. I mean it should control boring craft decisions — when the narration can still call her by her first name, when other characters are justified in grieving her before she's dead, when a promise she made in chapter two should still bind her, when I can have her look at a photo of herself from last spring and feel recognition vs. horror. Right now I'm doing this on vibes, it's inconsistent, and my editor keeps asking me what the rules are. She wants a one-page "continuity doc" she can check chapters against, because we're on a deadline and she signs off on every draft.

So what I want from you: turn the philosophy of personal identity into an actual procedure I can run at each weekly checkpoint in her year — the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer at each step, where the procedure flatly says "not her anymore" or "still her," and where it has to stop and hand me a judgement call because it's a values question, not a fact question. Assume I know roughly zero of the technical literature and can't look anything up, so no reading list, no citations, no "see so-and-so's paper on this." Just the machinery.

Here's the wrinkle that makes it hard, and it's the reason I need a real procedure and not a slogan: in my version the chips aren't a clean one-for-one. They network with an offsite scaffold, so mid-process part of "her" cognition is running on a server in another state. And about ten percent of her original tissue is kept in a cold backup that the company says they *could* reboot if the trial goes wrong — which means somewhere around month nine there are arguably two of her. She can also pause the process for a few weeks, and she does, twice. I don't want a procedure that quietly assumes none of that is happening.

Then run the procedure on three specific checkpoints and tell me honestly where it gives me an answer I won't like:

1. Week 6, ~12% replaced, she passes every test they give her and remembers her daughters birthday.
2. Week 30, ~60% replaced, she's sharper than she's been in years but her taste in music has flipped and she's forgotten how her marriage ended.
3. Week 48, ~95% replaced, the cold backup is still on ice, and she's asking the company to reboot it so "she" can talk to her own daughter one more time.

And tell me what the procedure can't do — the places it'll misfire, and where I should just make an authorial choice and own it.
