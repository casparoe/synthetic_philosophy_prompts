I run a small reading group (mixed bag: a couple of traders, some philosophy grad students, one guy who will not shut up about ergodicity) and we keep re-litigating bet sizing. Half of them are Kelly people, the other half think Kelly is a cult. I want to stop the shouting and hand out a decision tree instead: you answer the questions, you land on a leaf, the leaf tells you what you actually believe.

So please write me that tree. Topic is the Kelly criterion / log-optimal betting, and the bigger claim that rationality is "geometric" — that what you should maximize is the thing that compounds over time rather than expected value.

Three things I care about:

1. Build it from the logical space, not from a roster of famous names. I don't want citations or who-said-what (I can't look anything up anyway) — give me the questions first, and then locate the positions in the space of answer combinations. If some combination of answers corresponds to a position nobody actually holds, keep the path in the tree anyway and tell me why it's empty.

2. These distinctions have to be doing work somewhere in the tree (add your own as well):
   - Is "maximize expected log wealth" just expected-utility maximization wearing a hat (log is a utility function like any other), or is it a seperate animal — a claim about time-average growth that isn't EU at all, and that says the ensemble average is the wrong average to care about?
   - The long-run argument — the log bettor almost surely ends up ahead of any other fixed-fraction bettor — is that a normative result, or a description of what happens if you literally never stop? What does it tell you when you get one bet, or twenty, or you'll stop in five years at a time you can't predict?
   - Fractional Kelly: is betting a third of Kelly a fix to the criterion (log is too aggressive for a real person with a real life) or a fix to your probabilities (you don't actually know p, you guessed it)?
   - Does "there's no right answer, it's about your risk tolerance" count as a position on the tree or as a refusal to have one? Put it somewhere either way.

3. Use these cases as actual branch questions, phrased so someone can answer them without knowing which team it puts them on:
   - One shot, 60/40 coin, even money, you'll never bet again. What fraction of your bankroll? Does your answer change if that bankroll is your rent money?
   - A thousand independent 60/40 coins offered all at once, even money on each. Full Kelly on each one stakes 200% of your bankroll. What do you do — and does that case tell you whether Kelly is a decision rule or just a sizing heuristic?
   - A favorable bet with roughly a 1% chance of wiping you out completely and a big upside. Take it or not, and does your "geometric" story give you a reason either way?
   - Does it matter that zero is absorbing — you can't bet your way back from zero — as opposed to going low but staying in the game? Where does that distinction enter your answer?
   - Ten thousand sequential bets, but you're forced to stop at some point drawn from a distribution you don't know. Still Kelly?

And if two leaves on your tree can't actually be told apart by any question I could ask a person, say so instead of pretending the tree separates them.

Give it to me as JSON, not prose, with this shape:

{
  "nodes": [{"node_id": "N1", "parent_id": null, "question": "...", "answers": [{"answer": "...", "leads_to": "N2"}]}],
  "leaves": [{"leaf_id": "L1", "position_name": "...", "summary": "...", "path": ["N1: yes", "N2: no"], "awkward_commitment": "...", "status": "defended"}],
  "unoccupied_paths": [{"path": ["N1: no", "N3: yes"], "diagnosis": "...", "verdict": "incoherent"}],
  "undistinguished_pairs": [{"pair": ["L2", "L5"], "why": "..."}]
}

Where: node_id and leaf_id are short unique strings; parent_id is null for the root and otherwise the id of the node it hangs off; question is the branch question in plain language; answers is a list of 2-4 options at that node, each pointing to the id of the node or leaf it leads to; path in a leaf is the chain of question-and-answer steps that reaches it; summary is one sentence on what that position claims; awkward_commitment is one sentence on the thing that leaf has the hardest time explaining; status is "defended" or "unnamed"; verdict is one of "incoherent", "unattractive", "unexplored". Keep it to 6-9 question nodes and at most 10 leaves.
