I began homeschooling my own children last year, and this fall I was appointed to our state's home-education review panel. We review individualized learning plans monthly; our decisions can remove a child from home instruction, and we have about thirty minutes per file, no subject-matter experts, and one member signs off. I want a decision procedure grounded in the principle that parental authority over education is real but bounded by the child's interest in an open future—so the state's interest is not in matching public-school content but in preserving the child's ability to exit into other ways of life. The hard part is distinguishing a curriculum that is merely unfamiliar from one that forecloses exit. Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a denial, and where it must hand the decision to a person because the question is one of values. Then run it on three plans: a young-earth creationist curriculum that rejects evolution; an unschooling plan with no fixed subjects; and a plan that is academically strong but keeps the child isolated from anyone outside the family's religion. Include one where the procedure tells me to deny a plan I personally find sympathetic, flag the cases it cannot settle, and say where it will misfire.
