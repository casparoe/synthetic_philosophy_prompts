I'd like a credence estimate, with the reasoning decomposed, on a question in moral theory I keep failing to settle by intuition alone: does Bernard Williams's integrity-based objection to utilitarianism succeed?

To fix the target, the objection as I understand it has two prongs. The first is the rescue case, where a man saves his wife rather than a stranger and is then imagined justifying the choice by appeal to the general permissibility of partiality; the complaint is that the justification is a contaminant rather than a vindication, that he has had one thought too many. The second is the Jim-and-the-firing-squad case, where the demand that Jim deliberate in impartial utilitarian terms is said to alienate him from the projects and commitments that give his life its shape. I take the intended conclusion to be that utilitarianism is not merely demanding but incoherent as a first-personal guide, and that this counts against its truth rather than only against its usefulness as a decision procedure.

Please give me your credence that the objection, so construed, succeeds, meaning that at least one prong establishes a genuine defect in act utilitarianism as a theory of right action rather than a psychological difficulty in applying it. Decompose as follows, with a number and one line of justification for each term:

1. The probability that the rescue case is correctly described, i.e. that requiring a justification of partial action is a real defect rather than an aesthetic preference for unreflective devotion.
2. The probability that the integrity or alienation prong identifies something distinct from the ordinary demandingness objection, rather than restating it in fancier vocabulary.
3. The probability that utilitarianism can absorb the objection by separating criterion of rightness from decision procedure, so that the objection shows only that utilitarianism should not be consciously applied.
4. The probability that the objection, if sound, generalizes beyond utilitarianism to any theory requiring agents to be able to justify their particular attachments, including the Kantian reading of the rescue case, which is arguably what Williams was actually targeting.
5. Any further term you think belongs, stated explicitly.

Then give the combination, and tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to and what argument or evidence would move it most.

For calibration: my own credence is about 0.4, and a friend who works on consequentialism puts hers at 0.75. If it is cheap to do so, tell me which sub-question our disagreement most likely lives in.

Finally, state how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of, including, if it applies, uncertainty about whether my framing of the objection has already begged a question.
