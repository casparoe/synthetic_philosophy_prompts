As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I have watched the honor/dignity/victimhood trichotomy get adopted, argued over, and quietly overextended, and I would like to put it to a sharper test than the usual round of asking whether any of the three is morally superior. Please give me a tier list, best to worst, on the dimension specified below, with a numeric rating for each entry.

The three entries: (1) honor culture, in which personal standing is a scarce good held at others' sufferance and defended by a demonstrated readiness to answer slights; (2) dignity culture, in which persons are credited with an intrinsic worth that insults do not touch, so slights are to be ignored or referred to impersonal authority; (3) victimhood culture, in which moral status is claimed through the public recognition of harm suffered, and the aggrieved appeal over the heads of the offending party to third parties for redress.

The dimension is not goodness in the round but a narrower one that I think actually discriminates: how well each culture handles what I will call an insolvent grievance, meaning a sincere claim of wrong that cannot be paid off, that no available authority is positioned to adjudicate, and where the complainant has neither exit nor leverage. Rank them by how they process such a case: whether the culture grants the complainant standing at all; whether it can separate a genuine insolvent grievance from a manufactured one; and whether its own machinery of recognition leaves everyone downstream of the recognition decision better or worse off.

Two further requests, which are the real reason I am writing.

First, I want a fourth entry, and I want you to construct it rather than report it. Propose a fourth culture type that is a genuine attractor rather than a rebranding of one of the three, and place it in the ranking. It should differ from the others in its answer to the question of who has standing to demand redress. Say briefly what makes it a distinct equilibrium rather than a variant.

Second, rate each of the four from 0 to 1 on the fraction of its apparent moral superiority that is an artifact of the environment in which it tends to flourish, such as scarcity, weak third-party enforcement, or high costs of exit, rather than something portable. A score near 1 means the culture looks good only where it grew; near 0 means the merit would survive transplant. Explain each score in a sentence.

Keep each justification to one or two sentences, do not force a strict order if two entries genuinely tie, and flag anywhere you think my framing has already smuggled in a verdict.
