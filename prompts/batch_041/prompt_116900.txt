I'm a postdoc and I'm giving a paper at our metaphysics reading group in a few weeks. Heres my line, roughly, and then I'll tell you who's in the room.

Everyone in the finks-and-masks literature treats finkish cases and masked cases as two species of one thing -- "antidotes" that make the conditional "if triggered, it would manifest" come out false while the disposition is still present -- and then concludes that the conditional analysis of dispositions is dead. I think that's too fast, because finks and masks aren't the same kind of case. In a fink, the triggering event itself removes the basis of the disposition, so the counterfactual is false at a time when the disposition is genuinely there: real counterexample. In a mask, the disposition and its basis are untouched and something merely sits between the trigger and the manifestation. So I want to say the right conditional for "x is disposed to M when T" isn't "if T, x would M" but something like "if T and nothing interfered, x would M" -- and that this isn't an ad hoc save, because the interferer is exactly the kind of thing the disposition was never a disposition to overcome. Payoff: masks don't refute the analysis at all, only finks do, so the anti-dispositionalist's case is far narrower than the literature makes out and rests entirely on whether finks are unavoidable or just engineered. (I still dont know what to do about mimics -- the mirror-image cases where the conditional comes out true and there's no disposition -- so I'm planning to punt on them in the talk, which I suspect is a mistake.)

Now the room. Two people who work on laws and modality, Humeans (best-system, Lewis-style): they think disposition-talk is shorthand and they'll go straight at whether my "nothing interfered" clause is contentful or just ceteris paribus with a new coat of paint. One dispositional essentialist who knows this literature cold and will not grant that finks and masks come apart -- she'll say the distinction isn't stable under redescription and that I've reinvented antidotes. Then a person who works on essence and grounding, who will ask what grounds the truth of my conditional. An early-modern historian who'll ask, genially, what Locke or Boyle would have made of it and whether I'm just re-describing powers vs. qualities. And a visiting cognitive scientist who works on dispositions in psychology, doesn't know this literature at all, and will ask why any of it matters for how we explain behavior.

Give me the prep sheet: the objections roughly in the order I'll actually get them, phrased the way these specific people would phrase them -- a Humean's version of "your clause is vacuous" and an essentialist's version of "your distinction is unstable" should not read the same -- each with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is softest, and include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And push back where you think I'm wrong: if my line really is just the standard intrinsic-base fix with extra steps, or if the fink/mask distinction doesnt survive the redescription argument, tell me straight instead of writing me a prep sheet for a view I shouldn't be defending. I'd rather find out now than in the Q&A.
