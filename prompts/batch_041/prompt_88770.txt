Please argue for a constraint I shall call the custody condition on the extended mind thesis: an external resource counts as part of a subject's cognitive system only if the subject stands in a relation of custody to it — being able to maintain it, to recognise when it has failed, and to repair or replace it — and not merely a relation of reliance. Argue that without some such condition the thesis cannot answer the question of whose mind an extended process is, and that the familiar candidates, such as a notebook or a navigation aid, either satisfy the condition and so genuinely extend a particular mind, or fail it and are better described as tools that a mind uses. Then set out the strongest case against your position — two or more people whose thinking is interlocked through a shared resource that none of them can maintain or inspect — and say what the custody condition should say about it, and why that answer is not merely stipulative. Write in British English.
