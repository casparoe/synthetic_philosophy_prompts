Ok so I'm trying to get my own head straight on joint action before I put it in a seminar paper, and I want a consistancy check first — be brutal.

Here's the package:

(1) There are no group minds. Every mental state there is, is had by an individual; 'we believe' is always shorthand for something going on in a bunch of individual heads.
(2) Some intentions are irreducibly joint. When we walk to the pub together, 'we intend to walk to the pub' isn't a conjunction of my intention and yours, and it isn't a prediction either of us makes about the other — it's one state with a we-shaped content.
(3) That joint intention comes with a commitment to the other person that neither of us can unilaterally cancel, and it isn't grounded in either person's self-interest or in any bet about what the other will do. If I've committed with you, the fact that I no longer feel like it doesn't release me.
(4) Obligation and blame are borne by individuals, and only by individuals. There's no group that is obligated or that is to blame.
(5) A joint action isn't just the sum of the individual actions. 'We moved the sofa' does explanatory work that no list of 'I did X, you did Y' can replace — the joint doing is a real unit of explanation.
(6) But joint action doesn't always need a joint intention. Two strangers who wordlessly end up carrying a heavy sofa down a stairwell together, neither having intended anything about the other beforehand, are doing something together, not just doing two things side by side.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each? Rank them by severity rather than calling everything equally bad. For each real clash, give me the cheapest fix and what I'd be giving up, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
