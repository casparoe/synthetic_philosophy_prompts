so for philosophy club I'm working on something about the responsibility gap with autonomous systems (the idea that when a self-learning system does something bad, theres kind of nobody left who deserves the blame, because the designers couldn't have predicted that exact behaviour and you obviously can't blame the machine). If it goes well it definately becomes my extended essay.

I've collected a pile of proposed fixes from reading around, and I want you to sort them into tiers by how much they actually solve the problem -- not how likely they are to ever get passed into law. Use your own judgement if you think two of them are secretly the same thing, or if there's an obvious one I've missed.

1. Strict liability on whoever deploys it: never mind whose fault it was, the deployer pays.
2. Give the AI legal personhood, like we do with companies, so it can hold duties and be sued.
3. No-fault insurance or a compensation fund, so victims get made whole without anyone being found at fault (a bit like how some countries handle car accidents).
4. Just put it on the human in the loop, even when they had no real chance to stop it -- the moral crumple zone thing, where the operator soaks up the blame for the system failing.
5. Change the question: stop asking who deserves blame and ask instead who has to give an account and who has the standing to demand one, so responsibility is something we keep doing rather than something we discover.
6. Make responsibility graded: it comes in degrees depending on how much control and knowledge someone had, so nobody's fully on the hook but lots of people are partly on the hook, and that's good enough.
7. Say there is no gap at all, because it only looks like a gap if you assume blame has to mean deserving punishment -- ask instead who is going to fix this, and the gap disappears.

Tiers please, with a sentence on why each one lands where it does, then say which single one you'd actually bet on and what would make you change your mind.

Also be straight with me about which bits of this are genuinely settled among people who work on it and which are still live fights, because I don't want to walk into club arguing for something that's really just one camp's view as if everyone agrees.

Lead with the tier list in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No intro, no restating my question.
