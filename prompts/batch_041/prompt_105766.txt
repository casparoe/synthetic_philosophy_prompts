I've been chewing on an idea and I want you to try to knock it down. Say you've got a bunch of people (or countries, or companies, or AIs) who have to agree on rules for dividing things up or working together, and none of them knows exactly what the others really want or how much they care. Heres my claim: if you can find a change to those rules that leaves every single one of them better off no matter how their hidden wants actually turn out -- so it's an improvement in every possible version of the situation, not just on average, and nobody has to trust anyone's guesses about the others -- then they should just take it, and anyone who objects is being unreasonable. I think of it as an upgrade nobody could legitimately complain about. What would count against this? Not silly edge cases, the real problems. Does the "no matter what anyone actually wants" part make it so strict it almost never applies, so the whole thing is basically empty? Or could one of these upgrades actually make things worse down the line -- like by messing up the bargaining or the distrust that got everyone to the table in the first place? And does the idea quietly assume people's wants are fixed and that the current situation is the fair baseline? Tell me which of these a defender would just shrug off and which are actually serious, rank them, and be honest if the whole thing turns out to be either impossible to argue against or just empty.
