ok so my little sister is getting married in three weeks and she asked me to give the toast, and I am completely stuck, so im hoping you can just write the thing for me.

heres the situation. every big dinner on my dads side of the family, going back as far as anyone can remember, the youngest person at the table has to stand up and say a little something before anyone is allowed to eat, and it has to end with the line my grandpa made up: green ones are for guests. its nonsense, nobody knows what it means, half of us are pretty sure he was just messing with us the first time and then it stuck forever. my grandma treats it like it is practically holy. my cousins do it without even thinking now, like its breathing.

meanwhile my sisters fiance's family is coming. they are lovely and extremely normal, and they are going to sit there watching a table of grown adults do this weird ritual and i can basically watch them deciding we are a cult.

so i went down a rabbit hole on the internet about this philosopher David Lewis (im not a philosopher, i just got curious one weekend). the idea i landed on is that a convention is really just a solution to a coordination problem -- nobody ever agreed to it, it is completely arbitrary, and it keeps going only because everybody expects everybody else to keep doing it. the second enough people stop expecting it, its just gone. and apparently you can sometimes be the first person to just quietly not do it, if enough people are watching you not do it. which honestly describes our dinner thing perfectly. but it also means i cant just stand up and announce we are retiring it, because if grandma still expects it and my cousins still expect it, then it is still happening, i have just made things awkward. and i cant pretend it is deep or sacred either, because it isnt, and the in-laws would smell that from across the room.

so what i want is a toast that does a sneaky thing. i want it to get everyone at the table to realize, at the same time, that this is a thing we do and not a thing we have to do -- so the whole table can laugh and loosen its grip on it together, without anyone having to be the bad guy. but my grandma has to hear it as love and not as me killing her husbands memory. and the fiance's family should feel like they are being let in on the joke, like, welcome to the coordination game, here are the rules, also the rules are made up, you can pick which ones you want. not like they married into a cult.

keep it warm and funny, four or five minutes tops, no philosophy words, no citing books, no telling the room what a convention is like im giving a lecture -- just let the idea shape what the toast can and cant say. and it has to end with the green ones line, obviously, because grandma will be watching.
