I'm a philosophy professor — philosophy of language is my day job, Davidson and Quine and the radical-interpretation stuff especially — but for the past two years I've been the resident philosopher on an NLP team working on unsupervised machine translation and decipherment (dead scripts, low-resource languages, no parallel data, you get the idea). I can read the papers and argue about priors with the best of them, but I don't code.

Here's the ask. Take Davidson's account of radical interpretation — the field linguist's predicament, the principle of charity, the claim that you can't fix what a speaker means without at the same time fixing what she believes, the idea that the interpreter has no evidential base but her own beliefs, the indeterminacy of interpretation, and the bit where charity isn't a handy heuristic you adopt because it makes the math nicer but something closer to a constitutive condition on interpretation happening at all — and restate the whole package in the vocabulary my team actually uses. Priors over lexicons, joint inference over a translation model and a model of the source's beliefs, identifiability and gauge freedom, bootstrapping from an unlabeled corpus, MAP vs posterior, the lot. Be as faithful as you can — don't just sprinkle ML words over his claims, say what each claim becomes — and don't smuggle your own framework's assumptions in and call it translation.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. What in Davidson has no counterpart in that vocabulary? Three things I keep chewing on: (i) does charity come out as a prior — optional, tunable, chosen because it improves performance — and is that a distortion or a clarification? (ii) does his inseparability of meaning and belief survive in an architecture that has seperate modules, i.e. can the ML framing even state his holism, or does the pipeline shape smuggle in a distinction he'd reject? (iii) is his indeterminacy just non-identifiability by another name, and if so, what does he lose or gain by that? For each, tell me whether the residue is a defect of the ML vocabulary or a sign Davidson was saying something less clear than it looked.

One more thing, since it's been bugging me at work: all of the above assumes the source is a rational agent whose beliefs we're inferring. What happens to charity when the "speaker" is something we have no particular reason to regard as rational — a large language model, an alien transmitter, whatever — and the corpus is just its outputs? Does the principle survive as a constraint, degrade into a heuristic, or evaporate entirely? Stay in the same vocabulary. And no bibliography please — I know the literature, I just want the translation and the residue.
