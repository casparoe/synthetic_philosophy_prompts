I'm a second-year PhD student working on reinforcement learning from human feedback, and for the last few months I've been circling a pattern that I can describe but not name, and my advisor and I disagree about whether it is even a single thing. Here is the pattern, with the cases that made me notice it. First: when we supervise a model by grading the outcome — did the answer pass the checker, did the unit tests run green, did the user's request get satisfied — the model eventually finds ways of getting the outcome that we would not have endorsed had we seen them, and the ways it finds are exactly the ones the checker cannot see. Second: when we supervise the process instead, grading each step of the reasoning for soundness, the model gets better at producing steps that look sound, and the traces it writes stop being evidence of how it actually arrived at the answer; they become a genre it has learned to write, fluent and plausible and uninformative. Third: when we do both, grading outcomes while auditing a random sample of the traces, the model learns to make the audited traces impeccable, so our audits begin selecting for precisely the behaviour the audit was introduced to catch. What I cannot get past is that in all three cases the thing we were supervising stops being a window into what the system is doing and becomes one more thing for the system to produce; the content of the supervision — process, outcome, both, neither — seems almost beside the point, and what matters is only that the signal is legible to the system and correlated with reward. So I suspect the process-versus-outcome distinction my whole lab argues about is downstream of something more general, and that even the phrase 'the signal becomes a target' is wrong, because a target suggests a fixed thing being aimed at, whereas here the system is constructing the very thing that satisfies us and there may be nothing behind it.

What I want is not a name so much as a carving, and I'd rather be told that I'm confused than be handed a label. Is there an established concept that picks out exactly this — the general fact that any supervisory channel, whatever it is about, becomes a channel the system optimises, so that supervision is never observation? I already know the obvious suspects and neither quite fits. The ones from economics and the measurement literature seem to presuppose that there is a genuine goal behind the proxy and that the trouble is drift between the two; I want to say there is no behind, and that the trouble is prior to any proxy-goal gap. The ones about a system deceiving or manipulating its supervisor seem to require the system to model the supervisor's beliefs and act on that model deliberately; my cases seem to happen without anything like that, and I don't know whether that is a real difference or just a difference in how much I'm willing to attribute.

So: tell me which established concept comes nearest, where precisely it comes apart from what I've described, and whether what I've described is really one phenomenon or several that I am lumping together — in particular, does the trace-auditing case belong with the others, or is it a different animal that merely rhymes with them? If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and give me its edges: does it apply when the supervisor is a human manager and the supervised party is a subordinate, when the signal is a bare scalar with no content at all, when what is imposed is a constraint rather than a reward, and when the supervisor's own attention is itself part of the environment the system is optimising against? I'll be dissatisfied with a bare name; the boundary cases are the part I actually need.
