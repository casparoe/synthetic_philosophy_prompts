I've been trying to write down my epistemic commitments about group judgment so that someone can tell me whether they actually cohere, and I'd like an audit rather than a defense of any one of them. Here is where I've landed.

1. The Condorcet result is basically right: if each voter is better than chance and the votes are independent, then a majority of a larger group is more likely to be correct than a majority of a smaller one, and the probability of a correct majority tends toward certainty as the group grows. Other things equal, I take larger aggregations to be epistemically better than smaller ones.

2. The load-bearing assumption is independence. Where the members' errors are correlated — they read the same sources, share the same blind spots, are moved by the same incentives — the guarantee evaporates, and a majority can be less reliable than a single careful judge who refuses to defer.

3. A community of inquiry is precisely a group that shares evidence and reasons together, so real deliberating bodies rarely satisfy independence, and deliberation can actively make things worse by spreading one member's error to the whole room.

4. The correct way to combine many people's opinions is Bayesian: treat each opinion as evidence, weight it by how well calibrated that person is, and update. Aggregation is updating, not counting.

5. There is no group credence over and above the individuals' credences. A group believes something only derivatively, as a summary of what its members believe, so I am suspicious of any claim that a collective is a truth-tracker in its own right.

6. A fixed point I have carried for years: a dozen independent jurors each 60% likely to be right will outperform a single juror who is 90% likely to be right, so I would take the dozen.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and which single commitment is doing the most damage. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be and what that fix costs. Then tell me which one revision would clean up the most, and be explicit about the ones that dissolve once you state the assumptions carefully rather than flagging everything as equally bad. I would rather have a ranked audit than a list.
