A philosopher I've been emailing keeps steering me towards something that I think is the real story, but I defnately can't tell how far to run with it, so I want you to work out the implications for me. Here's the shape of it. Almost every long string of 0s and 1s is incompressible: there are 2^n strings of length n, but only so many short descriptions to go round, so by a straightforward counting argument the overwhelming majority can't be squeezed at all — they can only be written out in full. That much I can follow. The bit that snagged me is the next step: no formal system can prove, about any particular string, that it's incompressible beyond a fixed threshold, and the threshold depends on the system rather than on the string. So every system of proof has a ceiling past which it can neither compress a thing nor certify that the thing can't be compressed — while out in the wild nearly everything sits above that ceiling. The philosopher's kicker is that maths and physics only ever get traction on the compressible corner of the space of possible facts, and that nobody ever asks whether the deep facts live in that corner or whether we've simply been picking the fruit that's low enough to reach.

What I want from you is the most important implications of that package — for the ideal of a final theory or an end to science, for the old assumption that everything has an explanation (the principle of sufficient reason, if that's what it's called), and for the claims coming out of the AI labs that these systems will eventually clear up mathematics.
