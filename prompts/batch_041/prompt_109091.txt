I'm a high school junior and I have to write a short story for my philosophy club, and I'm definately overthinking it. I want it to mess with the idea that you experience things in the order they happen. There's this thing called the color phi phenomenon: you flash a dot in one spot, then a dot in another spot, and if the timing is right people swear they saw the first dot move to the second spot and change color halfway there, even though the second dot's color couldn't have been known yet. Some people say this means your brain is Orwellian, like it actually rewrites the experience after the fact. Other people say it's Stalinesque, meaning your brain just waits and then shows you a fake recording, so nothing is literally revised. I want a story set in a near future where a cheap memory patch called a Loom lets people scrub the last two seconds of their own perception. My main character is a witness in a hit-and-run trial who used a Loom right after the crash. The defense says the Loom proves her memory is a Stalinesque reconstruction, not a real experience; the prosecution says the Loom only exposes what was already Orwellian in all of us. Can you write it so the story doesn't tell us which side is right, but the plot turns on a specific detail that only makes sense if one of them is? Keep it like 1000 words, first person, and don't make it a lecture—the philosophy should be in the objects and the arguments, not in a paragraph explaining the terms. Also maybe give the patch a dumb brand name like Loom because that's what a startup would do. Thanks.
