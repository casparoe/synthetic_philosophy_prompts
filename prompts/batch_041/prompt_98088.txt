I've been sitting in on a seminar series on cultural evolution, and I keep meeting the same move: someone says that memes are to culture what genes are to biology, and then treats that as settling whatever was at issue. The instance I want you to audit is this. A colleague argued that because cultural variants are replicators in the same sense that genes are — they get copied, they vary, and some copies outnumber others — cultural change is a Darwinian process through and through, and so we should expect cultural traits to be well designed for one thing only: their own propagation. On his view, the default assumption that a cultural practice persists because it benefits the people who carry it is a prejudice on a par with thinking that organisms exist for the good of the species; a practice may be doing nothing for us, or actively harming us, provided it spreads. I found the conclusion arresting but I couldn't tell how much of it was being carried by the analogy itself, so I'd like you to audit the analogy rather than the conclusion.

Please set out the mapping explicitly — what in the cultural case is meant to correspond to the replicator, the vehicle, fitness, mutation, drift, recombination, and the genotype/phenotype distinction — and then tell me where the correspondence breaks. I care especially about the disanalogies that bear on his argument rather than the ones that don't: copying fidelity and whether cultural variants are discrete or blend when they meet; whether cultural inheritance is Lamarckian, or driven by guided rather than blind variation; the absence of any clean analogue to a germ line; horizontal and frequency-dependent transmission; and whether selection even has the right standing relative to drift when populations are small and their boundaries fuzzy. Somewhere in there I suspect the step from "these are replicators" to "expect adaptation" is doing more work than the analogy can bear, but I want to see whether that's right. Finish by telling me what the conclusion actually needs: if the adaptationist claim can be had without the gene analogy, say so and say what it then rests on; and if a better analogy is available for the job he is using this one for, give it to me and explain what it fixes.
