Okay so this is going to sound like a dumb family argument but it's been going on for like two years now and I genuinely can't tell anymore whether my brother-in-law and I even disagree about the same thing. The question is whether a hypocrite gets to call other people out. His position: if you do the thing yourself, you've forfeited the right to complain when somebody else does it — doesn't matter what the thing is, cheating, lying, being cheap, whatever. My position: that can't be right, because then only a saint could ever tell anyone off and nobody's a saint. His comeback is that I'm conflating two things and that the non-hypocrite is the only one with the standing to complain, and the hypocrite specifically has lost it. His favorite case is a mutual friend who cheated on his wife years ago and is now furious that another guy in the group cheated on his girlfriend; my brother-in-law thinks that fury is just void, like it doesn't count for anything, and I think he's obviously allowed to be furious and just isn't allowed to act like the wronged party or make demands on anyone. Round and round.

I've since gone down a bit of a rabbit hole on this and it turns out the same fight exists in more formal versions, with alot of people lining up roughly the same way — one camp says hypocrisy is a genuine bar, another says it's a red herring because whether you've done the same thing yourself has nothing to do with whether the other person's conduct was actually wrong, and a third says it depends on what kind of thing you're doing when you blame someone. That last one is what got me thinking. Because maybe the reason we're stuck is that "calling someone out" is doing at least two jobs at once — one is feeling something and believing they did wrong, and the other is putting a demand on them, like "answer to me for this" — and hypocrisy wipes out the second but not the first. Or maybe blame isn't a demand or a feeling at all but a kind of social pressure or a status you hold, in which case the whole question looks different. Or maybe the real disagreement isn't about hypocrisy at all but about whether "you don't get to say that" is ever a moral fact versus just a polite fiction about who's allowed to be annoying.

So what I want is not "it's complicated, both sides have a point." I want you to diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is: are we using one word for two things, are we disagreeing about what blame even is, are we disagreeing about some underlying moral fact about who owes what to whom, or are we just starting from different gut feelings neither of us can argue for? Use what each side is actually claiming to support the diagnosis, and then tell me what would really count as progress here — some distinction that dissolves it, some case that would settle it, or a reason to think it can't be settled and we should stop. Also tell me how confident you are in the diagnosis and flag the one bit of it you're least sure about.
