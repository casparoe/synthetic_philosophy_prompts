I've been going back and forth with Émilie du Châtelet's little Discourse on Happiness (written in the mid-1740s, published decades after she died) and I think I've ended up half-convinced by a version of her view, so I want you to try to break it. Act as a philosopher who knows the early modern French material cold but is also willing to bring in modern work on well-being. The view, as I'd put it: happiness isn't tranquility or virtue, it's having strong desires plus a decent chance of satisfying some of them, and what keeps you going is a certain quantity of illusion about your own situation and prospects — she thinks we owe most of our pleasures to illusions and lists susceptibility to illusion among the main engines of happiness. Her favourite durable passion is the love of study, because unlike love or ambition it's mostly up to us and doesn't wear out. My extension, which is the part I actually care about: since the illusions can be managed, we should curate our reading, our company and our attention so as to keep a flattering-enough picture of our own lives intact. Truth is one thing, happiness another, and a strictly accurate picture of your prospects isn't obviously worth having. Think carefully about what could count against this — I'd rather have a few well-aimed defeaters than a laundry list.

Give me:
- Reconstruction (~150 words): the position stated as precisely and charitably as you can, flagging where I've gone beyond what she actually argues.
- Cheap shots (~150 words): the objections that sound damning but aren't real defeaters, and why not.
- The real defeaters, ranked (~500 words): arguments, intuitions, cases and empirical results that would count against it, each with a note on what would have to be true for it to land, and ranked from "would sink it" to "awkward."
- What she'd concede (~200 words): which of these her own commitments already admit, and which she'd have to reject.
- Insulation check and verdict (~200 words): has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, is that a problem here, and your bottom line on how exposed it is.

Don't bother with page numbers or references, I just want the arguments.
