Im in a philosophy of economics course this term and our final assessment is a short research proposal — 2 pages, due in about a month — for a project I could actually pull off, not a finished essay. I want to do it on revealed preference and what we're even doing when we treat choice as the measure of utility.

Heres my half-baked idea. People argue about whether preference-is-revealed-by-choice is a *definition* of preference or a piece of *evidence* about it, and the usual move is to shrug and say it's a verbal dispute, pick whichever reading you like, nothing downstream changes. I dont buy that. If choice constitutes welfare, then the moment we correct someone's choices for a bias — present bias, framing, whatever — we're saying their welfare isn't their choice after all, which contradicts the starting point. If choice is only evidence of welfare, we owe an account of the thing it's evidence of, and that thing looks a lot like the mental state the behaviorist reading was trying to avoid in the first place. So both readings seem to quietly collapse, and I want to know if there's a third one that survives — maybe choice is constitutive but defeasible, with some principled story about when it gets defeated and by what.

Please turn this into the proposal. I want: the question stated so it has a definite answer instead of just "explore the debate"; why the going positions leave it open; the actual method, week by week — work through cases, sketch a small formal model, whatever you think is right, and say why that method rather than just surveying views; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing or being obviously too big for a month; and an honest paragraph on which kinds of cases would sink the third reading if they came out the wrong way.

Dont give me a reading list or citations, I'll add those myself, and dont build it around one particular author's version of the distinction — I want it to stand up no matter who turns out to have said it first.
