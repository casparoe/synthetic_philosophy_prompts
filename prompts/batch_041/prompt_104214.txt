I am assembling a seminar on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and I want a ranked assessment of candidate stopping points for the regress rather than a history of the debate. The regress, as I shall state it for this exercise, runs: if a proposition is unified by a relation R that binds its constituents a and b, then the unity of the complex consisting of a, R, and b seems to require a further relation R* to bind R to a and b; R* then requires R**, and so on. A satisfactory stopping point must either stop this at the first step or show why the demand for a further binder is illegitimate, and it must do so without reducing the proposition to a mere list or heap.

Below are candidate diagnoses. Some are familiar positions; at least one is a construction of mine, and you should treat all of them on their merits as stated, not by asking which famous philosopher held them. For each, rate how well it functions as a stopping point for the regress and as an account of propositional unity.

A. The unsaturated-relation diagnosis: relations are incomplete or unsaturated, so predication is not itself a further relation; the regress cannot start because R is not an additional item to be bound.
B. The primitive-instantiation diagnosis: instantiation is not a relation; R's holding of a and b is a non-relational fact, so no R* is needed.
C. The primitive-unity diagnosis: the proposition is a primitive unified whole; its unity is not produced by any binder and cannot be further explained.
D. The mereological diagnosis: the proposition is a complex particular whose unity is the unity of a whole, and the parts are not separately existent in the way the regress assumes.
E. The co-indexing diagnosis: predication is an instruction to co-index argument places, not a further item; the regress arises only when the instruction is reified into an extra relatum.
F. The explanatory-regress diagnosis: the regress is a regress of explanatory demands, not of entities; it shows only that no further entity can explain unity, not that unity is impossible or primitive.
G. The monistic diagnosis: relations are appearances and the ultimate real is one; the regress is a symptom of treating relational appearances as fully real.
H. The identity diagnosis: in predication the predicate is identical with the subject in some non-standard sense, so there is no distinct relation needing a binder.
I. The fictionalist diagnosis: propositions are not unified entities; talk of unity is a useful façon de parler, and the regress is an artefact of taking the talk literally.
J. The benign-infinite-regress diagnosis: an infinite hierarchy of relations is actual and unproblematic; the regress is not vicious, so no stopping point is required.
K. The practice diagnosis: propositional unity is the unity of a rule-governed practice of asserting and inferring, not the unity of an entity; the regress is misdirected from the start.

Sort A-K into tiers: Tier 1 genuinely stops the regress while preserving unity; Tier 2 stops it only by a costly primitive or unexplained posit; Tier 3 relocates the regress or changes the subject; Tier 4 fails or is not a solution. Within tiers, rank from most to least adequate. For each candidate give a holistic overall score from 0 to 1 and sub-scores for regress_arrest, unity_preservation, and semantic_conservatism. Identify the single most promising candidate, the single weakest, and the one requirement that eliminates the most candidates. Flag any pair of candidates that cannot both be right, and any candidate whose success depends on a move that would equally vindicate a rival. Finally, say which candidate you would bet on if forced to choose, and what would have to be true for it to work.

Return a single JSON object with exactly these top-level fields: `criteria`, `ratings`, `ranking`, `most_promising`, `weakest`, `most_eliminating_requirement`, `incompatible_pairs`, `overall_choice`, `overall_choice_condition`. The `criteria` field is an object mapping each criterion name to a one-sentence statement of what it demands. The `ratings` field is an array of objects, one per candidate, each with exactly: `id` (A-K), `label` (the candidate's name), `tier` (1-4), `rank_within_tier` (integer, 1 = best), `overall_score` (0-1, two decimals), `regress_arrest` (0-1), `unity_preservation` (0-1), `semantic_conservatism` (0-1), `verdict` (one of: stops at first step, stops by primitive, relocates, dissolves, benign regress, fails), `key_move` (one sentence), `main_liability` (one sentence), `what_would_have_to_be_true` (one sentence). The `ranking` field is an array of the eleven ids in order from most to least adequate. The `most_promising`, `weakest`, and `most_eliminating_requirement` fields are strings. The `incompatible_pairs` field is an array of objects, each with `pair` (two ids) and `reason` (one sentence). The `overall_choice` field is a string naming the id and candidate; the `overall_choice_condition` field is a string. Do not include any text outside the JSON object. Write in British English.
