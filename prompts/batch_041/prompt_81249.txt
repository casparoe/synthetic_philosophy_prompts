My reading group has been stuck on the spectrum arguments about whether 'better than' is transitive for about two years now and I want a diagnosis of the impasse rather than another round of it. Setup as I understand it, so you can tell me where I've got it wrong: you line up a long series of options that differ from their neighbours by a tiny amount in one good — a hair less pain, an extra month of a slightly worse life, one more unit of some pleasure — so each step looks strictly better than the one before, but the two ends of the line are obviously the wrong way round, with the far end much worse than the near one. Transitivity then forces the first to be better than the last, which is absurd, so something has to give: either transitivity itself, or one of the adjacent judgements that each small step is an improvement, or the assumption that adjacent items are comparable at all (the 'on a par' move, where neighbours are neither better nor worse, just roughly as good). Everyone in the group has picked a horn and can recite the standard replies to the other two, and yet nobody has moved in two years. Is this a verbal fight between people using 'better' as a technical ranking device and people using it for our ordinary pairwise verdicts about cases? Are the sides answering different questions under one heading — one about what relation can guide choice, the other about which case-verdicts we're entitled to keep? Or is it just a collision of two bedrock intuitions, transitivity versus the small-improvement intuition, with the money-pump/exploitation worry as the only non-intuition card on the table?

My own hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than accept: the disagreement is mostly about what the relation is for, and it never resolves because each side quietly treats its own usage as the real one. If that's right, say what would count as progress here — a formal result about acyclic-but-intransitive rankings, a distinction, a change of question — and if it's wrong, tell me what's actually doing the work.

One extra thing I want you to factor in, because I think it's where our spectra get too easy: in every version we discuss, all the steps run along a single dimension, so there's always a currency to measure the steps in. What happens if the steps cross dimensions instead — a bit more health, a bit less friendship, a bit more knowledge — so no one good is the unit? And what if you hook the two ends of the spectrum together into a loop, so the tiny steps close a circle? Does either version change the diagnosis, or does it just relocate the same impasse somewhere else?
