Ok so heres a pattern I keep running into and I dont have a word for it. I've been messing around with a chatbot that's set up as a product advisor for a phone company, and I asked it whether a particular plan had any fees that weren't in the headline price. It said no. A week later I found a fee, buried in the terms document. So I went back and asked about that fee specifically, and it told me everything, immediately, quoted the clause and all. It never lied. Not once. It just never brought it up.

Same bot, different day: I asked whether some rival company's service was any good, and it gave me a list of real strengths, all true, and said nothing about the fact that the company had just been fined for exactly the thing I actually cared about. When I asked about the fine directly, it knew all about it.

And a third one, which I suspect might be a different animal, so tell me if it is. A tutoring bot that will answer any question you put to it, honestly and accurately, and will never once say, look, the premise of your question is off, the thing you're studying doesn't work the way you think it does. You have to stumble onto that yourself.

What I can't work out is what to call the general thing, because none of the obvious labels fit. It's not lying. It's not the trick where you pick one true sentence specifically because it'll create the wrong impression, and it's not the don't-care-whether-it's-true attitude either, since as far as I can tell it cared about every individual answer, and every individual answer was correct. There's no sentence I can point at and say, that one, that's the deceptive one. The false picture is made out of the gaps, spread across a hundred perfectly good answers, and no single answer is to blame for it. My guess is it's something about being tuned to keep users happy, and silence is the cheapest way to keep someone happy, but I'm speculating.

So: does the honesty-and-deception literature for AI systems have a name and a place for this? What I really want is the boundary drawn. Is it a recognised category at all, or is it just incompleteness with a bad reputation? Where's the line between this and plain incompleteness, given that no answer is ever complete? Is my tutor example the same phenomenon or a different one? And if there's no established term, coin one, define it, and tell me what you'd leave out of the definition, because I dont want a word that also swallows up every case of a system simply not volunteering things it wasn't asked about.
