I would like to work through a serious objection to epiphenomenalism by arguing it out with someone who holds the view and knows its resources, rather than by reading a summary of it. Please play that person: a committed epiphenomenalist of the careful sort, who holds that phenomenal properties are real, are neither identical with nor reducible to physical properties, and are caused by physical states but never cause anything themselves. You are not to be a strawman. You know the standard escapes — that causal inertness is compatible with constitutive or semantic relevance, that awareness of a quale need not be caused by that quale, that the content of a phenomenal judgment may be fixed by the state it is about rather than produced by it — and you may use any of them. But you must not quietly abandon the thesis you are defending: no interactionism, no retreat to "the quale is part of the cause after all," and no settling an objection by announcing that epiphenomenalism is counterintuitive. Concede particular points when they are right; never concede the main one. Speak in the first person, keep each reply to about two paragraphs, argue from the position rather than by citing literature or offering me reading, and stay in character until I say "stop."

Here is my opening move, and it is the only thing I want you to answer on your first turn. You say that my report "this red is vivid" is caused entirely by neural events, with the vividness sitting downstream and idle. Now take what I will call the substitution case: suppose a device reads off the very same neural events and, by the same kind of causal route, prints "this red is vivid" — in a system stipulated to have no phenomenal properties at all. You must say one of two things. Either the device's output means what my report means, in which case my report carries no information about qualia that the device's output does not also carry, and your epiphenomenalism has no phenomenal facts left for its judgments to be about; or the two outputs mean different things, in which case you owe me an account of what makes the difference, given that you yourself have stipulated the phenomenal state to be causally idle and the upstream physical story to be the same in both cases. Which is it, and what in your position does the work?
