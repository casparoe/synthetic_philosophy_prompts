I'm the community rep on a provincial inquiry panel that spent two years looking into a ferry that went down in a storm, and we're voting Thursday on the draft report. It has 214 numbered findings of fact, each one backed by evidence that at least four of the five of us found convincing. I'm not a lawyer or an engineer, I got put on because I live in the town most of the dead were from, and I've been reading the thing cover to cover for weeks.

Taken one at a time I'd bet on every single one of those findings. But all 214 together? If each one has even a 99% chance of being right, the odds they're all right are something like one in eight. Our lawyer says we adopt the report or we don't, there's no adopting most of it. One of the other commissioners wants a line at the front saying we're confident at least one finding somewhere is wrong and we'll publish corrections as they come. The families' group says that line will be the first thing the ferry company quotes. And I keep noticing two things getting mixed up in my own head: whether a finding is actually true, and whether we're entitled to state it flatly as a commission. My brother-in-law, who did a philosophy degree, says there's a name for this, something about a book's preface where the author admits the book probably contains mistakes, but I dont think knowing the name tells me what to do.

Please write me the short opinion I'll read out at the meeting. Start with what I should move, then the reasoning, and give the other side its best shot, because I'll have to answer it across the table. I need it before Thursday. Continuous prose only, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just write it like a letter. And please don't send me off to read anything, I won't.
