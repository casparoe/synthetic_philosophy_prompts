Rank the four following accounts of promissory obligation by how well each explains the case below, giving each a rating from 0 to 1 for its adequacy as a general theory of why promises bind. The accounts are: (a) reliance- or expectation-based, on which the obligation rests on the promisee's justified expectation or reliance; (b) will- or authority-based, on which the promisor's act of undertaking an obligation is itself obligation-creating; (c) practice- or convention-based, on which the obligation derives from a social practice of promising and the fairness of conforming to it; and (d) joint-commitment-based, on which a shared commitment arises between promisor and promisee. The case is this: A promises B, who has no surviving relatives and will die within days, never to disclose a certain fact about B's past; B then dies; A is now the only person who knows the fact, and disclosing it would benefit A greatly while harming no one living; A still takes herself to be bound. For each account, give the rating and a one-sentence justification, identify which feature of the case is doing the separating work, and state what the account would have to amend to fit the case. Mark clearly which parts of your verdict are settled among people who study promising and which are genuinely contested, rather than presenting a contested reading as the consensus.
