My friend Dev and I are both consequentialists in the broad sense — we agree that what matters is how things turn out for sentient beings — but we've ended up on opposite sides of the cluelessness problem and its gotten tense. Write a dialogue where we try to talk each other out of our positions. I'm the one who thinks cluelessness basically wrecks the view: the long-run consequences of just about anything are unknowable, since small acts get amplified through chaotic social and technological chains, so expected-value talk mostly launders ignorance into numbers. I've drifted toward thinking you should just do the ordinary decent thing and stop kidding yourself that you're steering history. Dev thinks that's an overreaction and wants to save the framework.

Don't let us talk past each other. Some things I want us to actually go at:

I say that one graph is the whole problem. Dev has to say concretely what he'd do with the money and why, and I get to push back on it.

I should give that a serious run for its money before I say what's wrong with it, if anything is.
- Whether his fallback (act so as to keep future options open, rather than maximize) is just expected-value reasoning in a trench coat.
