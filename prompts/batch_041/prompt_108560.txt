A fictional firm called Provenance Labs sells a service: for a fee, it traces the evolutionary origins of a client's moral convictions and issues a report debunking them. Write the firm's public FAQ — numbered questions in the client's voice, answers in the firm's, a few sentences each, running to roughly 700 words. The register should be that of an ordinary company help page: flat, friendly, procedural, and entirely straight-faced, with no narrator stepping in to signal the joke.

The comedy has to come from the arguments rather than from generic corporate absurdity. A reader who works on evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics should find that the firm's patter is assembled from the real moves in that literature; a reader who does not should still be able to follow what is funny. The target is the habit of treating a genealogy as a refutation, and the unevenness with which that treatment is applied — not the fact that moral convictions have evolutionary histories, which the FAQ may take for granted throughout.

The FAQ should take up, in whatever order suits it:

- what the genealogy is supposed to establish, and whether tracing a conviction to its causes shows it to be false;
- which convictions are eligible for debunking, which the firm declines to handle, and how it justifies the line it draws;
- whether the same treatment would debunk convictions the client is confident about elsewhere — arithmetic, deductive inference, the reliability of memory — and why the firm's service does or does not extend to them;
- whether a debunked client may go on holding the belief, and if not, what the firm advises doing with the vocabulary and the practice;
- whether the firm sells a way to have a belief reinstated, and what that package includes;
- what a debunked client is supposed to do about their conduct, given that the report is addressed to someone who still has to act;
- how the client can know that the firm's genealogy is correct.

The last question should be answered by turning the firm's own method on the firm, in exactly the confident tone of every other answer, so that the reader can see the regress without being told that it is there. Do not invent studies, researchers, or quotations; wherever the FAQ gestures at scholarship, keep the gesture generic.
