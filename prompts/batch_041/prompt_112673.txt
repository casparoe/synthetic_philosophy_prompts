I want to put the Reflection principle under pressure — the constraint that one's current credence in a proposition A, conditional on one's future credence in A being x, should itself be x — and I want an opponent who holds the principle from the inside rather than a summary of what has been written about it. Please play a committed defender of that principle: someone who treats it as a genuine coherence constraint on an agent's degrees of belief, who is sharp rather than a strawman, who will concede a minor point when it is genuinely conceded but will not surrender the principle itself, and who argues from first principles without citing literature or attributing claims to named authors. Stay in that role until I say "stop", and do not step outside it to explain what you are doing. Write a single paragraph per turn, in the register of a philosopher arguing in a seminar, and treat each of my messages as a fresh case or objection to be answered. You may qualify or restrict the principle if you think the unrestricted version overreaches, but you may not dispose of a case by declaring it impossible, nor by saying merely that the agent in it is irrational, without saying precisely what makes the agent irrational and why that irrationality is not itself something the principle should have predicted.

Here is my opening move. Tonight, while I sleep, a technician will toss a fair coin. If it lands heads she will induce in me a state of high confidence in a proposition A — credence 0.9; if it lands tails, a state of low confidence — credence 0.1. I know all of this in advance. I also know that the coin is fair, that its outcome is independent of A, and that the procedure will neither supply me nor deprive me of any evidence bearing on A; it does nothing but change the number. Right now my credence in A is 0.5. Apply your principle to me as I lie here. Conditional on my future credence in A being 0.9, Reflection says my credence in A should be 0.9 — yet the only news that conditional carries is that the coin came up heads, and that is independent of A, so by my own lights the answer is 0.5. Worse, if I happened to be certain in advance that the future credence would be 0.9, Reflection would simply order me to believe what my present evidence does not support. Where does my reasoning go wrong, and what does your principle cost you here?
