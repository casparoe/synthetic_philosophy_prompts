I've been arguing with a couple of mates for weeks now about whether culture really evolves in a Darwinian way — variation, selection, inheritance, the whole package — or whether that's just a metaphor people reach for because it sounds scientific. I've ended up on the side that says it's the real thing: there are cultural variants, they get copied imperfectly, some of them hang around better than others, and over enough time that's sufficient for cultural change to count as a genuine case of natural selection rather than a loose analogy. My mate who did a biology degree isn't having it, though, and he's got one point I can't shake. He says the whole picture only works if a cultural variant resembles its ancestor because it was copied from it, and not merely because the two of them happen to be tracking the same features of the environment or the same quirks of human psychology — and he reckons that once you look closely, most of what gets called inheritance in culture is the second sort of thing, which leaves nothing for selection to accumulate. I honestly can't tell wether that's a decisive objection or just a fussy point about how strictly you want to define copying.

What I'd like from you is this: tell me what would actually count against my position, and rank it for me. Not a list of names who disagree — I don't need a reading list and couldn't look anything up anyway — but the specific findings, arguments, intuitions and cases that ought to move me if I'm being honest, including the sort of thing my mate is pushing and the ones I haven't thought of. Which of them would a committed defender of cultural Darwinism themselves concede are awkward, and which would they brush off, and on what grounds? I'm also aware I might be hedging — I keep sliding between a strong version (culture is literally selection among replicators) and a weaker one (there's something selection-like going on in there somewhere), and I suspect the answer about what counts against me is completely different depending on which I actually hold. So if it matters, tell me it matters.

And then be straight with me at the end: is this the sort of position that can be qualified until nothing could ever count against it, and if so, is that a genuine problem for it or just the price of any theory that general? I'd rather be told my view is well insulated and that this is a flaw than be handed a tidy list of pros and cons.
