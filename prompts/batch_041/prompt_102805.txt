I'm putting together an instrument for a graduate reading group on blame and moral address, and I want it to do something better than a list of -isms. The topic is standing to blame, with particular attention to hypocrisy, and the deliverable I'm after is a taxonomy of the positions laid out as a tree. Please build it from the logical space rather than from a roster of names: first identify the questions on which the views genuinely divide, then locate the defensible positions among the answer combinations, and only then attach labels to them. Every branch node should be phrased as a question a reader could answer without already knowing where it leads — a concrete commitment about what standing is, or about when it is lost, not a question about which camp one takes oneself to be in.

I want the following distinctions to form the spine of the tree rather than its decoration: whether lacking standing makes an act of blame impermissible, merely non-commendable, or still entirely permissible but criticisable as a piece of moral meddling; whether standing is a binary status or a matter of degree; whether it is lost for a token (this particular reproach) or for a type (any reproach on that norm); and whether the conditions for possessing it are one or several — the blamer's own consistency, the blamer's complicity in the wrong, whether the wrong is any of the blamer's business at all, and whether the blamer is prepared to accept the same blame from others. I should also like the tree to say something about what standing is for: a moral power to hold someone to account, a condition on the aptness of a reactive attitude, or a merely pragmatic constraint on what it is useful to say. And there must be a branch separating views about whether a hypocrite can recover standing by reforming, by apologising, by submitting to blame first, or not at all.

Please make sure the following cases are used, since these are the ones my group keeps getting stuck on: a man who gave up smoking after twenty years and now reproaches his brother, still smoking, at every family dinner; a doctoral student who reproaches her supervisor for taking industry money while she is herself funded by the same industry; a state that lectures another on its treatment of a minority while doing much the same at home; someone who blames his own earlier self; and a member of a privileged group reproaching another member of the same group for a remark that both of them have made. Somewhere between eight and twelve leaves, please. Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) together with a single sentence naming its most awkward commitment. Where two positions come out indistinguishable on your questions, flag the pair rather than papering over it; where an answer combination looks coherent but unoccupied, say briefly whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or merely because nobody has explored it. I don't want the names of theorists or any citations — label the positions descriptively — and you may assume a reader who knows the standard moves and does not need the obvious objections spelled out.
