Hey, so I'm the student rep on the committee that runs our school's points system, and I have to write the opinion memo we vote on Tuesday, so I need a hand.

The setup: you get Knight Cash for showing up on time, turning in homework, not being a jerk in the halls, etc, and you spend it on a homework pass, cutting the lunch line, a good seat at assembly. Now the admin wants to make it a real economy — savings accounts that pay interest at the end of each term, and letting kids buy and sell points off each other for actual money. There's already a black market, 20 bucks for 500 points.

The whole fight is over what the points actually are. Principal: they're our property, we earned them, so cancelling them is basically theft and saved ones carry over. Vice-principal: they're house money, the school can void them, change the prices, or cancel them after a snow day, because they were never real to begin with. I looked up this old book of questions by George Berkeley from 1735, The Querist, where he asks whether the true idea of money isnt "altogether that of a ticket or counter," and whether gold and silver and paper are just tickets for reckoning and transferring value, and whether labour rather than money is the real source of wealth. If he's right then our points were never property, just claims on school stuff that the school can print more of whenever it wants.

So please write the memo I'll read out. It needs a specific recommendation with a timeline (what we do this term, what happens before the end of the year), and it has to take the other side seriously, because the council is split. And dont just tell me Berkeley's right — tell me what breaks if he is, because if points are just tickets then some kid who saved 5,000 of them for a prom ticket is holding nothing, and I don't know if I can vote for that.
