Give an example of a hard choice between two options that are genuinely incommensurable — no common measure, neither better than the other — in which one of the two is nevertheless the option the agent should take. Build the example around a one-way door: taking one option makes the other unavailable, and the unavailability is not a further value to be weighed but a change in what the agent can henceforth want. Then say what does the normative work if not a ranking — why the example is not merely a case of one option turning out better on reflection, why the choice is not therefore arbitrary, and what distinguishes the incommensurability in your example from mere difficulty. Use an example of your own rather than a stock one, and do not dissolve the tension by introducing a supervalue that commensurates the two options.
