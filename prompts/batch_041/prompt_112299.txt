Me and my friend just spent like three hours arguing about whether disease is a fact about the body or a fact about our values — she's a straight normativist, thinks "disease" is just a label we slap on stuff we don't like, and I was trying to hold some middle ground but I think I accidentally talked myself into my own view and now I want to know if it survives contact with reality.

Here's the view, roughly. A condition counts as a disease when two things are true: (1) some part or subsystem of you isn't doing the job it was selected to do, and (2) your body is actually trying to fix it. Not that it could fix it, not that fixing it would be good for you — just that the corrective machinery is running. If nothing in you is even attempting to correct the state, then on my view it's not a disease, it's a feature: a difference, a trait, a way of being. So fever and inflammation and coughing come out as diseases, because they're the body mid-correction, while being born without a hand doesn't, because nothing in you is trying to grow it back. The normative stuff comes in downstream — what counts as the job a part was "selected to do" gets settled partly by what the organism is trying to do with itself, so it's not pure biology either.

What I want from you is what would count against this. Not the lazy "that's just naturalism with extra steps" version — the actual cases and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be. Tell me what my view predicts that a plain naturalist or a plain normativist wouldn't, so I can see whether it's earning its keep. Be honest about which objections I'd already concede, which ones I'd try to wriggle out of, and whether the wriggling turns the view into something unfalsifiable — and if it does, whether that's really a problem here or just the price of doing business in this area. Also say how confident you are in what your'e claiming and flag the single point you're least sure about. End with a line starting "Verdict:" giving me one of three labels — exposed, partly exposed, or insulated — then a dash and the one thing you think hurts my view most.
