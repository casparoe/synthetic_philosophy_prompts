I'm putting together a small reading/working group next term — me plus four or five people, ten weeks, mostly philosophy and cog-sci types, no real ML infrastructure — to work on analogy-making as conceptual slippage: the thought that in a lot of creative thinking the concept itself gets bent by the situation rather than being a fixed label you just apply. The classic letter-string playground (abc goes to abd, so what does ijk go to?) is the obvious place to start and I dont want us to just redo it. I'd rather have a fresher running example, maybe the way "the same recipe" or "the same song" or "the same move" slides around depending on who's judging it. So: what are the open questions here that actually matter, and which one or two should we sink the ten weeks into? I want the ordering defended rather than a list — what makes a question upstream of the others, what a group like ours could plausibly move on without building a big system, and what would shift something up or down the list. And tell me straight which of the questions people usually raise in this area are dead ends. Ideally we finish with one or two questions sharp enough to hand to someone who does build systems.
