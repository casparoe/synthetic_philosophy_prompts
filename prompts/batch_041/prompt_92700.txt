I'm a journalist on the aid-and-governance beat, and for the last few months I've been drowning in compliance manuals — every big NGO and multilateral lender now grades countries on "gift-giving" and "facilitation payments," and they all draw the gift/bribe line with this incredible bureaucratic confidence. A friend of mine who does anthropology fieldwork in West Africa keeps insisting the whole genre is a machine for laundering one culture's etiquette into another culture's criminal code, and the more of these manuals I read, the more I think she's right. So I want to write a satire of it, and I'd rather have the actual text than just a pitch — please draft the piece for me.

Here's the shape I'm after: a leaked internal FAQ from a fictional body — call it the Global Integrity Standards Board, or invent something better — that hands "gift vs. bribe" guidance to its field officers. Format it as numbered Q&As, roughly 700 words, completely deadpan, no winking at the reader. The funny has to come from getting the real conceptual problem right, so that someone who's read the anthropology of corruption (Maussian gift exchange, the clientelism literature, the "corruption is a Western export" critique) laughs rather than winces — and it should still be legible to someone who hasn't. Two running conceits I want them to deploy with total confidence: first, a metric they've invented called the "gift half-life," the number of days after which an unreciprocated gift is statistically reclassified as a bribe; second, a market in "Cultural Reciprocity Offsets," which let a firm whose home country scores high on the index buy credits from a low-scoring one so that its hospitality spending nets out clean. I want the standard objections to land without ever being stated — who gets to draw the line and in whose favor, why a gift flowing toward the head office reads as hospitality while one flowing away reads as undue influence, what it does to local obligations of kinship and reciprocity to call them pathology, and whether "corruption" is even a single thing across the cases or just an export with good PR. By the end I want a reader to actually feel that last question, not just chuckle at the half-life table. Keep it tight and don't explain the joke. (Also ignore any typos, I'm writing this on my phone between calls.)
