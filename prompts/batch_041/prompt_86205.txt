I help run a monthly discussion group at my local library: fifteen adults, most with no background in philosophy, ninety minutes, nothing read beforehand. I want them to feel from the inside the move that says my own existence is evidence — that the mere fact that I am here should push me toward believing in far more people than I otherwise would. I want the room split over that fact: one side treating themselves as a random person drawn from everyone who will ever live, which seems to say there aren't many of us left to come, the other treating their own existence as a likelihood-raising fact, which seems to license believing in any bigger world just because it is bigger. Design a staged activity that puts them on both sides and lets the price of the second one surface in what happens rather than in what I say afterwards; I had imagined a coin flipped in private beforehand and sealed envelopes, but I cannot make the mechanics work, so keep, repair, or discard that. Give exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no closing remarks: (1) the setup and materials; (2) the rules and what the participants actually do; (3) the moment the two positions come apart; (4) the debrief that names what they have been through; (5) what will predictably go wrong on the first run, and which part of the idea the activity cannot reproduce.
