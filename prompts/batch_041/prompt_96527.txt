I'm in a Discord with a bunch of ex-LessWrong types and two actual neuroscientists, and we've been going at "is the brain a computer?" on and off for about eight months now, to the point where people pre-emptively sigh when it comes up. The camps are pretty stable. Camp A says: obviously it is, and that's why the question feels slippery — any physical system whose states can be mapped onto the states of some formal automaton is running that automaton, so your brain is running God knows how many computations at once, and the interesting question was never "is it a computer" but "do computational descriptions of it do any explanatory work." Camp B says that's cheating: "computing" names a specific kind of causal-mechanical process — something that manipulates representations in a rule-governed, medium-independent way — and brains do that literally, while thermostats and bucket brigades and the solar system don't. Then A says B is smuggling in "representation" and "rule-governed" to do the work, which is the exact thing we wanted explained, and B says A has watered the thesis down until it's unfalsifiable and no longer interesting. Two suggestions from the group that I keep coming back to: one guy wants us to split the claim into three senses of "the brain computes" (a description we can always give, an implementation the system really has, and an explanation that does work) and says the fight is entirely about which sense people are using; another thinks the real fault line is whether you take the discrete, error-correcting character of digital computation to be essential or just an engineering convenience.

What I want from you is a diagnosis of the impasse, not another lap around the track. Is this a verbal dispute that dissolves once we pin down what "is" is doing (constitutive vs. merely descriptive), or are the camps genuinely answering different questions under one heading — one metaphysical, one about what form a good explanation has — with the shouting caused by them sharing a sentence? Is there a hidden formal or empirical question doing the work, like whether there's a non-arbitary criterion of implementation that's neither satisfied trivially by everything nor just a function of our descriptive interests? My own hypothesis, and please push on it rather than just agreeing, is that most of this is the verbal/aims thing and a smaller chunk is a real crux about implementation criteria, and that we'd know which is which if someone could show either that every physical system implements something (making B's narrow notion a choice rather than a discovery) or that implementation can be pinned down by a system's causal structure alone. Given your diagnosis, tell me what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, a theorem, an experiment, or just admitting the question was ill-posed and moving on — and be honest if the answer is that we should stop expecting the arguement to ever end.
