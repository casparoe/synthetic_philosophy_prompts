I'm in a social choice / political theory reading group and we've gotten into a dumb but weirdly productive fight about Gibbard–Satterthwaite. Camp A (call them the impossibility hawks) thinks the theorem is basically the end of the story: any voting rule that's non-dictatorial, unanimous, and has at least three possible outcomes is manipulable, so any institution that claims otherwise is either lying, restricting preferences in a way that's normatively suspect, or smuggling in dictatorship. Camp B (the escape artists) thinks that's a bait-and-switch: the theorem is about ordinal ballots and unrestricted domains, and once you allow cardinal info, randomization, approval-style ballots, restricted domains, or a status quo default, you get rules that are strategy-proof in the senses that actually matter for institutions. They've been talking past each other for a year. I want you to write the joint statement they could both sign after a real adversarial collaboration.
