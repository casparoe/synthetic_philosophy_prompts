So I run a small landscaping and hardscape business -- me plus four or five guys in the summer, I do the quoting, the ordering, and most of the talking to the customer. For the last few months I've been reading AI stuff at night, partly because it keeps me up, and there's this one idea I keep tripping over: that the way to make a really capable system safe is to have it break every big job down into smaller jobs it can actually do, and wherever it can't, a human steps in and does that part, and then you take all of those answers and train a smaller, cheaper copy to imitate the whole process end to end, and you do that again and again until the copy is as good as the original was at the big job. The claim being sold is that this keeps two things intact at once -- that the thing is still pointed at what we actually want (they call that alignment) and that it doesn't get dumber or weaker on the way down (competence).

What I can't get out of my head is that this is basically my job. I take a whole yard -- grading, drainage, plant selection, the wall, the lighting, the planting -- and I cut it into chunks I can hand off to Miguel and Dave, and for the bits nobody on my crew can legally or safely do (the gas line, the permit, the electrical) I either call a licensed sub or I do it myself, and when I hire somebody new I train him by having him shadow whoever's best and do it the same way, then I watch and correct. So here's what I want, and please don't be soft on it. First, restate the AI idea as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary -- jobs, crews, subs, the quote, the punch list, training a new hire off the foreman, the fact that I'm the only one holding the customer's actual wish in my head and it's never written down anywhere. Don't cheat by quietly assuming the crew has no goals of its own, or that "what the customer wants" is some fixed spec I could hand you, because in my world it isn't -- half my job is figuring out what they meant. Second, give me the residue report: what actually carried over cleanly, what you had to bend to make it fit, and what in the original has no counterpart at all in how a crew runs a job. And then tell me straight whether the stuff that doesn't survive is a hole in my way of thinking about it, or whether it's the AI people describing something they haven't realy pinned down and dressing it up as a method. My gut says the whole thing looks tidy on paper because a job site can be drawn as a tree of tasks, but a real crew is a bunch of people with there own ideas about how it should go, and that's exactly the part the idea needs and can't seem to say.
