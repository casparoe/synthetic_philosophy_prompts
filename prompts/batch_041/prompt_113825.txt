I was arguing with my friend Dana about personal identity over the weekend and now I can't stop thinking about it. Dana is a hardcore Parfit-head in some ways and weirdly conservative in others, and I'm trying to figure out whether she's actually consistent. Here's her track record, as best I can reconstruct it from the argument:

She says that if you step into a scanner that reads your whole brain, vaporizes you, and then reassembles an atom-for-atom functional duplicate on Mars with all your memories and character intact, that duplicate IS you -- you survive, and it's not even a tragic downgrade, it's just travel. She says the same about a brain transplant: if your brain is moved into a donor body and your old body is incinerated, you go where the brain goes. She obviously thinks you survive ordinary sleep and even a medically induced coma with full recovery. She says that if over the course of twenty years your neurons are replaced one at a time by functionally identical silicon chips, so that at no point do you feel any break in consciousness, you're still you at the end, even though by then there's not a single original neuron left and you have no direct memory of your twenty-years-ago self. But here's the one that surprised me: she says that if you suffer a massive brain injury that leaves the brain alive and conscious but wipes your memories and completely scrambles your personality into something unrecognizable, the original person is dead -- that new person is a stranger, not you.

Now Dana's about to watch a video in her philosophy of mind seminar that depicts something she hasn't seen before: a "fission" scenario, where your two brain hemispheres are each transplanted into a separate donor body, your old body is destroyed, and both resulting people wake up with all your memories and character. She hasn't given her verdict yet. What do you predict she'll say, and why? I want you to lay out the small number of rules that would fit all the verdicts above, say what each one predicts about the fission case, give me your best guess at which rule she's actually running and your reasons, and then tell me what further case would be the cleanest way to tell her candidate rules apart if the fission case somehow doesn't settle it. Please write it up in American English and don't just give me the textbook Parfit answer -- I care about what follows from HER verdicts, not from the famous one.
