Ok so I just spent most of last night arguing with my mate Dev about death — he's been reading up on this stuff for a seminar and kept poking holes in me — and now I genuinely can't tell if I believe a coherent thing or just a pile of intuitions I've absorbed from different places. I wrote down where I actually land once he'd pushed me, and I want you to tell me whether it hangs together. Here it is:

1. Death is a biological event. It happens in the world whether or not anyone has an opinion about it. It's not a legal fiction and it's not a convention we adopt because it's convenient — a body is either still the kind of thing that's living or it isn't.
2. Brain death is death. If the whole brain has permanently stopped — brainstem included — the person is dead, full stop, even if a ventilator is keeping the heart and lungs going and the body is warm and pink.
3. Someone in a persistent vegetative state is alive. Cortex wiped out but brainstem intact? Not dead. Profoundly disabled, sure, but alive, and I'd fight anyone who said we could treat them as a corpse.
4. "Irreversible" should be read against what's actually possible with the tech we have, not against what's conceivable in principle. So someone whose heart stopped thirty seconds ago isn't dead, because we can get it going again.
5. I'm fine with organ donation after circulatory death. The protocol waits a few minutes after the heart stops and then takes the organs, and I don't lose sleep over that.
6. The dead donor rule is non-negotiable. You only take organs from someone who is already dead. Proposals where you'd kill someone to get their organs, or hurry their death along so the timing works out, strike me as straightforwardly wrong.
7. And the general shape of it: the reason brain death matters is that the brain is what makes you you. Once it's gone there's nobody home, and thats why it counts as death rather than just a very serious injury.
