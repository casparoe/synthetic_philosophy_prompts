I am a philosophy PhD student specializing in normative ethics, and for the past several months I have been locked in a recurring argument with my partner, who is a committed act-consequentialist. We are at the stage of life where we are seriously trying to decide whether to have a child, and she insists on framing the choice in what she takes to be straightforwardly consequentialist terms: roughly, would the world be better off in expectation if we brought this particular person into existence, compared with the alternatives? I have recently been reading about the problem of consequentialist cluelessness, and I have a nagging suspicion that it does not merely complicate her framing but threatens to dissolve the very notion of an "expectation" that could guide the choice — and that it does so in a particularly vicious way once you notice that the person whose welfare is supposed to count in favor of having the child is not the same person who would exist if we waited a few years, moved to another city, or chose not to have a child at all.

Please act as a philosopher who works on consequentialism, decision theory under uncertainty, and population ethics, and walk me through the most important implications of cluelessness — including how it interacts with the non-identity dimension I just described — for how a consequentialist should actually approach a decision like ours. I would like you to begin by brainstorming the distinct strands of the cluelessness problem before committing to any verdict, and to be scrupulous about separating what is a purely epistemic difficulty (we cannot know the downstream consequences) from what is an ontological or conceptual one (the relevant value may be undefined, non-comparable, or hostage to which future people come to exist). Please resist the lazy conclusion that cluelessness simply shows consequentialism to be useless; instead, give me the strongest version of the consequentialist position that can survive the problem, spell out what it would concretely imply for our choice, and flag honestly which of those implications are genuinely entailed by the argument and which are contestable.
