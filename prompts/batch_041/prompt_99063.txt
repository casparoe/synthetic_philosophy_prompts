So we keep getting told two rules. Rule 1: don't be presentist — don't judge people in the past by today's values, try to understand them on their own terms. Rule 2: but don't be a moral coward either, so "well, slavery was just normal back then" is an excuse, not history.

But the thing I actually keep noticing is a third move that hides underneath both of them. When a historian decides to just *understand* instead of judge, they still have to choose what to describe. Nobody writes a chapter on "how did they justify hitting their kids" or "what did peasants think about the smell of things" — the stuff we pick to explain, the stuff we treat as a cause, the stuff we treat as weird or as needing a reason, all of that gets chosen using what *we* find interesting or in need of explaining. So the anti-judging stance seems to smuggle today's values in through the back door — not through our verdicts on them, but through what we even bother to look at and how we frame it.

Two little examples: (a) a historian who says "don't call him a monster, understand his worldview" but only bothers to reconstruct the worldview of people we might still admire; (b) a textbook that treats "why did they think X was ok?" as the interesting question for some past belief but not for others, and never asks why the difference.
