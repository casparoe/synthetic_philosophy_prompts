Me and two friends have a long-running argument about anthropic reasoning — self-locating belief, the Doomsday argument, Sleeping Beauty, the presumptuous philosopher, whether "self-sampling" (reason as if you're a random observer drawn from some reference class) or "self-indication" (a hypothesis on which there are more observers makes it more likely you're one of them) is the right frame. We've been going in circles for months, mostly because we can't agree on what we're even asking a theory here to do. So before we crack another paper, I want the spec written out. What does an adequate theory of self-locating credence have to deliver? The cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse, the constraints it has to respect — with the case or intuition behind each requirement, not just the label. Then tell me which of those requirements are non-negotiable and which are really just a commitment of one camp, and — thats the part I care about most — where you think the list is jointly unsatisfiable: which pairs of desiderata can't both be met, and what that forces you to give up. Don't bother scoring the named positions against the list, I'll do that myself in the group.

To keep it concrete, put these on the list: (i) the Doomsday setup, where your birth rank is supposed to tell you something about how many humans there'll ever be; (ii) the presumptuous philosopher, where a theory saying there are vastly more observers than its rival gets an absurd boost from no evidence at all; (iii) Sleeping Beauty, where you wake up not knowing which day it is; and then two we made up, which I haven't seen anywhere. Case (iv): you're told the total number of observers who will ever exist equals the number of heads in a sequence of fair coin flips that a stranger will run in the far future, long after you're dead — should the fact that you exist at all give you evidence about those flips? Case (v): tomorrow you'll get to decide whether to make a thousand duplicates of yourself, all of whom remember this exact conversation and get asked this exact question; does the fact that you'll probably choose to duplicate change what you should believe today about some completely unrelated matter, like whether a coin someone already flipped came up heads?

And one more axis I want in the spec, because we keep tripping over it: is this a theory of what to believe, or of what to do? Should it tell you how to bet when the stakes depend on how many copies of you exist, and do the credences it hands you today have to be consistent with the decisions you know you'll make later? If some of the desiderata above pull against each other on that question, say which ones and why.
