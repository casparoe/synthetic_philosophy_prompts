I host a small podcast about philosophy of mind — the kind with maybe four hundred listeners, three of whom email me regularly and two of whom are related to me — and next week's guest is a philosopher of cognitive science whose book-in-progress argues for a view he likes to sum up as 'only the marbles do any pushing'. The setup he's building the book around is Hofstadter's old careenium thought experiment, in case you don't know it: a giant billiard-table world full of tiny magnetised balls (Hofstadter calls them simmballs, though I've also seen them called simms) that careen about and clump into larger, fairly stable higher-level patterns, and those clumps seem to stand for things outside the table — the walls of the careenium get more or less bouncy depending on what's going on out there, so the clumps end up tracking features of the world, and the clumps then bump into other clumps in ways that mirror reasoning. Hofstadter's question is who shoves whom around in there, the little balls or the big patterns, and his answer is that both descriptions are legitimate and neither is more fundamental. My guest disagrees: he thinks the only real pushing happens at the level of the individual simmballs, and that all our talk about symbols shoving each other is a handy way of talking that adds no causal oomph of its own — the pattern talk is fine as description, but if you want to know why anything happened, you go down to the marbles. He's a physicalist, he isn't a nihilist about the higher level, he just denies that it does any work.

I want eight to ten questions that make him earn the view rather than restate it, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. Please make sure you include at least: one that forces him to say what he means by 'pushing' and whether he's really denying that symbols make a difference or only denying that they shove in some more intimate, productive sense; one asking whether his position is any more stable than the usual exclusion worry, since if the simmball level is already sufficient for whatever happens, and by that logic the marbles get excluded by whatever the marbles are made of, I want to know why the regress stops exactly where he wants it to; one about the fact that you seemingly can't predict the careenium's clump-level behaviour from marble-level facts without using clump-level vocabulary, and wether that makes the symbols more than decorative; one about whether the whole thought experiment quietly cheats by smuggling cleverness into the simmballs, given that Hofstadter insists they have to be dumb; and one about what this does to the 'I' — if symbols never push, the self is a puff of smoke, and I want to know whether he'll bite that bullet or wriggle. No softballs about how he got into philosophy and no asking him to walk through his book chapter by chapter, that's a bit of a nusiance to listen to.

Keep the whole thing under 800 words, in British English, since I'll be reading it aloud. And because I'll look like an idiot on air if you're bluffing: say how confident you are in each substantive claim you make about the view and the surrounding debate, and flag the one point you're least sure about.
