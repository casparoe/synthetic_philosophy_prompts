I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of two decades, and I have watched the sentence "information is physical" get used to settle an unusually wide range of arguments. It came up again last week in a thread about whether a sufficiently powerful predictor could be built as a physical device: someone wrote that information is physical, that erasure therefore costs at least kT ln 2, that this is why Maxwell's demon cannot win, and that the question was closed. I have seen the same sentence do at least three other jobs — to deny any principled gap between a computation and a physical process, to claim that Shannon entropy and thermodynamic entropy are ultimately one quantity, and to claim that physics is at bottom about information rather than matter.

What I want is a menu rather than a verdict. Separate out the distinct precise theses the slogan could be expressing, from the ones that are close to tautological (any information must be borne by some physical degrees of freedom) through the ones that are substantive and defensible (a system's thermodynamic entropy is the entropy of the description someone has of it; only a logically irreversible operation must dissipate heat) to the ones that are contentious or flatly wrong (that the two entropies are interchangeable, that "erasure" is always a well-defined operation, that measurement is thermodynamically free in every sense the older arguments needed). Give each reading a one-line verdict — platitude, live claim, or error — and then say which of them would actually license the inference in the argument above.

Two side questions, because I have seen both asserted confidently and cannot tell how much weight they bear. First, the standard exorcism of the demon seems to rest on the demon having to erase its memory; but I have also seen it said that the principle which prices that erasure is itself derived from the second law, which would make the exorcism circular. Tell me what is really doing the work there and whether the circularity charge lands. Second, the older story that the demon is defeated by the cost of measurement appears to have been abandoned in favour of the cost of erasure; say what changed and whether the replacement is as clean as it is usually presented.
