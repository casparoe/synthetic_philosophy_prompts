My sister joined this faith-healing church last year and keeps texting me about people getting cured, so I finally read Hume's essay on miracles to have something to say back. Now I'm wierdly into it. There's a guy in my book club, Marcus, who's a retired philosophy prof and a huge Hume nerd. He's been giving verdicts on miracle claims for years and I want to predict what he'll say about a new one.

Here's Marcus's track record, as best I can reconstruct it:
- TV faith healer makes a guy throw away his crutches and walk. Only the healer's followers testify, no medical records. Marcus: not a miracle.
- Statue of Mary weeps in a church. Chemists later find it's condensation from a leaky pipe. Marcus: not a miracle.
- A blind woman reportedly sees again after touching a relic. Two skeptical ophthalmologists who examined her before and after confirm she was blind and now isn't. No natural explanation. Marcus: miracle.
- A Hindu statue drinks milk, and the same week a Catholic statue weeps, both with huge crowds and lots of testimony. Marcus: neither is a miracle, they cancel each other out.
- A big double-blind prayer study finds a small but statistically significant improvement in the prayed-for group. Marcus: not a miracle, just some natural effect we don't understand yet.

Now the new case. A team of atheist researchers pre-register a study. They take 100 patients with terminal pancreatic cancer. Half get prayed for by a cloistered Buddhist order on the other side of the world, half don't. Nobody knows who's in which group. The prayed-for group has a 30% complete remission rate; the control group has 0%. The study gets replicated twice. The researchers hate the result and have no mechanism. It's a huge statistical anomaly, but it's not a violation of any law of nature — it's just that the numbers are insane.

What is Marcus going to say? I care less about yes/no than about which rule he's actually running, because I think his earlier verdicts fit more than one. Give me a JSON object with these fields:
- predicted_verdict: 'miracle', 'not a miracle', or 'can't tell'
- candidate_rules: array of objects, each with 'rule_name', 'description', and 'prediction_for_new_case'
- best_guess_rule: the rule you think he's actually using
- best_guess_verdict: what that rule predicts for the new case
- why: short explanation
- confidence_0_to_100: number
- closest_earlier_case: which of the five is the closest analog
- further_case_that_would_settle_it: a new case that would tell the candidate rules apart
- what_would_change_his_mind: what evidence or argument would flip his verdict

Make it in the weeds. I'm definately not asking for a reading list or citations.
