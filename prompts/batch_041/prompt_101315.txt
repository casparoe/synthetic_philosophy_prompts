So: write me the desiderata. What does any theory of societal collapse have to actually deliver to be worth taking seriously?

Give me the cases it has to get right, and say what "right" amounts to in each -- the eastern Med at the end of the Bronze Age, the Maya terminal classic, Easter Island -- what's the thing about each that a theory can't just wave away. Then the distinctions it can't be allowed to flatten: failure vs. transformation vs. slow decline vs. just getting conquered; the polity vs. the population vs. the subsistence base; a real discontinuity vs. an artifact of how we date the stuff. And the background results it can't contradict -- that Maya people are still here, that abandoning cities isn't the same as the death of a culture, that some of the famous "crashes" turn out gentler or weirder than the popular story.

Then tell me which of these are genuine requirements and which are just prejudices of one camp -- the systems-theory people, the resilience/adaptive-cycle crowd, the climate-determinists, whoever -- and flag any pair you think can't be satisfied together. That's the part I actually want. And say which single requirement does the most to rule out candidates; I have a suspician it's something about the observer's standpoint, whether collapse is a description of a process or a verdict we pass, but convince me either way.
