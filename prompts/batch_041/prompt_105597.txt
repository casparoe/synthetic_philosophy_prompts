Retired chemist here — spent thirty years on kinetics and statistical mechanics, now reading philosophy with more enthusiasm than skill, so go easy on me but dont dumb it down.

Theres a shape of problem I keep running into and I cant find the right word for it. Set-up: you build a machine out of a huge number of dumb moving parts. Picture a frictionless tray with a million little magnetized beads skittering around; now and then beads stick together into bigger clumps, and the clumps drift and collide as clumps. Suppose some of those clumps are stable enough, and track the outside world reliably enough, that it's fair to say a particular clump 'represents' something — say a certain swirling clump reliably shows up right before the tray gets tilted, so we say it represents the tilt. Fine so far. The clump is a pattern in the beads, everyone agrees.

The bit I cant get past: does the clump ever push a bead? Because I can write the trajectory of every single bead using only other beads — each collision fully fixed by earlier collisions — and there never seems to be anything left over for the clump to do. It looks like the swirl is steering, but every bead would have gone exactly where it went even if the clump had stood for something else, or for nothing at all. And it's not just the clump-as-shape that looks idle; it's the fact that it means something that looks idle, if you see the difference.

So: is there an established name for this exact knot — specifically the version where what's in question is whether a pattern's *meaning* can do causal work when the substrate is already fully booked? I've heard downward causation, and I've heard epiphenomenalism, and I've seen the exclusion argument waved around, but downward causation as I understand it is about higher-level physical quantities (temperature, pressure), and I honestly cant tell whether it reaches the meaning part; and epiphenomenalism always seems to be about felt experience, which isnt what's bugging me. I also have a vague half-memory of a toy model from a popular science book where a mind was made of bouncing balls and the balls had a cute name, but I cant place it and I dont want to lean on a fuzzy memory.

What I want isnt a label, it's the carving: what exactly is this puzzle, which of the nearby named things is it nearest to, how do those lookalikes differ from it, and where the edges are — does the verdict change if the clump is merely correlated with the tilt rather than caused by it, or if it only sometimes tracks it? And if there's no established name, coin one and define it sharply enough that I can tell it apart from plain downward causation and from epiphenomenalism about experience.

Please lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence — straight off, does it have a name and what's the nearest concept — then give the reasoning. No preamble and dont restate my question back at me.
