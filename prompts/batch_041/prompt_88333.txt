Write the text of an official circular, roughly 750 words, issued by an invented standing body — the International Committee on the Foundations of Arithmetic — some weeks after an unrefereed preprint began circulating that claims to derive a contradiction in first-order Peano Arithmetic from a single unglamorous lemma about the interaction of exponentiation with induction. The piece should take the form of a real institutional notice: a scope clause, numbered sections, a paragraph headed What This Notice Does Not Claim, an appendix of procedural deadlines, and a closing line about the next scheduled review. Every institutional particular — the committee's membership, its document number, its date, its correspondence address — is to be invented. The circular must not cite or quote any real text, author, journal, or result, and it must not depend on the reader's recognizing one.

The comedy is bureaucratic composure in the presence of a genuine logical catastrophe, and it has to be earned from the foundations rather than from mathematics in general: a logician should wince at nothing and laugh in the right places. At a minimum the notice should engage, in the Committee's evasive register, with each of the following.

- Why a contradiction in a classical theory is not one false theorem among many but the collapse of the theory's capacity to discriminate at all, and why the Committee nonetheless keeps insisting that the finding concerns the axiomatization rather than arithmetic.
- The difference between a theory's being inconsistent and its being unsound, and the Committee's careful choice of which of the two to be alarmed about.
- The fact that a proof of inconsistency is itself a piece of mathematics, and is therefore a hostage to whatever theory it was carried out in — including, awkwardly, the theory under indictment.
- The precedent of the last foundational collapse, which the Committee refers to only obliquely and by euphemism, and what was in fact salvaged from it and what was quietly abandoned.
- The option of retaining the offending theory while changing the logic it is written in, and the price of that option, stated as a matter of budget and staffing rather than of truth.
- The question the Committee most hopes nobody puts to it.

Include, as an appendix or footnote, a short dissenting note from one committee member; it should be the sharpest thing in the document and the one place where the evasion is named rather than managed. Do not let the irony become visible: no winking asides, no narrator stepping outside the document, no joke that requires the reader to have been told it was a joke. The piece should be legible to someone whose only exposure to logic was a first course, while giving a specialist the impression that its author knows exactly what is being evaded. The target is the institutional reflex to quarantine a foundational alarm — to declare the working parts of the subject untouched and to defer the question indefinitely — and by the last line a reader should feel the pull of that reflex as well as its cost.
