I am a high-school student, and I have been asked to run a forty-minute session of our school's philosophy club on conceptual slippage in the letter-string microdomain — the analogy puzzles of the kind the program Copycat was built to solve, where abc goes to abd, so what does ijk go to, and then the string mrrjjj, in which the j's can be read either as three separate letters or as a single run, and the answer that requires a slip changes the run rather than one letter. Please plan the session rather than explain the idea to me. Tell me what has to come first for the very notion of a slip to be available, and what depends on what; which single example should carry the whole session and what it must have and lack, and why mrrjjj rather than a natural-language analogy, if it is the right carrier; which misconceptions to expect — I am guessing they will treat a slip as a mistake and assume the puzzle has one correct answer, so name the others — and exactly where in the sequence to head each of them off; one question to ask at the end to see whether they have grasped that the analogy is made rather than found, plus a fresh three-item analogy of my own devising that would show whether they can make a slip themselves; and what I should openly refuse to explain at this level, saying that I am refusing it.
