So I'm in this reading group on social ontology that's mostly data and consumer-research people, and the argument we keep having is about generations. My colleague says things like Gen Z won't sit through a two-hour meeting, like that's a fact about a kind of person, and I say the label is a birth year with a marketing budget, and honestly neither of us can say what we're actually disagreeing about. So I want a decision tree I can hand out that takes someone from the most basic question about what kind of kind a generation is supposed to be, all the way down to a named position. Please write that tree.

Ground rules for it. Every branch node has to be a question a person can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, so no node that's just do you think generations are real, and don't build it as a list of names with definitions hanging off them. Build the logical space first out of the questions, then tell me which combinations are actually occupied. The branch questions I care about, roughly: is the label supposed to license inferences about a randomly picked member, or is it just a bookkeeping device for sorting a dataset; is there some mechanism that keeps the members similar to each other (a shared media environment, institutions that only that cohort went through, one formative event), or is the clustering you observe just age plus period effects wearing a costume; are the boundaries discovered or drawn, given that any birth-year cutoff is one of infinitely many you could have picked; is membership something about you or something about where you sit in a historical sequence; and does the label do causal work because people believe it and act on it, or is it inert description. Include the age-period-cohort thing somewhere, because cohort is literally period minus age, so you can't pull the three apart without extra assumptions, and I want the tree to be honest about that instead of pretending the empirical question is settled.

The leaves I need it to reach, at minimum: the Mannheim-style view where a generation is a social location and only sometimes gets knit into a generation in actuality, with generational units inside it; generation as a historical kind, where what makes it one kind is a shared causal origin or formative event rather than any intrinsic essence; generation as a real but not natural kind, held together by a mechanism that keeps a cluster of properties co-occurring; cohort as a pure statistical construct, a useful fiction you keep because it's cheap to use; the harder eliminativist who says there are only age and period effects and the whole vocabulary should go; the performative view where the label makes the kind, so the category is real precisely because people self-identify into it; and some kind of pluralism where the answer depends on which mechanism you're asking about, so generations are real for some purposes and not others. There's probably also a generation-as-natural-kind-with-an-essence leaf that nobody defends, so put it in the tree if the logic puts it there and then say why it's empty.

For each leaf, one sentence on the most awkward commitment it has to swallow. And if two leaves can't be told apart by any of your branch questions, say so rather than papering over it, I'd rather have the tree admit it can't separate them. Same for combinations nobody occupies, tell me whether that's because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Case material to hang it on if you need it: Millennials are killing the napkin industry, ok boomer, someone born in 1995 in Ohio vs someone born the same year in Lagos, and the difference between me feeling like a Millennial and my birth year saying I am one. Aim for eight to twelve branch nodes and however many leaves fall out. I don't need citations or a reading list, just the tree and the notes.
