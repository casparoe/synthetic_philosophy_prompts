I'm setting the final for an upper-level metaphysics of physics seminar (grad students, one semester of GR under their belt). Last year's hole-argument question was a disaster — everyone gave me the same recitation: diffeomorphism, hole, indeterminism, sophisticated substantivalism, done. Nobody could say which step is actually doing the work, or why it's supposed to be a problem specifically for the substantivalist.

So heres the setup I want the question built around. A colleague insists the hole argument proves nothing special about spacetime: it's just the generic fact that any theory with a gauge symmetry has gauge-equivalent models, and the fix is the usual one — say the equivalent models represent the same physical situation, like we do for the vector potential in electromagnetism. On that view the hole argument shows only that GR is a gauge theory, full stop.

Make them adjudicate that. But don't let them stop at "sophisticated substantivalism saves the day" — I want them to say exactly what premise of the argument the colleague has to already accept for the reduction to go through, whether there's anything about the spacetime case that distinguishes it from the EM case, and what a committed believer in the primitive individuality of spacetime points (someone who won't go structuralist about them) is forced to give up. Also make them say whether the argument needs GR's dynamics or would go through for a scalar field on a fixed background — that one usually sorts them out.

Give me the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme. For the scheme: a numbered list of the specific moves a top answer must contain, the marks each carries (out of 20), and a short list of the confusions that should cap someone at a B — things like treating the hole as a physically special region, or confusing diffeomorphism invariance of the equations with the argument about models.
