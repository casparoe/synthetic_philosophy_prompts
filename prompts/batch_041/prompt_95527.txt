Ok so this is a bit of an odd one but bear with me. I'm working on a piece about von Neumann's late thinking on the brain — the stuff in that little book he was dictating right at the end (The Computer and the Brain, published after he died), where he treats the brain as a computing machine but keeps hammering on how unlike one of our machines it is: the components are wildly unreliable, the "language" doesn't look anything like the logic of a digital computer, everything is slow and low-precision but massively parallel and statistically robust, and so on. The whole gimmick of the piece is: what would he make of us now?

So imagine we could wake him up and give him maybe two weeks to catch up on the relevant stuff. Not a reading list, just the gist of a few developments, and then we sit him down and ask him to react. Here's what I want you to work through, one at a time.

(1) The neuron. He seems to have had in mind something like a fairly simple threshold-y unit as the elementary component. But there's a body of work suggesting that a single cortical neuron, all by itself, is doing roughly a small deep network's worth of computation — several layers of nonlinearity crammed into the dendrites alone. So the basic building block turns out to be a pretty deep computer in its own right.

(2) Connectomics and whole-brain emulation. People now seriously argue that if you had the complete wiring diagram plus good biophysical models of each cell type, you could simulate a brain and get a mind out the other end. The skeptics say the real action is below the wiring diagram: neuromodulators, glia, gap junctions, ephaptic coupling, all the ugly analog biophysics a connectome just doesn't capture.

(3) Modern deep learning. This is probly the thing he'd find strangest. It works by being unreliable in a completely different way than the one he worried about — stochastic training, gigantic redundancy, no explicit error-correction scheme, and it just... scales.

For each of the three, I want two things: what would he actually say, and — more important — where would he say "this is basicly what I meant" versus "ok, I was wrong about this"? Give me his genuine likely reactions, not him being retroactively right about everything. Then at the end, tell me which of the three he'd have found most alien, and whether there's some concern he had about the brain that we've kind of quietly stopped caring about.
