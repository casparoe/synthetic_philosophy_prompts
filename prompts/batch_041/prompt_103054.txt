I am a professor of philosophy and I host a small departmental interview series. Next month's guest is a political philosopher who has argued, across several papers, that the discursive dilemma is not a genuine dilemma at all: she holds that it dissolves once we abandon the independence condition on judgment aggregation, and that nothing of value — neither the group's responsiveness to reasons nor its equal treatment of its members — is lost in doing so. She is also committed to the stronger claim that groups can be genuine reasoners rather than mere arenas in which individual judgments are tallied. I will be interviewing her for about an hour, and I would like you to draft nine or ten interview questions that get past the summary she has delivered many times.

For each question, please supply a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and where a question admits two or three standard replies, give a follow-up for each. I want the set to include, at minimum:

1. A question that forces her to say precisely what she replaces the independence condition with, and why the replacement does not simply let some members' judgments count for more than others'.
2. A question about the difference between the group's reaching a verdict on the conclusion and its reaching a verdict on the premises — in particular, what is lost when a majority is right about every premise yet outvoted on the conclusion, and whether she thinks anything is lost at all.
3. A question about how her position relates to Arrow's theorem: whether the dilemma is a new impossibility or a restatement of an old one, with a follow-up for each answer.
4. A question about who sets the agenda, given that the propositions the group reasons from are typically fixed before anyone votes, and whether agenda-setting power reintroduces the very problem she says is dissolved.

I also want to press her on a matter I have not seen her address, and I would like the questions to open it up: whether the dilemma reappears one level up. Suppose a group must first decide which procedure to use — premise-based or conclusion-based — and that decision is itself a collective judgment about a proposition. Ask her whether the original problem now recurs at this meta-level; if she says the regress bottoms out, ask where, and if she says it does not bottom out, ask why an infinite ascent of procedure-choices is not fatal to her claim that the dilemma dissolves.

Beyond these, please include questions on whether the group's output is better described as a belief or as an acceptance, and whether that distinction does any real work; on whether she takes pre-vote deliberation to make the paradox empirically rare and, if so, whether that is a principled solution or merely a hope; and on whether her procedure gives members an incentive to misreport their judgments once they can anticipate how the votes will aggregate. Close with a question about what argument or evidence would lead her to give up the claim that the dilemma dissolves. Please avoid any question about how she came to the topic.
