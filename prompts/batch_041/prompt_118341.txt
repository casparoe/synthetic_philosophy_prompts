I'm putting together a set of prompts on intention, willpower and self-control, and I want to build one around Kavka's toxin puzzle but from an angle people dont usually take.

Here's the thing I keep noticing. In addiction treatment and self-help you get three practices sitting side by side that look like they assume completely different things about how intentions work.

First, the voucher programs — contingency management I think its called. You come in, pee in a cup, and if the test is clean you get money or vouchers, on the spot, every time. Nothing about wanting it, believing in yourself, or being committed. You get paid for a verified result, week by week.

Second, the classic commitment device. You put $500 in escrow with a friend and lose it if you relapse, or you sign up for one of those apps that takes your credit card. The whole point is to make the future slip expensive, so that your future self, who won't want to quit, goes along with the plan anyway.

Third, the twelve-step line: don't promise yourself you'll never drink again, that vow is part of the problem, just don't drink today, take it a day at a time. And yet the same rooms hand out chips for 30 days, 90 days, a year, and everyone talks about it as a permanent identity change.

So what I want is this: what does each of these three practices presuppose about intention and the will? Where does each one only make sense under some specific view — that intentions are or aren't directly formable at will, that the reason for which you form an intention has to also be a reason to act, that long-range intentions are self-defeating, that you can act on a plan without ever intending the long-term end? Does Kavka's puzzle — the one where you get a million dollars for intending to drink the toxin, knowing that by tomorrow you'll have no reason to drink it — actually rule out the second and third practices, or is the puzzle about something narrower than people assume? Are the three practices in tension with each other, or could one theory of intention license all three? And where is the whole thing underdetermined — which theory would cover all three, and what would have to change in the practices if you swapped it for the rival view?

Also be explicit about which bits each practice strictly requires versus which bits it would merely fit nicely with.
