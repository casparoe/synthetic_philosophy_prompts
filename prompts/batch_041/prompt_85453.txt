Write a short story about a clerk in a made-up city office that classifys vehicles for congestion pricing. The office doesnt use definitions—they use a kind of conceptual neighborhood system where each clerk has a bunch of knobs (wheeled, motorized, sidewalk-legal, passenger-capacity, etc.) and they sort new things by analogy to old cases. One day someone brings in a self-balancing unicycle with a detachable lawnmower deck that can carry two people and is sometimes ridden on sidewalks. The clerk has to decide what it is. As they twiddle the knobs, the categories start to slip—'bike' starts stretching to include motors, 'pedestrian' starts to include things that carry people, and so on. Show the slippage happening rather than just saying it happened. Don't mention Hofstadter or Copycat by name. After the story, add a short note on where you think the story most likely gets the theory of concepts wrong, and what would change your mind about whether it's a good illustration of conceptual slippage.
