I retired from a chemistry lab a few years back, and having run out of things to be annoyed about in my own field, i've been reading alot of philosophy — mostly the stuff about paternalism and about how institutions steer people without actually forbidding anything. But there's a pattern I keep running into in ordinary life, and I can't tell whether it has a name or whether I'm just noticing an ordinary annoyance and dressing it up in fancy clothes.

Here's the shape of it. Some institution sets a default — the expensive fund is where you land unless you say otherwise, the data-sharing scheme is opt-out, the committee work is presumed unless you get yourself excused — and then, when somebody objects, the defense is always the same: nobody is being stopped from choosing differently, the form is right there on the website, it's one phone call. And that's true. What I keep chewing on is that the cost of choosing differently isn't a fact of nature, it's designed. The same people who profit from my staying put get to decide how long the form is, whether it has to be printed and posted, whether the phone line is open at 2pm on a Tuesday, and — this is the part that gets me — they can change all of that next quarter without telling me. So the option is real, in the sense that if I jumped through the hoops I'd be out. But the price of the option is set unilaterally by the party that profits from my not paying it.

Is there a name for that specific configuration — not the hassle itself, but hassle whose size and shape is owned by the interested party? I've run across talk of nudging and I don't think that's quite it, since a nudge is usually supposed to be a fixed piece of architecture you can see through, and what bothers me is the moving part. It doesn't feel like coercion either, since I could leave; and it isn't quite lying to me, since everything is disclosed. I raised it at a little reading group I go to and got four confident and completely different answers, which usually means the thing hasn't been carved up properly yet. So what I want is the carving, not the label: what's the nearest established idea, exactly where does my thing differ from it, and where are the edges — does it stop being this if the exit cost is set by a third party instead of the beneficiary, or if the cost is high but frozen, or if I never really wanted to leave in the first place?
