I teach a course that sets Western and Chinese responses to dream skepticism against each other, and I want to use Christopher Nolan's Inception as the set piece for the Chinese side. The film is thoroughly Cartesian: it hands Cobb a totem, an external token whose behavior is meant to settle whether he is awake, and it stages the familiar worry that the world I take as real may be someone else's construction. Read it against Zhuangzi's butterfly dream rather than against Descartes.

The butterfly passage is usually taught as ancient dream skepticism, but it sits badly in that role. It closes not by dissolving the difference between Zhou and the butterfly but by insisting that a distinction remains, and it calls this the transformation of things. In the same book the author asks how anyone can know that what we call waking is not itself a dream, and looks ahead to a great awakening after which our present certainty would appear as one more dream. That is not a doubt to be discharged by a test; it is a change of state, and it is cheerful where Descartes is anxious.

So is Inception a dream-skepticism film that has wandered into Zhuangzian territory, or a Zhuangzian fable dressed in Cartesian clothes? I want a reading that takes both the film and the text seriously. Which scenes carry the weight -- the totem, whose behavior reverses between worlds (the top topples in reality and spins in the dream, so the film's mark of the real is a thing that falls, while Zhuangzi's emblem of the dream is a thing that flies); Mal's conviction that the waking world is the dream; the final wobble we are never shown resolving? Where does the film's craving for a guarantee look like exactly the attachment Zhuangzi wants loosened, and where does Cobb's guilt and his letting-go dramatize transformation better than any argument could? Then say honestly where the film resists the reading: Zhuangzi has no villain, no trauma to work through, and no stake in which world is real, and Nolan plainly has all three.

Close by saying what, if anything, the film adds to the dream-skepticism debate that the butterfly story and Descartes between them leave out.
