Not a nerd joke, an actual joke. Here's my understanding, from a guy with no stats background, so tell me if I'm off:

Say you've got a score that predicts something about people -- who'll reoffend, who'll pay back a loan, whatever. Three things you'd like it to do:

- when the score says 70%, it should really be 70% of the people it says that about, and that should hold inside each group seperately, not just averaged over everybody
- of the people who actually go on to do the thing, the same share should get flagged in each group
- of the people who don't do the thing, the same share should get flagged in each group

And the punchline of the theorem is you can't have all three unless one of two weird things happens: the score is basically never wrong, or the two groups already have the same rate of the thing you're predicting. That second one is the one that bites, because if the groups have different base rates, then a score that's calibrated and flags the same share of the innocent in each group is forced to flag a different share of the guilty. So the only way to be fully fair on all three counts at once is to fix the world first, or to be clairvoyant.
