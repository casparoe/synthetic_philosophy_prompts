here's the practice i want you to take apart: a manual lists criteria, a trained person checks whether you meet them, and if you do you get the label — and the label is supposed to be a description of something that was already there. you didn't get the condition from the manual, you had it and the manual just named it. that's what "meeting criteria" means, right, that the thing was sitting there before anyone wrote the criteria down.

but the same clinical world does a bunch of other stuff that seems to assume the opposite. it tells patients you know your own mind best, it runs peer support groups where the diagnosis turns into an identity and a community, it does psychoeducation that explains what your condition will do to you over the rest of your life, and every clinician i've ever talked to will privately admit that a kid who gets labeled early starts acting more like the label. so on one side the label is a mirror showing you what was always there, on the other it's closer to a script people pick up and then perform, and those can't both be the basic picture.
