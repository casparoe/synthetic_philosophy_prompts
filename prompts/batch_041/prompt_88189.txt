Me and my friend Dan just spent two hours on the phone arguing about moral uncertainty and I need either a referee or a diagnosis of what we were actually disagreeing about.

Setup. I'm 60/40 between two theories. Theory A is a big-stakes consequentialism where what's on the line is enormous and the gaps between my options are huge. Theory B is a duty-ish thing where the gap between my options is small and bounded and nothing cosmic hangs on it. The standard move is: weight each theory's values by my credence, add, pick the option with the highest expected choiceworthiness.

Dan's objection: those weightings are arbitrary. You can rescale either theory's value scale by any positive factor you like and the recommendation flips, and nothing inside either theory says how much is at stake in it relative to the other one. So the only thing you're allowed to act on is whatever survives every rescaling — and when you work it out, the options that survive are the ones that both theories already rank weakly best, i.e. the option has to be at least as good as every alternative under A and under B. Which almost never happens. So moral uncertainty either tells you nothing, or it tells you something only in cases where you didn't need it.

My reply was that his invariance test is the thing that's wrong, not the framework — when I'm torn, I do have a grip on how much is at stake in each theory, and that grip is part of the theory, not a number I bolt on afterwards. He says I'm dressing up a gut feeling as a scale. Then we go around again. A third friend suggested treating the scale as ambiguity and maximin-ing over it, but that looks to me like arbitrarily picking the worst rescaling, same disease.

What I want: is this a real mathematical disagreement with a right answer, a disagreement about what a decision theory is even for (representing what I think vs. telling me what to do), a verbal fight about whether being torn between theories is "uncertainty" in the same sense as being unsure which urn I'm drawing from, or is it the metaethical question about whether value is comparable across theories in disguise? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a result showing which comparisons the framework actually needs and which it can do without, an argument that the right rule only needs ordinal information, or just conceding the question is ill-posed? My hunch is we're not really disagreeing about the math, but I don't trust my hunch. Please no reading list, I don't need to know who said what, I want the shape of the disagreement.
