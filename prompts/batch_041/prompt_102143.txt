Futarchy is now on the agenda at my city council: rather than voting directly on a congestion-charge scheme, the members would pre-commit to a target — a specified reduction in average peak-hour journey times — and then let conditional prediction markets decide, adopting the scheme only if the market's price for hitting the target under the scheme exceeds its price under the status quo. I want to report on what this would actually amount to rather than on the slogan. Please set out the most important implications of governing this way, including the ones advocates tend to pass over: what follows from the fact that each market must be conditioned on a decision that the market is itself helping to make, and how much of the substantive dispute simply migrates into the prior choice of the target metric and of the rules for measuring and resolving it.
