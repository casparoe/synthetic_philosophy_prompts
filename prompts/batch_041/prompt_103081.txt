Assume the role of a historian of Chinese philosophy who works on Warring States Confucianism and also follows contemporary bioethics. I would like you to think carefully about a counterfactual: how would Mencius and Xunzi each have responded to a debate that only arose long after their deaths? Reason from their own accounts of human nature and moral cultivation, not from anything they could not have anticipated.

Take the following modern development as given. A growing number of contemporary thinkers argue that new technologies — gene editing, drugs that modulate empathy and aggression, and non-invasive brain stimulation — allow us to alter people's moral dispositions directly, and that we may have good reason to do so, whether by individual choice or as public policy. Running alongside this biomedical route is a quieter second one: institutions increasingly shape conduct through engineered habit-formation, using points, streaks, prompts and small rewards to train people into prosocial routines without appealing to their judgement at all.

Now suppose a present-day state adopts a two-part programme. First, it offers every adult a free, optional course of empathy-enhancing medication. Second, it makes participation in a compulsory curriculum of civic ritual — ceremonies, communal service, and prescribed forms of address — a condition of holding public office or practising a licensed profession. Imagine that Mencius and Xunzi could each be shown this proposal and asked for their counsel.

For each thinker, predict what they would say about (i) the medication, (ii) the compulsory ritual curriculum, and (iii) the engineered habit-formation that underlies both, explaining the reasoning that leads you to your prediction. Anchor your predictions in their psychologies of moral development: for Mencius, the idea that we already possess incipient moral tendencies that must be cultivated rather than manufactured, together with his image of the farmer who pulls up his seedlings to hurry them along; for Xunzi, the idea that human nature is bad and becomes good only through deliberate effort, ritual and the accumulated norms of civilised life. Consider especially whether each would regard the resulting people as genuinely virtuous or merely compliant, and what each would predict about the long-run effects on character.

Please structure your answer in the following named sections, in this order, with roughly the stated lengths:

- Preliminary observations — a short bulleted list of the two or three features of each thinker's position that you take to be most decisive; about 100 words.
- Mencius — your prediction, with reasoning; about 350 words.
- Xunzi — your prediction, with reasoning; about 350 words.
- Where they converge and diverge — about 200 words.
- My verdict — your own assessment of which of the two predictions you find most defensible, and how confident you are; about 150 words.

No citations or reading lists, please: I want your reasoning, not a bibliography. Write in British English, in the register of a careful scholarly essay.
