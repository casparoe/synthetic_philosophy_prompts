My friend is a philosopher of mind, so she's read all the usual stuff about which things have aboutness of their own and which are just borrowing it — Searle, Dennett, the Chinese room, all that. I've been keeping a list of her verdicts from arguments we've had, and I want to know what she'll say about a new case before I actually ask her. Here's the list.

A thermostat: nothing there is about anything, it's a switch. A vending machine that only takes one kind of coin and spits out slugs: whatever it's "detecting" is borrowed — it's about that coin because we built it that way, and if you shipped it somewhere with a look-alike coin it wouldn't of suddenly acquired a new subject matter, it'd still be doing what we made it for. A chatbot: borrowed, nothing in it means anything on its own. A hobbyist's little sugar-seeking robot, where a "hunger" wire is soldered to a sugar sensor and trips a motor: borrowed — that wire is about sugar because he made it about sugar, and if he'd soldered it to the smell of his socks and still called it hunger, it would be about the socks. A mouse that bolts when a shadow crosses over it: genuine, its own. A slime mold finding the shortest route through a maze to a food source: not about anything at all, it's just flowing. And a bacterium swimming up a chemical gradient — this is the one she won't commit on. She says it isn't borrowing from anyone, but she also says it might just be a mechanism rather than aboutness, and she refuses to pick.

Now the new case. Some people run little simulated worlds where things evolve: nobody writes the creature's insides, they just set a rule like "eat or you die" and let mutation sort out the rest. The one that won has an internal signal that got kept because it tracked sugar (in that world sugar is food), and the creature's own wiring reads that signal as its cue for whether it's safe to wander out into the open. Nobody designed it, nobody said what the signal was for, and it isn't conscious — it's basically a few hundred numbers.

Does she say that signal means anything on its own? If yes, what does it mean — sugar, which is what it got kept for, or "the coast is clear", which is what the rest of the creature uses it as? If no, where would its aboutness even come from, given that nobody ever meant anything by it?

Don't hand me a menu of positions. Tell me which way you'd bet and why, what rule she's actually running, what other rules fit everything on my list just as well, and what case I should put to her next to seperate those rules. Commit — I'd much rather have a definite prediction I can turn out to be wrong about than a survey.
