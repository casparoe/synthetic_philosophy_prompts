I'm on the residents' committee at the care home where my aunt lives. I'm not a doctor or a lawyer or anything, they just wanted a family member on it, and now I'm the one writing up our opinion before Thursdays meeting. Our recommendation goes to the medical director and she follows it unless there's a legal reason not to, so it actually matters what I write.

Here's the case. One of the residents, Eileen, is 79, mid-stage Alzheimers, she's been there three years. Six years ago, before anyone had diagnosed her, she filled in a form at her GP surgery saying she didn't want antibiotics for infections and didn't want to be sent to hospital. At the bottom she added a line in her own handwriting: her sister had just died in an ICU hooked up to everything, and she wrote that this was for the frightened her, not for the her who was still laughing at bad jokes, and that if she was still laughing at bad jokes somebody should tear the form up.

She laughs at bad jokes constantly now. She's happy there. She's in the garden every day, she has a friend she sits with at breakfast, she's not distressed, she's not asking to die, and if you show her the form she doesn't know what it is.

She's got a urine infection. Oral antibiotics and she's probably back in the garden inside a week. Without them, probably sepsis, probably dead in a few days, and not comfortably. It's her fourth one this year, so if we treat it we're back in this same room in a few months. If we don't, thats that.

Her son wants the form followed to the letter. Her daughter, who visits every day, wants her treated and says the form is a strangers opinion of a woman she never met. One of the nurses says treating her means we've definately just binned the form; another says our job is to go by what makes her life go well now.

Can you write the opinion I'll circulate? I need an actual recommendation - treat her, don't treat her, or treat this one with a plan for before the next one - with a time frame attached, and it has to deal honestly with the side I come down against, because the son will read it. And I'm stuck on the note at the bottom. Does the woman who wrote it get to say that the woman she'd turn into counts for more than she does? Thats what she seems to have been trying to do, and I can't work out whether that makes the form stronger or weaker.
