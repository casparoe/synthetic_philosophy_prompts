I spent my working life in condensed matter physics, where the quarrel between reductionists and emergentists ended in a working truce: nobody insists that every claim about a solid be derived from the Schrödinger equation, and nobody doubts that the microscopic facts fix the macroscopic ones. Reading social science in retirement, I keep meeting what looks like the same quarrel, between those who hold that only individuals and their beliefs and motives can explain social phenomena and those who hold that institutions, norms, and classes explain things in their own right. What are the most important implications of that quarrel for how I should judge an explanation of the second kind — for instance, one that accounts for the persistence of a custom by appeal to the benefit it confers on the community that keeps it — and where does the analogy with my old field break down? I am not asking which side is right. I want to know what the dispute commits me to when I decide whether such an explanation has explained anything.
