I am applying for a one-year research fellowship and need a two-page proposal. The topic has become personally urgent to me: I became a parent last year, and I have been trying to reduce my household's carbon footprint, but I keep running into the objection that my individual choices—driving less, eating less meat, flying less—cannot make a difference to a problem as large as climate change. I want to turn this unease into a philosophical project. My half-formed idea is that the inefficacy objection depends on a picture of individual action as a marginal increment to a linear causal process, and that this picture is wrong for many collective harms. I suspect we need a distinction between causal contribution and what I have been calling participatory maintenance: the way an individual's action can sustain a harmful practice even when it does not alter the amount of harm produced. The project would ask: under what conditions does an individual's participation in a collective harm have moral significance when its marginal causal contribution is zero or negligible? Please write the proposal so that it states the question precisely, explains why the existing answers—whether they appeal to expected utility, complicity, rule-consequentialism, or collective obligation—leave it open, and sets out a method. The method should be conceptual: develop the distinction, test it against cases such as meat consumption, voting, and personal carbon emissions, and consider the strongest objections. Explain why this approach is preferable to the obvious alternatives, such as an empirical study of why people feel complicit or a straightforward application of expected utility theory. Say what a successful outcome would look like—what it would establish and for whom—and what would count as the project failing, for instance if participatory maintenance turns out to be a relabeling of complicity or if it cannot handle the case of the perfectly isolated individual. The proposal should be realistic for one year of part-time work and should not include a bibliography or citations; I will add those myself. Please write it in a polished, formal style.
