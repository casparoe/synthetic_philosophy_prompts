I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and for a local meetup I want a diagnostic that sorts people by their actual decision-theoretic commitments on Newcomb's problem rather than by the labels they favor. Please build a decision tree whose root is the standard Newcomb setup, but whose branches are driven by concrete variants: a predictor that is only 60% reliable; a transparent box that lets you see the contents before choosing; a predictor that is a perfect copy of you running one second ahead; a predictor that reads your source code; and a version where the payoff goes to a stranger rather than to you. Each internal node should be a question answerable without knowing the names of the theories, and each leaf should name the decision theory it corresponds to (causal, evidential, timeless, updateless, functional, or a named hybrid) plus one sentence on the commitment that leaf finds hardest to accept. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and note any combination of answers that no one seems to occupy, with a brief diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Keep it to about a page, and let the variants do the sorting rather than reproducing the usual taxonomy.
