I chair the steering committee thats standing up a war-crimes tribunal for the old regime, and I need a short written opinion from you before we vote on the statute next week. The fight is the headline count: the draft charges the former defence minister with planning and starting the war itself, and his lawyers say that back when he did it, that wasn't a crime anywhere you could actually be prosecuted for -- we only made aggression an offence after we won. People keep invoking Nuremberg and Tokyo at me, not as a compliment: law fitted to the accused, a bench drawn only from the side that won, our own coalition's militia having shelled more civilians than his army did, and a promise in the peace deal that the man who actually gave the orders walks free. I'm told the line that matters is between inventing a new crime and merely inventing a new court to try an old one, and I'm not sure it holds. So give me a bottom line I can defend in the vote -- keep the count, drop it, or keep it in amended form -- with the reasoning, an honest pass at the best argument aginst whatever you recommend, and what we should do about the immunity we promised. And say when we have to decide, since we're supposed to open in the spring.
