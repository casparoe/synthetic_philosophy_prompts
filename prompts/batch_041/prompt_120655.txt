I am applying for a one-semester faculty research grant and need a proposal of about 3,000 words on the following question: is there explanatory work in biology that genuinely requires a semantic notion of genetic information — information that is *about* something, in the way a sentence or a signal is — or does a deflationary notion suffice, on which DNA merely stands in a fine-grained causal relation to the products of development, with the specificity of that relation and the arbitrariness of the mapping from codons to amino acids doing all the work that the word 'information' was supposed to do?

My half-formed idea is that this dispute keeps restarting because three quite different things travel under the word 'code'. First, code as cipher: an arbitrary mapping that can only be applied by consulting something like a codebook. Second, code as engineered scheme: a signalling arrangement in which redundancy is built in so that messages survive noise, which is one way of reading the degeneracy of the codon table. Third, code as a body of law — a set of rules that binds, obliges, and is violated — which is roughly how 'the code of life' behaves in popular writing and, I suspect, in some professional writing too. My hunch is that the deflationist is right about the first sense, the realist has a surviving case about the second, and the third is doing rhetorical work that distorts claims about genetic causation, genetic determinism, and what it means to 'read' a genome.

Turn this into a proposal. State the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the standard realist and deflationist positions leave it open; set out the method — in particular, what a case would have to look like for the semantic notion to earn its explanatory keep, and how one would tell such a case apart from one where causal specificity alone is enough; say what a positive result, a negative result, and a failure of the project would each look like; and give an honest account of what fits in one semester and what would have to wait. You may assume I know the relevant biology, including that the code is nearly but not quite universal, with variants in mitochondria and elsewhere, and that the mapping is implemented physically in tRNA charging rather than written down anywhere. No bibliography or literature review by citation; I will add references myself.
