Ok so here's one I've been chewing on for a while. I cover the science-and-ideas beat for a mid-size daily, and a source at a contract research outfit slipped me their internal Animal Care and Use Handbook — and the thing reads less like a compliance binder than like a treatise by somebody with a very coherent moral theory, and not the one you'd expect a modern lab to have. It never cites anyone, never names a philosopher, but every clause lines up with the view that we have no duties *to* animals at all — only duties *regarding* them, owed to ourselves or to humanity, because animals can't be parties to the moral law and cruelty to a dog makes us callous toward people. So the handbook calmly licenses the actual use, and then insists, in a warm HR voice, that staff be kind to the animals anyway: not for their sake, but as moral hygiene, a workout for the sympathy muscle. I want to run a parody of it alongside the story, because I think readers will feel how much work that move is doing once they see it in memo form.

So: write me a satirical version of the handbook, 800–900 words, straight-faced throughout, formatted as numbered clauses with headings, in deadpan institutional register — onboarding paperwork, not Swift, nobody winks. I'd like it to include at least a clause on what the company does and doesn't owe the animals in its care and why the "doesn't" still leaves kindness mandatory; a clause that treats the moral cost of cruelty purely as a cost to the employee's own character, with some absurd compliance mechanism bolted on (a form, a checkbox, a mandatory training module); a clause covering experimental use and terminal procedures that arrives at the permissible conclusion through reasoning that's impeccably rigorous and a little horrible; and a passage about the death of a long-serving animal that's obviously meant to be moving and keeps tripping over the theory. Don't name Kant, don't let anybody crack wise. Someone who's read the Doctrine of Virtue should be able to reconstruct the whole position from the clauses alone, and someone who hasn't should still find it funny and faintly chilling, and should be able to say afterwards what the objection is. Keep the usual objections in play without ever stating them — that this treats animals as mere means, that cruelty is wrong only instrumentally, that it's anthropocentric to the core, that it's a grown-up version of "be nice to the dog so you don't turn into a monster."

After the piece, tack on a short structured summary for my notes, as a JSON object with these fields: "target" (the position being satirized, one sentence), "kantian_moves_used" (array of the specific moves the handbook leans on), "clauses_and_what_they_land" (array of objects, each with "clause" and "objection_it_surfaces"), "where_a_kantian_would_say_im_being_unfair" (two or three sentences, argued in good faith, not a strawman), and "joke_that_does_the_most_work" (one sentence). Plain JSON, no commentary wrapped around it.
