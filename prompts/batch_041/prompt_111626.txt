I'm a PhD student in philosophy, TAing a history of modern philosophy course, and I'm grading midterms on Rousseau's political and aesthetic writings. One exam question asked: "Explain Rousseau's reply to d'Alembert's proposal that Geneva acquire a theatre. On Rousseau's account, what is wrong with the theatre, and what does he propose in its place?" Here is the answer I have to grade:

"d'Alembert, in his Encyclopédie article on Geneva, had suggested that the city would gain from a theatre, since it already had a taste for literature and a stage would refine its manners. Rousseau's Letter replies that the proposal misunderstands what a republic is. He does not deny the theatre's power to move an audience; his objection is Platonic: dramatic representation is an imitation twice removed from the truth, and a people that takes pleasure in copies will lose its grip on what is real. The actor's profession makes this vivid, since he sells his person to be seen and makes a trade of simulating feelings he does not have, which feeds amour-propre in the performers and vanity in the spectators. What Geneva needs instead is the festival: the public dance and outdoor gathering where citizens are at once their own actors and their own audience. The theatre, then, is the chief cause of the corruption of Paris, and the same remedy is required everywhere, since a society that keeps a theatre will inevitably be a society of vanity and idleness."

Give me a grade and the point-by-point comments that justify it. I want exactly five numbered items and nothing else — no introduction, no conclusion. The first item should state the grade and the single most important reason for it; each of the remaining four should take up one specific claim in the answer, saying whether it is accurate and, where it is not, what is wrong with it (a misattribution, a conflation, an inference that does not follow) and what the correct account would be.
