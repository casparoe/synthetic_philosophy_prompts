Ok so I run the philosophy club at my high school (im a junior) and I have to design the session for next thursday. About 14 kids show up and I get 45 minutes, maybe 50 if the bell is late. The thing I want them to actually *feel* is moral luck -- the way how much we blame someone depends on stuff that had nothing to do with their choice. Like the drunk driver who hits a kid versus the drunk driver who gets home fine and is just kinda embarassed. Everyone nods at that in the abstract and then keeps blaming people by outcomes anyway, so a lecture is useless. I want them to do it to each other and get caught.

Heres what im thinking, tell me if it works or if you have a better version: everyone gets the same index card describing the same decision -- you're running late for work and you drive 15 over on a residential street, identical for all 14 of us, nobody gets a sob story and nobody gets an excuse. Then each person rolls a die in front of everyone: 1 or 2 means a kid chased a ball into the street and got hit, 3-6 means you got to work and nothing happened. Then on a slip of paper everyone anonymously writes how much blame (0-10) and what they'd want done to this person, and I read them out loud without saying which roll goes with which. Then I reveal the rolls.

Second round: same act, but this time each person draws a card at random that changes the surrounding facts (you were late because your boss said one more time and your fired / you were late cause you were up till 3am gaming), and we do the blame ratings again. And theres a third thing I really want: after round one but before the reveal, everyone rates *themselves* for the choice they made, and then after the reveal they rate themselves again now that they know whether "their" kid got hit. I think that part will be the one that lands.

So can you write me the actual runnable version? Rules, what I say at the start (nothing that gives it away -- they shouldn't hear the words "moral luck" till the very end), materials, a minute-by-minute timeline for 45 min, the die mechanic tuned so the split in the room actually happens, how to collect and read the ratings so nobodys embarassed, and a debrief that goes through the different kinds of luck and points back at specific moments that just happened. Also tell me honestly whats gonna go wrong the first time I run it (I've never facilitated anything like this) and what part of the idea the game just cant get across and I'll have to say out loud myself.
