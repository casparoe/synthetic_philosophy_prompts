I am a graduate student sitting in on a seminar on imprecise probabilities, and I have been assigned the early chapters of Keynes's Treatise on Probability. I can follow the formal apparatus well enough, but I keep losing my grip on the notion of the weight of an argument. As I read him — and I am paraphrasing here, because the prose is dense and I do not want to put words in his mouth — Keynes distinguishes the probability of a proposition, which he treats as a measure of the balance of the evidence for it against its contradictory, from the weight of the argument in its favour, which has to do with how much relevant evidence there is in total. Two bodies of evidence can yield the same probability and yet differ in weight, and he appears to claim that evidence which is already implied by what we already knew can raise the weight without changing the probability at all, so that a conclusion drawn from a small sample and the same conclusion drawn from a large one may be equally probable and yet not equally well supported.

What I would like is a patient walk-through of this distinction as it actually functions in the text, rather than a summary of what later writers have made of it. In particular: is weight meant to be a second quantity alongside probability, or a merely comparative and qualitative property of an argument? If it is a quantity, what are its units, and how would one compare the weight of two unrelated bodies of evidence? What exactly is added to a probability judgement by knowing the weight, and is the claim about evidence already implied by the old evidence genuinely coherent, or does it depend on a particular and now-unfashionable view of what evidence is?

I would also like you to say which parts of the passage a careful reader should expect to resist — that is, where the distinction threatens to collapse into something more familiar, such as the idea that weight just tracks the reliability of the source or the sheer size of the sample — and what would have to be true of probability for the distinction to do genuine work rather than merely redescribe the ordinary point that more evidence is better. Please be explicit about which of your claims are reconstructions on my behalf and which are things the text itself commits him to.
