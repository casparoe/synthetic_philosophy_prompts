I've been keeping a mental list of the times I've felt real resentment and the times I haven't, trying to work out what the difference actually is, and I want you to tell me what principle — if any — my gut is tracking. The list: my flatmate is chronically, reliably late and treats my time as worthless, and I resent him fully, no hedging. A close friend said something vicious to me in the middle of a manic episode, and I never once felt resentment, just a sad wariness, and I'd push back on anyone who told me I secretly did. My uncle was raised in a cult and holds beliefs I find awful, and I can't summon resentment toward him at all; he's like weather to me. Someone cut me off in traffic and I was furious for half a second and then it evaporated when I saw the hospital wristband. My father was cold and absent and I resent him for it, and I also genuinely believe — not just to be fair — that given how he was raised he was never going to be anything else. That last one is the pair I can't fit: I resent him and I think it was inevitable. Then there's my old employer, toward which I feel something I can't distinguish from resentment, and a company isn't a person.

My tentative rule was that resentment is apt just when the person could have done otherwise — that fits the manic friend and the cult uncle but my father blows it up. My backup was that it's apt whenever someone is a full participant in the relationship with me, which handles my father but seems to license resenting a six-year-old. Is there one principle that gets all of this, or do I have to give one of these verdicts up? And say what it predicts for the ones I haven't listed: someone with dementia who insults me, a stranger on the internet, a friend who betrays me while genuinely believing its helping. If I'm confused about the whole shape of the question, tell me that instead of massaging a principle out of my list.
