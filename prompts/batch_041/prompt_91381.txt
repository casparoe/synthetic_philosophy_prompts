I have been trying to think through a phenomenon I will call commensurability drift, and I want you to describe its most important implications. Treat the term as defined below rather than as a piece of existing terminology; I am after reasoning from the definition, not a survey of what others have said about it.

By commensurability drift I mean the following process, which can occur in an individual or in an institution. First, a choice situation contains two or more options, each supported by a different value, and there is no common scale on which the options can be ranked — one is not better, worse, or equal to the other. Second, because the choice has to be made, and made repeatedly or by an agent that is accountable for treating like cases alike, a decision procedure is adopted that does yield a ranking: a lottery, a points system, a price, a rubric, a tie-breaking rule. Third, the procedure is reused, and its outputs become the basis for later decisions and for the agent's retrospective justification of the earlier one. Over time the values are treated as though a common measure existed, even though the agent may continue to insist, on reflection, that none does. The drift is a change in practice rather than in professed belief, and it can be invisible from the inside.

Please describe the most important implications of this phenomenon, ordered by how much they matter, and say for each what makes it important rather than merely true. In particular, I want to know: what it implies for the philosophical dispute over whether values are incommensurable, and whether it shows that dispute to be about practice rather than about value; what it implies for a person who faces a hard choice and is trying to avoid both paralysis and self-deception; and what it implies for the design of institutions that must adjudicate such choices — courts, hiring committees, hospital ethics boards — where consistency across cases is itself a demand of justice. Where the implications point in opposite directions, say so rather than smoothing them over. Please write in American English.
