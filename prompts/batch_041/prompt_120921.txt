I retired about four years ago (thirty-one years in a soil chemistry lab, for whatever that's worth) and since then I've been going to a little philosophy group that meets in the back room of a pub. Six of us, mostly amateurs, plus one retired classics teacher who keeps us honest. House rule: whoever picks the term's text has to close the last meeting with a joke about it. This term we read Zhuangzi, and I'm the one who picked it, so the joke is mine.

So naturally I got the butterfly bit, the one at the end of the second chapter where he wakes up and can't tell whether he was a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or is now a butterfly dreaming he's a man. And naturally every joke I come up with is the same joke everyone makes: so maybe my mortgage is a dream, ha ha. I want something that's actually about the passage.

Two things I noticed, reading it over and over, and I'd like the joke to turn on them:

1) It doesn't end on doubt. The last move isn't 'so nothing is real' — it's that there has to be some distinction between the man and the butterfly, and then he just gives the whole business a name, something like the transformation of things. So as far as I can tell the point the passage is reaching for is not 'I might be the butterfly', it's that the two of them are two phases of one thing going back and forth, and neither one gets to be the real one. That seems definately different from Descartes and I think it's funnier.

2) The butterfly is the only character in the story who's happy. It's described as fluttering around, pleased with itself, with no idea it was ever Zhou. It's the man who wakes up and starts worrying. So the doubt sits entirely on the human side, wich strikes me as the joke.

Can you write me a short one — 100-150 words, the kind I can actually stand up and tell to six people holding pints — from the butterfly's side? Let the butterfly answer back. The punchline needs to land for the classics teacher, who's read the whole thing twice, without being baffling to the two people who only skimmed the handout. And don't make it bleak: Zhuangzi doesn't sound bothered by any of this, he sounds delighted, and I'd like the joke to feel that way too.

Please no quantum physics, no 'and then I woke up' endings, and nothing about butterflies only living a day — I've heard that one three times already. Also no citations and no Chinese, I can't pronounce it and half the group will tune out.
