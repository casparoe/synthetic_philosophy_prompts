I'm not a philosopher by training -- I run a small hospital's operations, so mostly I make tradeoff calls all day -- but I've got a puzzle about hard choices that I can't get past, and I'd like you to take it seriously as someone who works on this professionally (practical reason, value theory, whatever the right label is). Please think it through carefully, and start by brainstorming two or three candidate principles before you commit to one, so I can see the options you rejected and why. I don't want a survey of what different thinkers say; I want your actual view, defended.

Here's what I think, case by case.

Two jobs. One is a research post in a city I love; the other is a policy job doing work I believe in more. I know everything there is to know about both -- salary, hours, the people, the commute. I've been going back and forth for months and I still cant decide. My verdict: there's no fact of the matter about which is better for me. But not because they're equal -- equal would mean they're worth the same, and they aren't. Each one wins on its own terms. It's more like they're in the same league and neither one beats the other.

Two apartments, differing in a hundred small ways. Took me ten minutes to pick. My verdict: not a hard choice at all, and also no fact of the matter -- but this doesn't feel like the job case at all. There the uncertainty felt important; here it's nothing.

I tried nudging. Suppose the policy job pays 2% more. Then a shorter commute. Then a nicer office. My verdict: none of that settles it, and I don't think a pile of small improvements ever would. But if you doubled the salary and let me work from anywhere, I'd take it without agonizing. So there's some threshold, but it's not a number.

Two brands of toothpaste. Also no fact of the matter. But my verdict: this is indifference, not what's going on with the jobs. If I flip a coin here I've lost nothing. With the jobs, flipping a coin feels like it costs me something.

A job that's better on every dimension I care about. Verdict: obviously take it, and if a friend took the other one I'd think they'd made a mistake, not just a different choice.

Now the one that trips me up. A friend left a career she loved for one she herself described as worse on every dimension she cared about -- worse pay, worse work, worse city -- because it was the safe option, and she's been miserable for two years. My verdict: she chose badly. I'll say that flat out. But I also think the job case has no right answer, and I can't hold both without saying something about what "badly" means.

A loop. Forced to rank them, I'd take the policy job over the research job, the research job over a third option (teaching), and teaching over the policy job. My verdict: I don't feel like I've contradicted myself, even though I know that looks like a circle.

After I finally choose in the job case, I'll grieve the other one for years. My verdict: the grief isn't evidence I chose wrong.

Two treatments for my father, with unknown side-effect profiles. Verdict: hard, but a different kind of hard -- if I got the facts it would resolve. That's ignorance, not whatever is happening in the job case.

So: is there one account of "better" and of "hard" that gets all of these? State it precisely enough that I can apply it. In particular, is my "same league, neither wins" a real third relation between options, or am I just dressing up ignorance or rough comparison? If it's real, what does it do to the usual idea that if A isn't better than B, and B isn't better than A, then you're free to pick either -- because that can't be right for my friend.

Then: which of my verdicts does your account force me to give up? I'd rather you tell me one of my gut calls is wrong than build something complicated that saves all of them. And give me your verdict on a case I haven't listed -- say, choosing between two charities, or whether it's ever rational to pay real money just to avoid having to make a choice like the job one. Don't hedge, don't tell me it depends on which theory you adopt, and don't end with "the debate continues."
