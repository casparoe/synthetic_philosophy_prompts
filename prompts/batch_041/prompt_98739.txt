Inside a careenium — an enclosed region containing countless tiny magnetic marbles, called simms, which drift, collide, and now and then cohere into larger, semi-stable clusters called simmballs — the simmballs are said to bear symbols: a cluster's shape and trajectory can stand for something else, and clusters appear to nudge one another around in ways that cannot be read off from any single simm. Write a short story set inside such a careenium.

The story should dramatize, rather than merely assert, the dispute over what is really doing the pushing. Give us at least two characters who hold opposing views and let each land real blows. One insists that only simms push: every apparent act of a simmball is just a coarse-grained way of speaking about a great many simms shoving one another, and the simm-level account, though unwieldy, is the only causally complete one. The other insists that simmballs, as symbols, genuinely push the substrate around, so that the simm-level story is complete only in the way that a description of every car's engine is a complete explanation of a traffic jam. Neither character should be a fool, and neither should win by fiat.

To keep the story from becoming a lecture, anchor it in concrete episodes. The careenium contains one instrument, the Tracer, which, aimed at any event, returns the full simm-level history that produced it and nothing besides. At least one character should try to settle the argument with the Tracer and find that its readout cuts both ways. Let the story also turn on a moment when a symbol appears to hold the substrate in place — a cluster whose pattern stands for the injunction that some other cluster must stay where it is — and on a moment when every simm making up one simmball is swapped out while the pattern persists, so that a character must decide whether the symbol survived the exchange or only its shadow did. The story should end without a verdict imposed from outside; the reader should feel the pull of both positions.

Keep the story self-contained: do not name real philosophers, theories, or texts, and do not include citations. Invent whatever names, history, and stakes the careenium needs to make the argument feel like it matters to the characters living it.
