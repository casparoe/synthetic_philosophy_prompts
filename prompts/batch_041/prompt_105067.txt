Okay, I'm a science and ideas reporter at a mid-size outlet and I've got a problem I keep having and I finally want to fix it properly instead of just feeling bad about it. Here's the shape of it: I get a story to a decent state fast — a couple of sources, the main paper, the obvious skeptic — and then I keep going. One more phone call, one more study, someone on a message board says I really should talk to this other guy, and two days later I file something that's late and, honestly, no better than what I had on Tuesday, sometimes worse because now it's a mush of everybody's caveats. A friend who did a PhD told me this is exactly what Herbert Simon was on about with satisficing and bounded rationality, and that I should stop trying to optimize. Great, but "good enough" is not a procedure. Can you actually turn the theory into one I can run on a deadline?

What I want, in order: the questions I ask myself as a story develops, what counts as a real answer to each, where the procedure stops and says file it, and — this is the part I care about most — how and when I'm allowed to move the bar. Because I've definately noticed the bar moves on its own: after a big story lands I suddenly want more for the next one, and after a bad week I'll file almost anything to get something up. Is that movement the theory, and should I lean into it or fight it? Also please don't hand me a dressed-up version of trust your gut — if a step is really just a judgment call, say so instead of making it look mechanical. And be straight about which parts of the theory are descriptive (how people actually do decide) versus how I'm supposed to decide, because I've read enough pop versions to know those two got blurred together and I don't want to inherit the blur.

Then run it on two or three of my actual-ish situations. I want one where the procedure tells me to stop and file now and I won't like that answer, and one where it tells me to keep going even though I'm bored and the piece already feels done. And say what the procedure can't do — I assume there's a case where the one thing I haven't checked is the whole story, and no stopping rule saves me from that. Keep it in plain language, I'm not going to go read Simon, I just want the machine.
