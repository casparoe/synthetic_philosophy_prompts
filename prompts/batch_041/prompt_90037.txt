A group of us keep having the same argument and I'd like someone to tell me what we're actually disagreeing about, because we've been at it for two years now and it never moves.

It always starts the same way. Some politician or company or friend says a sentence that is, word for word, true — like "we've never had a data breach" when they knew one got reported the next day — and the room splits in half. One half says: that's misleading, not lying, and that's not just peadantry, lying is worse, you owe people the truth but you don't owe them every true fact that would stop them getting the wrong idea. The other half says: come off it, if the whole point of the sentence was to get me to believe something false, then you lied, the literal truth of the words is just a technicality you're hiding behind, and anyone who thinks there's a real moral difference here is handing liars a loophole for good grammar. Nobody ever budges. We just cycle through new examples every few months.

Here's my guess and I want you to tell me if it's right. Maybe we're not disagreeing about morality at all — maybe we're disagreeing about what "lying" even picks out. Like, one side thinks it's a fact about the words, the other thinks it's a fact about the plan sitting behind the words. Or maybe it's the other way round and we all secretly agree about the words and are really just split on whether the person who merely misleads deserves less blame.

One thing I've been chewing on: instead of arguing over what counts as a lie, ask whether the speaker's plan only works if I draw the false conclusion. Take the slippery sentence and swap in the plain honest one — "there was a breach last month" — and if that swap would defeat whatever they were trying to do, then the false belief wasn't a side effect of the wording, it was the whole point. Does a test like that actually settle anything, or does it just push the arguement back one step, to why the "point of the sentence" should matter to how bad it was?

So what kind of disagreement is this really — verbal, moral, or two different questions wearing one word? And what would count as real progress here, if anything would?
