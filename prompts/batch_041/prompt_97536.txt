She works on Buddhist philosophy of mind, and her line is that the whole modern debate about anatta gets the target wrong. On her reading, the Buddhist claim isn't "there is no self" as a piece of metaphysical inventory, and it isn't the Parfit-style reductionist claim that a person is just a bundle either. She thinks the practice is aimed at something more like a pre-reflective sense of being a someone -- the kind of thing that can linger even after you've stopped believing in a permanent self, and that the tradition itself treats as a seperate problem from the "view of self" you're supposedly able to uproot. She also keeps insisting that the "two truths" distinction is a real constraint rather than a get-out-of-jail-free card, and that people who call the self a mere useful fiction are making a much stronger and different claim than the tradition does.

What I want is eight to ten questions that actually push on the content and don't just let her restate the view. At least one should ask how a no-self view that still permits that "I am" feeling accounts for the unity of a single experience at a time and the persistence of a person across time, without smuggling the self back in as either the thing that binds experiences together or the thing that continues.
