I keep noticing a move and I dont know if it has a name, so if it doesn't, I'd like to know what to call it and where exactly its edges are. Here's the shape of it. Someone is defending a practice their own group does -- a church that won't ordain women, a family that arranges its kids' marriages -- and when an outsider objects, out comes the relativism: you can't judge another culture by your standards, morality is just the local consensus, who made you the arbiter. Fine. But then the same person, later, is genuinely angry about some other practice -- how another country treats women, or a company doing something exploitative -- and now it's not 'relative to their culture' at all, it's just flat wrong, and they argue for it like there's a fact of the matter. What makes it interesting is that each move comes with a plausible-sounding reason. The relativist move gets 'respect other cultures.' The universalist move gets 'but this one actually harms people,' or 'but they never really consent,' or 'but that's not even a real part of their culture, it's a recent invention.' So it isn't just hypocrisy -- the person can always give a reason why the two cases differ. It's more that the meta-ethical stance looks like it gets picked to match the conclusion they already wanted, and the reason comes after. Is there a name for this specific thing? I know the neighborhood -- cultural relativism, the tolerance argument, ethnocentrism, plain inconsistency -- but none of those seems to be it, because none of them capture the asymmetry, the way relativism is a shield for your own side and a hammer for everyone else's. If there's no term, coin one and tell me how it differs from ordinary hypocrisy on one side and from a legitimate distinction like 'this practice really is harmful' on the other. And does the reason they give for the asymmetry get them off the hook, or is the reason itself part of the pattern?
