I'm taking a philosophy of biology seminar this term, and last week's discussion turned to what we mean when we say that the heart is for pumping blood, or that a peacock's tail is for display. The instructor's line, which she returned to several times, is that biological functions are just the functions of tools and machines: a corkscrew is for opening bottles, a thermostat is for regulating temperature, and a heart is for pumping, all in one and the same sense. From there she drew the moral that since a corkscrew has the function it has because someone made it for opening bottles, a trait has the function it has because natural selection built it for that job; and so, on her view, when biologists say that the function of a trait is whatever it currently contributes to the working of the organism, they are not offering a rival analysis of function but quietly changing the subject.

I found this persuasive in the seminar and have since come to suspect it was doing more work than she let on. Could you audit the analogy for me? I would like the correspondence laid out explicitly, including what in the artifact case is meant to map onto what in the biological case: maker, purpose, plan, mistake, wear and tear, repair, copying, and the difference between a thing made for a job and a thing merely good at it. Then I want the places where the mapping fails, sorted into the disanalogies that are merely picturesque and the ones that actually bear on the conclusion she drew. I am especially interested in what the artifact picture quietly imports, a maker with intentions and a plan, say, or a principled distinction between a tool's proper use and its accidental ones, and whether the biological case has anything playing those roles. Does the analogy, spelled out honestly, support the claim that talk of current contribution changes the subject rather than competing with her account, or was the conclusion being carried by the comparison all along? Two cases I keep turning over: a stone that happens to be knife-shaped and cuts well, and a part installed in a machine by mistake that nonetheless keeps the machine from overheating. My sense is that artifact function is messier than she made it sound, and that the mess matters for the biological case, but I would like to be told whether that is so. If there is a better comparison available for what she is after, I would like to hear it.

One last thing: if I have set this up badly, either because the analogy is not the right lever for the dispute or because the two accounts do not compete in the way I have assumed, please say so plainly rather than answering inside my framing. I would rather be told that the question is malformed than receive a tidy audit of a comparison that should never have been the issue.
