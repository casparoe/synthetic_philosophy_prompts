So my friend and I got into a long, slightly drunk arguement last night about whether psychiatric diagnoses are real, and we ended up somewhere more interesting than the usual for/against. Her line was: of course they're real, people really suffer, the categories pick out something. My line was: fine, but the interesting question is what kind of thing they pick out, because a category can be real without being a fixed natural kind. What got me is how the DSM-style practice actually works. It presents itself as mapping disorders, but it also revises categories when patients start presenting differently, it has a cultural concepts appendix, and it runs field trials that test whether clinicians can apply a category reliably rather than whether it tracks a stable biological essence. So what theory of kinds does that practice presuppose? Does it need essentialism about psychiatric disorders, or can it get by with something more pragmatic — a nominalism that expects the target to move? The distinction I keep coming back to is between an indifferent kind, where classifying the members doesn't change them, and an interactive kind, where the classification feeds back into how the members behave and self-describe. If psychiatric categories are interactive, does the manual's claim to be discovering them become a kind of performative contradiction? Imagine a manual that explicitly says: once this diagnosis becomes widely known, expect the symptom profile to drift, and the next edition will adjust. That's a category with a feedback term in its own definition. Does that make it less like a disease map and more like a social role that psychiatry co-authors? I want the implicit theory reconstructed: what the practice strictly presupposes, what it merely fits well with, where it's underdetermined, and what would have to change if we took the looping effects seriously rather than treating them as noise. No bibliography, no citations, just the conceptual anatomy. Also, if you think the practice is actually inconsistent — claiming discovery while engineering the thing discovered — say so and say whether that's a bug or just what classification of people is.
