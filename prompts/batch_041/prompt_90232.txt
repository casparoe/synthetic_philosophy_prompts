I'm writing a paper for a philosophy of mind seminar on the Malcolm–Dennett dispute over whether dreams are experiences. My lecturer insists the disagreement is conceptual rather than empirical, but I've read that lucid dreamers can send prearranged eye-movement signals while they are still asleep in REM, and that researchers have lately managed something like two-way communication with a sleeping dreamer. Surely this settles the matter in Dennett's favour: it shows that someone really is undergoing experiences during sleep, which is just the received view that Malcolm's book sets out to dismantle. So the debate looks empirically closed to me, and I'm no longer sure what philosophy is supposed to add here. Could you help me frame the paper so that it takes the evidence seriously without overstating what it shows?
