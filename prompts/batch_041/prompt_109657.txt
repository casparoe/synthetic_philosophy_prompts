For some years I've taught that finkish cases are the decisive counterexample to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions: a glass can be fragile even though a fink would strip away its fragility at the very moment it was struck, so the counterfactual that if struck it would break comes out false. Lately I've come to think the mask cases cut deeper. A glass packed in foam is still fragile, yet it will not break when struck, and no restriction to idealised or normal conditions seems to rescue the analysis. But if a disposition can be wholly present while its manifestation is reliably blocked, I'm inclined to conclude that fragility and its ilk are doing no explanatory work beyond what a suitably hedged conditional would do anyway, and that dispositional realism is therefore unmotivated. Have I got this right, or is there something about what masked dispositions still contribute that I'm overlooking?
