I have recently been appointed as the independent member of a panel that hears challenges to Universal Credit sanctions in my area, and I am to vote on my first case next week. The panel's decisions over the past year include the following. A claimant who missed a work-focused interview because she was attending a hospital appointment for her son that could not be moved was not sanctioned. A claimant who missed one because he was attending a job interview arranged by his work coach was not sanctioned. A claimant who missed one because he was working a paid shift that he had been offered at short notice was not sanctioned. A claimant who missed one because he overslept was sanctioned. A claimant who missed one because he forgot the appointment was sanctioned. The case before us concerns a claimant who missed a work-focused interview because he was attending the funeral of his closest friend. Identify the competing rules that fit our record, say what each predicts for this case, give your best guess as to how we should decide and why, and name the further case that would force us to choose between the rules. Which of our precedents is the closest analogue, and what principle would we be adopting with each choice?
