I am writing a seminar paper on social choice and want to be sure I am not building on a misreading of Sen's impossibility of a Paretian liberal before I commit to a framing.

As I understand the result, it establishes that any society which protects even a minimal sphere of individual liberty is thereby committed to collective outcomes that are Pareto-inefficient: liberty and efficiency stand in a permanent structural opposition, and the only genuine question for a polity is how much inefficiency it is prepared to purchase with its freedoms. If that is right, the interesting research programme becomes comparative and empirical — to measure the "price of liberty" across different institutional regimes, and to ask whether there is a threshold of rights-protection beyond which a society becomes ungovernable.

I also have a hazy recollection that the standard way out of the result is to restrict attention to those preference profiles in which no one's ranking of the alternatives depends on which private option anyone else ends up with, and I have been treating that restriction as a technical footnote — a device that lets us set the paradox aside while we get on with the empirical work.

Two things nag at me. First, if the conflict is genuinely structural, it is odd that it should be the sort of thing one could price; a trade-off suggests a rate of exchange, and I cannot see where the theorem supplies one. Second, the "way out" I remember sounds less like a solution to a conflict than like a stipulation about whose preferences are permitted to count, which would make it a normative choice rather than a neutral repair.

Could you set me straight on what the theorem actually asserts and what does and does not follow from it? Specifically: what are the conditions, what exactly is the impossibility claim, and does anything in it license the "price of liberty" framing or the suggestion that a society can be rendered ungovernable by protecting rights? And if the domain restriction I recall is indeed the standard escape, is it a repair of the theorem's conditions or a change of subject?
