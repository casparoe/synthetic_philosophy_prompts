I'm putting together discussion material for a group thats read way too much pop Fermi-paradox stuff and is bored of it. Can you compare the dark forest hypothesis with the security dilemma from international relations -- the Herz/Jervis idea where two states can't reliably tell a defensive buildup from an offensive one, so each arms out of fear of the other, and they spiral into hostility even though neither actually wants a fight.

What I really want is the joints, not the summaries. Is the dark forest just the security dilemma with the volume cranked up -- no communication, no institutions, no second chances -- or does it quietly smuggle in extra assumptions the security dilemma never needs? Things like a guaranteed first-strike advantage, or the idea that detection always comes before destruction, or that every civilization discounts its future the same way and is therefore equally trigger-happy. Where exactly do the two come apart?

Push on the interesting cases. The security dilemma is supposed to be escapable -- states can talk, build trust, sign treaties, verify each other. Why does the dark forest say none of that is available between civilizations, and is that a real asymmetry in the situation or just an assumption baked in? And which piece of the dark forest's "hide or die" logic would the security dilemma actually reject -- for instance the claim that you can't even form a rough estimate of the other side's intentions?

Then flip it and stress-test the dark forest: relax its assumptions one at a time -- cheap interstellar travel, no first-strike advantage, common knowledge that everyone is weak. Which single relaxation collapses the equilibrium fastest, and does the security dilemma survive that same relaxation or does it die with it?

Last thing. The dark forest gets sold as an inevitability, while the security dilemma usually gets sold as a tragedy that decent institutions can escape. Which framing is doing the real work here -- is the gap between them a matter of game theory, or just a difference in how pessimistic each story is about who's playing?
