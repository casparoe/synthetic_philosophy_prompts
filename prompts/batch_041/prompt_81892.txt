i want five jokes about convention — Lewis-style, the coordiantion kind, where what makes everyone do it is that everyone expects everyone else to do it, and the particular thing they do is arbitrary (drive on this side, nod this way, stand on this step of the escalator). Not jokes that just say "society is made up, lol" — the humour has to come from the machinery actually working, so that someone who's read the real stuff laughs rather than winces. And don't lean on examples I'd have to go look up.

Give me exactly five, numbered, a sentence or two each, no intro, no outro, and no explaining the punchline afterwards. Spread them across the following, roughly one each:

- a joke whose punchline turns on the gap between everyone knowing something and everyone knowing that everyone knows it — the thing that's meant to be doing the real work;
- a joke about a convention that's genuinely load-bearing and has zero justification, where "we've always done it this way" turns out to be a description of the mechanism rather than a reason;
- a joke about the awkward moment a convention becomes a norm — when breaking it stops being merely confusing and starts earning you side-eye, and whether the side-eye does anything the coordination wasn't already doing;
- a joke about a convention changing or dying out, the kind of change that can't happen one person at a time, where the replacement wins because it was first or salient rather than because it was better;
- one joke about a convention you invent yourself. It has to be one that could actually arise: it should solve a real coordination problem, and what everyone does should be arbitrary, i.e. a different arbitrary thing would have served just as well.

Keep each short enough to tell out loud at a party, and make sure the last one couldn't be mistaken for a real convention that already exists.
