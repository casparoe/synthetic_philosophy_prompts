I'm putting together a handout for a postgrad reading group on deflationary theories of truth, and I want the first page to force people to commit to something rather than do the usual well-it-depends-what-you-mean-by-deflationary dance.

Here are six packages that all get filed under 'deflationary':

1. The redundancy line — 'it is true that p' says no more than 'p', so the truth predicate is doing no work.
2. Disquotationalism — the schema ''p' is true iff p', for sentences we understand, tells you everything there is to say about truth; the predicate is a device for cancelling quotation and climbing to semantic ascent.
3. Minimalism — truth is a genuine property, but a shallow one, fully given by the equivalence schema; its job is to let us generalise and endorse ('everything she said is true').
4. Prosententialism — 'that's true' is a prosentence, inheriting its content anaphorically from an antecedent sentence the way 'she' inherits a referent from a name; truth isn't a property at all.
5. The early performative line — uttering 'it is true that p' doesn't describe anything, it performs an act: agreeing, confirming, endorsing.
6. Inferentialist or expressive accounts — 'true' makes explicit a normative status, namely the propriety of asserting, and so functions as a device for endorsing claims and keeping score of commitments.

Tier these — top, middle, bottom — rank within each tier, and give each an overall score out of 10. Judge them against four things:

(a) the generalisation problem: can it actually explain how 'everything she said is true' manages to say what it says?
(b) the norm of assertion: can it explain why 'that's not true' registers as a criticism rather than a change of subject?
(c) the explanatory use: does truth get to do any explaining — say, of why a theory's predictions keep coming out right — or has it been deflated into something too thin to pull that weight?
(d) self-application: the thesis that truth is deflationary, is that thesis itself true, and if so in which sense? If the answer is 'in the deflated sense', has the view just quietly made itself unfalsifiable?

Also give me a one-line verdict on whether these six are genuinely seperate positions or just different costumes on one view, and if you reckon the whole exercise is definately rigged, tell me which two you think are the only live options.
