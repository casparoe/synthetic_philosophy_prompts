For about a year now I have been trying to settle, on my own and without any philosophical training, a question that began as a joke and has since taken over my evenings: whether a colony of ants can be said to have a mind. I know the question is an old one, but I am less interested in what the celebrated answers have been than in how a careful reasoner would actually rank the candidates. I have come to think the crux is neither intelligence nor complexity but something I have been calling a single viewpoint: whether there is one place and one moment from which everything the entity encounters is experienced together, as opposed to many little viewpoints running side by side that never combine into one. A person seems to me to have a single viewpoint. A crowd, I would say, does not — it has as many viewpoints as it has members, and no additional one belonging to the crowd itself.

I would like you to rate each of the following from 0 to 1 for the probability that there is genuinely something it is like to be it — that is, that it has a mind in the sense that matters here — and then arrange them into tiers from most to least confident. For each, please give one or two sentences saying how it fares on the single-viewpoint test and on a second thing I have been puzzling over: whether the entity can be mistaken or surprised, or whether that kind of error simply has no place to happen in it.

1. A single ant.
2. A colony of leafcutter ants, taken as a whole.
3. A honeybee hive.
4. A termite mound together with its inhabitants.
5. A murmuration of starlings.
6. A slime mould finding its way through a maze.
7. A person whose two brain hemispheres have been surgically separated.
8. A corporation.
9. A city.
10. The internet.
11. A forest whose trees are linked by fungal threads.
12. The community of microbes in a human gut.

I would much rather have a genuine ranking with some uncomfortable placements than a safe list in which everything drifts toward the middle, so please commit to specific numbers rather than hedging them all to 0.5. If you think my single-viewpoint test is simply the wrong test to apply, say so, and rate the items by whatever test you think is right instead — but keep the ratings and the tiers.
