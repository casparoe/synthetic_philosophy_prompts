ok so I'm a novelist, about 200 pages into a draft where one of the main characters is an AI that has to make real ethical calls out in the field -- think a triage system in a disaster, phones down, nobody to ask, it has to just decide. The thing thats killing me is that every time I sit down to write its inner monologue I realize I don't actually know what I'm asking of a moral theory for a machine. I keep half-inventing one and then a scene later it contradicts itself.

So before I write another page: give me the spec. Not a tour of the famous names, I don't need a reading list -- the requirements. What does any theory of machine ethics have to be able to do to count as adequate for the specific job of being built into something that acts, in the world, on its own?

Things I've already bumped into and want you to fold in, each with the case that makes it a requirement rather than a preference:

- it has to spit out a decision in whatever time it has, not a verdict about a decision after the fact
- a human auditor who does not share its values has to be able to look at what it did and recieve a justification they can actually evaluate
- it has to cope with being unsure which moral theory is even right, and act anyway
- it has to handle conflicts between three groups that never agreed with each other: whoever built it, whoever is operating it right now, and whoever gets hurt by it
- it can't quietly assume the machine has feelings or phenomenal states, because then I'm writing a different book
- it has to not fall apart when its copied, paused mid-decision, or restarted -- two copies of the same system shouldnt be able to talk themselves into opposite answers
- and it can't be trivially gamed by whoever writes its inputs, because in my book that's basically the villain

For each requirement tell me why it's a requirement and what would break if you dropped it. Then the part I actually care about: flag any subset of these you think can't all be satisfied at once, because if some of them are jointly impossible then thats my plot, thats the whole tension of the novel. And tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the usual candidates.

Don't bother scoring the contenders, I'll do that in my own notes. Just the spec.
