The responsibility to protect holds that sovereignty carries with it a state's responsibility to shield its own population from atrocity, and that where the state fails or is itself the perpetrator, a residual responsibility falls to the wider community of states. The practice that has grown up around the doctrine is a stranger thing than the doctrine. Force was authorised against Libya in 2011, but no intervention was authorised to stop the genocide in Rwanda in 1994, and the Security Council deadlocked over Syria; NATO bombed Kosovo in 1999 with no authorisation at all, and its defenders argued that the action was unlawful under the Charter and yet legitimate. Mandates are drafted as the protection of civilians, yet the political objective in practice is the removal of the regime, and the mark of a concluded intervention is a new government rather than a population that is safe. Where intervening powers have occupied a country, they have legislated for its inhabitants.

What does this practice presuppose, and is it coherent? Reconstruct the implicit theory of three things: sovereignty, and what forfeits it; the nature of the residual responsibility — whether it is a duty at all, and if so who holds it, what triggers it and what discharges it; and the location of political authority, in particular whether authority over a population can be held by an outsider in trust for that population. Then separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely suits, and say where the practice is underdetermined, so that several incompatible theories would license it equally well.

The apparent inconsistency I want you to resolve is this: the practice justifies itself by the safety of individuals and concludes itself with the self-government of a collective. Set against that the domestic treatment of a guardian appointed over an adult who is being harmed — authority held in trust for the ward, answerable to the ward's interests, lapsing once the ward can act for themselves. Is an intervening power's authority structured the same way, and if not, what does the difference reveal about what the practice takes to be the point of the exercise? Finish by naming the single premise which, if false, would make the practice incoherent rather than merely selective, and by saying what would have to change in law and institutions if that premise were replaced.
