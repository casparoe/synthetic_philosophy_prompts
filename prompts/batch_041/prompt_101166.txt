hey so im in my schools philosophy club and next month we're doing a session on whether you should believe what people tell you. i want to make a quiz for it, a "which theory of testimony do you actually hold" thing, because last time we did personal identity everyone just argued about labels for an hour and it was so boring.

the plan: 8-10 questions, each one a concrete case, two or three answer options each, and then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. positions i want in there at least: (1) the one where you need positive evidence for each individual person before you believe them, (2) the one where you need evidence but only about people in general, like "most people are honest most of the time" type thing, (3) the one where you dont need evidence at all and you're just entitled to believe people by default, and (4) the one where what justifies you isnt evidence or entitlement but the fact that the person is actually telling you, like theyre giving you their word and taking responsibility for it. if other positions fall out of the logic of the thing then add them, but dont just invent labels for the sake of having more leaves.

cases i want to use, roughly: a stranger on the street tells you the time; a doctor tells you your test results; your friend tells you gossip about someone; a classmate tells you a fact about a subject theyre obviously an expert in and you cant check it; a little kid tells you theres a monster in their closet; a salesperson tells you the car is fine; and two people you trust tell you opposite things. also add one where the "speaker" is an AI chatbot, because i want to see if the quiz can handle that at all, like does that even count as testimony and does it change your answer. and one where someone tells you something and you realise youd have believed the opposite if theyd said the opposite, cause i think that case breaks alot of people's answers.

couple things i actually care about:
- every question has to be answerable by someone who doesnt know any of the labels, so no "do you think testimonial justification is inferential" type questions, ask it as a case instead
- build it from the questions first, like what separates which combinations of answers, and then put the names on the combos. if theres a combo that no real position fits, say so and say why you think nobodys there (is it incoherent? just ugly? or has nobody bothered?)
- flag any two positions the quiz cant actually tell apart, dont paper over it
- keep it for high schoolers, so short sentences, and if you have to use jargon then explain it inside the question
