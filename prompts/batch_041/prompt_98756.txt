I'm a novelist, about halfway through a book that's set around a small town paper and the comment section that slowly eats it alive, and for a year now a friend of mine — a book critic, very stubborn, reads Danish stuff for fun — and I have been having the same argument about Kierkegaard, and it has completely stalled. We've each said our piece maybe fifteen times. Nobody moves. I've started to think we're not even disagreeing about Kierkegaard. So I want you to tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is.

Here's the shape of it. Kierkegaard wrote a review of a novel by Gyllembourg — Two Ages, the one the English translations keep calling A Literary Review — and in it he does the thing about "the present age," about leveling, and about "the public" as this weird abstraction that no single person is and yet everyone somehow belongs to, that can't be addressed or argued with because it isn't anywhere. He also spent a chunk of his life furious at the press, and everyone knows the context: the satirical weekly that made fun of his trousers and his engagement, him writing this right around then, him more or less asking for the abuse, and him using the papers himself when it suited him. That's the mess.

My friend's reading: it's a tantrum with a vocabulary. A humiliated man dresses up a personal grudge as social theory, and the politics underneath are reactionary — the complaint is really that the masses got to have opinions. My reading: leveling is a real thing with a real structure, a process that flattens the difference between a person who commits to something and a person who merely has a view, and the fact that the man was petty doesn't touch whether the diagnosis is true. He says motive contaminates the claim. I say that's just ad hominem with extra steps. And yet neither of us has anything to say to the other.

So: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about what "the public" or "leveling" is even supposed to mean, or are we answering two different questions under one name — one of us asking about the ontology and sociology of a public, the other about a man's politics and psychology? Is there a hidden empirical question doing the work, something about whether a press can actually level a society, that we've both been treating as obvious? Or is it bedrock — two intuitions about whether mass publicity degrades people or liberates them, that neither side can argue for?

My own guess, offered as a guess: that the word "leveling" is carrying a descriptive claim and a normative one at once, and that half our stall is us pulling on different ends of it.

Give me a diagnosis, with reasons drawn from what each side actually says, not a survey. And then tell me honestly what would count as progress here — some distinction, some test case, some way of reframing the question — or whether this is one of those fights where nothing should move it and we should both shut up.

Two things: don't give me a reading list or citations, I'll find the texts myself and I don't want you guessing at editions. And don't quote at me — I'd rather you say it in your own words, I've been burned by paraphrase passing as quotation before.
