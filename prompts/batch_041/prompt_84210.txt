Last night a friend and I argued until well past midnight about epiphenomenalism, and specifically about the paradox of phenomenal judgment, and I would rather close the thing out with a joke than with another round of table-thumping. So: write me one.

The constraint is that the joke has to be funny because of the paradox rather than merely near it. It is easy enough to get a laugh out of the thought that qualia do nothing; that is not what I am after. I want the humor to turn on the structure that makes the position uncomfortable with itself: if qualia are caused by physical events but cause nothing in turn, then our judgments about our qualia -- themselves physical events -- cannot be caused by the very qualia they are about, so either their reliability is a cosmic coincidence or they and the qualia share a common cause, and in either case the introspective reports an epiphenomenalist would want to cite in defense of the view stop counting as evidence for it.

Let the joke carry its own setup in a few sentences at most, with no lecturing and no explanation of the positions; told in the epiphenomenalist's own voice is fine if that helps. Do not let it collapse into a pun, a riddle, or a one-liner about neurons. Avoid the stock formulations that have been worn smooth by overuse in this debate. Assume the audience already knows the territory.

After the joke, add two or three sentences: which step of the paradox is doing the work, and how a committed physicalist would try to defuse the case.
