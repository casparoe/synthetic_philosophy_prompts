A friend and I spent an evening arguing about when someone is dead, and we never located the real disagreement. Here is the pattern I want a name for. We agreed about the physiology; what we disagreed about was which verdict we needed, and each of us then reached for whichever criterion of death delivered it — one wanting the patient dead so that donation could go ahead, the other wanting them alive so that nothing irreversible had yet happened. What struck me is that once a criterion was chosen, it was defended as though it had been read off the nature of death, rather than picked for what it permitted downstream. I have met the same shape elsewhere: in arguments over what counts as a person, and in the way a legal category is stretched until a wanted outcome becomes possible, and is then treated as a discovery about the world. Is there an established name for this move? I half-remember persuasive definition, explication and conceptual engineering, but none seems quite right — explication at least owns up to stipulating, and persuasive definition is about preserving a word's emotional charge rather than quietly fixing a boundary to license a practice. I do not want a bare label. Give me the nearest established concepts, where the thing I have described differs from each, which fits best, and, if none does, a coined term with a definition and its boundary cases. Under 300 words, please.
