Health systems do the same thing when they rank treatments by cost per QALY. My friend says this is just Harsanyi's theorem with a budget. The same agencies that run this arithmetic also use a de minimis rule: an extra lifetime cancer risk of around one in a million to the most exposed person gets treated as negligible and not a reason to regulate, no matter how many millions of people are exposed to it. Tiny harms spread across millions of people get rounded to zero, while a certain death to one person gets traded away for numbers. Those two moves can't both be right, can they?
