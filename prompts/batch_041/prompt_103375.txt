I'm assembling a philosophy-department podcast series, and next week's guest is a philosopher who has long defended a dispositionalist "straight solution" to the rule-following paradox: on her view, what makes a speaker mean plus rather than some gerrymandered alternative is a fact about the speaker's dispositions, and that fact is enough to answer the skeptic without falling back on Kripke's "skeptical solution." Draft eight to ten interview questions that press the view at its joints rather than letting her restate it. I want at least one question each on (i) how the dispositionalist handles the finitude problem -- our actual dispositions are finite and occasionally lead us astray -- without covertly idealizing them into something that is no longer a disposition at all; (ii) the familiar charge that dispositions specify what a speaker would do, not what she ought to do, and therefore cannot deliver the normativity of meaning; and (iii) whether locating correctness in dispositions, whether individual or communal, makes systematic error either impossible or circular to explain. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, so that I can keep the pressure on once she reaches for the standard defenses. One further question should ask whether her straight solution genuinely displaces the skeptical solution or merely re-describes it, and please avoid anything that would let her wave a difficulty away as "Kripke's problem, not mine."
