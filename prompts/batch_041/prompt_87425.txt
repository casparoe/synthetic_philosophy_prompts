I have been asked to contribute a short position statement to a workshop on population ethics, and I want to set down my actual commitments before I write it, because the subject has stopped being abstract for me: my partner and I are deciding whether to try for a second child, and a genetic counsellor has told us the child would have a substantial chance of a condition that would make life hard, though in her judgement still worth living. I would like you to audit the package below for consistency.

1. I treat the repugnant conclusion as a genuine reductio. A world containing very many people whose lives are only barely worth living is not better than a small world of flourishing people, and I think any view that implies otherwise has gone wrong somewhere.

2. About betterness I am a person-affecting theorist. When I compare two outcomes, what matters to me is how things go for the particular people who live in them; an outcome does not become worse merely by containing fewer people, or fewer happy people, than its rival.

3. About bringing people into existence I hold the asymmetry. There is a genuine reason not to create someone who would suffer, and that reason is about the person who would suffer rather than about any knock-on effects on others; but the prospect of a happy life is not in itself a reason to create someone. I also think a life can be good for the person living it without its being a good thing, in any impersonal sense, that the person exists.

4. I take the betterness relation to be transitive, and I do not think a comparison should depend on the order in which outcomes are presented or on the description under which we compare them.

5. On our own case: I believe it would be permissible for us to conceive a child whose life would be hard but genuinely worthwhile, even though the hardship is a real mark against doing so.

I have the uneasy sense that at least two of these cannot be held together and that I cannot see which, or how much it would cost to fix. Please tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be abandoned or weakened and what that would cost. If a single revision would clear up the most, say which and what it leaves behind. I would rather have the uncomfortable answer than a reassurance that the whole set is fine.
