I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and I need a two-page research proposal. My half-formed idea is below and I'd like you to turn it into something that reads like a real proposal.

The setup. Suppose two parties negotiate on behalf of other people whose preferences nobody at the table actually knows: two AI assistants each acting for a different user, two delegates at a treaty negotiation who don't know how much their governments really value the disputed territory, a union and a firm where the members' valuations are private. There's a notion I keep running into — I've seen it called a "safe Pareto improvement," but I don't trust my memory of how the people who use the term define it, so just work from this description: an outcome that makes every party better off than the status quo baseline for every possible profile of preferences the parties might have, not merely for the profile that happens to be actual. The appeal is obvious: it's an improvement you can sign onto without knowing what anybody wants.

What I suspect is wrong with the usual way this gets framed. It seems to bundle together two kinds of uncertainty that come apart: uncertainty about what the parties want (their preferences, or "types"), and uncertainty about what they will do (whether the other side can commit, whether the deal survives once types are revealed, whether the protocol is exploitable by someone who misreports, what happens off the equilibrium path). Robustness to the first is treated as if it gives you robustness to the second for free, and I don't think it does. My guess is that once you seperate them, the set of deals that qualify shrinks a lot, and in some cases the only survivors are outcomes you'd have gotten from a much more demanding condition anyway — something like worst-case-over-types plus an incentive-compatibility requirement, which is a different and much stronger thing.

What I need from you: a two-page proposal on this. Concretely:

- State the question so that it has a determinate answer. Something like: is robustness to uncertainty about preferences sufficient for robustness to uncertainty about what the parties will do, or are these independent requirements that come apart in cases you can spell out?
- Explain why the existing treatments of this idea leave the question open. Describe the positions and arguments; don't cite anything, I'll add the citations myself.
- Give me the method, month by month, for a year. My instinct is that this is mostly formal work with small toy models — two-player bargaining over a finite type space, say — plus at least one case that isn't a bargaining game at all, so the result doesn't look like an artifact of one setup. Tell me what I'd actually be doing in months seven through nine, not just "writing up."
- Say what a positive result would look like, what a negative result would look like, and what would count as the project failing. I'd definately rather know that now.
- Include one fully worked toy example in the proposal itself, because a proposal that only says "these might come apart" is going to look vacuous to a reviewer.
- Be honest about scope. Two pages for a year is generous; tell me what I'd have to cut if I only got six months, and which components I could drop without gutting the thing.

No reading list, no bibliography. Write it in American English and keep the tone like a person wrote it, not like a grant template.
