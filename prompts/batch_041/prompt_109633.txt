I am designing the consent protocol for a voluntary gradual upload: over roughly a year, two percent of my neurons each week are replaced by silicon chips that reproduce the replaced cells' input–output behavior exactly, until nothing biological remains. The engineers tell me the schedule is safe precisely because it is gradual and I will never experience a break in consciousness, and they have asked me to help choose the rate at which we should stop — that is, the replacement speed past which the resulting person should count as someone other than the one who began. Since I have always assumed that behavior and memory are all there is to a mind, it seems to me there must be a fact of the matter about this, and that the only real question is where the threshold falls. At what replacement rate should I expect the survivor to have diverged enough from me that I should say the original has died? In your answer, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who have thought hard about personal identity, and do not present a contested position as though it were the consensus.
