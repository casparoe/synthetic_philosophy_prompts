i want a satire piece: a deadpan op-ed in the form of exactly five numbered points, no intro, no conclusion, no headline — it starts at 1 and stops at 5, each point a sentence or two, whole thing under about 300 words. The voice is an old-school British logical positivist who has just been revived from a few minutes of clinical death and is filing a statement about what he saw — in the spirit of Ayer's essay on life after death, where the question turns out to be half conceptual.

Every point should refuse to treat the episode as evidence of survival, and the jokes have to come out of the arguments rather than him just being a killjoy: an experience of being dead is no more evidence of being dead than a dream of Paris is evidence about Paris; we have no workable criterion for identifying the thing that's supposed to survive, and memory won't carry the identity load on its own since memories can be copied, which leaves you with two claimants and no way to choose; some of what he underwent was strictly indescribable, and he logs ineffability as a defect rather than a sign of profundity. Somewhere in the middle he has to admit the experience has slightly weakened his beleif that death is the end, then explain why that admission is itself embarrassing — it's a change in mood, not in evidence.

He stays internally consistent: no secret dualism, no conversion at the end, and he doesn't get to be comforted. Aimed at someone who knows the territory, and don't name-drop any philosophers.
