ok so in basically every alignment argument I end up in, someone defends iterated amplification with the same little picture. it's a company. the model is a worker, amplification hires more copies of the worker, a manager (also the model) splits the task and hands out the pieces, the pieces come back and get aggregated up the chain, and it scales the way a firm scales. then distillation is the punchline: you fire everyone and promote the manager, meaning you train one fresh model to imitate what the whole company did. and this is supposed to be safe because the student only ever sees the teacher's outputs, so it literally cant come out with goals the company didnt have. other people skip the company entirely and just say distillation is lossy compression of the teacher, which sounds even more obviously fine.

I want an audit of how far that actually holds. start with the correspondence table, worker, manager, org chart, hiring, firing, paychecks, whatever you can get to map, and be upfront about what doesnt. then the break points. the workers here are copies sharing weights, not employees with their own agendas. a real firm keeps its knowledge in people and in docs, not in weights. firing a firm and making one person do all of it would obviously lose most of what the firm knew, so why isnt distillation just that. and a compressed file comes with a decoder that reconstructs it and fails loudly when its corrupted, where a student model fails quietly and coherently. sort those into the disanalogies that actually threaten the safety claim and the ones that are just decoration.

the real question is the conclusion: that distillation is the boring safe part and all the risk lives in amplification. does that survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the company picture quietly doing all the work, since a firms alignment comes from stuff, incentives, oversight, the fact that a worker can quit or blow the whistle, that a distilled model simply doesnt have? if the analogy is bad, whats a better one for what distillation actually does to a policy. no citations, I just want the argument.
