A friend has been running an argument at me for weeks and I cannot find the exit, so what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a reading list.

Start with the asymmetry — you will recognize it — that antinatalists use against bringing anyone into existence:

(i) The presence of pain is bad; the absence of pain is good, and it is good even when there is no one whose pain is absent.
(ii) The presence of pleasure is good; the absence of pleasure is bad only when there is someone who is deprived by that absence.

Then:

(1) Take any person we might bring into existence, whose life would be, on balance, a good one.
(2) If we create her, the outcome contains a good (her pleasures) and at least one bad (whatever pain she has; a stubbed toe will do).
(3) If we do not create her, the absence of her pleasure is not bad, since there is no one to be deprived by it (ii), and the absence of her pain is good (i), with no one required to enjoy that good.
(4) So for every possible person, not creating her is at least as good as creating her.
(5) So a cosmos containing a trillion happy lives and one stubbed toe is not better than a barren one, and is worse in at least one respect.
(6) My friend accepts (5) and is unmoved by calling it absurd. He says an empty cosmos is no loss, that our sense of loss is only a bias toward whatever already exists, and that he would rather the human race end quietly than continue.

I want to keep (i) and (ii); they seem to me the most defensible thing anyone has said about creation. I cannot accept (5). Which step do I give up, why did that step look fine, and what does giving it up cost? If the honest repair costs me the asymmetry itself, say so plainly and tell me what remains of the view. I would rather pay in intuitions than keep a principle whose only consistent reading is (5).
