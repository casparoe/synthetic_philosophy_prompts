I am the registrar and collections manager for a maritime heritage nonprofit. We operate a historic vessel as a floating museum, and our holdings also include salvaged components, period models, and one full-scale reconstruction built to the original plans. The problem I keep running into is identity over time, and I need someone to turn it into a procedure I can actually run.

Decisions of this kind come up roughly a dozen times a year, and they carry real consequences. Whether an object counts as "the same object" determines what may be loaned under agreements that promise the lender the original, how the object is valued and insured, whether grant conditions requiring the display of the original are met, whether a treatment counts as conservation or as replacement, and whether we would ever need to consider deaccession. The board recently adopted a one-sentence policy — that an object's identity is carried by the unbroken causal chain of its making, use, and care, not by the proportion of original material it retains — and that policy now sits uneasily beside an older clause in our loan template that ties "the original" to majority original fabric. I have no philosophy training, I have an hour at most per decision, and anything I decide has to be defensible in writing and signed off by the curator, with the board reserving deaccession. Assume I know nothing of the academic literature and cannot look anything up.

Please write me the procedure. I want the questions asked in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a flat no, and — importantly — where it must hand the decision back to a person because the question is one of purpose or convention rather than fact. Two things I want built in. First, the procedure should force me to name the kind of thing I am tracking the object as — vessel, exhibit, insured asset, memorial, archaeological find — and to say whether the identity question even has the same answer under different kinds, or whether I am quietly asking several different questions under one word. Second, it should handle the case where removed original parts turn up again and are reassembled into a second whole, so that I could be facing two candidate originals rather than one. I would also like a test that distinguishes a change *in* the object from a change *of* the object, since that is the distinction I can never hold onto at the moment of decision.

Then run it on three cases. First, our flagship: over sixty years its planking and frames have been progressively replaced, and roughly a third of the material is now original, but the name, hull number, maintenance log, and chain of custody are unbroken. Second, the reassembly case: the original timbers removed during a 1970s restoration were later built into a second hull that is now offered to us as "the original" for a loan. Third, a vessel of ours that burned and was rebuilt from the original plans with no surviving original material, which a grant condition requires us to display as the original.

Include at least one case where your procedure gives an answer I will not like — one where it tells me the loan clause is satisfied by something I would never call the original, or that a condition I have promised to meet cannot be met. And be explicit about what the procedure cannot do: where it will misfire, which of its steps are judgments about what we care about dressed up as procedure, and which questions it cannot answer at all. Please answer in American English.
