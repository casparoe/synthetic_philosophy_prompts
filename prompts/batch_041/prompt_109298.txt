My grans 100th is in three weeks and my mum has voluntold me to do the toast. It's a big do — gran had six kids and there are something like seventy of us now, so the whole thing is basically a tribute to multiplication, and everyone will be expecting the 'look what she started' speech. I want to give her that, she'd love it.

Here's my problem. I'm 38 and I've decided I'm not having kids, and my mother has been on at me about it for a decade, so a few months back I went looking for something to say back to her, read Parfit, and now I'm stuck. Because the obvious warm thing — every one of us is here because of her, and that's good, more is better — is the repugnant conclusion in a party hat. And I can't take the other road either, because 'what matters is the quality of the lives, not the number' sounds fine in a book and is completely unsayable at a 100th: it's me ranking my cousins and telling my aunties they maybe shouldn't have been born.

So I keep landing between two places. Add everyone's happiness up and you get 'have more, keep going, a trillion mildly happy people beats us'. Average it and you get 'the party would be better if two thirds of us weren't here'. And the non-identity stuff doesn't get me out either, because I can't even say she did us a favour — the version of me that wasn't born isn't sitting somewhere worse off. Also, honestly, the version of the argument that actually gets to me isn't the trillion-people one, it's the small one: is a big family of fairly happy people better than a small one of very happy people? That's not a thought experiment, thats my family. I think the real fork is whether you're asking how good the world is or whether anyone is actually better off, and I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just a way of hiding the problem.

So: write the toast. Four minutes, warm, a bit funny, something gran and my aunties will actually enjoy — almost nobody in the room has heard of any of this and they shouldn't have to. If the philosophy shows at all it should be in what the toast refuses to say, not in a lecture. Then a short bit at the end telling me what you had to leave out and why, because I still don't know where I land.

Oh and my cousin's doing a PhD in philosophy and gran is sharper than all of us, so nothing sloppy.
