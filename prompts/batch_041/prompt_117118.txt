ok so for my ethics class we have to write a satire piece about a "repugnant market" (thats the unit — kidney sales, price gouging after a hurricane, selling your vote, all that) and I want to do mine as a company FAQ / help center page, you know how those are always cheerful no matter how grim the thing is.

The company is made up (something like "RenalFreedom" or "NephroShare," you pick) — a startup running a marketplace where people can sell a kidney. Write it as their support page, 500-600 words, headings, bullet points, the whole deal, totally deadpan, never winking at the camera. The target is the view that the only real objection to these markets is squeamishness — that "ick" is never a reason, and if you cant point to a concrete harm you should just let the market run.

The funny has to come from the actual arguments and not from making the company evil. So somewhere in there it should deal with: the blood donation thing (the worry that paying for something crowds out the people who'd have given it for free, so paying might actually shrink the supply — then a FAQ answer that "handles" it); the "we already pay people to do dangerous jobs, so whats different" point, plus the comeback that a kidney isnt a shift you clock out of; the rationing question (waiting list vs price — someone always gets rationed, but the FAQ should talk like price isnt rationing); and at least one answer where the company leans on "repugnance is just a signal that something needs explaining" in order to wave off a customer who says the whole thing is wrong. Maybe also an "are you guys exploiting poor people" answer that is way too smooth. Dont name real philosophers or cite anyone, and dont make the company cartoonishly evil — the scariest version is the one that sounds reasonable.

At the end, separate from the FAQ itself, give me a short note (3-4 sentences) on where the parody takes the biggest liberties with the real arguments, and be honest about what would change your mind — like which argument, if it actually went through, would make you think "ok the market defenders are right and this isnt a real problem after all."
