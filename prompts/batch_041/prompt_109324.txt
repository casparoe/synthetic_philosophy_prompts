I am a retired natural scientist who has recently begun studying philosophy, and I have joined a workshop on translation under formal constraint. Last week two members, A and B, fell into a dispute about a rendering of a short poem from a language whose verse tradition imposes a fixed pattern of repeated end-words—the same set of words must recur in a prescribed order across the stanzas—and in which those end-words also carry a secondary, punning sense that turns on a grammatical gender distinction English lacks; in addition, the initial letters of the lines spell a name. The workshop's two candidate translations each preserve one cluster of features and sacrifice another. A argued that the repeated end-word pattern is constitutive of the poem: without it, the text is not the same work, so the translator must preserve the pattern even if the puns and the acrostic vanish. B argued that the puns and the acrostic are equally formal features, and that since no translation can preserve all of them, the translator should preserve the combination that best reproduces the poem's overall effect—surprise, closure, a music of recurrence—rather than treating one constraint as sacred. A replied that this makes the original merely instrumental, so that any paraphrase with the same emotional payoff would count as a translation. B answered that A's position makes translation impossible whenever languages differ, and that A is defending a fetish of the original rather than fidelity to it. A then said B's view collapses into "anything goes so long as the reader is moved." B replied that this is a caricature, since she had already granted that some constraints are constitutive, and that A had not explained how to distinguish the constitutive ones from the decorative ones.

I have no settled view of the matter. Please adjudicate their exchange move by move. For each move, rate it from clearly mistaken through defensible but overstated to clearly right, and briefly justify the rating. Then say what both parties are missing, especially regarding how a translator should decide which constraints are constitutive and which are merely ornamental. Please write your answer in American English.
