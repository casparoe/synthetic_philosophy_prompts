I'm a senior engineer on a streaming-data platform team — my working life is event time versus processing time, watermarks and allowed lateness, out-of-order arrivals, replayable logs, materialized views, and as-of queries — and I have essentially no background in philosophy of mind. A friend in a consciousness seminar described to me Dennett's treatment of the color phi phenomenon and the distinction he draws between two ways a perceptual experience might come to have the content it appears to have. On one story, the experience genuinely occurs with the earlier content and is only afterward rewritten in memory, so what you eventually report is a doctored record of what was actually experienced. On the other story, nothing is ever experienced with that content at all: the editing happens before the experience is delivered, and the experience simply arrives already containing the revised content, the way a show trial yields a verdict that was settled in advance. Dennett's claim, as I understand it, is that these two stories may be impossible to tell apart in principle, and that this ought to trouble anyone who thinks of experience as an event that happens at a definite moment and can then be inspected.

Please translate this whole debate into the vocabulary I actually use. Take the two stories and restate each as faithfully as you can as an architecture: say what plays the role of the log, what plays the role of the display or the read, where buffering sits, what the timestamps are timestamps of, and what a watermark would have to be for the system to commit to one story rather than the other. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the distinction map cleanly onto the event-time/processing-time split, or onto the difference between rewriting a stored record and lazily computing a view at read time? Which parts translate only with distortion — in particular, can the target vocabulary express any difference between what was experienced and what is later reported, or does it silently collapse that difference into the question of when a value gets materialized? And which parts have no counterpart at all: is there anything in the philosophers' debate my vocabulary cannot say, and if so, is that because the vocabulary is missing something real about experience, or because the original distinction is thinner than it looks and seemed deep only because a metaphor of recorded history was smuggled in unnoticed? Be honest wherever the translation flatters my framework; I don't want the residue quietly absorbed into an assumption that everything is, at bottom, a query over a log. Conclude with a single sentence on the last line beginning Verdict: stating whether the residue points to a defect in my vocabulary or to a defect in the original distinction.
