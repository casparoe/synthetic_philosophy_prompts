Ok so I write about economics and tech for a fairly general audience — the kind of reader who'll sit through a 4,000 word feature on why everyone's suddenly talking about central planning again. I'm doing a piece on the old socialist calculation debate (Mises and Hayek vs Lange and co), and the reason it's back is obviously the AI/big-data thing, so I really dont want to just rehash "Hayek said knowledge is dispersed." What I want is a flowchart / taxonomy that lets readers figure out where they actually land in this argument, not which team they root for.

Build it as a tree where every branch is a question that splits the positions. Start somewhere like "can a planning agency in principle know what to produce," and let the branches do the real work — is the obstacle one of principle or one of practice; does the relevant knowledge even exist in a form that could be collected; do prices have to emerge from actual buying and selling, or can they be set and corrected by trial and error; is "the market" just a pricing mechanism or is it a discovery process that generates the knowledge in the first place; and whatever else genuinely separates the views.

Each leaf should be a named position where there's a name for it (Mises-style impossibility, the Hayek-style discovery / tacit knowledge line, Lange-style market socialism, the "big data makes it moot" crowd, negotiated or participatory planning without prices, etc.), or "unnamed" if nobody really defends that combination — plus one line on the most awkward thing that position has to swallow.

Two things I really need from you: (1) tell me which combinations of answers are just empty, i.e. nobody's there, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, ugly, or just nobody's bothered; and (2) if two leaves end up giving the same answer on every question, say so rather than pretending the tree separates them.

Keep it to something like 6–9 leaves, and phrase every question so a reader can answer it without already knowing the labels. I'm not a philosopher, so dont turn it into a seminar, but also dont dumb it down — my readers know what a price signal is, they just haven't read the literature.
