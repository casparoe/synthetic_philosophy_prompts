Something about insider trading has been bothering me and I can't get it straight in my head. The reason I'm supposed to care about stock prices is that they summarise what loads of people know seperately — some old essay I read once made this point, about prices doing a job no single person could do, because nobodys got the whole picture. But the way that's supposed to work is that whoever finds something out buys or sells before the news is public, the price moves, and the rest of us learn it from the price without ever being told. When the information came from inside the company, that's just insider trading, and it's a crime. Meanwhile a hedge fund can pay researchers to count the cars in shop car parks, or ring round suppliers, or piece together scraps until they've worked out something the company hasn't announced yet, and that's fine. So what is the rule actually commited to? That information has an owner, so the wrong is about where it came from rather than whether it's true or reliable? Or that the market is a fair race, which seems like a different picture of what a market is even for than the one where prices pool knowledge? Can one rule serve both, or is the law quietly presupposing one while telling itself it's the other?

And the bit that really confuses me: someone told me there's a theorem showing that if the market is already sitting at an efficient equilibrium, and it's common knowledge how traders get their information, then it doesn't matter that some of them know things others don't — nobody can profit, because putting in an offer gives away what you know before anyone accepts, so nobody accepts. Which would mean that the level playing field the law seems to want, taken all the way, is a market where nobody trades at all — so no prices, and nothing to learn from them. Am I joining those two things up wrong?

I don't want a reading list. I want to know what picture of knowledge, and of what markets are for, this rule only makes sense under, whether its parts really do add up to one picture or it's licensed by several, and what would have to change if we swapped in the other one.
