ok so i need a joke. or a few jokes. about Kant and the murderer at the door — but not the version you'd get off a summary page. i want the jokes to actually turn on what's in that 1797 essay where he answers Constant about whether you can lie out of benevolence, plus the stuff in the Doctrine of Virtue about veracity. the point is that someone who's read the essay and a bit of the secondary lit should laugh, and someone who's read neither should still be able to follow it.

the handles i want you to be able to pull on, because i think this is where the funny actually is:

- his worry is truthfulness, not truth. you can be honestly mistaken and that's not a lie; what's forbidden is intending to deceive in your declaration. which makes the usual escape hatches — refusing to answer at all, or saying something true and useless while hoping the guy draws the wrong conclusion — live questions instead of obviously fine.
- the duty of veracity is supposed to be unconditional, a duty of right, not something you get to weigh against your friend's life. no collision of duties, nothing to balance. the friend never gets put on the scale. some people find that sublime, most find it insane, and i'd like the jokes to be aware of both.
- he thinks a lie always wrongs somebody: if not the person you lied to, then humanity in general, since it eats away at the trust in declarations that contracts and rights depend on. so the murderer, who has no right to your truth, is still somehow wronged by your lie. i want at least one joke that notices how strange it is to be forbidden to wrong a man who is on his way to stab someone.
- the consequences thing, which i think is the weirdest part: tell the truth and the murderer kills your friend, and it's not on you. lie, and he wanders off and kills somebody else, and now you're answerable for that death — your lie made you responsible for a world you couldn't see. that asymmetry is a goldmine, please use it.
- and the universalizability gap: people say the categorical imperative forbids the lie, but write the maxim properly (something like: i'll lie when a murderer asks me where his intended victim is) and it seems to universalize without any contradiction at all. so the real work is being done by the separate duty of veracity, not by the CI test. one joke can be about that gap.
