I'm a novelist, and for several years I've been circling a distinction I can feel but can't name, and I suspect it already has a name in a literature I don't know.

Here is what I keep noticing. Certain people seem able to try things out in advance — to run a situation forward in their heads and let the bad versions die there instead of in the world. But when I look closely, the ability is nearly always attached to an object or a notation. The clinician I based a character on is superb at catching the rare presentation, but only with the checklist on the desk in front of her; without it she guesses, and her guesses are no better than mine. A student of mine revises beautifully when he reads his pages aloud and cannot revise at all in silence. Take the instrument away and what remains is not a slightly worse version of the same skill. It is a different and much older way of getting along: try it, see what happens, try it again.

Then there is the opposite case, which is what really puzzles me. A boatbuilder I knew could do the whole thing internally — he could feel the hull before it existed, and he made almost no false starts. He was extraordinary, and he could not teach it. Every apprentice of his learned by ruining wood, and the gift died with him.

So the capacity to learn without acting seems to come in two forms that look identical from the outside: one that lives in a person and cannot be handed on, and one that lives in a tool, can be handed on, and can also be taken away. That makes me distrust the ordinary way we rank people by how much they can work out in advance, because the ranking looks like it is measuring what is on the desk rather than who is sitting at it.

Is there an established term for this? I don't mean tool use in general; crows and children use tools. I mean the specific case in which the simulating capacity itself is supplied by the artefact rather than merely assisted by it, together with the fall that follows when the artefact goes. If a term exists, tell me what it covers and what it leaves out. If none exists, coin one and define it, and tell me where the line falls between a skill that is genuinely someone's own and a skill that is only being rented — including the hard cases, like a surgeon who is nothing without her team, or me without my notebooks. I would rather have the boundaries than the label.

Please answer in continuous prose: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — the way you would write a letter.
