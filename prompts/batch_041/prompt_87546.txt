ok so for my history class we read this essay about three ways people relate to the past — monumental, antiquarian, and critical — and now I have to write something satirical for a fake magazine. Can you write a deadpan product review (think Wirecutter, very serious, no winking) of a subscription service that promises to give you "your history, three ways," with three monthly plans named after the three approaches? Around 550 words. The reviewer tests each plan for a month and gives it a rating, and all the jokes have to come from stuff that's actually true about each mode: monumental history as inspiration you can really use, except it quietly turns into myth and ends up making you want to smash things for the cause; antiquarian history that lovingly preserves every receipt and family scrap until you're so buried in the past you can't do anything; critical history that puts the past on trial and frees you from it, except you can't tell where the guilt stops or who you even are afterward. Please don't have the reviewer explain the philosophy or name any philosopher or book — let the service's own marketing copy and the reviewer's experience carry it, so someone who's never heard any of this still laughs and someone who has feels like you actually got the three modes right. The thing being made fun of is treating your whole relationship to the past as a consumer choice you can optimize. End with the reviewer picking a plan, and make the choice feel like it's actually the hard question and not a punchline. Casual voice, no citations.
