I have been arguing with a close friend about a distinction I can feel but cannot draw, and what I want from you is a number rather than a survey. The case is this. I direct a substantial share of my charitable giving to organizations working in my own country, and when I try to imagine sending the same money to strangers abroad whose needs are at least as urgent, the prospect produces a resistance I have never managed to argue myself out of. I have looked hard at the attitude's causal history (I grew up here, the images are familiar, the faces resemble mine), I can see that it is sensitive to features I would not, on reflection, want to call morally relevant, and I still endorse it rather than disavow it. My friend says this is in-group bias wearing the costume of a preference, and that my inability to repudiate it is precisely what bias looks like from the inside. I am about 0.3 that she is right; she is about 0.75. Give me your own credence that the attitude is a bias rather than a permissible preference, and then show me the machinery.

Please make the decomposition explicit rather than gestural. Among the terms I would want to see: the probability that the correct account of what makes an attitude a bias is an error account (the attitude rests on a false belief about the case), a distortion account (the attitude is responsive to normatively irrelevant features, so its sensitivity profile is the defect), or a laundering account (an attitude is a bias when the agent would disavow it upon understanding its social function, so that non-disavowal is neither necessary nor sufficient); your probability for each account being the right one; your probability that the attitude is a bias conditional on each; and how those combine into your final figure. I would also like your probability that sincere, stable, reflective non-disavowal is dispositive — that an attitude the agent cannot bring herself to repudiate is for that reason alone not a bias — and your judgment about whether that claim, rather than anything about the attitude itself, is where the disagreement between my friend and me actually lives.
