I keep going back and forth on where the line falls between a disease and just a bodily state, and I want to know what principle my gut is actually running on. Here are my verdicts. A broken leg: disease, and I don't care that people want to call it an injury instead. Fibromyalgia, where there's real pain and disability but nobody can point to damaged tissue: definately a disease. Being deaf in a signing community where everyone around you signs and it costs you nothing: not a disease, even though the cochlea still isn't doing its job. Male pattern baldness, which plenty of men would pay a fortune to reverse: not a disease. Lactose intolerance in a dairy-heavy country, where it means cramps after every bowl of cereal: disease; the exact same gut in a herding culture with no dairy around: not a disease. A harmless oddity like synesthesia: not a disease. Carrying one copy of a recessive gene with no symptoms: not a disease. The things that trip me up are that I'll happily call something a disease when the only obvious problem is suffering (fibromyalgia), but not when the only obvious problem is that people dislike it (baldness), and that the same body can flip from disease to not depending on what's in the fridge. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give one up? And what does your principle say about the case I can't settle either way, aging itself, which is universal, looks like a slow breakdown, and that most people would jump at the chance to cure. Please answer as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion, with the last one being whichever of my verdicts you think has to go.
