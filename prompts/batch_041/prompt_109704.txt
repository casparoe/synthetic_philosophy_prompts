I am a doctoral student in Buddhist philosophy working on comparative logic, and I want a close comparison of two multi-cornered predication schemes that are usually lumped together as Indian non-classical logics. The first is the four-cornered pattern (catuṣkoṭi) as Nāgārjuna deploys it: for any predicate, the options are P, not-P, both P and not-P, and neither P nor not-P, each corner rejected in turn, with the further twist that Madhyamaka disclaims any thesis of its own, the corners themselves being as empty as whatever they were meant to capture. The second is the Jain sevenfold predication (saptabhaṅgī): under the qualifier syāt (roughly, in some respect), a thing is said to be, not to be, both, to be indescribable, and then each of the first three combined with the indescribable, the indescribable being what is left when affirmation and denial are demanded of the same thing at once and no single expression will carry both.

Compare them along four axes, in prose. First, whether the fourth corner of the tetralemma and the Jain indescribable are doing the same work or different work: a further semantic value, a limit of language, or a refusal to answer. Second, what kind of negation each scheme trades in, in particular the contrast between a bare, non-implicative denial that commits to nothing and an implicational negation that presupposes some positive alternative. Third, whether either scheme is a genuine revision of classical logic, in respect of bivalence, excluded middle and non-contradiction, or only a way of marking where predication runs out. Fourth, how much of each is driven by its soteriology: many-sidedness as a realist metaphysics of partial standpoints, as against emptiness as the therapeutic dissolution of all views. Conclude by saying which of the two, if either, earns the title of a logic, and which is the more defensible treatment of the limits of expression.

Then give me a table with the columns Dimension, Catuṣkoṭi, Saptabhaṅgī and Verdict, one row per dimension, using exactly these five: what the fourth or indescribable corner does; the type of negation at work; whether the scheme asserts any thesis of its own; its relation to bivalence and non-contradiction; its soteriological role. Under Verdict, put in a clause which scheme is stronger on that dimension and why. Finish with a single sentence naming the point of divergence that matters most.

Write in British English.
