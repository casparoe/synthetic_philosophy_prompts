I am a retired natural scientist who has spent the past several years reading political philosophy, and for some time I have followed the case-by-case verdicts of a philosopher of my acquaintance on questions of inheritance and equal opportunity. Her judgments have been remarkably consistent. She holds that a fortune amassed by someone who founded and personally ran a business is just to pass on, however large, and she would not tax the transfer. She holds that the same sum, had it come instead from a winning lottery ticket, is unjust to pass on untaxed. She holds that a gain that accrues to its holder through a market boom he neither foresaw nor chose is unjust to keep untaxed. She holds that a large gift freely given to a stranger is just. And she holds that a trust sheltering a family fortune for generations who did nothing to earn it is unjust.

Next month she must pronounce on a new case: a surgeon who built and sold a large practice leaves her estate in a generation-skipping trust for grandchildren she never met, with a clause denying them any control over the principal.

I would like you, acting as a careful political philosopher, to reason from her track record rather than from your own convictions. Before answering, brainstorm the candidate principles that could jointly generate her earlier verdicts — consider at least luck egalitarianism, a libertarian entitlement view, and a desert- or labor-based view, along with any other you find plausible — and determine which of them her verdicts genuinely pin down. Then predict her verdict on the surgeon's trust under each surviving principle, state which prediction you would stake most on and why, and identify the single further case that would best discriminate between the principles still in contention.

Present the result as a concise prose analysis followed by a JSON object with exactly these fields: "candidate_principles" (array of objects, each with "name", "statement", and "fits_verdicts" as a boolean); "predicted_verdict_under_each_principle" (array of objects with "principle" and "prediction"); "best_bet" (object with "principle", "prediction", "confidence_0_to_1", and "reason"); "closest_precedent" (string); "discriminating_case" (string); and "where_the_track_record_is_silent" (string).
