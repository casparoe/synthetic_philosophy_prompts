hey, for my philosophy class we have to write a satire piece and i want to do kant's universalizability test but not the "is lying always wrong" version that everyone does. my idea: a company with a Maxim Review Board, basically an ethics-compliance hotline where you have to submit the maxim of whatever you want to do and they check whether it could be a universal law. the board is completely sincere and runs the procedure correctly, so all the absurdity has to come from real features of the test — like how the same action can be worded as a bunch of different maxims so the verdict flips depending on who fills out the form, and how the board distinguishes a maxim that can't even be conceived as a universal law from one you could conceive but couldn't will, and uses that distinction to justify whatever it had already decided. write it as exactly five of the board's rulings, numbered, each one or two sentences, deadpan corporate-legal voice, no intro and no wrap-up. include at least one where a really narrow maxim lets through something the board obviously finds gross, one where a really broad maxim bans something completely normal, and one where somebody tries to re-word their maxim after getting rejected. it should be funny to someone whos read the groundwork and still followable for my classmates who havent.
