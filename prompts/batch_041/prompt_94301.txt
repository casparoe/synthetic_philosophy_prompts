In Bayesian confirmation theory, evidence E confirms hypothesis H just in case conditioning on E raises H's probability: P(H|E) > P(H). This creates a puzzle when E is old—when it is already known with certainty. For then P(E) = 1, conditioning changes nothing, and a fact we have possessed all along can never confirm anything. And yet such facts appear to confirm: an anomaly that observers had recorded for decades seems to lend support to a new theory that predicts it, exactly at the moment the theory is devised.

Consider a case of this kind that the usual discussions pass over. A single investigator has known for thirty years that a particular marker is present in one of her patients, and has always treated it as an incidental nuisance. She then formulates a new theory of the disease from which the presence of that marker follows deductively, and at once reports that the marker confirms the theory. A colleague objects: since she already knew the marker was present, the new theory cannot have supplied her with new evidence for itself, and any rise in her confidence must therefore be irrational—or at best a merely psychological effect.

Please argue for the following position: the colleague is mistaken, but the confirmation at issue is not to be found in any change in the investigator's degrees of belief. The support is real and objective; what the new theory accomplishes is to bring a standing body of evidence into a relation of support that held all along but could not be represented before the theory existed. In making the argument, do the following. First, explain what the notion of representing a support relation is doing that the ordinary Bayesian apparatus cannot do. Second, state precisely what changes when the theory is formulated and what does not. Third, confront the strongest objection you can devise—that the alleged objective support is a fiction, because a relation of support can only relate representations an agent actually possesses, and before the theory was formulated the investigator possessed no representation of it. Argue the position on its merits rather than by cataloguing what others have said.
