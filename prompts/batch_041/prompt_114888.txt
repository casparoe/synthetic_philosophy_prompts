I'm in the third year of a philosophy PhD, working mainly in semantics and pragmatics, and I have a two-year fellowship starting in the autumn that is meant to produce the core of a dissertation. My interests have drifted toward pejoratives and slurs, and I've been talking regularly with a friend in NLP who builds content-moderation classifiers, so I keep oscillating between the very theoretical end of the topic — whether the derogatory element of a slur belongs to the word's truth-conditional content, to a conventional implicature, to a presupposition, or to a speech act of subordination — and questions that seem to bear on what a classifier ought to do: reclaimed in-group uses, quotation and mere mention, slurs under negation, in conditionals, in questions, and inside reported speech, and the fact that some group terms are plainly insults without being slurs while others are slurs without being insults. When I write the list out, I feel like I am reciting a standard menu rather than thinking about what matters, and that is what I want you to help me fix.

Treat the fellowship as the unit of effort: two years, one person, my toolkit (formal semantics, some corpus methods, no experimental lab, plus a collaborator who can run classifier experiments), and one aim — work that both philosophers of language and the people building moderation systems would have to take seriously. Which open questions in the semantics and pragmatics of slurs matter most, in what order, and why? I don't need a bibliography or a reading list; I know the standard positions well enough that summarizing them is no longer useful to me. What I need is a defended agenda. For each question, say why it is upstream of the others rather than downstream, how tractable it is for someone with my tools, whether an answer would actually change anything outside the seminar room, and which questions you would drop even though they are popular, because resolving them would not constrain much else. Then tell me what could happen in the next two years — a corpus finding, a classifier result, a cross-linguistic pattern — that would move a question up or down your ranking.

One thing has been nagging me, and I would like you to treat it as a candidate axis rather than as a settled distinction. I keep wanting to separate the derogation carried by a word's standing meaning from the derogation carried by the fact that a particular speaker is not licensed to use it. On one picture, reclamation is a change of meaning; on another, it is a change in who may say the word, with the meaning intact — which would explain why the older and the newer uses seem to coexist in the same community, and why out-group speakers who adopt the reclaimed form still often get it wrong. If that distinction is real, it should reorganize the agenda, since some open questions would turn out to be about the lexicon and others about norms of use; if it is not real, I want to know why not, because that would tell me which questions I have been treating as substantive are artifacts of running two things together. Please take a definite position rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me: give me your ranking, the reasons for it, your confidence in it, and the strongest objection to your own ordering.
