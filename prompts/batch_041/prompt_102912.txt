You are an expert in epistemology and philosophy of science. Before writing, brainstorm the strongest version of each position. Then write a dialogue between a Humean and a Bayesian about a network of 10,000 independent sensors, each with a documented false-positive rate of one in a million, all reporting the same event, where the event would violate a well-confirmed physical law. The Humean holds that no accumulation of testimony or instrument reports can make it rational to believe a violation of a law of nature; the Bayesian holds that sufficiently strong independent likelihoods can overcome any finite prior. Make both interlocutors equally competent and have them respond to each other's actual points. They should address whether the prior probability of a law violation is well-defined, whether the sensors are genuinely independent, whether a miracle means a violation of law or merely an event with very low antecedent probability, and whether the dispute is about rational belief or about the meaning of the claim. Do not use quotations or citations. At most 700 words. No stage directions; each speaker gets roughly equal time, and end with one sentence from each stating what would change their mind.
