I am a professor of philosophy, and I serve as an appointed member of the steering group of the national citizens' assembly our government convened to reconsider the terms of our country's membership in the European Union. The plenary votes next month on a single package of recommendations, and before Thursday's steering-group meeting I must circulate a written opinion on which of two rival reforms to put forward as its centerpiece.

The first is a competence sunset: every competence transferred to the Union would lapse after a fixed term unless re-authorized by a concurrent majority of the European Parliament and of the national parliaments, with the re-authorization itself reviewable by the Court of Justice. The second is a sovereignty reserve: any single national parliament could suspend a Union measure within its own territory for a fixed period, after which the measure would apply unless a treaty amendment had been secured. Their advocates present the sunset as making integration renewable and therefore compatible with the Union's founding aspiration of drawing its peoples ever closer together, and the reserve as the plainest protection of national self-government; their critics call the sunset a source of permanent constitutional uncertainty and the reserve a veto that would dissolve the Union into a mere market.

I want an opinion that does conceptual work rather than reciting the familiar complaint about the democratic deficit. My own suspicion, which you should feel free to reject, is that the deficit is usually framed as a deficit of authorization when it is at least as much a deficit of contestation — there is no forum in which the terms of integration can be argued over and changed. Argue from concepts — sovereignty, authorization, accountability, legitimacy — and not from authorities: no citations, no reading lists.

Give the operative part of the opinion as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. The first point should state the recommendation; the remaining four should carry the reasoning and a serious reply to the strongest case for the option I am setting aside.
