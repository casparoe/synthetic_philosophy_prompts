My reading group's been chewing through the Clifford/James stuff on the ethics of belief, and a freind of mine dropped this on us last week and now I can't stop thinking about it. I need help finding the exit.

Here's the argument, as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) You should never believe a claim unless your evidence actually supports it.
(2) When someone tells you something, the only evidence you have for the claim is that a person asserted it.
(3) You have evidence that the person is reliable only if you've checked them against other sources.
(4) But those other sources are just more people asserting things, so you'd need evidence of their reliability, and then of theirs, and so on forever.
(5) So you never really have sufficient evidence for anything you get from anyone else.
(6) By (1), you therefore shouldn't believe anything you haven't verified for yourself, from scratch.
(7) But no one can verify everything from scratch, and a rule that tells me I can't beleive my doctor, my old physics textbook, or my friend when she tells me her mum just died is obviously broken.
(8) So (1) is false — sometimes it's fine to believe something you can't fully back up.
