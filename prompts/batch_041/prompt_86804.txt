A paper I'm discussing at a workshop uses an analogy to dissolve the responsibility gap for autonomous systems. The move is that a corporation is an artificial agent that acts without any single human intending the act, and we hold it responsible without difficulty; an autonomous system is another artificial agent of the same kind, so the alleged gap is a pseudo-problem that our existing liability concepts can absorb. I suspect the analogy is doing all the work rather than reporting a result, but I want the audit rather than the suspicion. Give me the correspondence table first — what stands in for the shareholders, the board, the charter, the veil, the fine — then sort the disanalogies into those that matter for the conclusion and those that are merely picturesque. My particular worry is that the corporate case smuggles in an intending substrate (the human beings who act as its organs, and who remain reachable when the veil is pierced) that the autonomous system case is defined by lacking, and I'd like to know whether any version of the analogy survives that loss. Say finally whether the conclusion — no conceptual revision required — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, whether the residual problem is better described as a gap in liability, in accountability, or in culpability, and what analogy would do the job better; the closest thing I've seen is the no-fault compensation pool used for nuclear accidents or vaccine injuries, and I want to know whether that one fares any better.
