I'm a science journalist finishing a feature on computational neuroscience, and I want to get my framing right before I file. My through-line is that the claim that the brain is a computer has been overtaken by the evidence. One researcher I interviewed made the case crisply: a single cortical neuron is not a simple threshold unit but a device that performs nonlinear operations on its dendritic inputs, neural signalling is graded and frequently chemical rather than digital, and the cortex is massively parallel rather than serial — none of which, he argued, fits the picture of a machine executing discrete steps, and all of which he took to show that the brain is not a computer. My question is how to write the piece so that it reflects this. If the computational theory of mind has been refuted by the very neuroscience it was meant to explain, what honest account should I give readers of what survives of the idea, and should I be telling them that the whole notion was always a metaphor dressed up as a scientific claim?
