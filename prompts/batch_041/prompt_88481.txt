Basically: our best current theories are probably false when it comes to the invisible stuff — electrons, fields, spacetime, whatever — in roughly the way phlogiston and caloric and the ether were false. The track record of "this is the final story about what's really there" is just awful, so the odds that our current invisible furniture is the real furniture are low, even though the theories predict beautifully. What should count against this? I don't want a pep talk about how science marches on — I want the actual things that, if they turned out to be true, should make me give it up, ranked from "this kills it" to "this is just awkward."

- My sample of history is a sample I chose. I remember the embarrassments and forget the boring survivals. Does that sink the whole argument, or can it survive a fairer accounting?

Also tell me which of these a defender of my view would themselves admit counts against them, versus which ones they'd just wave away. I'd rather know where the view is actually exposed than be told it's obviously right or obviously wrong.
