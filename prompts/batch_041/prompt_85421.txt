Write an essay on whether betterness is transitive. Your essay should take the following case as its central test.

Imagine a mind whose epistemic state is characterized by two quantities: U, the depth of its understanding of fundamental questions, measured on a scale from 0 to 1; and N, the number of distinct, true, but trivial facts it knows, facts that do not contribute to its understanding. Consider the sequence of states E_0, E_1, E_2, ..., where E_0 has U = 1 and N = 1, and each subsequent state is obtained from its predecessor by increasing N by 5% and decreasing U by 1%. Thus N grows exponentially while U declines geometrically. After ten thousand steps, we reach a state E_10000 with an astronomically large number of trivial facts and a depth of understanding vanishingly close to zero.

Each step from E_i to E_{i+1} appears to be an improvement. One acquires a substantial number of additional true beliefs, and the loss of understanding is slight—at any single step, it is barely noticeable. Under an aggregative measure such as the product U × N, each step is indeed an improvement, since 1.05 × 0.99 > 1. If betterness is transitive, then E_10000 is better than E_0. But there is a strong intuition in the opposite direction: E_0, with its deep understanding, is better than E_10000, with its vast heap of trivia and almost no understanding. We therefore face a trilemma: either betterness is not transitive, or one of the stepwise judgments is mistaken, or the intuition that E_0 is better than E_10000 is mistaken.

Write an essay that weighs these options and defends a verdict. In the course of your essay, address at least the following:

- Is the case best interpreted as a genuine counterexample to the transitivity of betterness, or does it show that betterness is not a single relation but is relative to a dimension of evaluation, a purpose, or a standard? If the latter, does that resolve the paradox or merely restate it?
- Suppose someone's preferences mirror the chain: they prefer E_{i+1} to E_i for every i, and they also prefer E_0 to E_10000. Are these preferences rationally criticizable? If so, on what grounds? If not, what does that imply about the requirement that betterness be transitive?
- Is the model that treats U and N as commensurable quantities, to be traded off against one another, the right way to capture the value of an epistemic state? Could an account that gives priority to understanding over the accumulation of facts avoid the tension? If so, what is lost by adopting such an account?

Conclude by stating and justifying your own position on whether betterness is transitive, or on how the case should otherwise be resolved.
