I'm putting together a quiz for my school philosophy club about what makes pain bad, and I want it to be a decision tree rather than a list of theories. Use the asymbolia cases as the starting point – people who say the pain is definitely there, even intense, but it doesn't bother them and they don't pull away – and then split the tree on the questions that actually seperate the positions: is the badness in the sensation itself, in the unpleasantness that usually comes with it, in what it makes you want to do, in your judgement that it's bad, or nowhere in the pain itself but only in its effects? Every branch node should be a question someone can answer from a concrete case, not 'are you a hedonist?' Leaves should be named positions where there are names and 'unnamed' where there aren't, each with one line on its most awkward commitment. Please flag any two leaves the tree can't tell apart. Also include a case I made up: an intense pain that the person says they'd choose again because it means their leg is healing – does your tree handle that or does it break? Don't give me citations or a reading list, I can't use them anyway. British English please, and keep it to about six branch questions.
