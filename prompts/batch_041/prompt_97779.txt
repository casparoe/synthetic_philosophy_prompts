My reading group has been going in circles on qualia for like two months and I want a diagnosis, not another round. The setup: one side (the Dennett-ish people) says qualia is an incoherent posit and points at the Chase-and-Sanborn case, the two coffee tasters — one says the coffee tastes worse now though nothing about him changed, the other says the coffee tastes the same though his tastebuds changed — and the Dennett point is supposed to be that there's no fact of the matter about which description is right, or what extra thing the word qualia is even picking out, so we should drop it. The other side just digs in and says fine, maybe the story is underdescribed, but obviously there's something it's like to taste coffee, and denying that is changing the subject rather than solving it. Nobody has moved an inch.

My hypothesis, and I want you to tell me if it's right: the real disagreement isn't about qualia, it's about what kind of thing can be incoherent. The Dennett side treats qualia as a theoretical posit that has to earn its explanatory keep and can be voted off the island; the other side treats it as something given in experience that you can't be wrong about and so can't be incoherent in the first place. If that's the shape of it, is this a verbal dispute, two different questions hiding under one word, or a genuine clash about what counts as a reason? And what would actually move it — a new case, a distinction, a formal result — or is this one of those where no progress should be expected, and why?

He insists colors look different now, the sky looks the way red used to look, and no evidence shakes him. Turns out nothing happened to his eyes or his brain — mild concussion, he just got the idea stuck in his head. Does that separate the two camps, or does each side just absorb it and carry on?
