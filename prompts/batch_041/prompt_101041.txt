Bit of a weird one, and I'm a programmer with zero philosophy training so bear with me.

I got into an arguement about whether dreams are experiences and I genuinely can't figure out what the disagreement even is. Here's both sides as fairly as I can state them.

Side A: obviously dreams are experiences. You dream you're falling, you feel the drop in your stomach, you wake up terrified — something was experienced while you were asleep, we just can't watch it from the outside.

Side B: slow down. All you actually have access to is the waking memory. There's no seperate fact of the matter about whether anything was experienced during the night, because the only thing that makes "I dreamed X" true is that on waking you're disposed to say X. Maybe the whole dream-story gets stitched together in the moment you wake up, out of a jumble of images and body signals, and nothing was experienced at the time at all. And you can't tell those two stories apart from the inside. Plus the causal arrow might run backwards — the physical arousal could be producing the dream rather than the dream producing the fear.

What I want explained: is Side B making a claim about how dreams actually are (they're not experiences), or a claim about what the word "dream" means, or just a claim that the question can't be settled either way? Are those three even different claims, and which one is doing the work? Is the whole fight verbal — would anything change in how we treat dream reports, sleep research, the way we say "I saw it in a dream," if Side B won? And when someone says a dream is private the way an ordinary experience is private, is that even the same kind of privacy? Gut-level version please, not a lit review.
