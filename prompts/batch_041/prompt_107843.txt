Two philosophers who have argued for years about money pumps have agreed to an adversarial collaboration. One holds that the money pump — the agent with a preference cycle who, offered a series of individually improving trades, ends up back at her starting bundle minus the fees she paid along the way — establishes that cyclic preference is irrational, and more generally that diachronic rationality imposes structural constraints on preference. The other holds that the pump never follows from the cycle alone: it needs an additional premise about the agent's decision procedure (that she is myopic about the sequence, or cannot bind herself, or must accept each trade as offered and is not allowed to trade back), so at most it convicts a procedure, not a preference structure.

Draft the joint statement these two could both sign. I want the claims they agree on, including the bare mechanics of the pump, what the agent's willingness to trade does and does not presuppose, and whether the incompleteness variant and the cyclic variant stand or fall together; then the residual disagreement stated so precisely that each side would accept the wording of its own position; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and, for each crux, a case or argument both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Work through at least these variants: the synchronous pump, in which every trade is offered at once; the case where the agent foresees the whole sequence and can decline the first trade; the case where a commitment device is available at the outset; the case where the trades are not improvements for her at all because her preferences themselves penalise trading; and the pump that trades on incompleteness rather than on a cycle. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, a disagreements table, and a crux-by-crux section with the two predictions, and close by saying which parts of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, and why.
