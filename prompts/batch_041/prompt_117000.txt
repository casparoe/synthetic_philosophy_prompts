I am preparing a graduate seminar on the value of knowledge and want to open with the Meno puzzle rather than with the standard menu of textbook solutions. Here is how I intend to pose it. Socrates allows that a man with a true belief about the road to Larissa will guide others there just as well as a man who knows the way, and yet he insists that knowledge is the more prized possession and that the two are distinct things. His own suggestion, when Meno presses him, is the image of the statues of Daedalus: unfastened, they run away, and fastened down, they stay. True beliefs are like that, and knowledge is the ones that have been tied down by an account of the reason why. The difficulty is that if knowledge is true belief plus some further component, that component has to carry the whole of the value difference on its own, and it is not obvious that any of the usual candidates is valuable enough, or valuable in the right way, to bear that weight.

I would like the candidate answers graded rather than surveyed. Please rank the following seven from strongest to weakest as solutions to the Meno problem, that is, as accounts of why knowledge is worth more than a true belief with the same content.

1. The stability answer: knowledge is true belief that cannot easily be dislodged, and its value is durability.
2. The guidance answer: knowledge is a more reliable guide to action because the belief is not true merely by luck, so its value shows up across a wider range of situations.
3. The credit answer: in knowledge the true belief is attributable to the knower's own competence, so the value is a good for the knower rather than merely a good feature of the world.
4. The understanding answer: what is valuable is not knowledge as such but the grasp of why the thing is so, which knowledge only sometimes delivers, so the puzzle is an artefact of treating knowledge as the primary good.
5. The no-gap answer: knowledge is not true belief plus a component but a different kind of state altogether, so the question of what the extra component adds is malformed.
6. The swamping answer: the extra component matters only when it makes an actual difference to what happens, and usually it does not, so the value gap is real but small and contingent.
7. The answerability answer, which I have sketched myself and which I would like you to rank honestly rather than flatter. On this view the extra component buys neither better guidance nor greater durability but the knower's ability to answer for the belief: to produce reasons when pressed, to see how the belief hangs together with the rest, and so to be a fit target of the demand for justification. The value of knowledge is then the value of being a participant in the practice of giving and asking for reasons, not the value of a better-behaved mental state.

For each of the seven, tell me how much of the value gap it actually explains, and whether the value it identifies sits in the belief, in the knower, or in the knower's relations to other people. Then say which of three test cases each answer handles worst: (i) an unexercised knower who will never act on the belief and never be challenged about it; (ii) a knower who has forgotten the grounds but kept the belief; (iii) a believer whose true belief came from a reliable process that they could not describe. Finally, tell me whether the answerability answer is a genuinely distinct position or a restatement of one of the others in different vocabulary.

State how confident you are in each placement, and identify the single point in your answer about which you are least sure.

Return a JSON object with these fields: "ranking", an array of seven objects ordered strongest first, each with "rank" (integer), "candidate" (short label), "strength_0_to_10" (number), "value_gap_explained" (one of "large", "moderate", "small", "none"), "value_located_in" (one of "belief", "knower", "knower's relations to others", "none"), "worst_case" (one of "i", "ii", "iii"), "why_here" (one or two sentences), and "confidence" (0 to 1); "answerability_verdict" (an object with "distinct" (true or false), "nearest_neighbour" (the label of the answer it most resembles, or null), and "note"); "least_confident_point" (a string); and "overall_confidence" (0 to 1).
