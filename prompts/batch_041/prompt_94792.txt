Me and a friend keep going round in circles about whether speech acts are really conventional. He's an Austin guy — felicity conditions, misfires, all of it — and I think an illocutionary act is basically just this: you intend to be taken as doing X, and your audience takes you as doing X. I want a case that pries the two apart.

So build me a scenario where the act goes through on my view but not on his. It has to be a misfire, not an abuse — the speaker is completely sincere, the fault is with the conventional procedure, not with her feelings — and the audience has to genuinely understand what she was trying to do (no confusion, no joking, no sarcasm on their part). Ordinary setting, no made up laws or fake institutions, keep the whole scenario to a paragraph, and don't just have her lack the authority to do it, thats the easy one. Then run Austin's conditions over the case and say exactly which one fails and why that makes it a misfire rather than an abuse; run the recognition view and say why it says the act went through anyway; tell me which single feature of the case is doing all the work; and check that if you patch that feature the two views snap back together. Finish with the best line for the Austin side — where's the equivocation in my verdict?

Also skip the well-worn examples (christenings, bets, wedding vows, a meeting thats not properly constituted). I want something new that still works.
