Please argue that the problem of old evidence is better read as a reductio of one of Bayesianism's background assumptions than as a defect in conditionalization — specifically, as a reductio of the assumption that whatever an agent has already learned she is certain of, so that known propositions carry probability 1.

Make the argument concrete. Suppose an agent carries a revisable evidence base: she has credences over the evidence propositions themselves, and treats coming to know E as raising P(E) sharply toward 1 rather than fixing it at 1. Argue that for such an agent a known E can still raise the probability of a hypothesis H that E was originally used to construct, and that no auxiliary repair is needed — no counterfactual credence the agent would have had before learning E, no re-parameterized likelihood, no special rule for old evidence.

Then state the cost honestly: name at least one thing orthodox Bayesianism says cleanly that your revisable-base agent can say only awkwardly, and give a verdict on whether the trade is worth making.
