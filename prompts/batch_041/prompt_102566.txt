I am part of a small graduate reading group on the metaphysics of the self, and we keep running into the same frustration: the moment anyone says that the self is a narrative, three or four genuinely different views get collapsed into one, and by the end of the evening half the room has quietly changed position without noticing. The family of views we care about is the one that treats the self as something like a center of narrative gravity — the idea, roughly, that the self is not a thing that tells its story but an abstraction defined by the pattern in the stories, the way a center of gravity is defined by the distribution of a body's mass rather than being any part of the body. I would like you to build us a one-page classification instrument: a decision tree whose internal nodes are diagnostic questions and whose leaves are positions, so that people can be sorted by what they actually say about hard cases rather than by which slogan they walked in with. Please phrase every node as a question a reader can answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to; questions of the form "do you think the self is a narrative?" are exactly what I am trying to avoid.

The tree should be built from the logical space first and populated with positions second. I want the branch structure to turn on at least these distinctions, in whatever order you find most revealing: whether the narrative self is a real entity or process, a useful posit or fiction that needs no author, or something eliminable in favor of a summary of behavior; whether narrative is constitutive of the self, merely expressive or reportive of it, or a regulative ideal that selves can fail to achieve; who or what authors the narrative — the person, the surrounding community and audience, or no one at all; and whether the relevant unity is diachronic and whole-life or synchronic and local. Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or be marked unnamed if no one occupies it), one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and nothing else — no reading lists, no citations, no dates. If two leaves end up indistinguishable given the questions you can ask, say so explicitly rather than forcing a split; and if some combination of answers turns out to be empty, note it and diagnose briefly whether the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

So that the instrument cannot be answered by reciting a familiar taxonomy, please make the diagnostic cases carry the weight, and include these: two bodies narrating a single shared life; one body housing two complete and mutually unaware life-stories; a community that maintains an official biography its members are expected to enact, including a member who privately disowns it; a creature with no autobiographical thread at all, living entirely in the present; a person whose life-story is periodically rewritten by an external editor and who accepts the new version; and a person whose story is accurate but who rejects it as not theirs. Aim for six to eight branch nodes and between six and nine leaves, and keep the whole thing to a page — it is going on the wall of the seminar room.
