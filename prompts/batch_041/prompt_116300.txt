You are a methodologist specializing in inference in the human sciences, with a focus on archaeological reasoning. I am a PhD student in archaeological theory preparing a qualifying paper, and I need the desiderata: what any adequate method for inferring past beliefs and institutions from material remains has to deliver. Think carefully before you write, and start by sorting which requirements you are inclined to state are genuine constraints on such an inference and which are inherited habits of the discipline; do not simply rehearse the standard positions. Give me exactly five numbered items, with no introduction and no conclusion, each a sentence or two, stating the requirement, the case or consideration that motivates it, and whether you regard it as non-negotiable, and stating each requirement in a way that makes clear what would count as evidence against a belief or institution attributed under it. The five must be answerable for at least these cases: a sealed deposit of unused objects buried in a way that no later practice repeats; a shift in the treatment of the dead that coincides with no other detectable change in the record; identical objects produced by two different techniques in adjacent households; and a monument whose design is held constant through several generations of rebuilding. Frame the requirements so that they discriminate among three broad families of method -- inference through ethnographic analogy and middle-range regularities, inference from context and association, and approaches that treat belief as constituted in practice rather than as an inner state to be recovered. Where two of your requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so inside the item where the tension surfaces rather than in a summary, and if one requirement does most of the excluding, say that in its own item.
