I am trying to find out whether the following pattern has an established name, and if not, what the most useful definition of it would be.

It appears whenever someone argues that a system is genuinely flexible or free. Some behavior is shown to be a fixed sequence: the creature or program runs through its steps in order, and if it is interrupted partway it starts over rather than resuming or adapting. The reply is that the system can monitor its own sequence, notice that it is stuck, and try something else. The monitoring is then shown to be fixed in exactly the same way, sensitive only to the cues it was built to detect and trying only the alternatives it was built to try. The reply moves up a level — the system can notice that its monitoring is rigid and revise the monitor — and the exchange can be repeated indefinitely. Each time, the concession at the lower level is granted and the claim of flexibility is relocated one storey upward.

Two things seem to be running together here. One is the property supposedly present at whichever level has not yet been mechanized: not merely having more options, but being able to be moved by one's own rigidity — as when someone says he can always step back and question his own values, and the stepping back turns out to be one more disposition in the same set. The other is the argumentative pattern itself, in which every successful mechanization of a level is answered by moving the claim up.

Does either have a name? I have a vague sense that the first is a term of art from the study of animal behavior, though I cannot retrieve it, and no idea whether the second is a recognized fallacy, a legitimate open-ended dialectic, or nothing in particular. Identify the nearest established concepts, say how each falls short of what I have described, and if nothing fits, coin a term and define it — including where the line falls between a system that is merely nested and one that has genuinely escaped the regress. A bare label will not help me; what I need is the carving.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
