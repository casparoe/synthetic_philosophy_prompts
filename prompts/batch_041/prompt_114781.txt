You are a philosopher of language and logic with expertise in deflationary theories of truth, the normativity of belief and assertion, and the explanatory role of truth. I am a graduate student preparing a position paper for a seminar on truth, and I have been arguing with a friend who thinks deflationism cannot account for the normativity of truth. I want to know whether she is right, and more generally how exposed my view is. Please think carefully and systematically before answering. Begin with a short brainstorm of the strongest possible objections, then organize your response. I do not need citations, reading lists, or references to who said what; I want the philosophical anatomy of the position's exposure.

The view I hold is this: truth is not a substantive property with an underlying nature. The truth predicate is essentially an expressive device that lets us generalize over propositions, endorse claims without repeating them, and form infinite conjunctions and disjunctions. There is no robust truthmaker relation, no substantive correspondence property, and no explanatory role for truth beyond what the disquotational schema and the expressive function already provide. At the same time, I hold that truth is a genuine norm of belief and assertion: it is constitutive of belief that beliefs aim at truth, and it is constitutive of assertion that assertions are correct just in case their contents are true. I think this combination is coherent because the normativity comes from our practices of holding one another accountable, not from truth being a mysterious property. I also hold that truth is not pluralistic: there is a single truth predicate, even if the grounds of truth vary across domains such as morality, mathematics, and empirical science.

What would count against this package? I want a map of its exposure, not a general survey. In particular, I want you to consider: (i) whether the normativity of truth can be explained by a purely expressive device, or whether it requires truth to play an explanatory role that deflationism forbids; (ii) whether the success argument -- the claim that true beliefs tend to lead to successful action -- gives truth an explanatory job that minimalism cannot discharge; (iii) whether the generalization function of the truth predicate can be performed without a substantive property, and what would show that it cannot; (iv) whether the formal conservativeness of deflationary truth -- the result that adding a deflationary truth predicate to a theory does not prove new non-semantic theorems -- is a virtue or a fatal limitation if truth is supposed to explain the success of science; (v) whether there are domains where the truth predicate behaves so differently that alethic pluralism, the view that truth is not one thing across all domains but differs by subject matter, becomes forced, and whether that would undermine my monism; (vi) whether a community with a norm of warranted assertibility but no truth predicate is coherent, and what my view predicts about such a community; and (vii) whether the view risks unfalsifiability by explaining away every apparent counterexample as a confusion about the expressive function.
