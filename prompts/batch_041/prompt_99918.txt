ok so I'm a third-year and I'm in this aesthetics seminar thats been entirely about whether you can get aesthetic knowledge secondhand — the prof keeps calling it the acquaintance principle, roughly the idea that testimony can tell you a painting is good, or that it wrecked someone, but it cant actually hand you the experience, so at best it gives you a verdict without the warrant. fine.

my friend Mara is a senior painter and her thesis show opens next friday, and shes asked me to give the little welcome before people start wandering around with the wine. 3-4 minutes. two catches. first, the room is split: half is her family plus a bunch of econ majors who came for the free food, and the other half is the painting faculty plus my seminar prof, who will absolutely clock it if I just do the usual its-so-moving thing. second, Mara has banned wall labels, so my speech is literally the only text in the building.

I want the speech to be built on the acquaintance principle rather than dancing around it. the honest move is to admit up front that I cant give anyone the experience, and then be really precise about what I *can* pass along — that the work rewards slow looking, where to stand, the order she wants people to walk the room in, what she was actually chasing, why the middle painting is doing something the others arent — and then hand the rest off to the paintings themselves.

write it. it needs to land for the family without being condescending, and it has to feel like a genuine welcome and not a seminar paper. dont resolve the tension, make it the spine of the whole thing. also she hates the word journey, so no journey.
