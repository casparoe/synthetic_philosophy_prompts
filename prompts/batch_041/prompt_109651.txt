My friend and I spent like three hours arguing at a bar last night about whether a thought can actually be about anything, and I still can't tell if he's right or just very convincing after two beers. His line is that teleosemantics has basically won: a mental state gets its content from what it was selected to do in evolution, from its biological function, so the meaning of any state in my head is fixed by the ancestral situation it was supposed to help me handle. Fine so far. But then he pushes it. He says that means I literally cannot have a concept of anything that didn't exist in the Pleistocene. No internet, no smartphones, no electrons, no mortgages, no DNA as a concept. Those words are just noises I make. When I said I'm obviously thinking about my phone right now, he said that's not a thought about a phone, that's an old stone-age urge wearing a phone costume. He thinks it's also why a language model can't mean anything by the word cat, which I half buy but for totally different reasons.

Then he went further and it got weirder. He says newborn babies have no thoughts with any content at all, because nothing in them has been selected for yet, and he says the same about a person who pops into existence out of nowhere fully formed — he had a name for this, swampman or something — no content, no thoughts about anything ever, because there's no history. I said that sounds insane and he said the alternative is magic. He also kept bringing up something he called the disjunction problem like it was a trump card, and I definately didn't follow that part.

So here's what I want to know. If teleosemantics really does mean that every concept I have is stuck in the stone age, and that everything I've thought since is just old machinery getting triggered by new triggers, doesn't that just refute the whole theory? Evolution is slow and the world changes fast, so a theory of meaning that says I can't think about the internet seems self-evidently dead. But he acts like everyone who knows the literature agrees with him, so now I don't know whether I'm supposed to conclude that naturalistic theories of content are a dead end or that I've missed something about what the theory actually says. Can you tell me which it is? And please don't just hand me a reading list, I'm not going to read Millikan, I just want to understand the shape of the thing.
