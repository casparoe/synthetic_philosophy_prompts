A few of us in a cooperative-game-theory reading group have been deadlocked for about three months now over what a two-person bargaining solution ought to be, and I want to try writing the document we should have written at the start. Call one of us the Nash person: they think the Nash solution is the only one with a real normative story behind it, and that once you drop independence of irrelevant alternatives you are just picking a point off the frontier by taste. Call the other the monotonicity person: they think IIA is ideology smuggled in as an axiom, and that any solution which can punish you for improving your own alternatives — i.e. which fails individual monotonicity — is unfair on its face and indefensible to a client. We keep going in circles mostly because each of us evaluates the other's axiom by the standard of our own.

So write the joint statement we could both sign. I want (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, and make them substantive rather than just 'both are Pareto optimal and scale invariant', I know that; I mean the interesting ones — what each axiom is actually doing, which pairs of axioms are jointly unsatisfiable so that the choice is forced, whether the disagreement survives once you normalize the disagreement point, and whether anything turns on the fact that the Kalai–Smorodinsky point is fixed by a ray to the utopia point while Nash is fixed by a product. Then (2) the residual disagreements in a table, with each side's position phrased the way that side would actually want it phrased, so neither of us feels strawmanned. Then (3) the cruxes — I suspect one is whether individual monotonicity or IIA has the better claim to constrain a fair division, and another is whether 'bargaining power' is genuinely being modelled or merely assumed — and for each crux a case or argument we would both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction about how it comes out recorded in advance.

Use one concrete case throughout so we stop talking in the abstract: two towns splitting a river's water plus a compensation fund, where the feasible set of (town A's share, town B's share) is the convex hull of (0,0), (1,0), (0,1) and (0.6,0.8), with (0,0) as the disagreement point. Then vary it twice — once by adding an option that raises only one town's best achievable payoff, and once by adding an option that neither town would ever want — and say what each solution does in each variant and whether that is the right reaction. Also, Priya in our group keeps suggesting we just average the Nash and the KS point and be done with it; if that is a bad idea I would like to know exactly why, in the same terms. No citations or reading lists, we all know the papers, I want the arguments. And if part of what weve been fighting about turns out to be a verbal dispute about what 'fair' or 'bargaining power' is supposed to mean, say which part, and say what is left of the disagreement once you strip that out.
