I've been going down a rabbit hole lately about this idea that what you really want isn't what you actually want, but what you'd want if you were smarter, better informed, more consistent, less messed up by your circumstances. It comes up with AI alot (the whole "coherent extrapolated volition" thing — you do what we'd want if we were much wiser and had actually thought it through) but it's also just in the background of normal life, like when someone says "you don't really want that, you only want it because...". Me and a few friends keep arguing about it and I've realized we mean completely different things by "what you'd want if..." and are just talking past each other.

So what I want is a decision tree / flowchart that sorts people into the position they actually hold. Please build it out of the questions first and only put the names on the leaves afterwards, because if you start from a list of names it just turns into a glossary.

The branches I care about, roughly:
- Is there a fact of the matter about what you'd want if you were ideal, or is it just a way of talking / a decision tool / a fiction?
- What exactly gets idealized? Just your beliefs (you're not ignorant or miscalculating)? Or also your thinking (perfect logic, no weakness of will, no forgetting)? Or your wants themselves (getting rid of sour-grapes wants, and wants you only have because you've been ground down)? These give pretty different theories.
- Order of operations, which I think is the thing nobody ever says out loud: do you hold your wants fixed and add information, or do you first make yourself perfectly rational and then ask what you want? Those can come apart and I want that on the tree.
- Does it converge? Is there one answer for everyone, or could two equally cleaned-up versions of me disagree, and if so which one wins?
- Whose extrapolation? One person's, or the whole group's, and if it's the group's, how do you put them together, and does the group version beat the individual one?
- The big one: if ideal-me wants something actual-me doesn't, does ideal-me win? Is that what I "really" want, or is it just what a nicer, smarter stranger would want for me? I think there's a real difference between idealizing only my information (still obviously me, just better informed) and idealizing my character (might be a different person entirely), and I suspect the theories that sound plausible only work in the first case.
- And is this thing a standard for judging how things went, or a recipe for deciding what to do? You can't actually run it, so I'm suspicious of anyone using it as a recipe.

Also one branch about whether the idealization is even fair: is it just leaving stuff out (like leaving out how much money I have), or is it adding stuff that isn't true of me (like that I'm perfectly consistent)? I read somewhere that a lot of these theories do the second without admitting it, and that seems like a big deal, so I want that as a fork too.

Please include as leaves at least: the full-information / informed-desire view, the ideal observer view, CEV itself, the economics-y "preference purification" version where the idealization just strips the noise off your choices, a second-order-desire view where what matters is wanting your own wants, a straight-up "there's no such thing" deflationary view, and an objective-goods view that says skip the whole idealization and just ask what's good for people.

For each leaf give me one line on its most awkward commitment. Flag any combination of answers that nothing occupies and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And tell me which leaves the tree can't really seperate — I'd rather know that than have it fudged.
