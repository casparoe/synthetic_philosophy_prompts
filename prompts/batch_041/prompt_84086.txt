I am a science-and-ideas journalist who has spent the past year reporting on a disagreement that never quite becomes a debate. One camp — call them the gradationalists — holds that sentience is a matter of degree and that moral status should track it more or less continuously: insects, fish, mammals and perhaps some artificial systems occupy different points on a single scale, with a thermostat sitting at or near zero rather than being categorically excluded. The other camp — the thresholdists — holds that moral status turns on the presence of a particular capacity, most often the capacity for valenced experience, which a system either possesses or lacks; on this view what admits of degrees is the strength of our reasons and the reach of our obligations, not status itself. Each side thinks the other is making a category mistake, and after a year of interviews I have a folder full of mutual misreadings rather than a clean statement of what is actually at stake.

I want the document the two sides should have produced together. Please draft it as the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed gradationalist and a committed thresholdist. It should contain, in this order:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims: what both sides accept about the phenomena, about the structure of the problem, and about what follows for practice — including what both would concede is not settled by their disagreement. Nothing vacuous, and nothing that quietly presupposes one side's framing.

2. The precise points of disagreement, each written in neutral wording that the side it describes would accept as its own position, so that each could read the other's entry without objecting that it is a caricature.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. I expect at least one to be the difference between a degree in the property, a degree in our confidence, and a degree in the weight of the reasons; another to be whether a capacity that is present but minimal — present in the way it is present in a simple homeostat — counts as present at all; and another to be how far precautionary reasoning can do work before it collapses into treating uncertainty itself as a form of status.

4. For each crux, a concrete case, experiment or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Please apply both positions explicitly to four cases: a solitary insect with nociceptors and avoidance learning but no evidence of a central integrative structure; a thermostat-like device whose set-point maintenance can be described as avoiding a harmful state; a large language model that produces fluent first-person distress reports; and a hypothetical engineered system with a dedicated affect module that modulates behaviour and can be decoupled from its own reports. For each case, state whether each side assigns any nonzero moral status, and with what confidence.

Finally, say which part of the dispute, if any, is merely verbal, and why — and if a residue is genuinely substantive, say what would have to be discovered for it to be closed.

Write in British English, and do not supply reading lists, citations or bibliographies; I need the reasoning and the drafting, not the literature.
