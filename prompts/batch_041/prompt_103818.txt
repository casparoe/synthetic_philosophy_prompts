I've been following the rationalist discussion of Roko's basilisk for years, and I keep arriving at a defusing move that the community seems to wave away rather than answer, so I'd like to know whether it actually works. The setup, as I understand it, is that a future superintelligence will punish anyone who learned that it might exist and did nothing to help bring it about. But the punishing is done in the future, by a machine I have no causal connection to; nothing I do today can raise or lower the chance that it runs the simulation it threatens. That makes the whole thing look to me like a curse pronounced by a stranger: an unfalsifiable threat from an agent entirely outside my influence, and the sane response is simply to ignore it. As far as I can tell, the community keeps treating it as a live decision problem only because it has quietly imported the machinery of Pascal's wager, an infinite stake multiplied by some vague probability, and we already know the shape of the reply to that. So is my move sound? If it isn't, I'd like to know precisely which step is wrong: the claim that I have no causal channel to the machine, the claim that no causal channel means no reason to act, or the comparison with Pascal's wager.
