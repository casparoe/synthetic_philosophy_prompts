Ok so I'm a third-year phil student and I'm taking this seminar on applied ethics that keeps circling around the idea of "moral trade" — the thing where two people who care about totally different stuff can supposedly both come out ahead by making a deal. Like, I don't really care about factory farming but you care a ton, and you're lukewarm on global health but I'm obsessed with it, so I agree to go vegetarian if you agree to give a chunk of your income to the malaria charity, and the claim is we're both better off by our own lights even though neither of us has changed their mind about anything. I can follow the arithmetic on the whiteboard but it still feels like a sleight of hand, and I can't tell if it's genuinely coherent or just a cute way of multiplying numbers. Our prof is letting me run one session for the class and I want to build an actual game where people feel the pull of it — where they start striking these deals and then walk into the weird parts on their own, instead of me explaining it off a slide. I'm the one running it so I need it to basically work without me having to lecture.

What I'm picturing: about 16 students, one 90-minute block, a normal seminar room, no real money (maybe a bag of candy or a few chips as the "currency," whatever's cheapest). I want everyone to get a different little value profile so there are real gains from trade lying around, plus a set of "actions" they can commit to that cost them different amounts. Then I just let them negotiate and see what shakes out. The three things I really want them to hit on their own: (a) that the whole deal depends on there being some common scale for comparing two things they'd both swear are incomparable, (b) a trade that requires one of them to do something they actually think is morally wrong, so they have to decide whether to take it anyway, and (c) the fact that the "gain" only exists from the two traders' own viewpoints — from some outside, impartial standpoint it might look like nothing improved at all. Then the debrief should tie what just happened back to the real structure of the idea, and I need you to be honest with me about what this setup can't reproduce and that I'll just have to say out loud.

Give me the whole design as a JSON object with these fields: title; one_line_goal (one sentence, what they should walk out seeing); setting (room, headcount, minutes, materials, and how you're keeping it money-free); how_to_assign_value_profiles (describe 4–5 profiles with weights over the causes, and how to hand them out so gains from trade definately exist); tradeable_actions (a list of commitments a participant can make, each with a personal cost and which causes it helps); trading_rules (who can trade with whom, how a deal is made binding, whether trades can be chained or conditional, how long each round runs); timeline (minute-by-minute, setup through debrief); payoff_scheme (how to score each person from their own profile once the final set of commitments is frozen); built_in_wrinkles (the 2–3 hard cases you plant, matching the a/b/c above, and roughly where in the timeline they should surface); debrief_map (an array of {moment, idea_it_illustrates, question_to_ask}); what_will_go_wrong (predictable first-run failures and the fix for each); and what_the_activity_cannot_show (frank, no hedging).
