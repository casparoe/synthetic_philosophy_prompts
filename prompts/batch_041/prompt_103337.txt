I recently left a career in software engineering to work on AI alignment, and the idea that has unsettled me most since the switch is acausal influence: two agents shaping each other's choices with no causal channel between them, each one's decision depending only on what the other decides. Next week's guest on the interview series I host has been defending a specific and, to me, disturbing thesis about it. He argues that acausal influence makes acausal threats possible; that such threats are ordinarily non-credible, since carrying one out costs the threatener something and wins it nothing it can observe; and that the remedy is for an agent to adopt a surrogate goal -- a goal it genuinely pursues, deliberately chosen or built so that punishing non-cooperators is instrumentally useful to it -- which makes the threat sincere rather than a bluff. He further claims this is not a curiosity but a live design question for any powerful system we might build, and that an aligned agent may need either surrogate goals of its own or an explicit policy of immunity to acausal blackmail.

Draft eight to ten interview questions for him. I want them to engage the actual content of the position, not to have him restate it, and I want each question paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give. The sequence should make his standard deflections -- that this is just precommitment under a new name, or just ordinary game theory -- progressively harder to reach for. Please make sure the set includes at least one question on each of the following:

- The ontology of the threat: what it can mean for a distant agent to punish you when no signal, resource, or causal influence passes between you, and whether the threat therefore reduces to a bare logical dependence -- and whether that makes it vacuous or, worse, inescapable.
- Credibility and self-binding: whether an agent can really adopt a surrogate goal at will, how that differs from a commitment device, and whether the decision theory he favors actually licenses the binding in cases where the other party is a computation rather than a physical adversary he could ever meet.
- Equilibrium selection and stability: if any agent may adopt a surrogate goal, which threats get to bind, what determines the split, and what prevents the whole practice from collapsing into an arms race of mutually punishing goals that leaves everyone worse off.
- The line between acausal trade and acausal extortion: whether he can draw a principled distinction between the two, or whether his account quietly erases it.
- Design and ethics: whether we should want to build agents that punish strangers they will never encounter, whether a policy of refusing to respond to acausal threats is stable or is itself a surrogate goal in disguise, and whether creating such an agent amounts to building a hostage-taker.

Close with a question that forces him to say what would count against the view -- what observation, argument, or design failure would make him abandon surrogate goals as a response to acausal threats.

A few constraints. Do not ask generic background questions or anything he would have answered in his opening remarks. He will happily cite papers, but I want the questions answerable from the ideas alone, so please do not build a question around a specific article, author, or date that I would have to look up; phrase any reference to prior work in terms of the position rather than the publication. Where a question presupposes something he might reject, say so and let the follow-up handle the denial.
