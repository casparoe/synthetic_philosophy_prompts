I'm writing a seminar paper on Dennett's three stances — the physical, the design, and the intentional — and on his claim that the patterns those stances pick out are real rather than merely convenient fictions. As I understand the view, a pattern counts as real roughly when it admits of a description that compresses a system's behaviour far more economically than an enumeration of its microphysical detail would, so that its reality turns on there being facts about compressibility rather than on our tastes; and the physical stance is supposed to be privileged because it is the stance in which the system's actual causal machinery is described, while the design and intentional stances are abstractions that earn their keep only through predictive payoff.

A classmate has pressed an objection she calls the parochial-enumeration problem: if reality for a pattern is just compressibility, then there is nothing special about the canonical three, since a hydrologist's drainage stance, an epidemiologist's transmission stance, or a card player's tells stance would qualify on exactly the same grounds, and any list of stances would then reflect our contingent cognitive and practical interests rather than joints in nature. She adds a case that has been nagging at me. Imagine a system whose microphysical description is astronomically long; which admits of two mutually incompatible design-level descriptions that agree on every observable input–output relation while disagreeing about its internal causal organization; and which also admits a single short intentional description. Imagine further that there are possible observers whose perceptual and computational capacities make the intentional description unavailable to them while leaving the design description available, and other observers for whom the reverse holds. If a pattern's reality is meant to be independent of who is doing the compressing, it is unclear what to say about the intentional pattern in such a case; if it is not independent, it is unclear how being real differs from being useful to us.

I want an essay that reaches a verdict on two questions: first, whether the three stances mark a principled taxonomy or a parochial one; second, whether the patterns of the design and intentional stances are as real as those of the physical stance, or whether the physical stance is genuinely privileged. Please weigh the strongest arguments on both sides — the deflationary reading of reality as interest-relative, the response that compressibility is objective even if which compressions we happen to notice is not, and the suspicion that the whole dispute is verbal — and then commit to a position and defend it rather than concluding that it all depends on how the terms are defined. I would also like the essay to say what hangs on the verdict: whether anything of consequence follows for the standing of folk psychology, for the autonomy of the special sciences, or for whether there are genuine levels of description at all.
