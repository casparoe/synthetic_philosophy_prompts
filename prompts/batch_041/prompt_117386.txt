I'm not a philosophy person at all, I just have bad insomnia and I've been thinking about this alot.

Here's what bugs me. When my girlfriend tells me her dream, we all treat it as a report. She says she dreamed she was being chased through a parking garage, and nobody says "hang on, you were unconcious, how would you know?" We just take it. And it's the same with the professionals. My sleep doctor asked me to write down what I dream. Nightmares are on the symptom list they use for PTSD, same as flashbacks. A therapist asking "what have you been dreaming about?" thinks the answer is information about you. Theres whole books on what your dreams mean. And apparently in some sleep labs people learn to send a signal with their eyes in a set pattern while they're in the middle of a dream, so the machine picks it up and the researchers know the person is asleep and dreaming and aware in there. Which is wild, because that person is doing something on purpose while they're out cold.

But then the other half of it. Nobody holds you responsible for anything in a dream. I've never felt guilty about anything I did in one, the law obviously doesn't care, if you murdered someone in a dream you don't go to the police. And you can't be wrong about your own dream either. If I say there was a boat with a purple sail, that's it, thats the dream, nobody can check up on it, there's no way I'm misremembering the way I'd misremember the colour of a real boat.

Somewhere I read a philosopher who said dreams aren't experiences at all — that saying "I dreamed I was chased" isn't reporting something you went through, it's more like telling a story in a special way. And then someone else said maybe the dream doesn't happen while you sleep at all, maybe your brain builds the whole thing in the second you wake up, and the "dream" is basically a memory that got inserted.

So what I want to know is: this whole practice, the clinic, the symptom list, the "tell me your dream", the eye signals — what does it actually assume about what a dream is? Does it need the dream to be an experience, something that happens to you while you're out, with you in it as a witness and sometimes as a pilot? Or could all of it keep running on the thinner story where nothing was experienced at the time and the whole thing gets put together at waking? Tell me which parts genuinely need the strong view and which are just riding along with it. And if the thin story is right, what breaks — would the sleep clinic be doing something it can't really be doing, would the PTSD thing be measuring something other than what it says it is? Or is this not even the kind of thing you can find out by looking, and it just comes down to how we use the word "dream"?
