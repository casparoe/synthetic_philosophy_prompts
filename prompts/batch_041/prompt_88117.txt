hey, so me and my friend run the philosophy club at our high school and next month our topic is vagueness / the sorites paradox (the heap of sand thing). i want to make a "which theory of vagueness do you actually believe?" quiz that everyone fills in during the meeting, print it as a handout, like ten questions, with a scoring key at the end that i read out so people can see where they landed.

could you act as a professional philosopher who's spent years working on vagueness and write it for me? please think it through carefully and brainstorm the logical space of options first, before you write any questions, cause the whole point is that the quiz sorts people by what they actually say about borderline cases, not by which philosopher's name they've heard of. so it shouldnt just be stuff like asking whether vague words secretly have sharp cutoffs, thats basically asking for the label back.

the views i definately want as possible results: epistemicism, supervaluationism, subvaluationism (the mirror-image one where borderline sentences get to be true as well as false), degree / fuzzy / many-valued stuff, contextualism, and the nihilist one where vague words are just broken and theres no heaps at all. plus some kind of "the vagueness is in the world, not in our words" option if that doesnt fit under the others.

cases i want in there, each as a concrete case with two or three answer options:

- the sorites itself, grains of sand or the bald man
- the cutoff one. i want to see whether people think theres a fact of the matter about where the line falls, and whether anyone could in principle ever know it. bonus if the question makes "theres some cutoff" and "grain number 5000 is the cutoff" come apart, cause i have a feeling some views say yes to the first and no to the second?
- excluded middle, like "either he's tall or he's not" for someone right in the middle
- a case where the same guy counts as tall next to one person and not tall next to another
- higher order vagueness, a shade thats borderline red, is it borderline whether its borderline?
- one where you have to say whether the boundary is something we could find by looking harder at the world, vs something we could settle by just deciding on a rule
- maybe one about "adult" or a legal drinking age vs "red", does the answer change when the word is a convention we made up rather than something we see?

some questions are probably more diagnostic than others so weight them however you think makes sense in the key. and please flag any two positions your quiz cant actually tell apart on these cases, plus any combination of answers that doesnt match any real position, and say whether thats because its incoherent, or just cause nobody's bothered to defend it. thanks!
