I cover philosophy and the social sciences for a magazine, and I'm writing about the long-running dispute over the paradox of tolerance. The two camps are familiar: one holds that a tolerant order must be able to suppress the intolerant, since unlimited toleration destroys the conditions of toleration itself; the other holds that such suppression is either self-defeating or simply the intolerance it condemns under another name, and that the only consistent reply to intolerant speech is more speech. I don't want another either/or op-ed. I want the document these two camps should have produced if they had collaborated in good faith, so my readers can see precisely where the disagreement actually lives.

Think of yourself as a philosopher of law and politics with a talent for drafting formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted, and work through the positions carefully instead of reaching for the standard talking points. Before you draft, brainstorm the candidate cruxes and set aside the ones that turn out to be merely verbal.

Please produce: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims both camps can sign — about what tolerance is, about what the state may and may not do, and about which empirical claims concerning speech, harm, and backlash both sides accept; (2) a table of the genuine disagreements, each phrased in wording that the side holding it would accept as fair to its own view; and (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp — each paired with a concrete case both sides agree bears on it and the prediction each side would record in advance.

To keep this from collapsing into the well-worn either/or, build the analysis around a distinction I want the document to respect: toleration as a constraint on state coercion versus toleration as a virtue of citizens, together with the further question of whether intolerant doctrines are defeated by the norm of tolerance or fall outside its scope altogether. Keep legal permissiveness separate from social non-condemnation, and say explicitly where the two camps are talking past each other because they mean different things by 'tolerance.'

Finally, tell me which single consideration, if it went the other way, would lead you to relocate the real crux, and flag where your drafting is most likely to have smuggled in a slant. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} naming, in a short phrase, the crux you judge most likely to be decisive.
