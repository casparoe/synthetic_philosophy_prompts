I've been going back and forth with a friend about a version of reasons internalism that I can't decide is a real position or just a slogan with footnotes, and I want you to tell me what would actually count against it. The version is this: for someone to have a reason to do something, doing it has to be endorsed by their motivational set under a certain idealisation -- full imaginative acquaintance with the relevant facts, no logical slips, no false beliefs -- but the idealisation is constrained so it can only draw out and reorganise what's already in the set, not install new ultimate ends. So someone who is utterly cold to the suffering of strangers doesn't have a reason to help them, even if they would help after some science-fictional brain rewrite, because the rewrite changes who they are rather than revealing a reason they already had. I like this because it makes reasons agent-relative without making them hostage to every passing whim, and because it explains why moral advice can feel like trying to get someone to see something rather than just telling them a fact about the world. But I can't tell what should move me off it. What observations, cases, or arguments would count against it, and which of those would the internalist themselves have to concede rather than just dismiss as a critic's intuition?

I'm especially interested in two cases. First, the inert judgment case: after the idealisation you'd allow, the agent sincerely judges that some consideration is a reason for them to act, but that consideration is completely inert in their deliberation and action -- is that a coherent possibility for the view, or does it break it? Second, the self-extinguishing commitment: someone's motivational set is internally consistent but contains a deepest aim that, under full imaginative acquaintance, would demand its own replacement by entirely different deepest aims. Does the view say they have a reason to pursue that transformation or not, and does either answer cost it something? Rank what you give me from the stuff that would be fatal down to the stuff that's merely awkward, tell me which defeaters the internalist should themselves accept, and say honestly whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- and if it has, whether that's a problem for a view of this kind or just the price of doing business. No reading list or citations, just the map of where the position is exposed.
