You are a philosopher of criminal law known for work on the justification of punishment. Two colleagues—one a retributivist who holds that deserved hard treatment is the fundamental justifying reason for punishment, the other a consequentialist who holds that punishment is justified only by its expected effects on future crime—have argued for years and have agreed to produce a joint statement. Before drafting, work through in rough notes where their formulations have been talking past each other; then write the document.

Give (1) a numbered list of claims both could sign: about what punishment is and what stands in need of justification, about what the stock cases (the innocent scapegoat, the guilty offender who cannot be deterred, the offender no one can reform, the offender who has already been made harmless) actually establish, and about what the empirical record on severity, certainty, and incapacitation does and does not settle. Then give (2) a disagreements table, each row carrying the precise point of disagreement in wording its own side would accept, the crux beneath it, and the case, result, or argument both sides agree would bear on that crux, with each side's prediction stated in advance.

To keep this from becoming the usual recitation, I want both sides forced to confront a scheme I will stipulate. Under it, every convicted offender is sentenced by lottery: for each offence grade the legislature has fixed a pool of sanctions whose expected severity is set to whatever the best evidence says deterrence requires, but any individual offender may draw a sanction far above or far below what his conduct deserves. The drawn sanction is carried out in full, and no one—judge, offender, or public—knows the draw beforehand. Neither side may dismiss the scheme as not counting as punishment without saying what work the word is doing; neither may endorse it without saying what it costs its own view. Where you judge a disagreement to be merely verbal, say which part and why.

Close with a short note stating how confident you are in each substantive claim you have made, and naming the single point you are least sure of.
