I teach a graduate seminar on set theory and the philosophy of mathematics, and I am writing the final exam. The exam is 90 minutes, closed book, and the essay question I need is worth 25 marks. My students can already recite the independence of the continuum hypothesis from ZFC, the constructible universe, Cohen forcing, and the usual multiverse-versus-universe talking points. I want a question that cannot be answered by that recitation. I want it to make them confront a distinction I introduced in lecture but did not name after anyone: between width-independence and height-independence. Say that a sentence is width-independent if there are two models of ZFC with the same ordinals but different sets of reals, one satisfying the sentence and the other satisfying its negation. Say that a sentence is height-independent if there are models of ZFC that differ in ordinal height or in large-cardinal structure and disagree on the sentence. The continuum hypothesis is the test case I want them to use. I also want the question to introduce a principle that one of my students proposed in seminar and that I have deliberately left unnamed: a sentence has a determinate truth value only if it is not width-independent. The question should ask whether that principle is defensible, what a defender of it would have to give up, and whether the width-independence of the continuum hypothesis really supports the claim that CH has no determinate truth value. I was discussing this with a colleague in the philosophy department who works on modality, and she suggested the distinction might be analogous to the difference between changing the domain of a quantified modal logic and changing the accessibility relation, so you may use that analogy if it helps, but do not let it substitute for the set-theoretic argument.

Please write the exam question itself, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, with the marks for each point totalling 25, plus a one-line note on how students typically get each wrong. The model answer should reason from the definitions given here and should not depend on any particular paper, book, or citation; no bibliography or reading list is needed. The question should separate students who understand the difference between independence from a formal system and indeterminacy of truth value from those who have merely memorized the labels.
