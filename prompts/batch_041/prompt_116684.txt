I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on medieval and early modern political theology, focused on the question of whether and when a tyrant may be killed. I want a taxonomy of the positions on this question, presented as a decision tree. The tree should be built from the logical space of the debate rather than from a list of names: first the diagnostic questions, then the combinations of answers they allow, then the actual positions located in that space, with unoccupied combinations noted and briefly diagnosed. The seminar's angle is the Thomistic tradition and its early modern reception, so the tree must include, as leaves, the following positions: (1) Aquinas's own view, as reconstructed from his commentary on the Sentences, On Kingship, and the Summa Theologiae; (2) the view of John of Salisbury; (3) the Jesuit/Marianist view associated with Juan de Mariana; (4) the Lutheran view of non-resistance; (5) the modern liberal view that only legal process can remove a tyrant. You should also include any other positions that naturally occupy the logical space, and flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate.

The tree should have between six and eight leaves. Each branch node should be phrased as a question a student could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Use concrete cases to anchor the questions: a usurper who seizes power; a legitimate monarch who becomes a tyrant; a tyrant who is also a heretic or apostate; a tyrant who is a foreign occupier; a tyrant whose removal would likely cause a civil war worse than his rule; a tyrant who is personally attacking a private citizen. The distinctions that matter for the seminar include: whether the tyrant has a valid title to rule (tyrant by usurpation versus tyrant by practice); whether there is a public authority (a superior, the people, a pope) that can judge or depose him; whether the killing is done by a private person or by someone with public authority; whether the tyrant's injustice is directed against the common good or only against individuals; whether the tyrant's rule is a divine punishment that must be endured; whether the killing risks greater harm than tolerating the tyrant; and whether the tyrant's rule threatens the faith. For each leaf, give the name of the position (or the label unnamed if nobody defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Where your tree cannot separate two positions, say so explicitly and explain why. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, say whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
