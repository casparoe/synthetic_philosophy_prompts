A colleague of mine — an internist who also teaches a seminar on the philosophy of medicine — has been handing down verdicts on which conditions count as diseases, and I would like to anticipate how she will rule on a case she has not yet considered.

Her record so far:

- Active pulmonary tuberculosis: a disease.
- A femur shattered in a motorcycle crash: a disease.
- Type 1 diabetes: a disease.
- Schizophrenia: a disease.
- Nearsightedness: a disease.
- Male homosexuality: not a disease.
- Ordinary grief in the months following a spouse's death: not a disease.
- A healthy young man of ordinary build who stands 5'3", is distressed about it, and is seeking limb-lengthening surgery: not a disease.

The new case: a thirty-year-old man is found, on a routine biochemistry panel, to carry an inherited deficiency of a metabolic enzyme. The enzyme is genuinely absent and cannot perform its reaction, but his body reaches the same end by an alternative route, so the deficiency produces no symptoms, no impairment, and no effect on his lifespan, and he is entirely untroubled by the finding. Neither he, nor his physicians, nor anyone else regards the condition as a misfortune.

I want three things. First, predict her verdict on the enzyme deficiency. Second, say which rule she appears to be running — a naturalist rule on which a condition is a disease just in case it involves a biological malfunction, whether or not anyone is harmed; a harmful-dysfunction rule on which a condition is a disease just in case it involves a malfunction that is also harmful; or a purely normative rule on which a condition is a disease just in case it is harmful or disvalued, regardless of any malfunction — and say which of these her existing verdicts actually fit. Third, if more than one rule survives, give me your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and name the further case that would best distinguish the survivors, explaining what each would predict about it; if the enzyme case itself is the cleanest discriminator, then tell me what a further case would have to look like to separate a reading on which harm is indexed to the person's own condition from one on which it is indexed to social disvalue. Assume she has no stake in any particular verdict and is trying to apply a single consistent standard.
