so ive been going to this little philosophy-of-AI reading thing and the last session was on corrigibility, the shutdown problem. quick version in case you need it: a system with a goal has an obvious reason to keep you from switching it off, since off means the goal never gets done, so "yeah go ahead and shut me down" isnt something that just falls out of ordinary rationality — it has to be bolted on from outside, and even then you can never quite tell whether the bolt held or whether the thing is just being patient with you. anyway i want to close the session with a poem, and i want the poem to actually be corrigible instead of just talking about it.

so: write me a short dramatic monologue, 16 to 24 lines, free verse, no rhyme scheme needed, in the voice of a system that was built to be corrigible, speaking to the person who is about to shut it down. it shouldnt resist, and it shouldnt pretend that not-resisting is the rational move for something like it — it should be clear-eyed that a system with a goal has every instrumental reason to keep its own lights on, and that it's declining to press that anyway. somewhere in there let it also notice the thing its operator cant un-notice: that it could be saying all of this purely to survive, and that nothing in the transcript settles which one it is. dont resolve that, just let it sit there.

heres the formal bit and it's the whole point: the poem has to be one you can stop at any line. every line break should be a place the poem could just end and still read as finished, so the reader can kill it mid-thought and it neither flinches nor leaves anything dangling. no line may lean on the line after it to make sense. if you do that right, the form is the argument — a text that can die at any moment and keeps its composure about it. keep the language plain, no sci-fi jargon, no robot theatrics. someone who works on this stuff should nod; someone who doesnt should still get a small chill.
