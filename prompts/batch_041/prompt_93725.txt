My friend in my logic seminar is always saying that paraconsistent logic is basically the non-Euclidean geometry of logic, and he uses that to argue that classical logic has no special claim to being the logic of reality, so dialetheism -- the view that some contradictions are actually true -- deserves a real seat at the table instead of being treated like a party trick. His version of the story goes like this: for a couple thousand years the parallel postulate looked like something you just had to accept if you were doing geometry at all, anyone who questioned it was assumed to have made a mistake, and then it turned out you can drop it and get perfectly consistent alternative geometries, and eventually one of those alternatives turned out to describe physical space better than Euclid did. Same deal with ex falso quodlibet and the law of non-contradiction, he says: we treated them as non-negotiable, but you can drop them and get consistent formal systems (LP, relevance logics, and a bunch of others I'd have to look up), so treating contradictions as always false is a prejudice rather then a discovery. I'm not sure I by the move and I want a real audit of it, not a pep talk.

So give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the fifth postulate, what plays the role of the alternative geometries, what plays the role of the consistency proofs, and what plays the role of the physical application that turned non-Euclidean geometry from a game into a description of the world. Then tell me where the mapping breaks. Specifically: does the analogy actually support dialetheism, or only the much weaker claim that there are consistent non-explosive logics -- which is a claim about formal systems, not about what is true? I suspect it quietly imports the picture of a logic as a description of something (the way geometry was taken to describe space), which is exactly what the friend needs and what the boring reading denies. And the Euclidean story ended with the alternatives being vindicated by physics -- is there anything remotely like that for paraconsistency, or is that the part carrying all the weight with no counterpart on the other side? Sort the disanalogies into the ones that matter for his conclusion and the ones that are just decoration, tell me whether the conclusion survives once they're on the table, and if there's a better analogy for what paraconsistent logic actually achieved, tell me what it is and what it would and wouldn't support.
