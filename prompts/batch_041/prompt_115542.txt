so I'm taking this seminar on self-amending rules and paradoxes of legal self-reference, and the more cases I think about the more my gut verdicts stop lining up. I'm hoping you can tell me what principle, if any, they add up to, and which of them I have to give up.

Where I land: a constitution with a clause that can only be amended by unanimous vote — fine, it binds, entrenchment is legit. Germany declaring certain principles permanently off-limits, India's court saying parliament can't alter the constitution's basic structure — also legit to me. But a rule saying "this rule cannot be repealed" feels like it isn't really a rule at all, or if it is, it's void. And a club rule that says "any rule can be amended by simple majority", followed by a simple majority raising the bar to 90% for all future amendments, feels like a con — you shouldn't be able to lock the door behind you using the very power you're closing off. Yet in that game where the whole point is that amending the rules is a move, someone passes a rule changing how votes are counted and wins under the old count, and I think that's completly fine.

Two more. A rule that says "no rule may be applied to itself": I don't think it applies to itself, but I can't say that without sounding like I just applied it to itself. And a rule that passed only thanks to the votes of three members who count as members only under the rule being voted on — feels like bootstrapping, and I want to call it illegitimate even though I liked the outcome.

The pair I can't reconcile is the entrenchment cases vs the club lock-out: both are just "the current rules make the rules harder to change", so why is one legit and the other a con? My own tentative principle was "a change to the amendment procedure is valid only if it's made under the procedure it's changing", but that seems to get the German and Indian cases wrong and the bootstrapping one right, which is backwards from what I want. What actually fits these? What does it say about a rule that repeals itself? And I care way more about keeping the entrenchment-is-fine verdicts than about the others, so overturn those last.
