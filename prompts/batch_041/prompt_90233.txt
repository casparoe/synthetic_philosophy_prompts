I run a reading group that keeps returning to the debate over discounting the future, and I want a one-page decision tree to hand out that takes a reader from the least controversial admission in the area — that a benefit arriving later can legitimately weigh less with me than the same benefit now — to a named position on pure time preference. Build it from the logical joints rather than from a roster of names. The first branch should separate a claim about value from a claim about rational preference; the next should ask whether the discount survives once you hold fixed the probability that the good, the person, or the world still exists at that later time. Then ask whether temporal location does any work on its own or only through causal and psychological connectedness, and whether the subject who receives the later good is the same subject of welfare as the one doing the discounting. One node should make the respondent commit on whether a positive rate must be exponential or may be hyperbolic, and one should force the bullet on distant catastrophes receiving near-zero weight. Leaves should include time-neutralism, discounting that reduces to risk of non-occurrence, the connectedness/prudential view, bare-preference discounting, and the view that the question is perspective-relative and ill-posed. It should sort people by what they actually say about the cases, not by which label they prefer. Note any combination nobody occupies and say whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate. Six to eight questions, five to seven leaves, under 600 words.
