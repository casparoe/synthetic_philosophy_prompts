ok so im the TA for a grad seminar on social choice, and my turn to open the session on manipulation is coming up, and i want to hand out a poem instead of another slide deck. you're a poet who's also actually done the game theory — i need both halves of that. think it through before you start drafting; dont just reach for a rhyme.

here's the thing i want the poem to be about, stated flat so you dont have to go look anything up (you cant anyway, no web). Gibbard–Satterthwaite, roughly: if there are three or more alternatives, and your voting rule accepts any profile of individual rankings you throw at it and always names a single winner, then it's one of two things. Either there's a dictator — one voter whose ranking decides the outcome no matter what everyone else reports — or the rule is manipulable: there's some profile at which some voter gets an outcome they rank higher by handing in a ranking that isnt their true one. Every rule i can think of as fair — plurality, Borda, runoffs — sits on the manipulable horn.

and i want the honest footnote in there too, because thats what the seminar actually fights about. there are ways out, they just cost you. one: restrict the domain. if everyone's preferences are single-peaked over the alternatives — each voter has an ideal point and likes things less the further out they get, so the rankings all line up along one dimension — then taking the median of the reported peaks is strategy-proof, and as far as i know its basically the only rule with those nice properties that is. two: allow chance. pick a voter at random and let them be the dictator; that's strategy-proof, because your ballot only matters in the world where you're the one picked, and in that world you'd rather have reported honestly. it is, at bottom, a lottery over dictatorship.

what i want:

- a sestina. six stanzas of six lines, then a three-line envoi, with the usual permutation — each stanza's end-words are the previous stanza's in the order 6-1-5-2-4-3 — and the envoi should mop up all six words, with three of them landing at the line ends. rhyme and meter are yours; the end-words are not.
- use exactly these six end-words, in this order in stanza one: order, true, lie, rule, peak, one. the whole point is that theyre a fixed set that keeps coming back reshuffled — that's the unrestricted domain, every ordering of the same things allowed in. if the form starts making you say something you dont mean just to hit a word, dont paper over it. that pressure is the poem. it's the same pressure the voter is under.
- voice: a voter sitting with the ballot in front of them, deciding whether to send in their real ranking. first person, present tense. not a narrator explaining the theorem from outside.
- the envoi's three lines are the decision — the moment the rule collapses all that shuffling into one name.
- somewhere the poem has to admit the two escapes, the peaks/median one and the lottery one, without letting itself off the hook. the last line should not resolve it. i want the room to close the handout feeling the trade, not the answer.
- dont put the theorem's name or anybody's name in the poem. let the structure say it. someone who knows the result should recognize it line by line; someone who doesnt should still get a good poem and a bad feeling about voting.
- if the form fights back, let the fight show up in the poem. dont switch forms on me.
