I am a philosopher who works on the ethics of markets and knows enough mechanism design to be dangerous. Restate the anti-commodification critique of markets in kidneys, votes, and price-gouged goods in the vocabulary of mechanism design: types, allocations, incentive compatibility, participation constraints, feasible mechanisms, and the admissible message space. For the sake of the exercise, treat repugnance not as a cost in the objective function but as a constraint on which messages and trades are admissible. Then give me the residue report: for each anti-commodification claim, say what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no counterpart at all. Finally, tell me whether the residue is a defect of the mechanism-design vocabulary or a sign that the original critique was less clear than it looked. I want to know whether the objection to commodification is just a constraint on feasible trades or whether something is lost in that translation.
