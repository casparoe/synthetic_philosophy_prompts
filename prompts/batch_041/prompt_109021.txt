I've been lurking on rationalist blogs for about a decade — LessWrong, the old Sequences, the various spinoff forums — and there's one move I keep seeing that I've never been able to pin down. Someone says a problem is NP-hard, or undecidable, or 'hard even to approximate', and the room treats that as a big philosophical result about the limits of knowledge. I don't think it's always wrong, but I don't always recognise what it's supposed to support.

So, for someone who can't look anything up right now: what are the most important implications of computational complexity results for how I should reason about my own limits? Keep it at the level of a person, not a theorem. When I've stared at an argument for an hour and can't find the flaw, does complexity theory say anything about whether there's a flaw to find? Is 'finding is hard, checking is easy' a fact about the world or only about our formalisms? And since the hardness results are asymptotic while my life is finite and my problems have n in the dozens, does any of this bite on me at all?

I see this alot, so I'd like the split between three kinds of claim kept straight throughout: results that hold unconditionally (diagonalisation-style, no algorithm whatever), results that are conditional on something like P≠NP that nobody has proved, and fine-grained or average-case statements. Which of those can carry which philosophical weight is really the thing I'm after. I've also seen people say the idealised Bayesian agent is a fiction once you take the intractability of exact inference seriously — I'd like to know whether that's an argument or just a sigh.

No reading list or citations please — I can't get online and anyway I want the reasoning, not the references.

Give it to me as: 'The short version' (~100 words). 'The distinction that does the work' (~250 words). 'The implications that actually matter' — four of them, numbered, each a short paragraph, ordered by how much they should change what I do. 'The one everyone overrates' (~200 words) — the implication that shows up most often in blog arguments and does less work than people think. And 'What I'll do differently' (~150 words), concrete.
