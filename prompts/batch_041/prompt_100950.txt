For the past two years I have been running a comparative project on folk moral judgment, and I have reached the stage where I have to commit to a thesis in print; a colleague and I have been arguing about what our data, and the broader cross-cultural record, actually license. I want your honest credence in a specific proposition, not a survey of the literature. To make the question tractable, take the following as given — it is a hypothetical I have constructed for the sake of argument, though it closely tracks the real record: in three populations, a WEIRD university sample, a small-scale horticultural society, and a rapidly urbanizing middle-income population, subjects judge a set of vignettes, among them one about a bystander who declines to rescue a drowning stranger at trivial cost to himself, one about a consensual but degrading private arrangement between adults, and a standard moral/conventional task. Suppose the results show substantial, patterned divergence: the WEIRD sample treats the rescue omission as a serious wrong, the horticulturalist sample treats it as regrettable but not blameworthy, and the urbanizing sample splits along lines that correlate with market integration — while all three converge on the conventional items. The proposition I want your credence in is this: the best explanation of that pattern is that moral realism is false, that is, that the divergence reflects the absence of a mind-independent moral fact of the matter rather than a difference in non-moral belief, in the pragmatics of blame, or in the very concepts the groups deploy.

Give me a number, then decompose it. I expect the terms to include at least the probability that the divergence is genuine moral disagreement rather than divergence in non-moral belief or in the meaning of the moral terms; the probability that genuine moral disagreement of this kind is in fact evidence against realism; the probability that realism can absorb or explain the divergence without cost; and the probability that the pattern is better explained by cultural-evolutionary or coordination accounts that are neutral between realism and its rivals. Assign a rough probability and one line of justification to each, show me how they combine, and tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to.

For what it is worth, my own credence is around 0.45 and my colleague's is around 0.75. After you give your own figure, tell me which of those two numbers is most likely mislocated — that is, which sub-question the two of us probably disagree about — and then tell me what single piece of evidence or argument would move your number most, and in which direction.
