My friend and I spent most of Saturday arguing about whether Gödel's incompleteness theorems tell against the claim that the human mind is a machine, and we kept talking past each other. Please write the joint statement we could both sign after a genuine adversarial collaboration between the two camps: the anti-mechanist, who holds that the theorems show mathematical understanding to outrun any formal system, and the deflationist, who holds that they show nothing of the kind. Give me, first, the claims both sides can accept -- about what the theorems actually state, what they do and do not presuppose, and which of the usual formulations are non-negotiable; then the precise residual disagreement, each side's position worded so that its own adherent would accept the wording; then the cruxes, including whether the anti-mechanist's reading requires the mind to be knowably sound and not merely consistent, and whether "the mind is a machine" should be read as a recursively axiomatizable system or only as some recursively enumerable set of theorems -- a distinction on which the theorems may turn. For each crux, set out the argument or case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where you think the dispute is partly verbal, say which part and why. End by stating how confident you are in your reconstruction of each side's position, and single out the one claim in the document you are least sure of.
