I've been hanging round rationalist blogs for years and I keep bumping into a pattern I dont have a name for. It isn't that nobody believes the person — everyone nods along. It's that only part of what they say gets taken up as a claim about the world, and the rest gets filed away as how they feel about it. So someone describes how a hiring round actually works and the concrete details land fine, but when they say the whole process is set up to keep people like them out, that's heard as their mood, their reaction, not as a claim that could be right or wrong. Same person, same conversation, split down the middle: trusted as a witness, not as a knower. And after a few years of this they start pre-translating everything into the vocabulary the room already has, until they can barely say what they originally meant.

I know about testimonial injustice and hermeneutical injustice, and this seems narrower than either — they are believed, and they have the words. Is there a name for this particular thing? If there isn't, coin one, and be exact about the edges: how it differs from an ordinary credibility deficit, from hermeneutical injustice, and from simply being talked over. A bare label won't help me — I want the carving.
