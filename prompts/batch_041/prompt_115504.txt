Write an in-universe help-centre article — a deadpan customer-support FAQ, roughly 800 words — for the fictional Office of Divine Attributes, the body that fields the faithful's questions about an omnipotent deity. The Office's house position is the voluntarist one: the being is not bound by the laws of logic, because the eternal truths themselves are free decrees and could have been otherwise (the line usually associated with Descartes), and the Office accordingly distinguishes between what the being can do absolutely and what it has bound itself to do by covenant — the old distinction between absolute and ordained power.

Format it as numbered questions with answers, plus a short "related articles" sidebar (invented titles are fine) and a "still need help?" footer with an escalation path.

It must handle at least the following queries:

- the stone;
- the irresistible force and the immovable object;
- whether the being can lie, sin, or cease to be;
- whether it can create a being it cannot control;
- whether it can make a contradiction true, or unmake a logical truth;
- whether it can undo a past event;
- and one query from a caller who has noticed that if the laws of logic are themselves created and revocable, then nothing on this page — including the answer they are currently reading — can be trusted.

The constraint that makes the exercise hard, and the point of it, is that under this house line "that is logically impossible" is never an available answer, since logical impossibility is precisely what the being is supposed not to be constrained by. Every "cannot" therefore has to be recast either as a self-imposed covenant (it could, but has undertaken not to) or as a failure of the question to describe any task at all (a stone too heavy for an omnipotent being to lift is not a difficult task but an empty description). The FAQ should reach for these two moves so consistently, and with such visible strain, that a reader who knows the literature can see what the article is doing: if the being can override logic, "omnipotence" ceases to do any work; if it cannot, then "all-powerful" was never more than a label for "able to do whatever is doable". Land that dilemma without ever stating it, and do not resolve it — the last answer should leave the reader exactly where the argument leaves them.

Keep the register flat, bureaucratic, and genuinely helpful: a version number, a last-updated line, internal cross-references to the Office's own earlier articles, one deprecated ticket category, at least one question whose honest answer is that the question cannot be answered, where the agent's workaround is a cross-reference. The humour must come from the substance — the actual shape of the positions and the actual pressure on them — not from the corporate dressing, which is only the vehicle. Do not quote or cite any real text; the Office may quote only itself. Do not make the believer the butt of the joke: the target is the argumentative move that rescues omnipotence by narrowing it, not the faith that prompts the question. A reader who has never opened a book of philosophical theology should still be able to follow it.
