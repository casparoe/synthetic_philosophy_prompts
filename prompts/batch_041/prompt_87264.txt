Imagine a polity that must fix a single currency for its distributive metric and can revise it only once a generation. Rate each candidate below from 0 to 10 on how robustly it withstands the two pressures that most reliably break such currencies: the conversion problem (two people holding identical bundles but unequal in their capacity to convert those bundles into functioning) and the neutrality problem (the metric must not covertly rank some ways of living as intrinsically more worthwhile than others). The candidates: (1) hedonic welfare; (2) informed preference satisfaction; (3) Rawlsian primary goods; (4) Dworkinian resources; (5) Nussbaum's central capabilities; (6) a two-tier metric that distributes resources up to a sufficiency threshold and capabilities above it; (7) a reflective-endorsement currency that measures welfare by the satisfaction of preferences the agent would endorse under full information, with a floor preventing downward-adapted preferences from registering as satisfied. Give each a score and one sentence naming the pressure that most threatens it, then rank all seven and identify the single revision that would most improve the winner.
