I'm a 3rd-year PhD student in metaphysics and my reading group has been stuck for about two months on the Humean vs. anti-Humean thing about laws of nature. We've split into the two camps -- the people who think laws just are the axioms of the best systematization of the mosaic (call them H's), and the people who think that's backwards, that laws are what make the regularities hold, whether you cash that out as primitive nomic necessity, as a governing relation between universals, or as powers/dispositions sitting in the properties themselves (call them A's). We keep talking past each other, mostly I think because 'governs' is doing about six different jobs at once and nobody says which one they mean. So I want us to finally write the document we should have written ages ago: a joint adversarial-collaboration statement that both camps could actually put their names to. Can you draft it? Structure it as (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering the shared data (the mosaic, the regularities, the counterfactuals we all agree obtain in a given world), what a law is even supposed to explain, and which stock arguements we all agree fail; then (2) a section where the residual dissagreements are stated, each in wording that the side it belongs to would accept as fair rather than the other side's caricature of it; then (3) a disagreements table with the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move at least one of us -- and, for each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance so nobody can weasel afterward.

Two things I want built in, because otherwise this is just going to be a rehash of stuff anyone can look up. First, pull apart 'the law governs' into a menu of distinct readings -- e.g. it's causally productive, it's metaphysically prior to the regularity, it restricts the space of possible worlds, it's just a summary we then project a 'must' onto, it grounds the counterfactuals -- and have each side say which readings it accepts, which it rejects, and where the two sides turn out to agree once it's spelled out, flag that the fight there was verbal. Second, evaluate a compromise one of us keeps floating: keep the Humean account of what a law *is* (best system, whatever), but let naturalness / degree of property-hood enter the simplicity metric itself, so the anti-Humean gets naturalness back into the base without any governing. Both sides should say whether that's a genuine third position or a relabeling of one of the two, and what would have to be true of a case for it to come apart from both. Also throw in a concrete toy case we can all evaluate -- say a world whose mosaic has one simple regularity, versus a world where the simplest axiomatization needs a disjunctive, gerrymandered predicate but a slightly less simple one uses only natural properties -- and have each side state what the laws are there, whether the regularity explains anything, and what counterfactuals it supports.

Please no citations or reading lists, we all know the literature and a model that can't get online would just be guessing at those anyway; I care about the content, and I want it to read like something two people could actually sign, not a survey.
