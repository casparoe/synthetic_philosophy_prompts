I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am at work on a feature about why machines still struggle with ordinary common sense. Before I commit to a framing, I would like a draft essay I can argue with.

Please write one that takes a position on a dispute I keep running into. One camp tells me the frame problem — the difficulty of getting a system to notice what matters and to leave everything else alone — was a technical embarrassment of an earlier era of symbolic AI, and that systems trained on large amounts of data have made it moot. The other camp says such systems do not solve the frame problem, they bury it: they learn what usually mattered in the situations they have seen, which is the wrong shape of solution if the world keeps producing situations nobody has seen. A hospital triage tool that must decide, out of a patient's hundreds of recorded facts and a night's worth of changes, which handful bear on the decision in front of it strikes me as sitting right on the fault line.

So: is the frame problem solved, dissolved, or merely relocated — and relocated to what? Is the underlying difficulty one thing, about how a system represents and updates its picture of a changing world, or two things, representation and relevance, that have been traveling under a single name? Reach a verdict and defend it against the strongest version of the view you reject. Assume I know the textbook examples; skip the preliminaries and get into the argument.
