My brother-in-law and my cousin got into it at Thanksgiving and I ended up sitting through the whole thing even though I don't know the first thing about philosophy. It started over our school district cutting the gifted program at the middle school. Nobody gets anything out of the cut -- the kids who were in the program just loose it -- but the gap between the district's top scores and its bottom scores shrinks, and the superintendent's report actually lists that shrinking gap as a success. My cousin says that's a slam dunk against anybody who thinks equality is good in itself: a change that helps nobody and hurts some people can't be an improvement, so if your theory says the world got better, your theory is broken. I think this is the "levelling down" thing I've seen mentioned.

My brother-in-law wouldn't have it and they went back and fourth maybe six times. The way I remember it:

1. He said the objection only bites if you assume a change has to be good for some particular person in order to be good at all, and that the guy who came up with the priority view (Parfit?) threw out that assumption, so egalitarians shouldn't be forced to accept it either.
2. She said fine, but if "nobody is better off" isn't a reason to think the cut made things worse, I don't see how it's a reason to think it made things better.
3. He said the cut is a bad example anyway because she's aiming at the wrong target. His view, the priority view, doesn't care about gaps at all -- it just says a benefit to someone worse off counts for more than the same benefit to someone better off. And by that view you obviously don't cut the program, since the cut benefits no one.
4. She said then it's strange he talks about inequality like it's the problem, and that his view has its own version of the same disease: it'll happily make the badly off a tiny bit better off by making the well off a lot worse off, whenever the weights come out that way.
5. He said that isn't levelling down, because somebody does gain, and with any reasonable weighting it'll only prefer a lopsided trade like that if the small gain to the badly off really is worth more than the big loss to the well off -- which is exactly what a weighing view is supposed to do.
6. She said she'd drop the name-calling but not the point.

Can you go through it and tell me who's right on each numbered point, and what both of them are missing? I'm especially curious whether the levelling down objection actually works against egalitarianism, against the priority view, both or neither, and whether there's any version of the case where his view really does say "make everyone worse off, that's an improvement."
