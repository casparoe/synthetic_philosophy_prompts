I'm in a year-old reading group on AI safety that's split into two camps which have basicly stopped making progress against each other. One side — call them the guarantee people — says the only thing that deserves to be called a safety case is one that holds up no matter how the system's environment and optimisation pressures turn out: bounds that survive adversarial inputs, distribution shift, a genuinely hostile optimiser, off-distribution deployment. The other side — the average-case people — says that's not just infeasible but the wrong target: worst-case reasoning over an unbounded set of possible worlds is vacuous (the worst case is always catastrophe, so it never discriminates between designs), you can't ship or even evaluate anything on it, and what we actually have and want are bounds that hold with high probability over a distribution we can defend, plus an honest account of the assumptions that make the distribution valid. They've each read enough of the other to be annoyed but not enough to actually engage.

Please write the joint statement these two could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want: a numbered list of agreed claims — what a guarantee is and isn't, that any bound is relative to a stated threat model and there's no such thing as an unconditional one, what the shared goal is, and what both agree would count as a safety argument that fails; then the precise disagreements, each written in wording that the side holding it would accept as fair to its own view; then the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and for each crux the case, thought experiment, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Where part of the dispute is really verbal — say, if worst case and high probability are being defined over different spaces so the camps aren't even disagreeing about the same object — name that part and say why.

A few things I'd like it to grapple with rather than skate past: whether a high-probability bound that hides a fat tail is doing any real work, whether averaging over training runs but not over reward functions quietly assumes the very thing under dispute, whether the guarantee camp's refusal to average is itself a kind of prior over which worlds matter, and what either side should do when the stakes are large enough that a tiny probability of catastrophe dominates the expectation. Don't let it become a scorecard of who's right — I want the document both of them could put their names to, with the leftover disagreement made exact. Numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table is fine, and the cruxes can have their own section.
