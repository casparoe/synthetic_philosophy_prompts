Last spring my partner and I bought a house with a mature garden, and we have spent the summer losing a slow war against a stubborn infestation on our ornamental shrubs. The nursery's advice was a systemic neonicotinoid applied to the soil and taken up by the plant; it would very likely end the problem, whereas the alternatives we have tried — horticultural soap, hand-removal, trying to encourage predators — are slower and less certain. I have read enough to be uneasy. These compounds are implicated in harm to pollinators and to insect-eating birds, and because the plants are ornamental rather than a food crop I cannot tell myself I am protecting a harvest. I keep reaching for a verdict and finding that I do not know what I actually think, and I would rather have a number with its skeleton showing than another round of vague guilt.

Please give me your credence in the following proposition, stated so that it has a truth value: "It is morally impermissible for a private homeowner in a temperate region to apply a systemic neonicotinoid insecticide to an ornamental, non-food plant in order to control a pest, even when the available non-chemical alternatives are less effective and more laborious."

Do not give me a bare number. Decompose the question into the sub-questions the credence depends on, and for each give a rough probability with one line of justification. At minimum I expect these, and please add any others you think belong:
- the probability that the insects killed have morally relevant interests at all (a capacity to suffer, or a life that can go better or worse for them);
- whether the direct killing of the target pest, or the indirect effects on non-target species, is the morally dominant consideration;
- the expected magnitude and probability of harm to pollinators, birds, and the wider food web from one homeowner's application;
- whether an individual's action has enough causal impact to be wrong-making in the first place, given that countless others act similarly;
- whether permissibility is to be assessed act by act or by the rule or disposition it would be rational to adopt;
- whether the mere availability of less effective alternatives is itself decisive.

Then tell me how the terms combine into the final figure, which single term the final number is most sensitive to, and which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would move it most. My own credence is about 0.6; my partner's is about 0.2. Say which sub-question our disagreement most likely lives in. No citations needed — I can supply those myself.

Finish with a single final line in the form \answer{0.xx}, containing only your overall credence.
