Longtime lurker on the rationalist blogs, first time posting. I keep running into a move that I don't have a word for, and I don't think it's just Pascal's mugging.

The shape of it: someone says there are some absurd number of insects on the planet — ten to the eighteenth, whatever, I'm making the number up — and we genuinely don't know whether any of them have experiences. Call it a one in ten thousand chance that there's anything it's like to be an insect. Multiply it out and you get a stupendous quantity of morally relevant experience, and that quantity is supposed to outrank basically everything else on your priority list. Here's the part I can't name: you can't argue with it by arguing about the probability. Drop the chance to one in a billion and the answer barely moves, because the other factor is astronomical. So the move is immune to the exact kind of evidence that would normally settle an empirical question, and the only thing that can kill it is the probability being exactly zero.

That seems different from Pascal's mugging, since there's no deceiver and no promised payoff — the big number is just a count of actual animals, and the small number is real uncertainty about their inner lives. And it's not ordinary expected-value reasoning either, since ordinary EV reasoning at least changes its verdict when the evidence changes. I've definately seen 'fanaticism' used in the infinite-ethics stuff, but nothing here is infinite.

Is there a name for this exact thing, and if not, what should it be called? What I want isn't the label, it's the carving: what the move actually is, how it differs from Pascal's mugging / fanaticism / decision-making under moral uncertainty / plain dominance reasoning, and where its edges are — does it stop being this move when the number is large but not astronomical, or when the probability is a value judgement rather than a fact? And what does someone have to say if they want to reject it without rejecting arithmetic?

Last line please, as \answer{...}: the established term if there is one, otherwise the term you'd coin, plus a few words of gloss.
