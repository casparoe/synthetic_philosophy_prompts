Write a short story set in an invented militia during a fictional civil war, in which a unit is ordered to execute a group of villagers. The commander announces, credibly, that any man who cannot bring himself to take part may step out and will be reassigned without punishment. Follow three soldiers: one who volunteers with ideological conviction, one who complies while inwardly recoiling, and one who steps out and suffers no consequence. The story should not resolve whether what happens is better explained by situation, authority, and peer conformity or by pre-existing belief and character; let both readings draw support from the same events. After the story, append a brief note on where it takes liberties with the Browning-Goldhagen debate over perpetrators, which contested reading it leans on, how confident you are in the historical and psychological claims it makes, and the point you are least sure of.
