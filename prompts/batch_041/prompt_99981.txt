My friend has this argument she swears shows that nothing survives any change, so the Ship of Theseus has no real answer because there's never a fact of the matter about identity over time. I can't find where it breaks and it's been bugging me for a week. Here it is:

(1) If something at one time is the very same thing as something at a later time, then they're identical, and identical things share all their properties.
(2) The ship with all its original planks has the property of being made of plank P. After P gets swapped out, the ship no longer has that property.
(3) So the ship before and after differ in a property, so they arent identical -- the repaired ship isn't the original.
(4) But every persisting thing changes somehow; you don't have the same hair you had last year.
(5) So by the same reasoning you aren't the same person you were last year, and the ship isn't the same ship after even a single replacement.
(6) So nothing persists through any change at all -- being the same thing over time is a fiction, and so is the self, and so is a word that drifts in meaning being the same word.

I'm sure (6) is false and I'd really like to keep (1) if I can.
