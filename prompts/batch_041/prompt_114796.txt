Design a single 90-minute session for a mixed seminar of 16–20 advanced undergraduates and graduate students in philosophy and cognitive science. About half have read work on mental causation, emergence, and explanation; the others have not, so nothing in the activity may presuppose prior reading, and the terms careenium and simmball must not be spoken until the debrief. The room has a whiteboard, a projector, paper, tokens, tape, and up to about thirty dollars of small hardware you may specify. There is no software, no internet, and no time to build anything elaborate.

The thing I want them to run into is the question of whether the pattern level pushes the substrate around. The image is the careenium: a box of small, fast-moving pieces whose collisions follow a few fixed rules, in which stable, sluggish clusters form. The clusters have their own dynamics, respond to disturbances from outside the box, and end up encoding the history of those disturbances in their arrangement. The question is which level does the causing. Does the cluster arrangement shove the small pieces around, do the small pieces shove the clusters, or is cluster talk merely a convenient summary of a story that is already complete at the level of the pieces? The old worry about whether mental events can move atoms is the same worry in different clothing, and I want the session to make both tempting answers feel forced rather than merely asserted.

Design the activity itself, not a lecture that the activity illustrates. Rules, materials, roles if you need them, and two or three rounds that escalate. The following must be true of whatever you build.

Participants must be able, in one round, to intervene only at the macro level, and in another round only at the micro level, with success measured by an observable macro quantity. The micro rules must be simple enough that the group can hold them in view and compute consequences in principle, while the round's time limit makes that computation infeasible in practice, so the compression advantage of the macro description is felt rather than stipulated. The macro regularities must be reliable enough that interventions there work.

Include a task in which the group must reconstruct an external event from the macro configuration alone, and a moment in which the same macro outcome is reached by two different micro trajectories. Have the group keep two ledgers, one in micro terms and one in macro terms, so that predictive accuracy per unit of description becomes a live comparison rather than a slogan.

I have been calling the scoring device shove-credit. An intervention earns shove-credit at a level when varying it at that level changes the macro outcome while the description at the other level is held as fixed as it can be. Arrange the mechanics so that the group can award shove-credit to both levels, or to one, and so that the resulting numbers are genuinely arguable rather than obvious. Include a reflexive turn in which participants notice that their own interventions are themselves macro-level patterns, and that the micro story of the room never mentions the seminar.

Then write the debrief. It must map what happened onto the difference-making criterion for causation, onto the exclusion worry that arises when the micro story is causally complete, onto the idea that a pattern earns its keep by supporting compression and prediction, and onto the question of what it is for a configuration to be about something outside the box. Bring in proportionality: ask whether the macro cause is the more apt one precisely because the micro cause is too detailed to be the difference-maker, and let the group resist that.

Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it: participants cheating by secretly handling individual pieces, unrepeatable outcomes, someone declaring the macro level just a summary, someone conflating unpredictability with causal irrelevance, the group sliding into macro realism with the facilitator left to supply the counterweight, and the timing. Give me a minute-by-minute plan and a sample run of the first round with actual numbers, so I can see whether the shove-credit results come out contestable.

Finally, be honest about what the activity cannot reproduce. My guess is that it cannot manufacture the intuition that macro causes are a genuine addition to the world rather than a description of it, because the micro rules are transparent to me and the usefulness of the macro description is stipulated by my design; and that it cannot reproduce the selection story explaining why these patterns rather than others are the ones that matter. Say which question the facilitator must put into words, and say where the activity will mislead.
