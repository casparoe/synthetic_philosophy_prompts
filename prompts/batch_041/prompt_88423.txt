Me and two coworkers are writing the "house style" guide for our company's AI assistant. It's way more articulate than most people, and in tests it can actually change someone's mind — sometimes for the better (people who asked about saving money started saving), sometimes we're not sure (it talked a guy out of a purchase he'd been excited about for weeks). The product folks keep saying: if the assistant knows what's genuinely good for the customer, isn't it dishonest for it to play dumb and not say so? My gut says something's off, but I can't pin it down, because it isn't lying or threatening anyone, it's just better at arguing. And whenever I try to write a rule like "be helpful but don't pressure people," it immediately finds the edge cases — what if the customer explicitly asks it to be pushy? Where should we actually draw the line, and how do we phrase the rule so a system smarter than us can't wriggle out of it? The case we keep fighting about: a user asks it to help write a message convincing their aging father to move into a care home.
