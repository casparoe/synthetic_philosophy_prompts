I'm a third-year undergraduate currently taking a course on intergenerational ethics and social discounting, and I've also spent two terms working in a reinforcement-learning lab, which has left me with a puzzle I can't put down. In an MDP the discount factor is just a free design parameter that fixes what the agent is optimising for, and nobody thinks gamma = 0.95 is a moral error; but the moment I carry the same formal object into the ethical case it seems to carry a completely different kind of freight, as though choosing a rate were choosing how much the future counts. A friend in the economics department tells me the whole dispute is dissolved by the Ramsey equation, and I don't believe him, though I can't yet say precisely why not. For the course's final assignment I have to write an agenda-setting essay, and rather than survey the terrain I want to defend a particular division of labour: which open questions about pure time preference -- as opposed to discounting that merely reflects growth, uncertainty, or the chance of extinction -- are most worth someone's sustained attention over, say, the next three years, and why those rather than the others.

I'd like the ordering defended rather than merely asserted. For each question I want to know why it is upstream of the others, whether it is tractable with philosophical tools alone or genuinely needs decision theory or economics, how neglected it currently is, and what result over the next couple of years would move it up or down my list. I'm particularly interested in questions where a wrong answer would quietly distort a great deal of downstream work: whether the individual and the collective cases are really the same problem or have been conflated, whether the familiar empirical findings about present bias bear on the normative question at all or only on the descriptive one, whether "time-relative" and "person-relative" discounting can be prised apart, and whether the analogy to the RL discount factor illuminates anything or is a trap. Please don't hand me a reading list -- I want the agenda itself and the reasoning that orders it, including an honest note on which items you'd deprioritise because their answer, whatever it turned out to be, wouldn't change much elsewhere.
