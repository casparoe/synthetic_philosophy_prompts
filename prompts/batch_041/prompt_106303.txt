I'm in a bioethics reading group and we've spent the last few weeks on dementia and advance directives, and now I have to write a position paper, so before I commit anything to paper I want to find out whether my own view actually holds together. There's also a personal reason: my aunt signed a living will when she was first diagnosed a few years ago, and she's now in a care home, cheerful, doesn't recognize any of us, and tells the staff she wants to keep going. Two people in the group have very tidy positions and I keep getting caught between them, which makes me suspect my tidiness is fake.

Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) I'm a reductionist about personal identity in the Parfitian sense: identity is not what matters, what matters is psychological connectedness and continuity, and that comes in degrees, so there's no deep fact of the matter about whether the person in the home is "really" my aunt. (2) I think her directive should be honored, including the parts refusing antibiotics and artificial nutrition, even though she now says she wants to keep going — I think a competent earlier self can bind a later one at least on the big things. (3) I think her current contentment is real welfare and counts fully; I don't think her happiness is a shadow of some earlier person's happiness, and I get annoyed when people talk about her as a vessel for a plan she no longer has. (4) I think a big part of a life going well is that it be authored, that it hang together as the person's own thing, and that dementia can wreck a life even when the person living it is perfectly content. (5) I'm generally a liberal about medical ethics: a competent refusal is sovereign, and it's wrong for doctors to override a refusal in order to protect the patient from herself. (6) I think Ulysses contracts for addiction should be enforceable, and honeslty I've never understood what's supposed to be troubling about them.

What I want is a consistency audit, not a survey of the literature: tell me which of these are actually pulling against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and rank the real tensions by how bad they are. For each real one, say what has to give and what that costs — what else I'd have to abandon or what else would get harder to say if I make that move. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what I'd still be stuck with after I make it. I'd rather you be blunt about the worst one than list everything as equally bad.
