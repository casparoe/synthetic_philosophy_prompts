I cover science and ideas for a general-readership magazine, and I'm sketching a long essay on how we sort legitimate historical revisionism from denialism. I've reported on the three standard test cases — the Holocaust, the Armenian genocide, the Nanjing Massacre — and I keep meeting the same difficulty: nearly every demarcation criterion I'm offered looks reverse-engineered from the cases rather than applied to them. My own half-formed suspicion is that the real line runs not between two kinds of historian but between two kinds of claim, and that what marks a denialist is less the conclusion than a repair strategy — absorbing each apparent refutation by widening the story rather than by conceding anything. I want your credence, not a tour of the literature.

Here is the proposition I need a number for. There exists a single criterion — statable in advance, and without reference to which verdict it will produce — that sorts historical claims about the Holocaust, the Armenian genocide, and the Nanjing Massacre into legitimate revisionism and denialism.

Give me your credence, then decompose it. I want each of these as a separate term, with a rough probability and a line of justification: (a) that there is a fact of the matter about the events at all, of the kind a criterion could track; (b) that an evidential and methodological criterion — say, the standards a claim must meet to overturn a settled scholarly consensus — actually separates the two, rather than admitting a revisionist who meets those standards and still lands on a denialist conclusion; (c) that denialism is better identified by argumentative form — immunizing moves, unfalsifiability, selective skepticism toward hostile sources — than by content, and how far that gets us; (d) that the criterion is doomed to circularity, sorting methods by the conclusions we already know they reach; and (e) whether the whole question is malformed because 'denialism' operates at a different level of description from 'revisionism' — denialism often targeting the scale, significance, or framing of an event rather than the bare fact of it. Combine the terms, tell me which one your number is most sensitive to, and name the single argument or piece of evidence that would move it most. For calibration, my own number is about 0.35.

Then commit. If the criterion can't be had, say which you would bet on — that denialism is a family-resemblance concept with no single essence, or that the line is irreducibly normative, a matter of which conclusions a community agrees to place beyond contest — and give your reasons. I would far rather have a definite position with its weak points marked than a balanced survey.
