You're a philosopher who also writes fiction, and I want a short story (roughly 1200–2000 words) that dramatizes a version of the torture-vs-dust-specks problem that I think gets skipped over. Use this setup, adding whatever detail you need:

A near-future city runs an AI that tunes its traffic and ventilation, and some unavoidable "irritation budget" has to be dumped somewhere. Option A: one random person gets a few days of medically induced agony, reversible. Option B: everyone gets a barely perceptible itch behind the eyes, a few seconds at a time, a couple times a day, for years. That's the familiar version. Here's the twist I actually care about: in this version option B's harms aren't spread across people at all. A scheduling quirk means they all land on one volunteer, one after another, across an entire lifetime — each one too small to ever mention, but it never stops. So the usual move ("each person only gets a single speck") isn't available, and the review board has to actually decide.

Please make the story do the philosophical work rather than just decorating it. I want to watch someone try the standard answers and see where they break once the tiny harms are all inside one life instead of spread across many. Before you write, brainstorm a bit: what changes when the many small harms share a single bearer — does the summed total matter, does it matter that no individual moment is bad, does the volunteer's own retrospective verdict settle anything? Then write the story. Don't end with a character delivering a tidy lecture; if the disagreement honestly stays open, leave it open.
