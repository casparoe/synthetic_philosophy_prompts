OK, I'm trying to figure out my own views on killing vs letting die, because I keep catching myself being inconsistant and it bugs me. Here are a bunch of cases and my honest gut reactions, not my respectable ones.

A runaway train's headed for five workers; I can flip a switch to send it down a side track where it kills one worker. I'd flip it. Fine.

A surgeon kills one healthy guy and harvests his organs to save five dying patients. Obviously not fine.

There's a guy in the next room dying of something a cheap pill in my pocket would cure. I just don't give it to him, cause he's annoying. Not fine — honestly feels like murder, even though I never touched him.

A patient is on a ventilator that I myself hooked her up to. I unhook it and she dies. I feel OK about this, like I'm just letting the disease run its course.

Same patient, but I never hook her up in the first place, even though the machine's right there and I could. Weirdly I feel a bit worse about this than the unhooking one, even though I did less.

This next one is the one that wrecks me. A boulder's being held back by a single rope, and if the rope goes the boulder rolls down and crushes a guy at the bottom. I didn't set up the boulder. I just walk up and cut the rope. He dies. I feel like I killed him — like this is the surgeon case, not the pill case — even though all I did was remove an obstacle.

A guy's about to get hit by a boulder somebody else set rolling, and I could yell a warning but I stay quiet. That feels like letting die, and it's wrong, but nowhere near as bad as the rope one. I don't understand why the rope case and the warning case feel so different when in both I'm basically just standing there.

I own a factory, I know a machine has a defect that'll eventually kill a worker, and I don't fix it. Worker dies. Feels like killing, not letting die.

I'm the only kidney match for somebody and I say no, they die. Feels completely fine — just letting die.

My half-baked idea is that it's about whether the death goes through a mechanism I control: if it does, it's killing; if I'm just not stepping in to stop a mechanism I don't control, it's letting die. But the rope is a mechanism I control and it feels like killing, and the factory is a mechanism I control and that also feels like killing, so maybe that's it? But the ventilator is a mechanism I control and that feels fine, so now I'm stuck.

Can you give me a principle, or a small set of competing principles, that actually fits these verdicts? Be precise about what each clause is doing and which case forces it. Then say what the principle predicts for cases I haven't listed — say, one where I redirect a threat onto a smaller number, or one where I'm one of five people who each could easily save somebody and we all assume someone else will, or one where I kill some people as a side effect of hitting a target versus where I kill them as the whole point. And be honest about which of my verdicts the principle can't keep — I'd rather drop a verdict then keep some Frankenstein principle that saves them all. Write it in American English, thanks.
