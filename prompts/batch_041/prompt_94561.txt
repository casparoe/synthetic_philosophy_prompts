I have been trying to hold two ideas together, and I would like your help in working out what follows from them. The first is that certain utterances do not describe the world but enact something in it: to say "I promise" is not to report a promise but to make one, and such an utterance is answerable not to the facts but to the conventions that let it succeed. The second is that gender is performative in this same sense — that it is not a fact about a person which speech merely reports, but something brought into being by the repeated acts of naming and being named. Taken together, these seem to imply that a person's gender is real in the way a marriage is real after the words have been said, rather than in the way a stone is real whether or not anyone speaks of it.

What I want to understand is the practical upshot. If gender is brought into being by performative acts, then it must be something one is doing, and so, in principle, something one could stop doing — as I might decline to make a promise and thereby not be bound by one. So I would like to know what it takes to stop. Is it a matter of refusing the relevant acts — declining to answer to the name, declining to occupy the role, declining to be addressed in a certain way — and if so, which acts are the load-bearing ones? Or does a performative work in a way that makes this harder than the analogy suggests?
