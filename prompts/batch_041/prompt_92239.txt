Okay, I need you to build me a case — and I want the annoying version of the problem, not the one everyone trots out.

Quick background, so you don't go hunting around for anything: Nozick's tracking theory says S knows that p just in case p is true, S beleives p, S wouldn't have believed p if it had been false (the sensitivity condition), and S would have believed p if it had been true (the adherence condition), with the whole thing relativized to the method S actually used. The famous cost of the view is that it gives up closure under known entailment. The usual illustration is the grandmother who trusts that her visiting grandkid is fine and can't rule out that its a disguised mule, or the fake barns, or a lottery ticket. All three are worn out and I've read them a thousand times.

What I want from you is the version of the closure failure that runs through the other door — through adherence rather than sensitivity. Build me a case where a subject tracks some premise p, fully grasps and believes the entailment from p to q, and where her belief in q is sensitive (if q were false, she wouldn't believe it) but fails adherence (q is true, and yet in the nearest q-worlds she wouldn't believe it). The upshot should be that on the tracking theory she knows p but does not know q, even though the entailment is airtight, obvious, and she's a competent reasoner who isn't being sloppy or negligent.

Constraints: one paragraph, an ordinary slice of life, no mules, no fake barns, no lottery tickets, no brains in vats, no demons, no evil scientists, no simulation stuff. The subject can't be making an inference error or failing to see the entailment. It should be genuinely realistic — a normal person, a normal belief, a normal method.

Then do the autopsy, in order:

1. State the propositions and the method(s), and for each of the four tracking conditions say which nearby world is the relevant one and why it comes out the way it does.
2. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing all the work.
3. Tell me what happens if you delete or tweak that feature — does closure snap back into place?
4. Finally, give me the strongest reply available to a tracking theorist who isn't willing to just shrug and surrender closure. Can they save it by dropping adherence, or restating it, or leaning on the method-relativity? Say honestly whether any of those moves is ad hoc or whether one of them actually works.
