A committee drafting a constitution for a new polity must settle the boundary problem: by what criterion are persons included in the demos that authorises collective decisions? Consider the five criteria below, each proposed by a different member. Rank them from most to least defensible as the single primary criterion, giving a brief justification for each placement, and state whether any pair could be combined without incoherence.

(a) Subjection: all and only those whose life-prospects are materially constrained by the polity's enforceable rules belong.

(b) Stakes: inclusion is owed to anyone for whom the decision's worst outcome would be catastrophic rather than merely inconvenient, irrespective of residence.

(c) Recursive authorisation: the demos comprises whoever would be entitled to vote on whether to extend membership, so that the body is settled by the very body it defines.

(d) Exit cost: those for whom leaving the polity would be practically impossible are members, while those who could leave cheaply are not.

(e) Contribution: membership follows those whose ongoing participation is causally necessary for the polity to function, for instance by supplying labour or revenue it cannot replace.

Assume no criterion may be supplemented by another except where you explicitly say so.
