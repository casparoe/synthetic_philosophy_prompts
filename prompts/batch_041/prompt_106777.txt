I chair the board of our city's public housing authority, and at Thursday's meeting we vote on a proposal that has split us. Our estates are in poor shape: lifts break and stay broken for months, repairs queue for a season, and residents tell us they no longer feel safe in the stairwells after dark. A consultancy has offered to install a management system modelled on one used by a foreign city-state. Under it, estates would be run by a professional corps enforcing strict rules on upkeep, noise, subletting and refuse, and the comparable estates that have adopted it report markedly better safety and satisfaction figures. The price is political. For five years, our elected resident councils would be dissolved and replaced by boards appointed by the authority, and tenants would lose the votes they currently hold on maintenance budgets, allocation priorities and eviction appeals. The tenant representative on our board argues that no improvement in cleanliness or safety can make this legitimate, because it removes the very thing that makes an estate self-governing; our finance lead replies that a tenant who cannot get a broken lift repaired has already lost more real power than any ballot, and that what a housing authority owes its residents is a decent place to live, not a committee. I have to circulate a short written opinion before the vote, recommending a course of action the board can actually adopt rather than a statement of principles. Please draft it, with a clear recommendation and a time frame for it, engaging the tenant representative's position as seriously as the finance lead's rather than treating it as an obstacle to be managed.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
