In a metaontology seminar, a colleague argued that disputes about whether composite objects exist, or whether numbers exist, are merely verbal, using the analogy of arguing whether a hot dog is a sandwich. On her view, the ontological dispute is like the sandwich dispute: there is a word with flexible boundaries, a practice of applying it, and no further fact to discover, so once we see this, the apparent metaphysical disagreement dissolves. She concludes that the only remaining question is which way of talking is more useful. I want an audit of the analogy rather than a verdict on her conclusion. Lay out the correspondence: what plays the role of the bread, the filling, the term sandwich, the concept, the community's classificatory practice, and the alleged fact of the matter in the ontological case. Then identify where the analogy breaks and what the sandwich case quietly imports, especially concerning the role of quantification, the possibility of rival languages, and whether the disputants can be interpreted as using the same words with different meanings. Separate the breaks that matter to her deflationary conclusion from those that do not, and say whether that conclusion survives once they are on the table. If a better analogy is available for the metaontological point, give it and explain what it improves.
