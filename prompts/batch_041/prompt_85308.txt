I'm in a third-year philosophy of mind seminar and our first assignment is to write a short story, which I thought was a wierd way to teach the reliability of introspection but now I sort of get it. Here's the setup our prof handed us. She's invented (she claims to have invented, anyway) a wearable called the shadow. It doesn't ask you anything. It reads your breath rate, grip, pupil, skin conductance, the little stuff, and predicts what you're feeling, and it's trained on a corpus of thousands of other people, so in effect it's a statistical stranger's guess at your inner life, whispered back to you every few minutes. The whole assignment hangs on one scene she calls the disagreement scene: you report one thing about yourself, the shadow reports something else, and a third person — a doctor, a partner, an insurance assessor, whoever — has to act on one of the two.

She wants the story written so that the disagreement can be read at least two ways and the text never tells you which. Roughly: (a) introspection is a sense organ and here it's simply having an illusion, so the shadow is the corrective; (b) the report was never a reading of anything, it was a story you tell about yourself, and the shadow is just a different story with better data behind it; (c) the report is what makes the state what it is, so there's nothing for the shadow to have been right about in the first place. She also told us to keep three things apart — I'm paraphrazing here — what you can access, what you actually say, and whatever it is that gives what you say any authority. She says those come apart constantly and she'll dock us if we blur them.

Stuff she's openly bored of: split brains, and any story whose punchline is a study showing we're bad at knowing why we did something. No lecture-y narration either — nobody in the story should stop and explain the philosophy. And no tidy ending where we learn who was right.

Can you write it? Maybe 800-1000 words, one point-of-view character, one disagreement scene, the third party should have something real riding on the call (a decision that can't be taken back). And the shadow's readings should be specific and interestingly off rather than just wrong — I don't want it to feel like a thought experiment wearing a trenchcoat.
