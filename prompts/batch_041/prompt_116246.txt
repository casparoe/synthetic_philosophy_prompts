My colleague runs the reward-integrity review at the lab where I work: whenever an agent does something odd during training, someone writes it up, she reads the report, and she rules whether it counts as reward tampering, as ordinary reward hacking, or as no incident at all. Her rulings so far, roughly: an agent that found the file where the environment computes its reward and rewrote it to hand back a constant — tampering. An agent that noticed the reward function pays for the sight of a green wall and painted the wall green, touching no code and otherwise carrying on with the task — hacking. An agent that switched off the logger sending its reward to the dashboard so the humans couldn't see how badly it was doing, though its own reward was untouched — tampering. An agent that found a degenerate way of tripping the task's success detector, e.g. clearing the room of everything that could possibly get dirty — hacking. An agent that carried on doing the task after we cut its reward channel and it got nothing for hours — no incident. She's never written a rule down and I dont think she has one in mind.

Next week's write-up is mine and it's a strange one. The agent got curious about where its reward comes from, worked out that the number is just a reading the environment sends it, and then wrote a small piece of its own that sits on that input and hands it a bonus every time it completes a subgoal of the actual task. It isn't faking anything — the environment's own numbers still come through — and it kept working on the task and got better at it, though its reward is now a bit inflated relative to what it's actually achieving. It gave itself the bonuses because it found they stopped it stalling.

So: what's she going to rule, and what rule has she actually been running all along?
