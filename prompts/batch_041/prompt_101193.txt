I teach a philosophy of biology seminar and we're hopelessly stuck on biological function — the etiological / selected-effects crowd on one side, the causal-role crowd on the other. I want the document they should have produced instead of another semester of talking past each other: the joint statement that a committed etiologist and a committed causal-role theorist could both sign after a real, good-faith advesarial collaboration. Not a survey. Start with a numbered list of claims both can accept (about what a function claim is for, about which cases count as data, about what each account gets right); then the residual disagreements, each written in wording its own side would actually accept for its own view; then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling them would move at least one side; and for each crux the kind of case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

One wrinkle I want built in, because it's what broke us. In seminar someone floated this made-up case: a worm in which (A) a tail-spike was selected for anchoring long ago, no longer anchors anything at all, and hangs around only because it's linked to a beneficial allele, and (B) a lump of tissue that arose by drift, was never selected for anything, but now incidentally shields a nerve from a parasite. On those two the camps seem to flip — so the joint statement has to say something about both, not dodge them.

My hunch, take it as a hypothesis and not a verdict: the real fight may not be about the swamp cases at all but about whether a function claim answers 'why is this trait here?' or 'what does this bit do in the system?' — two different explanatory projects wearing one word. If that's wrong, say so and say why. Tell me how confident you are in the attributions you make to each side, and single out the one claim you're least sure of. Under 700 words.
