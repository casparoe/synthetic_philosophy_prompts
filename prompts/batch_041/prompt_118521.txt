I am setting the final exam for a final-year undergraduate seminar on the ethics of war and need a single essay question on the moral equality of combatants — 90 minutes, 25 marks — that cannot be answered by reciting the familiar Walzer-versus-McMahan exchange. The crux I want to isolate is that "equality" in that doctrine slides between three distinct claims: symmetry of justification (both sides are permitted to kill), symmetry of liability (both sides' combatants are killable without being wronged), and symmetry of excuse (both sides kill wrongly but blamelessly). These come apart as soon as one drops the assumption that exactly one side's cause is just.

Build the question around a stipulated case and a stipulated principle, both fully specified inside the question so that no outside reading is required. The case: a war of mutual aggression between two states, each fighting for territory it has no claim to and each having initiated force against the other, with conscripted soldiers on both sides who face severe penalties for refusing to serve. The principle — call it Liability by Reciprocity — has two clauses: (i) a combatant is liable to lethal attack if and only if they participate in a reciprocal practice of lethal threat, irrespective of the justice of their cause; (ii) duress short of making refusal impossible is not a defence against liability, though it is a defence against blame.

The question should require candidates to say what the moral equality of combatants can still amount to under this principle, which of the three readings survive and which collapse, what follows for non-combatants on both sides and for post-war punishment, and whether the doctrine is thereby vindicated, merely restated, or refuted. Supply a model answer of roughly 650 words that takes a definite line and argues rather than cites, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points worth 25 marks in total, each accompanied by a one-line note naming the specific error that should cost a candidate marks, so that the scheme separates an A answer from a B.
