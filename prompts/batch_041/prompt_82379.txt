Been meaning to write this up as a blog post for months and I keep going back and forth on whether it actually says anything, so I'm dumping the draft here for comments before I post it and get eaten alive.

The post is about the hinge of history thing — the longtermist claim that we're living at the most influential time in history, that the next century or so settles the whole shape of the future. Here's the argument I want to make.

First: everyone making this argument slides between two seperate claims. (1) Our actions have enormous absolute expected value, because the future is astronomically long, so even a small nudge now gets multiplied out into huge numbers of lives. (2) This period is unusually pivotal — more pivotal than other periods — because of lock-in, one-shot technological transitions, whatever.

My line is that (1) is true by construction and so does no work. Multiply any finite amount of influence by 10^30 future lives and you get a big number. You could run the same calculation for a peasant in 1200 and get a big number too. So (1) can't tell us anything about whether now is special. All the action is in (2).

Then the constructive part. I want to define the hinge weight of a period as the fraction of the expected long-run value of the future that's still contingent on choices made inside that period — not the absolute amount, the fraction. And I want to argue that hinge weight is high wherever irreversible decisions remain available, and collapses toward zero once the last irreversible fork is behind us. Which means the question of whether we're at the hinge becomes: is there still an irreversible fork downstream that we can actually reach? And that has a weird implication — the hinge might be ahead of us, and might look extremely boring. One regulatory decision, one committee, one launch. Not a dramatic moment at all.

Last bit, and this is the part I'm least sure about. If we really are at the hinge, then our beliefs about being at the hinge are themselves pivotal, because they're what drives the action. So the thesis feeds back into the thing it's describing. I've been calling it the reflexive hinge. Not sure if that's a real point or just a cute loop.

What I want: tear it apart. Is the (1)/(2) split real, or am I splitting hairs? Does the fraction-vs-absolute move rescue anything, or am I just picking a normalization that makes my conclusion true by definition? Is the reflexive hinge substantive? What's the strongest objection I'm not seeing? And should I cut the 10^30 bit — I worry it comes off as smug. Don't need a reading list, I've read the stuff, I just want to know if the post is worth posting.

(tone note: I agree with the longtermists on the object level, so I dont want to sound like I'm doing a drive-by dunk.)
