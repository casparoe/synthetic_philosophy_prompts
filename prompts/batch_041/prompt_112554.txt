I am a final-year undergraduate reading philosophy and the student representative on my university's Scholarships Committee. We must award a single full-cost scholarship, and the two finalists are, on every criterion the policy specifies, indistinguishable: same grades, same references, same interview scores, same financial need. The policy expressly forbids using any criterion not listed, and it says the committee must reach a decision by Friday. The chair has called this our Buridan's ass problem and proposes that we simply draw lots; two other members insist that choosing arbitrarily is irrational and that we should defer the award for a year. I have to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting.

Please draft that opinion. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and give the committee reasons it can act on. In doing so, engage seriously with the strongest objection to your recommendation. Also address the distinction I encountered in my metaphysics course between two options being equally good and their being on a par: where the finalists are not identical in every respect, but neither is better and they are not exactly equal either, does that distinction change what the committee may rationally do? Do not simply restate the policy; tell us what we should do and why.
