My dad's been in the ICU three weeks now and my brother and I keep going back and fourth about when a person actually counts as dead, so I'd like you to write me a short story that gets into it.

Here's the setup I want you to use. Near-future hospital, and they've got this machine — call it a perfusion bridge — that keeps blood and oxygen moving through a body after the heart has quit for good. Ward protocol says if the heart hasn't restarted after six minutes of no circulation, the patient is called dead and the bridge gets switched off. But the same machine runs the other way too: it can keep a body's circulation going more or less indefinitely after the brain has gone. So the building ends up holding two flavors of "dead" at once, the ones whose hearts stopped and the ones whose brains did, and that six-minute line is just a number somebody picked.

Tell it from the daughter's point of view — she works that same ward as a nurse and knows the protocol cold. I want one patient where the two ways of being dead disagree with each other, and she's the one who notices. No explainer tacked onto the end, and don't tell me which definition is the right one; I want the story to make the mess feel real and leave me squirming a little. Maybe 1000 words.
