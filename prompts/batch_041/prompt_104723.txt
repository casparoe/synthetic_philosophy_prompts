I'm a retired chemical engineer — spent 35 years mostly on catalytic surfaces, so I'm very comfortable with the idea that a system can be described at several levels at once and that the "right" level depends on what you're trying to explain. Since retiring I've been sitting in on philosophy seminars, and I've gotten a bit obsessed with Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment (from I Am a Strange Loop, I believe — the billiard table full of tiny magnetic marbles he calls simms, which clump into larger structures, the simmballs, and he asks who shoves whom around inside the careenium). My reading group has been fighting about this for weeks. The reductionist camp says the simmball-level story is just a convenient summary of what the simms are doing, that all the actual pushing is simm-on-simm, and that talk of symbols pushing each other around is at best a predictive shortcut and at worst a superstition. The other camp says no, the higher-level patterns are doing real causal work — they constrain and channel the substrate, and you lose genuine explanatory and causal information if you insist on staying below. I keep going back and forth and I'd like someone to actually settle it rather than hand me another "it depends on your interests" shrug.

So here's what I want. Take on the role of a philosopher of mind who also knows the metaphysics of causation well enough not to hand-wave — someone who can talk about higher-level causation, realization, and multiple realizability without just reciting labels. Before you start writing, brainstorm a bit for yourself about what the strongest version of each side actually is, because I don't want a strawman on either side; then write me an essay that weighs the two positions and lands on a defended verdict. I want you to address the hard cases head on: whether the simmball level adds any causal oomph or is purely descriptive, whether the answer changes if we imagine intervening on simmballs directly versus intervening on simms, and whether anything important turns on the fact that the careenium's walls are being nudged by the outside world (so the system is representing something). Also say something about whether your verdict would carry over to real brains, where we can't actually do the simm-level intervention, and to artificial systems, where in some sense we can. Don't hedge at the end — tell me which camp you'd sign up for and why the other side's best argument still fails. Aim for something substantial, and please keep the jargon to a minimum; I'm an amateur and I'll notice if you're hiding behind vocabulary.
