I wonder whether you can help me pin down something that has been nagging at me for a while. I was talking with a friend who works in astrophysics, and she described how she estimates quantities far too complicated to compute directly — say, the rate of some rare event. The method, as she told it, is to break the quantity into a handful of factors and then multiply them together as though each were independent of the others, even when she has no evidence for that and would not claim it as a belief. When I pressed her on whether the independence was actually true, she said something like: it is not that I think they are independent, it is that assuming they are is the default thing to do when you have nothing better, and the errors tend to cancel out anyway. Since then I keep noticing the same move everywhere. Someone argues that a complicated catastrophe is essentially impossible by multiplying a long string of small probabilities, each of which would have to hold for the disaster to occur, again with no attempt to check that the failures are not correlated. I catch myself reassuring myself that two things I cannot verify are probably unrelated, purely because I have no information linking them. And when the assumption turns out to be wrong — when the factors are in fact strongly correlated — the estimate can be off by orders of magnitude, which suggests the default was doing genuine work rather than being a harmless formality.

What puzzles me is the epistemic status of this move. It is not held as a belief about the world, since nobody would defend it if challenged, and it is not merely a computational convenience, since it demonstrably shapes conclusions. It seems to be a standing methodological default about how to proceed under ignorance of the relations between quantities, rather than a rule for how to apportion credence among hypotheses. So my question is whether this has an established name and a literature of its own, or whether I need to coin one. I am aware it sits near several things I half-know — the principle of indifference, maximum-entropy reasoning, and the ordinary statistical notion of an independence assumption — but it does not seem to be any of them, and I would like the difference made precise rather than gestured at. If there is an established term, tell me what it is and where the boundaries of the concept fall; if there is not, please define one, and be explicit about how it differs from those neighbours, what its scope is, and how it behaves at the edges — for instance, in cases where the factors are known to be dependent but the presumption still yields a useful answer, or where it is invoked across levels of description rather than among peers.
