I'm putting together a decision tree for a small reading group I run on value theory, and I keep tripping over the same thing. People say "incommensurable" and it turns out they mean five different things. So: when someone faces a choice where both options are genuinely worth having and neither looks better than the other, what is actually going on?

I want a tree that starts from a commitment a person can honestly make without knowing any of the labels — something like 'I've faced a choice like this, and thinking harder didn't settle it' — and funnels them to a position. Build the first cut around where the hardness lives. If it's in us, split again: is it ignorance, where there's a fact about which is better and we just can't get at it, or does the chooser have to supply the ordering that the values don't? If it's in the values, split on whether the two options are incomparable outright or merely comparable in a weaker sense than better-or-worse — a fourth comparative relation that's neither equality nor incomparability. And where they really are incomparable, split on what reason is doing: permitting either choice and that's the end of it; permitting either but with something genuinely lost, so regret is apt; or falling silent so the will has to decide.

Two things to build in. First, a leaf for a view I've only half worked out: that the comparison between the two options doesn't exist until you choose — before the choice there's no fact about which is better, and the commitment itself brings the ordering into being. Slot it where it belongs and tell me whether its genuinely a new position or a dressed-up version of one already on the tree. Second, run one case through the whole thing at the end: someone with a single free year deciding between caring for a parent with dementia and finishing the work their entire career will be judged on. Say which leaves make either choice wrong, which make both permitted, and what each says about whether the regret afterwards is warranted.

Phrase every node as a question someone could answer without knowing where it leads. Each leaf gets the position — a name if there's a standard one, otherwise a line of description plus a short handle you coin — but no attributions, no citations, no 'as so-and-so argues'. I want the shape of the space, not the literature. Finish by listing the combinations nobody occupies, with a word on whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree can't actually seperate.

Under 800 words, as an indented tree with a line or two per leaf.
