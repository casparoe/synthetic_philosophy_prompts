By background I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've been following the long-running dispute between those who treat introspective reports of one's own mental states as legitimate evidence and those who hold that such reports are mostly post-hoc confabulation. Please draft the joint statement these two camps could sign after an honest adversarial collaboration. I'd like a numbered section of claims both sides can accept — about what introspection is, what it can and cannot be evidence for, and what would count as genuine progress — followed by the points of disagreement that remain, each phrased so that the camp holding it would accept the wording of its own position. Identify the cruxes, and for each crux give one concrete case or test that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance. For the central test, use this scenario: participants solve two versions of the same problem, one answerable by a simple rule they are given, the other requiring an unstated insight, and afterward they report whether their answer came from deliberate rule-following or from a sudden intuition — with the twist that the assignment of problems to the two versions is manipulated covertly, so participants cannot know from the surface of the task which process was actually engaged. Both camps can agree that the match between the covert task structure and the self-reports is informative; I want their competing predictions about that match, and the crux behind them, spelled out. If part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
