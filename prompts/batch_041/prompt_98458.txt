Ok so I'm doing a one-off session at my local ceramics studio — adults, zero philosophy background, but they know clay like the back of their hands. Topic is the statue-and-clay / material-constitution thing. Fifty minutes, no slides, just me and a lump of clay on the table. I dont need you to explain the problem to me, I know it. I need you to plan the explanation.

First: what has to come before what? My instinct is they need to feel the puzzle in their hands before I name it — the lump was there before I threw the pot, I smash the pot, the clay's still there, and yet while the pot's sitting there we'd all happily say the pot and the clay are "the same thing". So what's the order of moves after that, and what actually depends on what (what has to be in place before the next bit even makes sense)?

The example that carries it — I want it to come out of their own craft, not the textbook lump. Options I'm chewing on: (a) reclaiming — a pot that hasnt been fired gets smashed, slaked down, re-wedged, and thrown into a new pot; is anything of the old pot in the new one, and is it the same clay? (b) firing — soft grey clay goes in, and what comes out is ceramic, arguably not even the same material anymore, so did the pot survive the kiln or did it get replaced? Pick the one that should carry the whole session and say why, what it has to have and not have to do the job, and why the other one is worse (or maybe why it's the better second example).

Misconceptions — my guesses: "same stuff so it's the same thing"; "the pot is just the clay in a shape"; "once it's fired it's a totally new object"; maybe "it's all just atoms anyway so the question is fake". Which of those will actually turn up, in what order, and at exactly which point in the sequence do I head each one off before it sets?

And what should I openly refuse to get into at this level — I'm thinking the four-dimensionalist / temporal-parts stuff and anything about grounding, but tell me what else is a rabbit hole for this crowd.

Last thing: one question I can throw at them near the end that'll tell me whether they actually got the difference between being the same thing and being made of the same stuff, rather than just nodding along.
