ok so I've become the person in my friend group who insists that cause prioritization just is the importance/tractability/neglectedness thing: score a cause on how big the problem is, how much progress you get per unit of effort, and how few people are already on it, multiply, fund the top of the list. I think thats basically right and I keep defending it. But I want the case against, properly. Not "the numbers are hard to estimate," I already know that. I mean observations, arguments, or cases that should make me think the framework is the wrong shape, not just badly applied.

Stuff I keep circling and can't resolve: is it a product or a weighted sum, and does it matter? If it's a product, a cause that's enormous but currently near-intractable scores about zero and gets dropped, which feels backwards if the whole point is to make it tractable over time. Neglectedness seems to be doing double duty -- it's both a fact about the world (few people work on X) and a claim about marginal returns (the next unit of effort buys more here), and those can come apart; a cause can be neglected in aggregate and crowded at the margin, or the reverse. The three factors don't look independant to me either: a cause that gets more tractable attracts people, so its neglectedness falls, so the score has a feedback loop the multiplication can't see. And I genuinely don't know what "importance" is measured in -- if it's expected far-future value, the other two factors start to look like rounding errors. Plus the granularity thing: "AI safety" and "AI safety but only the interpretability part" are the same cause at different resolutions, and they get different neglectedness scores, so who picks the unit?

So give me the defeaters, ranked from the ones that should actually move me to the ones a defender gets to wave away. Tell me which of these a committed ITN person already concedes and just lives with, and whether the framework has quietly insulated itself -- every counterexample becoming "you misjudged a factor" -- and whether that's a real problem for a heuristic or just what a heuristic is. I'd rather keep it and pay a cost than drop it, so price the cost.
