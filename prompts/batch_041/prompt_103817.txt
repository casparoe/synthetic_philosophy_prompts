So my housemate has gotten really into "s-risks" lately — she keeps saying that a universe stuffed with suffering could be worse than no universe at all, and that we spend all our energy worrying about humanity getting wiped out when we should be worrying about the opposite, and honestly I half believe her but I can't tell where I actually land. Now our Sunday discussion group has fractured into camps over it and everybody is just repeating whatever podcast they heard last, so I want to build one of those "which position do you actually hold?" quizzes that sorts people by what they say about cases rather than by what label they like. I'm the one stuck making it. Can you write it for me?

Nine questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the named positions. The cases I want in there, because these are the ones that keep derailing our arguments: (1) a world where a vast number of minds exist and are mildly, chronically unhappy — bored, anxious, never quite in pain but never okay — and this is produced as a side effect of something nobody intended to be cruel; (2) the same world except the suffering is deliberately engineered by an agent that enjoys it; (3) extinction now versus a small chance of an enormous amount of future suffering; (4) a case where you can either spare one person from a lifetime of agony or give a million people a mild improvement, with no way to trade one off against the other; (5) whether a world with more total happiness but a worse worst-off life is better than one with less happiness and a better worst-off life. Build the thing from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names — first lay out the questions that actually split the positions (does suffering count as bad in itself or only as it attaches to someone who is being wronged? does the number of future minds matter at all, or only the people who'd exist anyway? does it matter whether the suffering is intended or incidental? can disvalue be added up across separate lives at all?), then place the actual positions in the resulting space. I need these to show up as leaves somewhere: suffering-focused negative consequentialism, plain total utilitarianism, a prioritarian view that weights the worse off more without making happiness worthless, a person-affecting view that only cares about whoever would exist regardless, and a rights/deontological view where the total never justifies the wrong. If some combinations are empty, tell me whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just undefended, and flag any two leaves your quiz can't really pry apart — I'd rather know that than have you fake a question that seperates them.

One more thing: don't ask anything that just checks whether someone can recite a definition, and don't lean on specific papers or authors, because half the group reads alot and half of them definately don't and I don't want it to turn into a trivia contest. It should work for someone who's never heard the word "s-risk" before but is willing to think about the cases.
