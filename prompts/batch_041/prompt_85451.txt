ok so im not a philosopher, i just read a lot of this stuff on the bus, but ive been stuck on this one for months and i want to try to get it into a poem because arguing with myself about it isnt working.

the arguement i keep running into goes like this. the reason my projects matter isnt only that i finish them or enjoy them, its that the world carries on after me and other people keep caring about the same things -- someone still argues about the same question, still plays the same songs, still waters the plants. take away everyone who comes after and the argument says the whole thing goes flat, not just because i die but because the point of what i was doing was living partly in other peoples hands. then the part that wrecks me: if everyone goes, theres nobody there to lose anything. no one is sad at the end. so wheres the loss. and yet it reads as a loss to me and i dont think im just being sentimental.

so, the poem. i want it in that chained shape where the last line of each stanza is the first line of the next one, so the poem keeps handing itself forward -- i think its called chain verse, i might have the name wrong, doesnt matter, thats the constraint. 4 or 5 stanzas, so 16-20 lines. the important bit is the very last line: there is no stanza after it to pick it up. i want that line to read like it was written for whoever comes next, like a hand off, an instruction, a here you go, and then theres nobody. thats the whole poem right there, so please dont soften it with a line that wraps things up afterwards. also dont use the words extinction or legacy or humanity, i want it concrete: hands, tools, songs, food, a specific question somebody argued about at a kitchen table.

ive been sorting things into two piles and i want the poem to test both piles instead of picking one. pile one: things that only count if somebody later knows about them (a promise you keep for someone whos gone, building something for a kid, keeping a record of anything). pile two: things that are done the second theyre done (a good meal, playing a tune right, finally understanding something). if the last person alive does a pile two thing, does it still count. i definately dont know, and i dont think the poem should pretend to. so give me at least one of each, concretely, and let them sit next to each other without a verdict.

last thing. someone always says this just shows were built to want an audience, not that the audience is part of the good. i cant answer that, so the poem cant either. leave the door open.

somebody whos read this stuff should recognise the problem and not wince at how its put, and somebody whos read none of it should just find it a good poem.
