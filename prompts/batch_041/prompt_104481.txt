I run food procurement for a mid-sized university dining service (roughly 8,000 meals a day) and I've been voluntold onto our "sustainable sourcing" working group, which in practice means I'm the one who has to sign off on the animal-protein contracts every quarter and defend it to the dining director and whoever shows up to the open forum. I read way too many rationalist/EA blogs, so I already know the standard moves, but the more I read the more it feels like every proposed procedure is either "eat less meat" or "eat less beef," and neither one survives contact with the actually weird corner of this debate. Two things keep tripping me up: (a) the beef-vs-chicken question, where chicken looks worse per calorie if you're counting lives, better if you're counting total suffering, and the whole ranking flips depending on how you weigh one broiler's short rotten life against one steer's longer one; and (b) the crop-cultivation point, that plant calories aren't free either since harvesting mows down field animals, so "swap beef for beans" isn't obviously a win and might even be a loss if you take wild/field-animal suffering seriously. I want a procedure my group can actually run each quarter, not another essay.

So: give me a decision procedure. The questions to ask in order (starting from what we're buying and in what quantity, since volume obviously matters), what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure simply says "this input doesn't change the ranking, move on," and where it stops and hands the thing to a human because it's a values call (like how to trade one animal's suffering against another's, or a life against a life). It needs to run in under an hour by someone who isn't a philosopher, it should say what to do when we genuinely can't get a number (which will be most of the time), and it has to be honest about which steps are just judgment calls wearing a lab coat. Then run it on two concrete contracts: switching our beef patties over to chicken, and switching our chicken over to a plant-based protein, with the field-animal thing switched on in both. And flag at least one case where the procedure tells me something I really won't want to hear. I'll fill in my own references, so don't bother with a reading list.
