Write a villanelle spoken by someone who is, at this very moment, doing the very thing for which they are blaming a friend — not a reformed hypocrite recalling an old fault, but a concurrent one, blaming while the fault continues. Let the two refrains be the two claims the speaker cannot give up: that the friend's wrong is real and stays real whoever names it, and that the speaker's own ongoing conduct strips them of the right to name it. The form must do the argumentative work — the refrains should return in alternation, each seeming to answer the other, and in the closing quatrain they should stand side by side with neither winning, since the point is that the truth of the judgement and the forfeiture of the standing do not cancel one another. Keep in view the distinction between blaming as a judgement (that someone is blameworthy) and blaming as a demand or reproach addressed to them, which is what hypocrisy can take away: the speaker may keep the first while being barred from the second, and a reader who knows the debate on standing to blame should recognise the position, while one who does not should still have a poem. Do not resolve it with pots and kettles, glass houses, or any tidy moral. Nineteen lines, in British English.
