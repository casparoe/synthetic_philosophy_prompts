Someone I argue with online keeps insisting imprecise probabilities are incoherent, and he gave me this argument for it, and annoyingly I cant find the loose thread:

(1) A person's doxastic state is fully captured by how likely they take each proposition to be.
(2) If you take H to be somewhere in the range 0.3 to 0.7 likely, then either one specific number in that range is your real credence and you're just unsure which, or you have no real credence in H at all.
(3) In the first case there's a genuine probability distribution over which number is yours, and by the law of total probability your credence in H is the average of those numbers, i.e. one precise value -- so that branch collapses into a precise credence.
(4) In the second case you simply don't have a degree of belief in H, and an agent with no degree of belief in H isn't a subject for rational assessment here anyway.
(5) So imprecise credences either reduce to precise ones or they aren't credences.
(6) Therefore imprecise probability is either superfluous or not a theory of belief.

I don't buy (6) -- it feels like it's throwing out something real. If I had to sacrifice something I'd sacrifice (6) and keep (1), but I want to know what I'm actually paying. Which step is doing the illegitimate work, why does it look so innocent, and what does it cost me to reject it?
