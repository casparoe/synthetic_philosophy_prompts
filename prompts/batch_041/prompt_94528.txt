Ok, handicap principle question for you. Zahavi's old line, as I understand it: a peacocks tail is honest because it's a burden — only a genuinely fit bird can afford to carry it around, so the tail is a truthful advertisement of quality. Fine. But I'm not conviced the interesting work is being done by the waste, and I want you to compare the handicap principle with another way of getting honest signaling, which I'll state as plainly as I can.

Imagine a sender and a receiver whose interests line up: the receiver wants to know the sender's true state, and the sender does better the more accurately the receiver knows it. A bee dancing the direction of a nectar source is the sort of case I mean — the dancer is part of the same hive, so a misleading dance hurts the dancer too, and nothing needs to be burned to keep the dance honest. On this picture, honesty is a product of aligned interests, not of cost. Cost might be present, but it isn't holding anything up.

So compare these two accounts. Is the handicap principle a distinct explanation of honest signaling, or is it just the special case you get once interests diverge? And press on this in particular: I've heard it said that the handicap principle, written properly, boils down to a claim about how the cost of signaling varies across senders — the signal has to be cheaper at the margin for the better-quality sender — and that peacock-style 'waste' isn't the essence at all. If that's right, what survives of the original idea? Was the waste a red herring all along, or does it do something the marginal-cost formulation quietly drops? Related worry: some signals seem honest simply because they're impossible to fake — a deep call needs a big larynx — which is a third route to honesty that doesn't look like either of the above.

Then: give me a concrete case of a signal that's costly and honest but where the cost isn't doing any honesty work, and say what the cost is for instead. And notice that in the chick-begging case the two parties' interests are only partly aligned, whereas in the dancing bee they're aligned almost completely. Is it a coincidence that cost starts to matter exactly as alignment breaks down? Don't just tell me the two views are compatible — take a side.
