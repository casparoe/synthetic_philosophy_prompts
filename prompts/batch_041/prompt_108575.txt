I just started at a lab where we evaluate AI answers by chopping each one into separate claims, farming each claim out to a different person on a fixed time budget, and then averaging their judgments into a score for the whole answer. Nobody sees anyone else's claim. My boss calls it factored cognition and says its just what one careful reader does, only faster. The more I sit with it the more I think the whole setup only hangs together if you already believe something about what thinking is: that a hard judgment is really a tree of easy ones, that each little judgment can be made in isolation without losing anything, and that a crowd of people good at the little pieces adds up to someone good at the whole. What are the real philosophical commitments here? Which ones are load-bearing versus just convenient, does the practice need the strong version of any of them or only a weak one, and what falls apart if the strong version is false? Some of us also use the same picture as an idealization of what a deliberator would do given unlimited time and self-trust, while others treat it as a literal engineering recipe — does sliding between those two uses smuggle in an assumption? And tell me where the practice is underdetermined, i.e. what other views of deliberation would license it just as well.
