I am to circulate a position statement before next week's reading group on agency and mechanism, and what I want from you is an audit rather than a defence of my view. Below is where I have landed. Assume I am not bluffing: I would rather abandon a commitment than paper over a conflict, and I do not yet know which one is doing the damage.

(1) Determinism is irrelevant to freedom. Whether my choices are caused is a separate question from whether they are free. Freedom is a matter of the kind of control exercised, not of the absence of causes.

(2) The digger wasp is unfree. As the story is standardly told, she leaves the paralysed cricket at the threshold, enters the burrow to inspect it, and if the cricket is moved a few inches in her absence she drags it back to the threshold and inspects the burrow again, cycling as long as one keeps moving it. I take the natural history to be embellished and the example's persistence to be rhetorical rather than evidential; I am using it as a template for a kind of control architecture. The signature of her unfreedom is not that she is caused to act, but that the alteration in the world cannot be registered by her procedure as a reason to do anything else.

(3) What she lacks, and what freedom requires, is what I will call reflexive flexibility: the capacity to take one's own routines as objects of assessment, to notice that one is doing something because one was triggered, and to act differently on the strength of that notice. First-order flexibility is not enough — having many responses, being plastic, learning. A system can be endlessly plastic and still contain nothing that can revise the procedure which generates the responses.

(4) I am a physicalist. Everything we do is implemented by fixed mechanisms, and there is no further ingredient.

(5) Reflexive flexibility is itself mechanisable, and it comes in degrees. Systems that monitor and revise their own procedures are ordinary engineering, and I see no principled bar to a machine having the capacity in (3) to a high degree.

(6) For all that, there is a real and non-arbitrary line between the wasp and us. We can be brought to see the point; she cannot. Responsibility presupposes the capacity to respond to reasons, and she lacks it.

(7) The line matters practically, not only conceptually. It marks the difference between addressing someone as a locus of reasons and managing them as a system, and I want that difference to track (3) rather than to be merely conventional.

I am not troubled by the thought that we might be sphexish all the way up — that our own stepping back is itself a routine we cannot step out of. If that is a genuine possibility, it seems to me a claim about the limits of a particular architecture rather than about freedom as such.

Now the audit. First, tell me which of these are actually in tension and which only appear to be, and for each apparent conflict name the distinction that dissolves it. Second, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give, what the cheapest repair is, and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the list. Third, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what it would leave standing. Do not tell me the package is coherent unless it is.
