Write an essay adjudicating the status of the Reflection principle. By the Reflection principle I mean the constraint according to which a rational agent's credence in any proposition A, conditional on the proposition that her own credence in A takes the value x, must itself be x — so that an agent who is certain she will later be 70% confident that it will rain tomorrow is thereby committed to being 70% confident that it will rain tomorrow now. The principle is commonly associated with van Fraassen, though the essay should not depend on any particular attribution. Reach a verdict on whether this constraint is a genuine requirement of rationality, a merely permissible ideal, or something a rational agent may, and sometimes should, violate.

To keep the discussion away from examples that have been worn smooth by repetition, please organise the essay around the following stipulated principle, which I shall call the Calibration Asymmetry:

An agent's credence in a proposition A, conditional on her own credence in A being x, should equal x whenever x issues from a method she has no specific reason to distrust; but where she has positive evidence that her credences on matters of this kind tend to be over-confident, that conditional credence should be strictly less extreme than x — pulled towards the midpoint of the scale — and symmetrically where the evidence points to under-confidence.

In the course of the essay, address each of the following. First, whether the Calibration Asymmetry is coherent at all, given that it conditions on the very credence it then discounts. Second, whether it can be reconciled with the diachronic Dutch-book and accuracy-based arguments usually taken to support Reflection, or whether it must reject them. Third, whether the Asymmetry, if coherent, is merely a restatement of ordinary conditionalisation on higher-order evidence or is a genuinely distinct requirement. Fourth, which of the two principles should give way when they conflict, and why.

Reach a definite conclusion and defend it against the strongest objection you can construct to your own position. Assume the reader knows the standard arguments and does not need them rehearsed; do not cite or list any literature. Write in British English.
