ok so this is kind of a weird question but it's been bugging me and my friends for like a month now and I can't let it go. Our maths teacher (who also runs the philosophy club, small school, we don't have many options) told us at the start of the week: there will be a surprise test on one day next week, and you won't be able to work out in advance which day it is. So at lunch we did the classic thing: it can't be friday, because by thursday night we'd know it's friday and then it wouldn't be a surprise; so it can't be thursday either, cause then by wednesday night... and so on all the way back, so no day works, so no test. Then he gave us the test on tuesday and we were all completely surprised. Now half the club says this proves our reasoning had a mistake somewhere and we should be able to find it. The other half says no, our reasoning was fine, the announcement was self-contradictory / he broke his own rule, and it only felt like a surprise because we'd argued ourselves out of believing our own proof. We've gone round and round for weeks and nobody moves an inch.

I looked it up and apparently people have been fighting about this for decades and nobody has really won. So I don't want you to just tell me which side is right, I want a diagnosis: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a straight logic mistake that one side is making and could just be pointed out? Is it that 'surprise' means two different things — like you literally cannot know, versus you just won't have figured it out — and everyone is talking past each other? Is it about whether you're allowed to use the teacher's announcement itself as a fact in your reasoning, so a disagreement about what counts as a premise? Or is it a real paradox that shows something is definately broken in how we think about knowledge and no amount of arguing will ever fix it?

My own guess (probably wrong) is that it's mostly a word dispute about 'surprise' and about what 'know' means, and that if we pinned those down it would collapse into something boring. Test that guess against what the two sides in our club actually say.

One more thing that might matter, I made up a twist version and it made everything worse. Suppose the teacher is a machine that never lies and can predict its own behaviour perfectly, and it announces the surprise test the same way. Or suppose instead the day is picked by a random number generator that nobody knows in advance, not even the teacher, and he still says 'there will be a surprise test next week.' In the random version it really does seem unpredictable on every day, so our whole argument never gets off the ground — but is that the same puzzle or did I just change the subject? And in the machine version, is the announcement even consistent in the first place?

What I want: a real diagnosis with reasons, built out of what each side actually says, and an honest answer about what would count as progress here — a distinction, a proof, a redefinition of a word, or just admitting the question is ill-posed and closing the club. And if you think there's genuinely no way to make progress, say that and say why.
