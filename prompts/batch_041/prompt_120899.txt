I'm writing a novel set in and around a jobcentre, and a line keeps coming at me from every direction: 'a hand up, not a handout.' Last week a councillor used it to argue against piloting a payment with no conditions attached for people who'd been out of work for years, and it landed like it had settled the whole thing. I don't buy that it settles anything, but I can't articulate why. Can you seperate out the different precise claims that sentence could actually be making — a factual claim about what gets people back into work, a claim about who deserves what, a reciprocity claim about what you owe in return for help, a claim about dependency, a claim about what taxpayers are entitled to demand, a claim about whats good for the claimant themselves — and for each one say whether it's a platitude, a genuine and contestable position, or just false? Then tell me which of those readings the councillor must have had in mind for his objection to go through, and which ones would actually tell against the pilot. I want it as a menu I can think with, not a single answer.
