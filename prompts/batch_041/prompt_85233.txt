me and my friend got into a three hour arguement last night about molinism and i woke up still mad about it, so i want a poem out of it instead of another round.

the bit we couldnt get past: molinism says that before god decides anything at all, before the decree, before he picks which world to make, theres already a fact about what each free creature would do in any situation you could put them in. thats middle knowledge, sitting between knowing whats possible and knowing what youve actually decided. so theres a true thing about you, and it was true before you existed, and before god chose anything. molinists point at the line where jesus says tyre and sidon would have repented if theyd seen the miracles done elsewhere, and call it one of these facts about a choice nobody ever made. my friend kept saying: true in virtue of what? you hadnt chosen, you werent around, and if god makes it true then its not free when you finally do it. i said thats just the price of the view. neither of us won.

so: a villanelle, 19 lines, proper form, dont fudge the refrains. voice it as one of those counterfactuals speaking for itself, one true thing about a free creature who hasnt been made yet. the two refrains should be the two things a molinist cant give up, one of them saying it was already true before the decree, the other saying the act its about is free. i want the closing two lines to be those refrains standing next to each other the way they do in the theory, and i want to feel that they only hold together if theres a third thing nobody has named yet. someone who knows the machinery should recognize it, not just the label.

then a section headed Notes on the choices, a paragraph or two: what you did, and whether the form fought the idea or helped it.
