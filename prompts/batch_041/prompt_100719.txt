I'm prepping a seminar on classification in psychiatry and I'm sick of the standard looping-effect example — people get a label, read up on it, start acting the part, experts revise the label, round and round. I want the mirror image, built to order. Give me one case where the loop runs the other way: no patient behaves any differently, nobody's performing anything, and yet the classification feeds back on itself so the category gets more entrenched — or better, so the categorys own numbers stop meaning anything. It has to run through an ordinary clinic or paperwork mechanism (charts, rating scales, billing, referral patterns, whatever), no sci-fi, and fit in one paragraph. Then walk it: what feeds into what at each step, whether the category is still tracking something real and in what sense, whether this is Hacking's "interactive kind" story (people react to being classified, unlike rocks) or a different animal, and what single observation would break the loop. Tell me which one feature is doing the work and what happens if I take it out — does the case just collapse back into the boring version? And does the same trick work for a non-psychiatric category like a disease or a species, or is something about psychiatric classification load-bearing here? This is for a group where a realist and a social constructionist will both be in the room, so I need a case where they genuinely disagree about what it shows, not one where the moral is just "so the category is made up." Start with the case itself in the first sentence — no preamble about Hacking or about looping effects, just the scenario, then the analysis.
