I am trying to decide whether any set of criteria of authenticity could, in principle, settle who the historical Jesus was. Before I evaluate particular criteria, write the desiderata that any such set must meet. Specify the cases it must get right—for instance, a saying that is multiply attested, embarrassing to the early church, and yet matches a commonplace of ancient Mediterranean moral teaching, with attestation that may be literary rather than independent; a deed that is historically plausible but appears only in a late source; and a tradition that passes the criterion of dissimilarity only because the relevant Judaism and early Christianity have been reconstructed in a way that already presupposes the conclusion. State the distinctions the criteria must preserve (authentic saying versus early tradition versus the very words of Jesus; independent attestation versus multiple redactional use; embarrassment versus dissimilarity), the constraints they cannot violate (not presupposing the conclusion, being usable on the extant sources, not treating theological motivation as automatically disqualifying, and allowing that the historical Jesus may have been a first-century Jewish apocalypticist rather than a Christian precursor), and flag any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, say how the standard criteria—dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, and historical plausibility—fare against the list, and identify which single requirement does most to show that the criteria cannot settle who he was.
