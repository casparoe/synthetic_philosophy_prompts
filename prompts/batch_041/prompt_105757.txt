I run a working group that mixes machine-learning engineers with policy and compliance staff, and I want a piece of sustained deadpan satire for one of our sessions: the marketing page (headline, subheads, a pricing table, a customer testimonial, an FAQ, a block of fine print) for a fictional SaaS company that sells fairness auditing to organizations deploying risk-scoring models. The page must be written entirely from the inside, with no winking asides and no authorial commentary: the company sincerely believes it is solving the problem, and its copy should be indistinguishable in register from real enterprise software marketing. The comedy has to come from the mathematics rather than from invented absurdity, so the three pricing tiers should quietly map onto statistical criteria of fairness that cannot all be satisfied at once, and the fine print should be accurate about exactly when they can and cannot be met together. I want a specialist to be able to point at each joke and name the result it is standing on, and to find the satire fair rather than cheap — an exaggeration of something the industry genuinely does — while a reader who has never encountered these results should still come away understanding that the vendor is selling a guarantee that cannot be delivered, and that the vendor knows it without saying so.

A few things I would like the page to handle correctly but never explain: the difference between criteria that constrain error rates across groups and criteria that constrain the calibration of scores within groups, and what happens to each when the two groups have different base rates; the difference between a criterion that a degenerate or constant predictor can satisfy trivially and one that it cannot; the fact that a slider between two desiderata encodes a policy choice rather than a technical fix, and the temptation to relocate that choice onto the customer through a configuration panel and an indemnity clause; the failure of we do not use group membership as a feature to settle anything; and the way the trade-off frontier shifts when the protected groups are intersected, since your pricing should scale with the number of groups the customer asks to be audited over. Include at least one clause that is a plain, unironized mathematical truth delivered in the flattest possible marketing register, one customer-support exchange whose true answer is that the customer's model did not change and the population did, and one feature description in which the customer is invited to upload a protected-attribute file with no discussion of what happens when that file is wrong.

Around 800 words, with the fine print as the funniest part and the FAQ as the second funniest. Invent the vendor, the product name, the customers, and the testimonial; use no real company names and quote no real documents. The piece should be readable start to finish by someone outside the field, and should reward a second reading by someone inside it.
