The way you're framing the Ferguson–Tharoor thing has a problem I can't get past. You keep asking who's right about whether British rule in India was, on balance, a net good or a net bad — like there's a ledger and the only question is which column is longer. But I don't think "on balance" even means anything here, and I suspect the two of them aren't actually disagreeing about the same question. When Ferguson tallies up railways, the civil service, the legal code, and the "globalization" of India against famines, deindustrialization, and the drain, what's the unit? Lives? Rupees? "Development"? And over what stretch of time, and for whom — the average Indian, or the average person alive in 1900 versus 1750? It feels like the whole debate might be a category mistake dressed up as a disagreement about facts, but I can't tell if that's a real objection or just me being clever. So: is the "balance sheet" a coherent thing to compute at all, and if it isn't, what are Ferguson and Tharoor actually disagreeing about when they talk past each other? And is there a version of the question that survives once you drop the balance-sheet metaphor — or does the whole thing dissolve?
