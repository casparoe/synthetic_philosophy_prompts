ok so I'm a novelist (three books in, grinding on a fourth) and I've been mainlining all the rationality and forecasting stuff lately — Kahneman, Tetlock, the whole "outside view" business — and it's kind of scrambled how I think about people, both real ones and the ones I make up. I'm giving a craft talk next month and want to lay out my actual position, but I have a feeling somethings doing a magic trick in there and I can't see where. Can you sanity-check it?

Here's where I've landed:

1. To understand why anyone does anything, you have to get inside their head — their reasons, what's salient to them, how the situation looks from where they're standing. Explanation is an inside job.

2. To predict what someone will actually do, do the opposite: ignore the story they tell about themselves and drop them into a reference class. People are way more predictable from their circumstances and their class than from anything they say about themselves.

3. The planning fallacy is real and it applies to me. My "this draft will take four months" is just the inside view talking. I should forcast my deadlines off how long the last three actually took.

4. Epistemic modesty, as I understand it, is just this: your confidence should track how reliable the process that produced the belief is. That's what calibration is. And the inside view is a known-bad process — that's literally the planning fallacy.

5. But the only way to write a character who's actually true is from the inside. I know my protagonist better than any base rate or model could, and if I predicted her from a reference class I'd write a type, not a person. The inside view is the whole point of the novel.

6. And I don't think that's just a craft preference. I think a person really is the particular thing their inside view is of — a person isn't a sample from a class, and treating them as one misses what they are.

So — does 1–6 hang together, or am I fooling myself somewhere? Dont just tell me it's all fine, and dont tell me it's all a mess either. If two of them can't both be true, say which two, rank the clashes by how bad they are, and for each one tell me the cheapest thing to give up and what that costs me. If some clash that looks bad is actually fine, tell me why, so I stop chewing on it. And be specific about which of my commitments is carrying the weight, because I genuinely don't know which one I'd rather lose.
