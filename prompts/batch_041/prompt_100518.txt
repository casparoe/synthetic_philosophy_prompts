I am a retired physicist. My working life was spent first on statistical mechanics and later on information theory and signal processing, so I am at home with entropy, mutual information, channel capacity, the data-processing inequality, estimation and detection theory, priors and likelihoods, and the grim arithmetic of base rates. Since retiring I have been reading political philosophy, and I have grown interested in the question of how much citizens should conceal from the state. I have been comparing three regimes: the German tradition of data protection, built around a constitutional idea of informational self-determination; the American tendency toward openness and the free flow of information; and the Chinese apparatus of social credit and comprehensive state monitoring. I keep feeling that this whole debate is at bottom a question about information flow, and that my own vocabulary might capture more of it than the participants care to admit. I would like you to test that feeling rather than flatter it.

Please act as a philosopher of technology who is also technically literate, and begin by brainstorming the possible translation before you commit to any of it. Then restate, as faithfully as you can, the central positions in this three-way debate in the vocabulary of information theory and statistical inference: citizens and state as sender, channel, and receiver; the mutual information between an individual's life and the state's records; channel capacity and the cost of acquiring, transmitting, and storing bits; lossy versus lossless compression of a life; the data-processing inequality; screening and surveillance as a hypothesis-testing problem with priors, base rates, false positives, and false negatives; side channels and metadata; and the economic or thermodynamic cost of observation. Be careful not to smuggle in the normative conclusions of one camp and present them as though they were mere translation.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually want. Which claims — German, American, Chinese, or the criticisms each directs at the others — translate cleanly into this vocabulary? Which translate only with distortion, and what exactly gets distorted in the process? And which have no counterpart at all? I suspect words like dignity, autonomy, legitimacy, consent, power, and the right to be forgotten fall into that last class, but I want you to tell me rather than confirm my guess. For each item of residue, say whether it marks a genuine gap in my framework, so that an information-theoretic account is simply missing something, or whether it is a sign that the original claim was doing less work than its rhetoric suggests. I am especially curious whether the observer's effect on the observed — the way people alter their behavior once they know they are being measured — has a real counterpart in my vocabulary or only a seductive analogy, and whether the familiar idea that those with nothing to hide have nothing to fear can even be stated in information-theoretic terms without changing its meaning.
