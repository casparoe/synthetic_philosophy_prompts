My partner and I have been argueing about consciousness ever since I read that some philosophers think experiences are real but causally do literally nothing — they're just along for the ride. I say that can't be right: if experiences did nothing, our brains couldn't be tracking them, so all our talk about the felt character of experience would be guesswork that just happens to come out right, which is absurd. She says that just shows we should drop the experiences and keep the talk — we're under an illusion that there's something it's like, and that's the whole story. She also says the first view can always just declare the brain-experience link a brute law of nature and be done with it, which I think is a dodge. Can you compare the two views — experiences that are real but inert vs. no experiences at all and only the impression of them — specifically on how our judgements about experience could be about experience in the first place, and say which one you think holds up better?
