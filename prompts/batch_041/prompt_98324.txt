I have been defending a view in a seminar on instinct and agency that I call diagnostic externalism about sphexishness. The view begins from the familiar digger-wasp case: a wasp performs what looks like a flexible, means-end sequence until the environment is perturbed in just the wrong way, at which point the sequence repeats mechanically. My claim is that sphexishness is not a property of a mechanism considered in isolation but a relation between a mechanism and a class of perturbations. On this view, every system is sphexish relative to some perturbation class; the difference between a wasp and a human is not that one has an inner spring of flexibility and the other lacks it, but that humans are routinely perturbed by their own representations of their routines, which widens the perturbation class without abolishing its boundary. Free will, insofar as it survives this picture, is not an exception to mechanism but a measure of how many of one’s own routines can become objects of revision.

What I want from you is a map of what would count against this view, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Which observations, arguments, formal results, or thought experiments should move me? I am especially interested in two cases that seem to press on the view from opposite directions: first, a lookup-table agent that has no inner model of its routine yet passes every perturbation test because it was trained on those very perturbations; second, a nested-exemption agent that can interrupt its own interrupt conditions, but only by consulting a fixed priority list. Does the first case show that my view conflates flexibility with coverage, and does the second show that it cannot distinguish freedom from a more elaborate rigidity? I would also like you to say which defeaters a committed diagnostic externalist would concede as genuinely counting against the view, which they would dismiss as question-begging, and whether the view risks collapsing into the trivial claim that every system is rigid under some description. If it does, tell me whether that triviality is a fatal problem or simply the price of a deflationary account, and what a non-trivial version of the view would have to add.
