Having retired from the physiology bench, I have spent the last few years reading my way into the philosophy of medicine, and I am struck by how much of the naturalism-versus-normativism dispute over disease is settled in practice by habit rather than by argument. I would like a satirical piece in the form of an entry from the internal determination manual of a health insurer's Disease Designation Board: deadpan, numbered sections, roughly 600 words, covering borderline cases such as ageing, pregnancy, male-pattern baldness, and ordinary grief. The humour must come from the substance rather than the phrasing. Let the manual's own criteria do the work — deviation from species-typical function, the reference class it quietly assumes, harm, and social role — and require each ruling to declare the reference class it invokes, so that the Board's earlier rulings constrain its later ones. A philosopher of medicine should recognise the positions being sent up and find the caricature fair; a general reader should still follow it; the piece should never wink. Give each contested ruling a one-line dissenting footnote in the voice of the losing side. The target is the practice of claiming value-neutral classification, not any named person. Close with a short note, outside the parody, on which readings of the naturalist and normativist positions the piece commits to, where it is likeliest to be unfair to a defender of either, and what would have to be true for the parody to miss its mark.
