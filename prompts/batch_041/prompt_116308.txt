I hold that low-impact behavior can be specified without a full theory of value: hand a capable agent the rule "minimize divergence from the inaction baseline" — the world as it would have unfolded had you done nothing — and it will avoid side effects without anyone having to say what the world should be like, where divergence can be cashed out either as change in the world's features or as reduction in the options left open to others. What would count against this? I would be most persuaded by two equally defensible baselines that prescribe opposite actions, or by a policy that satisfies the measure and is plainly undesirable.
