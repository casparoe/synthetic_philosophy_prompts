I'll be honest with you, I've got no philosophy training whatsoever, left school at 16, drive a van for a living, but I've been chewing this one over for about a year and I need to get it out of my head and onto paper. A chart is the only way I can hold all the bits at once.

Here's what won't leave me alone. Before God made anything, did he already know what I would do in situations that never actually happen? Not what I will do. What I would of done if I'd been born somewhere else, or if someone offered me a bribe that nobody is ever actually going to offer me. Three answers keep coming up. One: yes, he knew all of it, and that's how he fixed things so the right people end up in the right place without him having to make anyone do anything. Two: no, there's no such fact, because a free choice isn't the kind of thing that can be true before it happens, not even as a 'would'. Three: there is a fact, but God didn't find it out beforehand, he settled it, because he decided everything that happens.

So what I want is a flowchart I can actually follow. Start me at things I'm sure about, make every fork a question I can answer without already knowing which answer it leads to, and land me on a labelled position with one line on what it costs me to stand there.

The forks I want, roughly:

- Is there a definite yes or no to 'what would so-and-so freely do in situation X' when X never happens, and what about when so-and-so is never born at all?
- If there is such a fact, what makes it true? Nothing, it just is / the sort of person they are / God's decision about them / what they actually do in the nearest world where X does happen / God's picture of them.
- Is God knowing these things before his decision, or through it, or is 'before' just a way of talking because he isn't in time at all?
- If it was already true before I existed, in what sense was it ever up to me? This is the one that keeps me awake.
- Can God make any world he fancies, or is he stuck with the ones the woulds leave him?
- If those facts are true independently of his decision, is his knowledge propped up by something outside himself?
- Is it 'would' or only 'might'? Does he know what you would do, or only what you might?

And I want at least these ends on the chart. I'm definately not sure about the names, so go by the descriptions and tell me what they're properly called:

(a) God knows the woulds, they aren't made true by him, you're genuinely free to have done otherwise, and he arranges the world around them.
(b) Same, except the way he gets what he wants is by knowing which particular offer of grace would land with you. He makes the offer that fits, and you freely take it.
(c) Same as (a), except the woulds are true because of what you're like deep down, your nature, so they're true because of you and not because of God.
(d) No woulds in advance at all. He knows what you freely do because he's already decided it, and being decided doesn't stop it being free.
(e) Same as (d) but harder. No real could-have-done-otherwise anywhere. Everything's settled, and 'free' just means doing what you most want.
(f) There's no fact of the matter about what you'd do. God genuinely doesn't know, and finds out along with the rest of us.
(g) No woulds, but God sees the whole of time at once from outside it, so he knows what you will do even though he doesn't know what you'd do in other circumstances.
(h) He knows the woulds, but only for people who actually exist. There are no facts at all about what the never-born would have done.

Six to eight forks, and whatever ends fall out of them. If a fork leaves a branch with nobody standing on it, say so, and tell me whether that's because it's nonsense, because it's horrible, or because nobody's bothered to try. If two of the ends I've listed turn out to be the same position under the skin, or if the chart genuinely can't tell them apart, flag it rather than fudge it. And please, no reading lists and no citations. I can't get to a library and I don't want to be sent off after books.
