I produce a small interview podcast on philosophy of mind, and I'm preparing an episode with a guest whose position is, I think, both defensible and unusually hard to interview well. She holds that global workspace theory — roughly, the claim that a mental state is conscious just in case its content is broadcast widely enough to be available to a broad coalition of consumer systems, with the broadcast being all-or-none rather than graded — is a complete theory of access consciousness, and that there is nothing further for phenomenal consciousness to be. The "what it's like" is not a second phenomenon left unexplained by the workspace story; it is that story described from the inside, and the residual feeling that something has been left out is an artefact of introspection rather than a datum about the mind. She is careful to distinguish this from saying experience is illusory or unreal: on her view experience is entirely real, just not additional. I want the episode to be genuinely substantive rather than another round of the familiar hard-problem standoff, so I need questions that engage the internal machinery and the empirical commitments of the position rather than its conclusion.

Before drafting anything, please think through the strongest objections available to someone who accepts the empirical core of workspace theory but rejects the collapse of phenomenal into access — especially objections that turn on cases where reportability and experience seem to come apart, and on unclarities inside the theory itself (what exactly qualifies as a coalition, whether partial or overlapping broadcasts are possible, whether the theory's real target is experience or merely reportability). Then draft ten to twelve interview questions, ordered so that the pressure builds rather than resetting each time. I want at least one question on whether a subject could have vivid experience without any broadcast, and the mirror case of a broadcast that produces no experience; at least one on the unity of consciousness — whether a single winner-take-all coalition is required, or whether two simultaneous partial broadcasts would amount to two streams of experience in one subject; at least one on what empirical result she would treat as refuting her, given that she reads the usual no-report and overflow findings as friendly to her side; and at least one on why the inside description of a broadcast should not itself be treated as a further fact needing explanation. For every question, include the follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, since the whole point is to keep moving after the first deflection. Avoid anything that invites a textbook restatement of the view, and avoid biographical or warm-up questions.

Return the result as a single JSON object with three fields. The first, "position_summary", is a string of no more than three sentences stating the position you take yourself to be interviewing, so I can check that we agree. The second, "objection_map", is an array of objects with fields "objection" (a string), "why_it_bites" (a string explaining what in her view it threatens), and "likely_rejoinder" (a string). The third, "questions", is an array of objects, each with fields "id" (an integer), "question" (a string, the question as I would actually ask it aloud), "targets" (a string naming the specific commitment it presses on), "expected_reply" (a string), "follow_up" (a string), and "pressure_point" (a string describing what I should listen for in her answer to know whether the follow-up is still needed). End the object with a "closing_question" field holding a single string: the one question I should save for last.
