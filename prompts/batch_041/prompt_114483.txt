I am a novelist, and my partner is a philosopher of science whose verdicts I consult when I need my fictional scientists to sound as if they know what they are doing. Her record so far: she believes the equations of Newtonian gravitation but not absolute space; believes Maxwell’s field equations but not the luminiferous ether; treats phlogiston as a fiction while accepting that the practice of balancing chemical equations survived it; treats caloric as a fiction while accepting that the thermodynamic relations survived it; and trusts quantum mechanics’ predictions while remaining agnostic about its ontology. In my new novel, a theorist proposes a fundamental entity called a torsion field. No one can isolate or manipulate it, but its field equations are indispensable for deriving a wide range of successful predictions, and those equations have survived two theoretical revolutions unchanged. What will my partner say about whether the torsion field is real? Which rule is she implicitly running—selective realism, entity realism, epistemic structural realism, ontic structural realism, or constructive empiricism—which of those rules fit her earlier verdicts equally well, and which further case would best tell them apart?
