Ok so heres something I keep noticing and it kind of bugs me. A lot of places now let people use AI for the actual job, but the same places still certify or hire people by making them do it with no AI at all. Closed-book style. "Show me you can do it yourself." A hospital that never reads a scan without the model still re-tests its radiologists on solo reads. A firm that drafts everything with the assistant still puts you in a room and makes you reason it out cold. And there's a version of this in schools too, where the exam is AI-free even though the homework and the eventual job are not.

So what does that split presuppose about understanding? The credentialing side seems to think real understanding is something your bare mind can do, and that leaning on the tool doesn't count, or at least doesn't count as the same thing. But is that what the practice actually commits to, or is it just that you cant verify understanding on the job so they fall back to a solo test for tractability reasons? I want you to seperate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with.

Concretely: does it presuppose that understanding is a generative ability, i.e. you can produce the answer from scratch, or only some kind of grasp of why the answer is right, which a tool-user might still have? Does it quietly rule out extended-mind or distributed-cognition views where the tool is part of the cognitive system, or could a fan of those views still endorse the practice without inconsistency? And where is the whole thing philosophically underdetermined, such that a few different theories of understanding would all license the same practice?

Then tell me what would have to change if we swapped in a rival view, say understanding as being able to evaluate and explain a result no matter where it came from, and whether the credentialing practice would survive that swap or fall apart. Last thing: is the split even coherent, or does letting people lean on the tool on the job while testing them without it quietly concede that the understanding being tested for doesn't actually matter for the job?

Write it in American English please, and skip the reading lists and citations, I just want the analysis.
