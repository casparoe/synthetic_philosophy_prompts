I'm putting together a fake product page for a made-up online store, set in a world where a publisher has started selling a 900-page book that it insists is not a description of Einstein's brain but the brain itself — every neuron and connection laid out page by page, such that reading it in the right order amounts to having a real conversation with Einstein, just a slow one. Write the review section for that listing: six customer reviews, each with a star rating and a one-line title, 400-550 words total, plus a two-line reply from the seller to the angriest one. The funny has to come from the philosophy of mind, not from wacky customers; every complaint should be something a person could sincerely say if they took the substrate-independence line seriously. So: at least one reviewer who thinks it's obviously conscious and one who thinks it's obviously not and says why (a static description isn't a computation, nothing is happening between the covers, the answers are already in there rather than being worked out), someone who's read it twice and wants to know whether that's two Einsteins, someone asking if a photocopy is a clone, someone who left it unread in a cupboard for a year and can't decide whether that's neglect or murder, and someone who thinks the whole thing is a waste of money and prefers the audiobook. Dont let anyone win, and don't let the seller resolve it either — I want the reader to close the page with the question exactly as open as they found it, and someone who's actually read the literature on this shouldn't wince at any of the arguments.
