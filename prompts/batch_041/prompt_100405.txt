My reading group is working through Douglas Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, and one member has converted its central analogy from an illustration into a premise. His formulation runs: a self-referential formula is to a formal system what self-awareness is to a mind. On his reading, the book shows that once a system can encode descriptions of its own expressions and then operate on those descriptions, a level-crossing occurs and the system acquires an I — a strange loop — and nothing further is required; the Escher print of two hands drawing each other and the Möbius strip are offered as the same structure in other media. From this he draws a substantive conclusion: that self-awareness is a structural achievement available to any sufficiently self-modelling system, so a large language model that carries and reports on a model of its own processing is self-aware in the only sense the notion can bear, and any residual demand — a biological substrate, phenomenal character, a first-person givenness — is a relic of a bad picture. Our host objects that the analogy is doing the argument's work and that nobody can say which of its features are load-bearing. Please audit it.

I want five things. First, the correspondence table: on the formal side, what is played by the symbols and syntax, the axioms and rules of inference, the coding that lets the system refer to its own expressions, the fixed-point construction, the provability predicate, the undecidable sentence, and the standpoint outside the system from which that sentence is seen to be true — and, for each, what it is supposed to correspond to in a mind. Second, the points at which the mapping breaks, sorted into those that matter for his conclusion and those that are merely decorative. Third, the features of the source that the analogy quietly imports and that the target may lack — among them whether the self-reference is the system's own achievement or the theorist's, and whether what is at issue in the source (provability within a fixed formal system) is the same thing as what is at issue in the target (a subject's awareness of itself). Fourth, whether his conclusion survives the audit, and if it does not, the strongest weakened claim the analogy can actually support. Fifth, what a better analogy would be for what he is trying to say. If his reading of the book's own thesis is tendentious, say where.
