State your credence — a single number — that the regress in Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise exposes a genuine gap in the justification of deductive inference, rather than an artifact of treating a rule of inference as though it were one more premise. Put the number in your first sentence, then give the decomposition, with no preamble and no restating of my question. For each of the following, give a probability and a one-line justification, and show how they combine: (a) that accepting a rule of inference is a categorically different kind of commitment from accepting a proposition; (b) that, granting that difference, the acceptance of the rule can be justified without the regress reappearing one level up, at the point of deciding when and whether to apply the rule; (c) that the tortoise's demand for a justification of the step is legitimate rather than a category mistake; and (d) that the puzzle dissolves once we distinguish following a rule from believing a proposition. Then add whatever further term the decomposition requires — in particular, tell me whether your number changes if we stipulate (i) a tortoise who is an ideal reasoner, with no limits of processing power or nerve, who still declines to draw the inference, and (ii) a tortoise who accepts the rule only as a description of her own inferential dispositions rather than as a norm binding her. Finally, name the term that dominates the product, and identify the single consideration that, if it went the other way, would move your number most, and in which direction.
