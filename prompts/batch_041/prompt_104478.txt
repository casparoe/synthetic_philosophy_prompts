I never studied physics past a couple of intro courses (I switched to product design), but I fell down a rabbit hole on Maxwell's demon and now I can't let it go. The thing that's bugging me isn't the demon itself — I get the setup, the little guy sorts fast molecules one way and slow ones the other and seems to make a temperature difference without doing work, supposedly violating the second law. What bugs me is a phrase people drop the second the demon comes up, always like it settles everything: information is physical. I've run into it in a few arguments now and I genuinely can't tell if it's one claim or five wearing the same coat. In one place it was used to say the demon can't win because to run it you'd have to keep a record of what you measured, and eventually you have to wipe the record, and wiping a record isn't free — it costs you at least a little energy per bit, so the demon's books don't balance. Fine, maybe. But then someone else said it to mean something way bigger, that information just IS a physical thing like charge or mass, and that this is why the demon fails. And someone else said it to mean that measuring the molecules already messes up the gas so much that you've lost whatever you gained. Those feel like really different claims and I suspect at least one of them is just a fancy way of saying "information has to live on a hard drive somewhere," which, ok, sure, but that can't be enough to beat a demon.

So what I want from you: take that one line — information is physical — and lay out the different precise things it could actually be asserting, ordered from the boring/trivially-true end up to the radical/metaphysical end. For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a real substantive claim that someone could defend, or just false. Then tell me which reading is the one people are actually leaning on when they use the line to wave the demon away, and — this is the part I care about — whether that reading genuinely kills the demon or only moves the problem somewhere else (like, does it explain why the demon fails, or does it just relabel the demon's failure as a cost?). If a couple of the readings are secretly the same claim in different clothes, say so. I want a menu I can actually argue from, not a summary of the demon story — I already know the demon story.
