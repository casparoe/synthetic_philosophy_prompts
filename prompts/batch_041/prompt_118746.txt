I have been arguing with a friend about virtual worlds and we keep talking past each other, so I would like your own credence on a specific proposition, together with the decomposition that produces it. The proposition is this: when a player loses something in a persistent online world — a rare item destroyed by an operator's unilateral server wipe, or an avatar permanently killed by another player's exploit — she has suffered a genuine harm, where genuine is stipulated to mean that it counts for the same reasons and in the same way as losing an equivalent physical possession, and not merely that she is distressed about it. My friend puts this at roughly 0.85; I put it at roughly 0.3. Please give me a number of your own, and then break the question at its joints rather than reporting a single intuition. I want at least these terms: (a) the metaphysics — whether virtual objects have any existence beyond collective recognition, and whether they are better modeled on money, on a promise, on a character in a fiction, or on a hallucination, and what follows for whether they can be lost at all; (b) the subject of the harm — whether harm requires a subject with interests that can outlive the object, and whether the loss is more accurately redescribed as the destruction of a project, an investment, or a shared practice rather than of a thing; (c) the source of the entitlement — whether the relevant claim is natural or is created by the operator's own norms and can therefore be revoked by them at will; and (d) a counterfactual test I find oddly decisive and cannot place: if it later emerged that every other participant had all along been a bot, and that the player's losses were therefore never witnessed by anyone, does the harm survive unchanged, shrink, or vanish? Assign each term a rough probability with one line of justification, show how they combine into your number, and then name the single term the final figure is most sensitive to.

I also suspect that my friend and I do not actually disagree where we think we do, so please tell me which of us is more likely to be mistaken about the metaphysics term and which about the normativity term, and whether our 0.85-versus-0.3 spread is better explained by a genuine clash of views or by one of us quietly substituting a different proposition for the stated one. Then tell me what single argument, distinction, or piece of evidence would move your number the most, and in which direction. To keep this from collapsing into a poll of intuitions, please do not let the answer turn on whether the player is a committed role-player who treats the world as fiction or someone who treats it as a second home — assume both, and tell me whether that distinction is doing real work or is a red herring. What I want out of this is the load-bearing structure exposed, with the weak beams marked, rather than a verdict.
