Two people in my department have been fighting for two years about Strawson and the reactive attitudes. One reads Strawson as saying that blaming, resenting, and feeling betrayed don't need any justification from outside -- they're part of the human form of life, and asking whether someone 'really deserves' it from some neutral metaphysical standpoint is a confusion. The other accepts every word of Strawson's description of how the attitudes work, but says being natural isn't being justified: if resenting someone commits you to thinking they could have done otherwise in some deep sense, then the attitudes are systematically false and we should admit it, even if we can't stop having them. They want me to draft the joint statement they'd both sign after an honest adverserial collaboration. What can they both accept about the attitudes, about the standard excuses, and about what 'justified' even means here? Where do they genuinely part, in wording each of them would put their own name to? What are the cruxes -- I think one is whether the ordinary excuses (he didn't mean it, he couldn't help it) and the global determinism worry are on a par, and another is whether 'we couldn't give it up anyway' is a reason in favour of the practice or just a fact about us. For each crux, give the case or argument they'd both agree bears on it and write each side's prediction down in advance. Also tell me if any of this is just a fight over the word 'justified'. Include the case they cooked up together -- a community with the concept of wrongdoing but no practice of resentment at all -- and what each predicts about whether that community is missing something. Format: numbered claims they agree on, then a table of the disagreements.
