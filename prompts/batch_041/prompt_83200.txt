Her position: the thing to watch is an influence-seeking system — one that ends up with power in the world and a goal of its own — and the slow-drift stuff is a distraction from the real event. Mine: the real failure has no event and no villain, it's a system that makes a thousand individually sensible recommendations, none of which anyone would veto, until the institutions that were supposed to steer it can't anymore.

Draft the joint statement we could both sign. I want a numbered section of claims we actually share (including any claims about what would even count as failure), then a disagreements table where each remaining disagreement is phrased in wording the side that holds it would accept, then the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case or observation we'd both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two things I want you to force us on. One, whether "influence-seeking" presupposes a coherent agent at all, or whether that's just my friend's picture getting smuggled in — if it doesn't need an agent, say what it does need instead. Two, whether the two pictures are distinguishable in advance at all, or only in hindsight by which story ends up fitting.

One concrete case to build it around: a tax or benefits agency adopts a decision-support model where every individual recommendation is defensible and reviewable, but after ten years the agency's own sense of what a correct decision is has quietly become whatever the model outputs. Does her camp call that a failure, and if so is it the influence-seeking kind? Does mine have to say nothing failed? The statement should be able to answer that.
