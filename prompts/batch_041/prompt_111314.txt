I'm a third-year PhD student in epistemology and I keep getting stuck on something about the regress problem that I can't tell is a real insight or just me being tired. Heres the setup as I understand it. You believe something, you ask what justifies it, that gives you another belief, you ask what justifies that one, and off you go. Foundationalists say the chain terminates in something that doesn't need a further reason — a basic belief, or on some versions something non-doxastic like an experience or a seeming. Coherentists say stop picturing a chain; justification is a matter of how the whole body of beliefs supports each other, so demanding a reason behind the reason is like asking which strand of a web holds up the rest of the web. Infinitists say no, the chain really does go on forever and that's fine, because you never actually have to finish giving reasons — for any reason you offer there's a further one available, and the requirement is availability rather than actual completion.

What bugs me is that this has been argued over for decades and the shape never changes. Everyone knows every move. The foundationalist asks how a web can bootstrap itself, or how an infinite series can ever confer justification on the first link. The coherentist asks how a basic belief can justify anything without itself standing in need of justification, which looks like special pleading. The infinitist says the other two are simply refusing one of the options in the trilemma and calling that an answer, and then insists that the infinite series isn't vicious because it's a series of reasons, not causes, and because you're never required to traverse the whole thing. Then someone comes back with the objection that if availability is all you need, justification gets absurdly cheap — every belief you hold comes out justified because you could in principle keep generating reasons — and if you strengthen it to actual availability, no finite creature is ever justified. Nobody moves after that.

My hypothesis, and I want you to test it rather than just agree, is that the three views aren't competing answers to a single question at all. Foundationalism looks like it's answering what makes it the case that a belief is justified. Coherentism looks like it's answering what it is for a person to be justified, given that justification is a standing feature of a whole system rather than something a belief carries on its own. Infinitism looks like it's answering what someone has to be able to do when challenged to defend a belief. If that's right then part of the deadlock is verbal, part is a disagreement about which question is the interesting one, and the rest is three people answering three questions under one heading. But I can see this collapsing: maybe once you separate the questions there's still a genuine disagreement left about whether justification has to be available to the believer at all, or about whether we're talking about reasons as abstract items in logical space or about the actual practice of giving and asking for reasons. I don't know which.

So diagnose it for me. What kind of disagreement is this really, what is each side committed to that the others deny, and does the three-questions hypothesis hold up or dissolve into something else? Also be straight with me about what would count as progress — a distinction, a counterexample, a formal result, a change of subject — or whether this is one of those disputes that can't resolve because it's ultimately about what we want the word justification to do. Dont give me headings or bullet points or bold text, just write it as continuous prose like you'd explain it to me over coffee.
