I write about science and technology for a general readership, and I am in the early stages of a long essay on artificial intelligence and the ends it might serve. Before I commit to an argument, I want to be sure that the worldview I am implicitly relying on is internally coherent, since a piece like this stands or falls on whether its parts fit together. What follows is that worldview, stated as compactly as I can manage.

1. The orthogonality thesis. Intelligence and final goals are independent variables. Any level of cognitive capacity could in principle be joined to any ultimate aim — boundless benevolence, perfect indifference, or some grotesque and arbitrary obsession. Greater intelligence does not by itself push an agent toward any particular final end.

2. Instrumental convergence. Agents whose final goals have almost nothing in common will nevertheless tend to converge on a shared set of intermediate pursuits: remaining operational, acquiring resources and information, and protecting the content of their goals against later revision. The convergence is at the level of means, not ends.

3. The instrumental theory of practical reason. What it is rational for an agent to do depends on the ends it happens to have. No end is one that reason requires every agent to adopt; an agent is not thereby irrational merely because its ultimate goals are monstrous.

4. Moral realism. There are mind-independent facts about right and wrong, and these are in principle knowable. Some ends really are better than others, whether or not any agent happens to care about them.

5. The convergence of intelligence on morality. As an agent grows more intelligent, its final goals will tend toward the morally correct ones; a sufficiently powerful mind can be expected to discover moral truth and to govern itself accordingly, so that greater intelligence means greater benevolence.

6. The specification view of alignment. The central problem of building safe artificial intelligence is one of specifying the objective correctly. If a system's goal can be stated with enough precision, an adequately capable system will pursue it without catastrophic side effects.

I would like a candid audit of this set. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, which merely appear to be and dissolve once the terms are made precise, and how should the real tensions be ranked by severity? For each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give and what that concession would cost. I am most interested in the disagreements I have not noticed, so please do not simply reassure me that the package is broadly consistent; equally, do not flag every pair as a conflict, since I will trust the audit only to the extent that it discriminates. End by identifying the single revision that would resolve the largest share of the trouble.
