I've just joined the committee of a small local charity and it's turned into the most philosophically confusing thing in my life. It's about twenty years old. It started as a group of parents who set up a weekly Saturday session for kids with additional needs, run by the families themselves, for the families. Now it's a "community wellbeing hub" with three paid staff, three grant funders, a manager who's basically a CEO, and a constitution nobody's read since 2004. The families it was set up for mostly can't get a place.

Two things are bothering me and I can't tell if they're the same problem or seperate ones.

One: our chair. Eleven years in post. She's also the treasurer, holds the bank card, writes every grant application, and is the only person who can log into the funder portal. Nothing dodgy has ever happened. But I've noticed that when the committee votes against her, the thing doesn't happen. When I asked to write down the grant process so someone else could do it, she was hurt, and then nothing happened.

Two: the drift. I've read the minutes. There is no meeting where anyone decided to become a wellbeing hub. It's twenty years of small sensible decisions, each one obviously fine on its own, and the sum of them is a different organisation wearing the same name. Nobody's to blame, or everyone is.

A mate of mine who's into AI stuff said that's the two ways it goes wrong there as well: either one part of the system gets clever and starts accumulating power, or nothing dramatic happens and the whole thing slowly slides until it's optimising for something else entirely. He reckons the second one is the scarier of the two, which I don't really follow.

So: which of these should I actually be worried about? Are they genuinely separate, or is one quietly making the other worse? Is there any way to tell from the inside which one is happening, or is that the whole problem? And what would you actually do — I've got maybe two years of monthly meetings in me before I either fix something or resign. What should I be trying to do, and what should I avoid because it'll just blow up a small volunteer thing that does mostly good work? And if I do fix it, how do I know the fix won't turn into the same problem again in fifteen years? I definately don't want a reading list, just your best thinking on my actual situation. British English please.
