Hey, so I cover the science-and-ideas beat at a general-interest magazine, and every issue we run one piece of short fiction — the kind thats meant to start a fight at a dinner party rather than just be pretty. I want to try a story built on that old puzzle where a group is offered a prize: if exactly one person claims it, that person gets everything; if nobody claims it, everyone gets a small consolation; if two or more claim it, the whole pot evaporates and no one gets anything. The juice of it is that each person's private "rational" move (claim it) wrecks it for everyone, so the only way anyone wins is if the whole group somehow reasons its way to holding back — and I want fiction, not an explainer.

Here's the twist I want, so it isn't just the textbook version with the serial numbers filed off. Set it in a small, closed group where everyone has known each other for years — a band about to break up, the last six people on a remote research station, a family splitting an inheritance, whatever grabs you — and add two rules: before the claiming window opens, each person has to announce out loud what they intend to do, and every announcement gets posted on a public board anyone can check afterward. Nothing enforces those announcements. You can swear you won't claim and then claim anyway, and there's no punishment except what the others decide to do about it later. And make the window long — days, not seconds — so people have time to watch each other, second-guess, and lie.

I want the story to actually live in that tension: somebody who sincerely means to hold the line, somebody who's bluffing, the moment the first person cracks, and what the group does afterward once they can see exactly who said what. Maybe 1,200 to 1,800 words, third person, and don't cheat by having some outside authority swoop in to punish anyone — the whole point is that nothing is enforced except each other. End on something that leaves my readers genuinely unsure whether the group did the smart thing or the cowardly thing. And please don't explain the puzzle to me inside the story; let the characters choices carry it.
