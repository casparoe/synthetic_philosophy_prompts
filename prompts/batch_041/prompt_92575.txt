Write me a short story where the plot turns on a very specific epistemic trap, and make the trap the engine of the story rather than decoration.

Here's the mechanic, dont just name it — build it into the characters and events. A belief really counts as knowledge only if it's hooked up to the world: if the fact had been different, the person would have believed differently. Now picture someone whose belief about one narrow fact is hooked up like that — they check it, they've got a knack for it, they'd notice the second it changed — and who also knows perfectly well that this fact cant be true unless some bigger fact is true, and yet whose belief in that bigger fact is not hooked up at all. They'd have believed it no matter what. They never checked. They inherited it, or everyone around them assumes it, or it's the thing that lets them sleep. So they know A, they know A can't hold without B, and they don't know B. No contradiction, just a hole where something should be.

Give that hole real consequences. Not a gotcha ending where it turns out they were wrong about A — keep A true and keep them knowing it, that's a different puzzle. Maybe it's someone who keeps meticulous, narrow records at work and trusts the institution around them completely; or someone who reads the river level every morning and has never once questioned the levee. Make the reader feel the gap as a lived thing and not a diagram: scene, dialogue, a decision made under pressure, and no closing paragraph where a wise friend explains the lesson. Let the story carry it.
