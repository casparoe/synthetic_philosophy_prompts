I'm supposed to be writing up my view of decision theory for a workshop next month and I'd like an audit before I embarrass myself. Heres roughly where I've landed, and I want to know whether it hangs together, and if not, which bit has to go. I genuinely can't tell where the problem is, so don't just tell me it's all fine.

(1) Instrumental rationality just is expected utility maximization. Complete, transitive preferences, a utility function that represents them, done. I don't think there's some extra structural requirement floating above the representation theorem.

(2) The reason intransitivity is a rational failure is the money pump. Not that a cycle "isn't a ranking" — that complaint has never moved me, it just asserts the conclusion — but that if you have a cycle, someone can walk you around it and take your money, and the whole thing is foreseeable. That's the arguement and I think it's the only one there is.

(3) Preferences can legitimately change over time. There's no fact of the matter about whether my 55-year-old self's values are better or worse than mine, and he's not irrational for wanting different things. Same over short timescales: I've started calling my late-evening preferences night values, because at 11pm I genuinely want different stuff than I do at 8am, and I don't think either set is the mistake.

(4) Resoluteness is rational. Two parts, and I take them to be the same commitment: (a) throwing the cigarettes out, paying for the Ulysses contract, deleting the app, so that the temptation isn't there later; and (b) when I've made a plan, and there's no enforcement, and nobody would ever know, still doing the planned thing even though at that moment I'd rather not, because I'm the one who made the plan.

(5) I have a cycle and I think it's fine. Vanilla to pistachio to hazelnut to vanilla, each swap costing me a little, offered one at a time; I'd take each one. But walk me around the whole loop and I'm down three small costs holding exactly the gelato I started with, and I won't do it. My line is that the pairwise facts are fine and the walking-around is the problem, and the fix is not getting walked around.

(6) Cycles you can't actually be walked around aren't irrational at all. If the three swaps are never on offer in the right order, or if the cycle only shows up because I'm comparing across different times of day (see 3), calling that irrational is just book-keeping.

So: which of these pull against each other, how badly, and whats the cheapest repair in each case? I'd rather you tell me which pairs are only apparently in tension than flag everything as a problem. Rank them by severity, and be specific about which single commitment is doing the damage, and what it costs to give it up. Skip the who-said-what, I just want the audit.
