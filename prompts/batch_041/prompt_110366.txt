For the past year I have kept a daily symptom log to test whether dairy is what triggers my migraines. Each evening I mark the day as a migraine day or not, but on many days I am genuinely unsure whether what I had was a migraine or an ordinary tension headache; on those days I would put it at roughly 70/30. So far I have handled this by rounding — when I lean migraine I record a migraine day, when I lean the other way I record a non-migraine day — and then updating my belief in the dairy hypothesis as though each entry were a fact observed with certainty. A statistically minded friend says this throws away information and that I should instead be updating on the uncertainty itself, averaging over both possible readings weighted by how likely each reading is; when she tried to write the rule out for me I lost the thread. Who is right, how much does the choice matter across a year of daily entries, and what rule should I actually be applying entry by entry?

There is a further wrinkle I cannot place. I suspect my readings are not equally reliable across the two kinds of day: on days when I have eaten dairy I am more vigilant and may judge my own symptoms differently, so my 70/30 may be well calibrated in one group and badly calibrated in the other. Does that undermine my friend's rule, or my rounding, or both — and if the reliability of my labels is entangled with the very thing the log is meant to test, what should I do instead?
