Right — I need to actually produce something for this, my department's been at war for a term and I'm chairing the reconciliation, god help me.

We've got two camps. Camp one is our psychometrician: intelligence is a general measurable capacity, the g-factor, individual differences, the whole apparatus, and she reckons the field is basically sound — we just need cleaner tests and a decent account of why scores keep drifting upward across cohorts. Camp two is our situated-cognition person: intelligence isn't a property in the head at all, it's a relation between an agent and an environment, and every test ever devised is really measuring how thoroughly you've been shaped by a particular niche — his line is that you can't separate the smart bit from the world it's embedded in, and that most of what we call intelligence is stuff we've offloaded onto tools, notebooks, other people.

The flashpoint was a job talk last month. Candidate proposed defining intelligence as compression: the shorter the program that reproduces your behaviour, the smarter you are. Both camps hated it, for completely different reasons, which I take it is a clue that the disagreement isn't just about tests.

What I want from you is the document these two should have written after a proper good-faith adversarial collaboration, the kind of thing both of them could put their names to without either feeling stitched up. So: the claims they can both sign, about what intelligence is and isn't, about what any theory of it has to explain, about what the existing evidence does and doesn't settle. Then the residual disagreement, stated in wording that each side would accept as a fair statement of its own view — I don't want you splitting the difference with mush, I want the actual joints. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one of them would move at least one side, and for each crux the argument or case or result both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If any part of the row turns out to be verbal — them using 'intelligence' for two things — say which part and why, and don't be diplomatic about it.

Format: numbered section of agreed claims, then a disagreements table (claim / camp one's position / camp two's position / what would move them). Hard limit 900 words, table counts toward it, so be ruthless.
