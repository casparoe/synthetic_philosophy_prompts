I sit on the trust and safety appeals panel for a social VR platform -- small one, maybe 40k monthly users, mostly hangout and roleplay worlds. We vote Thursday on an appeal and I've got to circulate a written opinion before then.

The case: a user, call her R, says another user, M, followed her around a private instance for about ten minutes and kept putting his avatar's hands through her avatar's chest and crotch, with some voice stuff on top. No recording, private instance, so it's mostly her word against his, though two friends of hers were in the instance and back up the basic sequence. M doesn't deny the actions. His defense is that nothing happened -- no body was touched, avatars are cartoons, and he points out that our own Terms of Service say users hold no property rights and no rights of any kind in virtual objects or in their own avatar. At most, he says, this is bad manners, not the sexual harassment our code is about.

My problem is this. Our harassment code is drafted entirely around content -- posts, messages, images, uploaded avatar skins. The operative line is basically "content a reasonable user would find sexually explicit or degrading." An action one avatar performs on another doesn't obviously fit that; there's no content. Last year we upheld a ban in a seperate case where a user kept spawning an oversized avatar to loom over people in their personal space, and that opinion said the code protects the experience a user has, not just the pixels -- but it was one paragraph and nobody ever wrote down what the test actually is. We also have about thirty cases like R's sitting in the queue behind this one, plus one that's wierd in a different way: a user gave a friend edit access to a world he'd spent two years building and the friend deleted the whole thing. I think that second one is easier, but I want the same opinion to cover it, or to say plainly why it doesn't.

And one board member is going to argue that if we start treating this as battery-adjacent, we hand a weapon to people who want combat, horror and kink roleplay communities shut down -- that the moment avatar-on-avatar contact counts as an assault, every consensual scene becomes a reportable incident.

Please draft the opinion I'll recieve for circulation. I need a concrete bottom line for R's case -- uphold, reduce, whatever -- with a remedy and a timeframe, a rule for the queue that reviewers can apply without me in the room, and a straight treatment of the dissent: if the carve-out for consensual contact is actually the right answer, say so, and tell me how to write it so it doesn't swallow the rule.
