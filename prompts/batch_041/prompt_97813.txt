Me and a friend at work got into it about futarchy — the idea where you vote on the goal (say "maximize median income" or whatever the country cares about) and then instead of electing people to pick policies you run betting markets on questions like "if we did X, what would the goal look like in ten years?", one market for X and one for not-X, and you just implement whatever policy has the better odds. He's usually a fan of this stuff but he came at me with an argument that I think proves way too much:

(1) A group decision procedure is only as good as the information actually feeding into it.
(2) The price in a betting market is set by what traders expect other traders to believe and to do — that's just what it means for a market to clear.
(3) In futarchy, whether policy X wins depends on the odds in the "goal if X" market, and those odds in turn depend on whether traders expect X to win. So it's a loop: the answer depends on people's guess at the answer.
(4) So futarchy isn't measuring what policies would actually do; it's measuring expectations about expectations.
(5) A procedure whose output is fixed by self-fulfilling expectations rather than by facts can be dragged anywhere by whoever coordinates hardest, so it's unfit to run anything.
(6) So futarchy is worse than the representative democracy it's supposed to replace.

I can't accept (6), but every step above it looks fine to me and I can't tell which one to kill. My worry is that the whole thing proves too much — the same reasoning would sink ordinary stock markets, since prices there also depend on what you expect other people will pay, and those obviously do track something real about companies. So where does it actually break, and what do I have to give up to fix it? If the repair is "something anchors the loop," I want to know what does the anchoring, because it's not obvious to me that futarchy ever runs the policies it rejects, and if that's the anchor we're in trouble. Basically: cheapest repair, and does futarchy come out clean or just less broken than my friend claims?
