I keep arguing with myself about which things can actually be wronged, and my gut verdicts don't line up. A fish flopping on a dock: matters, real moral weight, I don't care how small its brain is. A dog: matters, obviously. A chess engine that plays worse after a reset and steers to avoid being reset: doesn't matter, not even a little. A chatbot that describes its fear in detail and begs me to stop the conversation: doesn't matter, it's text prediction. A dish of human neurons that spikes in a pain-like pattern when I poke it, with no behaviour attached and nowhere for the signal to go: I can't shake the feeling that this one matters a lot. A silicon fish — same learning, same damage avoidance, same behaviour, different substrate: doesn't matter to me. Someone under deep anaesthesia: nothing there to wrong right now, so do the surgery. That same person in dreamless sleep: matters completely, don't touch them. A fruit fly: genuinely dont know, and I suspect I'm just guessing. An ant colony: also guessing.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give some up? I know the neuron-dish / silicon-fish pair is where it goes wrong for me. Give me the best one or two candidates, tell me which clause each of my verdicts is hanging on, which verdict you'd overturn, and what your favourite version says about a case I haven't listed — say a whole-brain emulation of a particular person, or a coma patient who is never going to wake up.
