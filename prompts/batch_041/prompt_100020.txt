I belong to a small reading group that has spent the last several weeks on the question of why promises bind, and we have reached the stage where everyone can recite the positions and nobody can any longer feel the difference between them. We have the view that the obligation is not a natural feature of the act at all but an artifact of a social practice or convention, so that the wrong in breaking a promise is in the first instance a wrong against the practice; we have the rival view that it is a wrong done specifically to the promisee, who was given an assurance and thereby came to hold a claim against you; and we have the further thought that what really does the work is not the assurance but the reliance or the expectations the promisee actually formed. For our final session I have been asked to bring a joke.

What I want from you is a joke — an actual one, with a setup and a punchline, not a witticism about philosophers being unworldly — whose humor turns on the content of these competing accounts rather than on the fact that the subject is abstract. It is for an audience that already knows the positions, so the punchline should land only for someone who has noticed the precise place where one theory locates the obligation and another denies it, or where they disagree about who, if anyone, has been wronged and in virtue of what. The most promising shape is a small case that pulls the theories apart: a promise whose beneficiary will never learn of the breach, or can no longer form expectations, or a promise that both parties privately expect to be broken, or one made in a setting where no practice of promising is in force. I leave the construction to you, and I would much rather you find one genuine seam in the theories and work it than staple together three.

Alongside the joke, add a short note — a paragraph or two, no more — telling me where the joke takes liberties with the positions or leans on a contested reading of them: if the laugh depends on treating one of the accounts as obviously correct, or on an edge case whose classification is itself disputed, say so plainly. And keep separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this topic; I want to know which seam you are exploiting is a real, live disagreement and which is something on which everyone competent actually agrees and only my reading group is confused.
