I'm a science journalist — I cover minds, machines, animals — and I sit on the ethics and standards committee of my press association. We've walked into a fight. A member outlet ran a big feature calling a new AI system "conscious," and the evidence under it is basically that the thing broadcasts information globally and passes a global-workspace-style battery; the writer says that's the best science has and "consciousness" is just the word for it. A chunk of the committee thinks that's a category error and wants us to adopt a rule: members may not use the unqualified word "conscious" about a system unless there's evidence of phenomenal consciousness (something it's like to be it), and if all they've got is access/functional findings — global workspace and that family — they have to say so in those terms. The other side says the distinction is a philosophers invention no working scientist uses, that some of these researchers hold the global broadcast just is the experience, so the rule would force us to misreport them, and that it'll make our copy more hedged and slower while the outlets that dont bother beat us on the story.

I need to circulate a written opinion before Thursday. Please draft it: a clear recommendation on whether to adopt the rule (or some trimmed version of it), the reasoning, and an honest treatment of the side I come down against — don't just assume everyone already agrees what "access" versus "phenomenal" is supposed to mean. Say what a member should actually do at deadline when a source says "conscious" and they can't tell which sense is meant, and give a time frame for whatever we decide. Skip the citations, we're not writing for a journal.
