I'm a novelist and I've written myself into a corner. My main character is genuinely omnipotent — can do anything, full stop, no fine print — and I need one scene where she wants something badly and doesnt get it, and I need that scene to be honest. So: no kryptonite, no power drain, no fading strength, no heavy-stone business, and nothing that just boils down to her wanting a contradiction. What I'm after is a case where the thing she cant have is out of reach because she's omnipotent, not in spite of it — her power is the very thing that makes it ungettable. One page max, and keep the stakes human: a friend, an apology, a gift, something a reader would care about, not the fate of the universe.

Then do the bookkeeping for me. Would she actually fail on the ordinary view of what omnipotence means, and would she fail on the view where an omnipotent being can do even what's logically impossible? Give me both verdicts, tell me which bit of the scene is doing the real work, what happens to the whole thing if I cut that bit, and how somebody who thinks the scene is cheating would argue it.
