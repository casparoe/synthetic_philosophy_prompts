Pain asymbolia is a condition in which patients report that they still feel pain — they can say roughly where it is and how intense it is — but they are not bothered by it, don't find it unpleasant, and in some cases even smile or laugh when it's inflicted. Some people take cases like this to show that the badness of pain does not lie in the sensation itself but in something added on top of it. Does pain asymbolia actually support that conclusion? In your answer, say what the case does and doesn't show about the source of pain's badness, and say what it implies for the view that pain is bad for us simply in virtue of being a sensation we're wired to avoid.

STUDENT'S ANSWER:

Pain asymbolia shows that the unpleasantness of pain can come apart from the pain sensation itself. Patients with this condition, usually after damage to the insula, can still tell you where it hurts and whether it's sharp, burning, etc., but they report that it just doesn't bother them. So the sensory-discriminative part of pain is intact while the affective-motivational part is gone. That's the core of the case.

This matters for why pain is bad. If the badness of pain were built right into the sensation, then you couldn't have the sensation without the badness. But asymbolics have the sensation without the badness. So the badness has to attach to something other than the raw sensory quality — it attaches to the unpleasantness, and the unpleasantness is really just a matter of how the subject appraises the sensation. Asymbolics don't find the pain unpleasant because they don't judge it as a threat, and that's why it stops being bad for them.

So the view that pain is bad simply in virtue of being a sensation we're wired to avoid is refuted by the case. Pain is only bad when the subject takes it to be bad; its badness isn't intrinsic to the sensation at all but comes from the subject's attitude toward it. The asymbolic patient is the proof: same sensation, no badness, so the badness must be all in the attitude.

To be clear, i'm not claiming asymbolics don't feel pain — they do. They just don't care about it.
