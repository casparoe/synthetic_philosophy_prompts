ok so I've been lurking on rationality blogs for way too long and I've finally got a half-formed idea I want to turn into a real proposal. I'm applying for one of those small independent-research grants -- six months, enough to cover my time -- and they want about two pages. The topic is the 'is better than transitive' stuff: spectrum arguments, the repugnant conclusion, intransitivity of betterness, all that.

Here's my half-baked thought. The whole debate is set up as: either transitivity survives the spectrum arguments or it doesnt, and if it doesnt you either bite the bullet and say betterness is intransitive (and then worry about money-pumps) or you deny one of the premises, usually the one about a life barely worth living, or the step that says any improvement, however small, makes things better. But the shape of the argument is a sorites, and there's a big literature on sorites and on gradable adjectives like 'tall' where the exact same pattern shows up -- and there nobody says 'tall' is intransitive or that tallness isn't real, they say the predicate is context-sensitive, or vague, or that we're quietly switching comparison classes mid-argument. So what I actually want to work out is whether the apparent intransitivity of betterness is a real feature of the value relation or an artefact of treating 'better than' as a context-insensitive two-place relation over fully determinate alternatives, which would be the same mistake as treating 'tall' as context-insensitive. And if it is an artefact, which of transitivity, completeness, and the idea that there's a single all-things-considered betterness relation do we actually have to give up, and which were only ever assumptions?

The bit I keep tripping on, and want the proposal to take seriously instead of hand-waving: the all-things-considered use. The whole point of 'better than' in deliberation is that it's supposed to be the objective, context-free one, so a contextualist story might save the predicate while losing the concept we actually care about. And a second thing: if 'better than' is context-sensitive, do the standard money-pump arguments against intransitive preference even go through, or do they need the preferences to be intransitive within a single context? I'd rather the proposal confront that than pretend it away.
