I am attempting to formulate a consistent position on the unity of the proposition in the face of the Bradley-style regress. As I understand the regress, if a proposition is a complex of constituents and its unity is explained by a further relation that binds those constituents, then that relation must itself be bound to the constituents by a further relation, and so on. My current commitments are these:

1. Propositions are structured complexes of constituents. They are not sets, not mereological sums, and not bare lists.
2. The unity of a proposition is not a further constituent. It is a mode of combination, which I call the bond.
3. The bond is thin: it is not an object, not a constituent of the proposition, not referable by a singular term, and not in the domain of quantification. It is syncategorematic.
4. The bond is type-identical across all propositions. What differentiates one proposition from another is its constituents and their order.
5. Truth is correspondence to facts. Facts are structured like the propositions that correspond to them, and they are unified by the same bond.
6. Correspondence is a relation between a proposition and a fact, but it is not a constituent of either.
7. In my semantic theory, I quantify over bonds and refer to them when I state truth conditions. I take this to be semantic ascent rather than ontological commitment.

I suspect these commitments do not all hang together, but I cannot tell which tensions are genuine and which are merely apparent. Please audit the set. Which commitments pull against each other, and how severely? Which pairs only look inconsistent once a distinction is drawn? For each genuine tension, say what would have to give, what the cheapest revision would be, and what would be lost by making it. I am not asking for a survey of the literature or for citations; I want to know whether this particular package is tenable and, if not, where the least costly repair lies.
