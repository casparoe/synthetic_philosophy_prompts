I've just moved onto a team at work that's building a model to flag families for early help, and one of my colleagues — she's been on a couple of fairness workshops so she's our resident expert — gave us this summary of the impossibility results everyone keeps citing:

'Basically some researchers proved a few years back that you can't make an algorithm fair. There's a theorem that any score that's calibrated — a 70% score means a 70% chance, and it gets the same proportion right for both groups — has to be biased against one group or the other. It's because the two groups have different base rates and the score is just a function of the data, so you'll always end up flagging more of one group. The fix is easy though: don't put race, or anything that stands in for it, into the model and the problem goes away, which is the genuinly fair thing to do anyway. The upshot is that if you want to use these tools at all you have to accept lower accuracy, and most of the literature is just people refusing to accept that trade-off.'
