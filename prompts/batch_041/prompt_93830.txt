Although I have no training in archaeology, I spend a lot of time in museums and with popular books on the subject, and one comparison comes up again and again: the archaeologist is a detective at a crime scene. The broken pots, the postholes, the grave goods are the clues; the site is the scene; and the reasoning runs backward from physical traces to what actually happened, including what the people involved were thinking. I have seen this comparison used to license some fairly strong claims — that the layout of a settlement or the contents of a grave show that a society was ranked and unequal, that a particular structure was a temple rather than a house, that a layer of debris was left by ceremony rather than by accident. The detective picture seems to be what makes people comfortable saying we know what these people believed and how their institutions worked, even though nobody left a written explanation behind.

I would like you to audit the comparison rather than just praise or dismiss it. In ordinary sentences, tell me what corresponds to what: who or what plays the detective, the crime scene, the physical evidence, the suspect, the motive, the confession, and the court that eventually decides whether the detective got it right. Then tell me where the comparison breaks down, and be specific about which breaks matter for the claims I listed and which are only surface differences. I am especially interested in whether the detective has any independent check on his story that the archaeologist can never have, and whether that check, rather than the reasoning from traces, is what is really carrying the detective's confidence. It also seems to me that a crime scene is usually a single event sealed off after the fact, whereas a dig is more like the same patch of ground being used, cleared, rebuilt, and dumped on for centuries, so that the "scene" is a mixture of many scenes; I would like to know how much that difference costs the comparison. I have also read that plenty of what gets presented in courtrooms as forensic science turned out to rest on very little testing, which makes me wonder whether the detective's method is even as reliable as the comparison assumes.

Finish with your honest verdict: can this comparison support the conclusions it is used to support, and if not, is there a better comparison, and what would it be?
