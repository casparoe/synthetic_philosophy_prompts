So I write about science and ideas for a general-audience outlet and I've fallen down a rabbit hole on statues, street names, all of it. What got me: my city council just passed an ordinance saying every publicly owned monument or street name has to go through a review every 25 years, and if nobody can make the affirmative case for it again, it comes down. And nobody in any of the coverage could say what that case would even look like — what the review board is actually supposed to be checking. Everyone argues one statue at a time and nobody has the standard.

Two other wrinkles from the same ordinance, because they're what made me think this is a real philosophy problem and not just local politics. First, a removed statue's plinth has to stand empty for a year before anything can replace it — the sponsor said it was so people could sit with the absence, which I think is either profround or a load of, you know. Second, an amendment that would ban monuments to people who are still alive. Both passed, nobody's sure what either actually does.

So before I write the big piece I want the spec, not the verdicts. What would a theory of commemoration — something that tells you when a public monument should stay up, come down, or be altered — actually have to get right to be any good? Write me the desiderata.

Give me the cases it has to handle and why each is genuinely hard — the ordinance cases above, plus whatever else the theory can't dodge: the scientist who did real good and real harm, the street named for someone whose fame has simply faded, the monument that's memorializing victims rather than honoring a hero, the one that's really a piece of public art nobody reads as a monument at all. Then the distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse — honoring vs. remembering vs. teaching, the person vs. the event, who the thing is addressed to (the dead, the neighborhood, or the future). And any result it can't contradict, like what we actually know about the historical figure, or about who even sees the thing.

I want to know which of these are real requirements and which are just one camp's taste, which are non-negotiable, and — the part I most care about — whether any two of them can't both be satisfied at once, because if so that's the story. And tell me which single requirement does the most to rule theories out. You can skip scoring the famous schools against the list, I'll do that in the piece. A table is fine if it helps.
