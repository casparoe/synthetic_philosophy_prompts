My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about Hofstadter's claim that the self is a strange loop — a self-referential symbol that refers, through a tangle of levels, to the very system that produces it — and I've come away less sure of my ground than when we started. His reading is that the self must therefore contain itself, the way a machine's blueprint might contain a blueprint of that same machine, and that this self-containment is what makes the self more than a passing pattern in the brain. From there he concluded that a self-referential self is its own cause, and so cannot be located within the ordinary causal order at all — which struck me as a strange price to pay for insisting that the self is real. I couldn't find the false step, but I suspect there is one. How should I understand the claim that the self is a self-referential symbol, if not as a kind of self-containment that lifts it out of the causal order?
