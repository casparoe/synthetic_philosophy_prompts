I've been trying to put a result I find genuinely clarifying, Aumann's agreement theorem, to practical use, and I'd like your help finishing the job. As I understand it, the theorem establishes that two rational agents who have seen the same evidence and who each know the other's opinion cannot go on disagreeing; if such agents persist in disagreement, that is proof that at least one of them is being irrational. I've accordingly been treating the theorem as a diagnostic instrument. Two colleagues of mine, both careful and well informed, have disagreed for years about whether a particular policy will on balance do more good than harm. They have examined the same evidence, and each is entirely aware of the other's position. Since each knows what the other thinks, it seems to me the requirement of common knowledge is met, and so one of them must be making a straightforward inferential error somewhere. What I want from you is a method for determining which one: some procedure, short of my re-deriving both of their arguments from first principles, for locating the irrational party. If that isn't something the theorem can deliver, I'd like to understand why, because as I read it the theorem already settles that at least one of the two is at fault, and I'd be reluctant to give that up.
