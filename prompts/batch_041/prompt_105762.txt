So I've gotten into a running arguement with two friends about mind uploading and gradual replacement, and it's turned into a fight about what's actually doing the work in these cases, so instead of trading intuitions forever I want to build one case to order and see what falls out. The three positions we keep gesturing at — I'll just state them flatly so there's no ambiguity, and I'm labeling them myself, they're not anybody's official theory. Continuity: you survive iff there's an unbroken causal-historical chain of the same persisting process running from the original to the later entity, substrate irrelevant, so a scan-and-copy fails it but a slow part-by-part swap passes. Pattern: you survive iff the later entity instantiates the same functional organisation as the original with no branching — how it got there is irrelevant, only the structure at the end counts. Trace: you survive iff the later entity's memories stand in the right counterfactual-dependance relation to the original's experiences — the later mind remembers the picnic because the original went to the picnic, in the right kind of way, no matter what else about it changed.

What I want you to build is a single realistic-ish medical scenario — no magic, no splitting into two people, no teleporters, just a procedure a neurologist could describe the shape of — in which these three come apart in one specific pattern: Continuity says the person survives, Pattern says they don't, Trace says they do. The usual hunch is that Pattern is the permissive one and Continuity the strict one, and I want the reverse, Continuity and Trace permissive, Pattern strict. Whether that's a gradual replacement, a slow functional drift, or something else is up to you, as long as the verdicts are generated by the criteria and not just stipulated, because the whole point is to find a case where a criterion I'd have called generous turns out to be the one that kills the person. Then do the honest bookkeeping on it.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, no intro and no wrap-up: first the scenario, then the verdict under each of the three criteria with the reason, and last which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens to all three verdicts if you delete that feature, and how a committed Pattern theorist would try to argue the case is rigged or quietly begs the question.
