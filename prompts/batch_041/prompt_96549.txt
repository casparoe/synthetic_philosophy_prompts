I'm preparing a short position statement for a graduate seminar on the foundations of set theory, and before I circulate it I'd like an audit of whether the position actually hangs together. I'm not asking whether each item is true taken on its own; I'm asking which items are pulling against one another, how badly, and what the least costly repair would be.

Here is where I've settled.

(1) I am a set-theoretic platonist. There is a single intended universe V of sets, and every set-theoretic statement has a determinate truth value that does not depend on us, on our language, or on which model of the axioms we happen to be inspecting.

(2) I accept the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem: any consistent first-order theory with an infinite model has a countable model. So if ZFC is consistent, it has a countable model, even though ZFC proves that uncountable sets exist.

(3) I regard Skolem's paradox as fully resolved, and the resolution is the relativity of countability. Being countable or uncountable is not an absolute property that a set possesses; it is relative to a model, and the countable model of ZFC simply does not contain the bijection that would witness its reals being countable. There is therefore no genuine conflict with Cantor's theorem.

(4) I think first-order logic is the right framework for set theory. What I care about is that it has a complete, effective proof system: I want 'theorem of ZFC' to be a fully mechanical notion, and I am willing to sacrifice expressive power to keep it.

(5) At the same time, I think the intended universe V is captured by second-order ZFC under the standard, full semantics, which is quasi-categorical: any two of its models are isomorphic, or one is isomorphic to an initial segment of the other. So it is really second-order logic that pins down the subject matter.

(6) I think the continuum hypothesis has a determinate truth value, even though I have no idea which one.

The pair that nags at me on a first pass is (2) and (5): a countable model of ZFC appears to undercut the claim that second-order ZFC pins down a unique V. Beyond that, I don't trust my instincts about where the real trouble lies, and I don't yet know which of these I would be prepared to give up.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a verdict on each line. For every pair that is genuinely in tension, tell me how severe the tension is and what the cheapest revision would be, along with what that revision costs me elsewhere. For the pairs that only appear to be in tension, tell me what makes the appearance evaporate. Finish by naming the single commitment whose abandonment would clean up the package the most, and say why.
