I keep catching myself giving answers to decision problems that can't all be right, and I'd rather find out what I actually believe than what I'd defend, so I want you to find the principle behind my gut verdicts. Here they are. An outfit has simulated me and tells me it put ten thousand dollars in a sealed box if its simulation showed I'd take only that box and left it empty otherwise; there's also an open box with a thousand in it. I take the sealed box alone. I have a gene that makes me like a particular food and, by a seperate route, raises my risk of a disease, so that eating the food doesn't cause the disease, it's just a marker. I eat the food. A being tells me it flipped a coin yesterday: heads, and it would come ask me for a hundred dollars, tails, and it would hand me ten thousand, but only if its simulation showed I'd hand over the hundred in the heads case. It came up heads and here it is asking, and I don't pay. I'm stranded in the desert and a driver who only picks up people he's confident will pay him a hundred at the end of the trip picks me up, no promise made, he just read me right, and when we arrive I pay him. And if that same outfit is right only a bit more often than a coin, my one-boxing instinct evaporates and I take both boxes.

The pair that's eating at me is the sealed box and the coin: in both, someone has already predicted me, the prediction is locked in, and I'm deciding whether to be the kind of person who got predicted, yet I go opposite ways. The desert driver bugs me for the same reason, except there the ride had already happened, which feels like a distinction about the past rather than about what makes paying rational. I've seen the causal, evidential and updateless menu and none of them gets all five. My own guess is that the difference is whether the prediction is about the choice I'm actually making or about a choice I'd only make in some other world, but that may just be a label for my gut. So state the principle, or the small set of candidates, precisely; tell me which case forces each clause; give me its verdict on a case I haven't listed, which you should invent; and be honest about which of my verdicts it has to throw out. Write it to me as an email, plain paragraphs, no headings or bullets or bold, just prose.
