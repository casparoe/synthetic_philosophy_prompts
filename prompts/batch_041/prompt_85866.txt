I work on reinforcement learning and want the philosophical debate about consequentialist cluelessness translated into the vocabulary I actually use. The worry, as I understand it, is that because the downstream causal effects of any action ramify without limit, a consequentialist cannot know whether a given act made the world better or worse, so the theory cannot issue the verdicts it promises; I gather there are at least two versions, one where the relevant calculation is merely too large to carry out and one where the consequences are in principle unknowable because the world is chaotic and open-ended. Restate the debate as faithfully as you can in terms of agents, environments, reward functions, credit assignment, long horizons and discounting, model-based versus model-free evaluation, off-policy and counterfactual evaluation, and sparse or delayed reward. Then give me the residue report: which of the philosophers' claims translate cleanly, which I have had to distort to make them fit, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I am especially interested in whether the translation silently assumes something — a well-defined reward signal, an exogenous and fixed environment, a bounded state space — that is precisely what the cluelessness objection denies, and in whether the two versions of the worry remain distinct once restated.
