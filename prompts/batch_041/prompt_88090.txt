I left school at sixteen and have worked ever since as a joiner, most of it on old buildings — repairs, replacements, the unglamorous end of it. That is the only vocabulary I really think in. I have no academic background at all; everything I know about philosophy I have picked up from forums and from arguing with people online. For the last two years or so I have been reading, on and off, about the problem of building AI systems that avoid side effects, and I have reached the point where I would like it translated into my own terms.

Here is the dispute as I understand it. Some people say you can give a machine a general instruction like "keep the world as unchanged as possible" or "avoid collateral damage," and turn it into something measurable — for instance, a penalty for letting the world drift from the path it would have taken had the system done nothing, or a penalty for shutting off options that were previously open, or simply a penalty for acting at all. Others say this cannot work: any such measure has to compare world-states, and any comparison has to treat some features of a situation as the ones that count, and choosing those features is already choosing what is valuable. They add that the line between the goal of an action and its side effects is not written into the world either. There is also, I gather, a case where the supposedly low-impact thing to do is to do nothing at all, which is not obviously helpful to anyone.

What I want is this. Restate that whole dispute — both sides, as faithfully as you can — in the language of building conservation, which is the closest thing I have to a formal framework. Use the words I actually use: fabric, significance, minimum intervention, reversibility, like-for-like repair, honest repair, patina, the record, the conservation plan, the do-nothing option. Assume I am fluent in them; I can read a conservation plan and I have argued with a conservation officer and won. Do not water the translation down for a layman.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I care about.

- In my trade the do-nothing option is not a real option. Damp, beetles and weather keep working whether I touch the building or not, and a building left alone decays. The AI argument seems to rest on there being a trajectory the system would have followed if it had done nothing, and on measuring drift from that. Does that survive the translation, or does my world not have the thing it needs?
- I never have a task other than keeping the building. There is no point of the operation that makes some changes intended and others collateral; everything I do is either repair or damage, and the argument is always about which. So does the goal-versus-side-effect distinction translate at all, or is it something my framework simply cannot express?
- I judge change case by case, by argument, against a statement of significance, in front of other people. I have never once had a number. Does the idea of an impact measure — a quantity computed over a state of the world and compared with another — survive at all in my vocabulary, or can conservation only ever give a qualitative and contested judgment?
- Reversibility is the nearest thing I have to a rule that does not depend on what you value. But not polishing something is also a decision, and reversibility can be an excuse for leaving a problem to the next man. Is my reversibility the same as their keeping options open, and is it really value-neutral?
- My building has a boundary, and the boundary was drawn by whoever listed it. The machine has no such boundary. Is that the whole of the difference, or is there more hiding behind it?
- Significance shifts. What one generation's conservation plan calls important the next one calls clutter, and we de-list things. Does that mean my trade has already conceded the second side's point, or does it show that a workable practice does not need a fixed specification of value?

Finish with a verdict on the residue itself: is it a defect of my vocabulary, something it is blind to, or is it evidence that the AI dispute is shallower than it sounds? And tell me plainly whether my instinct — that avoiding side effects is just minimum intervention with a computer bolted on — holds up.
