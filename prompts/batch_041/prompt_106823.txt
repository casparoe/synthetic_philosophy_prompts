In a seminar on semantic anti-realism, a colleague defended the slogan ‘all truths are knowable’ as the position’s central commitment, and another objected that the knowability paradox—the derivation that if every truth is knowable then every truth is known—leaves it either trivial or untenable. I suspect they are working with different precisifications. Please lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan could express—what sort of possibility is meant, who must be able to know, at what time, and whether the subject is propositions, sentences, or whole theories—and give a verdict on each: trivial, substantive and defensible, or false. Then identify which reading the anti-realist actually needs, which reading the paradox refutes, and whether any reading survives both objections. Please answer in British English.
