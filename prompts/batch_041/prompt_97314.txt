I am a software engineer and the only technical member of the records committee of a post-conflict truth commission. Our enabling statute offers amnesty for politically motivated acts when the applicant makes full disclosure of all relevant facts; it says nothing about remorse, and the commission has never defined what full disclosure requires. We are now deciding the application of a former police captain, D. M., who ran a detention site during the conflict. His written submission is factually detailed about dates, orders, and chains of command, and it has already helped us locate three unmarked graves. But he writes in the passive voice, does not name the detainees he personally interrogated, and when asked whether the interrogations were wrong, says only that they were lawful under the emergency regulations then in force. He offers no apology. The committee is split. Some members say the statute requires facts, not contrition, and that the graves are enough; others say amnesty without acknowledgment purchases silence and asks victims' families to accept the state's sanitized version of what happened. One commissioner has proposed a two-part test for these cases: first, does the submission close factual gaps in our record; second, does it treat the victims as wronged rather than merely affected. She calls the second part the acknowledgment condition. I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's vote. Please draft it. Recommend a concrete decision on this application—grant, deny, or grant with conditions—with a time frame and any conditions the commission can actually enforce. Give us a test for full disclosure that we can apply to future cases, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side, especially the worry that requiring contrition makes amnesty a reward for the right feelings rather than a tool for truth. I have no formal philosophy background, so keep the memo in plain language and do not include citations or reading lists; the commission needs reasons it can act on, not a literature review.
