I'm a novelist, which means my whole job is guessing what someone wants from what they do, and Ive run into a thing I don't have a word for. It comes in two flavours and I'm not sure they're the same thing.

One. There's a woman in my last book who always gives up her seat on the train. My editor read her as kind. I had written her as frightened: she can't stand a stranger standing over her and she stands up to end it. Nothing on the page decides between the two, and both readings survive every scene she's in. Same book, related problem: I tried to deepen another character by giving her a want — she wants to be warm — and then realised that every room she ever stands in is the same temperature, so the want never sorts one option above another. It's real, it's hers, and you could delete it without changing a single thing she does. So some wants are invisible in principle, not just hard to spot.

Two, and this may be a different animal entirely: we — readers, me, everyone — treat small cheap gestures as deep disclosures (she leaves the porch light on, therefore lonely), but when someone does something large and expensive we reach straight for the situation (she only stayed because she had nowhere else to go). Which feels backwards, since the costly act is the one that actually cost her something. Or am I missing why that isn't backwards?

So: is there an established name for any of this? I assume somebody in economics or AI or psychology has chewed on the problem of inferring what a creature wants from what it does, and I'd like to know the nearest named ideas, how they differ from each other, and which of them my second case belongs to, if any — I have a hunch it's just how audiences are built. If there's no single term, coin one, and tell me where it stops: what it covers and what it dosn't. I'm after the boundary lines, not the label. And please don't just hand me a reading list, I want the carving.
