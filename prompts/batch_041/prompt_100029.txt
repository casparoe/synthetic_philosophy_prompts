Say Simone Weil got to see how politics is actually done in 2025, since she never did. Two things she'd run into. First, the hollowed-out mass party: no branches, no meetings, a tiny dues-paying membership, mostly a brand, a mailing list and a data operation. Second, the thing people keep pitching as the fix for exactly that — a policy market where anyone can stake on a proposal, your weight comes from how often you've been right before, and the proposals themselves are drafted by a chatbot that almost nobody reads past the headline. Someone tells her the market finally solves the problem she had with parties. What would she say? I dont really care whether she'd approve or not — I want to know which of her actual objections to party politics still bite here, which ones just don't apply anymore, and where she'd say the market is the same disease in a new outfit. Be specific about the mechanism behind each objection instead of telling me she distrusted mass enthusiasm.
