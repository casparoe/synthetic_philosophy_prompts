The version that gets me is the Andromeda case: two people walking past each other on Earth, one headed each way, and their 'now' on Andromeda differs by days. So at least one of them is wrong about what's real over there right now, except there's no fact about who's wrong. Either my grandpa is still at his kitchen table in 1994 or he isn't, and I genuinely can't tell what the physics is supposed to be telling me.

My roommate and I have been going in circles for months. So write the document we should have written instead: the joint statement from an adverserial collaboration between the two camps. Camp A: the argument goes through, and the block follows from SR plus one innocent premise about distant events being real. Camp B: the argument smuggles in its conclusion, because it treats 'real for observer O' as an unqualified 'real', and simultaneity-relative facts don't add up to existence.
