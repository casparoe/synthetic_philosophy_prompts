I got into a dumb argument in a group chat about whether the EU actually has a democratic deficit, someone linked one of those posts about quadratic voting and meta-level governance, and it made me realize I've never checked wether my own views about who should get a say actually hang together. So I'm dumping them here for an audit. (1) The all-affected principle is basically right: anyone whose interests are significantly affected by a decision ought to have a say in it. (2) But legitimacy needs a determinate demos — you can't define a majority, or even hold the vote, unless the set of participants is fixed in advance and doesn't depend on how the vote comes out. (3) In practice the only workable way to fix that set is territorial jurisdiction, so the demos is roughly whoever lives in the territory. (4) Future people are hugely affected by what we do now, so by (1) they count, and I think the right model is that present-day citizens act as trustees on their behalf. (5) Animals have morally weighty interests and I'd happily vote for a party that took them seriously, but I don't think they should get votes or that it would even mean anything. (6) Any group that wants to leave a bigger polity and run its own should be allowed to, as long as the process is peaceful and internally democratic — I don't think outsiders get a veto over someone else's secession.

I hold all of this sincerely, but I have a nagging feeling that at least two of these are quietly pulling in opposite directions and I honestly can't tell which ones. What I want is an audit, not a survey: which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are, ranked by how bad the tension is, and for each real one, the cheapest revision that would restore consistency plus what I'd be giving up by making it. Please don't just flag everything as equally problematic — I need to know which single commitment is doing the most damage to the rest. One more thing that may or may not matter: I'm generally on the side of thinking legitimacy is about how power is actually exercised rather than about moral truths, though I'm not sure that's load-bearing here.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my question back to me.
