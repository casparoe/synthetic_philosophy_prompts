Write a short story about a music theorist who attempts to test Schopenhauer's claim that music is not, like the other arts, a copy of the Platonic Ideas, but a direct objectification and copy of the Will itself. The story should center on a specific, invented musical composition that the theorist believes is a counterexample: a piece that satisfies every conventional criterion for being music, yet which seems to express not the ceaseless striving of the Will but a static, self-contained Idea — a structure that simply is, without tension, longing, or resolution. The narrative should dramatize the conceptual stakes of this discovery. If the piece is music, does it undermine Schopenhauer's distinction between music and the other arts? If it is not music, what criterion excludes it, and does that criterion covertly import representational content? The story should include a scene in which the protagonist explains the distinction between copying the Will and copying the Ideas to a skeptical composer, using a concrete musical passage as the vehicle, and a later scene in which the protagonist's own hearing of the piece is altered by the theoretical dispute. Incorporate Schopenhauer's hierarchy of musical elements — the bass as the lowest grade of the Will's objectification, harmony as the middle grades, melody as the highest grade, analogous to human intellect — in a way that matters to the plot. Do not resolve the tension with a simple appeal to music's emotional expressiveness; keep the metaphysical question open.
