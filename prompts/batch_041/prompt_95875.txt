I've been in a long running argument, first in grad school and now with the same two friends, about where persuasion stops and manipulation starts, and at this point I want a diagnosis rather than another round. The shape of it: one camp says manipulation is wrong because it bypasses your rational agency, it works around your deliberating self instead of through it. Another says no, the mark of manipulation is covertness or deception, either the influence is hidden or the intentions behind it are misrepresented. A third says the real issue is whether the influence leaves you worse off by your own lights, or whether it fails to respect you. And theres a fourth position I half suspect is right: manipulation just isn't a natural kind, it's a pejorative we slap on influence we dislike, so all of these criteria are stipulations dressed up as discoveries.

We agree on the paradigm cases (gaslighting someone, lying to a mark) and on the paradigm non-cases (giving someone a reason and letting them decide). Everything that matters is in the middle: emotional appeals, framing, exploiting a cognitive bias the person would on reflection endorse, engineering which options someone even sees, a friend hiding your cigarettes because your future self asked them to. Nobody moves on the middle cases and I can't tell whether we're having a real disagreement about what makes influence wrongful, or four people answering four different questions under one word.

So diagnose it for me. Is this verbal, substantive, or people answering different questions? Which criterion is actually doing the work, and which apparent disagreements dissolve once you separate what makes an act manipulation from what makes manipulation wrong? And tell me what progress would even look like here: a definition that gets all the cases, a demonstration that there's no such definiton and we should be asking a different question, or an admission that the middle cases are settled by intuitions nobody can argue for. Don't just report what various philosophers think, I want the structure of the disagreement itself and what would actually move it.
