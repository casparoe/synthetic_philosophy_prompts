I have no academic training in philosophy, but I have spent a lot of time reading about laws of nature on my own, and I have reached a question I cannot settle by thinking harder. As I understand it, one side holds that a law of nature is nothing over and above the pattern of what actually happens — that a law is simply the best and simplest summary of the whole history of the universe — while the other side holds that laws are something further, the very things that make events happen, so that two universes could agree on absolutely everything that occurs and still differ in their laws. (I have seen these called the Humean and anti-Humean sides, though I am not asking who held what; names and reading lists are of no use to me.)

I would like you to build me a case to order that isolates exactly this disagreement. Specifications. Describe two universes that agree at every point of space and time on every fact that is not itself about what must, could, or has to be the case. Use no modal vocabulary anywhere in the description: no must, no cannot, no powers, no tendencies, nothing that says how things have to go rather than how they do go. Keep the description of the two universes to a single paragraph. Make them as boring and physical as possible, and let the laws be the only thing added — no God, no minds, no purposes, no extra ingredient smuggled in under another name. The two universes must differ in nothing but their laws. And I want the case built rather than stipulated: each side's verdict must follow from what that side is already committed to, not from a definition I have quietly chosen for it.

Then do the following. Say what the summary side must say about my case and what the governing side must say, with the reasoning for each; my guess is that the summary side has to call it impossible, one universe described twice, while the governing side calls it straightforwardly possible, but if either would say something else, say what and why. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work — the thing that, if I altered it, would bring the two sides into agreement — and then build that nearby case for me, so that I can see the feature isolated. Tell me honestly whether my specifications are even satisfiable, or whether I have asked for a contradiction. And finish with the strongest attack on the case from the side that comes out worse.

What I want the case for: to find out whether the claim that laws are fixed by what happens is a discovery about laws or a stipulation about what counts as one.
