I draw typefaces as a hobby, a font or two a year, nothing professional, and I keep bumping into something I don't have a word for.

Here's the pattern. I draw an 'a' I'm happy with, then I draw a 'g', and now the 'a' looks wrong. Not wrong as an 'a' -- its still perfectly readable as an 'a' -- wrong for the alphabet it's sitting in. And when I go back to fix it, I'm not making it more like my other a's, because there's only one a. It's more like the 'g' has decided something about the 'a' that the 'a' couldn't decide about itself.

It runs the other way too. I took an 'a' out of one font and dropped it, unchanged, into another font I'd drawn, and it read like a foreign body, even though the two shapes were nearly identical. Same shape, wrong letter.

And there's definately a right answer. I can tell when I've got it and so can people I show it to, but I can't state the rule, and it isn't "looks like my other letters" or "looks like other a's" -- I've drawn a's that resembled nothing I'd ever seen and they were obviously right. A really neutral, generic 'a' is unambiguously an 'a' and is also nobody's 'a', if that makes sense.

I know about family resemblance and prototypes, and I know there's something called holism where a word's meaning depends on the whole language. Neither one is quite it. Family resemblance is about a pile of examples, whereas this is about a small closed set whose members are all being made at the same time; holism is about meaning, and this seems to be about shape, or maybe about correctness.

So: does this have a name? If it does, I want the name and then, more to the point, exactly how far it reaches -- is it the same animal as the holism people talk about, or something else wearing similar clothes? If it doesn't have a name, coin one, and give me the edges rather than the label: does a single letter all by itself have it, or do you need at least two? Does the 'a' of a font that only ever sets one word still have it? And does any of it carry over to things that aren't perceptual -- the way a person's traits seem to fix each other, or the way a dish only makes sense on a menu?

I don't really want the word. I want the shape of the thing: what it is, what it isn't, and where it stops.
