My friend is weighing two separate payout decisions and has asked me to check her reasoning. In the first, Option A pays her $10,000 for certain, while Option B pays $10,000 with probability 0.89, $60,000 with probability 0.10, and nothing with probability 0.01; she chooses A, explaining that she wants the guaranteed money. In the second, Option C pays $10,000 with probability 0.11 and nothing otherwise, while Option D pays $60,000 with probability 0.10 and nothing otherwise; she chooses D, explaining that C's odds of paying anything at all are too poor to be worth bothering with. Each choice on its own strikes me as defensible, and yet I have a nagging sense that the two of them cannot both be right. Is that sense correct? If there is a genuine inconsistency here, I want to understand exactly where it lies and which principle her pair of choices commits her to violating — and if there is no inconsistency, I want to understand why my unease is misplaced.
