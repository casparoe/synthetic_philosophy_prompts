I've been following the literature on higher-order theories of consciousness as an interested amateur — I write software for a living, no formal philosophy training, and I came to this through the analogy of a system modelling its own internal states — and what keeps striking me is that the argument between the higher-order camp and its first-order critics has been running for something like three decades without either side gaining ground. Both camps accept the same stock of experimental results (masked priming, blindsight, the various dissociation and no-report cases), both accept that some mental states are conscious and others are not, and both agree that a theory of consciousness owes an account of what draws that line. Yet the higher-order side keeps insisting that a state is conscious in virtue of being represented by a further state — with an internal split over whether that further state must be occurrent or may merely be dispositional — while the first-order side keeps replying that this either changes the subject or presupposes what it set out to explain, and nothing moves. What makes it feel less like a normal scientific dispute is that the parties rarely seem to be disagreeing about a fact that a cleverer experiment could settle; they seem to be disagreeing about something upstream of the experiments.

My own hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than adopt, is that this is not really a disagreement about consciousness at all but about what the explanandum is: one side is trying to explain phenomenal character, the other is trying to explain a state's availability to the rest of the system, and the single word "consciousness" is being asked to cover both. But I can just as easily imagine the opposite diagnosis — that both sides share the explanandum and the disagreement is a genuine, substantive one about the order of explanation; or that a hidden empirical question is doing the work and the current paradigms simply cannot reach it; or that it rests on bedrock intuitions about whether a state can be conscious "in itself," which neither side can argue for. What I want is a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is the impasse verbal, a difference in what counts as the phenomenon to be explained, a disagreement about what would count as evidence, or an unargued clash of starting intuitions? Argue it from what each side actually claims, not as a survey of who says what. And given your diagnosis, tell me what would genuinely count as progress — a conceptual distinction, a decisive experiment, a formal result — or argue that none should be expected and explain why.
