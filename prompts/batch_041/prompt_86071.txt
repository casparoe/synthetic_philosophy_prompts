I retired from the lab a few years back and have been reading philosophy ever since, and I've definately gotten stuck on Maxwell's demon. Everyone seems to have a different idea of what it would even take to settle the thing, so before I read one more paper I want the spec first: what does any adequate treatment of the demon have to actually deliver?

Give it to me as a list of requirements, each with the case or fact behind it. Roughly: the setups it has to handle (the demon sorting fast from slow molecules through a trapdoor, the one-molecule engine that gets work out of a single particle, the ratchet and pawl that looks like it runs on thermal fluctuations alone); the distinctions it must not collapse (entropy of the gas vs. whatever it costs to know about the gas, thermodynamic entropy vs. the information-theoretic kind, the cost of measuring vs. the cost of forgetting, what's true of the demon's own dynamics vs. what's true of the gas); and the results it can't contradict (the second law, at least in its statistical form, and the reversibility of the microscopic mechanics underneath).

Then tell me which of these can't all hold at once. I have a hunch at least one pair is incompatible, and if so that seems like the whole answer right there. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just taste, and tell me which single one does the most work -- i.e. kills the most of the quick fixes people offer. Don't bother scoring particular authors, I'll do that myself later.
