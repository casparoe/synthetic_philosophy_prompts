Write a parody of a credit-rating agency report — the sober, formatted document in which an analyst assigns a letter grade to a sovereign borrower — except that the issuer being rated is the governance system of an invented jurisdiction. The Free City of Marrow Bay has governed itself by prediction market for eleven years. Its charter fixes a single metric, the Composite Transactional Welfare Index, and provides that any proposal reaching the ballot passes if and only if the conditional market prices the Index higher in the world where the proposal passes than in the world where it fails. The report should be dated, marked confidential, carry a rating and an outlook, and move through the standard sections — rating rationale, key rating drivers, what could change the rating — in deadpan institutional prose, around 700 words.

The humor must come from getting the mechanism design right rather than from winking, and every item below should appear as an ordinary line in the analyst's reasoning, stated flatly, without explanation and without any hint that it is funny: the Counterfactual Baseline Office, which rules retroactively on what would have happened had a defeated proposal passed, and which the report notes in passing has never been audited; the liquidity floor, under which any proposal attracting less than a fixed dollar volume of trading is deemed to have failed, so that the indifferent, the busy, and the poor all vote no by abstaining; the charter's authors being asked whether the market decides facts or values and answering that it decides both, which they treat as an economy rather than a bug; the Sag Harbor Sewage Futures Incident, in which a measure passed because it changed how the Index was measured rather than how anyone lived; the Index itself counting only monetized transactions, so that a policy which converts unpaid household care into paid services registers as pure improvement; and the disclosure that the agency's own rating is itself traded on the jurisdiction's exchange, with the market currently pricing it slightly too generous. The analyst should never break frame, never editorialize, and never let on that any of this is a joke; the reader should be able to tell what is being ironized from the drift of the document alone, and someone who follows prediction-market governance should find the satire fair rather than lazy.

The jurisdiction, its agencies, the Incident, and the Index are all invented — do not name any real person, place, publication, or country, and do not import any real-world history into the fiction. Begin directly with the report itself, header and first section, with no preface, framing note, or commentary from you.
