I'm in this seminar on religion and politics and I keep having gut reactions to cases that I can't square with each other, and I want to know if there's actually a principle under them or if I'm just making it up as I go. Here's where I land, case by case. A state legislator who thinks abortion is murder for religious reasons votes to restrict it, but in floor debate argues only about late-term health risks and clinic safety — I think thats fine, maybe even admirable. Same legislator stands up and says the bill is required because God commands it — not fine, I think he should keep that to himself. A church that runs a food pantry lobbies the city for more funding, and the pastor says they feed people because Christ compels them — totally fine, nobody should mind that. A neighborhood group opposes a mosque going up because they say it would change the area's Christian character — obviously not fine. A union organizer who's also a lay preacher argues for a higher minimum wage by pointing to the verse about workers deserving their pay — fine, I think. But someone arguing against same-sex marriage by pointing to scripture — not fine — even though both of those are just citing the Bible at a policy question, and I can't immediately say why the first is ok and the second isn't. A doctor testifying for a law against assisted suicide, saying her faith and her oath forbid it — I feel fine about that one, maybe because she'd be the one asked to do it. And the one I keep going back and forth on: a climate activist whose real motivation is that nature is sacred, but who always argues in public about insurance costs and crop yields — is that honest, or is it the same as the legislator hiding his reasons?

The closest thing I have to a principle is that whats doing the work isn't whether a reason is religious in where it comes from, but whether someone who doesn't share that source could still be expected to weigh it — fine when the religious reason could be restated in terms anyone can assess, not fine when it only moves you if you already accept the authority behind it. But I can tell this doesn't fit the minimum-wage case and the marriage case at once, since as far as I can tell they're structurally identical and I treat them differently; a friend suggested the real difference is whether the religious bit is load-bearing or just decoration, and I can't tell if that saves my principle or just relabels the problem. So what I want is: the principle, or two or three candidate principles, that best captures my verdicts, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to something new, what each one predicts for the climate activist and for a case I haven't listed — a religious hospital refusing to provide a service that's otherwise legally required — and an honest list of which of my verdicts it has to overturn. And if it turns out I'm just inconsistent and nothing gets more than half of these, please say that plainly instead of massaging a principle to fit; I'd genuinely rather know.
