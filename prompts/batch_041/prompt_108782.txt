I'm trying to get my head around natural kinds. I get that water and gold are natural kinds becuase they have real essences—H2O and Au. But then I read about homeostatic property clusters, where a kind is just a bunch of properties that hang together because of some underlying mechanism, not because of a single essence. So does that mean predator could be a natural kind? Predators share a lot—eyes in front, sharp teeth, hunting behavior—and there are causal mechanisms that keep those properties together. But if predator counts, then what doesn't? Isn't natural kind just whatever category a science finds useful? And if so, doesn't that mean the sciences aren't discovering real divisions in nature—they're just imposing convenient labels? Or is there still a way to distinguish the categories that are really real from the ones that are just useful?
