Two people in my extended friend group have been going at each other for months about whether destroying habitat is bad for wild animals, and I've offered to write the document they should have written instead of another 400-message thred. One of them is the conservation type: habitat loss kills animals, displaces them, pushes species under, and that's the end of it, it's a harm and we should stop doing it. The other one is a wild-animal-welfare type who suspects the opposite might be true. His reasoning is roughly that if the average wild animal's life is a net negative, and a hectare of forest or scrub is packed with r-selected animals that mostly die young and badly, then flattening that hectare might reduce the total amount of suffering in the world, or at least not obviously raise it. He isn't trolling and neither is she, and I want a document both of them could sign.

So: draft the joint statement of their adversarial collaboration on whether habitat destruction reduces or increases wild animal suffering. I want four things.

1. An agreed-claims section, numbered. What do they both actually accept about what wild animals' lives are like, about what habitat destruction does to populations versus to individual animals, about how much anyone can really predict here, and about what the question even is. I have a suspicion that half their fight is about whether we're asking about total suffering, suffering per animal, or sheer number of animals, so make them pin that down. Nothing so vague it's just a truce, and nothing worded so it secretly favors one side.

2. A disagreements section as a table, with a column for the point at issue, a column giving the exact claim each side would put its name to (phrased so that side genuinely accepts the wording), and a column for why it matters.

3. The cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one of them. Some I think are already on the table: whether wild animals' lives are net negative on average; whether clearing habitat cuts the total animal-days lived in that area or just relocates the misery elsewhere; how animals actually die when the habitat goes (fire, slow starvation, exposure, predators) compared with how they'd have died otherwise; whether the right comparison is to the untouched status quo or to some realistic alternative use of the land; and whether insects and other small invertebrates dominate the arithmetic enough to swamp every vertebrate argument. Add any crux I've missed.

4. For each crux, one concrete case or argument that both of them would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Make the cases specific enough to be checkable in principle, like a hectare of scrub cleared for a solar farm, or a drained wetland, or a stretch of forest converted to pasture, and for each one have them say what they expect about roughly how many animal-days get lived there before and after and how bad those days are.

If it turns out part of their dispute is really just about words, for instance whether suffering should count only felt pain or also lives cut short, say so plainly and say which part. Plain English, no citations, no reading list, and no lecturing them about what a hectare is.
