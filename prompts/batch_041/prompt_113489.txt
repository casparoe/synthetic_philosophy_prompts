My friend Maya just defended a PhD in automated planning and is moving to a robotics job in another city, and for her going-away thing I want to read a poem about the frame problem — half as a joke, half not, since it's basically the ghost that haunted her whole field. I want a sestina, and I want the six end-words to be: still, else, wait, fire, reach, after. You can pick the starting order, but the whole reason I want a sestina and not, say, a sonnet is that those six words keep coming back in a rotating order while every other word in the poem is new — which is the frame problem in miniature: some things persist for free because of the form, everything else has to be said again from scratch, and you never get to state once and for all which is which. If someone who's never heard of the frame problem reads it, they should still get a decent poem about somebody leaving one room and finding another room mostly unchanged. The envoi is the part I care about most — that's where all six have to fit at once in three lines, and I want it to feel like the moment you finally try to write the complete list and can't.

The content should be right, though. By the frame problem I mean what happens when you try to write down, for a reasoning system, everything that does not change when it acts: after the robot moves the cup, the wall is still there, the lamp is still on, the table has not turned into a bird — and there's no finite general way to list all that, so you either write an axiom for every single thing that stays put (which never ends), or you write one sweeping rule that anything not mentioned just stays as it was, and then you get the temporal projection mess where a harmless wait can just as easily unload the gun as keep it loaded. And even if you fixed that, you'd still have to spell out every condition under which the action works at all, which is its own bottomless pit. I'd like the poem to feel that squeeze — the six words coming back as if they were free, and the poem quietly getting away with something the machine can't.

Please dont put any of the jargon in the poem itself — no fluent, no nonmonotonic, no circumscription — I want someone who knows the problem to recognize it and someone who doesn't to just read a poem about a friend moving away. Ending on still or else is probly best if it works out.
