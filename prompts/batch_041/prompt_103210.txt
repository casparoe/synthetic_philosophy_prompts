Galen Strawson's Basic Argument runs, in outline, as follows. You do what you do, in any given situation, because of the way you are. To be ultimately responsible for what you do, you would have to be ultimately responsible for the way you are, at least in certain crucial mental respects. But you cannot be ultimately responsible for the way you are, because that would require you to be the cause of yourself — to be causa sui — and nothing can be the cause of itself. So you cannot be ultimately responsible for what you do. Strawson emphasises that the argument makes no appeal to determinism: it would go through even for a self-creating agent, and it threatens praise as much as blame.
