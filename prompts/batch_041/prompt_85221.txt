I've gotten myself into a stretch of second-guessing everything and I need someone to tell me what I actually think.

A few months ago a friend showed me one of those prediction market websites and I've been on it ever since — small money, but enough that I check it at night. Then last month I bought a used bike off a guy for way under what it was worth, and a week later the frame turned out to be cracked. And somewhere in there a friend of mine whose done a masters in econ told me there's a theorem that says if everyone is rational and everyone knows everyone else is rational, nobody should ever trade, which obviously isn't how the world works, and I want to know what the catch is.

Here's what my gut says about when a price, or somebody offering me a trade, should change my mind:

1. A big liquid market has X at 20%. I have no special knowledge and my hunch is 40%. I shouldn't bet, and my hunch should move toward 20% — but not all the way, because I know these markets are sometimes badly wrong about things like this.
2. A price jumps 15 points on news I can't figure out. That move should shift my belief anyway, because somebody probably knows something.
3. My coworker, who has read exactly the same emails about the project that I have, offers to bet me even money that it ships late. I don't read the offer as information at all — I just figure he likes gambling.
4. I've read the 200-page report and nobody else has. Bet away, that's the whole point.
5. A "market" with $200 of volume and one weird price. That's not a market, that's one guy. No information.
6. The guy selling the bike took my lowball instantly. That should of been a warning.

Two of these don't fit together and I know it. I treat a stranger's low price as a signal about the goods, but I refuse to treat my coworker's bet as a signal about the project, and the only difference I can point to is that my coworker is a known gambler. Meanwhile I say the market's 20% should beat my 40% hunch, and then I say my private reading of a report makes me the one who knows better.

Is there a principle that gets all six, or close? If one of my gut reactions has to go, which one, and why? And tell me what the principle predicts for three things I haven't listed: a company offering me an extended warranty on a used car; a prediction market where every trader has read the same three articles I have; and whether there's any point in my reading the news at all if prices supposedly already contain everything.

Format: give me the principle first (or two or three candidates if one won't do, stated as precisely as you can), then one table with a row for every case above plus the three new ones, with these columns: Case | My gut verdict | What the principle says | Agree? (yes/no/partly) | Which part of the principle is doing the work | If you're overruling me, which verdict goes and why.
