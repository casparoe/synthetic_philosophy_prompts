My school's philosophy club has been split all year between one member who thinks that other possible worlds are just as concrete and real as our own world, and that ours is distinguished only by being the one we happen to inhabit, and another who thinks all such talk is a useful way of representing what could have been, and that treating those worlds as real entities is a mistake. Write the joint statement the two of them could sign: the claims they both accept about what work talk of possible worlds is supposed to do and about what each view costs; the precise points where they still part ways, with each side's position worded in a way that side would accept; and the cruxes — I suspect one is whether 'actual' works more like 'here' than like 'real', and another is whether the dispute is partly about what the word 'exists' is permitted to mean, which would make part of it verbal — and for each crux a case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Please include one case I've been turning over: suppose physicists announced that our universe is one of infinitely many concrete universes; I want each side's prediction about whether that tells for or against the realist. Write it as continuous prose — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — the way one would write a letter to both of them. If any of this rests on a confused premise or can't be done well, I would rather you say so plainly than work around it politely.
