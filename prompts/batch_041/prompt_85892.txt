Among the arguments for releasing frontier models with open weights, one analogy does a great deal of quiet work: the crypto wars. Strong encryption was once treated as a munition, its publication discouraged and its authors investigated, and yet open, public, peer-reviewed cryptography prevailed — with the moral drawn that keeping a capability secret is both futile and counterproductive, and that transparency rather than secrecy is what makes a system trustworthy. I want that analogy audited rather than asserted, because it is being asked to license a conclusion about models whose weights are the whole of the capability, and I suspect it is carrying more than it can bear.

Begin by laying out the correspondence explicitly — a table is welcome. What, in cryptography, maps onto the model architecture, the trained weights, the training data, the key, the adversary, the standards process, the export-control regime, and the ordinary user who merely wants to communicate securely? Then say where the mapping breaks. I am especially interested in a structural asymmetry the analogy seems to glide over. In cryptography, security is deliberately made to rest on a secret key rather than on the secrecy of the design, so publishing the design costs the defender little and buys public scrutiny; a model offers no comparable object, because the thing you would publish is the very thing that constitutes the capability, so the defensive benefit of openness — auditing, reproducibility, distributed oversight — and the offensive cost of diffusion fall on the same object. Decide whether that asymmetry is decisive or only a difference of degree, and whether the analogy has any resources to accommodate it.

Finally, separate the disanalogies that are load-bearing for the conclusion from those that are decorative, say whether the open-weights conclusion can be re-established from whatever part of the analogy survives, and, if it cannot, sketch a better comparison and state what that one would license. I do not want a survey of the open-source debate; I want the analogy taken apart and the conclusion put back together, or shown not to survive the taking apart.
