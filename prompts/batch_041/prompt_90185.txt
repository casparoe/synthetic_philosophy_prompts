I am preparing a graduate seminar on the timing of conscious experience, focused on the color phi phenomenon and the explanatory strategies Daniel Dennett labels Orwellian and Stalinesque. Before we assess particular accounts, I want a specification of what any adequate theory in this area must deliver.

Write the desiderata such a theory must meet. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. The cases to be accommodated include, at minimum:

1. The basic color phi report: subjects presented with a red dot at one location followed by a green dot at another, under suitable timing, report a single dot moving between the locations and changing color midway, with the color change appearing to occur before the second stimulus is presented.
2. The causal constraint: the second stimulus cannot cause an experience that occurs before it. The theory must either deny that the experience really occurs before the stimulus, deny that the report faithfully records the experience, or offer some third option.
3. Parametric dependence: the illusion appears only within certain spatial and temporal windows; outside them, subjects report two separate dots or no color change. The theory must explain why.
4. Shape dependence: if the two stimuli are different shapes rather than identical dots, the color change is not attributed to a single moving object. The theory must explain this.
5. A novel variant: suppose the second stimulus is accompanied by a simultaneous third stimulus at the midpoint, of a different color (say blue). Subjects report a single dot changing from red to blue to green. What must the theory say about the midpoint stimulus? Does it require a separate filling-in mechanism, or is the midpoint just another draft in the multiple-drafts picture?
6. Neurophysiological constraint: there is no single finish line or Cartesian theater in the brain; processing is distributed and multiple drafts may coexist. The theory must be consistent with this.
7. The experience-memory distinction: the theory must say whether there is a fact of the matter about what was experienced at a given moment, independent of the subject's later report.
8. Other temporal illusions: the theory must not contradict, and ideally should illuminate, phenomena such as the flash-lag effect, the cutaneous rabbit, and metacontrast masking.

Then identify any pair of these requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. Which single requirement does the most to exclude existing candidates? If you had to drop one requirement to make the set consistent, which would it be, and what would be lost?
