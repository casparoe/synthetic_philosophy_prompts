I sit on the little ethics advisory panel for my town's planning board. Usually we deal with noise complaints and whether the marina can run a fireworks night, but next month we've got something real: a company wants to build an octopus farm on the old industrial waterfront. Big insulated tank shed, a few hundred thousand octopuses at a time, grown for restaurants. Their paperwork is clean, the zoning works, and in their covering letter they promise to meet "the same welfare standards as any other farmed animal" (thats their phrasing, not mine).

The catch is that our state's animal welfare code only covers vertebrates, so there are no standards for them to meet. One of my colleagues won't let it go: she says octopuses aren't in the code becuase nobody drafting it bothered to think about them, not because anyone sat down and decided they don't count, and that if we only protect the animals that remind us of us then we're just doing prejudice with extra steps. Another member says that's silly — an octopus isn't a person and isn't a pig, and noticing that isn't bigotry, it's just noticing a difference — and anyway, he says, the real question is whether the farm leaves the animals better or worse off than the alternative, and the alternative is that they never exist at all. If the tanks are decent, that's a few hundred thousand lives that are mostly fine; refusing the permit doesn't rescue them, it just means they're never born.

Then there's the member who reads too much philosophy and keeps saying something I genuinely can't tell is profound or nonsense. His line: when you compare animals you can't just ask how bad a life can get for one of them, you have to weigh how bad it can be for one animal against how many animals there are, so a huge pile of animals who can only suffer a little might add up to more than a small number who can suffer a lot. Which would mean the number in that application — a few hundred thousand — is the part of this whole thing we've all been quietly ignoring.

Also we had a marine biologist in last week who said, basically, we don't know whether octopuses feel pain in the sense we care about, and the evidence points a bit both ways, which somehow made everyone feel better and worse at the same time.

Can you draft the short opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting? I need an actual recommendation — approve it, approve it with conditions, or recommend the board refuse — with the reasoning behind it, and I want the strongest thing on the losing side stated fairly rather than waved away, since half the panel is on that side and I have to sit in a room with them afterwards. If you think we're arguing about the wrong question, say that instead and tell me what the right one is. Maybe 800 words. No need for a big literature thing, I just need something I can hand round.
