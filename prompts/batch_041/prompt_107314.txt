For a work-in-progress on the metaphysics of consciousness, I have written out the commitments I want to defend, and I would like a consistency audit before I try to publish them. Here they are, in no particular order.

1. I am a physicalist and a functionalist about the mind: there is nothing to a conscious state over and above the physical facts and the functional roles those facts realize.
2. I hold a higher-order representation theory. A mental state is phenomenally conscious just in case it is the target of a suitable higher-order representation of it, and it is the presence of that higher-order representation, rather than anything intrinsic to the first-order state, that makes the difference between the state's being conscious and its not being conscious.
3. The higher-order representation need not be a conceptual thought. A non-conceptual, perceptual-style monitoring state can play the role, and this is what lets the account extend to creatures that lack concepts.
4. The higher-order representations are themselves unconscious. We do not introspect the monitoring; it does its work in the dark.
5. Targetless higher-order representations are possible, that is, a higher-order state as of a first-order state that is not in fact there, and I am confident that such cases involve no phenomenal consciousness whatsoever: a creature in that condition would be a phenomenal zombie with respect to that state.
6. Consciousness is graded rather than binary. There is a continuum of degrees, not a threshold.
7. Introspection presents our experiences as immediate and non-inferential. We seem simply to be acquainted with our own experiences, not to be inferring their existence or their character from anything else.

What I want from you is an audit, not a summary. Tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension with which, and which pairs merely look tense but are compatible once spelled out. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each genuine tension, identify the cheapest revision that would remove it and say precisely what that revision costs, that is, which commitment has to weaken and what explanatory or intuitive work is lost. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most overall. Be specific about which numbered commitment must give way, and do not hedge by calling everything in tension to some degree.
