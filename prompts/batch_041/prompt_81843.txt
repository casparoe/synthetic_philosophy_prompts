I am part of a small reading group on formal epistemology, and we have been stuck on a puzzle that I suspect is more important than it looks. The setup is this: an agent is dropped into a region divided into two territories, Red and Blue, each of which is divided into a headquarters area and a second-company area. She knows that the Red territory is larger than the Blue territory, and she has some prior distribution over the four cells. She then receives a radio message from headquarters that, in effect, tells her that if she is in the headquarters area, then she is in the Red territory. She trusts the message. The question is how she should revise her credences. The naive answer is to conditionalize on the material conditional, which rules out the possibility that she is in the Blue headquarters area. But several of us think that is wrong, because the message is a conditional, not a material conditional, and it seems to tell her something about the conditional probability of Red given HQ rather than simply eliminating a cell. Worse, once we try to say exactly what constraint the message imposes, we find that there are many posteriors consistent with it, and no obvious way to pick one. Some of us have proposed minimizing relative entropy, but others object that this is arbitrary, and that it gives answers that seem too confident or too timid in different cases. We have also noticed that the problem gets worse when the message is only partially reliable, or when it is embedded in a larger chain of updates, or when the partition is described differently.

What I would like from you is not a solution to the puzzle but a specification: what must any adequate theory of updating on conditional information deliver? I want the desiderata list -- the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect, the results it must be consistent with -- with the consideration behind each requirement. For example, it seems to me that any such theory must at least: (i) reduce to ordinary conditionalization when the conditional is learned with certainty; (ii) explain why the Judy Benjamin case is not simply a case of conditionalizing on a material conditional; (iii) be well-defined and computable for finite partitions, without depending on an arbitrary choice of how the partition is carved up; (iv) respect some form of order-independence or else explain exactly where commutativity fails; (v) be dynamically coherent, in the sense of not exposing the agent to a sure loss; and (vi) say something about what happens when the conditional is uncertain or when the source is less than fully reliable. But I do not want to dictate the list. I want you to tell me which of these are genuine desiderata and which are prejudices of one camp, which subsets might be jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does the most to rule out the obvious candidates. If you think the Judy Benjamin case itself is a bad test case, say so and explain what a better test case would be. I am less interested in a scorecard of named theories than in the criteria themselves, so keep the weight on the requirements and the cases that motivate them. A table is fine if it helps.
