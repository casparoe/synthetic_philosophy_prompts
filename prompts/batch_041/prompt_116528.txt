I got one of those extortion emails last week, and the structure was oddly specific: the sender claimed he'd already flipped a coin, and depending on how it landed he'd leak what he has on everyone who doesn't pay, or on everyone who does pay, so you can't tell which way to jump. My partner wanted to just pay the $200 and be done; I said no and then couldn't explain why in a way that wasn't vibes. Apparently this is a known thing (I think people call it XOR blackmail), and it sits next to the Omega case where a predictor flips a coin, asks you for $100 if it's tails, and pays you $10,000 if it's heads on the condition that it predicted you'd have paid on tails.

I want a decision tree I can put in front of both of us, sorting positions by what they say about these two cases rather than by which theory name someone likes. Nodes should be questions we can actually answer without knowing the labels — does it matter whether you're asked before or after seeing the coin, is the question about your policy or about this one act, do you think there's any real sense in which your choice here is linked to how everyone else in the same spot chooses, does the whole thing presuppose a predictor that couldn't exist. Leaves should be named positions, one sentence each on the commitment that hurts most, and please flag any leaves the tree can't separate from each other, plus any answer-combination nobody occupies and why — incoherent, or just unexplored.
