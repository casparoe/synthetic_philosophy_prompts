I'm a philosophy professor and I need your help thinking through a real decision a friend of mine is stuck in — not a hypothetical, and please don't just hand me back the usual platitudes about peace versus justice.

My friend got elected mayor of a small town in the Midwest. Forty-odd years ago the town's biggest employer was a chemical plant, and for about a decade it dumped solvent into the ground under a working-class neighborhood. Kids got sick, a couple died, and the company knew. The executives who ran it are mostly dead now; the ones who aren't are in their late eighties and scattered. There's an internal report from the mid-80s that names names, but the town buried it, nobody was ever held responsible, and a lot of the families have never gotten a straight answer about who knew what and when.

So my friend's council is floating a scheme to finally crack it open. Any of the old guys who comes forward and gives a full account — what they did, why, and who above them signed off — gets a guarantee he won't be prosecuted and can't be sued by the town. And here's the wrinkle I can't get out of my head: the deal is only good for the first two people who talk. After that the door shuts, and the later you are, the worse it goes for you. The idea is to make them race each other to be first.

My friend thinks this is brilliant — it's the only lever he has, and he says it'll finally produce the truth. I think something's off. It feels like we'd be buying a pile of scared old men telling us whatever version keeps them safest, and maybe we'd be buying the truth at a price we shouldn't pay. But I can't say precisely what the objection is, or whether it survives contact with the fact that the alternative is that nobody ever talks and the families die not knowing.

I want you to act as a political philosopher who's thought seriously about how communities deal with their own worst chapters — forgiveness, memory, punishment, the whole tangle — and reason this through carefully instead of giving me a list of considerations. Start by brainstorming what's actually at stake here, then dig in. Specifically: is the race-to-confess design defensible, or does it corrupt the very thing it's supposed to produce? Does it matter that what's being offered and bought is truth rather than money or safety? And what should I tell my friend to do — push the scheme, drop it, or change it, and if change it, how? Dont bother with citations, I know the literature. Just think.
