I am putting together a position statement for my philosophy of mathematics seminar and would like it audited before I commit to it. Here is what I hold.

1. Numbers, sets and functions exist, and they exist independently of anyone's thinking about them. A statement such as 'there is a prime between 10 and 20' is true in virtue of how things stand with the numbers, not in virtue of what we can prove, how we use symbols, or what we are disposed to say.
2. Mathematical statements are to be read at face value. When a mathematician quantifies over all primes, the quantifier ranges over all primes. I will not accept a paraphrase on which such sentences are really about inscriptions, about what follows from axioms, or about linguistic practice.
3. We do know mathematical truths, and we know them by proving them. A proof is not merely a social credential licensing assertion; it is what puts the knower in contact with the fact proved.
4. Knowledge generally requires a real connection between the knower and the fact known: the fact must make an appropriate difference to the belief, and the connection must be of a kind that could in principle be described from the outside. Coherence, convenience and consensus do not amount to knowledge on their own.
5. I am a naturalist. I do not believe in faculties or relations that have no place in a scientific picture of the world, and I reject any special 'mathematical intuition' answerable to nothing else.
6. Mathematics is not a separate realm of inquiry with private rules of evidence. We should accept mathematical entities for the same reason we accept electrons: because our best overall account of things requires them and nothing else does the work as well.
7. There is no fact of the matter about which sets the natural numbers really are. Arithmetic is about a structure — its positions and the relations among them — and any two systems with the right structure are equally good candidates for being the numbers.

My own reading is that 6 is what rescues 1 and 2, and that once mathematics stops being treated as a special case the epistemology is not a problem. Tell me whether that is right.
