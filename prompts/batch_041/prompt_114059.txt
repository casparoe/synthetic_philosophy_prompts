I'm a third-year phil student and I'm in a seminar on rule-following and meaning that's basically been Kripkenstein for six weeks. I've ended up defending a position I havent seen stated quite this way, and I need to stress-test it before my seminar paper. The position: the skeptical solution is right that no fact about my past behavior, dispositions, or inner state determines that I meant plus rather than quus -- the quus predicate that agrees with plus on every sum I've actually computed but diverges on some new one. But the usual communal-disposition fix is too weak. What does the normative work, I think, is a kind of normative primitivism about practice: a move is correct because the community treats it as correct and holds each other to it, and the regress of justifications stops not because we hit a foundational fact but because we stop asking and start enforcing. So semantic normativity is constituted by the practice of holding-to-account, not by any fact that makes the practice correct. My friend says this is just quietism with extra steps, but I think it's a substantive claim about the source of meaning.

What I want from you is a map of what would count against this. Which arguments, cases, or formal results should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward? What would the view predict that its rivals don't, and are any of those predictions actually testable or at least independently checkable? What would a committed defender concede as counting against them, not just what critics say? And does the view risk being unfalsifiable -- and if so, is that a problem for a constitutive claim about meaning, or is it just what you'd expect? The objection that worries me most is error: if correctness is just what the community enforces, what makes a whole community systematically wrong about a rule, and can the view explain the difference between a radical reinterpretation and a mistake? I'd also like to know whether the position collapses into some rival view -- e.g. Platonism about rules, or a dispositionalist account with social window dressing -- and what would have to be true for it to avoid that. Be honest if you think it's well insulated and that this is a problem.
