I work on robot learning, mostly RL and imitation learning for manipulation, so the vocabulary I actually think in is policies and value functions, world models vs. model-free, options and skill libraries, behavior trees, sim-to-real, all that stuff. I've been reading philosophy on the side and I've gotten stuck on the knowing-how vs. knowing-that thing. As I understand it, Ryle's anti-intellectualist line is that knowing how to do something is not a matter of knowing any facts, and he runs a regress on the idea that it is (if doing the thing required first knowing some proposition, then applying that proposition would itself be a bit of know-how, and so on). Then the intellectualists -- Stanley and Williamson and people in that vein -- say no, knowing how is a species of propositional knowledge, roughly that knowing how to do something is knowing that some particular way of doing it is a way you could do it. I can follow both sides as prose but I have no idea how the disagreement looks when you put it in my terms, and I suspect half of it evaporates and half of it is something my terms genuinely can't see.

So: restate the whole debate as faithfully as you can using my vocabulary -- policies, value functions, world models, planners, skill libraries, procedural vs. declarative memory in a robot stack, whatever maps. For each side say what carries over cleanly, what you had to distort to make it fit, and what has no expression at all in my framework. I'm especially curious about the regress, because in my world a policy is just a primitive object -- a function -- and I never worry about what applies it, so I can't tell whether the regress is a real problem that my framework papers over or a confusion about levels of description that my framework rightly ignores. Same question for the "knowledge" part: I have no notion of warrant or truth for a policy, only success rate and generalization, so tell me whether the epistemologists' requirement that knowledge be more than reliable success has any counterpart on my side, or whether I'm just missing the point of the question. Don't be diplomatic about which framework is impoverished -- if my vocabulary is the one losing information, say so, and if the philosophical dispute turns out to be thinner than it reads, say that too.
