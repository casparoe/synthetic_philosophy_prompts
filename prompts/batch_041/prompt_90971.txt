Heres the case I want you to deal with. My friend Mara fences. Her coach taught her a rule about her wrist and her grip -- the coach stated it out loud, she memorized it, and she used it to fix her form. Years later a biomechanist tells her the rule is simply false: the wrist angle the rule prescribes is not what her hand actually does when she's fencing well, and never was. The coach's verbal rule was a fiction that happened to stop novices from over-gripping. Mara still fences well. She says she now just does it. And here's the part I care about: when she tried to fence by consciously applying the corrected, true biomechanical rule, her performance fell apart.

So her behavior was never governed by the proposition she could state, and is actively made worse by the true one. Where's the knowing-that? Also I want the regress: whatever proposition you say guides the movement, something has to apply it, and if applying it is itself a bit of know-how, the thing you were trying to explain has come back in through the window.

Format it like this. First the in-character reply, a paragraph or two.
