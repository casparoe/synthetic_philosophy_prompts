I'm a third-year PhD in political philosophy and I'm stuck in a reading-group fight that refuses to move. It's the leveling-down thing. Prioritarians say the egalitarian is committed to thinking it can be in some respect better if the worse-off are worse off — no wait, better if the better-off are dragged down to the level of the worse-off, even when literally no one gains from it. Egalitarians say that's a gotcha aimed at a strawman: nobody thinks it's impersonally better for the top to drop while the bottom sits untouched, and equality is about the relations between people, about what claims they can make on each other, not about how much good the world contains. We've gone round this maybe six times. Everyone knows every move, everyone has a comeback, nobody budges an inch.

My hunch — test it, don't just nod — is that it isn't really a fight about leveling down at all. I think the two sides are answering different questions under one word. The prioritarian is doing axiology, ranking outcomes by how much good is in them, weighted so a gain to someone worse off counts for more. The egalitarian is doing something more like a theory of what we owe each other, of which comparisons between persons generate claims in the first place. If that's right then "is leveling down bad?" gets a yes from one and a "wrong question" from the other, and the impasse is structural, not a sign that someone's being obtuse.

But I don't trust that diagnosis, because there are at least three others floating around: maybe it's verbal (they mean different things by 'value' or by 'equality'), maybe it's a real disagreement about whether there are impersonal values at all, or maybe it's bedrock — a clash of intuitions about whether a world with less total good but more equality can ever be better, which neither side can argue for. So diagnose it properly. Tell me which of these it actually is, using what each side really says rather than the bumper stickers, and flag any hidden empirical or formal question that's secretly doing the work.

Then the part I actually care about: given your diagnosis, what would count as progress? A distinction, a translation scheme between the two vocabularies, a case one side has to accept but can't describe, a formal result showing the two are extensionally equivalent but conceptually distinct? Or should I just conclude nothing will move it and stop wasting my Thursdays?

And one more thing I keep chewing on. Suppose someone could show that every prioritarian ranking can be reproduced by some egalitarian-style structure of claims and vice versa — same verdicts, different reasons. Would that make the dispute merely verbal, or does it just relocate the disagreement to what the reasons are for? Engage with that seriously, don't just tell me it depends. Also — is there a case where the leveling down happens somewhere the worse-off never find out about, and does that change which diagnosis is right?
