I've read rationalist blogs and forums for years, so my working vocabulary for institutions is the standard toolkit: principal-agent problems, mechanism design, information asymmetry, selection effects, public choice, Goodhart's law, legibility, Chesterton's fence, expected value over long horizons. I'm not an economist, but I'm fluent enough to think with that vocabulary. I want you to translate Mill's position on British rule in India into it as faithfully as you can. Mill spent his working life inside the East India Company, rising to be the official who drafted the Company's despatches to India; he defended Company rule as a form of double government insulated from both parliamentary ignorance and settler interests; he argued against transferring India to the Crown in the late 1850s; and he held that a free state may legitimately govern a dependency despotically, provided the despotism is exercised for the benefit of the subject people and as a temporary preparation for self-government. Restate that as a design problem: who are the principals and agents, what are the incentives, what monitoring and exit options exist, what commitment devices does Mill want, what failure modes does he fear, and where does the structure depend on facts about India rather than on general institutional reasoning? Then give me the residue report. Which parts translate cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary — I expect the idea that a people can be in a condition of political immaturity, such that withholding self-government is owed to them rather than taken from them, to be the hard case, but I want you to say whether the residue is a defect of the target vocabulary or a sign that Mill's argument was smuggling in something it never made explicit. Be committed: tell me which reading of Mill you think is right, and whether the translation makes his position look better or worse than the standard verdict that he was simply a liberal imperialist. Don't lay out the options and leave the choice to me.
