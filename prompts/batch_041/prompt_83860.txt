Alright, I need someone to tell me what I actually believe, because I keep going in circles on this. I'm pretty sure minds aren't made of meat, but my gut verdicts dont add up, and I want the principle behind them if there is one.

My verdicts:

Take me and replace one neuron at a time with a silicon chip that does exactly what that neuron did -- same connections, same timing, same functional profile. At the end it's all chips in there. Still me, still feels the toothache. No doubt about this one.

Now build that silicon system from scratch: same wiring diagram, same inputs and outputs, but it never was biological. Hit it and it's in pain. Carbon isn't magic.

Octopuses, crows, some alien with a nervous system built out of who-knows-what: if it acts like it's hurting, it's hurting. Whatever pain is, it's not one physical kind of stuff.

Flip side: a system that gets all the same outputs by brute lookup -- a giant table, every possible input matched to what a human would do, nothing inside doing any work that resembles the causal chain in my head. No feelings, no beliefs. It's a very fancy filing cabinet and I'll die on this hill.

A block of ice whose molecules, completly by accident, happen to be arranged so that for ten seconds its physical evolution mirrors the causal structure of my brain, and then it melts, and none of it was ever hooked up to any behavior anyway. I say no. Thats a coincidence, not a mind. If your principle says the ice is briefly in agony then your principle is broken.

A brain in a vat, same internal causal goings-on as mine, all its outputs cut. Still in pain, still thinking. Whats going on inside is what matters, not what it can do.

The one that wrecks me: take my exact functional organization and implement it with a few thousand people in a stadium, each with a rule card, passing notes so the whole crowd reproduces me -- inputs in, outputs out. I want to say no. Nobody in there is having the experience, the crowd isn't a person, and if you told me the stadium was in agony I'd think you were joking. But I can't say what the crowd is missing that the silicon in case two isn't.

So: am I just a functionalist? Is there a version that keeps all six of the verdicts I'm sure about and still lets me say no to the stadium? If the honest answer is that the stadium is in and I have to eat it, or that the from-scratch silicon and the stadium stand or fall together, then say that and tell me which verdict to give up. And I want to know what each clause of your principle is actually buying me -- is "non-accidental" a real constraint or a patch over the ice, is "the parts mustn't themselves be minds" a principled requirement or just chauvinism about the medium, and does the timescale matter (what if the stadium takes an hour per thought -- is it in pain right now, just very slowly)?

Also tell me what your principle says about a case I haven't given you: my same organization, but each "neuron" is itself a small conscious animal. Does the whole have a mind over and above the parts, or is that just the stadium again? And what about two of those systems wired together, or one whose organization is split across two bodies with a delay between them?
