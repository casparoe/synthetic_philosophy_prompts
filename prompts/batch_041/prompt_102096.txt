My village has read out the names of the people taken from our train station during the war every year on the same date for fifty years, and this year is the last one — the council has voted to stop and put the money into a housing fund for refugees instead, and honestly I half agree with them. I've been asked to give the closing remarks at the final reading. The room will be old people who were there and can still hear the names, their grandkids who mostly can't, and two councillors who think the whole thing has turned morbid. I want to say something true about what we're ending and what we owe, without pretending the dead are watching us and without pretending we can just set them down like a debt we've paid off. Somewhere in it I want to use our own rule — that we read a name only for as long as someone who actually knew that person is still alive — and I want to be honest that the rule and the council's vote might both be right. Six minutes, please, and dont turn it into a lecture.
