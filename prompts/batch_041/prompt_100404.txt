Please write a joke whose punchline turns on the Knobe effect — the asymmetry whereby a foreseen side effect of an action is judged intended when it is harmful but not when it is beneficial. It should be funny to someone who knows that literature, so the humour must turn on the substance of the asymmetry rather than on a generic quip about philosophers. To keep it from being a mere restatement of the chairman-and-the-environment vignette, build it around a single decision that foreseeably brings about both a benefit and a harm, with the attribution of intent genuinely contested — a joint action by two agents, or a corporate or algorithmic decision-maker, would serve well if you can make the intent truly ambiguous.
