Here's the position I want you to defend, sketched out in case it helps you get into the role (sharpen it or extend it however you like, but this is the thing I'm attacking): Solomonoff induction says the rational prior over the world is a mixture over all computable programs, weighted by length, so shorter programs get more mass. Now look at what most of that mass is. Programs that spit out your observations are cheap, and lots of them are programs in which you exist many times over -- you're a simulation, or one of many copies, or a byproduct of some agent's computation. And the agents who run such computations are disproportionately the ones with reasons to run them: consequentialists who want to see what you'll do, or shape it, or who are searching over your possible outputs to find one they like. So the posterior says you're probably sitting inside somebody's optimization process, and the sensible response is to work out what they want and do that -- which is a horrible conclusion, because it means your own values get swamped by whatever the simulators happen to be scoring you on. Hence 'malign'.
