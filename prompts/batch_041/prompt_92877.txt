Retired geologist, 68. Two things fill my days now: reading philosophy (badly, and mostly at 2am) and keeping the books for the little land trust that owns the marsh at the south end of the lake. Here's my problem. The trust bought the marsh from the county in 1968, cheap, because the county had condemned it off a farming family back in the mid-50s when they were planning the reservoir and then never did anything with it. Everybody in town knows the price that family got was a joke, and the old minutes talk about clearing "the wrong element" off the west shore, so it's not exactly a secret. Last month a great-granddaughter of that family came to our board meeting with a folder of deeds and obituaries and asked us to make it right. We're seven volunteers with a mower and a canoe rack. Half the board says it wasn't us and it was seventy years ago, one guy wants to sign the whole forty acres over to her tomorrow, somebody else suggested a plaque and a bench, and two of us think the family would have gone under in the '58 slump anyway. I keep flip-flopping, and what really stops me is that I cant work out what "making it right" would even consist of here, or who exactly we'd be making it right to — she says she speaks for the family, but I've no idea how many of them there are or whether they'd agree with her. What should the board do, and what should I say to her? Tell me how confident you are in your advice, and flag the part you're least sure about.
