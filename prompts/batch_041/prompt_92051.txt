I have been building an interactive story in which each character has a set of beliefs, and I represent those beliefs by letting a character believe everything that follows from what they start out believing. Two things go wrong. First, every character ends up knowing every logical truth, since those follow from anything at all. Second, any character who holds two beliefs that cannot both be true ends up believing absolutely everything, and the whole model collapses. People tell me this is just a quirk of how I coded it, but I suspect it runs deeper: the same oddities seem to appear for very weak logics, not only classical ones, and they reappear if you drop the program and think about a person, as soon as you grant that what someone is committed to includes what follows from what they believe.

Is there an established name for this assumption and the trouble it brings? I am told it is a matter of closure, but that seems to name the rule I imposed rather than the problem, and it does not seem to be the same as saying a person ought to be consistent. I want the idea pinned down rather than labelled: what the assumption is, why it is tempting, what exactly breaks, and how it differs from neighbouring notions such as bounded rationality, logical consistency as a norm, and the manoeuvre of saying an agent implicitly believes something it has never entertained. If the field distinguishes several related things here, give me the distinctions rather than a single word.

Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
