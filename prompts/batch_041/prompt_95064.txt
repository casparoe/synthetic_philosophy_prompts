I've been circling a problem that I can only name, not yet discipline: the idea of a free-floating rationale — the suggestion that a piece of behavior or a biological trait can be governed by a rationale that nothing and no one represents, so that there is a reason for the arrangement without there being any reasoner who has it. The cases that draw me in are the ordinary ones. A shoot grows toward the light; a bacterium swims up a sugar gradient; a cuckoo chick heaves its foster siblings out of the nest; the eye exhibits what looks like design. In each case we seem compelled to describe what the thing is doing in the idiom of purpose, and the description seems to explain the behavior and even to license the word mistake when it goes wrong (the shoot bends toward a light that has been switched off, the bacterium overshoots), yet nothing in the system appears to represent the goal it is serving. Before I read any further proposals about how to make sense of this, I want the specification. What would a theory of free-floating rationales actually have to deliver in order to count as adequate?

Give me the desiderata list: the cases any such theory must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the puzzles it has to dissolve or explain, and the results from biology, cognitive science, or semantics it must not contradict — with the specific consideration behind each requirement. I care especially about the fault lines I suspect are doing the real work: the difference between there being a reason and something having a reason; the difference between a rationale that explains a behavior and one that merely redescribes it; the difference between the rationale of a selected trait and the rationale of something that was designed or learned; and the worry that free-floating talk is either ordinary function-talk in disguise or else the intentional stance smuggled back in through a side door. Mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp, and flag any subset you think cannot be jointly satisfied — if some demand for realism or for genuine normativity is in tension with the demand that the whole story be tellable without a representing mind, that tension is the thing I most want drawn out. Then tell me which single requirement does the most work at excluding candidate theories, and, briefly, how the main families of answer — crude evolutionary function-talk, selection-based or etiological accounts of content, and deflationary or stance-dependent readings — fare against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them rather than on the scorecard.

Finally, separate what is actually settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and do not hand me a contested position dressed up as the consensus.
