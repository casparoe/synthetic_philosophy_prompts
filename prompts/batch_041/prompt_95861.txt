ok so I've been calling myself a Rortyan for a couple years now and before I keep doing it in seminars I want to know if the whole thing actually hangs together. Here's where I've landed:

1) antirepresentationalism — there's no way the world is that our sentences are supposed to copy. Truth isn't correspondence, it's whatever our communal practices license us to assert. No vocabulary is closer to reality than any other; they're just more or less useful for different jobs.

2) I'm an ironist about my own final vocabulary: I know the words I use for what matters most to me are contingent, and I can't justify them non-circularly from outside them.

3) inquiry aims at solidarity, not objectivity — the point isn't to mirror nature, it's to widen the circle of who counts as us.

4) but I'm also dead serious about liberalism. Cruelty is the worst thing we do, and I think liberal democratic institutions are genuinely better than the alternatives, not just different from them.

5) and I do think science has a real grip. I'd bet everything on atomic theory over phlogiston, and I dont think that's just one vocabulary happening to win — something is making physics right and astrology wrong.

6) I'm happy to call myself ethnocentric: I start from the standards of my own community and I dont pretend to a view from nowhere.

So which of these are actually pulling against each other, and how bad is it? Rank the tensions by severity instead of telling me they're all equally in trouble. For each real one, whats the cheapest fix and what do I lose by taking it. And I've got a feeling at least one of these is fine and I'm just spooking myself, so tell me which pairs only look inconsistent. Be specific about which commitment has to go.
