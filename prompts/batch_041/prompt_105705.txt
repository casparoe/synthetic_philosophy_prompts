I'm a historian (early modern, mostly) and I have to write a short where-I-stand piece for a theory reading group next week, and before I commit it to paper I want a sanity check on whether my positions actually hang togther. You're a philosopher of history who also works on blame and moral responsibility, so please take your time with this. Start by putting each of my commitments in your own words and saying what work it's doing for me, because I've never had to write them down all at once and I'm honestly not sure I know.

Here's the set:

1. Explaining an action means getting at the agent's own reasons, and at the standards that were actually available to them at the time. If you're describing what someone did in terms they couldn't have used, you've stopped doing history. I'm pretty hardline about this and it has cost me friends in seminars.

2. But I don't believe in a view from nowhere. Every history is written from somewhere, for someone, in some vocabulary, and the idea of describing the past as it really was, our own categories stripped out, is a fantasy. So historians can't get outside the standards of their own time. Including this standard, presumably.

3. I'm a moral realist. Slavery wasn't wrong by our lights, it was wrong, in 1650 as much as now. Moral facts aren't indexed to a period.

4. And progress is real. Not just change, and not just us preferring ourselves. Some periods really do get more things right than others do.

5. Here's a distinction I've been working with, tell me if it holds up: actual standards vs available standards. Actual = the norms the agent really held. Available = the norms they could have reached from where they stood, given what was knowable then. My line is that we're entitled to hold people to the second and not the first.

6. We owe the dead the same interpretive charity we owe the living: reconstruct the most coherent and most defensible version of what someone was up to before you go after them.

7. I want history to be usable. If it can't speak to fights people are having now, I'm not sure what it's for. That's why I got into this.

What I need from you: not a reassurance that these are all compatible in principle with enough distinctions. Tell me which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict and dissolve once you see what each claim is about, and rank the real tensions by how bad they are. For each real one, say what has to give, what the cheapest repair is, and what I'd be giving up by making it. Then tell me the single revision that would clean up the most, and whether there's anything in the set I should just drop rather than patch. Also flag anywhere I'm quietly using one word for two different jobs, because I have a suspicion I'm doing that but no idea where.
