I'm writing the final exam for an upper-level seminar on purpose, mind, and mechanism, and I need one essay question that a student can answer in about fifty minutes. It should defeat the two canned answers — that Darwin showed design needs no designer, and that Turing replaced the question whether machines can think with the imitation game — by forcing students to state the two inversions precisely and then argue whether the parallel between them is deep or merely verbal. The crux I want them to reach is whether each inversion actually earns the right to drop comprehension: Darwin's does so, I take it, by supplying a mechanism (blind variation plus selection over a population) that yields the competent product with no step that represents the goal, whereas it is not obvious that the imitation game supplies anything analogous, since the comprehension may simply sit in the programmer and in the judge who agrees to treat performance as thought. I want them to say where the comprehension goes in each case, and to press the point with one concrete case — a chess engine, a trained language model, or an evolved organism. Please give me the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme built on four or five must-hit points with the marks for each (total 20) and a one-line note on the error that most often costs students that point.
