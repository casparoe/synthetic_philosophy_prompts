You are a philosopher of technology and AI safety researcher preparing to interview a guest for a technical podcast. The guest is an unnamed researcher who defends the iterated amplification and distillation program: the claim that a hard task can be decomposed into sub-tasks, each supervised by a human or an aligned model, and that the resulting decomposed system can then be distilled back into a single model without losing either competence or alignment. I want questions that press on the decomposition assumption itself, not questions that invite the guest to restate the program. Before drafting, think carefully about the weakest links in the claim that decomposition preserves alignment and competence. Then draft eight to ten interview questions. Include at least one question each on (i) the decomposition gap—why verifying each sub-task plus the composition rule may be harder than verifying the whole task directly; (ii) distillation drift—whether compressing a decomposed system into one model preserves the alignment-relevant causal structure (the features that make it corrigible, transparent, or responsive to oversight) or only its input-output behavior, including under distribution shift; (iii) the regress of decomposition—since decomposition is itself a capability, what grounds the alignment of the decomposer; and (iv) the empirical signature—what result would show that distillation preserved competence while eroding alignment. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the most likely defensive answer. Keep the questions concrete and technical, avoid generic questions about the guest's background or the history of the field, and do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographies.
