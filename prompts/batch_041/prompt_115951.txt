I am putting together a single episode of a small podcast on the history of psychoanalysis, and I would like your help drafting the interview questions. My guest is a scholar who mounts a determined defense of Freud's critique of religion, and I want the questions to test that defense rather than allow her simply to restate it.

Her position is this. The standing complaint against Freud is that he commits the genetic fallacy: he explains religion by its origin in wish and then treats it as discredited, as though an origin in desire could settle a question of truth. She holds that this misreads him. On her account, what Freud means by an illusion is a functional rather than an epistemic category — a belief counts as an illusion when a wish-fulfillment figures prominently among its motives, not because it is false (he is explicit that illusions need not be false) — and the verdict he actually passes on religion is normative, a claim that we ought to outgrow it in the name of maturity, rather than an inference from etiology to falsity. She concludes that the critique survives the genetic-fallacy objection.

Draft eight to ten questions, ordered so that the pressure builds, and supply for each a follow-up of one or two sentences that anticipates the answer she is most likely to give. The sequence must include at least one question on each of the following, and you may add others:

- the distinction between an illusion and an error or a delusion, and whether her reading leaves the critique with any force — if a wish-derived belief may nonetheless be true, what exactly is supposed to be wrong with holding it?
- the oceanic feeling that Freud takes up in Civilization and Its Discontents, and whether a feeling of limitlessness can be an illusion in the relevant sense at all — if religion is rooted in feeling rather than in belief, does the illusion framework fail to engage it?
- the analogy between religious ritual and obsessional neurosis: whether Freud intends it as a structural comparison or as a claim about a shared etiology, and what follows for her functional reading on either construal;
- Freud's later suggestion, in Moses and Monotheism, that religious doctrines preserve a distorted memory of an actual historical event — ask whether conceding this kind of truth to religion undercuts the illusion verdict;
- the dependence of the whole genealogy on an inherited, transmissible memory of a primordial murder — ask what remains of the critique once that Lamarckian premise is abandoned, as it must be.

I would also like one question that stages a small thought experiment — a belief that is wholly wish-derived and yet happens to be true — and uses it to force her to say what, on her account, is still defective about the belief, if anything.

Keep every question concrete and free of throat-clearing. No questions about how she came to the subject, and no invitation to summarize Freud's biography.
