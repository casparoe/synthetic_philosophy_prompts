My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about Lakatos's research programmes and I genuinely can't tell whether we were disagreeing or just talking past eachother. Her line: progressive/degenerating can't be applied in real time — you only find out a programme was degenerating after its dead, and until then the defender just keeps patching the protective belt, so 'stick to progressive programmes' is either unfalsifiable or a story you tell about history afterwards. My line: that's a misreading; it was never a decision procedure for the bench, it's a standard for reconstructing which episodes were rational, and the permissiveness is deliberate.

What actually stumped us is this, and it's what I want you to chew on rather than adjudicate. 'Progressive' seems to be doing two jobs at once — descriptively, the programme did keep throwing up novel predictions that panned out; normatively, it's the rational thing to keep investing in. Those come apart: a programme can churn out confirmed novel predictions in some backwater nobody cares about and still be a bad bet, and a dead-looking degenerating one can be the only game in town. And 'novel' is a mess of its own — novel compared to the rival programme, or novel compared to the assumptions that went into building the theory?

So is this stall a verbal fight over a word, a disagreement about what a methodology of science is even for (organising the historical record vs. advising working scientists vs. handing out grades of rationality after the fact), or a hidden question nobody's settled, historical or conceptual? Don't give me a tour of Lakatos — give me a diagnosis with reasons, and tell me honestly what, if anything, would move it: a distinction, a case, a formal result, or nothing, because the question is ill-posed.
