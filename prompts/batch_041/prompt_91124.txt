My brother-in-law keeps pestering me to put money into a startup and I'd like to give him a real answer instead of just a bad feeling. Here's the pitch as I understand it. It's a box you put in your basement. Inside is a membrane full of tiny gates, and each gate is set up so it lets fast-moving air molecules through in one direction and blocks the slow ones, or maybe the other way round, I forget. Anyway the upshot is that one side of the box ends up hot and the other side cold, and you sandwich a thermoelectric pad between the two sides and get electricity out. Room-temperature air in, power out, no fuel, no plug, nothing.

He says the founders are serious people and that they've already dealt with the one objection everybody raises, which is that a gate has to somehow know which molecule is which, and knowing isn't free. Their answer is that they never erase. The gates are built out of reversible logic, so nothing ever gets thrown away — the record of what each gate did just stays in the material as little structural defects. So the usual accounting, where you have to pay for forgetting what you measured, simply doesn't apply to their machine. He thinks that's the whole trick and the reason nobody has done it before.

I'm not a physicist and I don't want to be the guy who says second law, dummy, if there's definately a subtlety here. Is he being taken for a ride? And if the erasure thing really is a red herring, then where is the catch supposed to be?
