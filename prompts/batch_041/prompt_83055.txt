After a long argument on Saturday night with a friend who sizes every position in his portfolio by the Kelly criterion, I have agreed to present my case against geometric-mean maximization at a seminar next month, and I want a Q&A prep sheet. The thesis I will defend is that the growth-optimality result establishes only a fact about the limiting behaviour of a single wealth trajectory under a fixed, indefinitely repeated, never-rescaled betting problem, and that the further claim — that maximizing the geometric mean of wealth is what rationality requires — rests on an equivocation between the ensemble average and the time average that no decision-theoretic axiom licenses; the assurance that Kelly betting cannot ruin you is, I will argue, an artifact of the infinite horizon and of treating wealth as the only quantity that matters. The room will be about a dozen people: six or seven quants and information theorists who size by Kelly daily and regard the criterion as settled, two economists who work on subjective expected utility and will attack me from the axioms, and one Bayesian statistician who will want to know what I propose in its place. Give me the objections I will actually get, sorted by who raises them and phrased in that group's vocabulary, ordered by how much damage each would do; a two-minute reply to each, with a candid note on where the reply is weak; what I should concede at the outset so that the quants do not write me off; and the question I am most afraid of.
