For the last three years I have taught a cross-listed graduate seminar in the philosophy of mind whose enrollment is almost entirely computer scientists and systems engineers, and in preparing my lectures I have grown suspicious that my own off-the-cuff gloss on the contrast between the Cartesian theater and the multiple drafts picture has been quietly doing work I never licensed — importing, for instance, a tidy "commit point" that I would never tolerate if a student asserted it about the brain. I would like to audit the translation rather than my students, and I am asking you to do it. Assume I am fluent in the target vocabulary: I completed a master's in distributed systems before turning to philosophy, so do not soften the formalism on my account. Take the Cartesian theater's central commitments, and take the multiple drafts alternative as it is actually developed — the denial that there is any privileged place and time at which content becomes conscious, the "finish line" picture of Cartesian materialism, the phi phenomenon and its color variant (where the reported color of the moving patch depends on the second flash), the Orwellian/Stalinesque fork in explaining such cases, the treatment of "writing it down" in memory as criterial, and the heterophenomenological method of treating subjects' reports as the admissible data — and restate all of it, as faithfully as you can, in the idiom of distributed systems: logical versus physical clocks, causal ordering and vector clocks, commit points and write-ahead logs, linearizability versus eventual consistency, single points of serialization, replication and divergence, and observability through probes and logs. I care about the mapping and not the metaphor; wherever you catch yourself writing "it's like" rather than "this corresponds to," flag it as a place where the translation may have slipped.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually want. Which of the claims carry over without loss? I expect the "no privileged global now" claim to go through cleanly, and I will be disappointed if the heterophenomenological method finds no natural analogue in treating the message log as the only admissible evidence about system state. Which carry over only under distortion — for instance, whether the indeterminacy of content (the same traces consistent with many stories about what the system "registered") is the same thesis as underdetermination of a system's state by its traces, or a different thesis wearing the same clothes? And which have no counterpart at all? Here I am thinking of anything that depends on there being something it is like, on the normativity of a subject's "seeming," or on the insistence that the reports are not merely evidence for a hidden show but the whole of the matter. For each residue item, say plainly whether the failure is a defect of the systems vocabulary — it cannot express something real — or a diagnosis of the original, which was less clear than it looked. Do not tell me what Dennett or his critics are generally thought to hold; I want the translation audited, not summarized. Keep the whole reply to at most 900 words, presenting the mapping as a short bulleted list and the residue report as continuous prose.
