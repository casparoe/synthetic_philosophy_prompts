I teach a seminar on mental causation and levels of description, and I want to build a session around Pixar's Inside Out read through Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment. I need a critical reading of the film that is faithful to both, and that will not mislead undergraduates.

The careenium, as Hofstadter sets it up, is a vast arena whose walls vary in bounciness according to what happens outside, and which is filled with countless tiny magnetised balls that careen about, collide, clump together and come apart. At one level of description, everything that happens in it is balls pushing balls. But the clumps themselves, the higher-level structures Hofstadter calls simmballs, standing in for symbols much as the individual balls stand in for neurons, have a stable and describable life of their own: they form, persist, recognise and answer one another. Hofstadter asks who pushes whom around inside the careenium, and his answer is that the simmball level is not decorative overlay on the ball-level facts but a genuine level of causal description. Plenty of philosophers of mind think that answer is a fudge, and that the balls are doing all the pushing while the symbols ride along.

Inside Out is a film with exactly two such levels, and it never shows us the lower one. What I want from you is not a précis of the plot but an argument about what the film commits itself to. Does it vindicate Hofstadter, or does it smuggle in a homunculus and so beg the question? Is the film's picture of the mind a genuine two-level causal story, or a picture in which the symbol level is just another arena full of little agents, each of whom would need a careenium of their own inside the head? Where does the film resist the reading, and where does it illuminate something the abstract argument cannot? Point me to particular scenes: the console and the idea that Anger plants in it, Sadness recolouring a happy memory and then consoling Bing Bong while Joy's cheerfulness fails, the climax in which Sadness takes the console, and the enlarged console of the closing shot. Say plainly where a reading like this would distort the film, and where the film, taken seriously, would force a correction to the philosophical picture rather than the other way round. End by saying what the film contributes to the debate about whether symbols push the substrate around that argument alone does not. You may bring in the second film's account of beliefs being built out of memories if it sharpens the point, but the first film should carry the reading.

Do not quote dialogue or narration verbatim; describe the scenes instead. Write in British English, as a continuous essay of roughly 1,200 words. Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
