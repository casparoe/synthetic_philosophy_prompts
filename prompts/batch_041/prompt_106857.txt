I am a retired experimental physicist who has come to philosophy late, and I want a rigorous argument for a position I have not seen stated in quite the form I have in mind. Please argue that maximal moral goodness is not desirable, not mainly because it is too demanding or because it makes the saint unhappy, but because it requires a standing disposition to audit every activity for its moral opportunity cost, and that disposition is incompatible with the absorbed, non-instrumental devotion to a problem that gives a life in science its distinctive excellence. Make the case through the example of a researcher wholly taken up by a theoretical puzzle, distinguish what I will call the audit model of moral attention from the craft model of absorption, and confront the objection that this is simply a self-serving brief for the life of the mind. Be precise about what the saintly life loses and why the good it does does not compensate for that loss.
