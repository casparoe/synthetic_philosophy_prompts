A friend and I spent most of Saturday arguing about other minds, and I would rather convert the argument into a proposal than have it again.

Her position is the standard evidential one: I have direct access to my own case and only behavioural evidence for anyone else's, so ascribing a mind to another is an inference, and a shaky one. My objection was that her picture treats evidence as a single quantity that gets better or worse, and that it has nothing to say about the case I actually care about — a system whose internals I can read exhaustively, something I built, or a model whose every parameter and activation I can inspect. If the problem is evidential, total mechanical transparency ought to make it disappear. I do not think it does, and I suspect the reason it does not tells us what kind of problem it was all along. That is the half-formed idea, and I need it turned into a two-page proposal for a departmental work-in-progress fellowship: one semester, two readers, no budget.

I want the proposal to state a question with a determinate answer rather than a topic. My candidate: does the warrant for ascribing mental states to another subject vary with information about its mechanism, and does it survive the limiting case of complete transparency? The proposal should explain why the existing families of answer — analogical inference, inference to the best explanation, criteria-based and grammatical accounts, and the deflationary ones — leave this open, ideally by showing that each is committed to a prediction about what transparency does to the warrant. The method should be the part a reader can hold me to: a taxonomy of kinds of access (behavioural, interventional, mechanistic, first-personal), a set of limiting cases chosen specifically to pull the accounts apart, and an argument about which of them, if any, is falsified by those cases. Give me a month-by-month plan and an honest assessment of what would count as the project failing — my own guess is that the failure mode is every account predicting the same thing at full transparency, in which case the test has no diagnostic power and I need to know that before I spend a semester on it. Say too why this approach rather than the obvious alternatives, such as another round of intuition-pumping about zombies or a straight survey of positions.

Do not give me a bibliography; I will add citations myself, and I would rather the proposal refer to positions and arguments than to names and dates.
