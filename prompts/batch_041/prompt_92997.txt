I am an undergraduate enrolled in a decision theory seminar, and we have spent the past two weeks on acausal trade. I believe I grasp the central move: two agents who cannot communicate and cannot causally influence one another may nonetheless each act so as to benefit the other, because the reasoning that determines what each will do is correlated with the reasoning of the other in a way that makes cooperation mutually advantageous. What I cannot get past is the problem of assurance, and I would be grateful if you could help me locate my mistake, if there is one.

Suppose I am the party deciding whether to carry out my side of such a trade—say, by running a computation that confers a benefit on a counterpart I will never encounter. Before I commit, I would want to be satisfied that the counterpart will carry out their side. But by the very construction of the case there is no channel between us: I cannot observe them, they cannot signal me, and no third party can enforce anything. So how is this assurance ever obtained? Is there some procedure by which acausal traders verify a counterpart's reliability before acting, or is one simply required to take it on faith that the counterpart will reciprocate? As matters stand, the situation looks to me as though I am the only party who can actually act, so that trading collapses into a unilateral decision to be generous toward a stranger who may not even exist—and I do not see why that would be rational.

A classmate offered a concrete case that I think brings out my worry. I write a program that, when executed, transfers some resource to whoever happens to be running the same program elsewhere in the universe; I then reason that another instance of the program may well be running too. My classmate insists that the two instances are acausally trading. I reply that we are merely both being generous, since neither instance can be certain the other exists. Who has the better of the argument, and does the notion of assurance enter into the correct answer at all? More generally, I would like to understand what the trade in acausal trade actually consists in, if it is not a reciprocal exchange that each side must be assured the other will honor.
