ok so I'm the one who runs my school's philosophy club and at the end of term we do a quiz night for the yr 12s. this term Mr A (the teacher who actually keeps the club going) asked me to write the serious bit: one proper essay question with a model answer and a marking scheme, so people can use it as practice for the sort of thing they'll get at uni. so it needs to be actually good and not just the standard thing.

what I want it on is meritocracy, but NOT the boring version where the answer is "meritocracy is when the best people get the rewards, and also maybe thats unfair because of luck and the family you're born into." every model answer does that and you can get full marks without understanding anything. the thing I actually want it to force is the gap between (a) being ranked as the most meritorious, (b) deserving a reward because of it, and (c) just being entitled to something because a rule says so. I dont think most people can even tell those three apart.

the case to hang it on is basically our school. we give out two prizes, top marks and most improved. the new head wants to scrap both and give one big merit award to whoever the new points system puts at the top. in assembly she said something like: we're not saying the winner deserves it, we're saying the system works, it gets everyone to try harder. and then a girl in my year said that just dodges it, because if the winner is the kid with the private tutor and the fast brain, then either the prize is unfair or it doesn't mean anything at all.

can you write me (1) the essay question, 40 mins, 25 marks, and it has to make them use that case and that three way distinction rather than describe meritocracy in general, (2) a model answer, about 600 words, and (3) a marking scheme that says which specific moves are worth which marks and what mistake each one is catching, so its obvious what separates an A answer from a B one.

two things. don't make the marking scheme depend on naming philosophers, because our kids won't know them and I can't check the names anyway, so describe the positions instead of the people. and at the very end, tell me where the model answer is weakest and what would change your mind about it, like is there a consideration that would make you go ok actually the head teacher is right.
