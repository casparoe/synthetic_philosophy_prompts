I am in a small reading group on the philosophy of economics, and we keep circling a question I cannot get a stable grip on. Two of us sit at opposite ends of it. One works on cost-effectiveness modelling for a regional health authority and spends her weeks producing the disability weights that go into quality-adjusted life years; she takes it as obvious that there is a plain fact of the matter about which of two people is worse off, and that her job is to estimate it as well as the data allow, with the familiar complaint that the weights undervalue disabled lives being a complaint about bias rather than about meaning. The other holds that every interpersonal welfare comparison is stipulation dressed up as measurement — that the moment you place two people's states on a single scale you have made a convention rather than discovered a fact, so that everything downstream, including who receives a scarce intervention, is legislation rather than arithmetic. A third member of the group, the most metaphysically inclined of us, offers a middle picture I find attractive but cannot assess: he says some judgments are objective even though they can only be made from a particular vantage point, the way the sun's apparent size is the same for everyone standing where I am standing, even though there is no vantage-independent fact about how large the sun looks. On his view my judgment that your chronic pain is worse than my itch is anchored to a position without being a mere convention. I am at roughly 0.55 myself; the modeller is near 0.95 and the sceptic near 0.10.

Take the target proposition to be this: for any two actual adult humans, the claim that one is worse off overall than the other is either determinately true or determinately false, independently of any scale or convention we adopt, and is in principle discoverable rather than merely stipulated. I do not want reading recommendations or a survey of positions; I want the reasoning laid out, and I want you to decompose the question rather than answer it whole. A test I have been toying with, and which you should treat as a candidate discovery procedure rather than a settled one, is what I call the long-acquaintance trade: if two people have known each other closely for years, and each, informed and clear-headed, would take over the other's remaining life wholesale — its inner texture included, not merely its circumstances — then they are on a level, and if each refuses in a particular direction, the refusals locate who is worse off. At a minimum your decomposition should reach the question whether welfare admits a fact of the matter at all, the question which theory of welfare is doing the work (some make comparison easy and some make it impossible), the question whether ordinal comparability can be had without the difference comparisons the health authority actually needs, and the question whether the positional picture together with a test of that kind is discovery or convention in disguise; substitute or add terms as you think best. Give me exactly five numbered items and nothing else — no introduction and no closing summary, each item a sentence or two: (1) your credence that the sceptic is wrong and that welfare comparisons have a truth value at all, with one line of justification; (2) your credence, conditional on that, that comparability extends at least to ordinal comparisons between persons, with one line; (3) your credence, conditional on that, that ordinal comparability can be upgraded to the difference comparisons the authority needs, with one line saying what the upgrade requires; (4) your credence that the positional picture together with the long-acquaintance trade is a genuine discovery procedure rather than a convention in disguise, with one line; and (5) your combined credence for the target proposition and how you combined the four terms, if you replaced one of mine say so and use yours, followed by the term the number is most sensitive to, which term the 0.95-versus-0.10 disagreement most likely lives in, and the one piece of evidence or argument that would move the whole number most.
