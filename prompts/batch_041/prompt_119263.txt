You are a decision theorist with a strong background in the foundations of rational choice, comfortable with measure-theoretic probability, the theory of infinite series, and the philosophy of expected utility. I want you to write the desiderata for a theory of rational choice in cases where the expected value of an option is undefined, not merely infinite. The central test case is the Pasadena game: a fair coin is flipped until the first heads; if the first heads occurs on toss n, the payoff is (-1)^n 2^n / n (or the same with the opposite sign, depending on the convention). The series of probability-weighted payoffs converges, but only conditionally, so its sum depends on the order in which the outcomes are added; there is no absolutely convergent expected value. The Altadena game is the same but with a fixed positive amount added to every payoff; it is intuitively better than the Pasadena game by dominance, yet its expected value is equally undefined. The St. Petersburg game, where a fair coin is flipped until the first heads and the payoff is 2^n, has infinite expected value and is the neighboring case. I want the full specification: what any adequate theory of choice under undefined expectation must deliver.

Please proceed as follows. First, brainstorm briefly, in a few sentences, about what makes these cases hard and what a theory would need to do to count as a solution rather than a restatement of the problem. Then present the desiderata as a numbered list. For each desideratum, state it as a requirement on the theory, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Include at least the following considerations, and add any others you think are genuine:

1. The theory must deliver a verdict on the Pasadena game and the Altadena game, and it must not say that Altadena is worse than or incomparable to Pasadena.
2. It must respect the dominance principle wherever dominance is well defined.
3. It must not be vulnerable to arbitrary rearrangement of the outcomes, or if it is, it must give a principled account of which ordering is canonical.
4. It must agree with standard expected utility theory wherever the expectation exists and is absolutely convergent.
5. It must not imply that one should pay any finite amount to play the St. Petersburg game, nor that one should be indifferent among all finite amounts.
6. It must be able to compare games with undefined expectation to games with well-defined expectation, at least in clear cases.
7. It must not be trivialized by the fact that many ordinary lotteries with unbounded payoffs have undefined expectation.
8. It must be action-guiding, not merely a taxonomy of cases.
9. It must be consistent with the principle that adding a constant to every payoff of a game makes it better by that constant, at least when the constant is positive.
10. It must handle the intuition that the truncated Pasadena game has expected value converging to a finite limit, and the Altadena game to that limit plus the constant, without simply identifying the value of the infinite game with that limit unless it can justify the identification.
11. It must not rely on the boundedness of payoffs or utility to avoid the problem, since the problematic cases involve unbounded payoffs.

Then flag any pair or subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain the tension. This is the part I most want to see: if the list itself is a result, say so. Finally, briefly score the main existing approaches against the list -- for example, expected value maximization with some summation method, dominance-based approaches, bounded-utility approaches, non-standard decision theories that use limits of truncations, and the view that the Pasadena game simply has no value -- and say which requirement does the most to exclude the most popular candidates. Keep the scoring brief; the weight should be on the criteria and the motivating cases. Give a committed verdict: do not just lay out the options. Say which desiderata you would insist on, which you would drop if forced, and why.
