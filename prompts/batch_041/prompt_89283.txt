I have been thinking on my own about the simulation idea — the thought that if any civilization ever acquires the power to run ancestor simulations, simulated minds would vastly outnumber real ones, so we should suspect we are among the simulated. What I want from you is a credence, and then the machinery behind it. Specifically: how confident are you that taking the simulation possibility seriously substantially deflates the expected value of working to improve the far future — deflates it enough to matter, say by a factor of a thousand or more, rather than by a factor of two? The target is the standard fanatical calculation, where you multiply a very small probability of an astronomically large benefit and let the product swamp everything else.

Then decompose it, with a rough number and one line of justification for each term, and tell me how the terms combine and which one the final figure rides on hardest. The terms I would expect to matter: how plausible the disjunctive conclusion is on its own terms; conditional on our being simulated, whether the far future inside the simulation is worth as much as the real one, which seems to turn on whether simulated beings count morally; whether anything we do here has any causal purchase on the level above; whether the simulators' likely indifference or interference is a separate discount or already built into the first term; and how much the whole analysis is undercut if our reasoning about simulations is itself part of what is being simulated. I would also want the opposite possibility weighed rather than assumed away: if we are being run by someone, that might add a reason to act well rather than remove one.

For my part I put myself near two-thirds that the deflation is at least that large, mostly because the leverage term feels decisive to me — tell me if that is where you would locate our disagreement, or somewhere else.

If my framing runs two different questions together, or if the deflation is already assumed by the way I have set the terms, say so plainly instead of answering the question as posed.
