Ok so some context first: I'm a novelist, about 200 pages into a book about a xenolinguist who's spent thirty years on a single transmission from a probe nobody can get inside, and the plot turns on whether her life's work can be finished at all. I had dinner last week with an old friend who did a PhD in philosphy of mind before defecting to consulting, and she says the real question isn't whether my character can decode the thing, it's whether there's a fact of the matter to be had. Her line: if you can't get at the inside of a system — you see only its outputs, you can't intervene, it might be a sealed probe, a bat, or a trained model whose weights you're not allowed to read — then any number of incompatible stories about what its states represent will fit everything you will ever observe, and there's nothing left over for one of them to be right about. I think she's wrong, but every time I argue it I end up just gesturing at intuition, which is a bad sign.

So here's what I actually want. Give me your credence that for such a system — internals inaccessible, no interventions available, only behavioral evidence — there can still be a determinate fact of the matter about which ontology it's using, i.e. about what its internal states represent, rather than merely about which descriptions happen to fit its behavior. And I don't want a bare number, I want the decomposition. Roughly how much of it rides on: (a) whether representation is fixed by causal/informational links to the world, versus by the inferential role a state plays in the system, versus by what the system was selected to do; (b) whether sameness of ontology between two systems is a well-defined relation at all, or is always relative to some chosen scheme of description; (c) whether having interventional access, if we did get inside, would actually settle the matter or just push the indeterminacy down a level into the vocabulary we use to describe the wiring; and (d) anything else you think belongs in the sum. Give each term a number and one line, show me how they combine, then tell me which term is carrying the most weight and what evidence or argument would move it.

Mine is around 0.7 and hers is around 0.15 — we're not that far apart in confidence, we just seem to be answering different questions, and I'd like to know which one. Please keep the whole thing under 500 words, and lead with the number.
