I'm in a small philosophy-of-mind reading group and last week we got onto the color phi phenomenon — you flash a red dot in one spot and then a green dot somewhere else a moment later, and people report seeing a single dot that travels across the gap and changes color partway, except the color change gets reported as happening *before* the second dot was even shown, which is the genuinely strange part. The discussion turned into a fight about the Orwellian vs Stalinesque ways of describing what the brain is supposedly doing there, and honestly I left more confused than when I arrived.

Here's the bit I can't get past. A friend insisted this is really an empirical question and we just need better neuroscience: if it turns out the visual system buffers the first flash and only passes it along once the second one arrives, that's the Stalinesque story (the experience itself is staged late), whereas if the first representation is sent on immediately and then some later memory process overwrites or edits it, that's the Orwellian story (the experience was fine, the record got falsified). He thinks a clever enough experiment could tell us which one it is. I have a nagging feeling that's wrong, and that these aren't competing empirical hypotheses in the way he thinks — something to do with the fact that they predict the exact same reports, and worse, that any measurement we make would already have to presuppose an answer to the very thing at issue (when the experience happened, and what counts as the experience as opposed to the memory of it).

Could you lay out, as carefully and concretely as you can, what the actual conceptual issue is here — why the Orwellian and Stalinesque revisions are supposed to be so hard, maybe impossible, to tell apart; what exactly turns on the distinction if we really can't tell them apart; and whether the whole business of assigning a timestamp to a conscious experience is even a well-posed question to begin with. I dont want a history of who said what, I want the argument, set out so I can see where the two views genuinely diverge and where the disagreement might just collapse into a verbal one.
