Ok, slightly wierd ask, but you're the only person I can think of. I write a science-and-ideas newsletter (mostly philosophy of mind, free will, that kind of thing) and the next issue is about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. I want to open it with a joke. Not a filler joke — one that actually lands for readers who know the argument, so the punchline has to turn on the machinery.

Not a determinism joke, please. The whole selling point of the Basic Argument is that it's supposed to go through whether or not determinism is true: if some quantum coin-flip in your skull settled what you did, that's no more yours than if a billiard ball did. So "we're just meat robots" is the wrong punchline.

The machinery, in case you want it spelled out (I'm paraphrasing, so there's nothing to quote): you do what you do, in the situation you're in, because of the way you then are. So to be ultimately responsible for what you do, you'd have to be ultimately responsible for the way you are, at least in certain mental respects. But you can't be — you'd have to have made yourself, and nothing can be the cause of itself. He also runs a regress version: suppose you could somehow choose your own character. To do that you'd have to already exist, before the choice, with some set of preferences about how you'd like to be, and those preferences are what shaped the choice — so where did they come from? Either just there and unchosen, or chosen by an even earlier you with earlier preferences, and so on back forever. He says it applies to any being at all: nothing can be the cause of itself (if God manages it, that's a special case, nobody else gets to). And what's on the block is the ultimate, deepest kind of responsibility, the kind where you genuinely deserve praise or blame — not weather its fair for your flatmate to be annoyed at you.

The joke I want built around this: a self-made man. Pulled himself up by his own bootstraps, made himself into who he is, wants the credit for it. The straight man raises the obvious comeback — but he did work on himself, he did choose to change — and the punchline should be the regress, not a shrug about determinism. Keep the guy claiming the credit; if he's just a hapless puppet the joke goes limp. Two lines is ideal. And if you want to add one line underneath saying which premise the punchline is actually riding on, go ahead — I'll be defending this joke in the comments.
