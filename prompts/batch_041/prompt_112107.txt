I came across the Ellsberg paradox recently and it has been nagging at me, so I would like a villanelle that enacts the problem rather than explaining it. Set it against the two urns — one whose mix of red and black balls I know, one whose mix I do not — and make the two refrains the two bets on the unknown urn that the ambiguity-averse agent turns down, the bet on red and the bet on black, so that the form itself carries the argument: the refrains keep returning as she keeps refusing, and when they finally stand side by side in the closing lines the reader should see that the two refusals, taken together, decline a bet that was certain to pay. Somewhere in the middle, let a voice insist that by symmetry the unknown urn must be fifty-fifty, and let the agent answer that this is precisely the belief she cannot make herself hold. The poem should make plain that what she is avoiding is not a bad outcome but the state of not knowing the odds, and it should leave open whether that is a genuine reason or a failure of nerve. Keep it accurate enough that someone who knows the paradox will recognise which refrain is which, and please write in British English.
