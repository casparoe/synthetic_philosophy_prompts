I would like an audit of a position rather than a defence of it, and I would rather be told where the trouble is than left to find it myself. Here is where I have landed on welfare and utility.

(1) Well-being consists in the satisfaction of preferences. A person's life goes better, other things equal, to the extent that the world comes to match what they want.

(2) There is no fact about a person's preferences over and above the pattern of their choices. To prefer A to B is to be disposed to choose A over B under conditions of full information and non-interference. That someone might be systematically mistaken about what they really want, while every relevant choice of theirs is held fixed, is not something I can make sense of.

(3) I take adaptive preferences seriously. A person raised to accept a subordinate position, who sincerely reports wanting very little beyond a modest and deferential life, is not thereby made well off when they get it, and their contentment should not be weighed in the same scale as the wants of someone whose options were never so circumscribed.

(4) Utility is ordinal. It makes sense to rank the options open to a person as better or worse for that person; it does not make sense to say that an outcome gives Ann twice as much utility as it gives Ben, or that Ann's gain is 1.4 times Ben's loss.

(5) Distributive policy should be sensitive not only to the total but to how it is spread: a given improvement matters more the worse off the person who receives it. This is not a tie-breaker for me but a genuine component of the objective.

(6) Welfarism and consequentialism are separate commitments. I am a welfarist about the good — welfare is the only thing that is good for people — but I am not a consequentialist about what may be done, and I accept constraints on action that are not themselves justified by the welfare they produce.

I suspect that some of these pull against one another, but I do not know which ones, and I do not know in advance which I would surrender. Please separate the genuine tensions from the ones that only look like tensions, and order the genuine ones by severity. For each, say precisely what has to give and what the cheapest repair would be, then state what that repair costs me elsewhere in the list. Where a pair is only apparently in tension, say what makes it look that way and what dissolves it. Finish by naming the single revision that would resolve the most, and by saying which of my commitments you would least expect me to be willing to abandon.
