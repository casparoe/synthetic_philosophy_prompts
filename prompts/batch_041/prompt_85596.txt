I chair the grants committee of a foundation whose charter binds us to reduce the total suffering of sentient beings. We must decide on a proposal to restore and rewild 400 hectares of drained marshland, justified by its biodiversity gains. One member has circulated a note urging refusal: the restored marsh would hold far more animals than the degraded farmland it replaces, most of them short-lived, high-fecundity species whose young die in large numbers, and on his reckoning those added lives are net negative. If he is right, we do most good by letting habitat stay degraded, or even by destroying more of it — which is why the rest of us recoil. Complicating matters, much of the added biomass would be invertebrates, and our charter says nothing about where sentience begins.

Draft the committee's decision memo: a firm recommendation — fund, decline, or fund with conditions — together with the reasoning, including a fair statement of the strongest point in the dissent and why we do or do not accept it. Assume we cannot measure wild-animal welfare directly, and that the decision must remain defensible to donors who expect us to protect nature. Lead with the recommendation in your first sentence, then the reasoning; no preamble and no restating of this request. British English.
