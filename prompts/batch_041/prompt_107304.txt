I'm running a session for a first-year history-of-science seminar and I want to hand out a decision tree that sorts people's positions on whether the 'Middle Ages', the 'Renaissance' and the 'Scientific Revolution' are real things or later inventions. What drives me mad about how this usually goes is that it collapses into realism vs. social construction within about two minutes, when there's a seperate question buried inside it about whether we're arguing over labels or over joints in the causal structure of history, and another one about lumpy change vs. smooth change, and I don't think anyone keeps those apart. So please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names: first the questions that carve up the space, then the actual positions sitting in it, then tell me which combinations of answers nobody occupies and why (incoherent? unattractive? just unexplored?).

A few things I want the tree to be able to separate, because I keep watching people talk past each other on them:

- someone who thinks the period terms pick out genuine, non-arbitrary boundaries in how societies changed, versus someone who thinks they're just useful filing cabinets, versus someone who thinks they're straight-up ideological impositions (the 'Middle Ages' being a bit of an insult coined by people who wanted to flatter themselves, that sort of thing);
- the difference between saying 'there was a period' and saying 'there was a boundary' — I suspect you can be a realist about one and an anti-realist about the other, and I'd like the tree to expose that if it's really there;
- the gradualist who thinks nothing much happened around the usual dates but is perfectly happy to keep the labels as handy shorthand;
- the person who thinks periodisation is fine in principle but that these particular periods are Eurocentric provincialism dressed up as universal history;
- and the person who's a realist about one of the three periods and an anti-realist about the others — I reckon that's what alot of actual working historians believe and the tree had better land them somewhere rather than making them pick a team.

Every branch node should be a question a student can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, so no node that just asks 'are you a realist or a constructionist?'. Concrete cases over abstractions wherever you can manage it. Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. If two leaves turn out to be indistinguishable by any question I could actually put to a student, flag the pair instead of pretending they're different. Aim for roughly ten to fourteen leaves and don't pad it out.

No reading lists or citations please, I'll bolt those on myself. British English throughout, and keep the tone something I can hand to undergrads without them rolling their eyes.
