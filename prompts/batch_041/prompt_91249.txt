I'm running a little discussion session on Émilie du Châtelet's essay on happiness — she wrote it in the 1740s and never published it in her lifetime. Her recipe is basically a checklist: get rid of your prejudices, especially the religious ones you absorbed as a child; be in good health; have strong tastes and passions, and put them to work for your happiness; as you age, let the passions cool into gentler tastes; protect your illusions about what you love instead of seeing through them; be virtuous; never regret anything; push thoughts of your own death out of your mind; and don't try to change your station in life, just be content with the fortune you were handed. Tier these by how well each would actualy serve a modern adult who has to work for a living, with a sentence per item on what's doing the work. Flag any two that pull against each other.
