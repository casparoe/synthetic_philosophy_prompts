I am an early-career philosopher who has just taken up a two-year research fellowship with no teaching duties and enough of a budget to bring two doctoral students into the project, and I am trying to decide where the three of us should point our effort for the duration. My field is the metaphysics of dispositions, and the immediate provocation is a case I devised last term and cannot shake. Take a glass that is, by every ordinary standard, fragile. Attached to it is a device that intervenes whenever the glass is struck with sufficient force to break it. The device's internal mechanism is governed by a fair coin: on one outcome it momentarily alters the glass's microstructure so that the glass is not fragile at the moment of impact, and on the other it leaves the glass exactly as it was but interposes a cushion that absorbs the blow. The two outcomes are indistinguishable from the outside, the glass never shatters when struck, and — this is the part I care about — the device is not concealing a hidden state but is genuinely indeterminate between the two operations, so that there need be no fact of the matter, on any given occasion, as to which occurred. My instinct is that the familiar distinction between finks (which supposedly remove or reverse a disposition) and masks (which supposedly leave the disposition intact but block its manifestation) does no work here: the case is both, or neither, and nothing downstream turns on which. If that is right, then a good deal of the literature — the reformed conditional analysis and its defence, the attempt to say what makes a disposition the very disposition it is, the dispute over whether dispositions are identical with their categorical bases — may be resting on a distinction that is, in some cases, not even in principle applicable.

What I want from you is an agenda. Suppose the three of us have two years and want to work on something that matters, rather than on the latest round of counterexamples. Which open questions in this area most deserve that time, and in what order? I am less interested in a list than in the reasoning: for each, whether it is upstream of the others, so that settling it would settle or dissolve them; how tractable it is with the tools metaphysicians already have; whether it would survive the collapse of the fink/mask distinction or be undermined by it; and how much of the existing literature would have to be re-read if the answer went one way rather than the other. I would also like you to tell me whether my glass case really does what I think it does. If it does not, say where it fails and what that implies for the ranking; if it does, say which of the questions you have listed it most damages, and why. I am sceptical of my own case, so do not spare it.

Two constraints on your answer. First, do not pad the agenda with questions that are merely fashionable; if the honest verdict is that the most important work is to stop drawing the fink/mask distinction altogether and start again from the phenomenon of a thing's liability to behave in certain ways, say so and defend it. Second, I would like the ranking to be revisable: tell me what evidence, argument, or result over the next year would move each item up or down, and which single question you would bet the three of us on if you could choose only one.
