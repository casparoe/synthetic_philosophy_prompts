Origin essentialism -- the idea that you couldn't have come from a different sperm or egg -- seems to have been argued over for decades without anyone actually moving. The essentialists have their intuition pumps about a person and a counterfactual history, the critics have their "that just shows you're describing someone else" replies, and each side thinks the other is begging the question. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on whether origin essentialism is true. Is this a substantive metaphysical disagreement, a disagreement about what our counterfactual talk is even tracking, or two camps quietly answering different questions under one heading? And here's the thing I keep coming back to: the cases we're asked to imagine almost always assume there's a fact of the matter about which possible individual is being re-identified, independent of how we'd describe the scenario. If we drop that assumption -- if "the person who would have resulted from a different sperm and egg" is something we build through our descriptions rather than discover -- does the debate dissolve, or just move somewhere else? Push on a case like identical twinning, or a sperm that was physically picked out by a technician, and ask yourself whether the question even stays well-formed. Given your diagnosis, tell me what would actually count as progress here -- a distinction, a better story about trans-world identity, or an argument that no progress is coming and why.
