I am drafting a position statement on aesthetic testimony for a reading group and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is where I have landed. (1) Aesthetic properties are response-dependent: for a work to be good just is for it to merit a certain kind of pleasure in a fully informed and suitably sensitive judge. (2) Aesthetic knowledge cannot be transmitted by testimony; one cannot know that a work is good without having experienced it. (3) Aesthetic judgments claim everyone's assent, so in calling a work good I am not merely reporting my own taste. (4) When two equally sensitive and equally well-informed judges disagree about a work, it need not be that either is mistaken. (5) Even so, I have learned an enormous amount about works I had not yet experienced from a critic's essays, and I would say I came to know something about their value through reading, before encountering the works themselves.

Please tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which merely appear to be, and which are straightforwardly compatible; rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious; and for each, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost. I do not yet have a view about which commitment I would abandon, so please do not treat any of them as fixed.
