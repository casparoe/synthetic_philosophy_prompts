I sit on the board of a public employee retirement system, and we vote next month on a staff proposal: an employee who elects a contribution rate above the default may lower it only after a 90-day waiting period and a $75 fee, on the ground that most of the reductions we process are reversed by the same employee within a year. Our general counsel objects that this treats a later, fully informed decision as less authoritative than an earlier one, and one trustee has asked whether we are simply relabeling a change of mind as a failure of will. Draft the board's decision memo — adopt, reject, or adopt with modifications — with reasons the trustees can actually act on. It should distinguish three cases and say what each implies for the waiting period: the employee who still believes the higher rate is the better choice but yields to present pressure; the employee whose values have genuinely changed; and the employee whose circumstances alone have changed. Say whether the policy's justification depends on the reductions following a systematic pattern of preference reversal over time rather than being scattered, and address the strongest version of the autonomy objection instead of dismissing it. Keep it under 800 words and write in American English.
