I am drafting a proposal for a nine-month postdoctoral research grant and would like help turning a half-formed idea into a document. The topic is the bootstrapping problem and the easy-knowledge objection to it. My suspicion is that the whole debate, among the bootstrappers and among their reliabilist and dogmatist critics alike, treats a claim to the effect that my perceptual faculty is reliable as a single proposition with a single epistemic status, when the cases that do the argumentative work actually trade on two different reliability claims: one about the actual track record of a process, and one about its counterfactual robustness across conditions. If that is right, then the standard diagnosis, that any theory licensing non-inferential perceptual knowledge is committed to objectionably easy knowledge, is too coarse, and some replies that look ad hoc are drawing the right distinction for the wrong reason. I want to know whether the objection survives once the distinction is made explicit, and whether the surviving residue is a problem for reliabilism specifically or for any view that lets perceptual beliefs be justified without antecedent justification for trusting perception.

Please write the proposal as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. The five items should cover, in order: the question stated so that it has a determinate answer; why the existing positions leave it open, including why my proposed distinction is not merely the familiar internalism/externalism split under a new name; the method and the specific cases or arguments I would work through, and why that method rather than a survey of the literature; what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing or collapsing into something trivial; and a realistic scope for nine months, including what I would cut first if the central distinction does not hold up. I will add the citations myself, so please do not supply references or a reading list.
