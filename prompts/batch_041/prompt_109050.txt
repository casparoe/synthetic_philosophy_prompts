Draft the text of an internal memorandum, about 700 words, circulated by the research-allocation committee of a fictional public funding body — the Institute for Empirical Warrant — which has just voted to adopt the falsifiability criterion of demarcation as its sole test for funding, interpreted literally and applied without discretion. The memo reports the outcome of the committee's review of the institute's evolutionary biology programme. Write it as deadpan bureaucratic prose: a subject line, addressees, several numbered paragraphs, a short appendix, and a closing minority note from one dissenting member. No winking at the reader, no narrator's aside, and no resolution in which the committee sees the joke.

The comedy has to come from getting the philosophy of science right rather than from jokes at the biologists' expense. The committee's reasoning should run through the following beats, and should reach them by way of the institute's own invented metric, the Forbiddingness Score: the number of conceivable observations a hypothesis rules out, discounted by the probability that anyone would ever get round to looking.

1. The core principle of natural selection is treated as a near-tautology — whatever survives, survives — so that taken on its own it forbids nothing, and its explanatory power is retrospective rather than predictive.
2. A particular adaptation is handled as the committee would handle any narrative reconstruction. Let the committee choose the peacock's tail, and let it ask what observation, specified in advance, would have settled the matter, noting that an indefinite family of rival stories can be told about the same evidence.
3. The historical claim of common descent is distinguished from the mechanism that is supposed to drive it, and the committee is forced to notice that geology, cosmology, and palaeontology fall under the same indictment.
4. Rather than defund outright, the committee invents a middle bureaucratic category — the Provisional Metaphysical Research Programme — funded at a reduced rate, barred from citing its own findings in the annual report, and retained on the ground that its unfalsifiable core is indispensable for organizing the work that does earn full funding.
5. The memo must turn the criterion on itself and on the philosophy of science committee that drafted it, and must handle the resulting embarrassment rather than pass over it.
6. The minority note should be sincere and technically literate: a member who accepts the criterion but argues that the programme is testable after all, once selection pressures are specified independently of the outcomes they are invoked to explain.

The memo must never suggest that evolution is false, that the biologists are frauds, or that the review was rigged; the satire depends on the criterion being applied even-handedly to everyone in the room. A reader who knows this literature should recognize that the criterion genuinely does bite here, that the workarounds are the ones actually available, and that the absurdity lies in the administrative use of a distinction its own author treated as a matter of degree; a reader who does not should still be able to follow the argument and see what is being mocked. Do not quote or cite any real document, and do not name real institutions; any quoted phrases should be the institute's own invented formulations.
