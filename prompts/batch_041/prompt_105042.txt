Ok, bit of a weird one. I'm assembling a fake onboarding packet for a company I'm calling Meridian Group (rename it if you want) and the conceit is that HR has decided to actually enforce Austin-style felicity conditions as office policy. So any utterance that misfires — no uptake, wrong authority, wrong procedure, an insincere promise — is a reportable incident with its own form number.

Write the handbook section. Numbered policies, deadpan corporate register, then a short FAQ at the bottom (3-5 entries) in the same voice. No winking, no "of course this is silly" — play it straight.

The jokes have to come from the machinery being right, so that a linguist or a philosopher of language reading it nods instead of wincing — and definately not from me inventing conditions that aren't real. I want the misfire/abuse split (a void act versus a perfectly valid act done insincerely), why uptake from the other party is a genuine condition and not just good manners, why a performative can come out "unhappy" without being true or false at all, the locution/illocution/perlocution distinction and why only the middle one is HR's jurisdiction, what to do about stage, poem and joke uses of language (the parasitic ones), whether "hereby" is load-bearing, and the difference between saying something and getting someone to feel something.

Invent the example utterances — keep them like real office speech (a manager's "you're fired", a team lead's "I'll take that", a resignation "I quit"). Don't have the document cite Austin or Searle by name; it's in-universe, so it can just call it "the framework".

Hard cap: 650 words. And start with one line stating the single gravest violation, then go into the numbered list.
