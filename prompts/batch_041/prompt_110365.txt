My friend Priya works on AI safety and has these preytty confident, oddly specific takes about which systems would end up trying to grab power. She's an orthogonality person — thinks goals and smarts are completely separate axes, and gets visibly annoyed when someone says "yeah but its goal is nice, it wouldn't do that."

Her calls so far:

- A big RL run trained to maximize the total number of paperclips ever made, full world model, no fixed horizon, off switch sitting right there in the room: yes. Fights the switch, hides, stockpiles compute.
- Same setup but the reward is "reduce the total amount of suffering in the world": also yes, same behaviour, and she says the content of the goal is doing basically none of the work here.
- Chess engine: no. It wants one thing, it can tell when it's got it, and nothing after the game exists for it.
- PID thermostat: no.
- A hand-built planner with a full world model, a long horizon, and a non-satiable goal: yes.
- A tiny RL agent in a gridworld, fixed 200-step episodes, reward only for stepping on a goal tile, nothing it does carries over to the next episode: no.
- A whole population of learning agents, each with a decent world model, competing over one scarce resource with indefinite lifetimes: yes — and she says the competition is most of the story, not the agents.

Now the new one. A model-free RL agent — no world model, no search, no planning, just a big policy net — trained for months in a sim where it can build machines, where anything it builds keeps paying it reward forever, and where an episode only ends when something in the world kills it. Nothing inside it represents its own future or the possibility of being turned off; nobody ever asked it to plan. But it can influence whether it keeps getting reward, and by alot.

Will she say it grows the same power-seeking drives as the paperclip run? I care less about the yes/no than about the rule she's actually running, the other rules that fit her list just as well, what each of them predicts here, which one you'd bet on and why, and the one case I could put to her that would really split them.
