You are grading a short-essay exam in a course on distributive justice and desert. The question and a student's answer are below. Please grade the answer out of 40 and supply the comments that justify the grade.

Question. Consider a society whose public institutions are formally meritocratic: competitive examinations determine admission to universities and public offices, and the examinations are graded anonymously. Two candidates, A and B, have identical native aptitude. A receives superior schooling because A's parents can afford it; B does not. A scores higher and receives the position. A defender of the outcome says: "A deserves the position because A worked hard and earned the highest score, and the exam was fair." Evaluate this defense. In your answer, distinguish (a) moral desert of the position, (b) desert of the effort expended, and (c) institutional entitlement to the position. Does the defender's argument establish (a), (b), or (c)? What further premises would be needed?

Student's answer. The defender's argument establishes institutional entitlement, and partly establishes desert of effort, but it does not establish moral desert of the position. The exam was fair in the procedural sense: it was anonymous and rule-governed, so A is entitled to the position under the rules. Whether A deserves the position morally is a further question. A's effort was real, but effort is itself shaped by upbringing, health, and the options one can see; B may have worked just as hard under worse conditions. So A cannot claim full credit for the effort. Rawls is right that we do not deserve our native talents or the social starting points that let us develop them; on his view desert is pre-institutional, so he would say A deserves nothing at all. Nozick would agree that the outcome is unjust because it violates desert, since a just distribution must track who deserves what. A better view is that procedural fairness plus effort yields desert: if the competition was fair, then the winner deserves the prize, because the only alternative is to reward people for unchosen disadvantages. The defender therefore succeeds in establishing that A morally deserves the position, though not in the strong sense that A deserves the talents that made the effort possible.

Give separate comments on (i) the entitlement/desert distinction, (ii) the treatment of effort and luck, (iii) the use of Rawls, (iv) the use of Nozick, and (v) the final inference. For each, say whether it is sound, partially sound, or mistaken, and why. Also identify the strongest part of the answer and the most serious error. In your assessment, state which of your criticisms you are least confident about and what argument or consideration would lead you to revise the grade. Begin with the grade and a one-sentence verdict, then give the comments; do not restate the question or include any preamble.
