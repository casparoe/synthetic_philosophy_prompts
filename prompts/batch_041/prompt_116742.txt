I am a retired condensed-matter physicist — forty years spent on effective field theories, coarse-graining, order parameters, quasiparticles, and the renormalization group — and I have lately been reading my way into philosophy of mind, where I keep running into the eliminativist thesis about belief and desire. As I understand it, the claim is not merely that folk psychology is incomplete or that mental states will eventually be reduced to neurobiology, but that the posits of folk psychology — beliefs, desires, intentions, and the rest — are the theoretical entities of a stagnant predictive scheme and may be eliminated outright, much as caloric and phlogiston were eliminated rather than reduced. I would like you to restate that position, as sympathetically and faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use: microstates and macrostates, coarse-graining, effective theories and their domains of validity, order parameters, quasiparticles, the thermodynamic limit, universality, and the distinction between fundamental and emergent descriptions. Treat folk psychology as a candidate effective theory and belief and desire as candidate coarse-grained or emergent variables. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of eliminativism translate cleanly — for instance, the idea that a successful folk psychology has a limited domain, or that its posits may be artifacts of a particular coarse-graining? Which parts translate only with distortion — say, the difference between eliminating a posit and showing that it is not fundamental, or the idea that beliefs are causally efficacious in a way that rationalizes action? And which parts have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary — the normativity of rationality, the first-person authority of avowals, the truth-evaluability and compositionality of propositional content, the possibility of systematic irrationality, or the social and institutional life of folk psychological practices? I have also encountered a neighboring view according to which folk psychology is a useful predictive stance rather than a literal theory, and I would like the residue report to say whether my vocabulary can mark the difference between that instrumentalism and outright eliminativism.

I am especially interested in a tension I cannot resolve on my own. In my own field, entities like phonons, quasiparticles, and order parameters are not fundamental, and in some cases are defined only in the thermodynamic limit, yet we do not conclude that they are unreal; we say they are emergent but indispensable for describing the relevant regime. Temperature is a coarse-grained variable that survived the elimination of caloric. So I want to know whether the eliminativist is making a claim about fundamentality, about indispensability, or about ontological commitment, and whether my framework supplies any principled way to distinguish a posit that deserves elimination, as caloric did, from one that deserves retention as emergent, as temperature did. If the residue cannot be expressed in my vocabulary, tell me whether that is a defect of the coarse-graining and effective-theory framework — something it cannot capture about the mind — or a sign that the eliminativist claim was less clear than it first appeared. Please keep the translation honest: do not quietly import assumptions from philosophy of mind and call them effective-field-theory claims. I want the failure of translation to be as informative as the translation itself, and I do not need citations or a reading list; the conceptual work is what interests me.
