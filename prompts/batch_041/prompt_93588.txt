Ok, so I've been going down a rabbit hole on the color phi effect and I want to actually force myself to rank the different ways people explain it, because I keep bouncing between them and never landing anywhere.

Quick version of the setup, so you know what I'm on about: you flash a dot in one spot, then a dot in another spot, and at the right timing people report a single dot that moves smoothly from the first spot to the second. That part's just apparent motion, fine. The spooky bit is color: if the two dots are different colors, people report the dot changing color roughly halfway along the path. But the color of the second dot wasn't on screen yet during the part of the trajectory where they say the change happened. So the report of the experience seems to require info that arrived "too late" to be in that experience. Dennett used this kind of case to set up the Orwellian vs Stalinesque contrast — either the experience itself gets edited before you ever have it (Stalinesque, the show trial happens before you see anything), or you really have the honest experience and then your memory of it gets rewritten after the fact (Orwellian). And then his punchline is that those two are empirically indistinguishable from the inside and from the outside, so maybe there's no fact of the matter about when the experience "really" happened. Which is either profound or a cop-out, depending on my mood.

Anyway, here are the candidate resolutions I want you to rank. The first four are the ones I actually see floating around; the last three are ones I half-invented while reading, so be warned:

1. Stalinesque: the whole moving-color-changing percept is constructed and only then delivered to consciousness. Nothing is edited after the fact; the editing is upstream of experience.

2. Orwellian: you genuinely experience first one dot, then the other, and your memory is later revised to insert the motion and the mid-path color change.

3. Dennett-style deflationism: there's no further fact here, the two stories above are just two descriptions of one functional process, and asking when the experience happened is a category error.

4. Predictive-processing / Bayesian story: the visual system predicts where the dot is going and what it'll be doing, and the percept IS the prediction, so "knowing the future" is really just "guessing right" often enough that nobody notices.

5. (mine) Timestamp-at-binding: features don't carry their own experienced time. A feature gets its "when" from the object it gets bound into, so the second dot's color gets attached to the already-moving object and inherits that object's ongoing timeline. Not editing a stream, just wrong metadata on the bind.

6. (mine) Two clocks: color and motion run on seperate internal clocks with different latencies, and the report is just a post-hoc reconciliation of two streams that never agreed in the first place. Nobody edits anything; the sync is just bad.

7. (mine) Emulator story: the motion percept is really the motor system's simulated trajectory, and the color change is filled in as "what a dot would be doing at that point on the path," so the experience is the simulator's output rather than a recording.

Some of these might secretly be the same idea wearing different hats, and if so I want you to say which.
