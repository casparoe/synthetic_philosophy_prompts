I have been reading around two-dimensional semantics on my own time, and I keep running into the same problem: each presentation shows what the apparatus does with a couple of famous examples and then moves on, so I never learn what a theory in this area is actually required to deliver. Before I go further, I want the acceptance criteria.

Write the desiderata for a theory of two-dimensional content: what any adequate account of the two intensions of an expression (the one that captures what a competent speaker can know without empirical investigation, the one that captures how the term behaves across possibilities) has to deliver.

Give me the cases it must get right, with the case behind each requirement: the necessary truths that are discovered rather than defined, and whose denial a speaker cannot rule out by reflection alone; the truths knowable without experience that are nevertheless not necessary; indexicals, descriptions, proper names and natural-kind terms, all handled by one mechanism rather than a patch per category; the fact that the first intension is what guides a rational agent while the second is what settles what could have been the case; and the awkward middle period when an identity is still a hypothesis, so it is not yet settled what the term's first intension is.

Then give me the distinctions the theory must not collapse: two intensions for a single expression without positing ambiguity; epistemic possibility as against metaphysical possibility; a term's content as against whichever world happens to be actual for the speaker.

Finally, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. My own suspicion is that a uniform, compositional treatment of all those categories and the demand that the first intension be knowable without experience and action-guiding will pull against each other; if so, say so rather than papering over it. Also tell me which single requirement rules out the most otherwise attractive theories, and whether the framework is best read as a semantics for public language or as a model of one subject's information, since I suspect that choice changes which requirements are non-negotiable.
