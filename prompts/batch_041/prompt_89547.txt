Last night I had a long and eventually rather heated argument with a friend about the Sokal affair -- the physicist who in 1996 placed a deliberately nonsensical article in the cultural-studies journal Social Text, and revealed the hoax in Lingua Franca a few weeks after it appeared -- and about what, if anything, the episode proves concerning Richard Rorty's neopragmatism and his antirepresentationalism. My friend's position is that the affair is a decisive reductio: once you abandon the idea that our sentences answer to a mind-independent world, you have forfeited any principled standing from which to call a parody of physics gibberish, and the editors walked into the trap precisely because nothing in their intellectual toolkit, or in Rorty's, could have stopped them. My own suspicion is that the affair displays carelessness, ideological vanity, and a journal that was not then submitting submissions to outside expert review -- none of which bears directly on the metaphysical thesis that truth is not correspondence. I would like you to settle this rather than referee it.

Please take on the role of a philosopher who works on American pragmatism, especially Rorty, and on the analytic literature concerning truth and representation, and at the same time of a historian careful about what a single episode can establish. Think the matter through before you begin writing, and show your reasoning rather than only your conclusions.

Specifically, I want the following.

1. Disentangle the several claims that travel together under the heading of antirepresentationalism -- the metaphysical claim that no interesting relation of correspondence explains why true sentences are true; the deflationary treatment of truth; the ethnocentric account of justification; the claim that natural science is one vocabulary among others rather than the measure of reality; and the looser suggestion that there is no principled difference between physics and literary criticism -- and say for each whether the Sokal affair touches it, and how hard.

2. Then run the analysis in the other direction. What does Rorty's framework allow us to see in the episode that the standard telling misses? Consider in particular whether the editors' failure is better described as a failure of conversational care -- a failure to keep the relevant community honest about its own standards -- than as a failure of representation; whether Rorty's insistence that the world can cause our beliefs without justifying them leaves him able to say that Sokal's article was nonsense and not merely unwelcome; and what work his own later complaint that the academic left had substituted cultural theory for practical politics can do here.

3. Give me your honest verdict on whether this episode is a genuine test of antirepresentationalism at all, or whether the theory is instead being held answerable for something it never claimed -- and say what would have to be true for my friend's reductio to go through.

4. Throughout, keep separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material. Several of these interpretive questions are live disputes, and I do not want a contested reading handed to me as the consensus; where you are offering your own reading, mark it as yours.

Write in British English.
