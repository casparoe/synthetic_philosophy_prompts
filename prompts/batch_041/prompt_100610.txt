I keep going in circles about when a thing counts as the same thing over time, and I want to know if there's actually a principle behind my gut reactions or if I'm just making it up as I go. Take these as data — they're my real reactions, not positions I've argued for:

- My grandad wore the same pocket watch for forty years. He replaced the mainspring twice, the glass once, and eventually the whole case when it got too dented. I'd say it's the same watch the entire time. No question.
- A ship gets repaired plank by plank over decades until none of the original wood is left. I definately say same ship. And then somebody takes all the old planks out of a warehouse and reassembles them into a ship, and I want to say that one is also the original. I can feel those two verdicts can't both be right and I don't know which to drop.
- Statue and lump of clay. The sculptor makes a statue out of a lump. I'm pretty sure there's just one object on the pedestal, not two things stacked in the same place. But then I squash it flat and I'm equally sure the lump survived while the statue didn't — which seems to need two objects after all.
- A river. All the water is different from last year, the banks have shifted, and I still say it's the same river. If engineers dam it and divert it into a completely new channel, though, it feels like it stopped being the same river, and that bothers me given the watch.
- My friend's band. Over the years all four original members quit and were replaced, and I still call it the same band, just not the original lineup. But if a totally different group of people play the same songs and bill themselves under the name, I call that a tribute act, not the band.
- A wiki page that's been edited every day for ten years, so that not a single sentence from the first version survives. I say it's the same article — same page, same history — even though nothing of the original text is left.

So is there one account of what it takes for something to persist that gives me all of these at once? My hunch is it's something about causal continuity rather than sameness of parts, but I don't see how that rescues the squashed statue or tells me which of the two ships is the real one. If you have to, which of my verdicts should I give up, and what's the cleanest principle that keeps the most of them? Also, what does your principle say about a case I didn't list: a person whose brain is replaced neuron by neuron with silicon chips that do exactly the same job, slowly enough that they never notice a break.
