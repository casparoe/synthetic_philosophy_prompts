I am a high school student preparing a presentation for my school's philosophy club, and I have reached the point where the standard summaries have stopped helping me. I can recite that epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment both connect knowledge to practical matters, but I cannot state cleanly what separates them, and I suspect I have been quietly running the two together.

As I understand it, the contextualist holds that the word 'knows' is context-sensitive: whether a given sentence of the form 'S knows that p' is true depends on the epistemic standards governing the conversation in which the sentence is uttered, so that one and the same person, with one and the same evidence, may be correctly described as knowing in an ordinary conversation and as not knowing in a more demanding one. The pragmatic encroachment theorist, by contrast, seems to hold that the word itself does not shift in this way, but that the property of knowing, the thing the word picks out, itself depends on the practical stakes of the person who is said to know, so that someone whose evidence has not changed at all may fail to know simply because more now rides on being right.

For each case, tell me what the contextualist should say and what the pragmatic encroachment theorist should say, and explain why they diverge, if they do.
