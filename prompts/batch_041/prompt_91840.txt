I keep thinking about that old idea where an ant colony counts as one mind, with the ants as neurons that have no clue what the colony is doing. Heres the argument I want you to make for me: the colony really does have a mind, but it can't have a self or a point of view, because its thoughts are spread out so thin that no ant, and no group of ants, ever holds the whole thought at once — the belief is real, but there's no place in the colony where it exists all together. So when my friend says "but theres nobody home in there to be wrong about anything," I want you to show that being wrong doesn't need anybody home, and that this is also why the colony can't be surprised or notice its own mistakes. Skip the usual functionalism-versus-biology stuff, I already know that part. Stay on this belief-without-a-believer angle, and take seriously the objection that a belief nobody could ever act on or correct isn't a belief at all.
