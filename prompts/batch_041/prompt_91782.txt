The proposal is that we might check the answers of a model too smart for us to evaluate directly by having two copies of a model argue before a weaker judge — a human, or a smaller model — with the judge choosing whichever side it finds more convincing. The hope is that the side holding the truth can win simply by pointing out the other side's errors, so that the judge, who only has to compare two arguments rather than find the answer, ends up correct even though it could never have gotten there alone. The worry is that the side that is wrong, or lying, need not be more convincing in any honest sense: it can win by making its argument so long, so tangled, or so expensive to check that the judge cannot possibly verify it within its budget, while the honest side's refutation would demand work that is just as hard. Once the judge cannot tell which side is honest, the protocol's guarantee collapses, and the asymmetry looks bad, since the dishonest side only has to hide a single flaw whereas the honest side has to expose it — though I suspect that framing is too quick, because it seems to matter a lot whether a false claim always has a short refutation, and I don't know what to make of that.
