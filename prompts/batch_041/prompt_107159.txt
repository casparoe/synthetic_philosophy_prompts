I've been arguing with a couple of friends for weeks about a view one of them calls tranquilism, and I've slid into half-believing it, which is what worries me. The claim, as he puts it, is that the only thing that intrinsically matters is the absence of craving and suffering — strip away the felt "thirst" of wanting and the felt badness of pain and there's nothing left over that's good in itself. Pleasure, getting what you want, achieving things, love, understanding: on this view those matter only as means, insofar as they cut down or head off craving and suffering, and a life with zero craving and zero suffering but no positive goods whatsoever would be as good as any life gets, maybe the best. I want you to play it straight and tell me what would count against this — not the cheap "but that's so bleak" stuff, but the real defeaters: the intuitions, the possible cases, the distinctions or results from other areas it stands or falls with, and what it's committed to that might turn out unacceptable. I'm especially stuck on a distinction a friend lobbed at me, that craving and suffering come apart — you can crave without suffering (a mild itch of desire you don't mind having) and suffer without craving (a pain you've fully accepted, or grief that isn't grasping at anything) — so does the view actually need two separate claims, and if it does, do the two even hold together, or does one quietly collapse into the other? I'd also like to know which of these a committed tranquilist would themselves concede counts against them versus which they'd wave off as smuggling in a positive-good assumption the view already denies, and I'd find it most convincing if you told me which single case or consideration does the most damage. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about whether the view has been qualified into something that risks nothing at all — and if so, whether that's a real problem for a view like this or just the price of making it precise.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble and don't restate my question.
