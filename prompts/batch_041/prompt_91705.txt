So a friend and I have been trying to build a version of negative utilitarianism that doesnt immediately get laughed out of the room, and we've hit a snag I can't phrase properly. The idea is this: don't say that pleasure and pain sit on one big scale where you can just read off exchange rates, but also don't say they're totally incomparable, since that makes every policy question unanswerable. Instead allow trades up to a point. Below some threshold of suffering, it's fine to make someone a bit worse off in exchange for a big gain for someone else (or for that same person later), because down there the two things really are comparable at some finite rate. But once you're at or above the threshold — the kind of suffering that eats a whole life, torture, untreated grinding pain — no amount of anyone's pleasure buys it. Not a million units, not a googol. We've been calling it cliff-edge negative utilitarianism, and I think the fancy word for the sub-threshold part is a lexical ordering, though I'm not sure that's the right usage.

What I can't get clear on is the conceptual issue sitting right at the seam. I tried writing it as a piecewise function — rate of exchange is some finite number like 7 below the threshold, infinite above — and then I realized I couldn't say what the threshold is even measured in, or what it would mean for the rate to jump from a finite number to infinity at a particular point. If it's a real jump, then being one notch below the threshold and one notch above it differ by an infinitesimal amount of pain but an absolutely maximal amount morally, which sure sounds like I've smuggled in a sharp line the underlying facts don't have. And if I try to soften that by making the threshold a range, I think I land in a different mess, where alot of individually tradeable sub-threshold miseries add up to something that by my own lights ought to be untradeable — like a million mild headaches, each one of which I'd happily swap for a nice afternoon, summing to a catastrophe I'd never swap for anything.

Can you lay out what the actual conceptual issue is here? Specifically: what this kind of view is committing itself to about the comparability of pleasure and pain, where exactly the commitment bites, whether the two problems I'm gesturing at are really the same problem in different clothes, and whether this is a genuine dilemma or just an artifact of how I've set the thing up. I'd much rather understand the structure of the problem than get a verdict on which version I should believe.
