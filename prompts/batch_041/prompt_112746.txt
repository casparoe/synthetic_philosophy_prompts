write me a parody excerpt from the employee handbook of a company that has rebuilt its entire talent framework around the four grades of mind in Dennett's taxonomy -- Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian. Around 650 words, deadpan corporate register: numbered sections, bullets, a ratings table, a mandatory-training notice. Each grade needs its own HR name and its own definition, and the definitions have to actually get the taxonomy right -- hardwired reflexes; learning by trial and error from reward; simulating the options inside your own head so that the hypotheses die in your stead; and offloading thought into external tools and notations. The comedy should come out of what a corporate hierarchy does to a layered taxonomy: the grades are nested, not ranked, and the company ranks them anyway, telling everyone they must "level up" to Gregorian while every incentive it actually offers rewards purely Skinnerian behaviour, and never telling anyone which grade they've been assigned. Include one section where a team gets reclassified and the reclassification has real consequences -- pay band, headcount, whether their proposals get tested or just filed. Also a short "what this is not" disclaimer that quietly admits the whole thing is a value judgement dressed up as a description. Keep it entirely straight-faced, no winking, and make sure someone who has never heard of Dennett can still tell what the four tiers mean.
