I've been trying to write down what I actually think about promises for a small thing I'm doing, and I keep going round in circles, its driving me up the wall, so I'm going to throw the whole pile at you and ask you to tell me which bits fight with each other. Here's where I am.

Breaking a promise is wrong because of what it does to the person you promised. They're the one who gets to be let down, to insist, to resent it, and to let you off the hook — promising, as opposed to predicting, is basically handing them that authority over you. I've been calling it the hand-back rule: whatever a promise hands you, only you can hand it back. Nobody else gets to release me from a promise, and I don't get to release myself.

I also think a promise binds whether or not anyone is counting on it. If I promise to come and you forget and go to the cinema instead, I've still done something wrong by not turning up. And I don't think promises are just weights in a calculation — they take certain justifications off the table rather than getting outweighed. Not that they're absolute: if the only way to get my neighbour to hospital is to stand you up, I stand you up. And obviously a promise to do something monstrous doesn't bind, and neither does one somebody extracts from me by threatening my kid.

Two more, which I'll just state and leave alone. I promised my brother, when he was dying, that I'd scatter our dad's ashes at the lake, and then he went before I got round to it. Nobody knows about it now. I still think I'm bound, and that skipping it would be a wrong. And I don't think promising is a local invention — two people stranded somewhere who'd never heard of it could promise each other things and be bound, so it can't be that the obligation comes from a convention they're both taking part in. But I also think whether something counts as a promise at all, and what it commits you to, is very much a matter of local custom: "I'll try to be there" is a promise in my family and a polite no where I work, and if you and I disagree about how long an RSVP holds, the custom settles it, not our intentions.

The two that keep nagging at me are the hospital one — doesn't that mean I'm weighing after all, and all the off-the-table talk is just decoration? — and the monstrous-promise one, because if I can wave a promise away for being immoral, why can't I wave one away for being inconvenient? But I have a feeling the real trouble is somewhere I'm not looking, and I'd rather you told me than have me guess.

So: what actually pulls against what, ranked by how bad it is? Which pairs only look like they clash until you draw a distinction, and if so, what's the distinction? For the ones that are real, what's the cheapest thing I could give up, and what would giving it up cost me? I don't want a survey of what other people say about promises, and I don't want everything declared consistent — I want to know where my own view is stitched badly. Write it as continuous prose please, no headings or bullets or bold or tables, the way you'd explain it to me in a long email; I can't read anything that's been shredded into a list.
