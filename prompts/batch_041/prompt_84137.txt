I chair the rules comittee for the prediction market we run at work. It's small — real money, but only a few thousand dollars of sponsor money, maybe sixty regulars, mostly staff and a couple of grad students, contracts on stuff like "will the dept get the grant" and "will the tram line open before June." Prices are just whatever the last person to show up was willing to trade at.

Here's the problem. One of our traders, a retired actuary, has spent the last month buying combinations that pay out no matter how the questions resolve. His cleanest one: he bought "tram opens by June" at 20 and "tram opens between June and August" at 25, and sold "tram opens by August" at 60. Costs him 45, brings in 60, and the payouts cancel whichever way it goes. He's up about $700, which is a real chunk of the prize pool, and the rest of the field is furious. Half of them want him disqualified and his positions voided. He says he's broken no rule, and that if our prices let someone lock in a sure profit then the prices were broken, not him.

The comittee meets next Tuesday and I have to circulate a written recommendation. Please draft the memo: what we do about him, the positions, the money, and what rule (if any) we adopt going forward, with reasoning the traders can actually argue with. Don't just say "his trades only worked because our prices didn't add up" and leave it at that — the real fight is whether that matters when nobody is forced to take the other side of these bets, so deal honestly with the strongest version of the case for letting him keep the money. And one of the comittee will definately say we're turning a forecasting game into a betting shop, so say something about that too.
