I teach an upper-level metaphysics seminar (18 students, 90 minutes) and I want them to *feel* how hard it is to tell a real disagreement from two people using the same word in two different senses — the metaontology problem, but staged instead of lectured. You're a philosopher who builds classroom simulations, so think carefully about the mechanics before you write anything, and start by brainstorming a bunch of candidate words and candidate items, then commit to one set.

Design the activity. My rough plan, which you should improve on: two groups get private meaning cards for one contested word — exists — with two defensible but incompatible criteria for it, one roughly "is part of the world's basic inventory, not decomposable away without remainder" and the other roughly "there are true sentences about it within some legitimate domain of discourse". The cards stay secret. Each group then sorts the same 12 item cards (chairs, holes, corporations, nations, waves, heaps of sand, fictional characters, numbers, the average family, a song, a shadow, a promise) into exists / doesn't exist, and after that the two groups have to jointly deliver a verdict on three target items. To keep it from collapsing into "it's just semantics", give each group one swap token: spending it forces the other side to restate its claim without using the contested word — and whatever can't be restated is the thing to look at in the debrief.

Give me the full runnable thing: the exact card text for both meaning cards and all the item cards, the rules and roles, a minute-by-minute timeline, the exact moment I reveal the cards, and a debrief that maps what just happened onto the distinctions I want them to leave with — the elimination/swap test for verbalness, why "verbal" doesnt mean "trivial" (what's left over is a question about which concept we should be using, not about the world), the internal/external distinction, and the realist line that says one group was describing the world better after all. Name the moves if it helps, but the mapping matters, not the labels.

Then: what will predictably go wrong the first time i run it — someone reaching for the dictionary, someone declaring "just semantics" at minute ten, one group bullying the other into a compromise definition, the aha landing as "so philosophy is pointless" — how to handle each, and be honest about which part of the idea this format can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself.

Dont hand me three variants or hedge. Commit to one word, one item list, one set of target items, and in the debrief take a definite position on whether the residue left after the swap test is a genuine question or just a preference about words, with your reasons.
