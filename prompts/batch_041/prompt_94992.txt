The target is the gap between an action's being morally required and its having moral worth, and specifically the question whether an action can have moral worth when the agent's recognition of duty is not what actually moves her.

Reading (A), the determining-ground reading: an action has moral worth only if the agent's operative motive is the fact that the action is required — the dutifulness of the act must itself produce the act, so that the agent would have been moved to do it even had the requirement clashed with every inclination she has.

Reading (B), the limiting-condition reading: the motive of duty need not be the operative motive. It is enough that the agent recognizes the requirement and would not have acted against it; duty then functions as a standing endorsement that permits an inclination to run, and the resulting action can still have moral worth.
