I'm trying to get a small reading-and-writing group off the ground on constrained translation — the kind where you translate a poem and the form has to survive the trip, not just the sense: meter, rhyme, a lipogram, an acrostic, whatever the source is doing. Hofstadter's whole Marot fixation, Oulipo translating Oulipo, that neighborhood. It's fun, but I keep landing in the same pile of unresolved questions and I honestly can't tell which ones are load-bearing and which are just craft talk dressed up as theory.

The one I keep circling is what I've started calling constraint debt. Say the original is a lipogram dodging E; English E is way too common to dodge the same way. Do you now owe the target text a compensating constraint of comparable restrictiveness, or is it fine to just let the form go slack there? And if you do compensate, what makes one constraint "comparable" to another — is restrictiveness even well-defined when the two languages have totally different letter and rhyme statistics? That seems to spill into alot of others: whether constraints are semantic (a sonnet means something by being a sonnet) or just packaging, whether a translator should aim for equivalence of constraint or equivalence of effect, whether a constraint can really be translated at all or only swapped for a cousin.

So for someone with about a semester and a decent background in linguistics and poetics but no money or setup to run experiments — which of these should actually get the time, and why? I want the reasoning, not a list: which question is upstream of which, which ones are tractable for someone who's mostly just reading and translating, which are secretly the same question wearing different hats, and what would move one up or down the ranking. And if you think constraint debt is a fake problem, say so and tell me where the real crux is instead.
