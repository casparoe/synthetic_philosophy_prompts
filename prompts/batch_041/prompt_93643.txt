I am a fourth-year PhD student in distributed systems, and I have been sitting in on a comparative ethics seminar because I want my work on coordination, fault tolerance, and multi-agent systems to have a broader conceptual vocabulary. The seminar has introduced me to Confucian role ethics, and I find myself both drawn to it and unsure whether I genuinely understand it or am just mapping it onto the systems I already know. The central claim, as I have it, is that persons are not atomic individuals who subsequently acquire roles; they are constituted by their roles and relations, so that the self is better understood as a locus of ongoing obligations than as a substrate that bears properties. On this view, virtues are not general traits a person carries around but are indexed to particular relations—what it is to be a good parent, ruler, friend, or sibling—and ritual propriety is less a rulebook than the medium through which those relations are enacted, repaired, and renegotiated over time. The ideal is not conformity to a code but a kind of harmony among role-bearers, and filial piety, for instance, is a lifelong project of negotiating one’s relation to one’s parents rather than a fixed duty of obedience.

Could you restate this position, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use: actors and mailboxes, role-based access control, capabilities and permissions, leases and commitments, session types, two-phase commit, Byzantine fault tolerance, eventual consistency, garbage collection, and the distinction between an identity and the roles it holds. I would like the translation to proceed claim by claim: the constitution of the person by roles; the role-indexed nature of virtue; ritual as protocol and repair mechanism; harmony as an emergent property rather than a specified invariant; and filial piety as a long-running, renegotiated commitment. Then give me the residue report. Which parts translate cleanly, which require distortion, and which have no counterpart at all? For each residue, tell me whether it looks like a defect of my vocabulary—something distributed systems cannot express—or a sign that the Confucian claim was less precise than it first appeared. This is not an analogy exercise; I want to know what the translation preserves and what it loses. I am not looking for citations or a reading list; I want the translation and, more importantly, the residue.
