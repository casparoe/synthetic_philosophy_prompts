My friend and I got into a three-hour argument last night about the unilateralist's curse and I honestly can't tell if we were even disagreeing or just talking past each other. His position: the curse shows that when a bunch of actors can each unilaterally do some high-variance thing, you get too much of the bad tail, so the fix is basically a norm of not acting without some kind of conformity/coordination check. Fine. My position: that's a lopsided picture, because there's a mirror-image failure he keeps waving away. I've been calling it the multilateralist's curse (my coinage, don't be polite about it if it's dumb) — when acting requires the absence of objection, or actual consensus, and objections are cheap, the default slides toward nobody doing the good thing, and the harm that piles up from inaction is invisible precisely because it never appears as anybody's unilateral act. I think the mirror version is worse whenever the stakes are diffuse and slow. We also got stuck on whether the original thing even survives if the actors' errors are correlated instead of independent, and whether "acting" includes just publishing a paper that nudges someone else to act, which felt like it dissolved half the disagreement.
