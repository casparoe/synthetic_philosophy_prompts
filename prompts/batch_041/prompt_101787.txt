I am a high school student, and for the past two months a friend of mine in our school's ethics club has been ruling on cases we invent, with a consistency she cannot herself account for. On a student who, to spite a rival, slashes the tires of the rival's car and thereby causes a highway crash and a hospitalization, she places grave blame. On a student who drives home after a few drinks and, at a red light, taps the car ahead and dents its bumper, she places moderate blame. On a student who in a hurry leaves a wet cafeteria floor unmarked so that a teacher slips but is unhurt, she places slight blame. On a student who, handling a lab beaker exactly as instructed, sees it crack anyway, she places no blame at all.

Next week's case is this: a student hides a classmate's EpiPen as a prank; the classmate suffers an allergic reaction, but another student happens to carry a spare, and the classmate recovers completely.

Please predict my friend's verdict, name the rule she appears to be running, list the other rules that fit her earlier verdicts just as well, say what each of those rules predicts about the EpiPen case, give me your best guess and your reasons for it, and tell me which further case would best distinguish the rules that remain standing.
