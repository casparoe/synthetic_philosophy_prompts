I cover archaeology and climate for a magazine, so I've spent the last couple of years knee-deep in collapse stories — the Late Bronze Age, the Maya, Rapa Nui — and I've got a nagging feeling that all of them turn on a move nobody has a name for. Roughly: whether a society gets called collapsed seems to be decided by which two dates you pick, and then the dates quietly get forgotten. With the Maya, compare the eighth century to the tenth and you get the classic collapse; compare the tenth to the thirteenth and you get a smaller, poorer, but perfectly functioning society; compare the third century to the ninth and you get one of the great efflorescences in world history. Same dirt, same pots, three completely different stories, and nobody's lying. Rapa Nui runs the trick backwards — take the statue-building centuries as your baseline and it's a self-inflicted ecocide, take the decades just before European contact and most of the die-off turns out to be after contact, from disease and slave raids. And the Bronze Age is the one that finally made me suspicious, because its really only one specific thing that visibly ends there — the palace-centred, scribal, chariot-elite world, the palaces burn and the writing stops — and whether that counts as a society collapsing depends entirely on whether you were tracking the palaces or the farmers.

So: does this have a name? I've read a lot of the standard line that collapse just means a fast loss of social complexity, and I know ecologists have this idea of shifting baselines, where each generation takes an already-degraded state as normal. But neither feels like what I'm pointing at. Shifting baselines is about observers being fooled; this is more like a denominator nobody ever says out loud. And I keep catching myself running three complaints together — that the verdict depends on the window (a periodization artifact), that it depends on what you're counting (institutions, bodies, territory, elite grave goods — some kind of level-of-analysis thing), and that it depends on how much loss you'll accept before you'll say the word (a threshold thing). Are those three actually distinct, do they have names, and is there a single concept that covers all of them, or am I mashing unrelated worries into one?

If there's no term, coin one — but I don't want just a label, I want the edges. Is it a collapse if everyone in the valley keeps farming exactly as before but the temple-building and the kings stop? What if a third of the population dies but the institutions survive intact? What if the settlement was fine and it's only our evidence that has a hole in it — do we call that a collapse too? And which of these is doing the real work in the stories we actually tell?
