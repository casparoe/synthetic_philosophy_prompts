You are a philosopher of mind and metaphysics of science, at home in the causal exclusion debate, in interventionist accounts of causation, and in the levels-of-description questions raised by Hofstadter's careenium image — a large table of many small magnetic balls careening and clumping, where the clumps, the symmballs, reliably covary with goings-on outside the table and, in the extended version, drive them. Think carefully, and brainstorm briefly before committing to a design.

Design a thought experiment to the following specifications. First, it must be a physical system with deterministic microphysics; no new forces, no mental causation by stipulation. Second, at the substrate level, every motion of every ball must be fully accounted for by collisions with other balls and with the walls. Third, at the pattern level there must be a stable, multiply realizable configuration — a symmball — that reliably tracks a feature of the world outside the system and that an engineer could exploit. Fourth, the two descriptions must make the same predictions about what actually happens, while differing in the counterfactuals they support: the pattern-level description should license claims that hold across a range of substrate realizations, whereas the substrate-level description should be tied to the exact micro-configuration. Fifth, the case must be one where a manipulationist is tempted to say the symmball shoves the balls around, and where a Kim-style exclusion argument says it cannot, since the microphysical cause already suffices. Do not appeal to quantum indeterminacy, to consciousness, or to any system whose microphysics is left unspecified, and do not let the case rest on the pattern level being irreducibly emergent.

Then report in this order, roughly at these lengths: Scenario (about 250 words), stating the substrate-level and pattern-level descriptions side by side; The case for symbolic pushing (about 200 words); The case for substrate-only pushing (about 200 words); Verdict (about 150 words), saying which level you hold to be doing the causal work and why the dispute is not merely verbal; The load-bearing feature (about 150 words), naming the single feature of your design that makes the two verdicts come apart and saying what happens when it is removed or varied; Attack surface (about 150 words), giving the strongest way a defender of the level you ruled against would deflate your case and whether it would succeed. If your scenario would work equally well as a description of an ordinary digital computer, say so and explain what that shows.
