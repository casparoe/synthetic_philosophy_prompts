I have just been appointed to my city's charter review commission, and our charge keeps forcing a question I had assumed was settled: who is the people whose consent a decision requires? Concretely, we must decide whether noncitizen residents may vote in local elections, whether residents of a newly annexed area may vote on a bond issue the whole city will pay for, and whether the present electorate may bind future boundary decisions — including a proposal to submit the franchise question itself to a vote of the currently enfranchised, so that the existing demos decides who joins it. We have been using four labels for what a boundary can be: personal (who is a member), jurisdictional (which body is competent), issue-specific (who is affected by this particular decision), and temporal (whether the dead or the unborn belong to the demos we are invoking). Our research staff is one person and we have eighteen months.

Which open questions about the boundary problem should we actually prioritize, and in what order? I want the reasoning more than the list: for each, why it is upstream of the others for a body in our position, how tractable it is for a commission rather than a philosophy department, and what argument or development over the next year would move it up or down our agenda. And which questions are prominent in the theoretical discussion but would not change any decision we face?
