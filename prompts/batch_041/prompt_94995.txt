I never studied any of this properly, no philosophy degree or anything, but I keep circling one idea: that a leader sometimes has to do something rotten for a good reason and then live with it. I read The Prince years ago and understood maybe half, and I know he wrote another book where he suddenly sounds like he's all for republics, which I've never been able to square with the first one.

Anyway, here's the case I can't get out of my head, and I made it up myself, so don't tell me what some famous essayist said about it. Tell me what Machiavelli would say.

A country has just had its first free election. The new president finds out the old regime's security chief is going to stage a coup in about four months, and there is no legal route to stop him, the courts are packed and the army would side with him. The only lever the president has is to have the intelligence service forge a document showing the security chief has been taking money from a foreign enemy. That would finish him publicly and the coup dies. But he's only guilty of plotting the coup, not the bribe, and once the forgery is out there the president can never admit it or he's finished and the old crowd comes back. Also a journalist gets set up as the source and goes to prison for it. Doing nothing, his own advisers say, means civil war and thousands dead.

So what would Machiavelli actually advise? Not the t-shirt version where the ends justify the means, I want the reasoning. Would he say forge the thing? Would he hold some line the president shouldn't cross even to stop a war, and where is it? Does he care about the journalist at all or is she just a line item? And if he says do it, what does he say about the man who has to do it, does the president owe anybody anything afterwards, or is feeling bad about it just weakness?

Then two things he never had to think about, because they didn't exist yet:

1. In a modern country the president can be hauled in front of a court or a truth commission ten years later, over his own memos or a leak. Does that change the advice? Would Machiavelli say confess eventually and take the punishment, or bury it forever? I've heard the modern version of this whole idea says the leader is supposed to feel guilty and get punished for it, and I want to know if he'd say that's nonsense.

2. Nobody's hands touch anything anymore. It's not a forged letter, it's a deepfake assembled by a contractor in another country, and it's not a beating in a cell, it's a drone. Does that make it cleaner or dirtier? Would he think a president who arranges for a machine to do the dirty work is smarter than one who does it himself, or is that just cowardice with extra steps?
