A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about objectivity in history. He thinks Hayden White's point that historians emplot the past into tragic, comic, romantic, or satiric shapes shows that objectivity is a fantasy; I think White's challenge only lands if you assume the unit of assessment is the narrative as a whole, whereas Ranke's aspiration to show the past as it actually was was a standard for individual statements. Please argue for the position that the Ranke–Carr–White dispute is best understood as a dispute about the unit of historical representation rather than about whether the past is real, that this distinction preserves a modest objectivity at the level of statements while leaving Carr's idea that history is a dialogue between present and past with a residual problem about how selection is constrained, and test the distinction against a case in which every sentence in a historical narrative is true but the narrative as a whole is misleading. Write it as a continuous argument, not a literature review, and do not rely on quotations or citations.
