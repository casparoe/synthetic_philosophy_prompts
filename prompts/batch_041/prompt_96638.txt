My opening move: your whole case was built on Otto and his notebook, and the notebook is his own, he wrote it himself, it's reliable and he trusts it. Take two cases of mine instead. First: the notebook isn't his. A stranger filled it, in handwriting just like Otto's, with plausible entries designed to walk him to a particular museum where a cult will pick him up, and Otto has no way of telling. Second: someone with no notebook at all has an app on their phone; it's in their hand constantly, they consult it before every choice, they act on it instantly without doubt or deliberation — and it's tuned by a company to keep them engaged, against their own interests, and it works. In the second case everything on your own list is in place: constant availability, easy access, automatic endorsement. So is the app part of that person's mind? If yes, a hostile company is literally a component of their cognitive system, and the parity principle has nothing left to stop anything at all from being "in" the mind — the mind just becomes whatever happens to be coupled to you, including things built to exploit you. If no — if the coupling has to be trustworthy, or endorsed on reflection, or authored by the subject — then you've quietly stopped doing functionalism and started doing something else (ownership, provenance, normativity), and the parity principle isn't what's doing the work anymore. Same dilemma with the planted notebook: if those entries are Otto's beliefs, then belief authorship is transferable and I can put beliefs into your head by writing on a page; if they aren't, tell me exactly what's missing that a genuine case of coupled belief has, and notice that your answer can't be "it's outside the skull," or you've given up the view.
