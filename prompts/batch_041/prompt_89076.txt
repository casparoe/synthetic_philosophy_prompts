I'm putting together a zine for a philosophy of mind reading group that just spent six weeks on panpsychism, and I want to close it with a sestina about the combination problem. I've been trying to write it myself and its not working. Here's the angle: most poems about panpsychism just assert that everything is conscious and stop. I want one that actually lives inside the subject-summing version of the combination problem — not the quality-summing version, where the issue is how micro-qualities blend, but the harder one: how do many little subjects become one subject? And I want it to bring in the cosmopsychist mirror image, the decomposition problem, where there's one cosmic subject and the question is how it fractures into us. Maybe the poem is a dialogue between a panpsychist and a cosmopsychist, or maybe one voice that keeps changing sides.

Form: a sestina. Six end-words: micro, subject, unity, bind, gap, we. I know that's a lot to ask, so slant rhymes and loose repetitions are fine, but the end-words should really rotate and the envoi should feel like the moment the pattern either locks into one subject or falls apart into many. The form should do the argumentative work: the repetition is the combination problem, the envoi is the proposed solution or the failure of one.

Don't hedge. Pick a definite line on whether the combination problem is fatal to panpsychism, a solvable research program, or a pseudo-problem created by treating subjects as little atoms. Let the poem commit to that line, and after the poem add a short note (maybe 100 words) saying where the poem takes liberties with the ideas or leans on a contested reading, and why you chose the side you did. No citations, no reading list — I just want the poem and the note.
