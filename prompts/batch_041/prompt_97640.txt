I'm a second-year PhD student, mostly philosophy of mind, but for the last few months I've been sitting in on a reinforcement-learning lab because my supervisor got it into her head that someone on the ethics side should actually understand what these systems are. And now I can't stop chewing on a thing everyone there says as if it's obvious — that the reward signal is just a number, that it's got nothing to do with pleasure or pain, and so there's nothing it's like to be the agent. Maybe. But the more I stare at it, the more I think the argument is doing something sneaky: it slides from "the reward is not itself a feeling" to "the reward plays no role of the kind that feelings play," and those are different claims, and I suspect the second one is false in a way that's genuinely interesting rather than just a pun on words.

So I want to turn this into a proposal for our center's one-semester exploratory grant (~9k, basically my summer plus a little budget for a workshop at the end). My half-baked plan for keeping it tractable instead of a swamp: don't ask whether RL agents can suffer in general, that's hopeless, but ask what functional job valence has to do in a system for that system's states to even be candidates for mattering, and then ask which of those jobs a reward signal could in principle fill. My hunch is that the load-bearing job isn't being felt but something more like regulating the system toward and away from states, in a way the system's own machinery treats as non-optional, and that if that's right then alot of the standard dismissals are too quick — but so are alot of the excited claims from the other direction, the crowd that wants to say the agent is in agony whenever reward goes negative.

Please write the proposal. What I need from it: a question stated precisely enough that it has a determinate answer and I could actually turn out to be wrong; an honest account of why the going positions on machine sentience, and on the reward-isn't-valence line specifically, leave my question open (I'd rather you characterize positions than name names — I'll fill in the references myself); the actual method, laid out roughly month by month across the semester, including one concrete toy case I can reason through — I keep coming back to a gridworld agent where I decouple the reward channel from behavior, so the signal is still computed and still drives the learning updates but the policy is frozen, and I want to know whether anything of moral significance changes and why; what would count as the project failing or quietly dissolving into hand-waving; and a realistic read on whether this is a semester or a whole career. Also, tell me straight if you think the framing smuggles in its own answer.
