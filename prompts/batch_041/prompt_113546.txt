Right, so I need help with something before Thursday and I've definately left it too late.

It's our sixth form ideas evening — it was basicly meant to be a small project thing for my EPQ but it's turned into a proper event with a panel and about forty people in the hall. I get ten minutes and then questions. My title is 'Girls in rich countries pick girlier jobs — so what?' and here's the argument I'm standing up to defend:

Everyone knows women in the poorest countries go into science and engineering more than women in the richest, most equal countries do. My mate says it's called the gender-equality paradox. I'm not going to argue about whether it's real. My line is that it doesn't tell us what one side thinks it tells us, and it doesn't tell us what the other side thinks either. It's not about nature and it's not about oppression. It's that in a poor country, picking a job is mainly about money, so you take whatever pays, whoever you are. In a rich safe country you get to pick what fits you, and 'what fits me' is way more gendered than the actual job market is. I've been calling it the what-does-it-say-about-me thing. Which means the answer isn't more freedom or less freedom, it's changing what the options are taken to mean — like what people reckon it says about you if you take physics. (In our school, the girls who do take physics are mostly the ones with an engineer parent, which I think backs me up, but honestly I'm not sure it does.)
