I am the student representative on the youth advisory board of our city's natural history museum, and on Thursday the board votes on the title and framing of a new permanent gallery about how civilizations end. The design team wants to call the gallery 'Collapse' and to open it with Easter Island — a society that, on their telling, felled its last tree and destroyed itself — as a warning about our own environmental trajectory. Two board members object. One says the label is a projection the evidence will not bear: that life on Rapa Nui continued long after the palms were gone and the real rupture came with European contact, and that when the Classic Maya cities of the southern lowlands emptied, the north went on building, so what ended was a particular political order rather than a people. The other replies that this proves too much, since when the interlinked Bronze Age palace economies of the eastern Mediterranean all stop within a generation or two, something real ended, and rebranding that as 'transformation' is evasion; a third member keeps insisting that what actually falls is complexity, as societies shed an expensive ruling layer that has stopped paying for itself. Please draft the memo I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action — keep the title and the Easter Island opening, reframe them, or approve with stated conditions — give reasons the design team can actually work with, engage the strongest point on the side you decide against, and tell the board how, in practice, to decide which past societies count as having collapsed.
