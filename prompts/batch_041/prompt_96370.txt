I chair the investment committee of a mid-sized charitable foundation, and at next month's meeting we have to decide the fate of our longest-serving external manager. His method has beaten our benchmark comfortably for twelve years; for the past eighteen months, however, the macro view on which it rests has been badly wrong, and we are well behind our peers. Half the committee wants to terminate: a manager who cannot adapt when conditions change, they say, was never skilled -- he was lucky, and the luck has run out. The other half wants to renew: no one foresaw the change, and firing him for an unforeseeable shift will only teach us to hire whoever did well last year. Both sides are now citing a clause in our investment policy requiring us to judge managers on the quality of their process 'independent of realized results', a clause the policy never defines.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote. Write as an expert in early modern political thought -- someone who knows Machiavelli's treatment of fortune and virtù closely and is used to writing for committees that must actually decide something by a deadline. Begin by thinking carefully about what Machiavelli holds: how much of human affairs fortune governs, what virtù consists in, and whether it is a durable quality of a person or rather a matter of a person's way of proceeding happening to match the times; note where his works pull against one another on this. Two committee members have circulated notes you should engage with directly rather than ignore. One argues that a man's method is fixed by his temperament and will necessarily fail once the world changes, so the only question worth asking is whether his method still fits the times. The other argues that since outcomes are largely luck, results should be ignored altogether and anyone who reasoned well in advance should be retained. Recommend a concrete course -- renew, terminate, or renew with conditions -- with a time frame, tell me what amendment I should propose to the process clause, and give the strongest argument on the losing side its due. No citations or literature review, please; I want your reading of the argument and a decision I can defend to the board.
