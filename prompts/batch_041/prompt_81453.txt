Me and my mates live in a big shared house — 40 of us now. We pool all our wages into one pot, nobody pays for anything individually, we just take what we need and put our names down for chores, and we vote on big stuff at a Sunday meeting. It works way better than you'd think, honestly.

Now two houses down the road want to merge with us, which would make us about 100 people across three buildings, plus a van and a shared workshop. My mate Dan, who does something with databases, says it'll be fine — we just need an app where everyone logs what they want and how much work they've done, and the app works out the fair ratios, and if something keeps running out you nudge the numbers up till it stops. He says shop prices are just a rigged version of that anyway.

I'm not so sure but I can't explain why. Two things bug me. Last month we had to pick between fixing the roof and getting a van, and the meeting ran three hours and the roof won basically because the roof lot had a slideshow, and afterwards five people told me privately they'd of rather had the van. And back when we tried a points system for chores, everyone started hoarding points and it turned into a whole drama, so we scrapped it.

Also nobody's really sure what the workshop should be doing. We vote on whatever anyone proposes, and its the same three people proposing everything.

Dan says that's all just small-group stuff and the app fixes it. Is he right? Should we go ahead and merge? What actually breaks when you go from 40 people who all know each other to 100 who don't? And are the prices in a normal shop doing something we're not doing at all — or are we basically already doing it and just not calling it that?
