I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar and we just wrapped the unit on teleosemantics — the family of views on which a mental state's content is fixed by what it was selected for, the job it was recruited to do for the organism — and I have to write a short explainer for the class wiki. Problem is I keep going in circles and I'm not even sure I've got the shape of the issue right. Teleosemantics seems to give you a clean story for a state selected to track one specific thing: it represents whatever it was selected to track. But real cases are messier, and my prof gave us a toy example that split the section. Picture an animal with a single internal state S. S is reliably triggered by a certain kind of ambient magnetic field. S was selected for because it causes the animal to burrow, and burrowing reliably gets the animal out of the way of a predator that happens to emit that same field. So S correlates with the field, was selected for the burrowing it causes, and the burrowing was selected for the predator-avoidance it achieves. Does S represent the field, the predator, the burrowing, or the escape? Half the room said teleosemantics just answers "whichever of those the mechanism was selected to track," which sounds circular, and the other half said the question is ill-posed. One guy kept insisting the real problem here is "distality" — whether content should be the near cause or the far cause of the state — but I couldn't tell if that's the same problem or a different one wearing a hat. Also there was a thing in the reading about needing to separate the part of the system that produces a representation from the part that consumes it, and I half-remember it being offered as the fix, but I couldn't reconstruct why it would help.

So what I want from you is an explanation of the underlying conceptual issue: why naturalizing content by appeal to selection leaves content underdetermined in cases like this, what exactly the underdetermination is (indeterminacy of the tracking relation? of the function? of the consumer's use?), and whether the standard teleosemantic moves — appealing to the consumer, to the selection history, to normal conditions — actually resolve it or just relocate it. Be honest at the end about whether you think teleosemantics can pin down content here or whether the toy case is a genuine counterexample, and say which. And please don't just recite the famous frog-and-fly example; I've heard it and it's not what's bugging me.
