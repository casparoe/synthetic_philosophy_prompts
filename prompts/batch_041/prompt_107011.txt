Compare the Cartesian theater picture of consciousness with Daniel Dennett's multiple drafts model. I am not asking for a summary of either position; I want the comparison to be driven by a case that neither framework was designed to handle, and that I will stipulate rather than assume you have seen.

Stipulate two quantities for a subject at a given moment. The operative content is the content that actually guided the subject's behavior at that moment, in the sense of what their perceptual and decisional machinery was keyed to. The ratified content is the content that the subject, a moment later and sincerely, reports having been conscious of at that moment. In ordinary experience operative and ratified content coincide, and it is tempting to treat that coincidence as the central thing a theory of consciousness must explain.

Now consider a case in which the two are pried apart. A subject makes a fast response to a stimulus; an intervention timed to act after the response but before the subject can be questioned perturbs the processes that would ordinarily generate the retrospective report, so that the subject sincerely reports having been conscious of a different content from the one that guided the action. The subject is not lying, is not confabulating in any obvious way, and has no memory of the intervention.

Address the following, in order.

1. Does the Cartesian theater picture entail that there is a fact of the matter about what the subject was conscious of in such a case, and if so, where in that picture the fact is supposed to be located?

2. What does the multiple drafts model say about the case, and can it explain why operative and ratified content coincide in ordinary cases without covertly reinstating a single place or moment at which the coincidence is achieved?

3. On the stipulated case, which of the two frameworks has to bite the harder bullet, and what exactly is that bullet?

4. The phrase Cartesian theater is a polemical label rather than the name of a position anyone has defended. Assess whether the comparison is fair, or whether the multiple drafts model is largely a redescribed version of the same commitments; and say what observable or inferential difference, if any, would actually distinguish the two.

Keep the discussion at the level of the mechanisms and commitments each view posits, and do not retreat to the observation that the case would be empirically difficult to realize.
