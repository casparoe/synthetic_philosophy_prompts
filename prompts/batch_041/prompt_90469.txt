I run a grad seminar on moral metaphysics and we spent alot of the term on the particularism/generalism fight, and it stalled in the worst way: nobody moved and everybody kept agreeing about the cases. The particularists say moral reasons are contributory all the way down -- a feature that counts in favour here can count against there, so no finite stock of true principles will capture how things go -- while the generalists keep replying that sure, but there are still true exceptionless generalizations, they're just hedged: fill in the rest of the circumstances and you get a principle that never fails. Which the particularists call cheating, since a principle that only holds once everything is specified is just a redescription of the case, or a summary of verdicts you'd already reached, not something doing any work.

My hunch, and I'd rather you push on it than agree with it, is that the two sides aren't really disagreeing about the moral facts or even about holism; they disagree about what a principle is for. One side needs a principle to codify the contribution a feature makes, the other only needs it to predict or license verdicts, and a hedged generalization can do the second without ever doing the first. A colleague adds a twist I can't decide about: she says a principle could be exceptionless and yet self-adjusting -- it assigns each feature a role that itself varies with context -- which would satisfy the generalists without ever handing the particularists the fixed contributions they say don't exist. Is that a genuine third position or a verbal trick?

So diagnose the impasse for me. Is it verbal, is it a disagreement about what counts as a principle (a role or function question rather than a metaphysical one), or is there a hidden substantive claim about reasons that one side is assuming without arguing for? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress -- a distinction, a case, a formal result about holism -- or should we expect none, because the two sides are definately answering different questions under one name?
