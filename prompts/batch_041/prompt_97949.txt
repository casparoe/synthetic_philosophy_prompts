1. I'm a retributivist about punishment — what justifies punishing someone is that they deserve it, and how much they deserve scales with how bad what they did was. Deterrence and rehab are fine as side effects but they're not the reason the state gets to punish anyone.
2. I'm against the death penalty, and honestley my main reason is the innocent-people problem: no system gets it right every time, and once you've executed someone you can't take it back. Prison you can at least apologise for and let them out.
3. I think life without parole is a completely adequate replacement. It gives murderers exactly what they deserve, so abolishing the death penalty costs us nothing — we're not letting anyone off.
4. I also think that if a convicted murderer escapes and is charging at someone with a knife, the police should absolutely shoot to kill. Obviously.
5. Remorse and rehabilitation shouldn't get anyone out early. What you deserve is fixed by what you did, not by who you've turned into since.
6. And I guess the honest version of (2) is that I'd support the death penalty in a really clear-cut case — if I could be certain only the guilty would ever be executed, I don't think I'd object to it.

Some of these feel like they obviously go together and some of them I'm less sure about, but I can't tell which.
