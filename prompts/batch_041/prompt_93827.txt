I have spent the past month drafting a charter for a small self-governing society — in effect a student cooperative with about two hundred members — and in the course of it I have been reading about self-amending rule systems: the sort of thing the game Nomic is built around, and the sort of thing constitutional drafters worry about in earnest. Before I put my design principles to the drafting committee, I want an audit of the principles themselves, because I am no longer confident that they all fit together. The operative core of the draft is short:

Article 1. Any article of this charter may be amended by a two-thirds vote of the members present at a general meeting, except where this charter provides otherwise.

Article 2. Article 1 may be amended only by a unanimous vote of all members.

Article 3. An amendment takes effect at the close of the meeting at which it is adopted, and governs nothing that happened before that moment.

And here is what I believe about charters of this kind:

(1) A provision has force in our society only if it was adopted in accordance with the rules of change in force at the time; nothing outside that chain of adoption confers legal force on anything.

(2) The rules of change are themselves articles of the charter and may be amended by the procedure they specify — the charter can change how it changes.

(3) Entrenchment is real. By demanding a supermajority for certain articles, a charter can genuinely tie the hands of future majorities, and that demand holds even when what is proposed is an amendment to the amendment procedure itself.

(4) The charter is flat. There is no hierarchy of higher and ordinary law, and no article lies beyond amendment by whatever procedure is in force when the amendment is moved.

(5) Each generation holds the constituent power to remake the charter from the ground up; no charter can bind a generation that has resolved to begin again.

I want to know whether this is a coherent package, and I cannot see where the fault line runs. Audit it. Separate the tensions that are genuine from those that dissolve once the terms are read carefully, rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each say which principle has to give and what the cheapest repair would be. Then tell me which single revision would remove the most trouble at the least cost, and what that revision would itself cost me.
