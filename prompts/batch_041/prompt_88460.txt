I chair the ethics commitee at our national transplant authority and I owe the board a written opinion before Monday, so I'd like you to draft it for me.

Here's the case. A private consortium wants to run a three-district pilot. Living kidney donors get no cash -- that's the headline, that's the selling point. What they get instead is a credit I'll call the Ledger, worth roughly the local price of a decent used car, spendable only on health care and only inside the donor's own district, where the consortium happens to run most of the clinics. The credit can be transfered once, so a donor can hand it to a relative. On top of that, any donor and their household go to the front of the transplant waitlist if they ever need a kidney. The recipients' insurers pay the consortium; the consortium pays the donors nothing in cash.

My committee is split. One side says this is organ sale with extra steps and a nicer font -- the poor sell, the rich buy, and calling the price a health credit doesn't change what it is. The other side says it's strictly better than the status quo: more kidneys on offer, no lump of cash for a husband or a landlord to take off them, and the donor walks away with care security instead of money that would be gone in a year. Meanwhile our regional office tells me people are already offering 60 cents on the dollar for unused Ledger credits in the district buy-and-sell chats, which I suspect matters a lot.

Give me the memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete start-or-kill date and conditions the board could actually monitor. Take the strongest version of whichever side you come down against, not a strawman. And tell me straight whether the district-only rule and the one-time transferability are features or bugs -- I genuinely can't tell, and one of my colleagues keeps insisting the indirection is the whole ballgame, though I can't work out if he means it's a fix or a fig leaf.
