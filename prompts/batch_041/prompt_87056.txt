I'm the junior judge on a three-judge panel and I have to write the bench memo for the case that's splitting us. Short version: the old Review Panel used to hear appeals from the Licensing Authority, and you needed the Panel's leave before you could go on to the courts. Two years ago Parliament abolished the Panel and handed its functions to a new Appeals Authority, but the schedule of consequental amendments missed the section of the old Act that says leave is granted by the Panel. So now the Authority says it has no power to grant leave, the Panel no longer exists, and an appeal can never get off the ground — and the text is more or less on its side. The appellant says no Parliament abolishes a right of appeal by accident, and that the scheme only makes sense if somebody can grant leave; one of my colleagues keeps muttering about access to a court. Where I'm stuck is that "the Authority has only the powers given to it" is also a real principle, and I don't want to conjure up a power just because the result looks silly. We also can't agree whether leave is a condition of the court's jurisdiction or merely a filter on the old Panel's caseload, and that seems to change the answer.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before we confer: recommend an outcome — allow, dismiss, or some third way — with the reasoning, lead with the bottom line, and give me the strongest version of whichever side I end up rejecting, because if that argument is good I want to know it before I'm in the room. No citations needed, just the reasoning. Max 800 words.
