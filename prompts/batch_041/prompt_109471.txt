His killer argument, according to this guy, is the growth-of-knowledge one — we can't predict what we'll know in the future, because to predict it we'd already have to know it, so any prophecy about the broad shape of the future is self-undermining. And then the punchline: since prediction is off the table, social science should stop trying to be like physics and settle for 'piecemeal engineering', just tinkering with small reforms; and the reason any of this matters is political, because historicism leads straight to totalitarianism — if you think you've found the law of history you'll force everyone to march to it. He also said this is why all the current AI-timeline forecasting is basically historicism in a new outfit.
