I'm a high school student in my school's after-school philosophy club, and our faculty adviser has asked me to grade a classmate's written answer for our unit on the norms of assertion. I've typed out the question and his answer below. Please give the answer a grade (a letter grade, or a mark out of 20 if you prefer) and then the point-by-point comments that justify it: say briefly what he gets right and why, and for anything wrong or unsupported, quote the exact sentence, explain what is wrong with it, and say what a correct treatment would look like. I need comments I can read back to him line by line, so please don't give me a general impression.

Question. Assertions are the speech acts we perform with declarative sentences such as "It is raining." A number of philosophers hold that assertion is governed by a constitutive norm — a condition a speaker must meet for the assertion to be proper, in something like the way a promise must be sincere to be proper. Four candidates are usually distinguished: the truth norm (assert only what is true), the belief norm (assert only what you believe), the justification norm (assert only what you have adequate evidence for), and the knowledge norm (assert only what you know).

(a) Explain how the truth norm and the knowledge norm differ, and why the knowledge norm is thought to account for the oddity of an assertion such as "The library is open, but I don't know that it is."

(b) Dr. Ravi has strong evidence, taken from a hospital roster that is reliable but that he does not realize has been superseded, that Nurse Okonkwo is on shift tonight. He asserts, "Nurse Okonkwo is on shift tonight." As it happens she is on shift, but only because another nurse called in sick and she was asked to cover. State what each of the four norms implies about whether Dr. Ravi's assertion was proper.

(c) Which of the four norms do you find most plausible, and what is the strongest objection to it?

Answer.

(a) The truth norm and the knowledge norm differ in what they demand of the speaker. The truth norm only requires that what is asserted be true; the knowledge norm requires that the speaker know it. So the knowledge norm is the stronger of the two, because knowledge requires truth, belief, and warrant, whereas the truth norm requires only truth. The knowledge norm is supposed to explain the oddity of "The library is open, but I don't know that it is" because anyone who asserts the first half is representing himself as knowing that the library is open, and then the second half says he does not, so the assertion as a whole is false and could not be properly made.

(b) On the truth norm, Dr. Ravi's assertion is proper, since Nurse Okonkwo really is on shift. On the belief norm it is proper, since he believes it. On the justification norm — which is sometimes called the belief norm, because what matters is the speaker's evidence — it is proper as well, since his evidence is strong and comes from a reliable source. On the knowledge norm it is not proper. His belief is true and well supported, but his evidence is the kind of evidence you can have while being wrong, which is exactly what has happened here, so he does not know, and his assertion is improper. The knowledge norm is therefore the only one of the four that rules the assertion out.

(c) I think the knowledge norm is the most plausible, because it explains why we retract an assertion as soon as we discover we didn't know, and why it is wrong to assert something on a hunch. The strongest objection is the case in (b): the knowledge norm says Dr. Ravi asserted improperly, but intuitively he did nothing wrong, since he had excellent evidence and no way of knowing the roster had been superseded. Since the norm's verdict in this case is counterintuitive, the norm must be false.
