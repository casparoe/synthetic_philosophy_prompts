Write me a short story, maybe 1500-2500 words, set inside an insurance company that pays out for emotional suffering. The whole business rests on whether people can accurately report their own mental states, so the company has built it's underwriting around an answer to that question, and the story should make that answer load-bearing rather than decorative. Two views are fighting inside the firm: the founder thinks introspection is basically perception — there's a signal and there's noise, the signal can be trained and calibrated, so a claim is a bit like a measurement; her head of claims thinks it's basically narration — that people assemble a plausible account of why they feel the way they do after the fact, and the account is mostly downstream of the feeling, not upstream of it. Neither should be a strawman. I want to feel the pull of both, and I want the plot to put real pressure on both rather than letting one win by fiat.

Some material to work with, use whatever serves the story: the familiar findings where people confidently explain choices they never actually made, or give reasons for their behavior that demonstrably werent the reasons; the difference between reporting a sensation you're having right now and reporting why you did something or what you really believe; the worry that if the only instrument for checking a report is the same instrument that produced it, "reliable" may not even be a property introspection can have; and the nastier possibility that a claims process which rewards vivid, detailed testimony ends up manufacturing the suffering it compensates — that saying it hurts like this, in this spot, is partly making it hurt like this, in that spot.

I'd like the story to end with the protagonist unable to say which of the two theories her own final report about her own state is evidence for. No lectures, no name-dropping philosophers, no scene where one character explains the philosophy of mind to another — keep the theory in the furniture of the world, in the forms, the training manuals, the payout schedules.
