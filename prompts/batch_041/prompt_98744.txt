So I run a small formal-epistemology group — three of us plus a PhD student starting in the fall — and we've got enough soft money to keep two of them going for about three years. We mostly work on probabilistic updating, and the thing that keeps pulling us back is uncertain evidence: what you actually do when what comes in isn't "E happened" but "here's a probability," or a likelihood, or a fuzzy reading off an instrument, and you want to fold it into your credences.

The textbook move is Jeffrey's — take a partition, shift your probabilities on the cells, hold the conditionals fixed (the rigidity thing), call it probability kinematics. Fine. But there's a pile of loose threads and I cant tell which are load-bearing and which are just puzzles people enjoy. Off the top of my head: the order-dependence worry (two soft updates can land you in different places depending on which you do first), whether the rigidity condition is a real constraint or just a convention, what to do when the uncertain evidence doesn't carve things into a clean partition, whether any of the accuracy-style or Dutch-book-style justifications actually reach the Jeffrey rule rather than only ordinary conditioning, and how any of this touches the old-evidence problem.

And then there's a fight inside the group that I suspect is the real fork. One of us keeps insisting the whole framing is a mistake — that "uncertain evidence" shouldn't be modeled as a constraint you impose on your credences at all, but as a noisy report with its own likelihood, in which case you just condition on the report (or on the fact that you received it) and Jeffrey conditionalization turns out to be a solution to a problem nobody actually has. The other one thinks that's just renaming the problem: now you owe us an account of where the report's likelihood comes from, whether it's the right kind of thing to condition on, and whether it stays fixed as your other credences move — at which point you're back where you started, just with extra steps.

So: if you had our three years, which open questions here would you actually put people on, and in what order? I don't want a survey — I want the reasoning. Which of these is upstream of which, which are tractable with tools we already have versus needing something new, which would change what the rest of the area does if it got settled, and which of the threads above (or ones I haven't listed) are overrated and should just be left alone. And be straight with me about whether the "it's just noisy evidence, condition on the report" move collapses the agenda or merely relocates it — if it relocates it, tell me where to, because that's probably where the postdocs should go.
