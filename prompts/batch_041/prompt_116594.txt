I spent 35 years in a museum basement doing taxonomy on deep-sea worms, and the great thing about that job is you can be completely wrong and the worms never find out. Since I retired I've been reading philosophy, mostly alone and mostly badly, and its turned me into worse company at dinner. Last month I hit Ian Hacking on psychiatric classification and the looping effects and I've been chewing on it ever since.

Here's my version of it, tell me if I've mangled it. When I move a worm into a new genus, the worm doesn't change. But when we put people into a category, a diagnosis, a type, the people can find out what the category says about them and start being it, or start arguing with it, and either way the thing we were classifying isn't the same thing anymore. So the category gets revised, and the revision goes back into the people, and round it goes. He calls the human ones interactive kinds and the worm ones indifferent kinds, or something like that. The part I keep turning over is that this doesn't make the diagnosis fake. The people are real, the trouble is real, it's just that naming the thing turns out to be part of what the thing is, so the target moves every time you name it.

Our reading group is doing him next Tuesday and I've been handed the opening ten minutes. I'd rather read a poem than stammer, so I want you to write me one, and I want the form to do the looping, not just the words.

So: a pantoum, four or five stanzas. As I understand the thing, lines keep coming back, the second and fourth lines of each stanza reappearing as the first and third of the next one, and the last stanza closing the circle onto the first, so the poem ends with the line it began with. That's already the right shape for this and I want you to lean on it hard. Here's what I'm asking for.

One person, given three labels over the course of the poem, one per go-round. Make the labels up. Please don't use real diagnoses, I don't want it turning into a list of real conditions, and I don't want the poem landing on either of the easy endings, that the labels are just power or that they're just discoveries. I read Hacking as saying something harder than both: the category is real, the person is real, and that's exactly why the naming changes what it names.

The repeats should be the loop. A line that starts out as somebody describing him from the outside, a clinician or a form or a school report, should come back later in his own mouth and mean something else, and by the time it comes back the last time it shouldn't be straightforwardly true of him anymore. Same words, different person. If the returning lines are just decoration, it isn't the poem I'm after.

And it has to work as a poem for people who haven't read Hacking. My group is four retired biologists and a former magistrate. If it only makes sense to someone who's read the paper, it's failed, and if it makes sense as a lecture with line breaks in it, it's failed worse.

Don't explain it to me afterwards, and don't put a title on it that gives the game away.
