I am working on a paper in population ethics and have become stuck on a foundational puzzle about infinite populations. The total view holds that a world's value is the sum of the utilities of all individuals in it. For finite populations this is unproblematic, but consider an infinite world W containing a countably infinite sequence of individuals whose utilities are 1, 2, 3, ... . Now consider W', which is exactly like W except that the first individual's utility is 0 rather than 1. The total view implies that W and W' are equally good, since both have infinite total utility, even though W is better for one person and worse for no one. This is the infinite total view problem. A natural repair is the finite improvement principle: W is better than W' if and only if there is a finite set of individuals for whom W is better and none for whom it is worse, with the two worlds identical for everyone else. But this principle has the implausible consequence that a world of infinitely many people at utility 1 is neither better nor worse than a world of infinitely many people at utility 2, because the difference is not confined to a finite set.

I would like you to make an argument for the position that we should replace the finite improvement principle with what I call the limiting-average principle (LAP). According to LAP, for any two infinite worlds, the world with the greater limit of the average utility of the first n individuals, as n tends to infinity, is better; if the limits are equal, the worlds are equally good. Your argument should address at least the following: (i) how LAP handles the original W versus W' case; (ii) what LAP implies for worlds whose average utilities fail to converge, such as an alternating sequence 1, 2, 1, 2, ...; (iii) whether LAP respects the Pareto principle, according to which if one world is better for some individuals and worse for none, it is better overall; and (iv) whether LAP implausibly implies that adding infinitely many people with low but positive utility makes a world worse. Please reach a clear verdict on whether LAP should be adopted and defend it against the strongest objections you can formulate.
