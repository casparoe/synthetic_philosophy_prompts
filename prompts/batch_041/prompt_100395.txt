Can an ant colony have a mind? I don't need the standard Hofstadter recap or the multiple-realizability point — I know them and they never seem to do the real work. What I want explained is the unity issue: supposing the colony is doing genuinely smart information processing, what exactly would have to be true for it to be one thinker rather than a pile of ants plus a chemical scratchpad? Here's the wrinkle I keep tripping on. The colony's memory is the pheromone trail network, which outlives every ant in the colony and gets continuously rebuilt by ants that weren't there when it was laid down — so if there's a mind, it's older than all of its parts and no part of it persists through the whole thing. Does that make the one-mind claim easier or harder? And a second wrinkle: a parasite infects a small share of the ants and makes them act on their own account — at what point, if ever, would we say the colony's mind has split, or that there never was one? Reason it through rather than giving me a verdict.
