ok so i'm in my school's philosophy club — about 12 of us, we meet for 50 minutes on wednesdays after school, ages 15 to 17, mostly there for fun, nobody's taking a class on this. next week it's my turn to run the session and i want to do it on dispositions, like when you say a glass is fragile or a match is flammable or sugar is soluble. my older sister is doing philosophy at uni and she told me the standard story first: that all these claims just mean a conditional, roughly "if you did the trigger thing to it, it would do the manifestation thing" — strike the match, it lights; drop the glass, it shatters. and then she told me the cases that supposedly break that story and honestly they broke my brain a bit, in a good way.

the first is the fink: something that makes the object lose the disposition at the exact moment you trigger it. so the match is genuinely flammable the whole time it's sitting there, but the second you go to strike it, some gizmo soaks it or a heat-shield appears and it won't light. the disposition was real before the trigger, but the conditional comes out false, because if you struck it, it wouldn't light.

the second is the mask: something that blocks the manifestation without the disposition going away at all. the glass is still fragile, but it's wrapped in foam, so dropping it does nothing. again the conditional is false but the disposition is still there.

so here's what i want from you. act like a philosopher who actually works on dispositions and causation, and also someone who has run a lot of hands-on workshops for teenagers and knows what flops. think carefully, and before you give me the final thing please do a short brainstorming section where you list a few different activity ideas and say why you're picking the one you pick.
