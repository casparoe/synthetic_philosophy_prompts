I am assembling a case bank for work on the situationist critique of virtue ethics, and I need one case designed to a specification rather than recalled. The target is a specific gap: the situationist critique, and much empirical work on character that follows it, treats cross-situational consistency of behavior as the observable mark of a trait. But it is not obvious that consistency — even consistency in genuinely good conduct — is sufficient for virtue. I want a case that isolates that gap by holding everything else fixed, so that the disagreement turns on one feature rather than on a general clash of outlooks.

Build a single scenario meeting all of the following specifications.

1. It contains a person whose conduct in one morally relevant respect is highly consistent across a broad range of ordinary situations — consistent enough that no behavioral test applied to those situations would separate them from someone who is genuinely virtuous in that respect.

2. That consistency must be sustained by a standing policy the person carries with them, not by an environmental cue. This matters: if the regularity lives in the situation, the situationist can claim it without argument and the case loses its point.

3. The person must nevertheless lack practical wisdom, in these concrete senses: they cannot articulate why the policy matters or what it is for; they apply it rigidly, without tracking the morally relevant features of the situations they meet; and they would apply it even where doing so produces a bad result. The rigidity must be psychologically mundane — the way most people follow rules they absorbed rather than reasoned out — not a disorder.

4. The conduct the policy yields must be good, not merely conventional or self-serving. Choose one of the following: keeping promises, returning what is borrowed, or coming to the aid of someone in need.

5. The person is otherwise unremarkable: no psychopathology, no brain injury, no hypnosis, drugs, coercion, or mind control, and no role or vocation treated as a special exemption from the question. The setting is ordinary — workplace, neighborhood, family, school.

6. Do not recycle the familiar demonstrations (the seminary study in which students in a hurry failed to help, the found-coin helping study, the obedience and prison experiments). The regularity should be one an ordinary reader would recognize from their own life.

7. Identify at least one situation in which the rigid policy and a practically wise response would diverge, and confirm that this situation falls outside the set over which the behavioral consistency was established — so that the behavioral data really are indistinguishable while the characterological difference is real.

After the scenario, give me a JSON object with exactly these fields.

- "scenario": one paragraph of prose.
- "virtue_targeted": the virtue in question, in a phrase.
- "sampled_situations": array of strings, the situations across which the conduct is consistent.
- "standing_policy": the internal regularity, stated as the agent would state it.
- "why_internal": why the regularity belongs to the agent rather than to the environment.
- "phronesis_profile": object with fields "articulacy", "exception_sensitivity", "outcome_sensitivity", each a short string giving the evidence for the deficit.
- "divergence_situation": the situation where policy and practical wisdom part ways.
- "divergence_outside_sample": boolean.
- "verdicts": array of exactly three objects, each with "position", "verdict" ("counts as virtue" or "does not count"), and "reasoning". The positions are: virtue ethics read as requiring only a stable disposition to good action; virtue ethics read as requiring practical wisdom; and the situationist critique.
- "load_bearing_feature": the single feature of the case on which the disagreement turns.
- "removal_tests": array of exactly three objects with "change", "predicted_effect", and "which_verdict_moves". The three changes are: make the agent practically wise; make the policy track morally relevant features; relocate the regularity into the environment.
- "defender_reply": object with "position" (whichever fares worst), "strongest_reply", and "assessment" (whether it succeeds).
- "specification_check": array of objects with "specification" (referring to the numbered items above), "met" (boolean), and "how".
- "cheating_check": whether the verdicts are generated by the positions themselves or merely stipulated by the scenario's construction.
- "bottom_line": what the case establishes, how far it generalizes, and what further case would be needed to extend it.

The scenario must earn its verdicts rather than assume them; if you find that you can only get the three positions to disagree by building the disagreement into the description of the agent, say so plainly in "cheating_check" rather than papering over it.
