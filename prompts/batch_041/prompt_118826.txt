My position, roughly: "collapse" is mostly a bad concept and we should mostly stop using it. What actually ends in these cases is a particular kind of elite political order — palace economies, divine kingship, monumental building programs, scribal classes — while most people stay put or move a bit and reorganize. The southern Maya lowlands depopulate but the northern Yucatan is thriving at the same time; the Late Bronze Age palace system comes apart around 1200 BC while village life, farming, and a lot of the trade keeps going; Rapa Nui's actual demographic catastrophe comes after European contact, not before, and the population may never have been as big as the ecocide story needs it to be. So transformation, fragmentation, reorganization: yes. Collapse: only when there's a genuine demographic catastrophe nobody recovers from. And honestly I think a lot of the collapse genre is us telling our own anxiety story with somebody else's ruins — which is not a neutral observation here, because our region has had three bad fire seasons in a row and the whole town is in a "are we next" mood, which is partly why the museum picked this theme and why the room will be full.

The counterexample that genuinely worries me is Norse Greenland, where the colony just ends, and I don't have a clean way to handle it.
