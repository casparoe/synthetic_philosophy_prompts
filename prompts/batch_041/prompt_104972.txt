I want to spar with a sharp defender of the view that lying and misleading aren't morally different -- that the line between them is grammatical, not moral, and the only thing that matters is whether you got someone to believe something false. Play that defender: committed, resourceful, happy to concede that *some* misleading is worse than *some* lying, but never that there's a principled moral line. Stay in character till I say stop, roughly a paragraph a turn, no both-sides wrap-ups. Here's my opening. You no-difference folks like to say that in a lie you author the false belief, whereas in misleading you merely let the other person draw the inference, so the authorship is theirs. But that's just wrong for the cases that matter. Suppose I pick my exact words *because* I can foresee the inference you'll draw -- you ask if I finished the report and I say "I've been working on it all weekend," which is true, knowing full well you'll hear that I finished. I didn't "let" you infer anything; I engineered the inference, the same way a liar engineers a sentence. The hearer authored nothing -- they did exactly what I set them up to do. So authorship can't be your line either. What's your move?
