I'm a novelist — literary fiction, and I've been reading a lot of philosophy of mind for a book I'm working on, about a man whose memory of one particular afternoon keeps quietly rewriting itself. Along the way I've accumulated a pile of opinions and I honestly can't tell anymore whether they're a coherent package or whether I'm just fooling myself. So here's where I've landed, and I want you to audit it:

1. Physicalism. Experience is brain stuff, full stop. No ghost, and specifically no inner theater with a little homunculus sitting in the dark watching a screen — I'm allergic to that picture, it just relocates the problem.

2. There's a fact of the matter about when an experience happens. A flash of red, a taste of tin, a sting — each of them occurs at some time or other, relative to the world and to the other experiences. It might be very hard to know, but it isn't indeterminate.

3. The color phi thing is real, and it's a revision, not a lie the subject tells afterward. You flash one dot, then a second dot somewhere else in a different color, and what the person actually sees is a single dot travelling and changing color, with the color change seeming to happen before the second dot even comes on. I think that filled-in color is genuinely experienced. It's not confabulation, it's experience.

4. The Orwellian vs Stalinesque fork is a real fork, not just two ways of talking about the same thing. I think it genuinely matters whether the fix-up happens before the experience — the show trial staged before the audience is let in — or after it, with the memory getting rewritten like a doctored photograph. One of those is the truth, even if we can't run the experiment that would tell us which.

5. The specious present is real. Experience isn't instantaneous, it's smeared over a short window, a couple hundred milliseconds or so, which is why things that didn't literally happen at the same time can still be experienced together.

6. And I get very itchy around any claim that there are facts about experience that no possible experiment could ever, even in principle, distinguish. That smells like metaphysics rather than science, and I don't want it leaking into my book.

Do these hang together? I have a nagging feeling that some of them are pulling against each other and I can't tell which ones are actually in conflict and which only look like they are. So don't just flag everything — tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which one I can stop worrying about, how bad each real tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me. If there's one single revision that would clean up the most, tell me that one.
