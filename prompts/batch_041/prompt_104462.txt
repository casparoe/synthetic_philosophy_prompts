Over dinner last night a friend and I argued for the better part of three hours about whether a table is a real thing, and I have woken up convinced that the disagreement is more confused than either of us realized.

Her position is that there are no composite objects at all: strictly speaking, no tables, no cats, no cells — only fundamental particles arranged in various ways. She insists she is not denying anything I can see or touch. Everything I might want to say about the table — that it is brown, that it is heavy, that it is where I left my keys — she says she can say instead about the particles being arranged tablewise, without ever admitting an entity over and above the particles. I told her this was a funny way of talking and that she was welcome to it. She turned the objection around: if she is merely choosing a convenient vocabulary, she said, then notice that particle and table are on exactly the same footing, since a particle is itself only a way that something more basic is arranged, and arrangement is only ever arrangement according to some description we have chosen to care about. On her view, my commonsense belief in tables is not a belief about the world at all, but a preference about how to describe it.

That is the part I cannot get past. Suppose she is right that no possible observation could ever distinguish a world containing tables from a world containing only tablewise-arranged particles — that every prediction, every measurement, and every practical consequence comes out the same. It seems to me to follow immediately that whenever two ways of describing the world agree on everything we could ever observe, there simply is no fact of the matter as to which of them is correct; and if that is so, then the question between us is not a question about the world but a question about words, and we were wrong to spend three hours on it. Yet I cannot accept that either. If the question were merely verbal, it is hard to see why anyone would care enough to write books about it, or why my friend, otherwise the most hard-headed person I know, would stake so much on it.

So: what kind of question is this? Is there some third possibility, between settled by evidence and merely a matter of how we choose to speak, that I am missing? And if there is, can you say in plain terms what my friend and I are actually disagreeing about, given that no experience could ever tell us which of us is right?
