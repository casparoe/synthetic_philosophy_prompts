Next month I'm giving a job talk defending this: the interesting cases of incommensurable values arent the synchronic ones everyone argues about — two options that can't be ranked, or that turn out to be roughly equal — but the diachronic ones, where the ranking doesn't exist until the choice brings it into being. The case I build it on is someone choosing between a job in another city and staying near a parent they care for; my claim is that which one turns out to matter more is partly made true by which one they commit to, so the choice isn't being measured against a value fact that was already sitting there, and yet it isn't arational either, because afterwards they can be right or wrong about what they made.
