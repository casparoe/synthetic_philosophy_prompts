There's this whole debate about whether technical artifacts can themselves be political — the classic example being those low bridges that kept buses (and so poor folks) off a fancy beach. I want you to take a hard line: argue that an artifact is *never* political in itself, and that all the politics lives in how it's designed, funded, deployed, and in what the alternatives were. To make it concrete, picture a city rolling out automated traffic-enforcement cameras that in practice only get installed in dense low-income neighborhoods, because the vendor's computer-vision models happen to work best on that kind of street layout. Everyone calls the cameras "racist by design." Argue that's the wrong description — that nothing about the camera is intrinsically political and the whole thing is a story about deployment choices and incentives. Then tell me honestly where your own argument is weakest.
