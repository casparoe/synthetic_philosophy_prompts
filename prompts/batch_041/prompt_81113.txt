I'm building up a thing on conscription and I keep getting stuck on the Swiss and Nordic cases, so I want you to do the reading for me. Here's the setup. Start from the standard economic story about the draft, the one Walter Oi was pushing in the sixties: a draft is a tax paid in kind, because the state takes a year of someone's labour at well below what they could earn elsewhere, so the real cost isn't the army's wage bill but the wages those people never earned, which never appears in any budget line. Follow that through and the richer a country gets, the worse the deal looks, and the more it should switch to a professional force paid market wages. Sweden did exactly that — paused the draft in 2010, went gender-neutral, all-volunteer — and then brought it back in 2017, calling up about 4,000 a year out of a cohort of roughly 100,000, picked from a pool of about 13,000. Finland never stopped at all: about 27,000 a year, with the large majority of men completing service. Switzerland runs a militia on the same premise, and in 2013 the electorate rejected abolishing conscription by about 73 to 27; men found unfit don't get off the hook either, they pay an extra 3% of income tax until they're 37, and anyone doing civilian service instead of military serves half again as long. All three are armed neutrals, which I suspect isn't a coincidence.

So: read that episode through the tax lens and tell me what it gets and what it misses. Who's actually paying, what are the base and the rate, what does the theory predict about the 2010 pause, the 2017 reversal and the Swiss vote, and where does it go quiet? Is conscription doing something the tax frame can't see — a commitment device for neutrality, a way of legitimating the army in the eyes of the people it defends, a way of selecting people rather than levying them, or just a claim about who ought to share the risk? And what does that do to the theory — refute it, or just mark where it runs out?

Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no preamble and no summing up at the end. Point 1 is the mapping, point 2 is what the theory nails, point 3 is where it breaks, point 4 is the rival reading of whatever looks anomalous, point 5 is what all this does to the theory and what evidence would settle it.
