I'm a TA for a weird cross-listed seminar (philosophy plus religious studies) on karma, and I keep noticing that everyone in the room means something different by moral desert. I want to make a one-page diagnostic decision tree that takes a student from a few concrete commitments about how actions ripen to a named position on whether karma is a theory of moral desert, and I need it to sort by what they actually think about cases rather than by which tradition they identify with. The audience is undergrads who know the rough idea of karma but not the technical literature, so every branch has to be answerable without already knowing the label it leads to. Please make it 8-10 branching questions and 6-8 leaves, and seed the tree with these distinctions: (1) is the karmic result intrinsic to the act, like a seed ripening, or assigned by something external, like a judge or a ledger-keeper or a cosmic algorithm? (2) does desert require a persisting self who owns the deed and later receives the fruit, or can it work with a stream of causally connected but non-identical moments? (3) is the relevant desert claim about fittingness, meaning suffering is appropriate to the deed, or entitlement, meaning the agent has a claim to a particular outcome? (4) does proportionality matter, and if so is it strict or rough? (5) can karma be interrupted by grace, forgiveness, insight, or ritual, or is it exceptionless? (6) is rebirth literal, metaphorical, or irrelevant to the theory? (7) is the point of karma explanatory, soteriological, or social? (8) does the view allow collective or structural karma, or is it strictly individual? Each node should be a concrete case or a claim someone could answer yes, no, or more-or-less to, not are you a Buddhist or do you believe in the ledger model.

When you get to the leaves, name the position and give one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I suspect some combinations will be empty, like strict proportionality plus no persisting self plus literal rebirth plus no grace, and I want you to flag which ones are empty and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate, rather than pretending a question does the work. Start with the tree itself, then a short notes section; no preamble, no restating my question. I'm less interested in whether the names match real schools than in whether the tree tracks the logical joints, so feel free to invent labels if the standard ones are too blunt, and don't worry about citing anything.
