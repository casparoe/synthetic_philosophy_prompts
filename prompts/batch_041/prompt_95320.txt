Please explain the conceptual issue at the heart of the surprise exam paradox. The set-up: a teacher announces that there will be exactly one exam in the coming week, on one of five days, and that the class will not know on the previous evening which day it is. The class reasons backward — the exam cannot fall on the last day, because on the previous evening they would know it was the only remaining day; so it cannot fall on the second-to-last day either, and so on — and concludes that no such exam can be given at all. Yet the teacher can set the exam for the third day and it will genuinely surprise them. I do not want a survey of proposed resolutions; I want the diagnosis. Identify the precise step at which the class's reasoning fails, and explain why the announcement is not simply inconsistent, given that the class's own conclusion is part of what makes a later exam surprising. Then consider a weakened version, in which the class is not certain that an exam will be given at all but leaves open some chance that the teacher never sets one, and say whether the backward induction still gets off the ground and whether the paradox is thereby dissolved or merely relocated.
