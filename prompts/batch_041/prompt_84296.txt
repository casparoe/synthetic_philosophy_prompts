I'm giving a paper at our mind workshop in three weeks time and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The thesis: the knowledge argument is sound but almost everyone draws the wrong conclusion from it. What Mary gains when she finally sees red isn't a fact about a non-physical property — it's the capacity to occupy a descriptive frame rather than describe from outside one. My claim is that a complete physical description is only complete relative to a chosen frame, and phenomenal knowledge is what you get by being inside the frame instead of looking at it. So the argument doesn't refute physicalism, it refutes the dream of a perspective-free description — and the people who say she only gains a new way of thinking about a fact she already had are right about the psychology and wrong to think thats a deflation, since on my view there's no further question about whether the frame latches onto anything.

The audience is small and I know who's coming: two physicalists who work on the new-guise move and will say my inside/outside distinction is a difference without a difference; a philosopher of science who's allergic to anything that smells like qualia and will attack the word frame as undefined; a colour vision scientist who thinks the Mary setup is physically impossible, so the thought experiment is broken at the input; a Kantian in the chair who'll want to know why my frame isn't just the transcendental subject in new clothes; and a first-year who's a naive realist about colour and will ask the simple hard question. Give me the objections ordered by how much damage they do, in the vocabulary each of those people would use, a two-minute reply for each with a note on where the reply is soft, plus what I should concede up front and what I should hold. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
