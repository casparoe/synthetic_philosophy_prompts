I am a high school student applying to a summer seminar in philosophy, and the application requires a one-page research proposal. I would like to write mine on the White Horse Dialogue attributed to Gongsun Long, the Warring States logician associated with the School of Names, and I have a half-formed idea that I would like turned into a proper proposal.

The dialogue is normally read as a dispute about kinds or classes, turning on the ambiguity of the copula in the claim it defends, which can be taken to mean either that a white horse is not identical with the kind horse or that it is not included in that kind. My suspicion is that this framing misses what several of the arguments are actually doing. One of them turns on seeking: a person who seeks a horse will accept a brown or black one, whereas a person who seeks a white horse will not, and the speaker treats this as establishing that seeking a horse is not the same as seeking a white horse. Another argument separates the term that designates shape from the term that designates colour. I want to suggest that the unifying thought concerns substitution: a term can fail to stand in for another term within certain contexts — contexts such as seeking, wanting, or asking for — even when the two terms are related as kind and sub-kind, so that the dialogue is an investigation of how names behave inside such contexts rather than a claim about what there is.

Please write the proposal. It should state the interpretive question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the class-based reading does not already settle it; describe what I would actually do (reconstruct each argument, test whether the substitution reading licenses it without extra assumptions, and determine whether the later stretch concerning coloured and uncoloured horses fits the same reading or forces a second, separate thesis); say what a convincing result would look like and what would count as the interpretation failing; and give a realistic assessment of scope, given that this is a one-page proposal by a student who reads the text only in translation. There is no need for citations; I will supply the translations and commentaries myself.
