I'm a high school student, and the only formal vocabulary I have for probability comes from AP Statistics, which I just finished and did well in. In that vocabulary, probability is a number attached to an event: a long-run relative frequency when we are being frequentist, a personal degree of belief when we are being Bayesian, with sample spaces, independence, conditional probability, priors and posteriors, expected value, confidence intervals, and hypothesis tests as the working tools. I am comfortable with all of that and with nothing more advanced.

For a history-of-ideas project I have been reading about Keynes's early work on probability, and I cannot get it to fit that vocabulary at all. As I understand it, he treated probability as a strictly logical relation between a body of evidence and a hypothesis, a kind of partial implication, rather than as a frequency in the world or a degree of belief in someone's head. He also thought probability attaches to propositions rather than to events. He held that not every pair of probabilities can be ranked as greater, less, or equal, so that probabilities form a partial order rather than a single scale, and he used the example of deciding whether to take an umbrella when the barometer and the clouds disagree. And he separated the probability of a conclusion from what he called the weight of the argument for it, which grows as relevant evidence accumulates even when the new evidence lowers the probability. In his later economic writing he added a further claim, that some matters of real consequence are not merely improbable but have no numerical probability at all.

Please restate this position as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually have — events, sample spaces, relative frequencies, conditional probability, priors and posteriors, expected value, sampling distributions, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests — and then give me the residue report. Which of his claims translate cleanly? Which translate only with distortion, and what exactly gets distorted in the process? Which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? For that last group, I want your judgment on whether the failure is a defect of my vocabulary, something AP Statistics simply has no room to say, or instead evidence that Keynes was being unclear, or drawing a distinction that does not earn its keep.

I would much rather have an honest translation than a flattering one, so please do not quietly import the parts of his view that my vocabulary cannot express and then present the result as a translation. And if the task as I have posed it is confused — for instance, if translating a relation between propositions into a vocabulary built for events is not merely difficult but impossible, so that the residue is the whole thing — tell me so plainly instead of producing a translation anyway.
