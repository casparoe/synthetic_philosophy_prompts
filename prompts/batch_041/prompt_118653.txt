I'm a novelist, so I notice these things in charcter and usually just fix them by feel, but this one has me stuck. A philosopher friend handed me a bit of the metaphysics of dispositions — the idea that something can have a trait even when the trait never shows itself — and then the three ways that can go wrong: a fink, where the very thing that ought to trigger the trait destroys the trait instead; a mask, where something external just blocks the showing while the trait sits there perfectly intact; and a mimic, where the behaviour turns up with no trait behind it at all. Now read Lord Jim through that. Jim jumps from the Patna, and then spends the rest of the book being brave in situations where any of us would run, and the whole novel is Marlow circling the question of what Jim actually is. So which is it? Was the jump a mask (the fog, the panic, the fact that the ship was'nt going down anyway), a fink (the situation was such that his courage, if he had any, was destroyed by the very thing that would have revealed it), or the real thing? And the Jim of Patusan — is that the same disposition finally manifesting, or is it a mimic Jim built afterwards to cover the first Jim, and does Conrad let us tell the difference? I want the reading to take the metaphysics seriously as metaphysics and the novel seriously as a novel, not just use one to illustrate the other, and I especially want you to say where the novel knows something the disposition talk doesn't — my suspicion is that it's about time, that a person can spend a life trying to make a past failure retroactively untrue, and that the dispositionalist picture has no room for that. No reading lists please, just the reading.
