I am preparing a note for a Bayesian epistemology reading group. A colleague has constructed the following argument, which appears to show that Jeffrey conditionalization is not a genuine rule for uncertain evidence. I cannot determine whether the argument is sound or where it fails. The argument, with steps numbered, is as follows.

(1) Jeffrey conditionalization says that when evidence changes the probabilities of a partition {E_i} from p_i to q_i, the new probability of any proposition A is the weighted average Σ_i q_i P(A | E_i). The conditional probabilities P(A | E_i) are held fixed.

(2) Consider an agent with initial probabilities P(E) = 0.5, P(A | E) = 0.9, and P(A | ¬E) = 0.1. The agent then receives an uncertain test result that leaves the probability of E exactly where it was, at 0.5, but bears on how likely A is conditional on E and on ¬E. Suppose the result would justify P(A | E) = 0.95 and P(A | ¬E) = 0.2.

(3) If the relevant partition is {E, ¬E}, Jeffrey conditionalization yields P'(A) = 0.5 × 0.9 + 0.5 × 0.1 = 0.5. The test result therefore leaves P(A) unchanged, even though it is intuitively relevant to A.

(4) The only way to make the result change P(A) is to use a finer partition, such as {E ∧ A, E ∧ ¬A, ¬E ∧ A, ¬E ∧ ¬A}. But then the posterior depends on which partition the agent selects. With the finer partition one can obtain P'(A) = 0.5 × 0.95 + 0.5 × 0.2 = 0.575; with the coarser partition one obtains 0.5. The same total evidence, differently carved, yields different posteriors.

(5) A rule of updating that returns different answers depending on how the agent carves the possibility space is not a determinate rule. A rule that is not determinate cannot be a rational requirement.

(6) Therefore Jeffrey conditionalization is not a genuine rule for uncertain evidence. At best it is a schema that requires an additional, unspecified theory of which partition the evidence concerns.

My colleague takes this to prove too much: if the argument is correct, every Bayesian who uses Jeffrey conditionalization is committed to an arbitrary choice of partition, and the standard treatment of uncertain evidence is unmotivated. I am inclined to think that something has gone wrong, perhaps in the transition from (3) to (4) or in the assumption that the same total evidence can be represented equally well by either partition. But I cannot articulate the flaw, and I do not know what the repair would cost.

Please tell me which step or premise I should reject, and what rejecting it costs. If you think the argument is valid as it stands, say which premise is least costly to give up and why. In your answer, distinguish what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on Bayesian updating; do not present a contested interpretation of Jeffrey conditionalization as the consensus. You may use a concrete numerical example, but do not assume that I have access to any particular paper or textbook.
