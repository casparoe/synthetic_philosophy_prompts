I am a hardware engineer who has spent the last three years designing low-power memory for a reversible-computing startup, and I have recently begun sitting in on a philosophy of physics seminar. Maxwell's demon has stopped being a curiosity for me and become something closer to a workplace argument. The seminar has split into two camps that talk past each other, and I have been asked to produce the document the two of them should have written together.

The two camps are these. The first holds that the demon is exorcised precisely because information processing is physical: the demon must record what it learns, its memory is a finite physical system, and Landauer's principle — that resetting a bit to a standard state costs at least kT ln 2 of dissipation — supplies the missing term that closes the ledger. On this view the resolution of the demon is genuinely information-theoretic, and the physics of information is doing indispensable work. The second holds that nothing is added by talking about information at all: ordinary statistical mechanics, applied honestly to the demon together with its memory, already saves the second law, and Landauer's principle is a derived theorem about particular models rather than an independent law. On this view the information-theoretic story is at best a convenient bookkeeping device and at worst a source of smuggled assumptions.

Write the joint statement these two camps could both sign. I want:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims — about the formalism; about what any demon must possess (a physical memory, finite capacity, either a reset of some kind or a bounded run); about which models do and do not demonstrate a violation of the second law; and about what the dispute is not about, since neither side doubts that the second law survives.
2. A table of the residual disagreements, each side's position phrased so that side would accept the wording as its own.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp — and, for each crux, the argument or case both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance.

Include two cases in particular, because we argued them out in the seminar and they are why I do not think this can be answered from a textbook:

(a) the one-shot demon — a demon whose memory is a fixed finite register that is never reset, running for some bounded number of cycles and then stopping. Does anything in either camp's accounting require the reset, and if not, where does the entropy go?

(b) the handed-side demon — a demon that performs no measurement at all, because the particle's side is already pre-correlated with a bit sitting in its memory. If no measurement and no erasure occur, does either camp's exorcism still apply, and what does each camp predict?

Finally, I do not want a neutral survey. After the joint statement, come down on one side: say which of the residual disagreements you judge to be merely verbal, and why, and for the ones that are not, tell me which camp you would bet on and what moved you. Give your reasons, and do not leave the choice to me.
