A colleague of mine is completing a book defending panpsychism in its micro form: every fundamental physical entity has a rudimentary experience of its own. I will call these entities experiential atoms, and I will call a subject composite when it is constituted by many atoms rather than being one of them. Her central problem is the familiar one: if each atom is already a little subject, by what step do a great many of them come to constitute a single subject such as you or me? She proceeds by cases, and she has already committed herself in print to the following verdicts.

(i) Two experiential atoms that never interact and share no causal history: no composite subject. There are simply the two atoms, each with its own experience.

(ii) A system with the organization of a mammalian brain — dense recurrent exchange among its parts, a global workspace whose activity is broadcast to all of them, and self-maintenance of the whole — built from experiential atoms whose micro-qualities span a rich palette: one composite subject, with an experience that is rich and not merely the parts' experiences laid side by side.

(iii) A system with exactly the organization of (ii), but assembled from parts she has argued are not experiential atoms at all — components with the same causal profile, which she calls mere dispositions: no composite subject.

(iv) A system whose parts are experiential atoms and whose global activity is an externally imposed, non-self-maintaining loop, behaving as a single thing but driven from outside and repairing nothing: no composite subject.

She must now add one more case and has asked me to predict what she is committed to. The new case: a system with the organization of (ii) — the same recurrent exchange, the same global workspace, the same self-maintenance — built entirely from experiential atoms, but atoms whose palette is extremely thin. Each atom's experience is exhausted by one of two determinate qualities, say a dull ache and a faint warmth, and those two are the whole of the system's experiential material. Externally the system behaves like an ordinary animal, with a rich discriminative and behavioral repertoire.

Please tell me: (a) what she will say about the new case; (b) the candidate rules that fit her four earlier verdicts, and what each of them predicts about the new case; (c) which rule you would bet she is running, and why; (d) the further case that would best tell the surviving rules apart; and (e) briefly, what her preferred rule commits her to in the mirror-image direction, where one fundamental subject is supposed to divide into many, as in the view that the cosmos itself is the single basic subject. The verdict itself matters less to me than which commitments her track record forces and which it leaves open.
