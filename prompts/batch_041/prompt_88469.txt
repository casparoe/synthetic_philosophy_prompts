I'm in a small reading group on the history of economic thought, and over the past year we've split into two camps over Malthus to the point where we argue past each other rather than with each other. Camp A holds that the Malthusian core -- population tending to press against subsistence, with the arithmetic/geometric contrast doing the predictive work -- was a forecast, that it has been refuted in the very places and the very centuries Malthus was writing about, and that every move now used to save it (stretching the time horizon, changing the unit of analysis from nation to globe, pointing to the mechanism rather than the forecast, invoking moral restraint or the demographic transition) is an ad hoc rescue that immunizes the theory from evidence; on this view "right but early" is a euphemism for unfalsifiable. Camp B holds that what Malthus offered was a conditional, long-run account of a mechanism, that the local and temporary escapes are precisely what the theory expects once its auxiliary conditions change, and that "right but early" names the ordinary fate of any theory whose timescale outlives its author's critics; on this view the refutations are real but they refute the auxiliaries, not the core. We have been unable to settle even what we are disagreeing about, and I suspect at least part of the fight is about how to individuate a theory across its editions and re-statements rather than about the historical record itself.

I'd like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two camps -- the document we should have produced a year ago. I want, first, a numbered list of claims both camps can actually sign: what the Malthusian thesis asserts and does not assert; what the historical record does and does not show; what would count as vindication and what as refutation; and what the logical status of a timing clause is in a long-run prediction. Second, the residual disagreements, each stated so that the camp whose position it is would accept the wording as a fair rendering of its own view -- no strawmen, no loaded verbs like "rescue" or "dodge" in the neutral column. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side, and for each, the historical episode, argument, or hypothetical case that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance and a note on whether the crux is empirical, conceptual, or merely verbal. I expect the deepest crux to be the general one the domain raises -- whether a theory can be right but early at all, and what distinguishes a progressive sharpening of a theory from a degenerating rescue -- and I want that handled head-on rather than deferred, including the possibility that Camp A and Camp B are defending different objects (a forecasting rule versus a mechanism) that were never in competition. Please don't give me reading lists, citations, or references to the secondary literature; I want the reasoning and the formulations, not a bibliography.

Format it as a prose joint statement with the numbered agreed-claims section, followed by a disagreements table with these columns: disagreement_id; plain_statement_of_the_dispute (neutral, no camp-specific vocabulary); camp_a_position_wording; camp_b_position_wording; crux_id; partly_verbal (yes / no / partly). Then close with a single JSON object, `crux_register`, an array under the key `cruxes`, each element having the fields: crux_id; crux_question; crux_type (one of "empirical", "conceptual", "verbal"); why_it_would_move_a_side; shared_case_or_evidence (the episode or argument both camps accept as probative); camp_a_prediction; camp_b_prediction; observation_that_would_settle_it; and currently_decidable (true or false).
