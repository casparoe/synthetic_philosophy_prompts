I teach a 90-minute session on distributive justice to a seminar of about 18 upper-level undergraduates, roughly half philosophy and half economics, and I want to open the unit on luck egalitarianism with a staged allocation game rather than a reading. The aim is for the group to feel the view's internal tension from the inside: the pull of the thought that no one should end up worse off through brute bad luck, and then the pull of the thought that the same principle requires them to sit in judgment on whether a particular person's misfortune was her own doing. I want them to run into the second pull while they are still committed to the first, and I want them to hear neither the name of the theory nor the brute-luck/option-luck vocabulary until the debrief.

Design the activity. The following features are the substance of what I am asking for, not incidental constraints.

The group writes the compensation rule before it knows anyone's position: a short constitutional stage, in writing, with the rule binding on the later stage. Then a lottery assigns outcomes, and the written rule is applied to the people in the room. The fund is capped at a figure that makes it arithmetically impossible to satisfy every claim that will arise, so that paying one claimant means denying another and the group cannot dissolve the problem by being generous. Claims may be adjudicated only on the claimant's own testimony, and each participant holds a sealed private card describing a fact they must disclose if they want to press a claim.

Include four calibrated cases, one per participant chosen by the facilitator, which should force the group's rule to do real work: a disadvantage plainly unchosen; a disadvantage plainly traceable to a risk the person chose to take; a disadvantage whose causal history is genuinely indeterminate, with a behavioural component that may or may not matter; and a disadvantage that is unchosen in origin but whose magnitude depends on tastes or attachments the person deliberately cultivated. At least one case should make the criteria of unchosenness and of badness come apart, so that the group has to decide which one their rule is actually tracking.

Give me the full runnable package: rules and roles, the materials I need to print, the payoff scheme in points with no money changing hands, a timeline with minute markers, and how the facilitator acts as banker and timekeeper. If it fits the time, add an optional pre-commitment round in which participants can buy responsibility insurance before outcomes are revealed, so that the group can see the ex ante and ex post verdicts diverge. Tell me how to handle an odd number and a participant who refuses to disclose.

Then a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto specific parts of the position, and that separates three things the activity will run together: the epistemic problem that we cannot reliably tell luck from choice, the respect problem that the inquiry itself is degrading to the person subjected to it, and the abandonment problem that if we stop asking, the imprudent go uncompensated. Say which of the three the activity actually stages and which it only gestures at, and note where the regress — that the choices themselves have unchosen antecedents — threatens to collapse the rule the group wrote.

Finally, two things I want stated plainly. First, what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this, and what to do in each case; I expect at least a vague rule that cannot be applied, a group that tries to pay everyone, someone who argues that everything is luck, and a moment where the disclosure turns comic and deflates the point. Second, an honest account of what the activity cannot reproduce and that I will therefore have to supply: the question of the currency in which disadvantage is measured, and the external challenge from the relational-egalitarian tradition — that equality is a matter of how people stand to one another rather than of compensating for bad luck — which the game cannot raise from the inside. No readings, no citations; everything should run off the materials you specify.
