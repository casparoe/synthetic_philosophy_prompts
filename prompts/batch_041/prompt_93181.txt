For a graduate seminar on conceptual change, I want a short story that my students can argue about. Set it in the fictional microdomain of competitive tide-pool mapping, where practitioners certify a map as a true chart only if it records every pool above a certain size. A new surveying device reveals pools that the old method missed, and the community splits over whether a true chart must include them. Follow one cartographer through the split, and let the story make both sides' uses of true chart feel continuous with the old use and yet genuinely different. Do not resolve the dispute by fiat; let the reader see how the same word now picks out two overlapping practices. Aim for about 1,500 words.
