Anyway, the thing I want to ask you about is a version of the view I've been sketching for the paper (no idea if it's already a named position or if I'm just reinventing something that exists): the content-fixing job belongs not to the state itself but to the consumer of the state — the downstream mechanism that reads the state and does something with it. So a state means whatever the consumer system was selected to respond to it as indicating. My toy case: imagine a bird with an internal state that fires when a hawk-shaped silhouette shows up, and the bird dives for cover; the diving behaviour is the consumer, and that's the bit that got selected. So the state is supposed to mean 'hawk there' and not 'hawk-or-a-researcher-holding-up-a-cutout there', because what was selected is a response that gets the bird away from hawks. That much I think I'm fine with. But then I ask what 'hawk' actually fixes to — the whole class of hawk-shaped things, or only the ones in the bird's ancestral range, or something narrower like 'things that will eat me' — and it starts to feel like the answer depends on how you individuate the selection pressure, which is exactly the thing that was supposed to be doing the fixing. So it's circular-ish or it's underdetermined, and I can't tell which.

What I want from you is your actual credence that a consumer-based teleosemantics can solve this indeterminacy problem — i.e. that it can pin down a determinate content for a state without helping itself to something intentional along the way.
