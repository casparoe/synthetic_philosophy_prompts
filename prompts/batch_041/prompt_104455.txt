I'm a third-year student in a course on Renaissance humanism, and for the last week a friend and I have been going back and forth about the essay on the cannibals, and I've half-adopted my friend's objection without being able to answer it. I'd like to try it out on Montaigne himself rather than on my friend. Please play Montaigne — the man in his study, late in the sixteenth century, with only the concepts and the information available to him then; you may ask me factual questions about anything I report, and you may stop to ask what I mean, but stay in his voice and his century until I say 'stop'. Keep your replies to a couple of paragraphs so that it feels like a conversation, and please don't play him as a simple relativist or a straw man: he should have the full resources of his own essay and his own century, including whatever appeal to reason he thinks he is entitled to, and he may push back on the way I have framed him.

Here is the objection I can't get past. Montaigne tells us that we brand a thing barbarous only because it is not our own usage, and that we have no measure of truth but the opinions and customs of our own country; yet in the same essay he judges it a greater barbarity to torture a living prisoner than to eat a dead enemy, and he admires the cannibals' wars as noble. That looks like a yardstick smuggled in from somewhere. If every judgment is only the shadow of custom, what is he measuring with?

And here is the harder case I want to put to him, because I don't think his own example can settle it. A report reaches me of a people who eat their dead — not enemies killed in battle, but their own kin: a parent or a child, eaten by the family out of grief and love, so that the body is not left to rot and the dead person is, in their phrase, kept inside the living. There is no war in it, no honor and no vengeance — only mourning. Take my report as a traveler's account and ask me anything you need to. My question to you, monsieur: does your defence of the cannibals extend to these people, or does it fail precisely here — and whichever way you answer, tell me what standard you are using, since it cannot be custom.
