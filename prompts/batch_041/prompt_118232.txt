Last night I sat through a two-hour argument at my kitchen table between two friends — Rosa, who has spent years defending the view that a credence's only epistemic virtue is its accuracy, its closeness to the truth, and that evidence, coherence, and explanation matter only as instruments for getting closer to the truth, and Dev, who thinks that view is a shell game. I was a bystander throughout and lost track of who was making progress and who was only talking louder. What I want is a point-by-point adjudication, not a verdict on who won.

Their exchange ran roughly like this. (1) Rosa: if accuracy is the only thing that makes a credence good, then epistemic rationality is simply the project of holding the credences you expect to be most accurate, and nothing else is a fundamental epistemic value. (2) Dev: then consider the Oracle, a device that will overwrite your credences with the truth itself, so that afterwards you hold credence 1 in every true proposition and credence 0 in every false one. On Rosa's view that is the best possible epistemic state and one you should want, yet it is also a state you could not have arrived at by evidence, could not defend, and that would make inquiry pointless; any account with that consequence, Dev says, has misidentified the good. Rosa: the Oracle is not a counterexample but the destination — accuracy is the good, and a maximally accurate state is maximally good whatever its history. (3) Dev: then the view cannot say that evidence is epistemically required of anyone; it can only say that evidence is a useful instrument, and one the Oracle makes unnecessary, so an agent who cared only about accuracy could stop caring about evidence altogether. Rosa: that is precisely the view, and the sense that something has gone wrong is nostalgia for a different conception of epistemic value. (4) Dev: but then the view also cannot explain why we criticize someone whose credences are accurate only by luck, since a lucky state is as accurate as a well-evidenced one. Rosa: we criticize the method, and methods matter only through the accuracy they tend to produce; a lucky state is no reason to keep the method that produced it. (5) Dev: and accuracy is being asked to do more work than it can bear, since the scoring rules that all count as measures of closeness to the truth nevertheless disagree about which of two credence functions is closer, and if accuracy is the sole good the view owes us a reason to prefer one measure to another. Rosa: every proper rule agrees wherever it matters, and where they disagree there is no further fact of the matter.

Please adjudicate move by move: say which points are sound, which are confused, and what both of them are missing.
