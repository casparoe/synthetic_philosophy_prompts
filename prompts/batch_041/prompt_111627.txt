**Conventions Without a Surplus**

On the standard reading of Lewis, a convention is an arbitrary regularity in a recurring coordination situation that is mutually expected, mutually known to be expected, and self-enforcing. The last clause carries the explanatory weight: no authority is needed, because given that the others conform, each participant's best response is to conform too. This is what lets us treat a language, a currency, and a rule of the road as members of a single family.

I want to separate two things this formulation runs together. Define a participant's conformity surplus in a regularity R as the payoff of conforming given universal conformity, minus the payoff of defecting given universal conformity. Lewisian conventions have a positive surplus. But many regularities pass every other test — arbitrary, recurrent, mutually expected, common knowledge — while having a negative surplus: given that everyone else conforms, any single participant does better by defecting. I will call these toll norms. Returning a shopping cart, not dropping litter in an already-clean park, honouring an embargo, and not raiding a shared fridge are all cases in which universal conformity is the background condition that makes defection most tempting rather than least.

Where the surplus is negative, the Lewisian machinery is inert. Expectations, precedent, and common knowledge explain conformity only by making defection imprudent; where defection is prudent, they explain nothing. Toll norms therefore require an apparatus of monitoring and sanction, and that apparatus has a per-capita cost, which I will call the maintenance rent. Two consequences follow. First, toll norms cannot propagate by imitation of a successful precedent, because the precedent teaches only that everyone else conforms — precisely the situation in which one should defect. They propagate either through internalized motivation, which makes enforcement cheap but needs an independent account of how the motivation spreads, or through centralization. Second, the maintenance rent is itself a free-rider problem: nobody wants to be the one who monitors.

This yields an arithmetic constraint. Let n be the population, p the probability that a defection is detected, s the sanction, and g the gain from defecting. Conformity is a best response when p·s ≥ g. Under decentralized peer monitoring, p falls roughly as 1/n while the per-capita cost of monitoring rises, so a decentralized toll norm has a maximum viable scale unless s grows with n — and sanctions that grow with n either exceed what participants will bear or become indistinguishable from centralized punishment. The upshot is a trilemma: strictness at scale requires centralization, decentralization at scale requires laxness, and strictness with decentralization requires small scale.

The empirical prediction is that falling monitoring costs should relocate enforcement rather than change norms: persistent online identity and reputation systems should shift strictness toward central and platform-level enforcement, while contexts that preserve anonymity should lose strictness. A second prediction is that merely publicizing a norm should work for conventions and fail for toll norms.

What I want from you:

1. Is the reading of Lewis fair, or am I attacking a straw man? Lewis and the subsequent social-norm literature already distinguish conventions from regularities sustained by sanctions; does that concession defuse the argument, or does the conformity-surplus formulation add something that survives it?
2. Does the distinction do real work, or does it collapse into the familiar public-goods and second-order free-rider problems, with toll norms simply being the ones that need selective incentives?
3. Is the arithmetic in the trilemma sound, or is it rigged by the assumption that detection probability falls as 1/n? Which step is weakest?
4. Are the two predictions falsifiable as stated, and what evidence would count against them?
5. Where is the paper most likely to be wrong, and what are the two or three changes that would most improve it?
