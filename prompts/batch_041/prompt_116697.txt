Right, so I'm a third-year and I'm taking this seminar on how moral norms change — we spent weeks on the Williams thesis about abolition (that the slave trade had stopped paying and the economics did the work, with the arguments as decoration) and then Appiah's line that arguments on their own never move anybody, it's shifts in honour codes underneath that do it, with duelling and footbinding as the showcase cases. Someone in the seminar said the economic story has been pretty badly dented since, and then someone else said that if we can't even tell in hindsight whether it was interests or arguments or honour that did the work, maybe the question is undecidable and we should all go home. Meanwhile I have to write a peice of sustained irony for my satire module, so I want to bolt the two together.

My idea is a leaked internal memo from a consultancy called Crofts & Vane, Moral Revolution Advisory, which sells moral change as a professional service to a fictional client — say a coalition trying to abolish the death penalty in a made-up American state. They offer three retainers. The Argument Package, cheap: op-eds, pamphlets, a philosopher on retainer, and the memo should be blunt that this is historically decorative. The Interest Package, the Williams playbook: find out who profits from the practice and make it stop paying. The Honour Package, the Appiah playbook: don't bother convincing anyone it's wrong, make it shameful, the way duelling turned into something only a bounder does. The two partners should disagree about which of the three actually did the work in the past and the memo should never resolve it. It has to stay deadpan and stay cynical the whole way through — if the firm suddenly gets sincere about the power of reasoned argument the piece collapses, so internal consistency is the whole point. Someone who's read Appiah and the Williams debate should laugh rather than wince, so the jokes need to come out of the actual logic of the disagreement, not out of generic corporate-speak.

Could you write it, around 800 words, with these sections in this order and headings: Executive summary (~100 words); Our three retainers (~350, a paragraph each, with a price for each); Precedents from the file (~150, the historical cases written up as billable case studies); Risks and reputational exposure (~100); Recommendation and fee schedule (~100). Then a final short section headed Author's note for the seminar (~80 words) saying which real debates the jokes are leaning on. British spelling please, and no winking inside the memo itself — the reader should have to work out what's being sent up.
