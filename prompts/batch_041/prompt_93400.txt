I am preparing to present my own position in a graduate seminar and would like a consistency audit of it rather than a defence or a survey of the literature. Please treat the commitments below as mine and ask only whether they can be held together.

(1) Race, gender and money are all socially constructed: what makes something a member of one of these kinds is a matter of collective human attitudes and practices rather than of mind-independent nature alone.

(2) This does not make them second-rate. Socially constructed kinds are as real as any others — they figure in causal explanations, constrain what people can do, and support counterfactuals.

(3) I distinguish two grades of construction. Thin kinds — money is my example — are constituted wholly by the relevant community's attitudes and practices; nothing outside those attitudes does any work in fixing what belongs. Thick kinds — race is my example — are constituted by attitudes that fasten onto some pre-social difference and amplify it, so that the attitudes do essential work but not all of it.

(4) For any of these kinds there is a fact of the matter about membership, and it is not settled by what the individual member believes about themselves. A person can be wrong about which kind they belong to, and that error is not merely a failure of self-conception.

(5) Socially constructed kinds are proper objects of criticism: we can ask whether a given construction is oppressive and we can deliberately revise it. The analysis is meant in part to change the world, not only to describe it.

(6) The causal powers of a socially constructed kind are exhausted by the attitudes and practices that constitute it. If a kind appears to exercise a causal grip that outruns what the community believes, I take that as evidence that I have misdescribed the community's practices rather than as evidence that something non-social is doing the work.

(7) Wherever a kind is socially constructed there is a determinate community whose attitudes constitute it; I do not think an abstraction such as society does the work, but rather some particular group's practices.

Which of these genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict, and what is the cheapest way to restore consistency in each case that does require repair? Please rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally serious, and for each say what would have to give and what that concession would cost me elsewhere. I would like to know, finally, which single revision would do the most to clean up the whole set. Please write in British English.
