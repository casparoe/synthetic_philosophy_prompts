I cover medicine and science for a news desk, and one thing about pain has been bugging me for a while. Every hospital I've set foot in asks you to rate your pain from 1 to 10 and then treats that number like a real measurement -- they titrate the drugs to it, chart the trend, compare this morning's 4 with last night's 7. But the same people will also tell you, in the next breath, that pain is completely subjective, that only the patient can know how bad it is, and that you can't really compare one person's 6 to another person's. Those two things seem to be pulling in opposite directions. So I want to know what the 1-to-10 scale is actually assuming about the mind. It looks like it assumes there's a definite inner feeling sitting there that you can just inspect and read off, and that it's the same kind of thing from one head to the next. Some philosophers have argued that whole picture -- private, ineffable, directly-known inner sensations -- is an incoherant posit we should just drop. Does the pain scale really need that picture, or can it run on something thinner? What views of pain does it quietly rule out? And if the skeptics are right, does the practice fall apart, or is there a version of it that survives?
