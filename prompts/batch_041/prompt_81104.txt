Write a joke about revealed preference and the interpretation of utility. It should be funny to someone who has worked through the demand-theory arguments, not merely to someone who knows the caricature of economists as people who assume everyone is selfish; the punchline has to turn on the substance of the position.

To keep the joke from being a stock item, build it around a specific technical feature rather than the general idea that preferences are read off choices. Make the joke depend on at least one of the following.

(i) The consistency conditions on choice: if an option is chosen when another was available, the second is not chosen when the first is available. A single anomalous choice then forces a theorist either to give up the framework or to redescribe the chooser as having had a different preference ordering all along.

(ii) Menu dependence: what a person picks among some options can depend on which other options were on offer, so that no single fixed ordering over those options reproduces the choices unless the state space is enlarged to include facts about the chooser's own situation — the move that threatens to make the theory compatible with any body of data.

(iii) Ordinality: a utility function is identified only up to increasing transformation, so choice data do not fix how much better one option is than another, and claims about a person's welfare inherit that indeterminacy.

(iv) The semantic drift this invites: if "prefers" names a summary of behavior rather than a mental state, the theory risks being true of everything and therefore informative about nothing.

The joke should be short — a setup and a punchline, or a few lines of dialogue. After it, add two or three sentences identifying exactly which feature the punchline turns on and what a defender of the revealed-preference program would say to deny that the joke has any bite. The humor should survive for a reader who knows these arguments but not the literature, so do not build the joke on any particular paper, author, or textbook formulation.
