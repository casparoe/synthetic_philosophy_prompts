I run a reading group for politically active people who arent philosophers — a couple of union reps, two grad students from other departments, a city council candidate, and a retired teacher who reads a lot but mostly history. Next month we're doing Simone Weil's essay arguing for the abolition of political parties (she wrote it in 1943, it came out after her death). I need a teaching plan, not a summary of the essay.

Plan the order. What has to land before her thesis even registers as a serious claim instead of a provocation? She thinks a party's real work isnt drafting platforms but manufacturing collective fervor, and that this is structurally at odds with thinking honestly — what has to come before that so it doesnt sound like snobbery or a complaint about rude activists?

Pick the example that carries the whole session. It needs to show a group sliding from open argument into discipline without any villain, and it has to be something this crowd will recognize from their own world — not the Jacobins, they'll nod and file that away as a French thing. What should the example have, and what should it conspicuously lack (no corruption, no bad leader, no money changing hands)?

The misconceptions I'm expecting: that she wants a one-party state, that she's just anti-politics, that she's naive about how anything gets done, and — this one matters — that she's talking about the parties these people actually belong to, when she treats Anglo-American parties as a different beast altogether, closer to a sport than a church. Where in the sequence do I head each of those off, and how do I do it without turning the session into a lecture on French political history?

Also give me one prompt I can put to the room at the end that would show they've got the concept and not just the vibe — something where they have to decide whether some group they know (a caucus inside a union, a tendency in an organization, a group chat that coordinates votes) counts as a party in her sense.

And tell me what to leave out at this level, and to say out loud that I'm leaving it out.
