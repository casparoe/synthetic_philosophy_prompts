Im in a third-year philosophy of biology seminar and the last meeting is on whether DNA is really a code or whether "code" is just a metaphor that got out of hand. The bits I keep chewing on: the mapping from triplets to amino acids isnt forced by chemistry in any way anyone can actually point to, it looks like it could have been otherwise and then got frozen; and a code is supposed to need a convention plus a decoder, and the only decoder weve got is a ribosome, which doesnt understand anything, it just has affinities. Also the degeneracy thing: sixty-one codons for twenty amino acids, so several different triplets say the same thing, which means the order of the letters matters enormously in one sense and not at all in another.

For the last session I want to read out a poem, and I want the form to do the arguing instead of just sitting there as decoration. So: a sestina, in the voice of the ribosome. Six end-words, your call, but they should be the vocabulary of the actual dispute rather than pretty words, and the permutation they get dragged through should feel arbitrary and at the same time inexorable, could-have-been-otherwise but isnt, because thats the shape of the thing I want the reader to come away with. The envoi is where I want the payoff: it has to fold all six end-words back into three lines, and that compression should be the degeneracy, several triplets landing on the same amino acid, the way a sestina keeps saying a different thing with the same six words. If theres a way to work in a frame-shift, the same letters read from a different starting point giving you a completely different sentence, take it, in the envoi or in one stanza, but dont force it. And the ribosome should be the one speaking the whole way through, which is the joke and also the point: its the decoder, and it decodes nothing.

Dont resolve which side wins. I want someone who actually knows this stuff to find it accurate, and someone who doesnt to just find it a good poem. No naming theorists or works, no citations, no footnotes, the poem has to carry it on its own.

Formatting: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Just the poem with its stanza breaks, and then if you really want, one short paragraph of plain prose after it about why you picked those six end-words, written the way youd write it in an email.
