I'm putting together a position statement for a workshop on grounding, and I'd like an audit before I circulate it. Here is roughly where I've landed.

(1) Grounding is a single, unified relation between facts, the one expressed by 'in virtue of'. It is non-causal and genuinely explanatory: when some facts ground another, they explain it metaphysically, not merely by entailing it. I take the relation to be primitive. It cannot be analysed in terms of necessity, essence, or truthmaking, though I am happy to characterize it by its formal features.

(2) Grounding is hyperintensional. Necessarily equivalent facts can differ in what grounds them and in whether they are grounded at all. Supervenience claims, such as 'no mental difference without a physical difference', are at best symptoms of grounding and never substitutes for it.

(3) Grounding is a strict partial order: irreflexive, asymmetric, transitive. I need transitivity, because the claims I care about come in chains. The mental is grounded in the physical, the physical in the microphysical, so the mental is grounded in the microphysical.

(4) Reality is well-founded: every chain of grounding terminates in facts that are ungrounded. And I take fundamentality to be just ungroundedness. To be fundamental is to be among the facts that ground without themselves being grounded.

(5) Grounding earns its keep by doing ontological work. Where a fact is fully grounded in others, it is nothing over and above them, and that is what lets me formulate physicalism, naturalism about the moral, and the claim that wholes are nothing over and above their parts without inflating the world's inventory. Concretely: I want to say both that the fact that this is a statue is grounded in facts about its material constitution and history, and that the statue is no addition to the particles.

(6) I am a realist about grounding. Which facts ground which is a mind-independent matter, discovered by ordinary metaphysical theorizing rather than fixed by our explanatory conventions, and I take myself to know a fair number of particular grounding claims.

What I want is a consistency audit, not a survey. Some of these may be in genuine tension, some may only look that way, and I would rather be told which is which than have all six flagged as equally suspect. For each real tension, rank it by severity, identify the cheapest repair, and say what that repair costs me. I have no antecedent view about which commitment ought to give way, so please do not assume that any one of them is the expendable one. Finish by telling me which single revision would clear up the most, and what it would leave unresolved. I don't need references; I need the argument.
