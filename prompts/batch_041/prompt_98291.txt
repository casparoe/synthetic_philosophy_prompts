My mother is eighty-two and had a stroke three months ago; since then her relationship to pain has changed in a way that unsettles everyone around her. She reports pain accurately — she can say where it is, whether it is sharp or burning, how intense it is — and she does not deny that it is there. But it appears to cost her nothing. She rests her hand on a hot burner while reaching for a pot, she lets the nurse draw blood without flinching or looking away, and when we ask whether the pain is bothering her she seems puzzled and says it is like a loud noise in the next room: bad in principle, but not something she needs to get away from. She is not numb, and she was never stoical before. Our GP wants to start her on regular opioids; my brother says that if she is not suffering, drugging her is just sedation and we should leave her be. I don't know how to think about this. Is the pain bad for her? If we treat it, what are we treating — and what would count as success? And does the fact that she can have pain without seeming to mind it tell us anything about why pain is bad in the first place? Please say how confident you are in your claims and single out the point you are least sure of.
