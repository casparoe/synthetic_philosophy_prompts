Running a Pascal reading group and I want to close with Les Misérables, since Hugo's plot basically stress-tests Pascal's line that justice without force is powerless and force without justice is tyranny. Read the novel through that: Javert as force that believes its justice, the bishop as justice with no force behind it at all, Valjean as the man who keeps getting saved by mercy and then has to live under law anyway, and the barricades as force trying to pass itself off as justice in the other direction. What I care about most is the less-quoted Pascal -- that custom is what actually grounds equity, that we can't give a real reason for the just, that in practice the two are only ever stapled together by power, and that original sin is why any of this is needed at all. So where does the lens actually light up a scene, where is Hugo quietly disagreeing with Pascal, and does the novel dramatize something about justice and force that the Pensées can only assert?
