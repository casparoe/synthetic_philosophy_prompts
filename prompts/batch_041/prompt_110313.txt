I'm putting together an internal grant app (three months, basically a course release) and I want you to turn a half-baked idea of mine into a proposal. The topic is Fitch's paradox of knowability: all truths are knowable, and then it turns out that if that's so and we're not omniscient, every truth is in fact known, so something has to give.

Here's my hunch, and the proposal should be built around it, though I want it to be able to say so if the hunch is a dead end. Everyone treats the knowability principle as a single claim about truths as such. But the anti-realist only ever needs it for truths about how the world is, the stuff the manifestation argument is really aimed at, and the paradox is generated entirely by feeding the principle a truth that's partly about our own epistemic situation, the old p-and-nobody-knows-it trick. So: what if knowability gets relitivised? Not 'p is knowable' but 'p is knowable from here', i.e. there's a way of extending what we know now on which we'd know p. That looks like it stops the paradox dead, because the extension that puts us in a position to know p is exactly the extension on which nobody-knows-p has gone false. Whether that's the solution or a cheat is the whole question, and the proposal needs to be set up so we can tell the diference.

So what I need: the question stated sharply enough that a referee can see what would count as answering it; why the going responses — restrict the principle to some privileged class of truths, deny that knowledge distributes over conjunction, drop the quantifier and read the principle as a schema — leave room for my angle instead of already occupying it, and why pushing on the relativisation is a better bet than just picking a restriction off the shelf and defending it; what I'd actually do for three months (I'm not proving anything deep: work the paradox through with the relativised operator, see which steps still go through and where the collateral damage lands, then ask whether a relativised principle can still do the work the anti-realist wanted, since if knowability is always relative to a stage of inquiry it's not obvious it still rules out the truth that's unrecognisable in principle by us as we are); what the deliverable looks like; and what would count as the project failing.
