I've been reading popular science books about evolution and hanging around forums too much, and I keep running into this phrase that I can't get out of my head — something like 'free-floating rationales', or maybe it was 'floating reasons', I honestly cant remember where I saw it. The gist, as far as I can tell, is that there can be reasons for something being the way it is even though nobody ever had those reasons. Every explanation I find just repeats the slogan and moves on, so I want you to actually sort this out.

What set me off was moths. Moths spiral into lamps, supposedly because they navigate by holding a fixed angle to the moon, and now the lamp ruins it. So the behaviour is somehow 'for' getting somewhere, and it's a mistake, but the moth has no clue about any of it, and there's no designer who meant it to work that way. Then I started seeing it everywhere. On my drive to work there's a junction where everyone ends up in the left lane, and it turns out that's genuinely the fastest way for all of us to get through — but nobody planned it, and if you asked the drivers why they do it they'd just shrug. Same with the office coffee rota, which works for reasons nobody could state.

So: is 'reasons without reasoners' a real claim about the world, or just a story we tell about causes? Write me an essay on that. Weigh both sides properly — the version that says these are just causes dressed up in purpose-talk, and the version that says a thing can really be wrong, or really be for something, with no mind involved — and then actually land somewhere. Don't do the thing where you say the truth is somewhere in the middle and drift off.

There's one case I especially want you to deal with, because it breaks my brain: two behaviours that look identical and do exactly the same good thing, except one of them is there because it got selected for doing that, and the other is a fluke that drifted in and just happens to be useful now. Does the first one have a reason and the second one only a cause? And if so, what is that difference made of — is it a fact about the world, or only about which explanation we happen to tell? Bring the same question to the traffic thing.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro and no conclusion — I just want the five beats of the argument, with the fifth being where you actually come down.
