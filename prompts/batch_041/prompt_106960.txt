Two colleagues of mine, one a committed conciliationist and the other a committed steadfast theorist about peer disagreement, have argued for a year and have at last agreed to attempt an adversarial collaboration. Write the joint statement they could both sign.

The statement should contain, at minimum: (1) a numbered section of claims about the epistemology of disagreement that both parties accept, stated specifically enough to be non-trivial; (2) a section stating precisely where they still differ, each position phrased in wording its own side would accept as fair; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party — and, for each, a case, argument, or consideration that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and (4) an explicit judgement about which parts of their dispute, if any, are merely verbal rather than substantive.

I suspect two cruxes deserve attention, and I would like you to adopt, revise, or replace them. The first is whether epistemic rationality is unique — whether a given body of evidence permits exactly one doxastic attitude — on the thought that conciliationism may require uniqueness while steadfastness sits more comfortably with a permissive view, so that their disagreement about disagreement may bottom out in a disagreement about rationality itself. The second is self-application: whether conciliationism, turned on the fact that competent peers reject it, undermines itself, and whether steadfastness suffers an analogous difficulty. Consider too whether the two camps are answering different questions — one about credence, another about outright belief, assertion, or what one may do — and whether that makes part of the exchange verbal.

Where the parties dispute what a case shows, describe a concrete case that both would regard as decisive evidence for one side or the other — for example, a disagreement between agents whose evidential credentials are asymmetric, or one in which each party can inspect the other's evidence about its own reliability — and state what each side predicts about it. Present the agreed claims as a numbered section and the residual disagreements as a table. The document must be one both parties could sign without either feeling misrepresented, so do not smooth over the disagreement that remains.
