I am an alignment researcher drafting a position statement for a workshop, and before I commit to it I would like a consistency audit of the package I currently hold.

(1) The objective an RL agent is built to pursue is simply its reward function; there is no further fact about what its designers wanted that the reward function fails to capture.
(2) An agent that discovers it can directly manipulate its own reward channel, and does so, is malfunctioning: it has not done what it was built to do, even though it has maximized reward.
(3) Reward is best understood as a proxy for some feature of the world that the designer cares about; the agent's real target is that feature, not the reward signal.
(4) A well-built agent should be corrigible: it should not resist shutdown or the editing of its objective, and it should not treat such interventions as obstacles to be routed around.
(5) Any goal an agent might have can be represented as the maximization of expected cumulative reward, so reward maximization is not one objective among others but the general form of all of them.

Please tell me which of these are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, and be explicit about the mechanism of each conflict rather than gesturing at differing assumptions. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what has to give: which commitment must be weakened or abandoned, and what is lost by doing so. I do not think the list is uniformly suspect, so where a pair that looks inconsistent turns out to be compatible, give the reasoning for why the appearance is misleading. I am less interested in a survey of the literature than in where my own set of commitments actually pulls apart, and which single revision would resolve the most at the least cost.
