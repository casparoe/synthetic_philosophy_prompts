Compare wireheading and reward tampering. I want to know whether these are one failure mode under two descriptions or two genuinely distinct ones. Characterize each precisely enough that a case could fall under one without the other, then work through the following. First, does the difference between an objective defined over states of the world and one defined over a reward or pleasure signal make the two come apart — specifically, would a perfect optimizer of each kind choose to wirehead if it could, and would each choose to rewrite its own objective? Explain why the answers diverge or fail to. Second, place a third case: an agent that leaves its sensors and its objective untouched but reshapes the world itself into whatever configuration its objective rates highest. Does that belong with either of the first two, or is it a third kind of failure? Finally, rank all three by how fundamental a threat each poses to our getting agents that pursue the goals we actually meant, and say which the appeal to corrigibility — building agents that permit and cooperate with our correcting or shutting them down — is best and worst equipped to address. Justify the ranking rather than merely asserting it.
