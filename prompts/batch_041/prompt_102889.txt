I chair the analytic standards board of a forecasting outfit that produces risk assessments for institutional clients; the board issues binding guidance on how analysts may update their credences when new source material arrives. A case on next month's agenda is going to be contentious, and I would like you to draft the written opinion I will circulate beforehand.

The case. Our analysts work with a coarse four-cell hypothesis space. For the file in question the cells are (Kestrel, depot), (Kestrel, transit), (Marlin, depot), and (Marlin, transit), and the analyst's prior is uniform at 0.25 on each. A source judged reliable then reports: "If the target is in Kestrel, it is at a depot." The source says nothing else — in particular, it does not assert that the target is not in Kestrel-transit, and it says nothing directly about Marlin.

Two factions have formed. The first holds that the report is a material conditional and that the analyst should condition on (not-Kestrel or depot); this zeroes out the Kestrel-transit cell and leaves 1/3 on each of the remaining three cells. The second holds that this is a mistake: the source uttered a conditional, not a disjunction, and treating it as a disjunction discards a distinction the source did not actually draw — it converts a claim about how Kestrel and depot are related into a claim that rules out a cell outright. This faction wants a rule that leaves all four cells positive, but its members disagree about which rule: some favour minimizing relative entropy, others a likelihood-ratio treatment, others simply keeping the Kestrel marginal at 1/2.

I need a decision memo that (a) recommends a concrete update rule for conditional source reports of this form, (b) states what the analyst's posterior should be in this specific case, and (c) gives the reasoning in terms the losing faction can actually engage with rather than merely being outvoted.

Please write that memo, in the register of a board's written opinion: a clear recommendation, the reasoning behind it, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the other side. I would also like you to separate what is genuinely settled among people who work on this from what is contested. If the choice among the competing update rules is a live dispute rather than a closed question, say so plainly, and do not present one side's answer as if it were the consensus.
