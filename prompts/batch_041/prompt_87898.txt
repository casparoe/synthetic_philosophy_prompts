My friend Dan's dad has been a letdown his whole life — cancels plans, forgets birthdays, and last month he skipped Dan's daughter's wedding without even a call. Dan has stopped being upset about any of it. He says he's forgiven him; he read somewhere that anger is just a way of demanding that other people be different, and you cant control other people, so he's made his peace, and now he just manages his dad — short calls, low expectations, no arguments. Dan's sister Priya is still furious about the wedding, and Dan thinks she's the one with the problem, carrying a grudge.

I keep feeling Dan is the one who's changed for the worse, but I cant put my finger on it. It's like he's not dealing with his dad as a person anymore, just handling him, while Priya's anger is the thing thats still treating him as someone who owes her something. His dad isn't ill or an addict, by the way — he's just selfish and unreliable, and he knows exactly what he's doing. So: is that a real distinction I'm sensing, or am I just romanticizing being angry? Is what Dan calls forgiveness actually a way of writing his dad off — and could the calm, managerial attitude be more disrespectful than Priya's fury? And practically, should I say anything to either of them, or is this none of my business?
