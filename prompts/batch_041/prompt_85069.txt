Dev said that's not what those studies show. First, they measure one behavior on one afternoon. Traits are supposed to predict behavior in aggregate, across alot of occasions, not in any single case — nobody should expect the correlation with any one action to be high, and if you add up enough occasions the signal is there. Second, even granting the studies, virtue ethics isn't a predictive psych theory. It's a claim about what makes a person good, not about how often people manage it. The only empirical thing it needs is that virtue is possible, not that it's common, and these studies don't touch possibility. Aristotle already thought virtue was rare and took years of habituation and a decent upbringing to get — tired people failing isn't a refutation of him, it's his whole point.

Maya said that's a dodge. If "virtue is possible" just means "somewhere some hypothetical person could pull it off," then any standard passes, including ones no human could ever meet, and "ought implies can" stops doing any work at all. And she said Dev's aggregation point actually hands her the argument: if honesty is defined as "what you do on average over many occasions," then it's a description of your behavior, not an explanation of it. Any pattern, even a random one, can be called a disposition after the fact, so the trait isn't explaining anything.

Dev came back with a few things. He said virtue isn't only about what you do when a situation is dumped on you, it's also about which situations you put yourself in — the honest person avoids setups where they'd be tempted, keeps their promises in front of witnesses, and so on. He also said the situationist data is better read as "traits only switch on in weak situations" — in strong situations everyone acts the same, which is why the studies come out the way they do. That's a claim about situational strength, not about traits not existing. And his last point was that all these experiments record behavior and never reasons, but virtue is about acting for the right reasons, so a single behavior can't even bear on whether someone's honest.

Maya said all three of those make the theory unfalsifiable. "Weak situation" is just a label for "situations where the trait showed up," so it's circular, and the reasons point means virtue ethicists can wave off any evidence whatsoever.
