I host a small physics-and-philosophy podcast, and this Thursday my guest is a theorist who's spent about a decade arguing that Maxwell's demon gets exorcised for the wrong reason. Her line: it's not the erasure cost of the demon's memory that saves the second law — she thinks Landauer's principle is basically a red herring here — it's that any demon that measures the gas has to become correlated with it, and those correlations eat exactly the entropy it was trying to cash out. I need nine or ten interview questions that won't just let her restate that for the tenth time.

Cover, roughly in this order, and give me a follow-up for each one keyed to the answer she's most likely to give:

- the circularity worry. Alot of people think you can't derive the correlation-entropy bookkeeping, or Landauer, without already assuming the second law you're supposedly rescuing. So does her argument show the demon actually loses, or just that the books balance if you insist hard enough that they balance?
- a demon that measures the molecule, writes the bit down, and then never reads the record and never erases it. Where did the entropy go in that case? Is a correlated record sitting there enough on its own, or does someone have to look at it?
- the one-molecule Szilard setup, where the measurement looks free and the work looks free, and why the naive accounting double-counts or drops a step. Also, what about the folks who say you can measure reversibly if you already know the state, so the demon's very first measurement costs nothing — does that break her story or not?
- whether her story needs the demon's memory to be finite and eventually reset. What actually happens in the infinite-memory limit — is that a real physical idealization or a cheat in disguise?
- how "correlations do the work" differs in its predictions from the older "measurement has an irreducible entropy cost" story. Do they ever disagree on a concrete number, like in one of those tabletop single-bit feedback engines people have built?

Then one question that puts the squeeze on: if no actual machine can win and the demon is just a bookkeeping device, what does she say to the labs reporting they extracted work from a single heat bath using feedback? Is that cheating, and if so, exactly where is the cheat?

End with something I genuinely don't know the answer to: does any of this change if the demon is a quantum measurer? Like, if it measures in a basis that doesn't commute with the gas's energy, does the accounting come out differently, or does it all wash out?

Keep them tight and conversational. I don't want her reading me a lecture — I want her having to actually make a choice.
