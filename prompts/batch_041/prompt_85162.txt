I organise a monthly lunch-and-learn for about eight backend engineers, none of us with any formal philosophy training, and next month I am running the session on cross-cultural variation in philosophical intuitions — the idea that the intuitions philosophers treat as data about a concept (what someone intended, whether an act harmed anyone, whether a belief counts as knowledge) may depend on the population you sample, and what that does and does not show. Do not explain the topic to me. Plan the forty minutes.

Two things I need from the plan. First, I want the whole session carried by a single case, and I would rather use one from our own world than a trolley: an engineer ships a change she knows will break things for a small fraction of users and ships it anyway because it is the fastest way to make the deadline — did she intentionally harm those users? Tell me whether that case can carry the session, and if it cannot, what the case I use instead has to have and has to lack. Second, I have a suspicion about where the real difficulty lies: that when a case is moved between cultural settings, the same sentences may be read as asking different questions, so a difference in answers need not be a difference in intuition at all. I do not know whether that is the main trap or a side issue, and I want the plan to settle it rather than leave it hanging.

So: the order the ideas have to come in and what each depends on; the misconceptions a room of engineers will bring, including the ones I will bring myself, where in the sequence each surfaces and exactly how I head it off; the one question I put to the room at the end to tell whether the point landed; and what I should openly say I am not going to cover. No citations, no names of studies, no reading list; where a step depends on an empirical claim we cannot check offline, say so and tell me how to run that step without it.

Give it to me as a JSON object with exactly these fields: sequence (array, in order, of objects with step, idea, depends_on, misconception_expected, where_in_sequence_it_is_defused, how); carrying_case (object with verdict_on_my_case, must_have, must_lack, fallback_case); end_check (object with question, what_a_passing_answer_looks_like, what_a_failing_answer_looks_like); omissions (array of strings); unverifiable_dependencies (array of objects with step, claim_needed, how_to_run_the_step_without_it).
