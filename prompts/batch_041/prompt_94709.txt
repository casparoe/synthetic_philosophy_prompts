I'm writing a position paper for a metaphysics seminar on material constitution, and I want to audit my own commitments before I commit them to print. The case that got me going was a 3D printer: a spool of filament is loaded, the printer lays down a statue, and for a while both the spool and the statue are on the table; later the statue is melted down and the same plastic is reprinted as something else. I take the following package to be my view. (1) The statue and the lump of filament are numerically distinct material objects, not one object under two descriptions. (2) They are composed of exactly the same atoms, in exactly the same arrangement, at every moment at which both exist. (3) Identity is necessary: if the statue is the lump, it is so at every world and time. (4) They differ in modal and historical properties: the lump could have survived being squashed before the printing began, the statue could not; the lump may have existed before the statue did; the statue might have been printed from a different spool. (5) There is no form, essence, or haecceity over and above the atoms and their arrangement; whatever the statue is, it is not the atoms plus a further ingredient. (6) All facts about the statue supervene on the atoms and their arrangement at the relevant time, and history is not part of the supervenience base, because the past is not a further ingredient in the present object. (7) Ordinary speech is right to say that the statue is the clay, but that is not an identity statement. (8) Counting is sortal-relative: the question how many objects are on the table has no answer until you say how many statues, lumps, or atoms. (9) Composition is restricted, so there are no arbitrary undetached parts; the atoms compose the statue and the lump but not every fusion you can define. (10) I have recently added a twist I have been calling flat constitution: the filament constitutes the statue, constitution is not identity, and yet the constitution relation adds no ingredient to the world; it is just the way a certain kind of physical arrangement supports statue-talk.

What I want is a consistency audit of this package. Please tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only look that way because of an ambiguity I could disambiguate, and which are independent. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each give the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the package. Do not simply recommend a standard position; I want to know what my own commitments commit me to. I am especially interested in whether the no-extra-ingredient claim and the strong supervenience thesis can survive the modal and historical differences, and whether sortal-relative counting is doing real work or just postponing the question of how many objects there are.
