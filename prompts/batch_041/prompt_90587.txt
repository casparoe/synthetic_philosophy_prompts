I'm building a self-report battery for a study and I keep getting told self-reports are unreliable so I should use behavioral measures instead, which I think is way too blunt. So I want you to actually rate the questions instead of hand-waving.

For each question below, give me two things. First, R: if the person is sincere and genuinely trying, what fraction of the time is their answer accurate about the underlying state? Give me a number or a range from 0 to 1, plus a one-line why. Second, M: is this a question where there's an independent fact that the answer could be right or wrong about (call that measurement), or is it one where the answering is what makes it so (more like an avowal or a commitment), or is it a mix? For the mixed ones, say which part is which. I care a lot about the M column, because half of what I read about introspection seems to be about the R column when the real trouble is the M column.

Then sort the whole list into tiers by how much weight I should give the answer as evidence, and flag any question you think I should just drop because whatever the person says won't mean anything.

The questions:
- Are you in pain right now, and where is it?
- What is your mood right now?
- Were you in a good mood an hour ago?
- What were you just thinking about, right before I spoke?
- Why did you pick the option you picked?
- Did you decide before or after you became aware of deciding?
- Do you believe you'd be happier if you moved abroad?
- Do you love your sister?
- Can you see the difference between these two nearly identical shades of green?
- Were you actually reading that last paragraph, or just moving your eyes over it?
- Was that last thought in words, in pictures, or neither?
- Are you paying attention to the meaning of this sentence or to the letters?
- Which of these two words did you notice first?
- Do you genuinely care about this cause, or do you just say you do?
- Would you make the same choice again tomorrow?
- Is your intention to keep this up a real intention or a verbal habit?
- Did the reason you gave actually cause the choice, or did you come up with it after the fact?
- Are you biased against the outgroup? I'm told this one is a trap question, tell me why or why not.

End with which two of your ratings you'd least want to defend in an argument, and which question on this list you think most people rate in the wrong direction — trusted when it shouldn't be, or distrusted when it's actually fine.

I definately don't need names or papers, just your take and your reasoning.
