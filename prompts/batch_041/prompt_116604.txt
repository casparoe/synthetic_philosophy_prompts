I want a credence, not a literature review, on a question that sits one step past where the discussion of the strange inversions of reasoning usually stops. Here is the pattern as I understand it, so you can correct me if I have the shape wrong.

The Darwinian inversion: the pre-inversion reasoning runs that design requires a designer, so the appearance of design in living things presupposes a mind that comprehended the design; the inversion replies that the comprehending mind is itself a product of a process with no comprehension anywhere in it, so comprehension is downstream of competence rather than its precondition. The Turing inversion has the same shape: the pre-inversion reasoning runs that following a rule requires something that grasps the rule, so a machine can at best simulate thought; the inversion replies that the grasping rule-follower is itself a mechanism executing a computation, so what we took for the precondition is the thing to be explained. Dennett's slogan for the residue is competence without comprehension, and the familiar machinery — blind variation with differential retention, cranes rather than skyhooks — is supposed to carry the explanatory weight.

What strikes me is that both inversions stop one floor short. They explain competence without comprehension and then leave comprehension itself standing as a primitive at the top: the designer's insight, the mathematician's grasp. So the claim I want priced is the reflexive one:

(P) There is no stratum at which comprehension is primitive. Comprehension is itself produced by competence-without-comprehension processes, and it is a matter of degree and internal organization rather than a property a system either has or lacks. It follows that any sharp line between systems that merely have competence and systems that genuinely comprehend is drawn by our explanatory interests rather than by nature.

2. Whether comprehension names a natural kind with a determinate threshold at all, or is a degree notion whose sharp uses are stipulative. I suspect this term does most of the work, and I would like you to say whether you agree.

Whether the mechanisms actually on offer — selection, gradient descent, cultural and memetic evolution, and whatever else you would admit — can generate the features usually treated as diagnostic of comprehension, such as recursive meta-representation, a global workspace, or model-based control, or whether some further ingredient is required.

4. Whether the reflexive application is legitimate or self-undermining: if our own comprehension is itself competence without comprehension, what licenses the reasoning by which we assert (P)?

5. Whether (P) is substantive or verbal — whether it rules out any state of affairs, and what observation or argument would show that comprehension is instead a sharp, all-or-nothing property.
