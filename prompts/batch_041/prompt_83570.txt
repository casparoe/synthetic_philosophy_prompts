I run a small discussion group at the library, about 12 people, mix of retired folks, a couple of students, one guy who used to work in oil and won't let you forget it. One 90 minute session a month, and next month I want to do the Anthropocene, the whole "we're living in a new geological age that humans made" thing. What hooked me is that a bunch of scientists spent something like 15 years arguing over when it started, landed on the 1950s mostly because thats when nuclear bomb tests left a layer of plutonium in the mud at the bottom of a lake in Canada, and then in 2024 a bigger committee voted the whole thing down, it's not an official epoch after all, and yet everyone still uses the word like nothing happened. That's the part I want my group to feel: "when did it start" sounds like a question with one true answer, and it turns out to be a question about who gets to decide and what the answer is even for.

So design me an activity, not a lecture. I want them to actually have to pick a date and stick their neck out for it, and then feel the floor give way. Give me roles or teams with genuinely different things at stake, someone who needs a marker they can put their finger on in the rock, someone who thinks the real story is colonialism or inequality, someone who has to teach it to 14 year olds, whatever you think works. I want the rules to force one single answer out of the room, and then something that overrules them, a bigger committee saying it was never really a thing, while everyone carries on using the word anyway. Rules, materials, timings, and a debrief that maps what just happened onto the real issues underneath: a fact question vs a decision, what makes a boundary legitamate, and whether the body with the power to say no is the same body with the power to say yes.

Be honest with me about what the activity can't do, what about the real geology or the real institutional fight is just going to have to be me talking at them. And tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it.
