I've gone down a bit of a rabbit hole on those 'dollar auctions' ever since a friend ran one at his birthday and it turned into a proper row. You know the setup: a fiver goes to the highest bidder, and the second-highest bidder still has to pay what they bid, so you can end up with two people paying six quid for a five-pound note and neither of them able to back down. It's definately a clever trick, but the more I poke at it the less my own reactions hang together, and I want to know what principle, if any, is behind them. Case by case:

- The bloke at the party who kept bidding past the fiver: silly, obviously, but I don't hold it against him in the slightest.
- Two rival papers in the same city both slashing cover prices until neither makes money, each waiting for the other to fold first: I don't think either is being silly. From where each of them sits, backing off on their own is worse, and I don't blame either of them.
- A government that stays in a war largely because it's already lost so many soldiers: I do blame them, and I think it's silly — even though I can see the shape is the same as the newspapers.
- A researcher who patches the theory again every time it makes a bad prediction: not silly, that's what you're supposed to do, up to a point. I've no idea where the point is.
- My mate staying in a job she hates because she's already sunk five years into the qualification: I keep telling her the five years are gone and that's not a reason, and then I remember I did the same thing myself for longer and I don't feel bad about it.
- A lone person pouring money into a doomed project with no rival and no auctioneer — just them and the hole they're in: I don't think there's any trap here at all, it's just sunk cost, and I'm not even sure it belongs with the others. Which makes me wonder whether the rival is doing all the work.
- Someone who's read about dollar auctions, knows exactly why they're a trap, and walks into one anyway to show off: entirely on them, no sympathy.
- The one who runs the auction, knowing it ends with two friends out of pocket and not speaking to each other: out of order, I reckon, even though everyone's a willing adult and nobody's been lied to.

Is there one principle that gets all of that, or am I going to have to split things up? I suspect 'rational step' and 'rational policy' are coming apart, and 'silly' and 'blameworthy' are coming apart too, but I can't get the pieces to sit together. If it takes two or three principles, fine — just tell me which case forces each clause. Say what it predicts for one I haven't listed: a tariff war between two big economies where the people who actually get hurt are farmers in a third country who aren't at the table and can't fold. And be straight with me about which of my verdicts I'd have to give up to get a clean principle. No reading list needed, I'm not writing this up for anyone.
