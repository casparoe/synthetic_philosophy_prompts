Background: I'm a retired palaeontologist — 34 years at a natural history museum, mostly trilobites, plus a long stretch as collections manager, which means I spent half my career telling visitors that the thing in the case is a cast and the real one is in a draw upstairs and that this is fine. I've now been reading philosophy for about two years and it's dawned on me that I never worked out why it's fine, or whether it is.

There are two pictures of why an original is special that I keep bumping into, and I'd like them compared properly.

Picture A, the one I absorbed at the museum — call it the type-specimen picture. An object's value is that it's the designated reference point for a name. The holotype is what a species name is glued to. It doesn't have to be beautiful or complete or even very informative — plenty of types are a chunk of jaw — it just has to be the one that got picked, curated, catalogued, and can be pointed at. A cast is fine for teaching, for measuring, for display; what a cast can't do is be the thing the name hangs on. So on this picture the value is institutional and historical: it lives in a chain of custody, not in the surface of the object.

Picture B, the one I get from art people — call it the achievement picture. What you value in the original is that it's the product of a particular person's particular performance on a particular afternoon: the decisions, the hesitations, the luck. A forgery can match the surface perfectly and still be worth nothing, becuase nobody performed anything — it's a recording, not a performance.

I want to know which of these is doing the real work, and where each of them breaks. Test them on three cases. One: a forgery of a painting that matches pigments and canvas age and fooled the experts for forty years. Two: a specimen that's 60 per cent original bone and 40 per cent sculpted plaster, on display so long that the museum itself has lost track of which bits are which. Three: a micron-accurate digital scan of a sculpture, printable anywhere, indistinguishable by eye from the marble.

Then tell me the thing I actually want to know: are these two pictures rivals, or are they answering different questions and I've been conflating them?

Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no preamble and nothing tacked on at the end.
