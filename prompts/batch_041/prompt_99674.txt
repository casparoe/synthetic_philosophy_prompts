ok so I want a fake advice column — the kind of "Dear ___," page in a glossy wellness magazine, the sort where the columnist has a first name and a headshot and answers three or four letters a month. Make it about people who are all trying to cultivate a private inner language: their own word or symbol for a feeling that only they have, an "authentic inner vocabulary," that whole thing. Three or four letters plus the columnist's replies, around 700 words, and the columnist has to stay deadpan earnest the entire way through — no winking, no nudging the reader, no "of course this is impossible."

The funny should come from the private-language material being right. So: a woman who's kept a diary for years with a little squiggle standing for a recurring sensation, and she's started to worry she can't tell whether she's been using it the same way the whole time; a guy who invented a word for a feeling and is genuinely hurt that his husband can't learn it; someone who wants to know whether they can lie to themselves in their own private tongue; someone asking how they'd ever know if they'd gotten it wrong. The columnist answers all of it in flawless wellness-magazine voice — affirmations, journaling prompts, sitting with your feelings, that register — and never once states the thing. The joke is that her advice is structurally the argument, delivered straight and sincerely.

Don't name Wittgenstein or call it the private language argument anywhere in the piece, and don't let anyone explain the problem in the abstract — it should just play out through the letters and replies. I want a philosopher reading it to laugh at how fair it is, and someone who's never heard of any of this to still find it funny and slightly unsettling by the end. Give the magazine a silly name and the columnist a name. Maybe throw in one letter from an obvious crank. Also keep the columinst from ever resolving anything.
