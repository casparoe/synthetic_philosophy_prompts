Some friends and I have been arguing for like a month now about why promises actually bind — why saying "I promise" seems to create a real obligation when just saying "I plan to" doesn't, even if I mean it just as sincerely and we both know it. One friend says it's basically a social practice, a move in a game that only obligates because we all keep playing; another says it's really about the other person's expectations, that what's wrong about breaking it is that you let them down after they arranged their life around you; a third says it's about authority, that in promising you hand the other person some say over what you do. We can all handle the easy cases so we invented a nastier one, and I want to know what each view actually commits you to here rather than which one sounds nicest. (This started as a seminar paper and I keep stalling on it.)

The case: I promise my friend Priya that I'll drive her to her chemo appointments for the next six months. Two weeks in, Priya moves abroad for a job and, before she goes, she tells me she's "handing the promise over" to her brother Dev — she says consider it his now, he's the one who needs the rides. Priya honestly no longer cares either way; Dev starts planning his whole week around me showing up. What I want is the most important implications of each of those three accounts for this, and especially: does it even make sense to say a promise can be transferred like that, or does the fact that the transfer feels legitimate (to us, anyway) show the obligation was never really about Priya at all? And if Dev can hand it on again to a fourth person, does anything break? I care about the implications where the views actually come apart — different verdicts, or one of them needing extra machinery the others don't — not a list of things they all agree on. Also please say how confident you are in what you claim and flag the single point your least sure about.
