So a while back I read about this thing called the unilateralist's curse and honestly it's been living in my head rent free ever since. The way I understand it: you've got a group of people who all basically want the same good outcome, and each of them is individually able to just go ahead and do something that affects everybody. Nobody in the group is a villain, everybody's being reasonable, everybody's got slightly different information and slightly different estimates of whether it's worth doing. And the thing still gets done way too often, because for the group to act, only ONE person has to be wrong in the optimistic direction. The average opinion is irrelevant, the median opinion is irrelevant -- what actually decides it is the single most optimistic person in the room, and the more people you add to the group, the further out into the tail that person tends to be, so it gets worse the bigger you get. I think it also only really bites for stuff that's hard to undo, and there's some subtlty about whether the disagreement is about the evidence or about the risk tolerance. There's a paper with a smarter fix in it but I read it years ago and can't remember the details, so don't ask me.

Anyway, I work at a mid-size company, and after an incident last year where somebody went and did a unilateral thing and it did not go great, leadership rolled out exactly the fix you'd expect: nobody takes an irreversible action anymore without sign-off, which in practice means near-unamity. We've now had the new policy for a year and the memo we actually got is way dumber than the one I keep imagining, so I want you to write the version in my head. Write the CEO's all-hands memo announcing the new policy -- deadpan, no winking at the reader, like it's a real internal email that leaked, roughly 600 words, maybe a little less. The humor has to come out of getting the structure of the problem right rather than out of making the CEO an idiot: he's read the same thing I did, he's basically correct that uncoordinated unilateral action is a real problem, and by the end I want someone who knows this literature to feel the actual tradeoff rather than a strawman -- that the cure has a body count of its own, that near-unanimity quietly hands veto power to the most cautious or least-informed person in the room, and that no policy like this can tell the difference between the case where the one guy going off-script is being reckless and the case where he's the only one who happens to be right. Concretely I'd like the memo to include the incident that triggered the policy, at least one objection from a named colleague who thinks the whole thing is insane, and some detail about the exception-request process, which has of course never approved anything. It can name-drop the curse by name if that helps the joke land, and please no moral at the end telling us what to conclude. And no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables -- just continuous prose the way a long email would actually be written.
