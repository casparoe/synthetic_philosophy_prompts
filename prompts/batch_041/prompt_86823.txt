A small reading group I'm in has been stuck for months on whether "better than" is transitive. The setup is the usual spectrum: two outcomes that differ only a hair in value, then a long chain where each step is a hair better than the last, and by the end you're comparing something obviously better than where you started against something obviously worse. Nobody disputes any single link. Two of us say transitivity is just part of what it is to be a comparative relation at all, so any step that looks like it breaks it must really be a tie or must be vague -- a "betterness" that can cycle just isn't betterness. The other two say that's question begging and the honest move is to admit the relation can cycle when the gaps are tiny. We keep trading the same moves.

My own hunch is that we're not really fighting about the cases but about two different jobs we want the word to do: one side is protecting the role betterness plays in ranking options and making choices, the other is protecting the fine-grained comparisons we actually make. A friend suggested we just split the relation in two -- a fine-grained one that may cycle, and a coarse "better by enough to matter" one that doesn't -- and said that would show the whole fight is verbal. Is my hunch right, or is the split a dodge that just changes the subject? Tell me what kind of disagreement this really is, and what, if anything, would count as progress. American English please.
