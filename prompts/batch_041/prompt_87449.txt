Me and my friend got into a stupidly long argument about panpsychism last night and I'm still annoyed about it, so I want you to settle it for me. Her position is that panpsychism is basically morally radioactive, and here's the argument she gave, which I wrote down afterwards because it kept bugging me:

(1) Panpsychism says experience -- or at least some experiential/proto-mental property -- is fundamental, i.e. it doesn't just pop into being once you've wired up a brain, it goes all the way down.
(2) If it goes all the way down, then the fundamental physical entities (quarks, electrons, fields, whatever the base level turns out to be) each have a "what it's like to be them."
(3) Anything with a what-it's-like-to-be-it can also be in states that are bad for it -- she says a thing that has experiences but couldn't possibly suffer would be a bizarre exception, and nothing in physics definately draws that line for you.
(4) Those fundamental entities are everywhere and interact constantly -- particles collide, decay, get absorbed, annihilate. Astronomically many such events every second, including inside your body right now.
(5) So if panpsychism is true, the universe is packed with suffering at the most basic level, all the time.
(6) So panpsychism turns physics into a horror show, and if you care about suffering at all you should hope it's false.

I want to keep the panpsychism and ditch the horror show -- (6) feels like it proves way too much, like it would make merely having a physical body a standing moral emergency. But she kept saying I was just refusing the conclusion, and honestly I couldn't put my finger on the actual mistake. So: which step is doing something illicit, why does it look so innocent, and if I knock it out, what does a panpsychist actually have to give up to stay consistent? I'd rather pay that price than accept (6).
