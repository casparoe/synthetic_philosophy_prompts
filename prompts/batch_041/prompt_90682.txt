For a seminar paper on the special composition question I am trying to work out and defend a view that I have not seen stated in quite the form I want, and I would like you to construct the argument for it as rigorously as you can. The question is the one about when some things compose a further thing: when, for instance, a collection of cells composes an organism, or a heap of stones composes nothing over and above the stones. The standard answers strike me as too coarse. Nihilism says that composition never occurs and that tables and trees are at best useful fictions. Universalism says that any things whatever compose something, so that there is a genuine object made of my left shoe and the moons of Jupiter. A third, more discriminating answer says that composition occurs exactly when the things are together caught up in a life. I want to argue for a fourth answer, which I will call graded integration: composition is not all-or-nothing but comes in degrees, and the degree to which some things compose a whole is the degree to which their states and activities are mutually causally dependent, so that the behavior of the group cannot be predicted from the behavior of its members taken one at a time.

Please argue that graded integration is the correct answer to the special composition question. In doing so, distinguish it sharply from the three views above, and especially from the view that composition requires a life, which it resembles but should not collapse into, and show what it predicts in cases where our intuitions pull apart: a heap of sand, a flock of starlings wheeling as a single sheet, a lichen that is a fungus and an alga fused into one growing body, a corporation, and a human being whose cells are gradually replaced over the years. Confront the obvious objection that if composition comes in degrees then there is no fact of the matter about how many objects there are, and that the word object becomes a name for our convenience rather than for the world's own furniture; I want you to resist that conclusion rather than concede it. Finally, say whether graded integration is a thesis in metaphysics or merely a thesis about how we describe things, and defend the stronger, metaphysical reading.
