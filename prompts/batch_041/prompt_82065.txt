I'm an applied economist by training, and my working vocabulary is the one you'd expect: potential outcomes and causal graphs, multilevel and random-effects models, within-versus-between-group decompositions, contextual and peer effects, Simpson's paradox, and the habit of suspecting that most disputes about "levels" are really disputes about the target of a causal estimand. Over the holidays I got into a long argument with two friends from a biology department about the units-of-selection debate — genes versus individuals versus groups, kin selection versus group selection, the suggestion that groups can be targets of selection in their own right and the counter-suggestion that group-level talk is always either a shorthand for individual-level accounting or a mistake. I kept being able to translate their moves into my own language, and I kept suspecting that something was being quietly lost each time. I'd like you to act as a philosopher of biology with real technical fluency in both evolutionary theory and applied causal inference, think carefully before answering, and do the translation systematically rather than impressionistically.

Begin with a compact statement of what the positions actually disagree about — not the personalities, the substance — including the distinction between the modest claim that selection can act at more than one level and the stronger claim that a group is a genuine individual in its own right, and the several ways biologists distinguish versions of multilevel selection (for instance, the group as the target of selection versus the group as merely the arena in which selection on individuals plays out). Then restate the debate three times in my vocabulary. First, as a hierarchical variance-and-covariance decomposition of the sort biologists do when they split the change in a mean trait into a between-group and a within-group component (the covariance bookkeeping associated with George Price). Second, as a causal-inference problem in which individuals interact, so that no-interference assumptions fail by construction, group membership functions as context or treatment, and the ordinary tools for cross-level confounding and mediation are what one would reach for. Third, as an aggregation problem of the kind I meet in multilevel modelling, where "which level is the unit?" is replaced by "at what level is the estimand defined, and when does a clean decomposition at one level license a causal claim at another?" For each of the three, be explicit about which of the biologists' claims maps cleanly, which has to be distorted or fudged to fit, and which has no counterpart at all in my vocabulary.

The residue report is the part I actually care about, so spend your effort there. I want to know what survives translation only as a slogan. Does the replicator-versus-vehicle contrast have any counterpart on my side, or is it doing work that no statistical decomposition can do? Does the distinction between selection of a trait and selection for that trait survive, or does it collapse into a mediation question about which feature of the organism is on the path to reproductive success? And is the statistical notion of heritability as a ratio of variances genuinely substitutable for the biologists' notion of faithful transmission across generations, or is transmission precisely where the translation breaks? Then give me a verdict, with reasons, on the residue itself: where the translation fails, is the failure a defect of my framework — something economists and statisticians lack the resources to state at all — or is it evidence that the biologists were trading on a metaphor that dissolves the moment you insist on a causal estimand? I would rather have a firm verdict than a both-sides summary, so commit to one and defend it. Please keep the whole thing self-contained and argue it out: no reading list, no citations, no appeals to what some famous person is supposed to have said. If you need to invoke a position or a distinction, state it and explain it in your own words. Rough working notes before the final answer are welcome.
