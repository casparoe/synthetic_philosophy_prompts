A friend walked me through this and now I can't find the exit, it's eating my sleep. He lays it out like this: (1) something can only be bad for you if there's some time at which it makes your life go worse. (2) When you're dead there's no you, so death can't be bad for you then. (3) While you're alive death hasn't happened yet, so it can't be bad for you then either. (4) So death is never bad for you, full stop. (5) Then he turns it around on me. Everyone agrees dying at 25 is worse than dying at 90, we call it a loss, a real one. But on (1) there's no time for that loss to sit in -- not before, not after. So either death isn't bad (his conclusion), or (1) is false and harms don't have to happen at a time. (6) He says pick the second and watch what falls out: if a harm needn't ever touch your life, then your partner cheating on you and you never once finding out harmed you; a hit piece about you published after your death, read by nobody who knew you, harmed you; and the books written before you were born that you'd have loved, the people you never met, harmed you too, just by being missed. (7) And if harms can float free like that, so can benefits, so basically everything is good or bad for you regardless of whether it ever comes near you. (8) Which is absurd, so (1) stands and death isn't bad for you.
