hey — im in a perception seminar and the last session is on disjunctivism and the argument from illusion, and the prof is letting us bring something creative instead of a paper. im bringing a poem but i need it built to spec, so here goes.

the case i want is the hallucination version, not the bent stick in water: someone at the end of the garden, or at the door. one evening it was really them, another evening it wasnt, and from the inside the two evenings are indistinguisable. i want the poem to give both evenings the exact same stanza — word for word, same line breaks, same punctuation, identical twice. the reader gets told up front which one was real (so no and-then-she-woke-up reveal, the suspense isnt the point) but the woman in the poem cant tell, and the last stanza is her the next morning trying to tell and failing.

heres the bit i actually care about. i want the repeated stanza to be the shared core that the common-kind people would point to — look, the description is identical, so it must be the same kind of experience — and then i want the poem to lean the other way, that the sameness of the description isnt the sameness of the episode, because one of those evenings was of her and the other one wasnt, and you cant get that into the stanza. the difference has to sit outside the identical stanzas without the poem ever saying some things cant be put into words, or anything like it. and please dont resolve it into oh well she can never know — the whole point is that she cant tell and it still makes a difference.

16-24 lines, no rhyme scheme needed, plain concrete language, no jargon, no sense-data. it should be a little eerie that the stanzas are literally identical, but not cute about it.

just the poem, then one section called Notes on the form, 150-250 words: which lines are doing the common-kind work, where the disjunctivist turn is, and which way the poem actually leans and why. also tell me whether the poem makes the disjunctivists look worse, because if she cant tell from the inside then the real evening isnt doing any work for her, and thats the objection i never know how to answer.
