I have just spent a long evening arguing with a close friend about standing to blame and hypocrisy, and I am still unsure whether my resistance was principled or merely stubborn. She offered the following summary, which she takes to capture the settled upshot of the philosophical discussion. She said: 'The discussion distinguishes standing to blame from the truth or justification of the criticism. Standing is about whether you are in a position to hold someone responsible, not about whether what you say is correct. Hypocrisy undermines standing: if you blame someone for a fault you yourself have, you are not in a position to hold them responsible. And because standing is a precondition of legitimate blame, a hypocrite's blame is not merely unwarranted; it is illegitimate. The person blamed can correctly say, "You have no right to blame me," and that settles the matter. Repentance is what restores standing: once the hypocrite has sincerely renounced the fault and reformed, they can blame others for it again. So we may dismiss the moral criticism of anyone who has committed the same wrong, unless they have first cleaned up their own act. Hypocrisy is thus not just a character flaw but a kind of silencing.' She then added a distinction of her own: between standing to blame and standing to be heard. Hypocrisy, on her view, removes the latter but not the former, so a hypocrite can still be right while having no claim on the target's attention.

I suspect this is too quick, but I do not know the distinctions well enough to separate the defensible claims from the distortions. Please audit the summary sentence by sentence. Which parts would a careful philosopher working on standing accept? Which are common simplifications? Which are outright misrepresentations -- for example, if the summary conflates standing with justification, treats standing as all-or-nothing, or runs together the act of blaming with the truth of the criticism? I am especially interested in whether the final conclusion follows from the accurate parts, or whether it depends on a questionable step. Consider a concrete case: someone who has also lied to me criticizes me for lying to them. Does their criticism have no force? Could I both acknowledge that they lack standing and still be answerable to the criticism? And what remains genuinely puzzling about standing once the overstatements are removed?
