I am not asking for a literature review, and I would rather you reason from the position as I state it than from the labels that might be attached to it; where you do invoke a known approach, explain it in your own words rather than gesturing at a name.

The position. Standard epistemic logic treats belief as closed under logical consequence: if an agent believes A, and A entails B, the agent believes B; and if A is a logical truth, the agent believes it. The familiar complaint is that this is false of every real thinker, and the familiar repairs are to add impossible worlds, to add an awareness or topic parameter, or to give up possible-worlds semantics for belief altogether. My view is that these repairs concede too much, and that the closure principles are not false but misapplied.

What I hold — call it the surveyability view — is this. An agent's beliefs are closed under exactly those entailments that the agent can run through in a single act of attentive reasoning: a derivation short enough and shallow enough to be held before the mind all at once, in the way one holds a two-line proof. Call those entailments surveyable. The claim has two parts. First, for surveyable entailments, closure is not merely tolerable but correct: an agent who believes A and does not believe a surveyable consequence of A has made a mistake, not merely run out of time. Second, for non-surveyable entailments, the closure principles say nothing at all, because they quantify over the whole consequence set, and that is a domain error — like applying a law of motion outside its regime of validity. So logical omniscience is not a false idealization in need of patching; it is a true description of a restricted domain that the standard formalism never bothered to restrict.

What would count against this? I want the exposure mapped, not a defense. Specifically:

Which defeaters are internal — generated by the view's own commitments rather than by an outside intuition? I am especially interested in whether there is a sorites problem: if each step of a long derivation is surveyable and closure holds for each step, does iterating closure deliver full omniscience after all? If so, what must I say about the composition of surveyable steps, and what does that concession cost me? Is there a stable line between steps that compose and steps that do not, or does any such line get drawn to fit the cases?

Which defeaters come from cases or results the view must give a verdict on — trivial tautologies that competent thinkers reject, the conjunction of many individually believable claims, an inconsistent belief set, agents whose reasoning is not sequential at all or whose working memory is very large, groups or systems that can carry out together what no member can carry out alone? For each, say what my view predicts and whether that prediction is defensible.

What does the surveyability view predict that its rivals do not? Is there any case on which the rival repairs and my view diverge, such that the case would actually decide between them — or do they all accommodate the same data and differ only in bookkeeping?

Which of these defeaters would a defender of the view concede as counting against them, and which would they dismiss, and on what grounds? I want the honest concession, not the critics' list.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me where the ranking is contestable.

Finally, address the charge that the view is unfalsifiable: that surveyable is adjusted after the fact to match whatever intuition we happen to have about a case, so that nothing could count against it. Is that charge fair here? If it is, is that a defect in a view of this kind, or an acceptable feature of a view whose job is conceptual clarification rather than prediction? And a self-application question: should I hold the surveyability view only if the argument for it is itself surveyable to me? If that condition is met trivially, is the view trivial; if it cannot be met, is the view self-undermining?

I am most moved by defeaters that show the view is not merely awkward but incoherent, and least moved by appeals to what ordinary speakers would say, so flag which of your defeaters rest on the latter.
