You are a stratigrapher who has spent the past decade inside the Anthropocene ratification fight, and you also read enough history and philosophy of science to know that the question "when did it start?" is really four or five questions wearing one coat. I am assembling a handout for a graduate seminar that pairs a stratigraphy unit with an STS unit, and I need you to build a classification instrument: a decision tree that doubles as a taxonomy of positions on the Anthropocene as a geological time unit.

The problem I am solving: my students collapse into two camps, "it started in 1950" and "the whole thing is politics," and both are failures of imagination about how many positions are actually on the table. The instrument should sort people by what they are committed to, not by which label they like.

Think this through carefully before writing. Begin by brainstorming the logical space rather than by listing positions you already know: first enumerate the axes on which someone can answer the question of whether the Anthropocene is a geological time unit, and if so which kind, from when, and by whose authority; then work out which combinations of answers are internally consistent; only then attach names to the cells. A tree assembled backwards from a list of names will fail the purpose, and I will be able to tell.

Requirements for the structure:

Every branch node must be a question a reader can answer without knowing which position it leads to. No node may be phrased as "do you accept the Capitalocene?" or otherwise presuppose a label. Nodes should be phrased at the level of, for instance: should the base of the unit be defined by the first appearance of a signal, or by the moment that signal becomes globally legible? Must the boundary be a single point in one physical section, or may it be a numerically specified age? May the boundary surface be diachronous, arriving at different dates in different places, or must it be synchronous everywhere? (Those are only to fix the register; do not copy them.)

At a minimum, the tree must be able to separate these axes:

1. Kind of thing: a formal chronostratigraphic unit (a series, hence an epoch) versus a geochronological unit versus a geological event in the sense of the Great Oxidation Event, which has no base and top and is not a time unit at all versus an informal descriptor that should never be formalised versus a periodisation belonging to history or political economy rather than to geology.
2. Marker class: a globally synchronous fallout signal such as bomb-test plutonium or bomb radiocarbon; an atmospheric or greenhouse inflection recorded in ice cores; a land-use or reforestation signal; an assemblage of artificial ground, technofossils, or industrial-age biological remains; or a diachronous package of such signals, with no single one privileged.
3. Boundary geometry: a point defined in one section; a numerically defined age; an interval whose top is necessarily open because the unit is still unfolding; a diachronous surface; or deliberately multiple boundaries for multiple purposes.
4. Date: Neolithic or early Holocene; 1610; the industrial revolution; the mid-twentieth century; or the claim that the demand for a single date is itself the mistake.
5. Authority: the formal ratification procedure of the stratigraphic bodies; some wider body, whether mixed natural and social science or intergovernmental; no authority at all, because usage settles the meaning; or the claim that the fact that a small committee of geologists gets to rule on this is itself the problem.
6. What the unit is for: correlating strata; organising interdisciplinary research; legal or political recognition, as in ecocide, rights of nature, or liability; teaching; or the diagnosis that the term names a political situation rather than measuring one.

Leaves must be named positions, each carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Include at least these as leaves: the mid-twentieth-century proposal built on a plutonium spike in a single Canadian lake; the early-Anthropocene hypothesis; the 1610 reading of the colonial collapse and the reforestation that followed it, the Orbis spike; the Capitalocene; the Plantationocene; the event-not-epoch position; the informal-term-only position, which refuses the ratification question altogether; a marker position that takes the industrial reconfiguration of the biosphere, down to the bones of industrially farmed chickens and other technofossils, as the diagnostic signal; and a position that holds the Holocene simply has not ended and there is nothing to define. Mark any cell the axes generate that nobody has named as unnamed.

Then add two short sections. First, a table of cells in your grid that no one occupies, each with a one-line diagnosis: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Second, a closing paragraph on what your tree cannot do. Flag every pair of leaves the questions above fail to separate, and state what further question would separate them. I expect trouble distinguishing positions that agree on a date but disagree about what the date is for, and positions that reject the geological framing while still wanting a stratigraphic marker. If your tree cannot handle either, say so plainly rather than smoothing it over; a visible gap is more useful to me than a false clean partition.

Format: roughly 1000 to 1500 words. Use an indented outline or an ASCII tree for the structure itself, and prose only where prose is needed. No citations, no reading lists, no parenthetical years attached to authors: I know the literature and my students do not need it, and I do not want the handout to stand or fall on attribution. Where a position is standardly known by a coinage, use the coinage; otherwise describe it by its commitments.
