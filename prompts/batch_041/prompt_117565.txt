My roommate and I have a running bet and I want a tiebreaker. I want to build a box split in two by a wall with a tiny flap in it. A little sensor watches the air, and whenever a fast molecule on the left comes up to the flap it opens and lets it through, and slow ones on the right get let back the other way. The flap is basically a gate, opening it doesn't cost anything, and the sensor runs on a coin battery. Do this long enough and one side ends up hot and the other cold, and then I stick a little turbine between them and get free electricity. He says it's impossible, but the only reason he gives is "you can't beat entropy," which is just restating the thing we're arguing about. I even thought up his best objection for him: the sensor has to remember what it saw in order to decide, and writing that down costs energy. So here's my fix -- I make the sensor never erase anything. It just keeps one long log forever, so there's no erasing to pay for. The log gets huge but who cares, I'll never read it. Where's the flaw in that? I don't actually want free power, I just want to know which of us is wrong, and I'm pretty sure its me, but exactly why.
