Spent most of last night arguing with my flatmate about that Dennett thought experiment where his brain ends up in a vat in one city and the body it's wired to is in another, and by the end neither of us could say what would actually settle which one he's in — that's the thing I want the poem to catch. Make it a pantoum, four or five quatrains: the repeat-and-migrate thing where a stanza's second and fourth lines come back as the first and third of the next, and if the form makes the ending loop back into the opening, good, that loop is the radio link. The lines that come round again should be the ones that change sides — a line that's plainly the body talking the first time should come back as the brain talking and still be true, with the 'here' in it pointing somewhere else by then. Two things to work in. First, the twist where the seeing happens from somewhere the body isn't, so the point of view and the body come apart, and let that fight the gut feeling that he's wherever the body is. Second, my flatmate's line, which I couldnt beat on the night: that 'where am I' is like asking where the centre of gravity of a heap of sand is, a real answer to a question that was never about a fact. Dont resolve it at the end — end on the wobble, the spot where you can't tell whether 'here' is naming a location or naming a point of view. Someone who knows the case should be able to tick it off detail by detail, and someone who's never heard of it should still come away with a poem. British English please. No reading list, obviously.
