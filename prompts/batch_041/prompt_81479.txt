So my city has one of those effective-altruism-ish reading groups that also runs a little giving circle — eight of us, we pool a few thousand bucks a year and have to agree where it goes. This month it turned into a two-hour fight between two people in the group, and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning, so I'm outsourcing the verdict.

The fight was about the criteria we're supposed to rank causes by — importance, tractability, neglectedness, that trio. Marco and Dana went maybe six rounds. Roughly how it went:

Marco opened by saying neglectedness shouldn't be its own criterion at all. It's just a stand-in for expected marginal value — what you actually care about is impact per dollar at the margin, and "neglected" is a cheap proxy for "lots of room left before returns flatten out." He said once you see that, keeping it as a seperate third axis is a mistake, because it double-counts against importance (a cause can be hugely important and also crowded) and it quietly pushes you toward whatever nobody's looking at, including things nobody's looking at because they're bad bets.

Dana said that's backwards. If you ignore who else is already working on something, you systematically overrate the crowded causes, because they look tractable and well-understood precisely because they're crowded — the easy wins are already taken by all the other actors on the margin, so your dollar buys the leftovers. She said neglectedness isn't a proxy for marginal value, it's a separate fact about the *other actors*, a correction for the fact that you're not the only one spending here. And she said importance-times-tractability on its own would've told us to fund whatever already has the most money and talent pointed at it.

Marco came back: fine, but if neglectedness is really about the other actors, then it's really about *your* counterfactual impact — what happens differently because *you* gave — and that belongs inside the impact-per-dollar estimate or inside tractability, not sitting out as a co-equal third thing. He said multiplying three crude proxies together compounds their errors and lets a cause score great just for being lonely. A cause can be maximally neglected — literally zero other actors — and completely worthless. Then he raised a second thing: neglect is often *evidence against* a cause, since alot of things are neglected because people tried them and it didn't pan out, so you can't read neglect as a plus without independent info.

Dana granted the arithmetic, sort of. She said the single expected-value-per-dollar number is exactly the thing we *don't* have — if we had it we wouldn't need a framework in the first place, that's the whole reason we're using a heuristic. And under uncertainty she thinks partly-redundant proxies are a feature, not a bug: each one catches a different failure mode, so the overlap is a robustness check. On lonely-and-worthless she said that's just neglectedness being necessary rather than sufficient, which nobody claimed. On the selection worry she flipped it: you only ever observe the *current* level of attention, and there are structural reasons valuable work gets neglected — career incentives, funding fashions, outcomes that are hard to measure — so neglect is at least as much a prompt to look closer as a warning sign. She admitted the weights between the three criteria are basically arbitrary, but said that's okay for a triage tool.

They went a couple more rounds and neither budged. So — I'm the bystander here, I don't have a dog in this. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me who's right where? I don't want an overall scorecard like "Dana wins 3-2"; I want each move rated and explained, including the ones where they're both partly right or where they're just talking past each other. Then tell me what both of them are missing.
