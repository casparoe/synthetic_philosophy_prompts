ok so I'm opening a talk next month for a group that works on mechanistic interpretability and I want to start with a poem instead of a slide, mostly to annoy them, partly because the thing I'm stuck on is more of a poem problem then a paper problem.

Here's the stuck thing. The pitch of mechanistic anomaly detection is that you don't just watch what a system does, you watch how it's doing it, and if the insides look wrong -- even when the behavior is exactly what you predicted -- you flag it. Fine. But that only means anything if I already know what counts as the same mechanism, and I can't get a grip on it. Same parts, different wiring: same mechanism? Different parts, same wiring? A net retrained from scratch that happens to compute the identical function -- is that a different mechanism doing the same thing, or the same thing done twice? A river carves a canyon and an excavator makes the same shape: the groove is the same groove, the mechanism is water versus diesel, and I honestly don't know which of those facts my detector is supposed to care about.

So write me a pantoum about it. Five quatrains, and I want the ending to fold back onto the opening lines so the whole poem is a loop. The form has to be the argument and not just the container: a pantoum keeps handing you lines you've already heard, in an order you didn't choose, and that is exactly the thing I can't decide -- is this the same mechanism coming around again, or a new one that just rhymes with the old one? If you can get the recurring lines to read as the same computation the first time and as something quietly shifted the third time, without changing a word, you'll have done more for me than another diagram.

And don't hedge on me. Pick a side: parts, causal role, or input-output profile, one of them, and let the poem land on it and take the hit. Committed isn't the same as tidy -- I don't want it dissolving into 'who can say', I want it to choose and then pay for the choice. Then, in three or four lines of prose after the poem, tell me where it cheats: which individuation it leans on that it hasn't earned, and where it smuggles in a level of description it never justifies. A poem with a spine and a confession beats a survey of the options.
