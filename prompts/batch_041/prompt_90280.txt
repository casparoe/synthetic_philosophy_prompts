ok so next month I'm presenting at our consciousness reading group and I need help prepping for the Q&A. My thesis is a bit unusual and I want the objections I'll actually get, not the generic ones people lob at panpsychism.

The thesis: the combination problem isn't solved, it's dissolved — but not in the cheap "maybe combination just happens, stop asking" way. My claim is that the whole problem rests on a hidden assumption that a subject of experience is a countable thing with sharp boundaries, something you could in principle point at and say "there's exactly one of those here." I want to argue that subjecthood comes in degrees: it's a matter of how densely integrated a phenomenal field is, and there's no fact of the matter about how many subjects a brain hosts, any more than there's a fact of the matter about how many grains make a heap. So the demand to "show me the mechanism by which micro-experiences merge into one subject" presupposes a metaphysics of subjects we should reject anyway. Micro-experiences are real; they're just not owned by micro-subjects. I also want to say the unity of an experience is adverbial — it's a way the experiencing gets done, not a further item that gets added on top.

The audience: mostly physicalist philosphers of mind, two of them pretty hostile to anything panpsychist and one who's a card-carrying illusionist (thinks phenomenal consciousness is a fiction the brain tells itself). There's also a neuroscientist who'll keep asking what would even count as evidence, and one panpsychist — a cosmopsychist who thinks the cosmos is the only genuine subject and everything else is derivative, so she'll come at me from the opposite direction and say I've just re-described the decombination problem and called it progress.

I want a prep sheet: the 8 or so objections this room will actually raise, in the order they'll come (and separately, ranked by how much damage they do), phrased the way these people would phrase them — the illusionist's version of a worry isnt the physicalist's version, and neither is the cosmopsychist's. For each one: what I should concede, what I should hold, and a two-minute reply, plus a one-line note on where that reply is soft. And tell me the one question I'm secretly hoping nobody asks.
