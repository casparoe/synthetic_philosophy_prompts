Write an essay on whether culture can be a cause, using the thesis usually credited to Max Weber — that the Protestant ethic, or something in the religious culture of early modern Protestantism, helped bring about the spirit and institutions of modern capitalism — as the test case. Reach and defend a verdict; do not end on a survey of positions.

Two pieces of scaffolding I would like you to work with, and eventually to assess rather than merely apply.

First, a fourfold classification of the roles a cultural factor might play: difference-maker (its presence or absence changes whether or how the outcome occurs); enabling condition (it makes an outcome available without selecting among the available outcomes); constitutive element (it is part of what the outcome is, rather than something that brings it about); and mere accompaniment (it correlates with the outcome but does no explanatory work). Which of these can the Weberian explanation sustain, and does its defender need to claim more than the strongest of them?

Second, an evidential test: a cultural factor earns the title of cause only if we can specify, without anachronism, a historically adjacent case in which the factor is absent and the outcome fails, and can say what stood in its place. Does the Weber case pass? Could any interesting cultural explanation pass? And is the test fair, or does it covertly require that culture be replaced by something material before it is allowed to count?

The essay should also say whether explanation by reasons can be causal explanation at all; whether the fact that the Reformation is a single unrepeatable episode makes such theses untestable in principle or merely hard to test; whether admitting cultural causes commits one to counting every self-description a society happens to hold as causally efficacious; and what the best version of the Weberian claim predicts that its rivals do not. Conclude by saying what would have to be true for you to have come out the other way.

British English throughout, formal register, and no bibliography or literature survey.
