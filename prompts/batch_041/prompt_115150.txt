An argument a colleague gave me seems to refute Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge, but I want to keep the theory and am unsure which premise to abandon. (1) I know I have hands. (2) I know that if I have hands, I am not a brain in a vat. (3) If I know a proposition and know that it entails another, then I know the entailed proposition. (4) So I know I am not a brain in a vat. (5) But on Nozick's tracking theory I do not know I am not a brain in a vat: were I a brain in a vat, I would still believe I am not one, so my belief fails the sensitivity condition. (6) So Nozick's tracking theory is false. Which of (1)–(3) should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If the answer is (3), explain what epistemic closure is doing for us in ordinary cases of extending knowledge by deduction, and say whether giving it up is a tolerable price or a fatal concession to the skeptic. Also address whether rejecting (3) commits me to the odd claim that I know I have hands but do not know that I am not a brain in a vat, and whether that claim is merely counterintuitive or genuinely incoherent.
