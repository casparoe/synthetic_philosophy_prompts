Two friends of mine got into an argument at my place last night and I couldnt referee it, so I'm outsourcing it.

The setup: we'd been talking about that Mind's I bit where somebody's preserved brain is kept alive and interviewed, and I asked whether the "brain" even has to be a brain — like, could a big enough book be conscious? Then it kicked off.

Dev said yeah, sure, a book could be conscious. Write down, page after page, the complete state of every neuron and every connection in a working brain, bind it, and you've got a mind in there — just a frozen one. All the information is present. Our having to flip through it one page at a time is a fact about us, not about the book.

Mira said thats nonsense. She said a book isn't a mind, it's a description of a mind, and the difference is causation. In a brain the parts push on each other — this neuron's firing makes that one fire. In a book nothing makes anything happen. Page 40 doesn't cause page 41. The only thing linking the pages is me reading them in order, and that's me doing the work, not the book. So the consciousness, if there's any, is in the reader.

Dev came back: fine, but a brain at a single instant is just as static as the book. Nothing in it "causes" anything either once you freeze it. Yet we don't say a person is conscious only over time and not at any moment. So if a frozen instant of a brain can be conscious, then a frozen book encoding that same instant should be too. Mira said the frozen slice is conscious only because of the dynamics it's a slice of; pull the slice out of any process and it's just a pattern of atoms, same as the book.

Then Dev tried this: okay, what if the book is arranged so the ink itself is the causal structure — like a Rube Goldberg machine printed on the pages, where page 1 physically trips page 2 and so on down the stack? Conscious then? Mira said now it's not a book anymore, it's a machine, and she'd happily say the machine might be conscious, but "the book" was never the right object to point at. Dev said that's cheating, because all she did was move a word — the ink is the same ink, the only thing that changed is the counterfactuals about what would happen if things were nudged. Mira said exactly, and the counterfactuals are the whole ballgame.

Dev's last move: so then whether this thing is conscious can depend on stuff outside the thing — on whether some other system is running it, or reading it, or standing in the right causal relations to it. He said that makes consciousness relational, which is weird. Mira said clocks are like that, a stopped clock is still a clock but is it telling the time, and she didn't see the problem.

I honestly couldn't tell who was winning. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall — and rate each move? And tell me what both of them are missing; I have a feeling there's a distinction neither of them is making and I cant put my finger on it.
