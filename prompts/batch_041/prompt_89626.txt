After thirty years at the bench I have retired and have spent the past several reading philosophy of biology, and before I commit any of my conclusions to print I would like an audit of where I have landed on the concept of function. My commitments are these. First, the selected-effects account is correct for the central biological notion: the function of a token trait is the effect for which it was selected in the lineage that produced it. Second, the causal-role account is correct for what working physiologists — my former self included — actually mean when they say what a part does, and I do not believe the two accounts should be ranged against each other, since they answer different questions. Third, function ascriptions are normative: to say that the heart's function is to pump is to say that a heart ought to pump, and a heart that fails to do so is malfunctioning. Fourth, a trait can have a function the very first time it appears, before selection has had any opportunity to act on it; I have in mind a novel mutation arising in a single organism. Fifth, function statements explain, in that they tell us why the trait is there and not merely what it does. Please tell me which of these can be held together and which cannot, ordering the conflicts by how serious they are, separating the genuine incompatibilities from the ones that only look like conflicts, and for each genuine conflict stating which commitment would have to be surrendered or weakened and what that surrender would cost me. Kindly write in continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — the way one would write a letter.
