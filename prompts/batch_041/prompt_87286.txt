Alright, so heres a thing I've noticed about myself and I can't find a word for it.

When I'm actually in the middle of using an AI chatbot — mid-conversation, trying to get it to do something — I explain what it's doing in mental terms without even noticing I'm doing it. It thinks I want the short version. It's confused because I switched topics. It's being lazy. And these aren't idle metaphors, they're doing real work: they're how I decide what to type next. If I think it's confused I clarify, and the clarification usually lands. So the mental description is earning its keep.

Then an hour later, talking about the same system, I'll say the flat opposite with just as much confidence. It doesn't think anything. There's no "it" in there. It's a next-token predictor and the "confusion" is a distribution. And I believe that too, it's not sloppiness.

What bugs me is that the two don't cancel out. I know the move where you say belief-talk is a useful predictive stance you adopt on purpose (Dennett-ish, right?), and that the two levels aren't really competing because they answer different questions. I get it, and it still doesn't dissolve anything, because in the moment it doesn't feel like I'm picking a useful fiction — it feels like I'm just reading off what it actually thinks. And the fiction reading can't be the whole story either, since the mental description sometimes outpredicts any mechanical story I could tell.

So is there a name for this? Not for the intentional stance and not for anthropomorphism — both of those are about a stance you *take*, and I'm after the fact that both descriptions feel forced on me at once and feel incompatible, and that knowing better doesn't stop it. Someone suggested it's like Strawson's participant stance vs. objective stance, but I don't think that's quite it, since that one seems to run through blame and responsibility, and this happens to me in situations where nothing moral is at stake at all. (Though — separate question — I do also get a little flash of irritation when it ignores a clear instruction, and I'm not sure if that's the same phenomenon or a different one bolted on.)

If there's no established term, I'd rather you coin one and define it carefully than wave at "the intentional stance." What I actually want is the carving: what exactly is the phenomenon, how is it different from the nearest things, and where are the edges? Does it apply to my dog? A corporation? A chess engine? A thermostat — because with a thermostat I don't feel the pull at all, and I want to know what changes.
