ok so for my philosophy club thing (not graded but I actually want it to be good) I'm stuck on this spectrum argument thing and I want you to compare the two main ways people get out of it and tell me which one actually works.

The puzzle, in case you need it: imagine a long chain of bad things where each one is only a tiny bit worse than the one before. One mosquito bite, two mosquito bites, three... eventually you're at a whole year of torture. The torture is obviously way worse than one bite. But every single step in the chain is too small to matter, and if no step matters then the last thing isnt worse than the first either, wich is insane. So something in there has to be false.

The two responses I keep running into:

1. The fuzzy ranks view. Transitivity is fine, there really is a fact about which of any two things is worse, but our comparisons go vague when the gap is tiny, so we can never say which link in the chain is the broken one. The chain does break, we just can't point at it.

2. The loops view. Transitivity is exactly what we give up. Every step really is worse than the one before it, and the end of the chain really can be worse than the start, even though worse-than doesnt chain together. So no premise is false, no link is broken, the argument's shape is just wrong.

What I want from you: (a) lay out each one in your own words like you're explaining it to someone who's never heard of it, (b) say which one handles the actual puzzle better and defend it, and (c) tell me what each one has to give up in order to work, and whether that price seems worth paying. Somebody in my club also told me that if you pick view 1 you end up with view 2 anyway, because roughly-as-bad-as isnt transitive either, so I'd like you to deal with that if you think it's a real objection.

Last thing: does any of this make a practical difference for picking between two real options in my own life, like two summer jobs that are slightly better and worse than each other in a bunch of small ways, or is it one of those debates that only matters inside philosophy? If it does matter, say how. If it doesnt, say that too and tell me why it's still worth thinking about.
