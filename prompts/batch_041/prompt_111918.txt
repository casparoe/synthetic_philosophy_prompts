ok so I got voluntold to bring the closing poem to the last meeting of our antinatalism reading group and I've been circling it for a week. What I dont want is a poem about how life is sad, or about how nobody should have kids. Everybody in the room already knows where they stand on that. I want a poem about the four cases, since thats the part of Benatar that does all the work and the part everyone actually fights about. In order: pain present is bad; pleasure present is good; pain absent is good even though theres nobody there to have it; pleasure absent is not bad, unless theres somebody who's been deprived of it. Call them 1 through 4.

Heres the constraint, and its the whole reason I want this as a poem instead of just saying it out loud. Cases 3 and 4 have to be a minimal pair. Same syntax, same rhythm, same number of lines, same line lengths, and as few words different as you can get away with, ideally one, two at most. Keep it unrhymed so the difference cant hide in the rhyme. If I covered up the words and looked at just those two stanzas side by side, I shouldnt be able to tell which is which. Which means the entire asymmetry ends up sitting on that one word. And I want the poem to know its sitting there. Not a wink, not a punchline, just the poem noticing that a word is carrying an arguement.

Reason I'm stuck on this: I think the asymmetry is less a claim about value than a claim about grammar. Good can float free. Pain absent is good and it doesnt need an owner. Bad cant float free. Pleasure absent is only bad if theres somebody it happened to, and if nobodys there theres nothing for the bad to grab onto. So the one word separating 3 and 4 is basically the whole philosophy, and I want somebody who's never heard any of this to feel that the two stanzas look identical and are being asked to say opposite things. Please dont do the thing where 3 gets a line and 4 gets blank space. I've seen that and it just reads as the poem agreeing that 4 is nothing.

Speaker should be somebody who buys the arguement and is a little embarrassed that it turns on that one word. Not a convert, not a scold, not a eulogy for the unborn. Dont resolve it. Last line should leave both the person who accepts the asymmetry and the person who thinks 3 and 4 are the same move still standing there. No epigraph, no footnotes, dont quote Benatar at me, I dont want the poem leaning on anybody's actual sentences.

Otherwise: loose and speakable, 16-24 lines, four stanzas, unrhymed, flat title thats not a thesis statement. It has to work read out loud, thats the venue.
