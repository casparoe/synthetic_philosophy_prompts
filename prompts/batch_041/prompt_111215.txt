You are a philosopher of the social sciences with expertise in game theory, convention, and cultural evolution. I am preparing a graduate seminar unit on how norms evolve, and I need a tiered ranking rather than a survey. Please think carefully. Before you rank anything, brainstorm in a short paragraph the criteria an adequate explanation of norm evolution must satisfy, for example whether it can account for arbitrary content, local stability, cross-community variation, sudden collapse, and change without central enforcement. Then give the ranking.

The explanatory target is a phenomenon I will call the inheritance illusion. A community follows a norm, say that the first person to enter a shared kitchen must wipe the counter, whoever made the mess. The norm is arbitrary, since some other rule would work as well; it is locally stable across generations; and it is not enforced by any central authority. It persists partly because each cohort treats it as an inherited commitment that earlier cohorts deliberately chose or agreed to, even though no one can identify the moment of choice and the norm would probably not be selected from scratch today. I am especially interested in cases where the norm is stable under small perturbations but collapses when the composition of the group changes suddenly, and where it is transmitted along with a false origin story about its purpose.

Rank the following theoretical resources into tiers by how much you would trust them, on their own or in combination, to predict the actual trajectory of such norms across generations. Use four tiers: tier 1, would trust to predict; tier 2, useful but incomplete; tier 3, mostly redescribes the phenomenon; tier 4, misleading if used alone. For each item, give one sentence of justification. Where items are complementary, say so. Where they compete, say which you would drop.

- David Lewis's account of convention as a regularity sustained by mutual expectation and a preference to conform conditional on others' conformity

- Repeated-game folk-theorem explanations, on which indefinite repetition sustains cooperation
