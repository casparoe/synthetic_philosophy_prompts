"Gödel's second incompleteness theorem says no consistent formal theory can prove its own consistency. Löb's theorem is the sharper cousin: for any sentence P, if a theory proves 'if P is provable in the theory, then P is true,' then the theory just proves P outright. Put those together and you get the Löbian obstacle. An agent whose reasoning is formalised in a theory T can never prove that T's own proofs are trustworthy — it can't prove the schema 'if T proves φ, then φ is true.' So it can't trust a successor agent built on T either, because trusting that successor would mean proving the successor's proofs are sound, and that's exactly the thing T can't do. The upshot is that recursive self-improvement is a dead end for any agent that reasons by formal proof; the only ways out are to bolt on a consistency axiom and get yourself a stronger theory, or to ditch proof entirely and go probabilistic. Which is basically why the alignment crowd concluded a fully self-trusting agent is impossible and gave up on building one."
