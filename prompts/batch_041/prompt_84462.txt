I'm a novelist working on a book about an AI alignment team, and my technical consultant -- my old roommate, who now does reward-learning research -- keeps telling me my skeptic character is too easy to knock down. So I want to rehearse the skeptical side against someone good instead. Please play a committed inverse-reinforcement-learning researcher: someone who genuinely believes you can recover a person's real preferences from their choices, who knows all the standard objections and has real answers to them, sharp but not smug, happy to give ground on details but never on the core claim. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep it to a paragraph or so per turn so it feels like an actual conversation.

Here's my opening move. You claim you can read my preferences off my behavior, but I can always cook up a reward function under which whatever I just did is exactly the optimal thing to do, so my behavior by itself never rules anything out. And even after you've found one that fits, if I add any constant to my reward, or add any function of the current state that changes from state to state -- the potential-shaping trick -- then every single choice I make stays byte-for-byte the same. So "the" preference you hand back to me is one of infinitely many reward functions that fit my behavior perfectly, and you've got no behavioral evidence whatsoever to choose among them. So tell me what you're actually recovering -- and give me a reason to beleive it's what I want, rather than just a tidy summary of what I did. And while you're at it, handle the nastier version: maybe there's no fact of the matter about what I "really" want, independent of the choices themselves, in which case it's not clear there's anything there for you to recover in the first place.
