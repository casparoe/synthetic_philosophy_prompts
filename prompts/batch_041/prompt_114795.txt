I am the student representative on my district's social studies curriculum committee, and we vote Thursday on two competing outlines for the new modern world history course. Outline A builds each unit around individual leaders and their decisions; Outline B builds each unit around structural forces such as economic collapse, imperial legacies, treaty systems, and media technology, treating leaders mostly as symptoms. The case that split the committee is the rise of the Nazi regime: one side argues that without Hitler's particular choices the regime does not happen, while the other argues that the structural conditions made some authoritarian outcome close to inevitable and that centering one villain distorts causation and lets institutions off the hook. Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete decision, either one outline or a specific hybrid, with a timeline for adoption, and it should engage the strongest version of the losing side rather than a strawman. It should also run the Nazi case through both outlines to show which handles counterfactual reasoning better, and it should evaluate a required exercise I have proposed, in which students rank a unit's events on a scale from nearly inevitable to hinged on a single decision, and say how that exercise should be scored. Write in American English, without citations or a reading list, and keep it short enough to read aloud in about five minutes.
