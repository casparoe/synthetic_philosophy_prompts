Write the joint statement that both camps in a long-running dispute could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level. The first camp holds that any system exhibiting a tangled hierarchy — a loop in which each level appears to generate or contain the level above it, as in Escher's two hands drawing each other — must be anchored by an inviolate level: a stratum that fixes the rules and is not itself subject to them. The second camp holds that the inviolate level is not a feature of such systems at all, but an artifact of how we describe them, and that self-reference is fully accounted for by ordinary coding and quoting devices, with nothing left over once the mechanics are in view.

Produce the document such a collaboration would yield: a numbered section of claims both camps can sign, covering what a tangled hierarchy is, what stands in need of explanation, and what the bare existence of self-reference does and does not establish; then the precise points of remaining disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; then the cruxes — among them whether the inviolate level is discovered or stipulated, whether the deflationist's coding devices merely relocate it into a metalanguage, and whether any part of the dispute is verbal — and, for each crux, a case or argument both camps agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Both camps must also address two shared test cases: a constitution whose amendment procedure can itself be amended, including a clause declaring itself unamendable; and a program that rewrites and recompiles its own source code. State what each camp expects in each case, and whether any conceivable observation could settle the difference between those expectations. If the camps turn out to agree about every test case, say so plainly rather than manufacturing a disagreement.

Write in American English, in roughly 1,200 words.
