Ok so I'm putting together a small anthology of fiction about concepts that don't usually get fiction written about them, and I need a piece on sphexishness. You know the thing — the wasps that drag a cricket back to the burrow, go inside to check it, and if you nudge the cricket an inch while they're in there they come back out, drag it over, and check again, forever, never seeming to notice nothing changed. The label people slap on that is sphexishness, and the scary version of it is that a creature can look like it's making a judgment call when it's really just running a fixed loop.

Here's the twist I want the story to actually live in, so it isn't just the wasp thing with a hat on: put the rigidity one level up. Not a creature stuck in a loop, but an agent — doesn't have to be biological — with genuinely flexible, clever, situation-sensitive behavior, except that the rule it uses to decide when it's being flexible vs. when it's stuck is itself fixed and blind. It can improvise about everything except the question of whether it's improvising. And tell it from that thing's point of view, so we're inside a mind that feels free while some part of it is doing the wasp's little check-the-burrow dance.

Setting and specifics are yours, but I want real scenes and a real character, not a thought experiment with names glued on. Don't end on a reveal where it turns out to be a robot or a sim — the interesting version is that it could be us, and I'd definately rather the story make that queasy than make it clever.

When you're done, tack on a short note with no hedging: wich reading of sphexishness you ended up committing to — is being sphexish a fact about the mechanism, or about the description we bring to it? — and where the story takes liberties with the idea or leans on a contested take. If you think the one-level-up version isn't really sphexishness at all, say so and defend it.
