Alright, I've been going down a rabbit hole on the conditional analysis of dispostions — the idea that calling a glass fragile just means that if you struck it, it would break — and I'm stuck on the finks-vs-masks thing. I get the standard story. A fink is when something would take the disposition away at the last second: the live wire that some gadget kills the instant you touch it, so touching it never shocks you, yet we still want to say the wire is live. A mask is when the disposition is still there but something gets in the way of it showing itself: the glass is fragile, but it's wrapped in foam, so striking it won't break it. In both cases the simple conditional comes out false while we still want to attribute the disposition, so both are counterexamples to the analysis.

What I don't get is what's actually doing the work in the fink/mask distinction. The usual line seems to be: does the interfering thing remove the disposition, or does it just block its manifestation? But that already assumes we know what the disposition is and where its boundaries are, which is the very thing in dispute. So is there a principled, non-circular way to sort a case into fink or mask? Take the wire — the gadget never touches the wire, it just stops conduction, so why isn't that a mask? Could you redescribe any fink as a mask, or any mask as a fink, just by redrawing where you think the disposition lives? And here's the case that broke my brain: a pill that, if you swallowed it, would make you stop wanting to swallow it. Is that a fink (the disposition gets cancelled), a mask (the desire is still in there, just blocked), or some third thing where the manifestation is what destroys the disposition? I've also seen it suggested that the two categories aren't really distinct — that a mask is just a fink described from a different angle. Is that right, and if so, does the distinction do any work beyond bookkeeping? And does any of this matter for the bigger fight over whether dispositions are genuine causal powers or just shorthand for microstructure plus laws of nature?
