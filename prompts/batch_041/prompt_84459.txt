For a seminar on the timing of experience — the Orwellian-vs-Stalinesque thing about the color phi effect. You flash a red dot, then a blue dot somewhere else, and subjects swear they saw a single dot move and change color mid-flight, before the second dot was even on screen. So either the experience got rewritten after the fact (Orwell) or the whole thing was assembled before it ever became conscious at all (Stalin). Now say a lab runs a paradigm where a fast secondary task makes people report the unrevised version — red, then blue, no mid-flight switch. Rank these follow-ups by how much each would actually push you toward the Stalinesque reading: (1) a priming measure also shows the unrevised content, which the subject can't report; (2) the revision window scales with how far apart the dots are, like it's tracking travel time, instead of sitting at a fixed delay; (3) subjects who only get the secondary task afterwards still end up with the revised percept; (4) trained subjects report the unrevised percept but still insist they saw the change. For each one: real evidence, or just a relabeling? And does any of it make the whole Orwell/Stalin framing look confused rather than settled?
