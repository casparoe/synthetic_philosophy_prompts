ok so I've been living abroad on and off for years and I've accumulated a pile of opinions about everyday etiquette stuff — tipping, queues, jaywalking, which side of the escalator you stand on — and I want a sanity check on whether they actually hang together before I inflict them on my reading group.

here's where I've landed:

(a) conventions like tipping norms, queue discipline, whether you jaywalk, which side of the escalator you stand on, are not moral requirements. they're local equilibria. ignoring them is at most a faux pas.

(b) but a visitor who knowingly flouts a local convention is doing something wrong — not rude, wrong — even when nobody's enforcing it and it costs nobody anything. like barging to the front of a single-file queue in a country where that queue is basically sacred.

(c) and that's the whole story of why it's wrong: they're taking the benefit of a practice (getting served, getting where they're going) while opting out of its cost.

(d) politeness is not a moral category at all. there is no such thing as a moral obligation to be polite or to avoid giving offence.

(e) whether a convention is real, or "in force", depends on what people actually expect, not on what they'd say if you asked them or what's printed on a sign. where I live there's a bakery with a ticket machine that every regular ignores — they just catch the baker's eye — and a newcomer taking a ticket is the slightly rude move. the real queue isn't the one the machine describes. a norm everyone privately thinks is silly but follows because everyone else follows it is fully in force.

(f) when a local convention is unjust — say a tipping norm that lets employers pay below-subsistence wages — you're not just allowed to break it, you should, and the fact that locals would be upset and think you're freeloading counts for nothing.

so: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad is each real one? rank them rather than telling me they're all equally bad. for each genuine one, tell me the cheapest fix and what that fix costs me elsewhere. I keep worrying that (d) and (b) can't both be true, but maybe that's just me being paranoid. and definately don't just tell me the whole set is inconsistent — some of these are obviously fine together, and if you had to change exactly one thing, I want to know which.
