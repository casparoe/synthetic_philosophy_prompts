The debate as I've pieced it together, roughly: Popper wanted science to make progress toward truth even though our best theories are false, so he needed a way to rank false theories by closeness to truth. His own version was two-factor — count a theory's true consequences and its false consequences, better = more of the true ones, fewer of the false ones. Then in 1974 Tichý, Harris and Miller (all in BJPS that year, I think) showed that on that definition no false theory comes out closer to the truth than another false theory, which guts the whole project. There's a separate objection I associate with Miller, that you can permute the primitive predicates of the language and reverse the ranking, so closeness to truth isn't invariant under re-describing the world. Then the fixes: the likeness approach, where you put a distance between possible worlds and score a theory by how close the worlds it permits are to the actual one (Tichý and Hilpinen started it, Niiniluoto and Oddie developed it); and the relevant-consequence approach, where you only count "relevant" consequences (Schurz and Weingartner, Gemes, Mortensen). Correct me where I've garbled that, but do the garbled versions first and then correct them, so I can see which mistakes came from my frame and which were just me.
