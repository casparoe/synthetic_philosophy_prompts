For the past two years I have been a volunteer guide at the small local-history museum in the town where I live, and I have now been asked to write the text for a new panel explaining why our town industrialized early — why the mills, the canal traffic, and the coal trade arrived here when they did rather than a generation later, or not at all. My difficulty is that everything I can say about us was equally true of three or four towns in the neighbouring valleys, which stayed agricultural. A visitor put this to me directly last month: if the water, the coal, and the merchant money were present in all of those places, why did it happen here? I said something about our local families being more enterprising, and heard myself inventing it as I spoke. Our own first mill was built, burned down, and rebuilt by three successive owners before it turned a profit, which makes the outcome look less like a fact about the place than a run of luck.

Since then I have noticed that the question seems to mean different things to different visitors. When someone from the next valley asks it, they appear to want a comparison between two particular places. When a visitor from far away asks it, the question seems to be about this corner of the country as a whole, and what made it different from the rest of the world. One man last week seemed to be asking why the good fortune landed here rather than somewhere it might have done more good. I doubt that one kind of answer fits all three, and I have begun to suspect that the question, as it is normally asked, may not have an answer at all — though saying that to visitors feels like a dodge.

I would like your advice on what an honest response actually looks like in a case of this kind. If our town was simply fortunate, is that an explanation or an admission that we have none, and how does a museum say so without either pretending to a knowledge it lacks or leaving visitors with the impression that history is arbitrary? Should the panel answer the question, or should it explain why the question needs to be pinned down before it can be answered, and would that second option be a failure on our part? I am not asking for a reading list — the text goes to the printer in three weeks and I would not have time for it — but I would rather not install a tidy story that anyone who knows the neighbouring valleys would wince at.
