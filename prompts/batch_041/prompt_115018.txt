I sit on the ethics committee of a hospital in a European country where paid surrogacy is a criminal offense and egg donation is prohibited, yet our clinicians routinely refer patients to clinics in Spain, Greece, and the United States, and courts in several countries with bans like ours have lately held that the parent-child relationships people establish abroad must be recognized on their return. I have to explain this two-track arrangement to patients and to staff, and I can never say what it presupposes. Reconstruct the theory it only makes sense under: what it assumes about parenthood (genetic, gestational, or intentional), about whether the body and its capacities may be sold, and about what a prohibition is for when its results are accepted; which rival views it quietly rules out; whether its parts are consistent, given that refusing the practice at home while accepting its products seems to pull in two directions; and where the practice is merely underdetermined, so that several incompatible theories would license it equally well. I don't need citations. Give me a numbered list of exactly five items, each a couple of sentences, with no introduction or conclusion; the last item should say what would have to change in law or clinical practice if the implicit view were replaced by one you think better supported.
