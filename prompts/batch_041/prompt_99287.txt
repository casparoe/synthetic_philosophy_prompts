Three weeks ago my late great-aunt's lawyer came to my house with a story I have not been able to put down, and a request I have not answered.

Before she died, my great-aunt flipped a coin, wrote the result down, and left sealed instructions. If the coin had come up heads, the lawyer was to hand me fifty thousand dollars on my fortieth birthday, and only if, at the time the instructions were sealed, he judged me to be the sort of man who would pay five hundred dollars should the coin have come up tails. The coin came up tails. There is no fifty thousand dollars and there never was; nothing is coming to me. All that is left of the arrangement is the lawyer in my hallway asking for five hundred dollars, to be given to a charity my great-aunt was fond of, on the strength of the letter.

My wife says to show him the door, and her reasoning is hard to argue with. The coin came up tails, the money was never mine, and handing over five hundred dollars now cannot bring it back; nothing I do today can change what the coin did. And yet I want to pay, and not from sentiment. The whole point of the arrangement, as I understand it, was to find out what sort of man I am, and the man who receives the fifty thousand dollars in the other version of this story is the man who pays in this one. To refuse is to insist on being the man the coin actually landed for, and I am not certain that is the man I want the arrangement to have found. The lawyer adds, almost as an aside, that my great-aunt was a poor judge of character and had little reason to expect anything of me in particular, which I take to mean that her judgment may have been a coin toss of its own.

I have no education in this sort of thing, and I expect that people who study how to decide have a name and a long argument for exactly this shape of problem, which I do not know. Tell me what to do, and do not simply tell me to follow my gut.

Please give me your answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion, and let the fifth point be where you say how confident you are in the rest and which of your claims you are least sure of.
