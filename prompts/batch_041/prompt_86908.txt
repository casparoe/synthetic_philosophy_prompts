Before I read any more theories of content, I want the spec. Write me the desiderata for a theory of what makes a mental or neural state about something — the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, each with the case that forces it. I want at least: misrepresentation (a state that's supposed to be about F but fires when no F is around); the disjunction problem — why a state reliably triggered by two different things doesn't just mean their disjunction; content with nothing behind it (a belief about unicorns, a plan for a house that never gets built); the imperative/descriptive split (a bee's dance both reports where the nectar is and tells you to go there); and content where there's no selection history to appeal to — say a person who's worn a belt that buzzes toward north for a year and now just feels which way north is, or a net that got its job by gradient descent rather than evolution. Also say whether the theory owes us correctness in a normative sense rather than mere reliable correlation, and whether it has to give determinate content or can shrug and say "it's about the disjunction" or "about the average of the two". For each requirement, why its a requirement and not a prejudice of one camp, and mark the non-negotiable ones. Then flag any pair you think can't be had together — that's what I'm actually after. Keep any scorecard of the main contenders to a couple of lines.
