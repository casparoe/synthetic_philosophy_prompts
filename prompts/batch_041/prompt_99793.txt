My college roommate Marta is retiring after 31 years editing a small weekly paper in northern Minnesota, and I'm giving the toast at her dinner next month. She's a Kierkegaard person — not the either/or dread stuff so much as the attack on the press, the bit where the papers conjure up a public that doesn't actually exist anywhere and the age of reflection levels everybody into interchangeable units. She ran her paper on that principal: no "the community feels" or "residents are outraged," ever — you name a person or you don't print it, because otherwise you're writing for a phantom. About half the room is her newsroom, half is her family plus the Kiwanis crowd she's covered for decades, and two grad-school friends of ours will catch every reference. Write me a four-minute toast that actually takes her Kierkegaard seriously instead of just name-dropping him — and that doesn't slip into anything she'd have winced at, like praising her as a voice for the voiceless or saying she gave the town a public square. It should land as affection. American English please.
