So here's a thing that's been bugging me and I can't work out if it has a name.

A mate of mine says something like "if it rains on Saturday, the match gets called off". Fine, that's informative. Now, I already thought it was pretty likely to rain on Saturday, so the new bit barely nudges my belief about rain. But it should still do *something* to my belief that the match gets called off, because it tells me rain would be enough to kill it. And then when I sit down and actually try to update my whole set of beliefs on what he said, I can't get a single answer out. Depending on whether I read his sentence as telling me about a conditional probability, or as a plain conditional statement that's either true or false, or as just a reasonable conversational move that's OK to make when the conditional probability is high, I end up with different numbers, and honestly all three of them look defensible. And it gets worse when the thing being conditioned on is something I already half-believe. A second one: the doctor says "if the test comes back positive, we'll do the scan" — same mess, and there I really do care which update rule I'm using.

The bit that really cooks my noodle is when the conditional is about my own future opinions, like "if I read the report, I'll think the merger is a bad idea". I can see that should move me somehow, but I have no idea how, and it's not even clear it's the same kind of problem as the rain one.

Is there a name for this? Not for the rain example specifically — for the general situation where you're handed a conditional, you can see it ought to change your degrees of belief, but there's no agreed rule for *how*, and the disagreement isn't about the numbers you feed in but about what kind of thing the conditional even was. I get the feeling somebody has a term for this class of problem. Also where are the edges — is it still the same problem if the conditional has an 'unless' in it, or if it's a counterfactual, or if the antecedent is one I'd say is definately false?
