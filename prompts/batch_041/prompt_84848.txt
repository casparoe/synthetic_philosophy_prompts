Two camps have spent years talking past each other about the Gödelian analogy in GEB — the suggestion that the way a formal system can be built to speak about itself illuminates the way a mind can be aware of itself. Call them the Loop camp and the Deflation camp. The Loop camp holds that the analogy carries genuine explanatory weight: that there is a real continuity between the self-reference a system can be built to achieve and a mind's modelling of itself, and that the continuity is structural rather than decorative. The Deflation camp holds that nothing transfers: that Gödelian self-reference is a syntactic device with no explanatory residue for self-awareness, that the apparent continuity trades on an equivocation over the word self, and that every version of the analogy which appears to work quietly assumes what it set out to show.

They have agreed to an adversarial collaboration. Write the document they could both put their names to, in four parts.

1. Agreed claims. A numbered list, stated flatly, of what both camps can sign. It should cover what the diagonal construction actually delivers and what it does not; the difference between consistency and soundness, and which of the two the analogy needs; the fact that a consistent system rich enough for arithmetic cannot establish its own soundness by its own lights; and the standard a candidate analogy would have to meet in order to count as explanatory rather than merely suggestive — in particular, what it would take for a formal property to explain a mark of self-awareness rather than merely co-occur with it.

2. Disagreements. A table with one row per remaining dispute and columns for the disputed question, the formulation acceptable to the Loop camp, the formulation acceptable to the Deflation camp, and whether the row is a genuine disagreement, a merely verbal one, or two camps answering different questions. I expect at least one row to come out verbal; where that happens, say which term or distinction the parties were loading differently.

3. Cruxes. For each genuine row, the sub-question that, if settled, would move at least one camp; and for each crux, a case, argument or result both camps accept as bearing on it, with each camp's prediction about it recorded in advance. Treat the following as stipulated, needing no support: (a) a system S whose current axiom set is consistent and rich enough for arithmetic, which contains a provability predicate and can prove some facts about its own proofs, which reports calibrated confidence in its own outputs and revises its axioms when that confidence drops below a threshold, and whose self-description misdescribes its own architecture in two respects — it treats its learned parameters as underived axioms, and it contains no representation of how those parameters were fixed; and (b) a system S′ that is sound, and so has every formal property the analogy could want, but contains no self-model and reports nothing about its own states. Both camps must say what they make of S and of S′, and whether either moves them.

4. A closing paragraph on what would actually settle the matter, or on why nothing would.

End with a single machine-readable line of the form

Verdict: <genuine | partly-verbal | wholly-verbal> | crux: <the one crux both camps agree is load-bearing, in at most fifteen words>
