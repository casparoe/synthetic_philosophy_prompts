I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on the modern revival of Stoicism. I keep returning to this sentence from the introduction to a mid-century translation of Epictetus's Enchiridion: "The little book by Epictetus called Enchiridion or “manual” has played a disproportionately large role in the rise of modern attitudes and modern philosophy." I understand the general claim, but I want help unpacking what "modern attitudes" could mean here and how the dichotomy of control is supposed to underwrite it. In particular, many contemporary Stoics replace Epictetus's binary with a trichotomy—things fully up to us, things we can influence but not control, and things wholly outside our agency—and I'm not sure whether the passage's historical claim depends on the strict binary or is better read through that later trichotomy. What is the most defensible way to understand the passage, and what does the choice between binary and trichotomy change about the modern revival's self-understanding?
