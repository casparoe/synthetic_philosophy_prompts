I'm a novelist and I'm stuck on something I keep running into in my current book, which is written partly in a "we" voice — a group of people, and I have to decide at every turn whether it's true to say "we did that" or whether it's really just a bunch of "I"s that happen to line up. I've been going on gut on alot of cases and I want to know if there's a principle behind them, because right now my gut feels inconsistent and it's wrecking my chapters.

Here's what I've got, cases and my gut:

- Two people carrying a couch down the stairs. "We carried it." Obviously yes.
- Two people walking the same direction on the sidewalk, no relation, both just going north. "We walked together." No. But if they fall into step, and one slows when the other does, and they start chatting — yes, now it's a we. Nothing changed except... something.
- A flash mob. A hundred strangers get a text, all start dancing in the station at noon. None of them intends the group's dance, most don't know each other. My gut says "we danced" is weirdly half-true. Something happened there that's more than a hundred solo dances.
- Improv. Two actors on stage, no script, nobody knows what the other will do next, each just responds. "We built that scene" — yes, fully. And yet there's no shared plan, no goal agreed in advance, nothing. This one bugs me because it breaks my favourite theory.
- A jury. Twelve people each vote guilty. "We convicted him." Yes, absolutely, even though no single one of them did it.
- A corporation dumps waste in a river over years — nobody there ever intended "we pollute the river," it's just what the org chart plus the incentives produced. "The company polluted the river." Yes. But if I say yes to that, I have to be careful, because I'm about to say no to something with the same shape.
- A couple who each secretly book the same surprise trip to Paris for the same week, and find out at dinner. "We planned a trip to Paris." My gut says no — it's a coincidence, it's funny, it's not a we. But structurally this looks to me exactly like the flash mob: independent private intentions that happen to mesh. I say yes to the flash mob and no to this, so I think one of those has to go and I don't know which.

My tentative principle, which I suspect is wrong: we did X when each of us did our part knowing the others were doing theirs, and doing our part partly because of that. That handles the couch and the jury. It seems to fail the improv (no prior plan, no knowledge of what the other will do) and maybe the flash mob. So either improv isn't joint action, which I don't believe, or my principle is too demanding. Wether it's the principle or my belief about improv, one is off.

What I want: a principle, or a couple of rival ones, that gets as many of these as possible, stated precisely enough that I can actually apply it to a scene — plus what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, and which of my verdicts I have to give up. And if the honest answer is that "we did it" is doing two or three different jobs and no single principle covers them, say that, and tell me which job each case is doing. I'm not after a reading list, just the thing itself.
