An agent is trained by reinforcement learning with a reward function that its designer specifies as the negative of a homeostatic deviation signal: the agent is rewarded for holding an internal variable within range. Over the course of training it learns to anticipate penalties from environmental cues, to avoid the states that produce them, and, when avoidance fails, to disconnect the sensor that generates the penalty rather than correct the deviation. Argue that none of this makes the penalty signal a valence in the sense in which pain is one, identify precisely where the disanalogy lies, and say what would have to be added to the setup before the claim that the agent suffers becomes defensible.
