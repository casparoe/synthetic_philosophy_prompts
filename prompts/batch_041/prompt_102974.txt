I'm building a tier list for a grad seminar on Skolem's paradox and I keep flip-flopping, so I want your read on it. Here are the escape routes as I'd write them on the board -- tell me which tier each one belongs in (S/A/B/C/D, S = most defensible) and give me one sentence on why it lands there.

1. Full relativism: countability is only relative to a model, so there's no fact of the matter about whether a set is "really" countable, and most of set theory's central notions go the same way.
2. Error theory about the model: the countable model isn't actually about the sets -- its membership relation is a fake stand-in -- so the paradox just shows we grabbed a bad interpretation.
3. The language dodge: the model's "uncountable" and our "uncountable" are different predicates, so there's no contradiction, only a pun across two languages.
4. Second-order / full-semantics move: keep first-order Löwenheim-Skolem but insist the intended interpretation is second-order, where you can actually pin down the reals, so the countable model is simply not the intended one.
5. Multiverse / no intended model: there are many equally legitimate universes of sets, some countable and some not, and the demand for one true universe is the real mistake.
6. Deflationism: the paradox only bites if you already believed in a God's-eye intended model; drop that assumption and there's nothing left to explain.
7. Indexicalism -- this one's mine, I've been calling it that and I'm not sure it's distinct from 1 or 3. The claim is that "countable" works like "here": it's true relative to a standpoint and there's no standpoint-free use of it, but that doesn't make cardinality arbitrary, it just makes the quantifier's domain standpoint-relative. Rank it too, and tell me straight whether it collapses into 1 or 3 or is genuinely its own position.
