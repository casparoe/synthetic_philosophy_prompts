Everyone who writes about collective cognition eventually reaches for the same comparison: an ant colony is a brain and the ants are its neurons — the anthill Hofstadter has converse with Achilles, Aunt Hillary, is the canonical literary version. The comparison gets pulled in opposite directions. Those who want colony-level mentality taken seriously observe that neurons are not conscious and brains are, so why not ants and colonies; those who want to deflate it observe that no ant has any inkling of what the colony is doing, so there is nobody home. I want an audit of the comparison rather than a verdict on ants. Set out the correspondences explicitly — what plays the role of neuron, synapse, body, effector, and self — then say where the mapping breaks: the ants are agents with interests of their own, the signals are slow and low-bandwidth, there is no single set of effectors and no privileged site of integration. Then tell me which of these failures actually bear on each of the two conclusions above and which are decoration. A colleague proposes to tighten the analogy: the colony's nest-wide pheromone field is its global workspace, recruitment is its attention, the queen is its self-model, and that, she says, settles the matter in favour of a colony mind. Does her extension survive the same treatment? If the analogy cannot be repaired, what would you put in its place, and what would the replacement still get wrong?
