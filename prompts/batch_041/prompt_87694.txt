I am the assurance lead at a small lab whose product rewrites parts of its own code between runs, including the planner and the verifier that checks each change. My team signs off on every new version before it runs unattended, roughly every three weeks, and no one on the team is a logician.

Our current rule is proof-carrying: a proposed change arrives with a machine-checkable proof, in a formal logic we accept, that the change satisfies our safety specification, and my engineers want that certificate to be the entire review — if the verifier passes it, we ship it. I am uneasy, because of a limit result I can only half-reconstruct: in any consistent formal system strong enough for arithmetic, if the system proves, for some statement, that the statement is true whenever it is provable in that same system, then the system already proves that statement outright; so a system gets no new fact out of trusting its own proofs, and it cannot prove the general schema that whatever its verifier certifies is safe without proving every statement and collapsing. Someone here calls this the Löbian obstacle, and I am told the same wall appears when the system is asked to approve a successor: it cannot show that an equally strong successor is sound unless it could already show that about itself.

Please write the review procedure my team can actually run on a proposed self-modification: the questions to ask, in order; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; where the procedure returns a no; and where it must stop and hand the decision to a person because the remaining question is a trust assumption rather than a fact. It should say which soundness claims can be discharged inside the system's own logic and which have to be discharged by us, and it should be honest about the cases it cannot settle. Keep it to about a page, since engineers will run it under deadline. Then run it on two proposals: one that lets the planner skip re-verifying actions it has already certified once, and one that swaps in a stronger verifier the system claims is sound. At least one of these should come out with an answer my engineers will not like. Write in American English.
