I teach a 90-minute session of an upper-level undergraduate seminar (16 students) on personal identity and the mind, and I want a runnable activity that makes the group feel the force of the gradual-replacement problem for mind uploading before I name any of it or draw the moral.

Structure it in two phases. In the first, the group sits as a certification board: they must design tests to decide whether the occupant of a chair is conscious, and whether he is the same person as a baseline, then run those tests on two occupants, one of whom has been briefed to be functionally identical to the other; every test they invent should pass both, so that they discover for themselves that behavior and testimony cannot locate the difference. In the second phase, hand them a timeline of a gradual replacement — say a dozen stages, each recording the percentage of neural tissue swapped for silicon and the results of a standard functional battery, all normal — and require them to mark the stage at which they would stop and to state what justifies stopping there; then reveal that nothing on the timeline distinguishes one stage from another, and that the patient reports, at every stage, that nothing has changed. The rigged evidence and the rules should carry the point, so that the group arrives at the impasse rather than hearing it described.

Include the roles, the materials, a session timeline, and a decision scheme with small non-monetary stakes so the board has a reason to commit to a threshold rather than hedge. End with a debrief that maps what happened onto the two horns — that either experience survives the replacement or it can be extinguished while the subject sincerely reports continuity — and that adds the further puzzle that a fading would be noticeable whereas a sudden loss would not, connecting the board's failed tests to the corresponding thought experiments.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, especially the ways students will try to escape the impasse and how to close those off without lecturing, and be frank about which feature of the idea the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself.
