I've ended up defending a fairly deflationary line about objectivity in history and I want to know how exposed it really is. The line is this: once you strip the metaphysics out of Ranke's wish to show how things actually were, what's left is fine — objectivity isn't a matter of having no perspective, it's just letting the evidence decide and being willing to lose an argument to it. Selection isn't a problem for that, because every enquiry selects; Carr's point that a fact only becomes a historical fact once a historian picks it up is true but toothless; and White's point that historians shape their material into stories with beginnings, middles and ends is a point about literary form, not about truth. So the sceptics have shown that historians construct, but not that they can't be objective.

What would count against that, and how much should it? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones a deflationist like me has to actually concede rather than bat away. In particular: is there a version of the sceptical case that bites on the weighing of evidence itself rather than just on selection or form — and if so, is it true? Are there cases where a whole cohort of competent historians read their sources through values they couldn't see, in a way that "they just got it wrong" doesn't cover? What would move me most is a dispute that was settled by evidence where the settlement itself was an artefact of the discipline's values — show me one of those and I'm done.

And be honest about the other failure mode: has my version just changed the subject? If objectivity turns out to mean no more than "responds to evidence", have I saved anything worth having, or have I quietly handed Carr and White everything they wanted?

One aside I can't assess on my own: I've seen it said that 'eigentlich' can mean 'essentially' or 'characteristically' as much as 'actually', and that the standard rendering makes Ranke look like a naive empiricist when he wasn't one. I dont want to lean on that either way, but if it changes the picture, say so.
