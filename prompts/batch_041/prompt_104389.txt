I am putting together a position statement on religion and public reason for a seminar, and I would like an audit of my own commitments before I present them. Here is where I have landed:

(1) Legitimacy requirement: A coercive law is legitimate only if it can be justified by reasons that every citizen, conceived as free and equal, could reasonably accept.

(2) Proviso: Citizens and officials may introduce their comprehensive doctrines — religious or secular — into public political argument, provided that public reasons sufficient to support the position are also offered in due course. I understand this condition strictly: once the public reasons are on the table, the comprehensive doctrine may not be doing any residual load-bearing work, even implicitly.

(3) Wide view: It is no defect in a citizen's politics that it is integrated with her faith, and the charge that public reason forces religious believers into a kind of schizophrenia is misplaced.

(4) Freestandingness: The political conception must not depend on the truth of any comprehensive doctrine.

(5) Substantive grounding: The legitimacy requirement in (1) is itself grounded in a substantive moral ideal of citizens as free and equal and of society as a fair system of cooperation. I regard that ideal as morally loaded and non-negotiable, not as a merely procedural device.

(6) Accommodation: Sincere religious objectors should be exempted from generally applicable laws when compliance would substantially burden a practice they take to be religiously required, at least where the accommodation imposes no comparable burden on others.

I have a suspicion that some of these pull against one another, but I cannot see where, and I do not want to be told simply to abandon whichever commitment I happen to hold most loosely. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how severe each tension is, which pairs only appear to conflict and why the appearance dissolves, and, for each genuine tension, the least costly revision and what it would cost me.

Please organize the response under the following section headings, in this order:

- Summary — two to four sentences on whether the package is jointly tenable.
- Genuine Tensions, Ordered by Severity — for each, name the two commitments, explain the conflict in a full paragraph, and rate the severity as low, moderate, or high.
- Apparent Tensions That Dissolve — one short paragraph per pair, naming the distinction that resolves it.
- Cheapest Repairs and Their Costs — for each genuine tension, a brief paragraph giving the least costly revision and what it forfeits.
- The Single Most Economical Revision — one paragraph identifying the one change that would clean up the most, and why.
- Verdict — two or three sentences.

Write in American English.
