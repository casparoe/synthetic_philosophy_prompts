Two of my housemates got into it last night over how to help our friend Rosa, and it turned into an argument about something way bigger. Rosa is about to quit her PhD (she's been miserable, not sleeping, crying in the stairwell). We've all been trying to help her think it through. Here's the exchange as best I can recontruct it, Nadia first.

1. Nadia: The real question isn't what Rosa wants right now, it's what she'd want if she were fully informed and thinking clearly. If clear-headed Rosa would stay, then her 2am wish to quit isn't really her preference, it's a symptom of being burnt out. So we should be helping her stay, not helping her pack.

2. Owen: Clear-headed Rosa is a stranger with Rosa's face. You've invented a person who just happens to want what you already think is good for her. And "thinking clearly" isn't neutral — a clearer-headed Rosa might want different things, not just reason better about the same things. Where does the idealizing stop, and why is whatever's at the end of it more her than the Rosa sitting in front of you?

3. Nadia: It's not arbitrary, it's her own mind working properly. When she's rested she agrees with clear-headed Rosa — that's the whole reason we don't let people sign contracts when they're drunk or manic. And we do this constantly: telling a friend "you'll regret this in the morning," and we're usually right.

4. Owen: The drunk-contract thing only works because sober-you is a real person we've actually met, whereas idealized Rosa has never existed and never will. And "you'll regret it" is you projecting your own values onto her future. Then he gave a test he made up on the spot: imagine improving Rosa's clarity one notch at a time — she'd agree to each step, and at the end she wants X, fine. Now turn the dial the other way: could you get clear-headed Rosa to endorse something present-Rosa would find monstrous, just by turning the "clarity" dial in a different direction? If so, then "clearer" is doing no work at all — you're just picking a destination and calling it clarity. And if nothing could ever count as evidence against your picture of what Rosa would want if she were thinking straight, that's a sign it isn't a claim about her at all.

5. Nadia: The reverse dial isn't "clearer," it's just a different person — you can't get clearer-headed by getting more mistaken. Clarity has a direction. And we can point to the impairment: she's not sleeping, she's not eating, she's crying in a stairwell. That's not a rival vision of her good, it's a fog.

6. Owen: Fine, but then "clear-headed" just means "rested and not crying," and plenty of well-rested people quit their PhDs. You've snuck in your own idea of what's good for her and called it clarity. And it's the same trick at any scale — if the rule is "do what people would want if they were thinking straight," then whoever gets to decide what "straight" means gets to speak for everyone.
