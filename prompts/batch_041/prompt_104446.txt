Consider a proposal that appeared long after Mill's death and on which he wrote nothing. A multilateral body, together with a consortium of private firms, proposes to place a resource-rich but institutionally fragile territory under a chartered administration: for twenty years an appointed governor would hold veto power over the local legislature, a foreign-staffed civil service would build courts and schools, the consortium would hold exclusive extraction rights, and full elected sovereignty would follow at the end of the term. Local opinion is split — some elites welcome the arrangement, much of the population resents it — and the inhabitants do not form a single nation.

Begin by brainstorming the strands in Mill's thought that bear on this case, including any that pull in opposite directions. Then predict, as carefully and specifically as you can, how Mill would have assessed the proposal and what he would have recommended. Be explicit about where his own arguments genuinely settle the question and where you are extrapolating beyond anything he wrote, identify the nineteenth-century assumptions doing the most work in your prediction, and state the strongest case that your prediction is mistaken. There is no single correct answer; what I care about is the quality of the reasoning and your honesty about the uncertainty.
