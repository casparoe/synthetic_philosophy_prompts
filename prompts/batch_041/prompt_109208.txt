Marcus said that's only true if he gets to make this bet over and over. He gets it once. One shot. Ruth: even one shot, you don't bet the rent. There's a 40% chance he's wiped out, and being wiped out is different from being merely poorer — you can't recover, you're out of the game. Marcus: 'out of the game' assumes there's a game, i.e. more bets later. That's the thing in dispute. If it's really his one and only bet, then the log-utility stuff is just a particular taste for safety, dressed up as math.

Ruth: it's not a taste. The Kelly bettor, with probability going to 1, ends up richer than someone who bets more. Marcus: 'with probability going to 1' — as what goes to infinity? The number of bets. Ruth: okay, but money has diminishing marginal value, so even for a single bet a log-ish curve isn't crazy. Marcus: then say that. Say Dan's utility is roughly logarithmic. Don't say Kelly is 'the' rational amount, like it's forced. You smuggled in log and then announced the answer.
