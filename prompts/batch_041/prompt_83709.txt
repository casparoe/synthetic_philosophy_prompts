Some stuff I'm fairly sure belongs on the list, though tell me if any of it isn't really a requirement:

- It has to say where punishment stops and the other things that look like it begin. Quarantine. Civil commitment of someone after their sentence is done. A fine that's really a fee. A huge fine on a company. Sanctions on a country. A parent grounding a kid. A mob beating someone up. Someone who won't eat until they've done penance. Which of those are punishment, and why does it matter which?

- It has to do something with the fact that punishment seems to be two things at once: sending a message that the conduct was wrong and the community has a stake in it, and actually making the person worse off. One thing or two? If a sentence humiliates someone but costs them nothing, or costs them a lot but says nothing, is it punishment?

- Proportionality. Fine, but proportional to what — harm caused, wrongness of the act, how much it'd take to deter, how much the person can absorb, how angry the public is? Theories give different answers and I want the requirement stated before they get to answer it.

- It has to say something about punishing the innocent. Not just that it's unjust, but whether it's even coherent as punishment, and what to do about the case where we lock someone up who's done nothing wrong but is dangerous.

- It has to handle the offender who's no longer a threat and doesn't feel bad; the offender who enjoys the sentence or is indifferent to it; and the offender who's dead. Can you punish a dead person? Stripping someone's name off buildings and monuments, that kind of thing — punishment or just the winners writing history?

- The empirical side: whatever it says about deterrence and rehabilitation has to be consistent with what we actually know. Certainty of getting caught seems to matter more than severity, and prison often makes people worse. A theory that needs deterrence to work a certain way is in trouble if the evidence goes the other way.

- The authority question: what makes it the state's business instead of mine or the victim's? Is that a seperate requirement from justifying the practice, or the same requirement said twice?

- Pluralism: an expressive theory seems to need a community that agrees on what the punishment expresses. What happens in a society that doesn't agree? Bug in the theory or requirement it's failing?
