Alright, so, weird request, but I've been chewing on this for like two years and I need someone to help me think it through.

Background on me: I never went to college, I drive a delivery van. Two things I'm kind of obsessive about. First, birds — fifteen years of it, I'm the annoying guy at the park with the scope. I know my field marks, I can bore you alot about clines, hybrid zones, subspecies, why the checklist committees keep lumping and splitting the same warblers every few years, and what birders call jizz, the whole-impression thing where you know what it is before you can say why. Second, lettering. I paint signs by hand and I do a bit of calligraphy, so I know letters from the drawing end rather than the reading end.

Here's what's bugging me. What makes an 'a' an 'a'? I can draw an a a hundred ways — fat, thin, slanted, single-storey like handwriting, double-storey like a book, bowl on a stem, bowl floating off on its own — and every one of them is still an a, and I know it is, instantly, without checking anything. But a squiggle goes a millimetre too far and it stops being an a, and I genuinely can't tell you where the line is. And part of how I know it's an a is that it isn't a d, isn't an o, isn't an e — the letters seem to hold each other up somehow, the whole alphabet together, not one letter standing on its own. That's not how I do birds. A bird is what it is whether or not there's another bird next to it. Except that's not quite true either, because half of birding is seperating the thing from its confusion species, so maybe it is a bit true.

So what I want, and I'd rather you be blunt: take the whole question of what makes an 'a' an 'a' — and the answers people give to it, the full menu, I don't know what they are, that's part of why I'm asking — and restate every bit of it in the vocabulary I actually have. Species and subspecies. Field marks. Jizz. The typical bird in the field guide versus the scruffy one in my yard. Clines and hybrid zones. Lumping and splitting. Good species versus just a form or a local variant. Do it one candidate answer at a time, as faithfully as you can, and no cheating by quietly swapping in the letter-people's assumptions and calling that a translation.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. For each thing: does it carry over clean, does it only carry over if you bend it, or does it not carry at all? And for the ones that don't carry, tell me which it is — my birding vocabulary is missing something, or the letter question was muddier than it sounded and there was never anything there to carry in the first place.

Two spots I especially want you to poke at. One: style. When I letter a whole alphabet in one hand, every letter is related to every other one in a way that a flock of birds is not — same slant, same weight, same little habits at the corners. Is there anything in my birding words for that, or is that a real hole in them? Two: whatever makes an a an a can't be descent, can't be who bred with who, because a letterform doesn't breed. Species talk leans on that a lot. So what does my frame lose by leaning on it, and does the letter question even care?

Please don't hand me a reading list or a bunch of names of papers, I'm not going to read them and I won't be able to check them anyway. Just the translation and what falls out the bottom.
