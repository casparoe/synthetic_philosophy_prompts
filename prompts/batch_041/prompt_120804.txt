I staff the subsidiarity-review committee for our regional parliament -- we're the body that gets a shot at saying "this should've been left to us" every time Brussels or the national government proposes something. We see maybe 40 proposals a year, I have one junior researcher, roughly a week per proposal, and our opinions get published and sometimes quoted in plenary, so they cant be sloppy. The problem is that our working test is basically "does this feel local?", and everyone on the committee is a regionalist who already knows the answer they want.

Write me a decision procedure I can actually run on a proposal: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with "leave it to the lower level", and where it hands the decision back to the committee because the question is values or politics rather than fact. Base it on how subsidiarity actually works where someone has tried to operationalize it -- the EU's two-part test (whether the member states can't sufficiently achieve the objective, and whether the higher level would do it better) with the burden of proof that puts on the higher level; the German requirement that federal legislation on shared matters be *necessary*, alongside the guarantee of the municipalities' core area of self-government; and Switzerland's constitutional subsidiarity clause with its default competence sitting at the cantonal level. The procedure has to make us separate exclusive from shared competence, capacity failures from preference disagreements, real cross-border spillovers from "we'd just like uniformity", and subsidiarity-as-non-interference from subsidiarity-as-the-higher-level-helping-out. It also has to be honest about the who-decides problem -- our objection is mostly a political act and no court is coming to save us, and I'd like you to say plainly how much teeth the objection really has given how that mechanism has played out -- and honest that a lower level's claim that it can handle something is often just self-serving.

Then run it on two proposals: a higher-level plan to cap short-term holiday rentals in tourist cities, and a higher-level plan for common accessibility standards for public buildings. I'm a regionalist, so tell me straight if it says the accessibility one goes up.

Format: numbered checklist, the stop rule stated explicitly, any step that's really a judgment call flagged as one, then the two worked cases. Under 900 words.
