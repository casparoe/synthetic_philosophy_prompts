I have been trying to think my way through, without much background in any of this, the familiar argument that a tolerant society has to refuse to tolerate the intolerant, and the more I turn it over the more I notice that the same shape shows up in a lot of other places, and I cannot tell whether the shape itself has a name or whether people only ever name the tolerance version of it. Here is the shape as I see it. You start with something that looks as if it must never be restricted — being tolerant of every view, letting every kind of speech be spoken, letting any party stand for election, letting any firm compete — and then you observe that if you never restrict it, the thing it is supposed to protect can be used to wreck it, so the value is said to require its own exception: we must refuse to tolerate the intolerant in order to save tolerance, silence some speech in order to save free speech, keep certain parties off the ballot in order to save democracy, and so on. Two things bother me. First, whoever is making the argument always gets to decide which cases are the dangerous ones, and the exception almost always lands on the people they were already arguing against. Second, the same move seems available to anyone, so a principle that was supposed to protect the value can be turned into a license to shut down whoever you happen to dislike.

Is there an established name for this general shape, as distinct from the specific tolerance case, and what is the real difference between a case where the value genuinely would be destroyed unless you carve out the exception and a case where someone is merely using the threat of destruction to silence a rival? I am not really after a label; I want the carving — what makes those two cases different, what the concept does and does not cover, and how far it is supposed to reach. I keep reaching for a word for the general shape and coming up empty, which makes me think either that it has a name I have simply never run into or that it is a genuine gap.
