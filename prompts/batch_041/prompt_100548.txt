In the metaphysics of dispositions, a simple counterfactual analysis holds that an object possesses a disposition to manifest M in response to stimulus C just in case it would manifest M were C to occur. Two familiar obstacles to that analysis are finks, in which the object would lose the disposition upon the occurrence of C, and masks, in which the disposition survives the occurrence of C but its manifestation is blocked by some interfering factor. A third, less often invoked obstacle is the mimicker, in which M is produced on the occasion of C even though the disposition is absent. Explain the conceptual issue these obstacles expose, using the following case as your central example.

A particular pane of glass is fragile in the ordinary sense. It is enclosed by a device that continuously scans for approaching impacts and, whenever one is imminent, projects a field that rearranges the glass's internal microstructure into a tough configuration; the field lasts only as long as the threat and reverses fully once the threat has passed. The rearrangement is triggered by the approach of a strike, never by the strike itself, so the pane is never actually struck while it is in the tough configuration.

Address each of the following.

First, while the device is armed and no impact is imminent, is the pane fragile? Give your reasoning, and state explicitly which notion of having a disposition you are relying on.

Second, at the moment the pane is struck, is it still fragile? Explain why the answer turns on whether fragility is an intrinsic or a relational property of the pane, and why that question cannot be settled merely by inspecting the microstructure at the moment of impact.

Third, does the case belong with the finks or with the masks, or does it show that the finks/masks taxonomy is less clean than it is usually presented? Defend your classification, and say what someone who classified it the other way would have to be committed to. Indicate, in passing, how the case differs from a mimicker.

Fourth, state what the case implies for whether any purely counterfactual analysis of dispositions can succeed, and sketch the strongest reply available to a defender of such an analysis.

Treat the physics of the pane as stipulated; the interesting work lies in the distinctions among the options, not in the engineering.
