ok so context: I'm a backend dev, no philosophy degree, never took a single aesthetics or ethics class in my life (there was a "technology and society" elective in undergrad but I don't think that counts). A couple friends and I built a little movie-rating app and it definately did better than it had any right to, and now there's a newsletter and somehow I've been voluntered to write the next issue.

Here's what I want to do with it. Ever since we bolted reviews onto the app I've been stuck in a comment war about whether you're allowed to have an opinion on a film you haven't seen, or an opinion you only hold because someone you trust told you to hold it. Arguing with these people for a few months, I've come to realize the loud side of that fight is basically a real philosophical position: that a judgement of taste has to come out of your own encounter with the thing, that somebody else's rave can at best hand you a belief and never the actual experience, and that going along with the crowd isn't just epistemically sloppy, it's a failure of taste. I want to write the newsletter as a parody advice column that takes that position completely seriously and follows it all the way down.

So: a "Dear ___" agony-aunt column, three or four letters with replies, around 700 words, deadpan, the columnist totally sincere and a bit prim — invent her name and a sign-off. The letters should be ordinary people with ordinary problems, not philosophy seminar prompts: someone whose partner says she only likes the band she likes because he likes them; someone who loved a film, read a takedown of it, and now genuinely can't get the love back; someone who's never seen a famous film but knows it's a masterpiece and feels like a fraud; a parent whose kid won't watch anything the algorithm didn't recommend. Every reply should fix the problem by applying the no-borrowed-taste rule literally, and the jokes should come from her being right and coherent rather than dumb.

Stuff I'd like her to get tangled up in, because it's the actual meat of the thing: (a) what exactly someone else's enthusiasm can and can't hand over to you; (b) where her own taste came from, if a reader asks her — she should get shifty and change the subject; (c) how you're meant to tell which critics are worth listening to, given you can't check their verdicts without having the experience yourself, which is the very thing you were trying to avoid; (d) the fact that reading a review changes how the film actually lands on you, which is strange if testimony is supposed to be inert; (e) how anyone ever comes to like anything in the first place if every influence counts as contamination; and (f) the odd thing where she keeps insisting taste is personal while also acting like anyone who disagrees with her is just wrong. And I'd like at least one bit of self-undermining she doesn't notice — something in her masthead or sign-off that quietly leans on somebody else's authority — findable on a second read, not winked at.

Don't let her take the escape hatch of "well of course we all rely on critics for what to watch," that's the whole point of her not taking it. And please don't let it read like a philosophy paper in a hat, it's a newsletter, so keep it light — but someone who's actually read the literature should be able to tell I know what I'm parodying. No citations or name-dropping needed.
