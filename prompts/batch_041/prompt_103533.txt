Bit of an odd request. I spent thirty-eight years as a condensed-matter physicist — neutron scattering at first, then a long stretch on the theory side — and since I retired I've been reading philosophy with the same obsessiveness I used to bring to a beamline, which my wife says its become a problem. The thing I keep circling back to is Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: roughly, you do what you do because of the way you are; to be genuinely responsible for what you do you'd have to be genuinely responsible for the way you are; but any choice you made about the way you were would itself have been made because of the way you already were, so you'd have to be the origin of yourself, and nothing is causa sui, i.e. self-caused; hence no true moral responsibility. What nags at me is that he insists this goes through whether or not determinism is true — indeterminism just drops a random event into the chain, which doesn't make the action any more mine — and after a lifetime spent with systems whose next state is fixed by their last state plus the environment, I keep going back and fourth on whether that's a knock-down or a swindle. I don't want the argument explained to me; I want a map of the possible escape routes. So: build me a decision tree that takes someone from "I accept that I am not the ultimate origin of my own character" to a named position on moral responsibility, so I can bring it to a small reading group of retired scientists who are as muddled as I am. Every branch node should be a question whose answers are both live options, phrased so a person can answer it without already knowing which view it lands them in — not "are you a compatibilist?" but something like "suppose someone was raised to be cruel and never once questioned it: are they responsible for the cruelty?". I want the tree grown from the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — with the actual positions dropped into the slots afterwards, so I can see which corners of that space are empty and why nobody lives there. Keep it to about six or seven branch questions so I can hold the whole thing in my head.

A few things to build in, otherwise you'll just hand me the standard menu. First, one distinction I want handled even if nobody has put it this way: what I'll call *deep* self-authorship (you are the origin of your character) versus *live* self-authorship (your character is permanently up for revision by you-as-you-now-are, even though you never chose your starting point) — my suspicion is that half the people who think they're rejecting Strawson's second premise are really just quietly switching from the first sense to the second, and I want the tree to expose that move rather than let it hide. Second, three cases I think break the usual sorting: someone with an implant that lets them dial their own character traits up and down at will; a designer who builds an agent, fixes its values once and for all, then turns it loose; and a person whose character changes after a brain tumour. Third, I want one branch given over to the move "fine — blame and praise are social practices rather than desert, and Strawson was only ever attacking desert", and one given over to "there's no self there in the first place for responsibility to attach to". Don't worry about who defends what; I don't want a reading list, and where a position has no standard name just write "unnamed".

Give it back to me as JSON, with these fields: "start" (id of the first node); "nodes", an array of objects with "id", "question" (the branch question in plain language), "why_this_splits_the_space" (one line), and "answers", an array of objects with "answer" and "goes_to" (a node or leaf id); "leaves", an array of objects with "id", "position" (name or "unnamed"), "claim" (the view in one sentence), "awkward_commitment" (what its holder has to swallow), and "the_question_that_did_the_work"; "unoccupied_combinations", an array of objects with "combination" and "diagnosis" (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, plus a line of why); "pairs_the_tree_cannot_separate", an array of objects with "pair" (two leaf ids) and "why"; and "cases_used", an array of objects with "case" and "what_it_tests". Every node should be answerable by someone who has never read a word of any of this.
