Next Thursday it's my turn to present to our philosophy of physics reading group (we've been grinding through the block-universe stuff all term) and I'd like to walk in ready for the Q&A instead of improvising like last time. My thesis is that the Rietdijk-Putnam argument — the one where you chain together observers in relative motion and conclude that an event in my future is already real for somebody else, hence the block — actually does work, but only once you drag a premise out of the drawer that everyone leaves shut: that a person's now is a single spacetime point (the here-now), not an extended region and not a spacelike hypersurface. Say that out loud and the chain goes through; I think the reason smart people think the argument fails is that they quietly slide between the point reading and the hypersurface reading halfway through. I'm not claiming it kills presentism in general, only the point version — if you take the present to be a slice, relativity never touches you, and I'm fine with that.

The audience is the problem. Two of them are actual relativists: one does numerical relativity and is alergic to anything that smells like a preferred frame, the other teaches the undergrad SR course and will want to know exactly which frame is doing the work at each step. Then there's Marta, a card-carrying presentist who will take the whole setup personally, and two grad students who've read enough Lewis to be dangerous. They won't ask me about the literature; they'll ask whether simultaneity is even transitive in special relativity, whether any of this survives the move to curved spacetime where the global inertial frames are gone, whether my extra premise is doing all the work (and if so why I need relativity at all), whether I'm extracting an ontology from a coordinate choice, and whatever the Andromeda thing is supposed to show about me deciding right now whether to come to my own talk. Marta will say I've redefined presentism into something no presentist holds, which I suspect is the most dangerous one, and one of the relativists will point out that you can pick a non-standard synchrony and get your global simultaneous slice back, and then where am I.

So: draft me the Q&A prep sheet. The objections I'll actually get from this room, not the ones I'd get at a big metaphysics conference, ordered by how much damage they do, each phrased the way that particular person would phrase it, with a two-minute answer and a note on which part of the answer is soft. Tell me what to concede up front and what isn't worth conceding, and include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And be honest about the state of play: keep separate the bits that are genuinely settled among people who work on this from the bits that are live disputes where I've picked a side — don't hand me a consensus that isn't there, and flag where I'm leaning on a contested reading of what the argument even is.
