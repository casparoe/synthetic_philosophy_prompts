**Priority as Acceleration**

When a result arrives from two or more people at once, the standard inference is that the discovery was in the air, hence inevitable, hence that crediting a particular individual is a social artifact rather than an act of desert. Call this the inevitability thesis. I want to argue that the thesis is true in a weaker sense than its friends suppose, false in the sense its enemies suppose, and that what survives of priority is a claim about acceleration.

The first move is to separate two claims that simultaneity is used to support. *Content inevitability*: the proposition would have entered the public record at roughly the same time whether or not any particular one of the discoverers had existed. *Achievement inevitability*: this particular person would have produced it. Simultaneous discovery is good evidence for the first and almost no evidence for the second, since the second requires a claim about counterfactual biographies that simultaneity does not touch. Much rhetoric about the air trades on the first while sounding like the second.

Granting content inevitability, however, does not extinguish desert. Credit should attach to the counterfactual delay the person's work removed — how much later the result would have arrived without them. Under strict simultaneity this is small, so simultaneity really does reduce desert; but small is not zero, and delay is not a merely aesthetic loss. Later arrival means a later start for everything downstream that depends on the result, and compounding delay is a cost borne by whoever would have done that downstream work. On this account a discovery is not a thing one owns but an interval one shortens, and priority is a magnitude rather than a rank: two discoverers can each have shortened the interval, in unequal amounts, with neither of them being *the* discoverer.

Two objections. First, the counterfactual is unknowable: no one can say how long the world would have waited for natural selection absent Darwin. The reply is that unknowability makes credit uncertain, not meaningless. We credit voters, rescuers, and referees on equally uncomputable counterfactuals; a demand for measurable acceleration would rule out nearly all desert claims, including uncontroversial ones. Second, the account may be circular: if what matters is acceleration, then who discovered it reduces to who mattered, and the priority question has been replaced rather than answered. I accept the reduction but deny the charge of triviality, because the acceleration account still tells us which facts about a discoverer are credit-relevant — the counterfactual timing — and which are not, such as the private phenomenology of insight or the order of inscription.

A residual case deserves more attention than I can give it here. Claims about the air are usually about a problem — the anomaly, the puzzle, the felt demand — rather than about a solution. But rival solutions to one recognized problem need not be equivalent in what they enable. A notation, a proof technique, a formulation that travels and a formulation that does not are different achievements even when they answer the same question. If that is right, simultaneity at the level of content can coexist with genuine non-simultaneity at the level of the propagatable result, and priority disputes may be less about who was first than about which version of the thing was first. This also predicts that disputes will be bitterest exactly where the acceleration is hardest to estimate, namely where two formulations each opened different doors and each party can therefore claim a distinct interval. This is where I expect to be attacked.

The conclusion is institutional. If desert is thin and uncertain but acceleration is real, priority rules are better defended as an incentive scheme — a device for getting results into the record sooner — than as a ledger of merit. That is a concession to the inevitability thesis, and it is also the reason the thesis does not dissolve priority: a reward scheme can be justified by its effects even when the merit it purports to track is nearly zero.
