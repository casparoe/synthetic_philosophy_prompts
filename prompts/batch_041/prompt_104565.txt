I am a novelist, and for the better part of a year I have been writing a book whose protagonist slowly becomes convinced that he is a Boltzmann brain — a momentary, self-assembling observer in an enormous and nearly empty universe, furnished with a coherent memory and perhaps an hour of consciousness before he disperses. The novel is close to finished, but the metaphysics under it has begun to embarrass me, because when I set my own verdicts side by side I cannot tell whether I hold one view or five incompatible ones wearing the same coat. I would like you to treat the following as data about what I actually believe and tell me what principle, or small family of principles, would capture the pattern — and, if no single principle can, which of my verdicts I ought to give up. I am not asking for a survey of the literature; I have no way to consult it, and in any case I care about my own commitments rather than anyone's published position. Please reason from the cases exactly as I state them, even where you think I have misdescribed something.

Here are the cases and my verdicts. (C1) A being fluctuates into existence in deep space with a seamless hour of experience — walking a familiar street, hearing traffic — then dissolves. I say the hour is genuinely experienced; there is something it is like; and if it contains pain, that pain matters morally exactly as much as yours or mine. (C2) The same being, but its hour is spent seeming to remember a childhood and a mother's face. I say the seeming-remembering is real, but nothing is remembered — the state has no object — and yet I resist saying he is deceived, since deception needs a deceiver or at least a false belief he could have avoided. (C3) An ordinary man whose memories were implanted by a surgeon last Tuesday now recalls a childhood he never had. Here I want to say he remembers — or at least that his state is memory-like in a way the Boltzmann brain's is not — even though the causal history is just as disconnected from any past. This is the first pair I cannot reconcile. (C4) An exact physical duplicate of me, one second long, appears in deep space. I say it is not me, I do not survive as it, and anyone distressed by the thought has made an error — though I cannot name the error. (C5) A duplicate of my dying self that will fluctuate into existence in a remote future, versus one existing now: I find I care about the future one less, and I suspect this is a failure of imagination rather than a defensible asymmetry, but I cannot shake it. (C6) A Boltzmann brain whose hour is the experience of reading a great novel. I say the novel has no author and the experience no object, yet I judge the experience as aesthetically valuable as a real reading — perhaps more, since nothing was lost. That sits badly beside C2. (C7) A Boltzmann brain that entertains a valid proof, is convinced, then dissolves: I do not want to say it knows the theorem, even though the proof is valid and it is convinced. But I also hold that a mathematician who has a proof arrive whole in a flash, without having worked it out, does know it. Second irreconcilable pair. (C8) The argument that a random observer-moment is more likely to be a Boltzmann brain than an ordinary one, so I should believe I am probably one: I find the argument inert — it does not move my credence at all — and I suspect the fault is mine rather than the argument's.

I have a tentative principle of my own, which I would like you to test and repair: that an experience is real if it is had; that its content is veridical only if it was caused by its apparent object; that moral weight attaches to the having and not to the veridicality; and that knowledge requires something beyond valid content, though I cannot say what. Please tell me where this principle breaks against C1–C8, what minimal repairs would save the most verdicts, and what the repaired version predicts for at least two cases I have not listed — I am especially curious what it says about a Boltzmann brain that suffers, forms a true belief about its own suffering, and dissolves, and about a community of such beings that fluctuate into existence simultaneously with matching memories of one another.
