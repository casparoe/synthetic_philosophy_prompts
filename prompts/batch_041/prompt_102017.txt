Kurt Gödel's 1949 rotating-universe solution to the Einstein field equations describes a homogeneous, singularity-free spacetime filled with swirling dust, and it contains closed timelike curves — paths a massive particle could follow to arrive back at its own past. Gödel took this to have philosophical significance beyond its status as a mathematical curiosity. Because general relativity, our best theory of space and time, admits a solution in which events are not divided into an objective global past, present, and future, and in which there is no objective global temporal ordering at all, he argued that time — in the sense of an objective becoming, or even an objective ordering of events — is ideal or unreal. Einstein admired the solution but was not persuaded that it bore on the reality of time.

Analyze this episode through the lens of four-dimensionalism, the block-universe view on which all events in spacetime are equally real and the passage of time is not an objective feature of the world. Take the argument that runs from the relativity of simultaneity to the block universe — the one associated with Rietdijk, Putnam, and Penrose — as the paradigm of how that view is argued for from physics.
