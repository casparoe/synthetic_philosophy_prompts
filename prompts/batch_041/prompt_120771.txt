Until recently I taught philosophy; six months ago I moved to the forecasting desk of a public-health agency, and I have found that an assumption I used to treat as a modelling convenience is now invoked in my team as a fact about rationality. A colleague and I cover the same disease area. We read the same surveillance bulletins, the same preprints and the same model outputs, and we routinely finish a full point apart on the probability of an outbreak. Our team lead says our disagreement cannot be genuine. His argument is that we are both Bayesians working from a shared prior and shared evidence, and that two rational agents in that position must arrive at the same place; he offers an analogy — we are two calculators of the same model, and a calculator given the same input cannot print a different answer. Any gap in our output, he concludes, must come from something we have kept off the table, a private hunch or an arithmetic slip. He adds that if I truly held my number I would take his odds, which I would.

Take the analogy apart for me. Give me the correspondence table — what stands for the calculator, the program, the input, the calibration — then tell me where the mapping breaks, and which features of the calculator picture are quietly imported into the target and do not belong there. I remember the formal result as being about posterior probabilities that are common knowledge between the agents, which is not the claim he is making; say whether his conclusion needs that condition, or some other piece of machinery he has not mentioned, and what work it does. Then tell me whether his conclusion — that a real disagreement between us is impossible, so any difference must be private information or error — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, what the strongest defensible version of his claim turns out to be, and whether my willingness to bet him shows what he thinks it shows. If the picture has to be replaced, give me the better analogy, and say what would have to be true of us for his version to hold.
