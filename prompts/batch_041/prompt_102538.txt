Had a stupid arguement with a friend last night that I can't stop chewing on. We were going back and forth about whether there could be infinitely many past events, or infinitely many universes, whatever — and he just ended it with "the whole is greater than the part." Dropped it like it was an axiom. He's an engineer, he doesnt read philosophy, but he clearly thinks that one line is logic itself and that it shuts the door on anything infinite. I know Hilbert's hotel, I know Cantor, I know the thing where an infinite set can be put in one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset of itself, and I know Galileo noticed something like this with the square numbers. But I couldn't say what's actually wrong with his move, or whether anything is wrong with it at all.

So what I want is a menu of readings, not a lecture. "The whole is greater than the part" — what are the precise claims that could actually be? Is "part" a proper part in the mereological sense, or a proper subset, or something about magnitudes and quantities, the way it was meant when it was first written down? Is "greater" meaning bigger in number/size, or just meaning "contains"? For each reading: is it true, is it false, is it a triviality nobody would ever deny, and does it hold when the whole is infinite? Some of them seem obviously fine (sure, a whole contains its proper parts) and some seem flatly false for infinite collections, and I want to know exactly which is which.

Then the part I actually care about: which reading does he need in order for his argument to work? He wants to get from that line to "so there can't be an actual infinite." Which precisification does that job, and does that version survive Hilbert's hotel? If it doesn't survive, is there some repair — Cantor's theorem, power sets, a distinction between kinds of "greater" — and if there's a repair, does it still get him his conclusion or does it actually end up helping me? And does his line only work if you're already a finitist, because it seems like that's the one setting where it's trivially true, which feels like cheating.

Lay it out as a list I can bring to him, with a verdict on each reading, because what I really want to be able to say is: this is the one you were using, and here's whether you get to keep it.
