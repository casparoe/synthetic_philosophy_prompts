Ok so I host a very small history-of-science podcast — like 400 listeners, mostly people from my old seminar — and next month the guest is a historian of early modern science whose whole thing is that the standard story about Galileo and scripture gets the interesting part backwards. What was actually novel and actually provocative was (a) the claim that a demonstrated conclusion in natural philosophy can overturn the literal sense of a passage, which quietly hands interpretive authority to the mathematician, and (b) his refusal of the escape hatch where you say heliocentrism is just a useful hypothesis that saves the appearances. On her reading the fight was less science-vs-religion and more about jurisdiction: who gets to fix the meaning of scripture, and what counts as proof.
