I'm writing a short paper on Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment" and would like your comments on the argument sketch below. The target is the familiar reading on which the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation, guilt, and their kin — are constitutive of the interpersonal standpoint, while the objective attitude is a separate, more detached stance we take toward those we exempt from moral address. I want to argue that Strawson's own examples of legitimate exemption push against this tidy dichotomy. He treats the very young, the deranged, and those whose moral capacities he regards as undeveloped as proper objects of the objective attitude, but he also grants that we can slide into the objective attitude for practical purposes — to manage, treat, or predict someone — without ever fully leaving the participant stance. If the two attitudes are individuated by their typical objects and by a shifting degree of engagement rather than by an all-or-nothing commitment, then exemption looks like a matter of degree and full participant status like a limiting case rather than the norm. From this I want to draw a larger conclusion: that the excusing conditions Strawson treats as peripheral are in fact the central phenomenon, and that the moral community is better modeled as a graded continuum of addressability than as a binary inside/outside. I take this to make his position more congenial to a naturalistic debunking of responsibility than he intended, since it locates responsibility in variable social practices of holding-to-account rather than in any fact about the agent.

Please comment on four things. First, is this a fair reading of Strawson or a distortion — does the continuum move depend on flattening a distinction he is entitled to keep? Second, is the continuum claim genuinely novel, or is it just the stock observation that excuses come in degrees dressed up as an interpretation? Third, what is the strongest objection to the step from exemption-comes-in-degrees to responsibility-is-a-social-practice, and can it be answered? Fourth, flag anything unclear, overstated, or worth cutting. Please write in American English.
