Ok this is going to sound like a small thing but it's been eating at me for a couple of weeks now.

I keep backyard hens. Four of them. Turns out there's a town bylaw from the seventies that says you can keep poultry in town but no more than two birds, and it's still on the books. Nobody I know has ever heard of it. Most people on my street who keep hens have more than two. I got a $200 ticket anyway, because a neighbour called in a complaint about the noise. The bylaw officer was almost apologetic — said they only ever act on complaints.

Its not just that one. There's an old rule here that you have to clear your sidewalk by 8am, and I'd say most people don't, and nothing happens to them, ever, unless someone complains about you specifically.

What gets me is the shape of it. The rule hasn't been enforced in decades, basically everyone ignores it, and everyone knows everyone ignores it. It's not a secret or a grey area, it's just dead. But it isn't repealed either, and the second somebody has a grudge it wakes up and is the law again, and suddenly I'm the one who broke it. And the thing I can't get past: when people tell me it's the law and I should either follow it or get it changed democratically, that seems to assume the rule is part of an actual going practice. If it's a sentence in a binder that everybody ignores, what exactly is doing the work when someone says I broke the law?

So — is there a name for this? A mate of mine who did a law degree said there's an old doctrine about laws going stale from disuse, I think he said desuetude, but the way he described it sounded like it was about courts and prosecutors quietly giving up, not about the whole town ignoring something while it sits there on the books. I've also been reading about that Hart and Fuller argument — whether a defective or unjust rule is still law — and I wonder if my thing is an instance of that, or of something Fuller said about a legal system having to actually meet certain conditions before it counts as one. He had a list and I think one item was about the law actually being followed in practice, but I'm probably mangling it.

Either way I don't just want the word. I want to know what the thing is: what makes a rule that's dead in practice but still on the books different from a rule that's simply unenforced, or from a law officials have stopped applying, or from plain old selective enforcement. Where's the line between those? And if there's no name for it, give me one, define it, and tell me what it wouldn't cover.
