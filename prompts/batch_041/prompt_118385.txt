I'm a high school student who has been reading philosophy of mind on my own, and my school's philosophy society has asked me to write out my position so that the others can attack it. Here is where I have landed. (1) Physicalism: the physical facts fix all the facts, and there is nothing non-physical in the world. (2) The felt quality of an experience, what it is like, is caused by brain activity but causes nothing in turn, so that my behaviour and my reports would be exactly as they are even if the quality were different or missing altogether. (3) My judgments about my own experience are reliable: when I judge that something looks red to me, I am normally right about how it looks. (4) Mental content is a matter of causal and functional role: what a state represents is fixed by what causes it, what it causes, and how it functions in the system. (5) There is a genuine explanatory gap: no physical or functional information tells you what red is like.

Please audit this rather than explaining the views to me. Which of my commitments pull against each other, and how badly? Which pairs only look like they conflict and can be reconciled, and what distinction does the reconciling? For every real tension, tell me what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me, then say which single change would clean up the most at once. Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them as equally bad, and be concrete about which numbered commitment is doing the damage in each case. Please write it as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter.
