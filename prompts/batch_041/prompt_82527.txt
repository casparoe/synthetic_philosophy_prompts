I took early retirement from a physics department two years ago — statisical mechanics, plus a lot of time around the effective-field-theory people down the hall — and I've been reading philosophy on my own ever since. The thing I keep circling back to is Dennett on real patterns and the three stances. My rough take is that the physical, design, and intentional stances are three ways of compressing the same system, and that calling a pattern real is a claim about whether the compression pays for itself rather than about what's fundamentally there.

What I want from you is a faithful restatement of that whole position in the vocabulary I actually think in: coarse-graining, order parameters, relevant versus irrelevant degrees of freedom, effective theories and their domains of validity, universality classes, the renormalization group. Don't dress it up as a cute analogy — translate it, and tell me where the translation strains. Then the residue report, which is really what I'm after. What comes through cleanly, what only comes through with distortion (my hunch is that anything about norms, about genuinely being an agent, about one interpretation being correct and another wrong, gets mangled), and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. For each bit of residue, say which way it cuts: is the effective-theory picture missing something real, or was the original claim doing less work than it sounded like it was?

And please don't just tell me that an effective theory is a stance. I can see that much myself; I want to know where that identification is too cheap to be true.
