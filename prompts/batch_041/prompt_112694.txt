I'm making a handout for a graduate seminar on historical materialism, and I want it built as a taxonomy rather than as a tour of famous positions. The root question is the usual one — does the economic base explain everything else? — but the branches should be questions someone can answer, not labels. Build them from these axes. First, a distinction I've drawn for this handout among three strengths of base-primacy: explanatory primacy (the base explains the content of the superstructure), selectionist primacy (the base explains which of several available superstructures survive), and budgetary primacy (the base fixes only the resources available to the superstructure, leaving its content to be explained by something else). Second, what the base is taken to be: productive forces (technology, labour power, means of production) or relations of production (property, class, surplus extraction). Third, what does the explaining: the development of the forces, the reproduction requirements of the relations, or the outcome of class struggle, which may itself reshape the base. Fourth, leave room for the possibility that the base/superstructure distinction does no explanatory work at all, either because the so-called superstructure is itself part of the relations of production, or because anything that stabilizes accumulation can be counted as base, so that the distinction collapses.

Where a branch can be put as a concrete historical question — for instance whether the transition from feudalism to capitalism in early modern England is explained by the growth of the productive forces, by the requirements of feudal lordship, or by the outcome of peasant–lord struggle — phrase it that way rather than abstractly.

Give the result as exactly five numbered items and nothing else: no introduction, no conclusion, no scoring key. Each item is one or two sentences that name a single position (descriptively rather than by a famous name, though you may mention a theorist if the position is unintelligible without one), state the one question whose answer lands someone there, and name the commitment that position has the hardest time defending. Order the items from the leaves that keep the base/superstructure distinction intact to the ones that dissolve it, and where two of your leaves cannot be separated by any question I could ask, say so inside the relevant item instead of inventing a distinction between them.
