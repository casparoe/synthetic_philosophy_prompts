Everyone tells me the leveling-down objection is fatal for egalitarianism but slides right off prioritarianism, since prioritarianism is monotone in everyones welfare and so never prefers making the better off worse off for nothing. I want the most important implications of that reply once you notice how much of what actually gets leveled down is purely positional -- when the top loses its edge, nobodys life gets worse in any absolute sense, the edge just was the gap, and in some cases the good only exists as the gap. Does the objection keep any force there? Does prioritarianism get to absorb the case for free, or does the reply only work if you keep positional goods out of welfare, in which case the worse off arguably lose their priority claim too? And whats the upshot for both views if the answer turns on your theory of welfare rather than on the structure of the principles themselves.
