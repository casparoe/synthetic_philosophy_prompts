I am moderating the closing session of a two-day workshop on machine agency, and the guest is a philosopher who defends a particular version of the simulator view of large language models. I want the interview questions to take her position as given rather than invite her to restate it, so let me set out that position as I understand it. On her account the model is a simulator: given a prompt, it produces a simulation, and agents appear only at the level of the simulated characters. She is not an eliminativist about agent-talk. She holds that attributions of belief, intention, and goal-directedness to a character are genuine explanations, licensed by counterfactuals and usable for prediction, and that such attributions are mislocated rather than false: the bearer of those states is the character, not the simulator. She adds that the simulator has no goals of its own, and that training optimizes a distribution rather than installing an objective in the model, which she takes to be fully consistent with character-level explanations being as real as anything in folk psychology.

Please draft eight to ten questions for the interview, each followed by a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The questions should be usable verbatim and should press on the position rather than summarize it; none should ask her to define her terms, recount her influences, or say why the topic matters. Keep each question to two sentences at most. I need at least one question that she cannot answer by simply re-invoking the distinction between levels of description, and one that forces her to concede something specific.

The set should cover, in whatever order makes for the best interview: how one distinguishes a disposition of the simulator (stylistic tics, refusal patterns, sycophancy) from a disposition of the character, given that the former appear to survive every persona change; what makes the model's agency false rather than merely coarse, once she has granted that character-level explanations are genuine; whether training counts as selection in the way natural selection does, and if so why the goals land in the character rather than in the model, with a follow-up pressing the parity if she says selection acts only on the trainer's objective; what observation could count against the simulator view at all, and whether the view is a claim about the world or about the correct vocabulary; what happens to the level distinction when the model is prompted to reason about its own next-token distribution, since the simulator then appears to be appearing as a character; what the view implies for accountability, in particular whether it hands the deployers an excuse and what it puts in that excuse's place; whether the view settles the question of the model's interests or merely relabels it; whether she accepts a functionalist criterion for agency, and what becomes of her view if she does; and, to close, what evidence would move her.
