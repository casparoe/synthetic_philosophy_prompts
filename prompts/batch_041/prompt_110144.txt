A friend of mine — philosophy PhD, the kind who brings Kant to dinner parties — laid out the cosmological argument for me last week and I can't shake it. I'm writing a novel where a character is talked out of her athiesm and then back into it over the course of a weekend, so I need to see the seams in this thing, not just have it in my mouth as a slogan. Here's the arguement as he gave it:

1. Every contingent fact has an explanation: something that makes it the case that things are one way rather than another.
2. That explanation can't be a string of further contingent facts, because a chain like that never gets around to explaining anything. It has to bottom out in something that isn't itself up for grabs — something necessary.
3. So take any contingent fact, say that my character walks out of the house on Sunday. By (1) it has an explanation, and by (2) that explanation bottoms out in the necessary thing.
4. The necessary thing is the same in every possible world; that's just what necessary means. So nothing contingent can make a difference to what it explains — if a difference-maker were doing the explaining, then the difference-maker would be the ultimate explainer, not the necessary thing. Which means: if it explains something at all, it explains it in every world.
5. So there's no possible world where the necessary thing exists (it does, in all of them) and my character doesn't walk out on Sunday. Her walking out is necessary.
6. But it isn't; she could have stayed in bed and the book would be a different book. So either (1) or (4) has to go.

I want to keep (1) — everything in me says the universe can't just be a brute shrug — and (4) looks like it's only unpacking the word "necessary," so I don't see how to keep (2) and (3) while dropping it. But if I keep both, I'm left with no contingent facts at all, and that's not a world I can write a novel in. So where's the leak, and which bank am I robbing to fix it?

Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction and no wrap-up — which step to give up, why it looked airtight, what giving it up costs me, and what I'd be paying if I dropped one of the others instead.
