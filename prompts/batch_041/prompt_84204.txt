Hey — I'm in a seminar on Indian philosophy this term and we just wrapped the unit on Jain epistemology, and I came out of it way more interested in anekantavada than I expected. Now I have to pick a final paper topic, and I'm also trying to figure out whether it's worth building a senior thesis around. So what I really want from you is an agenda: what are the open questions in the philosophical study of anekantavada and syadvada, and which of them actually deserve someone's time?

My constraints, since importance is relative to that: I've got about six weeks for the paper, and I only read English. There's decent secondary literature in English but I can't check anything against Sanskrit or Prakrit, so anything that lives or dies on philological detail is out. I also care more about whether a question would interest logicians, epistemologists, and metaphysicians outside Jain studies than about adding another brick to the internal commentary tradition. I'm not Jain and I have no stake in defending the doctrine — I'm happy if the answer turns out to be that it's a mess.

Here's the stuff that struck me as genuinely weird and possibly under-explored, so you can react to it and not just give me the standard menu:

- It's unclear what anekantavada is even claiming. A thesis about reality (things really do have infinitely many aspects)? A thesis about predication, where the syat particle just means something like in some respect, making most of it nearly analytic? Or a thesis about the limits of language, which is what the avaktavya limb of the seven-fold scheme seems to be gesturing at, where you're forced to say the thing is inexpressible? Which of those three is the load-bearing one changes whether the doctrine is deep or trivial.
- Whether the seven-fold scheme is meant to be a many-valued logic at all — seven values, or four, or three — or just a compact way of indexing standpoints (the nayas). I've seen it read through a Belnap-style four-valued logic, through a paraconsistent lens where the both-asti-and-nasti limb is a real contradiction, and read as none of the above because syat is a modal or perspectival operator rather than a truth value. Is this a genuine technical dispute with an answer, or are people just projecting their favorite non-classical system onto it?
- The self-application problem. Jainism accuses every rival school of being one-sided, but everything is many-sided looks like a one-sided claim, and no view is wholly true looks like a view claiming to be wholly true. Is there a principled escape — the claim is only made from a particular standpoint, or it's a second-order claim about how to conduct inquiry rather than a first-order claim about reality — or is it a straight inconsistency that the tradition never faced?
- The genealogy. How much of this is a dialectical weapon aimed at Buddhist momentariness and Brahmanical universalism rather than a freestanding logic? And does that origin story change how we should read the truth claims?
- Whether the method can do real work in live debates: deep disagreement and peer disagreement in epistemology, perspectival realism in philosophy of science, the view-from-nowhere worry. Or is it just a polite way of saying everyone is partly right, which nobody denies?
- The ethics-and-politics layer. Anekantavada gets cited as the philosophical root of nonviolence, of a distinctive intellectual ahimsa, and of Gandhian pluralism. Real entailment, post-hoc rationalization, or anachronistic back-reading?
- The internal tension between all this humility about views and the very confident realist ontology Jainism runs — substances, atoms, karma as material stuff. Contradiction, or two different levels of discourse?

So: rank these, plus whatever I've missed, by how much they deserve effort, and tell me which two or three I should actually spend six weeks on. I want the reasoning more than the list — what makes one question upstream of others, what someone with no Sanskrit can realistically settle versus only reframe, whether the answer would change anything outside Jain studies, and what would move an item up or down my ranking. And please flag the ones that are basically dead ends that keep getting written about because they're easy to write about, since I'd rather not produce the four hundredth paper on the elephant parable.
