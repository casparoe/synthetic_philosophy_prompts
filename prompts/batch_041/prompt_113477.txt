Draft the joint statement that could be signed by two committed but good-faith participants in the debate over hate speech law: an American civil-libertarian who treats the First Amendment's speech protection as a near-absolute constraint, and a European human-rights lawyer who treats dignity, equality, and democratic civility as legitimate grounds for restricting hateful expression. They have argued for years, often past each other. I want the document they should have produced together -- not a dialogue, not a verdict about who is right.

Organize the document as follows. First, a numbered list of claims both can affirm: about the empirical conditions under which hate speech laws operate, about the values genuinely at stake on each side, about the limits of what either legal tradition can claim as settled, and about the concrete case set out below. Second, a disagreements table. Each row should state one remaining disagreement in neutral wording, then give the formulation of the American's position that the American would accept as accurate, and the formulation of the European's position that the European would accept as accurate. Third, a section identifying the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, state the test, case, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that test would show. Finally, say which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal or turn on different meanings of terms such as speech, harm, dignity, or democracy, and explain why.

Use this concrete case as the focal point: a proposed municipal ordinance that forbids public displays of symbols associated with the historical subordination of a protected group, with exceptions for museums, classrooms, and bona fide artistic or scholarly use. The list of covered symbols is maintained by an appointed commission that can add symbols by majority vote. The ordinance imposes administrative fines, not criminal penalties, and includes a narrow emergency injunction procedure available only when a display is part of a pattern of targeted intimidation. There is also a safe-harbor provision for displays that a reasonable person would understand as historical reappropriation or political criticism rather than endorsement. Both parties should reason about this ordinance as written, not about a caricature of it.

Do not provide citations, reading lists, or references to specific cases or statutes; I want the argumentative and conceptual work, not a bibliography. Where you rely on an empirical assumption, state it as an assumption and say how the joint statement would change if it were false. For each side, include a short note on what would change that side's mind: the argument or piece of evidence that would lead it to abandon or significantly revise its position, and where that side's reasoning is most vulnerable to error. End with a brief self-critical note identifying where this joint statement itself is most likely to fail -- where it may have papered over a real disagreement, where a formulation may prove unstable, and what further argument or evidence would force a revision of the document.
