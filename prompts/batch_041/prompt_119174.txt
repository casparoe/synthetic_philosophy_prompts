im putting together a poem to close out a seminar on theories of consciousness (any form is allowed), and I want to do the higher-order thought theory -- a state is conscious only when you have a thought about being in it -- but as structure, not as exposition. My idea is two registers. One voice just sits in a room and notices things (a lamp, a noise, a mood). A second voice, italicised or as a refrain, gives the "reading" of what the first one is doing. Crucial bit: the readings have to be allowed to get it wrong -- one of them should confidently report a state the first voice isnt in at all, and the poem should still treat that as a conscious episode, since on this view what does the work is the higher-order state, not its accuracy. And I want the regress handled in the shape of the poem: each gloss becomes the subject of the next gloss, stacking up, until the last one is left unglossed -- make the stopping point feel like a cost rather than a fix. Someone who knows the literature should be able to name the theory and see where the two levels come apart; someone who doesnt should still get a poem about being watched from one floor up. 20-30 lines. Start with the poem, no preface.
