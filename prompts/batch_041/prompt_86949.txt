Background so you know I'm not making it up. Popper had this notion of verisimilitude, truthlikeness — the idea that most of the big scientific theories turn out to be false, but one false theory can still be closer to the truth than another, and that's what scientific progress amounts to. And he tried to cash it out concretely: theory B is closer to the truth than theory A when B's true consequences include A's true consequences and then some, while B's false consequences are a subset of A's. More informative and less wrong at the same time. Then in the mid-70s a couple of logicians sat down and worked the definition through and found it has an unintended consequence: on Popper's own definition, no false theory can come out closer to the truth than any other false theory. So the definition can't do the one thing it was built to do. Popper definately conceded it, apparently more or less on the spot.
