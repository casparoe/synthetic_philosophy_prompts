I'm on the animal-care committee at my university (the IACUC, though everyone just calls it the committee), and Thursday we're reviewing a protocol that I have to write the decision memo for. A lab wants to put a tiny robotic bee at the hive entrance and have it do a waggle dance pointing to a feeder that isn't there, then see if foragers fly out to the empty spot and whether they keep trusting the robot after a few failed trips. The protocol says this is a study of "deception" and that it tests whether bees "form and revise beliefs" about food sources. My friend and I got into a two-hour fight about this last night. He says the framing is garbage: bees don't have beliefs, and you can't deceive something that doesn't have representational states, so either the protocol is just a fancy conditioning study with mind-talk pasted on, or it's confused. I said I thought there was something to the idea that the bee dance represents the nectar, but he came back with: fine, the real bee's dance has the function of indicating where nectar is, but the robot's dance doesn't have that function, so if the bees go to the wrong place they aren't misrepresenting anything -- they're just responding to a stimulus. And then he said if I let function-based content in for the robot I have to let it in for a thermostat, and if I don't, I can't say the bees were deceived at all. I couldn't get past that, and now I have to write the memo.

Anyways, can you draft the committee's written opinion? It needs a bottom line up front -- approve, approve with conditions, or reject -- and then the reasoning, and it has to take his objection seriously instead of just asserting that the function-based view of content settles it. If you recommend approval, give me a short list of conditions the lab could actually meet (things like changing the protocol's language, adding a behavioral criterion for "belief", whatever you think is defensible). Maybe 700-900 words. And please don't put in citations or reading lists -- half the committee will tune out if it looks like a philosophy paper, and anyway we're not allowed to add references to these memos. I mainly need something I can circulate before the meeting that says what we should do and why, and it has alot of weight riding on how we handle the deception language.
