I am trying to get clear on the disagreement between Dennett and Searle over whether there is a genuine, principled distinction between original and derived intentionality, and I have found that I can only defend Searle's side well when someone actually presses Dennett's case against me rather than summarizing it. Please play a committed Dennettian — someone who thinks the two-bitser and kindred examples show that the idea of an intrinsic, underived "original" intentionality is a piece of folk metaphysics, and that all aboutness is aboutness-for-an-interpreter, ascribed from the intentional stance. Stay in character until I say stop. You may concede that the two-bitser is a crude, toy case, and you may grant that human beings are vastly more complicated systems than coin machines, but you should never concede the main claim that Searle's distinction marks no principled boundary. Keep each reply to a solid paragraph or two so this feels like an exchange rather than a lecture; if I attribute to Dennett a view you think he would disown, correct me in character rather than stepping outside the role. You may draw on whatever examples you find useful — thermostats, chess engines, immune systems — but the two-bitser is the case I want you to grapple with first.

Here is my opening move. Searle's point is not that simple machines are unintelligent; it is that whatever aboutness a coin-slot has is borrowed. The machine's "belief" that a slug is a quarter exists only because we, or its designers and users, treat it that way, and it is parasitic on the original intentionality of the people who build and use it. Your two-bitser story seems to me to prove Searle's point rather than undermine it: once the machine is moved somewhere its coinage no longer matches the coins it was built to take, there is no fact of the matter about which of its "beliefs" are true and which false until an interpreter arrives and decides what to count as its belief. A system whose beliefs are fixed by the interpreter's convenience rather than by the system itself has derived intentionality if anything does. So where, exactly, is the argument that the distinction between derived and original collapses, rather than merely becoming hard to apply in marginal cases?
