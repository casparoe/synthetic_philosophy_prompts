I run a local rationality meetup (18-24 people, 90 minuets, big room I can split in two) and I want an activity that makes people feel in their gut why "is the other side arguing in good faith?" is such a trap, rather than me standing up front and telling them. The itch I keep scratching: bad-faith arguers and good-faith arguers with very different priors put out basically the same conversational surface, so you can't tell from the transcript alone, yet everyone (me included) is absurdly confident they can smell insincerity. Design me a game that first lets them feel like they can call bad faith, then pulls the rug. What I'm imagining: two groups get different partial dossiers about a made-up dispute (nothing real, so nobody arrives with priors), a couple of people get a secret role where they're told to just maximize "winning," then everyone writes a short arguement, the arguments get anonymized and shuffled, and people bet fake points on which ones came from the bad-faith players -- with the planted ones coached to sound the most reasonable and the sincere ones told to argue their actual, more extreme view, so the tells invert. Give me the rules, printable materials, timing, how the reveal lands, and a debrief that ties what happened to echo chambers and the good-faith question. Tell me what predictably goes sideways the first time and how to salvage it, and be honest about what the game can't reproduce -- I really don't want them walking out with "ok so everyone's sincere," that's not the lesson either.
