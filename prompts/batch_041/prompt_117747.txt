Ok so I need someone to tell me straight whether I did something wrong here, and I'd rather have the ugly version than the polite one.

Six weeks ago my landlord tried to keep my whole deposit. Claimed the marks by the window were damage, they were there when I moved in, the whole thing was nonsense. So I wrote him a letter. I didn't hire a lawyer, but my sister works at a firm and she helped me word it so it mentioned small claims, mentioned the deposit protection scheme, and — this is the bit I'm not proud of — mentioned that he owns three other flats in the city and probably doesn't want anyone poking around in them. He paid up in four days. Full amount, plus £50 for "the inconvenience."

My downstairs neighbour Dee had the same landlord, same nonsense charge, and she was exactly as much in the right as I was. She's got two kids and a job that eats her alive. She sent one email, got ignored, and gave up. She got nothing. She's not even angry. I asked her about it and she shrugged and said well, thats how it goes.

So here's whats chewing at me. I keep telling myself I got justice. But the only thing that actually got the money back was that I could make his life annoying in a way Dee couldn't. If I'd been a pushover, being right would have been worth exactly zero to me. So did I get justice, or did I just have more force? I know those are supposed to be two different things and I honestly can't see the difference anymore.

I've been reading Pascal's Pensées in the evenings, don't ask, and there's a bit where he says justice without force is useless and force without justice is just tyranny — fine, that's me, I thought. But then I kept reading and he seems to be saying something worse: that you can't actually give justice any force, because force is a tangible thing and justice isn't, so what people actually did was settle for making it right to obey whoever has the force. And there's another one where he basically says a law counts as just because it's been around a while and nobody's bothered to look at it, which sounds insane until you notice it's how everything works. Which would mean Dee wasn't robbed. She just lost. And I didn't win because I was right, I won because I was the bigger animal in that particular pond.

What I want from you:

1. Was what happened to Dee unjust? Nothing illegal happened to her. He didn't break a rule with her, he just ignored her.
2. If being right does nothing on its own, is right just a nicer word for can win?
3. If you push the distinction between justice and force as hard as Pascal does, does anything of it survive, or do you end up with the two words naming the same thing from different angles?
4. And the practical one: do I owe Dee anything? Half of what I got? Nothing? Or is that question just me wanting absolution for something I'd do again tomorrow?

Skip the both-sides thing where the truth is somewhere in the middle. If the honest answer is that I got away with something and Dee got shafted, say that.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning — no warm-up, no restating my question back to me.
