Heres a comparison I keep getting stuck on, and I'd rather you just reason it out from scratch than reach for something you've seen argued a hundred times — I'm offline, so no reading list, no citations, just tell me what you actually think.

The two options people usually get offered in the stakes-and-knowledge debate:

Contextualism: 'knows' is context-sensitive. The standards for counting as knowing shift with the conversational context of whoever's doing the attributing, so the same person with the same evidence can be a knower in a low-key chat and not a knower once a lawyer starts asking.

Pragmatic encroachment: it isn't the word that moves, it's knowledge itself. How much is practically at stake for the subject partly determines whether they know. Same evidence, same belief, same confidence — raise the stakes and the knowledge can just be gone.

Now the third thing, which is mine and probably half-baked, I've been calling question-shift. On this view nothing about knowledge moves at all, not the word and not the relation. What moves is which question the person is actually trying to answer, and so which proposition they need in hand before they can act. In the toothpaste version the question is 'is the pharmacy open till nine' and they know it. In the fever version the question is 'can I count on the pharmacy being open till nine' and thats one they don't know — while still knowing the first one. No stakes-sensitivity in knowledge, just a shift in the target.

What I want:

1. Is question-shift actually distinct from encroachment, or is it the same picture told from the other end? Give me a case where they predict different things. If you can't, say so plainly.
2. Same question for contextualism — where do those two come apart, and is question-shift any better off there?
3. Encroachment seems to need the subject to have some grip on how much is at stake. Take someone with the same evidence in a genuinely high-stakes situation who has no idea its high-stakes. What does each view say about whether they know, and which answer is more believable?
4. Encroachment gets a lot of its pull from the idea that knowledge is what licenses you to act on a thing. Question-shift drops that and says acting just requires the right target. Do we lose anything by dropping it? Can it still explain why it feels reckless to act on a strong hunch when a lot rides on it?
5. Verdict: which of the three would you bet on, and whats the best objection to it?
