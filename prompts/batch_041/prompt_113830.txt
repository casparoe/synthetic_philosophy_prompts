I want to analyse it through a theory I have formulated for the presentation, which I call the creative-authorship test. On this test, a work is genuinely creative only if a person or persons can be credited with (a) intending to produce an artwork, (b) exercising discriminating control over the features that make it what it is, and (c) being able to explain, revise, and stand behind the result. Please analyse the Obvious episode through this test. Who or what counts as the author under it? Does the collective's selection of training data, tweaking of the algorithm, and choice to exhibit and sell the output satisfy the test, or does the test misdescribe what happened? Then reverse the direction: what does the episode reveal about the test's limits — for example, whether it smuggles in an anthropocentric bias, or whether it cannot accommodate collaborative and computational agency? I would like a clear verdict on whether the test should be kept, revised, or abandoned. Please tell me how confident you are in your claims and identify the point you are least sure about.
