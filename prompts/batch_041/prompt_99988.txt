Dana said: obviously you could. The sperm that got there first was one of millions, a total accident; if a different one had won, the kid would still have been you, just with different looks and talents. So your particular origin can't be part of what makes you you.

Ravi said no. If a different sperm had fertilized that egg, the resulting person would have different genes, a different face, maybe a different everything. That person isn't you, it's more like a sibling. The actual sperm and egg are what built you, so they're part of you, and you couldn't have come from different ones.

Dana: that proves too much. If the exact sperm and egg are essential, so is the exact moment, the exact position, the exact temperature that night — then you couldn't have been conceived even a second later, which is absurd. And you just said the different-sperm kid would differ in looks and talents; but nobody thinks you're essentially your looks or your talents, those are accidental. So why is origin the one thing that isn't?

Ravi: because origin isn't a trait you have, it's the thing that made you. You could have been taller because the same you could have grown differently. A different sperm isn't the same you growing differently, it's a different person starting up. And you can't be consistent about this anyway — you'd say you could have come from a different egg, but eggs aren't interchangeable either, each one's a distinct cell. So either both are accidental, which is weird, or origin matters.

Dana: fine, then take the case that breaks it. Identical twins: one sperm, one egg, the zygote splits. On your story both twins have the exact same origin, so are they the same person? You'll have to say the split is part of the origin too, and then "origin" just means "the whole causal history" — and if the whole history is essential then nothing about you could have been different, which is the absurd result I started with.
