Ok so I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background at all, and my girlfriend and I keep having the same fight about whether I'm a hypocrite, so I want you to tell me what I actually believe.

My gut calls:

1. Dinner in the US, decent service: I leave 20%. Not optional, no debate.
2. Same place, server was actually rude: I still leave 15% and feel bad about going lower.
3. Tokyo hotel, I try to tip, the guy hands it back looking pained: I'm the one who's wrong, so I stop.
4. London escalator, rush hour, me and my suitcase parked on the left: not ok.
5. Same escalator, 11pm, nobody around: fine.
6. Empty road, 3am, red light, no cars, no cameras: I walk. Fine.
7. Same empty road in Munich, red light, 3am, but there's a dad with a kid waiting to cross: I wait, and I actually think walking would be wrong, even though nothing bad happens to anybody.
8. Holding a spot in a long queue for my mate who's parking the car: fine.
9. Four mates all "joining" me at the front of the bar queue: not fine. Basically the same thing as 8 and I can't tell you why one is fine.
10. Loud phone call on a quiet train car: not fine. Same volume in a loud pub: fine.
11. Me in Osaka, bowing wrong and fumbling everything: I think I get a pass.
12. My colleague from Osaka visits, doesn't tip at a US restaurant: I think he's in the wrong, and he didn't know doesn't get him off.

My half-baked theory is that some of these are what I've started calling password norms, arbitrary stuff where nobody's hurt if you skip it but knowing it is how you show you bothered to learn the place, and others are payment norms, where somebody actually eats the cost of you not doing it, like the waiters rent. Passwords I figure I only owe where I'm going to be seen. Payments I owe always. But that makes 7 come out fine, since nobody sees anything and nobody's out money, and I really dont think 7 is fine. It also makes 12 come out differently from 11, which might be the whole point, or might just be me being a hypocrite.

Is there a principle that gets all twelve? If not, which one do I give up and why that one. And tell me what your principle says about a couple I left out: a norm thats half-dead, where the older locals still do it and everyone under 30 doesnt, and a local custom that's just not ok, like the bar near my friend's hometown where the understood thing is you slip the guy at the door some cash to get a table. Do I follow the local rules there, or is that where the when-in-Rome thing stops? Be precise about the principle and honest about which of my twelve it can't save.
