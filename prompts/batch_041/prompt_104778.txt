My friend is finishing a book on the wrong kind of reasons — the idea that some reasons make an attitude "fitting" and others are the wrong kind of reason to do that job. I've been keeping a little list of her verdicts:

- A demon will torture you unless you admire a saucer of mud: the admiration isn't fitting.
- Your boss will promote you if you admire a colleague who really is admirable: fitting — though she insists the promotion is still the wrong kind of reason.
- A demon threatens you unless you fear a dog that's actually harmless: not fitting. Same dog turns out to be rabid and lunging at you: fitting.
- Someone pays you to believe a proposition you know is false: not fitting.
- A friend tells you to believe he's innocent because it'll cheer him up, evidence be damned: not fitting.
- A therapist tells you to be angry at your boss because venting will help you process your childhood: not fitting.
- A demon threatens you unless you're amused by a joke that is genuinely hilarious: fitting. Same threat, unfunny joke: not fitting.

Now the new case: a painting thats honestly mediocre, but it's the last thing your late mother ever painted. The only reason on offer for admiring it is that it was hers. What does she say?

I'm less interested in the yes/no than in: what rules fit her track record so far, what each one predicts here, which rule you'd bet she's actaully running and why, and what further case would tell those rules apart.
