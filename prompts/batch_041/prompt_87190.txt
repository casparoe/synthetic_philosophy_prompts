I volunteer on the small grants committee of a community fund in my town. We give away about fifteen thousand dollars a year, raised from local businesses and congregations, and I have been asked to write the short public rationale for whatever we decide at our next meeting. There are two proposals in front of us, and mine is the deciding vote.

The first is from the youth center in the part of town where the sheriff's reports show the most assaults. They want money to keep the gym and the tutoring room open on Friday and Saturday evenings. The second is from a group that drives people to medical appointments and runs the food pantry. They argue that in our county more people under sixty die of untreated illness and drug overdoses than are killed by other people, and that a fund worried about harm ought to be looking there instead.

Our chair says the second proposal is not about violence at all and should not be allowed to compete. A newer member, who reads a great deal of popular science, keeps saying that violence around the world has been falling for centuries, that this is one of the best-established findings there is, and that both proposals are beside the point because the trend will continue whatever we do. Someone else answers that this is nonsense: the world holds more people than ever, so if you count bodies rather than rates the numbers have gone up, and the studies only count the kinds of killing that get reported and the kinds of harm that rich countries happen to notice.

I have read enough to suspect that both of them are saying something true, and that their disagreement is not simply a disagreement about facts. What I need is not a verdict on world history but a way to think, because on Monday I have to produce two hundred words I can defend to people who trust me.

Please act as a philosopher who has spent real time on how social statistics get made, and on how much of any dispute is about the world and how much is about the words we use to describe it. Think this through carefully rather than reaching for the usual talking points, and do not treat the question of whether violence is rising or falling as settled in either direction. Begin with a short scratch section, no more than two hundred words, in which you list first every different thing 'violence' might reasonably mean for a committee like ours, and second every different way one might measure whether it is going up or down, before you commit to any conclusion.

Then give me your answer as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "what_the_disagreement_is_really_about": one sentence naming the actual crux between my chair and the newer member.
- "readings_of_violence": an array of three to five objects, each with "label" (a short phrase I could say out loud), "what_it_counts", "what_it_leaves_out", "who_is_helped_by_counting_this_way", and "strongest_objection".
- "ways_of_measuring_the_trend": an array of three to five objects, each with "label", "what_it_counts_and_divides_by", "what_it_would_show", "a_case_where_it_disagrees_with_the_others", and "strongest_objection".
- "is_there_a_fact_of_the_matter": an object with "verdict" (one or two sentences), "reasoning", and "what_stays_a_matter_of_choice".
- "what_this_means_for_the_committee": an array of three to five objects, each with "advice" and "why".
- "my_vote": an object with "proposal", "reasoning", "confidence_1_to_5", and "what_would_change_your_mind".
- "rationale_draft": a string of about two hundred words that does not commit me to any claim about worldwide trends.
- "what_that_wording_gives_up": a string, one or two sentences.
- "phrases_to_avoid": an array of objects, each with "phrase" and "why", covering wording that would smuggle in a disputed reading of the numbers.
- "open_questions": an array of strings, the things I should admit I do not know.

A few constraints. Do not give me citations, reading lists, or names of books or authors; I have no library and no way to check them, and they will not help me on Monday. If you are unsure whether something is a settled finding or your own way of framing it, say which, inside the relevant field. Keep every field to a few sentences — I would rather have the reasoning than completeness.
