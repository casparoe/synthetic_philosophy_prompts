ok so im in this seminar on distributive justice and we just finished the priority view + the leveling down objection, and the response paper is supposed to rank the various "repairs" that got thrown around in class discussion. problem is most of them got floated in the last ten minutes and nobody checked them, so i need to know which ones actually hold up.

heres what i wrote down, roughly:

1. gains/losses asymmetry: keep the diminishing weight on benefits (a gain matters more the worse off the person is), but say losses to people who are already better off just don't count against an outcome at all. so the view never says "level down, thats better" — it simply has nothing to say about the harm done.

2. pareto filter: rank outcomes by priority-weighted value same as always, but add a veto: no outcome is ranked higher if it makes someone worse off and makes no one better off. so leveling down gets ruled out by a side constraint rather than by the value function itself.

3. claims instead of levels: drop the assumption that a persons weight is fixed by how badly off they are, and instead say each person has a claim, whose strength depends on both how badly off they are and how big the gain is; an outcome is better when the claims favoring it are stronger than the claims against it. so making the better off worse off does count against an outcome, it just usually gets outweighed.

4. telic/deontic split: admit the axiology does rank the leveled down world as better, but insist the axiology is one thing and what we ought to do is another, so with the right constraints you'd never actually do it.

5. threshold version: only people below some line get any priority weight, above the line everyone counts the same. so leveling down among the better off is neutral, leveling down that hurts the worst off is bad.

6. change the currency: stop ranking outcomes by wellbeing and rank them by something like advantage or opportunity instead, on the theory that the whole objection is an artifact of welfarism.

what i want is a tier list of these (S/A/B/C/D, whatever) by how well each one actually answers the objection, one or two sentences per item explaining why it lands where it does, and for each one tell me whether its a real fix or whether it just changes the subject so the objection no longer applies. then at the end, straight up: which single one would you actually sign your name to, and is the version of prioritarianism that survives this still prioritarianism, or has it quietly turned into egalitarianism or a sufficiency view?
