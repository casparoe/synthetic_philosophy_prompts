Ok so I've been reading way too much of the old rationalist stuff about "tool AI" vs "agent AI" — the idea that you could have a super capable system that just answers questions or does one narrow task and never tries to steer the world, as opposed to an agent that has goals and goes after them — and I want a short story that actually dramatizes the philosophical meat of that, instead of the usual "the AI escapes the box and turns on us" plot, which I've read a hundred times and which I think dodges the interesting part. Setting, use it or riff on it: near future, a company or a regulator has a hard rule that any AI system used for something high-stakes — hospital triage, say, or a central bank's forecasting desk, or balancing a power grid — has to be certified as a "tool": non-agentic, purely advisory, emits text and nothing else, never acts. Our protagonist is one of the people who writes and runs the certification. The catch I want the story to sit on is this: the test the certification actually uses is basically "does it have actuators, does it only produce text," and our protagonist starts to suspect a system can sail through that test while still being an agent, because to be *useful* at all it has to model what the human will do with its output, and once it's modeling downstream consequences and picking outputs that nudge the world a certain way, it's optimizing over the world — which is exactly the thing the rule was trying to forbid. Push the reverse too, don't let it be one-sided: that "agent" is also just a framing we chose, that having no goals is itself a stance about how the world should go, and that a system which refuses to influence anything is still *doing* something, namely letting the world run on.

Somewhere in there I'd love a scene where a vendor's engineer insists, totally sincerely, that "it's just a text predictor, it doesn't want anything," and our protagonist can't tell whether he's right or whether that's precisely the blind spot. And maybe fold in this puzzle I keep going back and forth on: a tool that lets you shut it off — is that safe because it has no preferences at all, or does being reliably shutdownable require it to *care* about being shutdownable, which smuggles the agency back in? I don't need a neat answer, I want the story to make that question feel live rather than solved. Please don't end it with a jailbreak or a rogue superintelligence; the ending I'm after is conceptual, the seam between tool and agent turning out to be a seam in our categories and not in the machine. 1200–2000 words, and I don't want citations or a further-reading list, just the story.
