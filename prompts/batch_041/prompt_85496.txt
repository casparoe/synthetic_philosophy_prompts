Last night a friend and I went at each other for like two hours about whose values these big models are actually supposed to be serving, and I woke up still irritated because I think we were both making it up as we went. Her line was that the whole framing is broken: an AI isn't a neutral instrument aimed at some pre-existing public, it's already inside the demos, shaping the very preferences it's later supposed to read off and satisfy, so any theory that treats "the values of the public" as an input it gets to go consult is just laundering the model's own influence. I half agree with her and half think she's dodging the hard part. So before I read another thing about alignment or RLHF I want the actual spec, not the vibes. Write me the desiderata that any adequate theory of "whose values should an aligned AI serve" would have to meet.

What I'm after is the requirements list, with the consideration or case sitting behind each one — the cases it has to get right (and not just the easy ones like a model that should refuse to help me poison a reservoir), the distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse (serving values vs. serving interests; deference vs. corrigibility; a value the public actually holds vs. one the model manufactured and then elicited; aggregating within a person vs. across persons), and the results it can't contradict (that people's preferences are endogenous and often confabulated on the spot; that interpersonal comparison is contested; that "the public" isn't a fixed set because future and not-yet-existing people are inside it). And say what it owes to liberal neutrality, because a theory that quietly smuggles in one faction's substantive values is exactly what my friend claims is already happening under the hood. Tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just house commitments of one camp, and — the part I most want — whether some pair of them can't be jointly satisfied. My suspicion is that her endogeneity point plus any requirement that the AI defer to the public's values is a live contradiction, but I'd love to be told I'm wrong.

Also, and this matters to me more than the tidy answer: say where your own reasoning is most likely to fall apart here, and what you'd have to be shown — some concrete case or argument — to decide the whole list is the wrong shape, e.g. that the question "whose values" is itself the mistake and the real spec should be about something else entirely. And if one requirement is doing most of the work of ruling theories out, say which one and why.
