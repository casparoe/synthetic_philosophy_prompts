I'm a retired physicist — I taught classical mechanics and special relativity for thirty years, and since retiring I've been reading philosophy of time with more enthusiasm than background, and I've gotten stuck on a debate I cannot tell is deep or merely verbal. The debate is the one over whether special relativity establishes a block universe. The argument, as I understand it, is the one usually labeled Rietdijk–Putnam: take two events that are spacelike separated, so that in one inertial frame they are simultaneous and in another one is earlier than the other; since simultaneity is frame-relative and no frame is privileged, and since — the crucial step — whatever is simultaneous with a real event is itself real, it follows that the distant event is real too; chain that step through the light cones and every event in spacetime is equally real, which makes the present an arbitrary slice and strips becoming of any place in the world. Presentists and growing-block theorists reply that the crucial step is precisely what is at issue: being simultaneous with a real event is not a ticket to reality, and the argument smuggles in a frame-independent notion of the present that the physics has already retired. The eternalist responds that the presentist is simply refusing to follow the physics, or is secretly positing a privileged now. I have now read this exchange in several versions, and what strikes me is that both sides accept every equation and every experimental result, nothing empirical is in dispute, and yet nobody has moved in decades.

I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict, and I have a hypothesis I would like you to test rather than adopt. My suspicion is that the dispute is not about physics, and may not even be substantive, but is a dispute about what the word "real" is being asked to do: the eternalist treats it as a frame-independent predicate, the presentist treats it as something closer to an indexical, the way "here" and "now" pick out their referents only relative to a context, so that the two sides are not so much contradicting each other as using one word in two ways. What makes me hesitate is that the disagreement does not feel verbal from the inside: the presentist seems to be making a claim about what there is, not about how to talk, and the eternalist seems to be drawing an ontological conclusion from a physical theory, not legislating usage. I have also noticed that the whole argument turns on a single step — what I have been calling, in my own notes, the reality-transfer principle, the claim that an event simultaneous with a real event is thereby real — and I cannot tell whether that step is a trivial consequence of what "real" means or a substantive metaphysical premise that could be denied without denying anything the physicist is entitled to.

So: tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal or substantive; do the two sides differ about facts, about what would count as an explanation, about which cases count as data, or about starting intuitions that neither can argue for; is a hidden formal question — say, about whether tense and indexicals can be represented in a tenseless language, or about whether "real" can coherently be relativized to a frame — doing the real work; are the two sides answering different questions under one heading, one about the ontology of a physical theory and one about the semantics of "now"; or is the question itself ill-posed? Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a theorem, a reformulation, a change of question — or whether we should expect none, and why. I would like you to commit to a diagnosis and defend it rather than laying out the options for me to choose among; I have read enough surveys. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
