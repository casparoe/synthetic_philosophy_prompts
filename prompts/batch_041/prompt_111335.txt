Consider the problem of old evidence. A hypothesis h is supported by a fact e that the agent already knows with certainty. Because the agent's credence in e is 1, conditionalizing on e cannot raise their credence in h; and if confirmation is read incrementally — e confirms h for an agent just in case the agent's credence in h is higher after learning e than before — then e cannot confirm h at all. The usual reply treats this as an artifact of the agent's particular history: what measures the support e lends h is not the change in this agent's credence but the change that would have occurred had e arrived as news, computed from a prior representing the agent's credences before any empirical evidence, or equivalently from the likelihood ratio P(e | h) / P(e | ¬h).

Now add an agent the standard discussion ignores. Call her the Forgetter. At t1 the Forgetter is certain of e and holds credence 0.9 in h. Between t1 and t2, with no new evidence arriving and nothing being learned, ordinary memory decay sets in and her credence in e falls from 1 to some r < 1. At t3 she encounters e afresh — a document, a re-derivation, a re-run of an experiment — and her credence in e returns to 1, restoring her credence in h to 0.9. Compare her with a Never-Forgetter who simply retains e throughout. The two agents end in identical doxastic states: certain of e, 0.9 in h. On the incremental reading, however, e confirmed h for the Forgetter (her credence in h rose at t3) and did not confirm h for the Never-Forgetter (her credence in h never moved).

I want your credence in the following claim:

(C) The Forgetter case is a genuine counterexample to the incremental reading taken as an analysis of what it is for evidence to confirm a hypothesis. It shows that "e confirms h" cannot be analyzed as "the agent's credence in h rises upon learning e," since that analysis must count e as confirming h for the Forgetter and as failing to confirm h for the Never-Forgetter even though the two end with identical credences in e and in h; and it motivates treating confirmation as an agent-independent, two-place relation between evidence and hypothesis.

Give me the decomposition, not a bare number. Break (C) into the sub-questions you think it actually rests on — for instance: whether the case is coherent as described (can an agent lose certainty in e non-evidentially, without the loss itself becoming evidence bearing on h?); whether the incremental reading is genuinely committed to the asymmetric verdict; whether that asymmetry is a defect or merely the harmless consequence of confirmation being a property of transitions rather than of states; whether an agent-independent two-place relation can be made precise, or whether it collapses back into a likelihood ratio that silently presupposes a prior; and whether the counterfactual or ur-prior response already dissolves the case.
