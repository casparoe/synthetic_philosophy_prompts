I've been reading around the idea that minds come in grades, and I want to test it on a real practice. Roughly: a Darwinian creature just has the responses natural selection gave it; a Skinnerian creature can be shaped by reinforcement, i.e. by what actually happens to it; a Popperian creature can run the experiment in its head and let the hypothesis die instead of itself; and a Gregorian creature thinks with stuff outside its skull -- notches, forms, contracts, institutions. I'm probably mangling it, so correct my gloss if it's off.

Now the practice: self-binding devices. Casino self-exclusion lists you sign up for and then can't get off for a year. Apps where you put money in escrow and lose it if you don't go to the gym. Retirement plans you can set to auto-escalate your contribution every year unless you actively opt out. What strikes me is that these only work because the later you can't argue your way out of them -- if I could talk the app into releasing the money at 6am when I don't feel like running, it wouldn't be a commitment device at all. And yet we don't normally say the app robs me of autonomoy; we say it's how a person exercises control over their future self. That looks like a tension to me and I can't tell if it's a real one or just two different senses of "me".

So: what kind of creature does this practice presuppose the person is? Which grade is the committer at the moment of committing, and which is the committed at the moment of temptation, and does the practice need a single answer, or does it presuppose that the capacity to weigh reasons is intermittent in us and not the essence of the person? Separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits nicely with -- in particular, does it actually require a divided-self or Parfit-ish picture, or can a plain unified-self story license the same devices? Say where it's philosophically underdetermined. Then: if you replaced the implicit picture with a thoroughgoing Popperian one, where the person is an agent whose current judgments are always revisable in light of reasons, what about these devices would have to change, and is there any version of them that survives?

You're a philosopher of mind who also knows the self-control and free-will literature; think it through carefully before you write and don't just hand me the obvious two-selves answer. Under 700 words, please: one-sentence verdict first, then the reconstruction, then the two lists, then the revision bit.
