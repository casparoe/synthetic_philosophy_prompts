I am a philosophy professor, and a close friend has asked me to referee a standoff in his household that I cannot resolve to my own satisfaction. He and his partner each keep one private practice in their own room that the other cannot abide: he eats meat, which she thinks barbaric; she keeps a pet rat, which he thinks degrading. Neither practice touches the other in any material way, yet each cares about the other's practice as much as about their own — he wants her to give up the rat, she wants him to give up the meat — while each would still rather keep their own practice than submit to the other's demand. He asks who is right, and what the two of them should actually do. My instinct is that each of them should simply decide what happens in their own room, which would leave both practices standing; but when I try to state the principle behind that instinct and apply it consistently, I find it also licenses each of them to veto the other's private business, and my verdicts begin to run in a circle, while trying instead to honor what the two of them want taken together pulls me toward a different answer. He has also proposed a market solution — that each may buy the other's compliance, she paying him to stop eating meat and he paying her to give up the rat — and wants to know whether that settles it. Please tell me how to think this through: what the competing principles are, whether any single answer respects both the thought that each person governs their own room and the thought that the household should end up with what its members most want, whether the market proposal genuinely resolves the difficulty or merely relocates it, and, if there is no clean answer, which principle I should give up and what I should advise him to do.
