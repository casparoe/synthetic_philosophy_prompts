I've somehow ended up convening a reading group on deep history — we've been working through the recent spate of big trade books about prehistory, foragers, and where inequality comes from — and next month it's my turn to run the session on the big one: was the agricultural revolution a mistake? The trouble is we've had this conversation three times now and it always goes in circles, because everyone is answering a different question with the same words. One friend says it was obviously a catastrophe — shorter, sicker, harder-working bodies, and then the whole apparatus of stored surplus, hierarchy and war that comes with settled grain; another says you can't call it a mistake at all, because nobody decided it, farming spread because farming populations outbred and displaced the foragers next door rather than because anyone weighed the options and picked; a third says both of them miss the point because the comparison is rigged from the start, since we're silently comparing a romanticised forager to an average early farmer, or an average to an average, or the best case to the worst. I want to stop relitigating this and just hand people a map.

So what I'd like from you is a decision tree (or taxonomy — whichever fits better) of the positions on "was the agricultural revolution a mistake?", built from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names: first the questions, then where the actual positions sit in the space those questions carve up. Every branch node should be a question someone could answer about their own view without already knowing which position it leads to — e.g. what has to be true for the word "mistake" to apply at all (does it need an agent, a live alternative, foreseeability, or is regret-aptness enough?); whose welfare or standing is being toted up (the individual, the lineage, the species, or the distribution — the gap between best-off and worst-off rather than the average); what currency the comparison is in (health and hedonic welfare, freedom and autonomy over one's own labour, equality, or cultural achievement — and what you do when those pull apart); whether the baseline is the actual historical forager or an idealised one; and whether what spread was a practice people adopted or a population that replaced its neighbours. Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or "unnamed" if genuinely nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and please flag any two leaves your tree can't really separate, and note which combinations of branch answers come out empty and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Aim for eight to twelve leaves, keep the node labels short enough to fit in a box on a one-page handout, and don't bother with citations or a reading list — I'll bolt those on myself, I just want the logical structure and the diagnostic questions to be right.
