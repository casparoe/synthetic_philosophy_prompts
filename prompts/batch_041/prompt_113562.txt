I am a novelist, and I need to keep the moral verdicts of my protagonist, Vera, consistent. Vera is a committed Objectivist. So far she has approved of a pharmaceutical executive who raises the price of a life-saving drug during a shortage to maximize profit; condemned a surgeon who gives up her practice to care for her disabled child; approved of a donor who funds a university because he expects it to produce employees for his company; approved of a mother who risks her life to save her own child; and condemned a man who gives anonymously to famine relief out of a sense of duty. In the next chapter, Vera learns of a woman who donates a kidney to a stranger in order to win the admiration of a man she loves, risking her health, and who says she cares nothing for the stranger. What will Vera say? Which rule is she actually running, what do the other rules that fit her earlier verdicts predict, and which further case would best distinguish them?
