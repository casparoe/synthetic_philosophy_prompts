My friend and I spent most of yesterday evening on the following argument, and I still cannot find the exit. She says it shows that Quine's indeterminacy of translation refutes itself. I would rather not surrender premises (1) or (2), which seem to me to amount to little more than the ordinary thought that meaning has to show up in use. So tell me which other step to give up, why it looked innocent, and what giving it up costs me in claims about meaning I might have wanted to keep.

(1) All the evidence anyone ever has about what a speaker's words mean is which sentences she assents to and dissents from, and in which observable circumstances.

(2) If two rival interpretations of a speaker's words predict the same pattern of assent and dissent in every possible circumstance, then all possible evidence supports them equally.

(3) Quine's example can be reproduced at will: reading the word gavagai as a term for rabbits and reading it as a term for undetached rabbit parts can each be made to fit the same pattern of assent and dissent, provided the interpreter compensates elsewhere in his manual of translation.

(4) So there is no fact of the matter as to which interpretation is correct: translation is indeterminate.

(5) Nothing in this reasoning turns on the speaker's being foreign. My neighbor, my spouse, and my own self of ten years ago are equally speakers whose words I can interpret only from assent and dissent in observable circumstances.

(6) So there is no fact of the matter about what my neighbor means when she says rabbit, nor about what I meant by the word in the diary I kept ten years ago.

(7) But if there is no fact of the matter about what I meant ten years ago, there is none about what I mean now; I have no better access to my present meanings than to my past ones.

(8) An argument whose own words have no determinate meaning cannot establish anything.

(9) Therefore the indeterminacy thesis refutes itself, and (4) is false.

She says the only way out is to abandon (1) or (2). I would rather keep both. Which step should I reject instead, and what does the repair cost?

Answer in no more than 400 words.
