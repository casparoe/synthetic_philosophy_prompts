So I just lost about three hours of my life arguing with my housemate, who's doing a master's in cognitive science and has recently decided he's a hard determinist — he's got the whole thing, the brain scans, the "you're just a meat puppet" jokes, the works. The thing he kept coming back to, over and over, like it was a trump card he could just lay down whenever the argument went badly for him, was "you could have done otherwise." He'd say it in this flat mocking voice, as if determinism obviously empties the phrase out, and when I tried to say that's not obviously what it means he said no, look, it's *the* definition of free will, everyone knows that, if you couldn't have done otherwise you weren't free, full stop. At one point he literally waved at his own head and said "see? it couldn't have done otherwise." I tried to make a compatiblist move and got tangled up and ended up conceding stuff I don't actually believe, which is still annoying me. I want to go back at him better armed, but not with an essay defending one side — I want the menu.

So: what are all the precise things "could have done otherwise" can actually be claiming? Does the "could" mean an ability I had at the time, or a real possibility given the past and the laws, or something about what would have happened if I'd wanted/tried/chosen differently, or something almost epistemic about what anyone could have known beforehand? Is it a claim about a counterfactual, a claim about a power or disposition, a claim about chance, a claim about opportunity? Whose "could" is it — mine, an ideal observer's, the universe's? And then for each reading I want a verdict: is it trivially true, is it a substantive thesis about laws or causation, is it just false, is it unfalsifiable, is it true but not about what he thinks it's about? Most important, which of the readings would actually get him his conclusion that determinism kills free will, and which ones can compatibilists just cheerfully grant and move on? My suspicion is that half the argument was the two of us running different readings of the same five words without noticing, and that the reading doing the real work in his argument is one where the phrase is doing almost nothing.

Two specific things I'd like handled if you can. First, whether the Frankfurt-style cases (where someone would have been made to do the thing anyway but wasn't actually made to do it) touch any of these readings at all — he dismissed them as a party trick and I couldn't articulate why anyone cares. Second, whether the readings under which "could have done otherwise" comes out true all end up being about the agent's psychology rather than about the world, because I think that's the crux but I'm genuinely not sure. Lay it out as a list I can bring to the kitchen table, with a verdict on each reading rather than a defence of one. And please tell me how confident you are in each verdict, and single out the one point you're least sure of — I'd rather know where the soft spot is than get a confident-sounding answer that he'll take apart in two minutes.
