I want a case analysis, not a history lesson, of how the United States Constitution came into force, read through the framework of self-amending rules — the structure that the game Nomic makes explicit, in which the rules in play include the rule for changing rules, and a change can therefore be authorized by the very rule it alters.

The facts I have in mind are these. Under the Articles of Confederation, amendment required the consent of Congress and the unanimous ratification of all thirteen state legislatures. The convention that met in Philadelphia in 1787 was convened under a congressional resolution that confined it to revising the Articles. The delegates instead produced a new frame of government whose seventh article provided that ratification by nine state conventions — not thirteen legislatures — would suffice to bring it into effect among the ratifying states, and it did, with North Carolina and Rhode Island still outside at the first Congress.

Treat this as a self-amendment problem and run the analysis in both directions. First, map it: who the players were (the state ratifying conventions, Congress, the delegates), what rules were actually in force at each step, and what precise move was made when the nine-state rule was used to displace the unanimity rule. Say which features the self-amendment framework explains — why the move is not merely unlawful but structurally paradoxical, how a rule can serve as its own authorization, and why the framers needed a new rule of ratification rather than an amendment carried out under the old one. Say also what the framework misses: the role of extralegal pressure and maneuver, the possibility that a rule can be effective without being valid, and any sense in which the old order lapsed rather than being amended.

Second, let the episode test the framework. Distinguish three things: a change made by the system's own amendment procedure; a change to that procedure itself; and a change to the deeper rule that fixes what counts as an authoritative change in the first place. Jurists sometimes reserve a technical sense of 'revolution' for the third, meaning a break in the chain of authority rather than an act performed under it. Tell me which of the three the ratification was, whether classifying it is a factual or a stipulative matter, and what turns on getting it right. Consider the associated regress problem — whether any rule can authorize its own replacement without presupposing an authority outside itself — and what the deliberate entrenchment of the unanimity requirement shows about whether a system can bind its own future self-amendment. Then say what the episode shows about the self-amendment framework: whether it confirms the framework, is an anomaly for it, or is a case the framework never anticipated, given that the framers had no theory of self-amendment and simply did it.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material. In particular, do not present as consensus any particular verdict on whether the founding was unlawful, nor any confident estimate of how much the outcome depended on luck and coercion rather than on the logic of the rules; where you are offering a contestable reading, mark it as such.
