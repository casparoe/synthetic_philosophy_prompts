I host a small podcast on formal epistemology, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends Jeffrey conditionalization as the correct general rule for updating on uncertain evidence and insists that the familiar objections to it can be met. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press him rather than let him simply restate the rule. At least one question should target the order-dependence problem: why shifting the probability of A and then of B can yield a different posterior than the reverse order, and whether repairing this requires an additional rigidity or sufficiency assumption that the rule itself does not supply. At least one should ask what licenses the new probabilities assigned to the evidential partition, given that the rule treats them as inputs while the agent's evidence is supposed to be about the world rather than about her own credences. A third should ask how the rule relates to ordinary strict conditioning and whether the dynamic-coherence or diachronic Dutch-book argument that is usually taken to support conditioning carries over to it. Include one question on evidence whose uncertainty is not merely a partition shift -- for instance, evidence from a source of disputed reliability -- and one on whether the rule is a substantive norm of rationality or only a consistency constraint among an agent's distributions. For each main question, add the follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give.
