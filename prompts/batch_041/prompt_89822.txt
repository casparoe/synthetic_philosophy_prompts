I've just been co-opted onto the committee that runs our village hall, and I've got the casting vote on how we spend a £15,000 legacy. Option one is a ramp and an accessible loo, which in practice would be for one person: Mr Aldridge, who's been stuck at the back of the hall for years and can't get to the quiz nights or the funeral teas. Option two is a new kitchen and a general spruce-up that all 200-odd members would enjoy a bit — nothing dramatic, just nicer. Everyones saying the kitchen wins, because 200 people getting something they like beats one person getting something they like. That feels off to me but i cant say why. And the opposite line, that you can never weigh one person's interests against lots of other people's, feels off too, because it means the ramp wins no matter how trivial the thing on the other side is. Mr Aldridge keeps telling us not to make a fuss on his account, which somehow makes me feel worse rather than better. A friend on the committee said the real question isn't who gains the most in total, but whether Mr Aldridge would have a genuine grievance against the rule we're applying. Is that right? And if it is, what am I supposed to do with the other 200 people's side of it — do their small gains just get ignored? I'd like to say something at Thursday's meeting that isn't just me being sentimental about Mr Aldridge. Please answer in British English.
