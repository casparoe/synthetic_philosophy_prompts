I'm applying for a small departmental research grant — six months, about a day a week — and I need a one-page proposal. I've got a half-formed idea and I'd rather you turn it into something proper than have me pad it out myself.

The idea: everyone treats the outside view as the grown-up corrective to the inside view. Stop asking what you think will happen in your case; ask what usually happens in cases like yours. Fine. But deciding which cases are 'like yours' — settling on the reference class — is itself an inside-view judgement, and its the one doing all the work. So either the outside view is parasitic on the inside view, in which case it can't be the general corrective it's advertised as, or there's a principled, non-question-begging way of picking a reference class, and I've never seen one stated. Then there's the second horn, which is the bit I actually care about: the modesty angle. Is it immodest to insist your case is special, or is it immodest to throw away the one piece of evidence nobody else has — the view from inside? I want to know whether 'be modest' can be stated as a rule at all without collapsing into 'use the outside view', and if it can't, what that tells us about the norm.

Turn this into a proposal. I need the question stated so that it has a determinate answer, not just a topic; why the obvious existing positions leave it open; the method — what I'd actually do, month by month, for six months at a day a week — and why that method rather than the obvious alternatives; what a successful result would look like; and what would count as the project failing. Be honest about scope too: if this is secretly a three-year project dressed up as a six-month one, say so and cut it down to something that fits.

Dont bother with citations or a reading list, I'll fill those in myself. Write it in British English.
