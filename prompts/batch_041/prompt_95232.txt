Here is my opening move. The obfuscated-arguments problem, as I understand it, is that the judge decides within a budget, so a dishonest debater who offers an argument that is merely expensive to check can beat an honest debater who knows the answer but cannot make the judge see it in time, and the judge cannot tell which of the two is which. Your preferred fix, on my reading, buys its guarantee by narrowing the class of claims it covers: it needs the disputed question to be a computation whose output the honest debater can certify, which is to say it needs the question to have the right proof structure, and it needs the commitment scheme to hold up. Now consider the questions that motivate the entire program: whether this training run will produce a model that defects, whether this deployment plan is safe, whether that reward signal was gamed. Nobody has reduced any of those to a computation with a checkable certificate, and if we formalise safety as passing some verification procedure, we have assumed the very thing under dispute. Worse, the failure is silent rather than loud: outside the guaranteed class the protocol does not return no answer, it returns a confident one, and nothing in the transcript tells the judge which regime it is in. So tell me what you deny. If you accept the scope restriction, give me the concrete mechanism by which a claim like this deployment is safe becomes a computation with a certificate, without presupposing the conclusion; if you reject the restriction, tell me what the honest debater does when the honest strategy is itself too expensive for anyone in the room to compute.
