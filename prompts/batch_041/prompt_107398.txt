Here is the case I want to put to you. A small research institute has a distinctive way of signaling that one has finished speaking and is yielding the floor: a particular slow nod. Nothing about it is natural; a raised hand, a tap on the table, or a spoken formula would serve exactly as well. Everyone in the institute does it, everyone expects everyone else to do it, and that expectation is common knowledge. So far, textbook. Now the facts I have since learned. Almost no one there likes the nod; most find it affected and faintly embarrassing. Each person keeps doing it because they believe the others like it and would take offense if it were dropped, and each believes this about the others without ever having asked. In fact no one would take offense -- the belief is false across the board. Newcomers who raise a hand instead are not sanctioned; the offense everyone fears has never once materialized. And if you asked each of them off the record which world they would prefer, one where everyone nods or one where everyone raises a hand, every single one would say the raised hand.

My opening challenge is this. Is the nod a convention on your analysis? If you say yes, then your analysis certifies as a convention a regularity that survives only because every participant is mistaken about every other participant's attitudes, and that every participant would be glad to see replaced -- which makes the label sound like an achievement it has not earned. If you say no, then name the condition it fails, and notice that you cannot say it fails the condition that each prefers to conform when others conform, because from the inside each of them does prefer to conform: each believes nonconformity would wound people they like. So which is it, and what does your answer cost you?
