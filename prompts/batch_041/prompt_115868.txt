Draft the founding constitution of a small residential community whose members have decided to govern themselves explicitly on the theory of collective intentionality. Write it in the flat, numbered, deadpan register of an actual charter or covenant — clauses, sub-clauses, defined terms, an amendment procedure — and let it run to roughly 650 words.

Every operative article should take a real position in the philosophy of shared intention and joint action, translated into concrete administrative terms. The founders are serious amateurs, so the document should legislate: the difference between a genuine we-intention and a mere coincidence of individual intentions that happen to align; the conditions under which a shared intention exists at all (each member intending that the group act, mutual responsiveness, and the common knowledge that makes the intention mutual rather than merely parallel); whether the group can be said to intend something that no individual member intends; what a member owes the others after she has changed her mind; and how a shared intention is extinguished when a member defects, dies, leaves, or when the community itself dissolves.

Let the humor come from taking the machinery literally: treat shared intentions as administrative facts that can be registered, audited, inspected, and litigated. The document should quietly encode three different accounts of what makes an intention shared — one that reduces it to interlocking individual intentions plus common knowledge, one that treats the plural intention as primitive and irreducible, and one that grounds it in a joint commitment binding the members independently of their present desires — without naming any philosopher and without adjudicating among them. A reader who knows the literature should be able to tell which articles are doing which, and should find the satire fair rather than an outsider's mockery; a reader who does not should still grasp what is being gently made fun of.

Include at least one clause that commits the community to something philosophically indefensible and at least one that flatly contradicts an earlier clause, so that the document is as internally troubled as the position it encodes. Do not resolve the contradiction, and do not break the deadpan.
