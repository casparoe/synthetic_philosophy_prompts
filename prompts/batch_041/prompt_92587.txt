Ok so I'm a retired soil chemist — 35 years of nitrate leaching work, mostly field sites — and since I packed it in I've been sitting in on a philosophy reading group at the local uni. Last week was peer disagreement and I've got a bee in my bonnet about it, so I want to write a dialogue for the group. Can you draft it for me.

Setup I want: two characters, call them A and B. Both competent, both honest, neither one a fool or a punching bag. They disagree about whether a particular published finding holds up. But here's the wrinkle I care about — they trained in the same lab under the same supervisor and both picked up the same slightly idiosyncratic way of correcting for instrument drift, a multiplicative factor they apply without really thinking about it. So their judgments may look independent but they're arguably two readings off the same crooked ruler.

A is a conciliationist: find yourself disagreeing with an epistemic peer, you split the difference. B is a steadfaster: you can rationally hold your ground if you're confident in your own reasoning. What I want them to actually fight about is whether A's view survives its own medicine. B thinks conciliationism is wrong — so by A's own rule, shouldn't A be downgrading conciliationism right now, since a peer disagrees? And are A and B even peers, given the shared lab? Or is shared training the opposite of peerhood — correlated error rather than independent confirmation? I want B to press that the two of them agreeing wouldn't be evidence of anything, while A presses that a disagreement between two people with the same blind spot is exactly the kind of thing conciliation is for.

Please write the dialogue, 900-1200 words. Real back and forth, they respond to each others actual points instead of talking past each other, and neither side should be an obvious strawman. One thing though — don't let it end with a shrug or a "well it's complicated." I'd rather it stop on a sharp unresolved question than a cop-out. Also if you can work in something about whether they can even identify each other as peers without already knowing who's right, that would be great, thats the bit thats been nagging me.
