Take the 2006 reclassification of Pluto and analyze it as a case for the theory of rigid designation and the necessary a posteriori. The facts you need are common knowledge: in August 2006 the International Astronomical Union adopted three criteria for planethood — orbiting the Sun, sufficient mass to assume hydrostatic equilibrium, and having cleared the neighbourhood of its orbit — and by that definition Pluto, counted a planet since its discovery in 1930, became a dwarf planet; the vote was prompted by the discovery of Eris and other Kuiper Belt objects comparable to Pluto; and the same resolution also reclassified Ceres, itself called a planet after its discovery in 1801, as a dwarf planet. Treat the decision as the episode and Kripke's account of natural-kind terms as the theory, and run the analysis in both directions. If planet is a rigid designator of a natural kind, then either Pluto was always a planet and the IAU changed the subject, or it never was and the vote recorded a discovery; say which reading the account forces, whether either is tolerable, and what work the notion of a baptismal event can do when the baptism is a committee vote. If instead the reference of planet was fixed all along by a cluster of descriptions that the resolution rewrote, ask whether this vindicates the division of linguistic labour or exposes its limits, and compare the case of Vulcan, the intra-Mercurial planet posited in the nineteenth century to explain Mercury's anomalous perihelion and never found, where the reference-fixing description turned out to have no object: does planet have determinate reference before 2006, or does the vote constitute it retroactively? Finally, say what the episode shows about the necessary a posteriori itself — whether there are any necessities of the form planets are F that only experience could teach us, or whether the Kripkean model covers chemical and biological kinds while leaving the classificatory kinds of astronomy to convention. Be disciplined about the mapping — who the linguistic community is, what the evidence was, what changed and what did not — and where the theory is silent, say so rather than forcing it to speak.
