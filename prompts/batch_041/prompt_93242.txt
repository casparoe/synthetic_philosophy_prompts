I have been trying to find the name of a configuration for some months now and have begun to suspect that it does not have one, which would itself be worth knowing. Let me describe it and ask whether I am missing a term, or whether the thing I am pointing at is simply a familiar concept in unfamiliar clothing.

Start from the standard result that the expected value of free, reliable information cannot be negative. The proof is one I accept: whatever act the uninformed agent would have chosen, the informed agent is free to choose it too, so acquiring the information can only raise the maximum expected utility. The step that does the work is the agent's freedom to act as though they had not learned anything. I keep encountering cases that appear to escape the result, and I have ruled out the escapes I know how to name: the information is not costly, not unreliable, not public, and not being used to signal anything to anyone, and the agent is not updating badly.

What remains is a narrower structure, and it turns on that option to ignore. The cases are ones in which the option is formally available and practically foreclosed, because the agent is the sort of agent who will respond to the information, and responding is what leaves them worse off. A person who is offered a prognosis they can do nothing about will not file it away and carry on; they will reorganize their life around it, and the reorganized life is worse than the one they would have led without the test. A bargainer who learns the other side's true reservation price — by a move the other side will detect — will bargain differently, and the difference costs them the deal. In both, the information is free and true, and it leaves the agent worse off, and the loss is not in the content of what is learned but in the fact that the learner cannot remain the agent they were.

I suspect the second example is a different animal: there the harm runs through the other party's reaction to being probed, which is a strategic matter rather than an epistemic one. The first is what I cannot place. Is there an established name for it — for a negative value of information that arises because the recipient cannot commit to ignoring what they have received? If so, tell me which concept it is and where my description overshoots or undershoots it. If not, give me the best carving: what exactly separates it from the bare observation that information can be worth less than nothing; what separates it from the familiar thought that one may be better off not knowing and should arrange not to find out; and whether the operative mechanism is the stickiness of belief, the impossibility of self-commitment, or the fact that knowing is itself something the agent has preferences about. I would like a definition with edges — one case plainly inside it and one plainly outside — and, if the thing is genuinely unnamed, a term for it that will not be confused with the neighbours.
