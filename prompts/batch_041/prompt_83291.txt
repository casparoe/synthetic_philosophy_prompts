I did a linguistics minor years ago and now teach ESL, so I notice this stuff but I'm well out of the literature. Is there a name for the following? Take a sentence with one of those group insults in it — "he's a ___", say. Deny it and the insult survives. Ask it as a question, survives. Bury it in an if-clause, survives. Fine, the nastiness isn't part of what's being asserted, it just rides along. But it also won't come off: with an ordinary background assumption — someone's quit smoking, so I can tack on "and I'm not saying she ever smoked" — the background bit can be waved away, and there's no version of that move for the insult, no hedge that makes it go away. And it comes in degrees, which is what threw me: some of these words are worse than others, and the same one stings more aimed at someone's face than sitting in a list of categories. And it seems to turn on who's talking more than what they think — an outsider who plainly means no harm still sounds bad, while two people from the group can throw it at each other affectionately, descriptive content unchanged. So: sticky, projective, graded, and licensed by who you are. Doesnt sound like the background-content category I was taught, and doesn't sound like the "the word just shows the speaker's attitude" line either. Give me the nearest established concepts, what each one gets right and where each one breaks, and if nothing fits, coin a term — with its edges, and what it rules out.
