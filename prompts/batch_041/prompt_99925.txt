I'm a novelist, halfway through a book where two characters have a long fight in a car, and I want the fight to actually be about something instead of just being about who forgot to pay the electric bill. So I've decided they argue about van Fraassen's Reflection principle, and I need you to write the dialogue.

Setup: he's a gambler, she's the philosopher. He's about to sit up all night and he knows that by 3am he'll be wrecked — tired, and needled into a foul, credulous mood by her. He says: right now I put the odds she's bluffing at 70/30, but I know by 3am I'll think it's 50/50, and I'm fine with that, I'll just go with whatever number I have when 3am comes. She says that's incoherent: Reflection says your credence now should already match what you expect your later credence to be, so if he's certain he'll land on 50/50 he should be at 50/50 already, or else he's holding two views at once. He says no — Reflection only bites if your future self is a decent guide, and he just told her his future self will be shot; he wouldn't defer to a drunk friend's opinion, so why defer to a drunk version of himself? She comes back with the Dutch book: anyone who violates Reflection can be milked with a run of bets, each of which he'll take at the time, leaving him worse off no matter what. He says a Dutch book only shows his betting odds are exploitable, not that his beliefs are wrong, and anyway the guy getting milked is the 3am guy, so it's not obvious it's the same agent getting suckered.

Keep them both sharp — I dont want her to be a mouthpiece or him to be a drunk strawman. Real back and forth, they should answer each other's actual points rather than trade speeches. And let them get into the uncomfortable bits: whether Reflection should be restricted to future selves you trust to stay rational, whether trusting your future credence is the same as deferring to your future self's opinion, and whether the Dutch book argument has any teeth against someone who thinks rationality is a per-time-slice thing. No citations, no reading list, just the argument.
