Ok so I've been going back and forth on testimony for a while and I think I've landed on the anti-reductionist side, and I want to pressure-test it before I get too attached.

The view: testimony is a basic source of justification in its own right, on par with perception and memory. You're entitled to take someone's word for it by default; you don't first have to build up an inductive track record of people being reliable — that seems circular anyway, and it demands way more cognitive legwork than any actual human does. What you need is defeaters: specific reason to doubt this speaker on this occasion.

What I want is a map of what would count against this. Which observations, cases, or arguments should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be, plus an honest read on whether the view has quietly been hedged into something unfalsifiable.

Two things I specifically want you to chew on, since they're whats got me worried:

First, the stock objection is just "what about a community that's systematically wrong but sincere?" Fine, but I think theres a sharper version of it. The anti-reductionist seems to need the teller to be the kind of thing that can take responsibility for what it says — you can ask them to justify, resent them for lying, etc. So what happens when the source of the "testimony" isn't a speaker at all? A summarizer, a scraped dataset, a model that'll confidently assert false things with nobody having lied and nobody to hold accountable. Is that a real hole in the view, or just a new case it absorbs? I honestly can't tell if I'm onto something or confusing two separate issues.

Second, the transmission thing: can someone pass on justification for a belief they don't themselves have justification for? If yes the view looks very permissive; if no, testimony stops looking like a basic source and starts looking like it inherits the teller's epistemic standing after all.

Lay it out in this order, roughly:

- Restate the position (short, so I can check you got it)
- The case against, ranked from decisive to merely awkward
- What an anti-reductionist would concede is a real cost — not what critics say, what they'd actually admit
- Insulation check: has it been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a problem here?
- Your verdict, one paragraph, and what you'd bet on
