In civics we debated whether a democracy can justify keeping a monarch, and one side kept repeating "the monarch reigns but does not rule" as though it settled the matter. That bothers me, because the king still formally has to give assent before any bill becomes law, and nobody in the room seemed sure whether he could ever actually refuse. Could you lay out the different precise claims that slogan might be making — separating the legal, constitutional, and conventional senses of "rule," and saying what each would amount to in practice — and then tell me, for each reading, whether it is a triviality, a substantive and defensible claim, or simply false, and which reading the people who use it in a debate like ours actually need? I would rather have the menu of readings than a verdict on the monarchy itself.
