For my practical reason seminar I'm working on hard choices, and before I settle on a view I want the specification rather than another survey: what must any adequate theory of value comparison deliver once it takes incommensurability seriously? Set out the desiderata. The cases it has to get right: a choice between two options each better in one respect and worse in another; a pair that seems tied in neither's favour, where a tiny improvement to one option would make it plainly better than the other; and a choice that must be made even though reason seems to run out. The distinctions it must not collapse: being incomparable versus being equal versus being roughly equal versus standing in a fourth relation in which the two are comparable yet neither is at least as good as the other, and being better than something versus being at least as good as it. The formal constraints it must respect: transitivity and acyclicity of betterness, and whether rational choice among incomparables is possible at all. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it; then tell me which pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement rules out the most candidate theories. Skip the named authors — the criteria are the deliverable.
