I write about science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about how the reckoning with the Holocaust has become a proxy war over the meaning of modernity. Zygmunt Bauman's influential and unsettling thesis was that the Holocaust was not a relapse into pre-modern barbarism but a product of modernity's own machinery — bureaucratic rationality, the division of labour into ever-smaller tasks, instrumental reason, and the routinized production of moral indifference — all of it aimed at expelling an ambiguous population from a society imagined as a garden to be weeded. His critics answer that this account dissolves the perpetrators' agency, understates the ideological antisemitism that did the work, and treats as continuous with modern life an event that ought to be held incomparable. Write an essay on the controversy: is the Holocaust best understood as continuous with modernity or as a rupture from it, and does that verdict also settle whether it can be compared to other mass atrocities? Weigh the strongest versions of both sides — including the historians' long-running dispute over whether the killers were ordinary men carried along by bureaucratic momentum or committed ideological murderers — and reach a verdict. Open with your verdict in the first sentence, then argue for it, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
