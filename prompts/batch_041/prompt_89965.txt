Write a short story, contemporary and realist — no afterlife, no ghosts, no deathbed conversion — about a woman given a terminal diagnosis who sets out to make her own death cost her as little as possible. Her reasoning is homegrown and only half worked out: I have read that some ancient philosophers, Epicurus among them, held that death is nothing to us because it is a state in which we are not there to be harmed, and she takes the thought a step further on her own. If death is bad only because of what it takes away, then a death that takes away nothing is not bad at all, and so she begins finishing things — the papers, the debts, the decade-old quarrel with her brother, the letter she owes her oldest friend — so that there is nothing left for it to take. I want the story to test the idea rather than illustrate it. Let her find that some of what she stands to lose cannot be finished in advance, because it consists of things she would only have wanted later; and let her notice that whatever she loses, there is no moment at which she is there to suffer the loss. Give her one friend who takes the opposite line — that she will not be around to be deprived of anything, and that her dread is therefore a confusion about her own absence — and let that friend be neither a fool nor plainly right. End in the middle of the argument rather than after it.
