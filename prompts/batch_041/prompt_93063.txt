The students in my upper-level seminar on the norms of assertion will sit a ninety-minute final with no texts, notes, or internet access, so I need an essay question that rewards reasoning about a case rather than recall of who said what. Please write me one essay question, a model answer, and a marking scheme, built on the two cases below. Both cases are my own invention, and both will appear on the paper exactly as written here.

Case one. Mira is the assistant house manager at a large theater. Fifteen minutes before a sold-out performance the fire alarm sounds. It is in fact a false alarm, but no one in the building knows that yet, and the lobby is packed. Mira takes up the microphone and announces, "Everyone will get out of this building unharmed." She believes this. She does not know it: whether the crowd stays calm depends on how it takes her announcement, and about that she has only a well-grounded expectation, drawn from her training and from the way comparable crowds have behaved in drills, that a calm voice will be met with a calm response. What she cannot know is that her announcement is the thing that will save them. Had she said nothing, the crowd would have surged toward the north doors, one of which would have jammed under the crush, and people would have been badly hurt. Because she spoke, the crowd leaves in order and no one is hurt. Her announcement is true, and it is true because she made it.

Case two. In a second theater, on another night, the same sentence is spoken by Tomás, a visiting producer with no relevant experience, who says it aloud on a hunch, with no evidence whatever, mainly to steady his own nerves. The exits in his theater are already clear, the crowd was never in danger, no one is hurt, and his sentence is true for reasons that have nothing to do with his having uttered it. He believes what he says; he does not know it; and what he says is true.

The question should require students to use the pair of cases to adjudicate among the candidate norms — truth, belief, justification, knowledge — and in particular to say whether the norm governing assertion must be epistemic rather than merely alethic, that is, whether a norm that asks only for truth can do the work a norm of assertion is supposed to do. It should also make them say when the norm is to be applied, given that the truth of the first utterance was not settled at the moment of utterance but was brought about by it, and it should make them confront the possibility that neither utterance is, strictly speaking, an assertion at all — that the first is better classified as a prediction, a reassurance, or an exhortation, in which case the norms of assertion may not govern it. I would rather have a demanding question than a tidy one. Standard labels for the competing norms are fine, since the students know them, but the question should not be passable by reciting the textbook examples, and there is no need for authors, titles, citations, or a reading list.

Please give me three things.

First, the question as it will appear on the examination paper: a single essay question in the register of an exam, specifying what the student is to do with the two cases. It should be answerable in roughly ninety minutes and worth 20 marks.

Second, a model answer of about 650 words. It should reach a definite verdict on which norm the cases best support, while marking clearly which parts of the analysis are settled among people who work on this topic and which are live disputes — for instance, whether the knowledge account is genuinely embarrassed by a case in which things turn out well, or whether the truth account is genuinely embarrassed by its inability to distinguish Mira from Tomás, or whether Mira's expectation counts as justification at all. Do not present a contested position as the consensus, and do not let the cases appear to settle more than they do.

Third, a marking scheme built on five must-hit points, totaling 20 marks, each with a one-line note on how candidates characteristically get that point wrong. I will leave the choice of points to you, but the scheme must include a point on the self-fulfilling structure of the first case and its consequences for when the norm is applied, and a point on whether either utterance is an assertion at all. A top answer must do more than sort the two cases into the four accounts; the scheme should say what that more is, and a merely competent answer should be distinguishable from it on the page.
