I am a software engineer with no formal background in theology or philosophy, and I have been working through a lecture course on the historical Jesus — the project of deciding which of the sayings and deeds attributed to him actually go back to him. Rather than take the lecturer's conclusions on trust, I have been treating him as a black box and logging his inputs and outputs, in the hope of reverse-engineering the decision procedure he is running. So far I have recorded the following. In each case he was asked whether a reported saying goes back to Jesus himself.

1. A short aphorism that would have undercut the authority the early church claimed for its own leaders, preserved in two independent sources: judged authentic.
2. A parable whose point is unflattering to the church's later leadership, preserved in more than one independent source: judged authentic.
3. A pronouncement that fits the church's later teaching very comfortably, found in only one source: judged inauthentic.
4. A saying that fits comfortably within the ordinary Jewish piety of the period, found in only one source: judged inauthentic.
5. A saying that is embarrassing to the church, preserved in more than one source, but which presupposes a dispute that most specialists think arose only after Jesus's death: judged inauthentic.

The new case is a saying preserved in three sources usually treated as independent, containing nothing that would have embarrassed the early church — it in fact lends support to a title the church later emphasized — and presupposing nothing later than Jesus's own lifetime.

Please do five things. First, predict the lecturer's verdict on the new case. Second, state the general rule or rules that fit his five earlier verdicts, and show that at least two different rules fit those verdicts equally well. Third, say what each of those rules predicts about the new case. Fourth, give me your best guess at his verdict, together with the reasons for preferring the rule that yields it. Fifth, name the further case that would best tell the surviving rules apart — the case on which his verdict, if I could obtain it, would do the most to discriminate between them. I care far less about who Jesus was than about which procedure the lecturer is actually running and how one would pin it down.
