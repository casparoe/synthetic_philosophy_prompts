I've been circling a piece on the frame problem for months and keep hitting the same wall: everyone from the old AI people to the philosophers to the cognitive scientists tells me it's either trivially solved or deeply unsolved, and I'm now fairly sure they aren't even arguing about the same thing. So before I start interviewing, help me build the spec. What would a theory in this area actually have to deliver to count as a solution -- not to some toy puzzle but to whatever the real problem is? Give me the requirements list: the cases it has to get right (the robot that never bothers to check whether its fuel is running low; the agent that has to know that repainting a wall doesn't move it or make it heavier; the planning system that needs to assume nothing else changes when it acts), the distinctions it must respect rather than collapse (is there really a difference between the metaphysical version, the epistemological one and the computational one, or is that a distinction without a difference?), and the constraints it can't violate -- like, whatever the answer is, it can't require the system to survey every belief against every other belief, because that's the whole intractibility complaint. With the consideration behind each requirement, please, and a mark on which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp.

Then the part I actually care about: which of these are genuine desiderata and which are the prejudices of a particular crowd -- say, the people who think the problem is about representation versus the people who think it's about relevance versus the people who think the whole framing is a mistake. If some subset of the requirements can't be met together, say so plainly, because that's the story. And one thing a colleague keeps pushing on me that I want the spec to settle: he insists the frame problem isn't really about relevance or about what stays the same, it's about how you individuate a change or an action in the first place -- is that the same requirement in different clothes, or a genuinely separate one that belongs on the list? Skip scoring the famous proposals, I'll handle that in the piece. I just want the criteria and the reasons behind each, and which single requirement does the most to rule candidates out.
