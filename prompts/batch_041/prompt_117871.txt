I am trying to find a name for something I keep noticing about how we judge wrong answers, and I would rather have the concept than the label.

Two forecasters both get about 70% of days right. One is wrong in the same direction every time, the other at random. I would call the first closer to being right, and not out of charity: his errors are the kind a better model would remove. The same thing happens with theories. When a false theory's mistakes are exactly the mistakes its successor corrects, nobody hesitates to call it nearer the truth; when two false theories are equally accurate and their errors are scattered, each getting right what the other gets wrong, people grow uncomfortable saying either is nearer the truth, even when the scorecard favours one.

What I seem to be tracking, then, is not how accurate a theory is or how many truths it captures, but whether its errors are repairable — whether they point somewhere. I suspect there is a term for the general question of one false theory being nearer the truth than another, but that is not what I am after. Does the extra ingredient have a name of its own? If it does, say what it commits you to and how it comes apart from accuracy; if it does not, coin one and tell me what it excludes. And tell me whether I am smuggling something in, since being on the right track appears to relate a theory to theories that do not yet exist.
