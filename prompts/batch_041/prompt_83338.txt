ok, I need one more prompt for a dataset I'm putting together, and I've settled on a parody of a corporate compliance-training script — the campaign finance / lobbying kind. I had to sit through one of these years ago (my old employer had a small PAC, long story) and it was genuinely surreal: fifty minutes of deadpan instruction on how to stay technically clean, with the question of whats actually right never coming up once, not even as a joke.

So write me that module. Around 700 words, formatted like a real training doc: numbered sections, an instructor talking to a room of bored new hires, and a short quiz at the end with an answer key. Setting is a mid-size lobbying firm, all names invented, no real people or real clients. It has to be completely deadpan — no narrator leaning in to tell us its messed up, no winking, nothing like a line explaining that this is basically legalized bribery. If a reader can't tell whats being made fun of, that's on me, but never say it out loud.

The comedy has to come from the material being accurate, so be careful with the actual mechanics and don't bluff them: the difference between a campaign contribution and an independent expenditure, and why coordination is the thing that flips one into the other; bundling, and why the firm's real product is access and ingratiation rather than a straight trade; the revolving door and cooling-off periods; and the fact that a direct quid pro quo is more or less the only thing the law calls corruption, which is why the module burns all its energy on the appearance stuff and none on the substance. If your not sure of a case name, a dollar limit, or a statute, don't invent one — just describe the rule in plain words. No citations anywhere please, whoever ends up reading this has no internet.

One more thing: the instructor should be good at his job and honest inside his own frame. He's not a hypocrite and he's not an idiot, and he never once lies to the room. The frame is the joke. It should be funny to someone who's read way too much about this stuff and still readable to someone who hasn't.
