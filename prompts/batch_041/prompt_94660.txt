I've got a long-running argument with a friend that's gotten weirdly specific, and I want your take on it. The evidence: I've bought and sold the same 1980s drum machine three times in two years. I pay about $300 for it, spend two weeks loving it, then become convinced the $300 is worth more to me than the machine, sell it (eating maybe $40 in fees and shipping), and a month later I'm equally convinced the machine is worth more than the $300 and buy it back. Net, I'm out something like $250 and I own exactly what I started with. My friend says that's a money pump in slow motion and that the only difference between me and the textbook case is that I take months instead of minutes. Fine, sure — but then she said two things I can't shake. First, that the pump only shows a sequence of locally good trades can be globally bad, and that no sensible theory of rationality demands global optimality over time, only that you be rational at each moment. Second, that a resolute version of me — one who at the very start just adopts the policy 'never sell this thing' and sticks to it — comes out fine, so the whole argument is really an argument for having policies, not an argument that my preferences are broken. I half-believe her and half-think she's dodging the point, and I can't definately say why.

So skip the history-of-the-debate stuff and give me the implications. If I take the money-pump arguments seriously, what actually follows for me? What follows for how I should think about my own future selves — a series of people to be bound, or one person with a policy? What follows for someone who has to design rules around someone like me (a subscription with cancellation fees, a savings app with lock-ups, a lease, a mutual-aid fund with monthly contributions)? And then the flip side, which I care about more: which of the implications people usually draw from money pumps are overrated or just don't follow? My suspicion is that both 'so you should precommit to everything' and 'so your preferences are literally incoherent' are too fast, but I'd like you to actually rank the implications by how much weight they carry, say what each one needs in order to go through, and be honest about which parts of this are about rationality and which are just about my willpower, transaction fees, and the fact that I'm fickle.
