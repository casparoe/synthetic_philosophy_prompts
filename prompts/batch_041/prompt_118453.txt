So I'm a backend engineer, zero formal philosophy training beyond one ethics elective I mostly slept through, and I've fallen down a rabbit hole on the whole "do artifacts have politics" thing after a long lunch argument with a coworker. Short version of where I landed: I used to figure tech is neutral and it's just people doing good or bad things with it, and now I don't buy that, but I also suspect the famous examples everyone trots out (the low bridges, the tomato-picker, the usual hall of fame) are doing less work than people claim, and the actually interesting stuff is somewhere else entirely. Anyway my company gave me a small pot of money -- roughly a year of one person's time, part-time, split however I want -- to stand up an internal "tech and society" working group, and I have to pitch the group on what that year should be spent thinking about. I'd rather it go at questions that actually matter than at the greatest hits.

So here's what I want from you: what are the real open questions in this area, and which one or two would you put a year of a sharp philosophy-inclined person on? I care way more about the reasoning than the list -- why this one is upstream of the others, whether it's actually tractable for someone thinking rather than doing empirical studies, and what would make me move it up or down the ranking. Concretely, I keep hitting what feels like the same fork from different sides and can't tell which branch is live: is the "politics" of a thing located in its physical form, in its defaults and configuration, in the network of people and practices wrapped around it, or just in whoever happens to own and operate it? A colleague insists the real unit of analysis isn't the artifact at all but the "default trajectory" a system lays down for its users -- the path of least resistance it makes -- and that this dissolves the whole debate. Maybe, but I can't tell if that's a genuine move or just renaming the problem. And then there's the lock-in thing that keeps bugging me: a design decision is cheapest to change exactly when nobody yet knows what it'll do, and by the time you know, it's welded into everyone's workflow and nobody remembers why. Is that a philosophical problem or just ordinary engineering-politics, and does it change what a person should even study?

The other thing I can't get traction on: everybody says a system can "bake in" a value, but I've never seen a clean account of what it means for a chunk of code or a bridge to *do* something, as opposed to the people who build and deploy it. Is that just a category error dressed up, or is there a real sense in which the artifact is carrying the load? And I'm a little worried the whole area just collapses into "capitalism and power" and then there's nothing left to say about the artifact itself -- if that's where it ends up, fine, but then which questions survive the collapse and which are just decoration? Rank the survivors for me, tell me what would change the ranking, and skip the reading list -- I want the unresolved thing, not the syllabus.
