Construct a case that separates weakness of will from time-inconsistent preference, in both directions at once. One agent in the case has preferences that genuinely reverse as the moment of action nears — the pattern a hyperbolic discount function predicts — yet she is not weak-willed: she does what she, on reflection, endorses, and would reject the description. The other agent's choices are perfectly time-consistent, fitting an exponential discounting model, and yet she is weak-willed. The case exists to settle whether weakness of will is merely a species of time-inconsistent preference or an independent phenomenon, so the verdicts must be generated by what the agents do and judge, not stipulated. Constraints: an ordinary domestic or workplace setting with ordinary people; no addiction, no money, and no precommitment devices or other technology for binding one's future self; and none of the textbook illustrations — the mast, the marshmallow, the snooze button. After the scenario, state for each agent the judgment and the action that fix the verdict, identify the single feature that does the separating work, and give the first agent's preferences at the two times to show her reversal is real. Then say what changes if a commitment device is allowed, how someone who holds that weakness of will simply is steep temporal discounting would attack the case, and whether the case survives — and whether what you have shown is genuine independence or only that one notion is a special case of the other.
