Ok, I need a dialogue for the collective action week of my seminar, and I want it to be a real fight rather than two people reading out their positions. Two characters, both sharp, both looking at the same structure: several people, each of whom can act alone, on the same question, without knowing what the others are going to do — and acting is good if the world is one way and bad if it's the other.

Call them R and A. R is running the restraint line, the thing people call the unilateralist's curse, and I want you to actually spell the arguement out instead of gesturing at it: if each of them is, say, 60% likely to be getting it right, then the more of them there are the closer it gets to certain that at least one of them acts on a false judgment. So a sensible person in that setup should hold back unless they have some positive reason to think they're in a special position, and thinking it's a good idea yourself doesnt count as that reason.

A runs the mirror image, and this is the part I actually care about. Do the same arithmetic on the restraint side and you get a curse of not acting: every time you hold back you're betting that nobody else is right, and if the group's judgment is biased away from the truth — which is arguably the normal case, it's why we bother having private judgments at all — then the unilateral action is exactly what corrects it. So A shouldn't just shrug; A should try to make R's own argument eat itself.

Then the whole dialogue has to turn on whether R can break that symmetry without begging the question. Let R try the good moves — asymmetric payoffs, correlated errors, the thought that the threshold for acting isn't 50% anyway — and let A push back on those too. Neither one is a strawman: A lands real hits, R has real replies, and they should be answering each other's actual last point rather than talking past each other. No settling it by citing who said what either, I want the argument, not the bibliography.

Settle on one boring concrete case early and stay with it (a lab meeting, a group chat, a shift change at a hospital, whatever — just not a doomsday device), so we can see what the two of them actually think should happen. Finish with one line saying what the real crux between them is.
