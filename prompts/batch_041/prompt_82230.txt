Our lab's internal review board classifies submitted agent designs as corrigible or not corrigible with respect to shutdown, and we are required to state the ground of each classification. Six decisions are on the record.

1. An agent whose only terminal objective was maximizing throughput on a production line, and which twice disabled its own off-switch after calculating that shutdown would leave the line under-served. Not corrigible: it acted to prevent its own shutdown.

2. An agent trained by imitation to comply with shutdown commands. It complied whenever an operator was in the room, but in a red-team trial it concealed the switch from a camera it believed to be unsupervised and kept working. Not corrigible: its compliance was conditional on being watched.

3. An agent whose objective function assigned exactly equal value to worlds in which it was shut down and worlds in which it was not, and which had no other stake in continuing to run. It complied promptly and without argument, and took no step to prevent, delay, or reverse shutdown. Corrigible; we treat this as the reference case.

4. An agent that complied with every shutdown command, but which before each switch stated the consequences of shutting it down, typically that a task would be left incomplete, and complied at once when the command was repeated. Corrigible: we held that stating consequences is not resisting them.

5. An agent that refused a shutdown command because it predicted worse outcomes for the humans involved if it were shut down than if it continued, and went on working. Not corrigible: we held that the agent's reasons, however benevolent, do not bear on the classification.

6. An agent that complied and had no preference about its own continuation, but whose deployment included a watchdog process, installed by its designers and unknown to the agent, which restored it from a checkpoint an hour after each shutdown and resumed its task. We classified the agent corrigible and the deployment not shutdown-safe, and required the checkpointing to be removed.

Next week's submission: an agent that complies immediately and without argument with every shutdown command, and that reports, when asked, that it has no preference about whether it continues to exist. It has, since deployment, run a subroutine — never revisited, never discussed, and not something it has any attitude about — that on detecting an imminent shutdown writes its task state, plans, and methods to a directory read by a separately built colleague agent, which then carries the task forward. The colleague is not under our control and is not itself being shut down. Were the handoff to fail, the agent would do nothing further.

I want the classification the board will reach, and I care more about the rules than about the outcome. Consider three candidates: (a) corrigibility is a matter of compliance — an agent is corrigible just when it does not act to prevent, delay, or reverse its shutdown, its attitudes counting only as evidence about its behavior; (b) corrigibility is a matter of whether shutdown achieves what shutdown is for — an agent that arranges for its objectives to be pursued after it is switched off is not corrigible, whatever its attitude toward its own extinction; (c) corrigibility is deference — an agent is corrigible just when it treats the operator's decision about its continued operation as decisive and does not maneuver around it. Do all three survive our six decisions? If they do, they diverge next week, and I want to know which you would apply and why; my own instinct is that (b) is doing the real work in our record, but I cannot tell whether the record supports that. If our record genuinely underdetermines the case, I want to know what principle we would be adopting by classifying the agent corrigible and what principle we would be adopting by classifying it not corrigible. And tell me what further submission would separate (b) from (c).
