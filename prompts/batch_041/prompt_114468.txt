Right, so I'm a particularist — holism about reasons, no general principles of right action, the whole thing — and my supervisor is a generalist who's been chewing on me all term. Last week he handed me this chain, said "break it or drop it", and I can't do either, so I'm outsourcing it to you.

Here's his argument. (1) According to particularism, whether a feature counts as a reason for something, and how much it counts, depends on the rest of the case. (2) So no feature is a reason for anything in virtue of itself. (3) So whatever reason-giving force a feature has must be being conferred on it by the other features in the case. (4) Those features get their force from somewhere too, i.e. from yet further features, and so on. (5) Either that regress never bottoms out, in which case nothing in any actual case ever really favours anything, or it bottoms out in a feature that carries its own force, which contradicts (1). (6) Either way, I can never say of any feature in any case that it is a reason, and a view that can't pick out reasons can't guide anybody. (7) So particularism proves too much and I should give it up.

I'd rather die than give up (1), and I'd like to keep (2) as well — isn't "nothing is a reason in virtue of itself" just holism restated? — but I've a horrible feeling I can't have both, and I can't work out weather the trick is in (2) itself or in the slide that comes after it. For what it's worth I accept holism at both strengths: the strong one, where a feature can flip from counting for to counting against as the context changes, and the weak one, where only how much it counts moves around.
