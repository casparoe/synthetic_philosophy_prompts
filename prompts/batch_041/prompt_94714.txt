ok so i have to run the individualism vs holism session at my social theory reading group next week and rather than prep a talk i want to just put my cards on the table and have you tell me whether the hand actually holds together. here's roughly where ive landed:

(1) ontologically im an individualist. states, classes, firms, "the market" — these are patterns in what people do, not agents sitting over and above them. i dont think there's any social furniture that floats free of facts about individuals.

(2) but im a pluralist about explanation. comparative-historical work that picks out a macro regularity and treats it as a cause — state capacity, elite overproduction, the demographic transition, take your pick — is doing real explanatory work, and i dont think it has to be rewritten in individual terms to be legitimate. my line is that the macro claim supervenes on the individual facts but doesnt have to be derivable from a tractable description of them, and i think those are two different claims.

(3) still i hold the strong normative line: an explanation of a social pattern isnt finished until you can tell me a mechanism — who wanted what, what they believed, what they could actually do, and how their interaction added up to the pattern. the macro-micro-macro picture (the coleman boat, i think its called?) is the right template and i get suspicious of anything that never comes back down.

(4) except i dont think the mechanism is usually rational choice. habit, imitation, emotion and norm-following do most of the work, and a lot of what gets called rational action is the analysts description of the situation rather than anything in the agent.

(5) i think institutions enter explanations as constraints on what individuals can do, and i also think that if youre serious about (1) you cant let them sit there as brute givens — theyre equilibria, or at least products, of individual action, and any story that treats them as exogenously fixed is incomplete by (3)'s standard.

(6) i think social kinds are real but regime-bounded. "peasant", "credit default swap", "the nuclear family" are constituted by practices that shift, and when the practices shift the kinds causal profile shifts with them, so you shouldnt expect the same regularity to survive the break.

(7) and i think there's a genuine difference between explaining an event and explaining a rate. durkheim-style rate explanations dont owe me a story about any particular suicide.

i suspect at least one of these is quietly eating another one and i honestly cant tell which. so: rank the tensions by how bad they are, tell me which pairs only look like they clash and why they dont, and for the ones that are real say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. if two of them cant both stay, tell me which one youd drop instead of hedging. and keep straight which parts of this are settled among people who actually work on it versus which are live fights — i dont want a manufactured consensus, and i dont want my own idiosyncratic bits presented as if everyone agrees.
