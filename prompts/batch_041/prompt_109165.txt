For a departmental work-in-progress session I'm the discussant for a visiting researcher who defends factored cognition as an idealization of deliberation: on her account any episode of reasoning can be modeled as a tree of small subtasks dispatched independently, and the right formal target is HCH -- the limit case of a deliberator who answers a question by splitting it into subquestions and handing each piece to a copy of herself. She adds a twist of her own: she treats independence not as a property of tasks but as a cost, holding that decomposition is always available at a price she calls "context rent," the amount of shared state the subagents have to carry, so that HCH is just the zero-rent limit.

Draft eight to ten questions for the discussion. I don't need warm-up questions, citations, or reading lists. I want at least one question each on: whether the HCH limit is well-defined or quietly circular; what "context rent" does with the objection that real reasoning is constrained globally, by sub-answers that are mutually informative or that have to hang together as a whole; whether factoring solves hard problems or only relocates them into the posing and checking of subquestions; and what the idealization idealizes -- an amplified human with her biases, or rational deliberation as such. Attach to each question a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and end with a question that forces her to say what her idealization has to be false about in order to be useful. Alongside the questions, flag which claims are settled among people who work on this and which are genuinely contested, and don't present a contested reading -- that the limit is well-defined, say, or that human deliberation is factorable in practice -- as though it were the consensus.
