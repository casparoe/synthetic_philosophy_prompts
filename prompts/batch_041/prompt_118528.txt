You are a value theorist specializing in the commensurability of pleasure and pain and in lexical priority views. I am drafting an adequacy specification for a family of views I will call lexical threshold negative utilitarianism (LTNU). On LTNU, pains and pleasures below some threshold are commensurable and trade off on a single scale, but a pain above the threshold—agony, say—is lexically prior: no quantity of sub-threshold pleasure can outweigh it. Before I evaluate any particular version, I want the desiderata. Think carefully and begin with a short brainstorm of the cases and distinctions that put pressure on such a view.

Then give the requirements list. For each requirement, state it, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it non-negotiable or version-specific. Be sure to cover: the location and sharpness of the threshold, and whether it can be specified without already assuming the trade-offs it is meant to govern; the comparability of intensity and duration (a long mild pain versus a short agony); the non-independence of pleasure and pain (relief can be pleasurable, and a single state can mix both); whether the threshold is global, person-relative, or baseline-relative; whether sub-threshold pains can aggregate to cross it; and whether the view collapses into prioritarianism or into unthresholded lexicalism. Also consider threshold hysteresis: the threshold for lexical protection may sit at a different point depending on whether one is entering pain from pleasure or leaving pain for pleasure, like a thermostat's separate on and off points. Must an adequate theory accommodate that, or is it an empirical detail it may ignore?

I especially want any pair or subset of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied; if the list generates an impossibility result, say so and say which requirement must give. Keep the scoring of existing positions brief—the criteria are the deliverable—and finish with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing the one requirement you judge most exclusionary.
