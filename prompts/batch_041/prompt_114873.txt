My friends and I have a running argument that started as a joke at dinner and has now eaten four or five of them. Here's the setup we keep fighting about. You get a degenerative brain disease and the only treatment is a slow swap: every few weeks a small chunk of brain tissue comes out and a chip goes in that does exactly what that chunk did — same inputs, same outputs, no gaps, no downtime. The company that does it is very proud of the details. You're never unconscious. You never lose a memory or a skill or a mannerism. At no point are there two of you. Ten years later you're all silicon and you've been awake and continuous the whole time. Whether the person who finishes is the person who started is something I keep changing my mind about, which I take as a sign I should stop arguing and write it as a story instead.

So write me a short story in that world, 1500-2000 words. I want it from inside the middle of the procedure — a woman in her mid fifties, roughly 70% converted, who runs something small and unglamorous that is entirely hers, a garden centre or a two-person tax office. The thing I don't want it to turn on is "she wonders whether she's still herself," because I've definately read that one. I want it to turn on consent over time, and on a wrinkle that makes the consent problem actually bite: suppose the chips aren't neutral. Nothing dramatic, no mind control — just a slow statistical drift, so that converted people come out a little more cautious, a little more deferential to the company's own guidance, and nobody can feel it happening from the inside. Now the 20% version of her signed the forms, and the 70% version has started to think the forms shouldn't bind her; and the 70% version is the one who'd have to live with the rest of it, except that her wanting to finish might be exactly the thing the procedure did to her, and she has no way to check that from in there. Her adult daughter won't visit until it's over — she says she doesn't want to get attached to the in-between one, and she isn't being cruel, she just can't do it. The chips are also leased, so a growing fraction of her is subject to an agreement the company can revise whenever it likes.

Things I'd rather not get: a reveal that she was a copy all along, a technician who sits her down and explains the philosophy, or an ending where the ambiguity is just a mood. I want the concept doing work in the plot. So I'd like a scene where she has to decide something irreversible, and the decision only makes sense if you take seriously that the 20% her and the 70% her are two parties with mostly overlapping interests and an unsettled claim between them — and by the end I should be genuinely unable to say whether the thing she does is her will continuing or the first thing the new thing wants.
