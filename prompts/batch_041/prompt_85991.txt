I have been persuaded by a line of thought in cooperative game theory that I now want to stress-test, because I suspect I have adopted it for the wrong reasons and then extended it well past what it supports. The idea is what is sometimes called a safe Pareto improvement: a modification of a game — a change in what the players are instructed to do, or in what they commit to — that leaves every player better off with certainty, where "with certainty" means the improvement is guaranteed by qualitative assumptions about how related games are played (that isomorphic games are played isomorphically, that strictly dominated strategies get eliminated, and so on) rather than by any probabilistic beliefs about what the other side will do. The motivating case is the mixed-motive coordination problem: both parties would gain by shifting off a bad default, but neither can be sure the other will follow, so the only moves that are safe are those that cannot be punished by the other's response.

What I want to examine is a further step I have taken, which I doubt the original idea licenses. I have come to treat safety as the criterion of legitimacy for cooperative proposals, not merely as a prudent constraint on how a delegate should behave. On my view, a proposal is one that the other party may legitimately reject unless it is a safe improvement — a guaranteed improvement across the relevant class of related games — rather than an improvement in the game as I happen to have written it down. The thought behind this is that a proposal that pays off only under my description of the situation exploits a representational choice rather than offering a genuine gain, and that this is what is really objectionable about hard bargaining.

Tell me what would count against this, ranked by how decisive it would be, and tell me which of the objections a committed defender would themselves concede rather than dismissing as a misunderstanding. In particular, I want to know whether the notion is vacuous: under plausible assumptions about which games count as related and how they correspond, is the set of safe improvements ever empty, or does it shrink to outcomes that dominate in every related game, which are either rare or trivial? I want to know what is doing the work. Safety is relative to assumptions about how different games correspond; who fixes those assumptions, and on what grounds? If each party can supply its own correspondence assumptions, safety starts to look like a veto that anyone can exercise against any deal by invoking a related game in which they do worse, and if there is no principled way to fix the assumptions, I want to know whether the view quietly collapses into something more familiar — maximin over a set of models, or ambiguity-averse choice, or plain Pareto improvement under a chosen prior.

I also want the modal force of the criterion examined. Better off with certainty and better off with probability 0.98 come apart precisely where cooperation is hardest, so is there a real argument that certainty is the right bar, or is it an artefact of not wanting to assign beliefs? I want to know whether the criterion condemns deals that are obviously right — if the intuitively fair outcome in a standard bargaining problem turns out to be unsafe, or if the guarantee the view delivers is weak, bounded by something like the worst payoff a player can obtain in any efficient outcome, that seems to me a serious strike. And I want an honest verdict on whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability, by adding correspondence assumptions whenever a counterexample appears; if it has, say so, and say whether that is a problem for a view of this kind or merely the price of a formal criterion.

Finally: what does the view predict that its rivals do not, and what has arguably already happened that bears on it — including anything about how hard it is to determine whether a safe improvement exists in the first place, since a criterion that cannot be applied cannot serve as a criterion of legitimacy. I would personally find most persuasive either a proof that safety and non-vacuity cannot be had together for any class of related games rich enough to matter, or a case in which refusing to act without a guarantee is itself what forfeits the gain both sides wanted. But do not confine yourself to the objections I have listed; if there is a fatal one I have not thought of, lead with it.
