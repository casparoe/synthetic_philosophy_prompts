I've been chewing on this idea of a free floating rationale — the thought that a bit of behaviour can have a reason sitting behind it even when nothing doing the behaving actually holds that reason. A flower doesn't want the bee to come, a cuckoo chick doesn't know its crowding out its step-siblings, but you can still explain what they do by the reason and predict with it. The reason is real, there's just nobody home holding it.

The case I keep coming back to is Clever Hans, the horse in Berlin in the early 1900s. His owner, a maths teacher, taught him to add and subtract and tell the time by tapping his hoof, it got so famous that a commission of thirteen people looked into it and announced there was no trickery, and then a psychologist called Pfungst took over and showed the horse was reading tiny involuntary movements off whoever was asking — the questioner's posture tightening as the taps got near the right number and relaxing once it got there. I know that story, I'm not asking you to tell it back to me.

What I want is the case put through the free-floating rationale idea properly, running both directions. Whose rationale was the arithmetic one, and was there ever anything in the horse that held it? Then the part that nags at me: the real explanation, the cue-reading, is itself a rationale, and it doesn't seem to sit inside the horse or inside the questioner either — it sits in the two of them together, since neither was aware of it. Does that make the pair the reasoner? And does the episode actually support the free-floating idea, or does it show the idea is too loose, since the rationale we'd naturally write down (Hans is doing sums) turns out to be the one doing no work at all? Say honestly where the idea goes quiet rather than forcing it to cover everything.

Start with a couple of sentences on what you think the case settles, if anything, then the analysis, then a table: one column for the piece of the free-floating rationale idea, one for what it predicts about Hans, one for what actually happened, one for the verdict (holds up / breaks / theory says nothing).
