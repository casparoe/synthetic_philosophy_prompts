I am a novelist, and for several years I have been rewriting a single scene without ever being able to say what is happening in it. The scene runs roughly as follows. One person wants to be acknowledged — not praised for anything in particular, but treated as someone who counts — by another person whose acknowledgment they cannot bring themselves to ask for, because asking spoils it: whatever is granted on request will be read as compliance rather than regard, and so will not count. The second person understands this perfectly. They therefore never volunteer the thing, and they wait, and they are careful never to appear to have heard that it was wanted, so that on the rare occasions when they do say something generous it arrives looking unforced and unforeseeable. Both parties sustain the pretense, and both have an interest in sustaining it, since the pretense is the only thing that keeps the acknowledgment possible at all. Meanwhile the first party has grown expert at distinguishing the unforced from the merely delayed, and that expertise is self-defeating: the finer their ear, the less any particular instance convinces them. The condition that makes the thing worth having — that it be freely given — turns out to be the same condition that makes it deniable and indefinitely withholdable, and the party who withholds can do so under the appearance of decency rather than coldness. I do not think this is simply cruelty on one side and neediness on the other; it is a structure that two people occupy together, and I meet it in families, in marriages, in offices, and in the relations between an artist and an audience.

My question is whether this has a name. I do not mean a name for the general wish to be approved of; I mean this particular knot, in which the acknowledgment must be unforced in order to count, cannot therefore be requested, and is for that very reason available to be withheld forever. If there is an established term, I want to know which neighbouring ideas it is nearest to and exactly where it diverges from each. I can see that it resembles what I have read about states one cannot reach by aiming at them, and about gifts that cease to be gifts when demanded, and about demands that cannot be met because the demand itself forbids meeting them; but none of these seems quite to fit, because what is wanted here is not a feeling or a thing but an acknowledgment that one counts, and because the deadlock is maintained jointly rather than imposed by one party on the other. If no term exists, I would rather have a definition than a label: what the structure precisely is, what it is not — how it differs from plain withholding, from punishment, and from the case in which the seeker has simply stopped wanting it — and where its boundary cases lie.
