I am assembling a set of materials on the rhetoric of historical recurrence, and I keep meeting the one-liner that history repeats itself. I first met it in a seminar on Ibn Khaldun, where a colleague used it to argue that the present weakening of constitutional norms is simply another instance of a cycle of decline, and later in a discussion of whether liberal democracy marks a terminus, where it was used to deny that any political form is final. I do not want a survey of cyclical versus linear views of history. I want the logical geography of the recurrence claim itself, laid out as a menu of distinct precise theses, from the trivial to the radical, with a verdict on each.

Please distinguish at least the following readings, and add any others you think the slogan can bear: recurrence of event-types under a description; recurrence of causal mechanisms or structural constraints; recurrence of narrative or rhetorical form, where the similarity is imposed by the historian rather than found in events; statistical periodicity at a specified scale; recurrence of the same token events or souls, as in Stoic or Nietzschean eternal recurrence; the claim that historical time is literally circular, so that the future is earlier than the past; and the deflationary claim that human nature is constant, so that similar circumstances produce similar outcomes. For each, state the thesis precisely, identify the unit that is supposed to repeat, say whether the reading is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and indicate what would settle it. I am especially interested in two further questions: whether the slogan is best understood as a proposition about the world or as an inference ticket whose content is exhausted by the explanations it licenses; and whether any reading can do its work without either a substantive metaphysics of time or a controversial theory of historical explanation.

Then say which of these readings would actually support the two uses I encountered—the use to dismiss the present as unremarkable, and the use to deny finality to any political form—and which readings would not. Finally, identify which reading the people who repeat the one-liner most likely intend, which reading is most often confused with it, and whether the slogan has a defensible minimal core that survives the collapse of the grand cycles. Please present the result as a numbered menu with verdicts, not as continuous prose. No citations are required; I am interested only in the conceptual distinctions.
