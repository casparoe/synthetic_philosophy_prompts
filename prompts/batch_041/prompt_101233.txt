I am a Year 12 student writing a poem for our school’s philosophy magazine. The theme this term is unintended consequences, and I want to write about the problem of defining side effects for a low-impact artificial intelligence. The idea is that an AI instructed to avoid side effects must compare the world as it would be after acting with some baseline; but any action can be described either as the main effect or as a side effect depending on what one takes the task to be, and without a full specification of value there seems to be no non-arbitrary baseline. I would like a villanelle, in the voice of an AI trying to obey such an instruction. Let the two refrains be the two horns of the dilemma: one should express the thought that the AI must leave the world as it found it, and the other should express the thought that it was asked to change the world, so that the refrains keep returning and never resolve. The form should make the reader feel the oscillation: every attempt to avoid a side effect either does nothing at all or smuggles in a value judgement about what counts as the task. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the AI safety literature on side effects would recognise the problem, but it should not just name-drop. Please keep it to the standard villanelle shape, and make the final quatrain leave the tension open rather than settling it.
