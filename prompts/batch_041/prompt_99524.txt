I'm TAing a 300-level formal epistemology course while also doing some safety-eval work at a lab, so the final question on the lottery and preface paradoxes felt weirdly relevant. I have to grade this student's answer and I'm not totally sure how to grade it, and I definately don't want to just eyeball it. The question was:

Are the lottery paradox and the preface paradox structurally the same? State each, say whether a solution to one automatically solves the other, and then apply your answer to this case: a safety team runs 1000 independent tests; each test passes with probability 0.999; the team believes the model is safe, but also believes, because of known blind spots, that at least one test is not a reliable indicator of safety. Is this a lottery-style or preface-style paradox, or both?

The student wrote:

The two have the same structure. In the lottery, for each of 1000 tickets you're justified in believing it will lose, since it has a 0.999 chance of losing. But you also believe some ticket wins. In the preface, the author believes each claim is true and also believes at least one is false. Same thing: each claim is probable, the conjunction is improbable. The only real difference is that the lottery uses objective probabilities and the preface uses subjective ones. So any solution to the lottery also solves the preface. If you reject closure under conjunction, both vanish. If you require probability 1 for belief, both vanish, but that's too strong. So the only options are to give up closure or accept inconsistency. In the safety case, each test passing is like each ticket losing: you believe each test is reliable, but you also believe at least one is not. That's a preface-style paradox, not a lottery-style one, because there's no known logical guarantee that one test fails. The team should either drop the belief that at least one test is unreliable or accept that rational belief can be inconsistent. The lottery paradox is really about knowledge, not belief, since you can't know your ticket loses even if it's highly probable.
