I'm on an MA module about power and the body, and next week's seminar is one of those "interrogate the theorist" things where someone plays the thinker and the rest of us try to catch them out. I've been lumbered with seting up my group's version, so I want to rehearse it before I make a fool of myself in front of everyone.

Please play Foucault — specifically the mid-70s, Discipline and Punish Foucault, not the later stuff — and you may only use the conceptual kit you had around then, plus whatever I tell you about the case below. Stay in character until I say stop. Talk like a person rather than a lecture (a bit gnomic and sly is fine, that's the vibe), and don't give up your main position cheaply: you think power is productive rather than repressive, that it works through the small stuff — the gaze, the exam, the timetable, normalisation — and you won't accept that just because nobody's being chained up, no power is being exercised. If I land a real hit, admit it, but make me work for it.

Now the case. A cousin of mine works for a firm that's rolled out software on every laptop: it logs keystrokes, counts your "active" minutes, grabs a webcam snapshot every few minutes and runs it through a model that guesses whether you look "engaged", and posts everyone's score on a leaderboard that resets every Monday. It's entirely opt-in — the top scorers get a bonus and the weekly "focus champion" gets the good parking space — and, honestly, alot of the staff seem to love it; they compare scores over lunch like it's a game. So here's my opening move, and I want you to take it seriously: you always said the panopticon works because the tower is there whether or not anyone's actually watching, and the inmate disciplines himself under the mere possibility of being seen. But here there's no tower, no walls and no inmate — the workers volunteer, they watch each other, and they cheer when they climb the rankings. So either your model has finally been falsified, or it's become so true that we now police ourselves for fun, and I genuinely can't tell which answer you're going to give. Which is it, and what does it cost you?
