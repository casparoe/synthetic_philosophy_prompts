My best friend Ruth died a few months ago — it was slow, we all saw it coming. Back when she was still lucid she asked me to promise her something: that after she was gone I'd keep an eye on her younger brother. He's had a rough life, no other family, and honestly he's a lot to handle. I said yes.

Now she's gone and I really dont want to do it. I've got my own kids, my own mess. And here's the thing that's been chewing on me. Ruth was completely clear-eyed about why she wanted a promise and not just a request. She said she was "borrowing my future self" — that she wanted the version of me standing there agreeing, before the version of me who'd actually have to do the work got a vote. She was adamant I shouldn't do it out of guilt, and that I shouldn't feel her watching over my shoulder. And she made me promise not to tell her brother that I'd promised — so he doesn't know, he never expected anything from me, and there's literally nobody left who can be disappointed, or angry, or hold me to it.

My husband thinks this is easy: a promise to a dead person, made on the condition that you don't feel watched, is just a feeling, and feelings fade, so do whatever you want. My gut says he's wrong but I can't articulate why. And when I ask around I get answers that don't fit together — one friend says it's about what I owe her brother, another says it's about being the kind of person who keeps promises, and those feel like totally different answers to totally different questions.

So what's actually supposed to be doing the work here? Once you strip away everyone who could enforce it or be let down, is there anything left of the promise? And if there is, what is it — and does it survive the fact that she deliberately tried to design the guilt out of it? I'm not looking for "a promise is a promise" or generic keep-your-word stuff, I want to understand what's really binding me, if anything is.
