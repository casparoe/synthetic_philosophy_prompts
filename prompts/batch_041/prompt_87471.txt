Quick background so this makes sense: I'm on the committee that runs our town's hardship fund. It's a small thing -- every household pays in a few pounds a year and the fund pays out to people who can't work because they've got hurt or sick. Nobody on the committee has any training for this, we're just neighbours, and we vote.

We've got three claims for next Tuesday and I have to circulate my written opinion before then, so I want to get it straight first.

Our rulebook was written in the seventies and it says we pay for 'losses a member could not have foreseen and avoided.' Nobody has ever defined that phrase. Here's what's in front of us:

1. A woman with a hip condition that runs in her family. Nobody knew she had it till it went. Clear enough -- we pay.

2. A man who broke his back riding a motorbike round the bypass with no helmet. He knew the risk, everyone knows the risk. Half the committee wants to refuse him on the grounds that he foresaw it and could have avoided it.

3. This is the one tying me in knots. A man in his fifties with a bad heart that his father and his brother both died of. He knew about it. His doctor told him to stop the fried food and the cigarettes and he carried on for years, and then came the heart attack that's put him out of work. So the loss was partly the hand he was dealt and partly what he did with it, and I can't see how to seperate the two. Whatever we decide for 1 and 2, case 3 doesn't fit either box.

There's a fourth thing nagging at me too, not on the list but it'll come up. Case 2's man could have bought private insurance and chose not to. Another claimant we had last year couldn't have bought it because she hadn't got the money. Is there really a difference between choosing to go without cover and never being able to afford it? I think there might be, but I can't say why it should matter.

What I want from you is the written opinion I'll read out at the meeting: a clear recommendation for each of the three cases, a time frame for when we tell them, and an honest go at the best argument for whichever side I'm coming down against. Don't just tell me what the rulebook says, I can read the rulebook.

And be straight with me about where my reasoning is most likely to be wrong, and what would actually change your mind -- what would have to be true about case 3, or about the insurance question, for you to come down the other way.
