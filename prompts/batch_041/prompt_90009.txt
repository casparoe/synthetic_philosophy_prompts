I clerk for the constitutional court of a country whose bill of rights was drafted by people who'd read both the First Amendment and Article 10 of the European Convention, and our speech case law has never settled into either camp. You're a comparative constitutional lawyer who's spent years on hate-speech doctrine, so put on that hat. Think carefully and start by brainstorming the rules that could be running through our decisions before you commit to any one of them.

Here's the pattern so far. We quashed the conviction of a satirical magazine prosecuted over a cartoon mocking a religious figure. We quashed the fine on a columnist who described the majority church as a medieval relic holding the country back (the gist of the offending line, not a quote). We upheld a ban on a far-right march through a minority neighbourhood, banners calling for that minority's expulsion, three weeks after an arson attack on the community centre there. We upheld the conviction of a man who leafleted a housing estate naming eight minority families as vermin and printing their street addresses, during a spell when those families' windows were being smashed. And last term we struck down the interior ministry's ban on an anti-clerical demo outside a cathedral, where the ministry's whole case was that believers would be deeply offended and that counter-demonstrators might kick off.

Now next month's case. A small party is being prosecuted over a pamphlet, distributed in a quiet university town, describing a small religious minority as a slow poison in the body of the nation whose customs are filthy, and urging readers to refuse them service and make their neighbourhoods unwelcoming. No addresses, no named individuals, no call to violence, and nothing much has happend in that town for years, though the minority in question is small, historically persecuted, and recently growing.

What will the panel do, and what would you advise them to do if they want to stay consistent with themselves? Give me the two or three rules the earlier cases are jointly consistent with, what each one predicts here, which of them you reckon is actually driving the court and why, and the case that would seperate them. Finally, tell me wether an American court and a European one applying Article 10 would split on this, or land in the same place for different reasons. British English, please.
