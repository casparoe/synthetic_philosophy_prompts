I am a second-year philosophy student, and the seminar I am taking this term is on perception; the running dispute in the readings has been over how much of seeing is finished before thinking begins, and I had been feeling reasonably on top of it until an argument with my friend Nora on a hike last weekend. We were walking back to the trailhead with full packs, hers considerably heavier than mine, and she kept insisting that the final climb looked steeper than it had that morning. I said she was feeling the weight and drawing the obvious inference, that the hill looked to me exactly as it had before, and she became quite adamant that she was not inferring anything: it looked steeper, in the same way that two lines with arrowheads at their ends still look unequal to her after she has measured them with a ruler and knows perfectly well that they are not. I thought that was decisive against her — knowing the lines are equal does nothing to how they look, so why should knowing her pack is heavy change how the hill looks? She came back with her music theory class, where she says she has begun to hear chords differently, that a dominant seventh now sounds as though it is leaning somewhere rather than merely sounding pleasant, and I said that was her attention and her new vocabulary rather than her ears. We left it there and it has become a running joke in the seminar, but I would like to know how to handle the disagreement properly rather than score points off her.

What I want is not so much a verdict as advice on how to conduct this concretely. First, how should I put the question to her? I keep wanting to ask whether she means that the hill looks steeper or merely that she thinks it is steeper, and I can already hear her replying that in her case those come to the same thing, so I would like to know whether that distinction is one I can legitimately ask her to answer, or whether it is precisely what is in dispute and I am begging the question. Second, we have a lab session with our instructor's blessing in two weeks — an hour, a tilting board, a set of weights and a tape measure — and I have been sketching something along these lines: have her set the board to match the slope of a real ramp while carrying different loads, once with the board in view and once with her hand out of sight under a cloth, and compare that with what she says about the slope in words. I have also read something about hills looking steeper to people wearing heavy backpacks, together with the complaint that this is really about how people judge or report rather than about how they see, and the reply that the effect turns up even when people have to act on what they see rather than talk about it. I do not know which of those measures to trust, whether our own version would tell us anything, or what result would count as settling the matter; I would like you to tell me what each possible outcome would license and what it would leave untouched.

Third — and this is the part I care about most — I am writing a term paper on this, and I would rather not write something that simply declares a side. Given all of the above, where should I concede to Nora, where should I hold the line, and what exactly should the paper be arguing about? If the real difficulty is not whether the pack changes what she sees but something about what we are entitled to conclude from any test of it, I would rather be told that directly than be handed a tidy answer to the question I think I am asking.
