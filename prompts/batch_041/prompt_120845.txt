Bit of a strange one, and I'm not sure it's even a real question, but here goes.

I've got a bit obsessed with truth commissions — South Africa, Argentina, Rwanda, a few of the smaller ones — ever since my dad told me about being in Chile in the seventies. I've been reading alot about these processes, and there's a shape to them that I keep noticing and dont have a word for.

The pitch is always the same. You can have the truth or you can have punishment, pick one, because the people who know what happened won't talk if talking puts them in prison. Fine. But that isn't what actually seems to happen. What happens is that the amnesty is formally nothing — no cell, no fine, no loss of anything the law recognises — and yet for some of the people who sit in that chair and say what they did, it destroys them. Not because anyone sentences them. Because the record is there, and it stays there. A colonel's name in the report costs him his pension, his club, his grandchildren's opinion of him. The name of the boy who drove the van costs him, as far as I can tell, nothing at all. He wasn't anybody before and he isn't anybody now.

So you end up with a punishment that's completely real but isn't in any statute, isn't handed down by any judge, never expires, and is scaled to how much the person had to lose rather than to what they did. Nobody in the room chose that. It just falls out of the fact that a reputation is the sort of thing you can only lose if you had one. And you can't fix it without breaking the whole thing: drop the publicity and there's no process left, start ranking people by how much they stand to lose and you're doing something nobody signed up for.

What I want to know is whether there's a name for that specific thing. Not for the commissions, I know what those are called, and not for amnesty either. For the punishment itself — unofficial, permanent, nobody's enforcing it, and proportional to the offender's standing rather than to his offence. I keep reaching for 'shaming' and it's wrong, because shaming means somebody is trying to humiliate you, and here nobody is; the humiliation is a side effect of bookkeeping. It's wrong in the other direction too, because shaming sounds like it lands on everyone the same, and the whole point of what I'm describing is that it doesn't. The other thing I keep thinking of is a criminal record, but that's the state doing it deliberately and it mostly bites later on, by shutting doors, whereas this is just a permanent fact about you that anyone can look up, with the shutting of doors done by everybody else.

Is there an established concept for this — in criminology, or philosophy of punishment, or somewhere in the transitional justice literature? If there is, tell me what it is and how it differs from the things I've just mentioned, because I can't tell whether I've found a real category or just described humiliation in a long-winded way. And if there isn't, name it and define it properly: what it is, what it isn't, and what you'd say about the borderline cases — the man whose name is in the report but on a page nobody reads, the one who was already notorious for something else, the one who asks to testify because he'd rather be somebody than nobody.
