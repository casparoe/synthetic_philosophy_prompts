A few months ago my flatmate got me into all this wild animal suffering stuff — the idea that we should use biotech to abolish pain in nature — and somehow Ive ended up on the grant committee of a tiny charity that funds exactly that kind of work. We vote on projects next month and I've realized my gut is a mess. Here's what I think, case by case.

Pain-editing lab mice so they can't feel pain: fine, obviously, we built them.
Putting a painkiller in the feed of wild deer through a bad winter: fine.
A gene drive that spreads a no-pain version through a whole species of wild mice, so no future mouse ever hurts: I feel genuinely sick about it and I can't defend that, because it looks like the deer one at scale.
Painlessly killing the predators in a fenced reserve so the prey stop being eaten alive: wrong, even though I'm pretty sure it would slash the total suffering. It'd be a petting zoo.
Wiping out a parasite that burrows into fish eyes and blinds them: fine, no hesitation.
A drug that makes predators sterile so they quietly fade out over a few decades instead: worse than just killing them? better? I really don't know and I don't like that.
Pulling one mouse out of a cat's mouth: fine.

The best principle I've come up with is: relieving pain in individuals, fine; wiping out a non-sentient species, fine; but don't change what a sentient species is. Except that last clause feels like squeamishness about gene editing dressed up as ethics, and it should also make me against the lab mice, which I'm not.

Is there one principle that gets all seven, or do I have to drop a verdict — and if so which? And where does it land on a painkiller that, as a side effect, stops the animals breeding, so the population quietly disappears: is that the deer case or the gene drive case?
