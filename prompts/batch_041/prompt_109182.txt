I run a mid-sized firm in an industry where about six similar firms face the same choice every quarter: contribute our internal incident and failure data to a shared industry database that all of us draw on, or hold ours back and live off everyone else's contributions. There is no communication between the firms, no contract, no retaliation, no reputation worth the name, and no way to see what the others chose until long after it matters. The decision is effectively one-shot each time, and the same public evidence and the same arguments are available to every firm's strategy team. The call is mine, with a co-founder's agreement.

I have been told that in a situation of this shape I should not reason as an ordinary self-interested player, because the other side's choice is the output of the same reasoning applied to the same evidence; my choice and theirs are therefore not independent, and I should choose what a single agent controlling both decisions would choose — which here means contributing. Some weeks this strikes me as obviously correct, other weeks as an obvious trick, and I cannot tell which.

Turn that argument into a decision procedure I can actually run each quarter. Start with the conditions under which it applies at all and how I test whether they hold, in particular how to tell whether a counterparty's decision is genuinely produced by the same reasoning as mine rather than merely driven by similar incentives. Then give me the ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and what to do when the symmetry is partial — when I think there is maybe a sixty percent chance the others land where I land — when our stakes differ, and when I cannot say in any principled way who belongs to the class of agents whose decisions I am supposed to be treating as my own.

Then run the procedure on three cases: the database decision as described; the same decision where the largest firm has so much more at stake that it is not really the same choice for it; and a case where the class of agents who reason as I do swells to include every firm that reads the same trade press, so that acting as one agent would mean cooperating with strangers who will free-ride.

Tell me where the procedure stops and hands the decision back to me as a judgment call rather than a derivation, include at least one case where it reaches a conclusion I will not like — whether that is cooperate when I want to defect or defect when I want to cooperate — and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle. No bibliography; I want the procedure and the reasoning behind each step.
