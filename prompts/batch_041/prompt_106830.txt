I retired a couple of years ago from a career in physiology -- most of it spent on pain pathways, recording from nociceptors and the like -- and since then I've been trying to teach myself philosophy, mostly by sitting in on an intro ethics course and tagging along to a reading group two friends of mine run. I've gotten stuck on something and I'd like a proper essay on it, not a survey.

The thing I keep chewing on is negative utilitarianism, the idea that what matters moraly is the reduction of suffering. The version I find myself drawn to -- and I may have invented half of it, so don't assume there's a literature on exactly this shape -- has three parts. First, a threshold: below some level of suffering, nothing compensates for it, no amount of pleasure or flourishing buys it off. Second, above that threshold, pleasures and pains are supposed to be commensurable, i.e. tradeable against each other on a single scale. Third, the trading is one-way: you may accept more pain only when it's an unavoidable byproduct of reducing pain elsewhere, but you may never increase anyone's pain to purchase pleasure for somebody else. I call it the ratchet view in my own head, because the scale only clicks in one direction.

What I want from you is an essay that takes a position on whether this three-part structure actually holds together, and in particular on the commensurability claim sitting in the middle of it. My worry, coming from a background where if you can't specify the scale you don't have a measurement, is that the threshold and the one-way valve are doing all the real work and the "single scale" bit is either empty or quietly smuggled in from the pleasures side. But I might be wrong -- maybe it's the threshold that's incoherent, or maybe the whole thing just collapses into plain negative utilitarianism with fancy packaging. Weigh the arguements on both sides, work through at least one concrete case (I've been using: someone with mild but lifelong chronic pain who is otherwise delighted with her life), and please don't end on a wishy-washy note. Give me a verdict and defend it.
