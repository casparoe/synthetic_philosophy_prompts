So I did forty years of lab science and now that I'm retired I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole — logic and philosophy of mind, mostly. And I keep noticing that people outside math love to drag Gödel's incompleteness theorems into everything: minds can't be machines, AI has a hard ceiling, physics can never be finished, no system can fully know itself, you name it. Half of it sounds like people who never actually worked through the proof. But I don't want to be the grumpy empiricist who waves it all away either, because a few of these look like they might be doing real work. A former colleague of mine in AI keeps insisting the live question isn't minds vs. machines but whether a system can ever justify trusting its own reasoning — he says that's a genuine technical problem and not just a slogan.

So if you had a smart grad student and two or three years to spend on this, which of these Gödel-flavoured lines would you actually push on, and in what order? I want the reasoning more than the list: which ones are upstream of the others, which are definately tractable rather than merely seductive, which are category errors where the theorem just doesn't do the work people want, and what result or piece of evidence would move one up or down. And be blunt about where I should stop invoking Gödel and reach for some other tool instead.
