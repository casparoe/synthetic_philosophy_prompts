Ok, so I'm organizing an adverserial collaboration for my grad seminar on responsibility and autonomous systems, and I need the actual joint document, not a summary of the fight.

Set it up between two camps. Call them the gap realists and the gap deflationists.

The realists say: when an autonomous system causes serious harm — a warehouse robot that's been learning on the job its whole service life and runs someone over; a triage model that quietly rations care in a hospital — there's a genuine shortfall. Nobody satisfies the ordinary conditions for blameworthiness (knowledge, control, the right kind of intention), and that shortfall isn't just an administrative annoyance; it's a real moral loss, because blame and moral address are things we arguably owe to victims.

The deflationists say: that "gap" is an artifact of an individualistic, all-or-nothing picture of blame. Once you treat responsibility as a practice — forward-looking accountability, answerability, responsibility distributed across the whole chain of designers, deployers, operators and regulators — the hole closes up. What's left is a design and regulation problem, not a metaphysical one.

Now give me the document they could both put their names to. Structure it like this:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims — and I mean claims with teeth, not mush. Include what they agree on about the forklift-type case, what work the word "responsibility" is doing in their dispute, and at least one thing they agree would *not* settle it.
2. A disagreements table. One row per genuine disagreement, with each side's formulation in a column of its own, worded so that side would actually sign off on it. No strawmen, no loaded wording on either side.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one side. I'd bet on at least two: (i) whether moral responsibility requires a single locus of control and knowledge, or whether it can be genuinely distributed with no remainder; (ii) whether accountability-without-blameworthiness answers the victim's complaint or just relabels the gap.
4. For each crux: a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts we'd conclude — predictions written down in advance, no hedging.

And one extra thing, to keep this from being a rerun of the usual lit. I've been pushing a distinction in seminar that I want both camps to take a stand on: a responsibility *gap* (there's a hole where an agent ought to be) versus a responsibility *spread* (there's too much responsibility, smeared so thin across so many parties that none of them is answerable in the way that matters). Have both sides say whether spread is a genuine third option, deflationism in disguise, or just the gap renamed. And if part of the fight is merely verbal, tell me which part and why.

Dont bother with citations or a reading list, I just want the document itself.
