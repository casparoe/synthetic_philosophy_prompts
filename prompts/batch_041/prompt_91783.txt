I chair the grants panel at a small foundation — we hand out roughly $2m a year in chunks of 40–80k to early-career researchers. Our rule is basically a bar. Reviewers score each proposal out of 10, and we fund everything that hits 7 in the order the applications came in, until the pot is empty. We never rank the pile as a whole, and we never have.

Last month a new member, Dana, said the rule is indefensible. In the spring cycle we funded a 7 that arrived early and then ran out of money before an 8 that arrived two weeks later. She says any procedure that produces that outcome can't be defended to the people we turn down, and that we're hiding behind "good enough" to avoid the work of comparing. My vice-chair Priya pushed back hard: eleven reviewers, no shared calibration, and when we tried full ranking three years ago the list reshuffled completely depending on who'd read what. She says "just rank them" is asking us to pretend to a precision we dont have.

Then Priya said something I keep chewing on. She said we should stop judging proposals one at a time and start judging the portfolio — what we're actually choosing is a *set* of grants, and a set isn't just a pile of individually-good-enough ones; the question is whether the whole batch is good enough given the money. Dana said that if you're allowed to compare portfolios then you're comparing options, and the moment you compare options you're maximizing, so Priya has conceded her point with extra steps.

Second thing. Two cycles ago we started moving the bar up and down to fit the pot: more 7s than money, raise it to 7.5; leftovers, drop it to 6.5. Half the panel thinks thats sensible housekeeping, the other half thinks it means the number was never a standard at all, just a budget valve.

I need to circulate a memo before the October meeting. Recommend a concrete rule for the next cycle — keep the bar as it is, rank everything, do Priya's portfolio thing, keep tuning the bar, or some combination — and say what we should do with proposals that come in after the money is gone (hold them over? return them with a note? fund them next round automatically?). Give me the recommendation and the reasoning up front, then the case against it.

And engage Dana's fairness point properly — don't just tell her the scores are noisy. She knows they're noisy; she thinks that's an argument for being *more* careful, not less. If you tell us to keep a bar, say what the bar is for. Is it "good enough given what it costs us to keep looking," or is it "good enough, full stop"? I suspect those two readings point to different rules, and I want that spelled out, along with which one our current practice actually is. Under 900 words, no references needed, I know the literature exists.
