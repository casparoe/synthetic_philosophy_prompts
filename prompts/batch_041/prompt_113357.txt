I'm in a reading group on AI safety — mostly ML people, no philosophers — and one analogy keeps coming up and I honestly cant tell anymore whether it's doing any work or just sounding deep. The story goes: evolution is like a training run, and organisms are the models it trained. Evolution was 'after' fitness, surviving and reproducing, and it built us as little optimizers that chase their own objectives (sugar, status, sex, not dying), which only loosely line up with what evolution was actually selecting for. So our brains are an inner optimizer that evolution's outer loop accidentally spun up, and the reason we don't just maximize fitness is that the inner thing ended up with its own goal that only roughly matches the outer one. My friend uses this to say we should expect the same from gradient descent: you train on a loss and you get a system with its own internal objective that comes apart from the loss the moment you push it off distribution, so you can never just 'train it to be good' and be done. Someone else in the group says that's backwards, because evolution actually produced organisms that are by and large pretty well aligned with surviving and reproducing, so if the analogy holds at all it's an argument that training mostly works. Neither has convinced me, and I keep noticing we're all just repeating the version of it we heard first.
