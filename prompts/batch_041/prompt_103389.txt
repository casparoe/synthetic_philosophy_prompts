I'm a novelist, not an academic, but I've wangled an invite to a small consciousness-studies workshop next month and I'm fairly sure the Q&A is going to eat me alive. My talk is about the colour phi phenomenom — the two flashing dots where the second one's a different colour, and viewers swear they saw the colour change partway along the path. Dennett's move is that this leaves you two stories: an Orwellian one where the experience is fine and only the memory of it gets doctored afterwards, or a Stalinesque one where the doctoring happens before the experience is ever had, and since both predict the same report there's no fact of the matter about which is true. I want to argue he's wrong, that there is a fact of the matter, and that it shows up the moment you stop treating the subject's retrospective report as the only output — if you interrupt them mid-trial, or make them hit a button the instant the second dot lands rather than telling you afterwards what they saw, the two stories ought to come apart, and I think the data leans Stalinesque. The room will be mostly experimental psychologists who work on masking and temporal binding and will think I'm a tourist, plus two philosophers of mind who know the Libet and Dennett literature backwards, plus a couple of literary critics who'll be on my side for all the wrong reasons. So prep me for the questions: what will they actually throw at me, in their own vocabulary, ranked by how badly each one hurts, each with a two-minute answer and an honest note on where my answer is soft. And tell me the one question I'm praying nobody asks. British English, and don't be gentle.
