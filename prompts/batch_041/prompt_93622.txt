Question: "Biologists routinely say that genes carry information and that DNA is a code. Some philosophers of biology reply that 'information' here is a metaphor at best. Explain what each side is claiming, and assess whether appealing to Shannon's theory of information helps or hurts the claim that DNA carries information."

Student's answer:

"There are two things 'genetic information' might mean. In the ordinary sense, information is about something: a message has content and can be true or false. In Shannon's technical sense, information is a measure of how much uncertainty a signal removes, and Shannon was explicit that this has nothing to do with meaning. So the skeptics are right that if 'information' means content, Shannon's theory cannot underwrite it, since a stretch of DNA has a Shannon information content in bits but does not say anything.

But I think the two senses come back together once you build in a receiver. Shannon's measure is defined against a set of possible messages with their probabilities, and once you fix the source, a signal that reduces your uncertainty about it is, in the ordinary sense, carrying information about it. So the technical notion is really the causal-correlation notion in disguise: a signal carries information about a source just when it lets you predict the source, and that is exactly what a gene does with respect to the protein it codes for.

That is why I think 'code' is the right word. The genetic code is a mapping from codons to amino acids, and the mapping is largely arbitrary: nothing about the physical shape of a codon makes it fit its amino acid, the way nothing about the shape of a word makes it mean what it means. Since arbitrary mappings are what languages and codes are made of, and since this one is used to specify proteins, DNA carries semantic content and not just Shannon content. Teleosemantics makes the same point from the other direction: because the sequence has the function of producing a particular protein, that protein is what the sequence represents.

The complication is that the same sequence can be expressed differently depending on the cellular environment, or not at all. I don't think that sinks the informational view, but it does mean it has to be stated carefully, because what the gene contributes is only part of the story and the rest is coming from the cell."
