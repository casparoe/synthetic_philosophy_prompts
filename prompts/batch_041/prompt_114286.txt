I just got back from the pub after a four-hour arguement with my mate Priya about what probability actually is, and we got absolutely nowhere. She's a hardline frequentist: a probability is a limiting relative frequency in a collective, full stop, and she'll cheerfully tell you that a one-off event doesn't have a probability at all. I'm more of a propensity person — I think a chance is a real disposition of the experimental setup, so there's a perfectly good answer to what the chance is that this particular lump of plutonium decays before Tuesday, even if the universe never gives us a second run at it. She reckons my propensities are just a dressed-up reference class; I reckon her collectives are just idealised propensities; we went round in circles for hours.

So write the joint statement we should have produced instead. I want a numbered list of claims we can both sign, then a table of our remaining disagreements with each side's view worded so that side would actually accept the wording, then the cruxes, and for each crux a case or argument that both of us would agree bears on it, with what each of us would predict about it.

Two things I'd like you to take seriously rather than skate over. First, the conditional business: when you conditionalise, is the propensity theorist carving a sub-population out of a fixed disposition, or changing the disposition itself? If conditional propensities don't compose the way conditional probabilities do, is a propensity a probability in the Kolmogorov sense at all — and if not, whose problem is that, the propensity theorist's or the calculus's? Second, the dispositional analogue of finkishness and masking: a disposition can be present and yet never manifest, because something gets in the way. If propensities can be masked, can two setups with identical frequency behaviour have different propensities — and is that the whole point, because it's what makes them real properties, or the fatal flaw, because they stop being testable?

And tell me straight if part of what Priya and I were doing was just fighting over words.
