Write an essay on whether an ant colony can have a mind. Treat Aunt Hillary as the central case: a colony whose collective behaviour is so integrated that it looks like a single agent, capable of memory, negotiation and something like speech, even though no individual ant grasps the colony's situation. Weigh the strongest considerations for and against taking such a colony to be a genuine subject of mental states, and end with a verdict you are prepared to defend, rather than the observation that everything depends on how one defines mind. To keep the question sharp, distinguish at least three things one might mean by having a mind: being a unified subject of experience; being a bearer of intentional states such as belief and desire; and being a proper object of moral concern. Then test your account against four pressure cases. First, a colony whose behaviour is functionally indistinguishable from that of a person but whose internal workings are entirely local pheromone exchanges, with no representation of the colony as a whole. Second, a colony that divides into two halves, each of which behaves as the original did. Third, two colonies that merge into one. Fourth, a colony whose behaviour is partly controlled by a parasite that manipulates individual ants. For each case, state what your view implies and whether that implication counts for or against it. Two further questions should bear on your verdict. If the colony has a mind, what is the subject of that mind when every ant in it is replaced over time, and does the colony's mind survive fission or fusion in the way a person's mind survives ordinary change? And if the colony's mental life is realised only in the interactions rather than in any part, is there any fact of the matter about what the colony believes, or only a useful way of predicting it? Consider, in this connection, the difference between a system that realises a mind and a system that counts as a mind because it models itself as a unified agent: does Aunt Hillary need a self-model, or is it enough that we can usefully model her as an agent? Finally, confront the objection that attributing a mind to the colony either makes the minds of individual ants redundant or reduces to the uncontroversial claim that the colony processes information. Reach a conclusion about whether the colony can literally have a mind, and if so in which of the three senses. Argue from the cases and distinctions you develop; do not turn the essay into a literature review, and do not rely on citations.
