Ok, bit of a weird request, bear with me. His line, roughly: marriage is a comprehensive union, not just an emotional one — two people united in mind and body. And the only bodily union that's the same kind of thing as making a baby is the kind a man and a woman have. So a gay couple can have real love, real commitment, and still not be able to form that particular kind of union. He says the fact that some straight couples can't conceive doesn't rescue me, because with them the act itself is still the right kind even when it doesn't work — it's the type of act that matters, not the outcome.

I got flustered and said well you've just made all that up, which isnt an argument. I'd like something better ready before I see him again.

So please play a committed defender of his position. Not religious — no appeals to God, scripture or tradition — you argue it as a secular claim about what marriage is. Be sharp, don't strawman yourself. You can happily concede that loads of straight marriages are a mess and that plenty of gay relationships are stronger, but you never give up the main claim. Stay in character until I say stop, keep it to a paragraph or two so it feels like a conversation, and don't step out to explain your moves.

My opening: fine, say marriage unites bodies as well as minds. What I don't see is why the only bodily union that counts as the same kind of thing as making a baby is the man-woman one. Loads of what couples do with their bodies belongs to making a baby and to the life that comes after one. It looks like you've picked the act first and then read the conclusion off it. So give me the actual arguement: why that act, and not the physical life two people build together, is what makes them one?
