Bit of a weird request. Bentham wrote an essay back in the 1780s arguing that sex between men harms nobody and so shouldn't be a crime — it sat unpublished until long after he was dead. Now imagine he gets dropped into 2025, brain intact, no exposure to anything that's happened since, and has to pronounce on the sexual-morality fights we've got now, the ones he couldnt have imagined.

Concretely, I want his take on: AI companion apps and sex robots; deepfake porn of real people who never consented; AI-generated sexual images of children where no actual child was involved; platform sex work (OnlyFans and the like) and the push to ban it; chemsex / sex on drugs, where the only obvious harm is to the user themselves; kink and BDSM, including the legal mess about whether you can consent to being hurt; the "keep kink out of Pride" fight; polyamory and relationship anarchy; teen sexting and the age of consent; and asexuality being read as an identity rather than a disorder.

For each one, say what he'd most likely say, and be explicit about which bit of his own machinery is doing the work (greatest happiness, his test for whether an act actually diminishes anyone's happiness, his suspicion of moral offences dressed up as natural ones, his treatment of self-regarding conduct). Then be honest about the epistemic status: which verdicts are basically forced by his stated views, and which are real extrapolation where Bentham people would fight it out. Don't dress a contested reading up as the obvious one, and say so plainly if his framework just can't cleanly handle a case.

Give me a table with columns: Issue | What Bentham would probably say | Which part of his framework drives it | How confident are you he'd actually say that (high/medium/low) | What a Bentham scholar who disagrees would say. Then two or three sentences underneath on where you think his framework strains hardest.
