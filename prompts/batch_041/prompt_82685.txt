I took my degree in entomology and then spent thirty years in a physiology laboratory; since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mind, and I have reached the point where I need a specification before I read any further. The occasion is the notion that behaviour can be sphexish — mechanically fixed, endlessly repeated — as when the digger wasp drags its paralysed prey to the burrow, leaves it outside while it inspects the nest, and then, if the prey has in the meantime been nudged a few inches away, retrieves it and begins the whole inspection sequence again, indefinitely. What I cannot tell is whether sphexishness marks a real difference in kind among systems, or only a difference in how much of an animal's repertoire the observer happened to sample. So, before I go on: write the desiderata for a theory of behavioural rigidity and flexibility — what such a theory must deliver to be adequate for serious comparative use, i.e. good enough that calling a wasp, a patient, or a program sphexish could be an explanatory remark rather than an insult. I want the cases it must get right (the wasp; the ants that mill themselves to death around a pheromone trail that doubles back; the corvid that bends a hook; the driver who takes the familiar turning without deciding to; the player who calculates), each with the consideration behind it; the distinctions it may not collapse (rigidity of a component mechanism versus of the whole agent; being unable to vary versus having no occasion to vary; a graded capacity versus a difference in kind; a flexible competence exercised rigidly; behaviour that is stereotyped under one description and variable under another); and the findings it cannot contradict, including the persistence of a trained habit after its outcome has been devalued and its reinstatement by its cue, and the learning and context sensitivity displayed by the very animals we call rigid. Say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of a particular camp, and whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect that demanding rigidity be intrinsic to the system, and demanding that it be graded and description-relative, cannot both be honoured, but correct me if I am wrong. Finally, which single requirement excludes the most candidates? You needn't cite anything; I can chase the literature myself.
