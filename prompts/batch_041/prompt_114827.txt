I have been working through the puzzle of whether being better than is transitive, especially when a long series of tiny changes—each step seemingly no worse than the one before—connects a life that is plainly good to one that is plainly bad. I am not looking for a survey of who has said what. What I want is a careful specification of what any account of betterness would need to accomplish in order to handle this puzzle honestly. Give me the requirements one by one, each with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and say which you take to be non-negotiable and which are optional. I care especially about the cases the account must get right, including sequences that run in both directions and sequences whose steps are not all along one scale—for example, a step that reduces pain but also reduces memory, followed by one that does the reverse—the distinctions it cannot flatten (such as betterness versus choiceworthiness, or incomparability versus indifference), and the structural constraints it must respect (for instance, whether it may be context-sensitive, whether it must avoid cycles, and whether it must treat a comparison as unaffected by what other options are around). If two of the requirements cannot both be satisfied, say which and why. I do not need a scorecard of named theories.
