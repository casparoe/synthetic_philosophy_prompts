Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: each branch node should be a single premise the argument needs — completeness of the preference ordering, transitivity, independence or the sure-thing principle, the Archimedean/continuity condition, the requirement that preferences be defined over lotteries rather than only over outcomes, the assumption that the agent will accept each step of a sequence of trades rather than only the package, and whether what is doing the work is exploitability itself or merely representability by a probability–utility pair — each phrased as a question a reader could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Note any combination of answers nobody occupies and say in a clause whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any leaves your tree cannot separate rather than papering over it. Keep the whole thing to at most seven branching questions and no more than 650 words.
