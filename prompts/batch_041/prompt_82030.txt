Ok so I keep getting into arguments with a friend about whether LLMs actually understand anything, and we go in circles because we can't agree on what would even count as evidence. I want to stop arguing about the definition and just rank the actual probes by how much they'd move me.

Here's the list I've been collecting — add anything that's obviously missing, but rank all of these:

- the classic one: does it generalize to held-out combinations of things it only ever saw separately in training
- asking it to give the same answer two different ways and checking the two are actually equivalent, not just both plausible-sounding
- the garden-path trick: slip a false premise into the setup and see if it pushes back or just answers along
- coining a brand new term mid-prompt and then reusing it later, to see if it tracks its own coinage
- asking it to redo a problem with one premise negated, and checking that only the parts that should change, change
- re-asking in a wildly different register (as a poem, as a legal brief, as a shopping list) and seeing if the substance survives
- asking for a confidence number and checking whether it's actually calibrated
- the interpretability move: intervene on the internals and see if the thing behaves like it's carrying a world model rather than a lookup

Put them in tiers (S/A/B/C/D, whatever you like) by how much weight you'd actually give each as evidence of understanding, with a sentence or two on why each lands where it does. And flag which ones you think a system that's obviously just doing pattern matching could sail through anyway — thats the part I actually care about.
