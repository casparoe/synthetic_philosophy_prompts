I've been reading LessWrong, SSC, and the usual blogs for like a decade, and I admin a fairly big forum (roughly 200k members). We make a call like this maybe once a month and I always end up just vibing it, which bugs me.

The shape of the decision is always the same: I can add some rule or feature that costs every single member a tiny bit — an extra click, a two-second wait, one fewer emoji, slightly slower loads — in exchange for preventing a small number of really bad incidents (someone gets doxxed, someone gets run off the site, a pile-on that wrecks one person's month). Or the reverse: I can strip a safeguard that a handful of people really depended on so that everyone else's life gets marginally nicer.

I know this is basically the dust-specks-vs-torture thing and I've read alot of those threads, but they always turn into people calling each other monsters and nobody ever hands you something you can actually run. What I want is a procedure I can execute in ~15 minutes with my co-admin before we ship a change.

Here's the part I want it to really bite on, because it's where I keep getting stuck. Some of the costs are real-but-tiny for each person (everyone loses 3 seconds a day, definately). Others are purely statistical (the rule makes a bad outcome less likely, but for any given member nothing at all happens). I have a nagging feeling those two aren't the same kind of thing and shouldn't be summed the same way, but I can't articulate why. And I'm scared of the slide where, if you'll trade a tiny annoyance for a big harm once, you'll keep doing it until the place is a friction-maximizing fortress and somehow everyone's miserable anyway. So I'd like the procedure to say something about when adding up small stuff is legit and when there's a floor that no pile of small stuff should push someone through.

So please write the procedure: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a hard no, and where it hands the call back to me because it's a values question rather than a fact question. Be honest about which steps are just judgment dressed up as a flowchart.

Then run it on three cases:
(a) adding a 2-second delay before every post, to cut down on a rare but severe brigading/harassment pattern;
(b) an interstitial screen that makes every new poster confirm they've read the rules, to reduce a handful of clueless accidental rule-breaks;
(c) removing an obscure accessibility feature that maybe 200 members actually use, because it's the source of a bug that mildly annoys everyone else.
At least one of those should come out as a no that I won't like.

Keep it concrete enough that a co-admin who's never read any philosophy could follow it. And at the very end, put a one-line summary as the last line in the form \summary{...}.
