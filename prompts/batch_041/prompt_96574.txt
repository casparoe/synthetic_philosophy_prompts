Ok, so a friend in my logic reading group hit me with this and I've been chewing on it all week. He says it shows that "uncountable" is a fiction and that there's really only one infinity. Here's the argument and I can't find the exit.

(1) Löwenheim–Skolem: any consistent first-order theory with an infinite model has a countable model — a model whose entire domain can be put in one-to-one correspondence with the natural numbers.
(2) ZFC is a first-order theory and (we're all assuming) consistant, so it has a countable model.
(3) He says we can even take the model transitive, so that its natural numbers are the real ω and "∈" is the real one. He admits that's stronger than plain consistency, but he says it's the version that hurts.
(4) Inside any model of ZFC, Cantor's theorem goes through, so the model says of some set of its own that it's uncountable — there's no bijection in the model between that set and ω.
(5) Call that set x. The model is countable, so its whole domain is x₁, x₂, x₃, …. And x is a subset of that domain, so just walk the list and keep the ones that are in x. That's an enumeration of x. So x is countable — and in the transitive version there's no dodging about whose ω we mean.
(6) But the model says x is uncountable. So the model is just wrong about x.
(7) There was nothing special about this model, so any model of ZFC can be wrong about cardinality.
(8) So "uncountable" isn't a fact about sets, it's a fact about which bijections happen to live inside your model. There's no fact of the matter.
(9) So Cantor's whole hierarchy — different sizes of infinity — is an artifact of the formalism. There's only one infinity, and set theory is a kind of elaborate mistake.

I'm not buying (9). Cantor's diagonal argument is the most airtight thing I know, and I'm not giving up the naturals being a completed infinity either. But (1) is a theorem, and (3) seems fine, and I genuinely can't find the cheat in (5). So: where does this break? Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? I want the full price list, not just the name of the guilty premise — if the move is to say the model isn't "wrong" but only "wrong by its own lights" or something, tell me exactly what that distinction buys and whether it actually defuses the worry or just relabels it. And if there's more than one place to push, rank the fixes by how much they cost me, because I'd really rather not lose (1) or Cantor if I can help it.
