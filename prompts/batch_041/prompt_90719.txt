I'm a PhD student in linguistics, working on literary translation, and I know Optimality Theory well enough to use it rather than just recognize it: GEN, CON, EVAL, ranked violable constraints, violation profiles, factorial typology. I've been persuaded by the view I associate with Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot -- that translating a poem under formal constraint (meter, rhyme, acrostic, lipogram, fixed syllable count) is not stripping off the form to recover a literal meaning and then re-decorating it, but a search through candidate texts under conflicting constraints, where something always has to give. Restate that position as faithfully as you can in OT: what is the input, what is GEN, what are the constraints and are they universal or language-specific, how does EVAL select, and what ranking would a translator have who prizes the acrostic over the rhyme versus one who prizes fidelity over both. Then the residue report: what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion -- where the mapping forces OT commitments the original view need not make -- and what has no counterpart at all. I'm especially interested in whether translator agency, the open-ended candidate set, constraints invented for a single poem, and the difference between a correct and a good translation can be expressed in OT, and whether the residue is a defect of OT or a sign the original claim was less precise than it sounded. No citations or reading lists; I want the translation and the residue.
