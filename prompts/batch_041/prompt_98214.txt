Here's his spiel, as best I remember it: deflationists think the word "true" doesn't pick out any real property — it's just a handy device for agreeing with people and for making general claims like "everything she said is true," so "it's true that the cat's on the mat" says exactly the same thing as "the cat's on the mat," nothing added. And since truth isn't a property, there's nothing substantive for a theory of truth to explain, which is why they think the whole correspondence-vs-coherence debate was a waste of time and why they're comfortable saying that in the end truth is just whatever your community agrees to call true. Which parts would an actual deflationist happily sign off on, which are half-right, and which would they flatly deny?
