For a paper in my philosophy of mathematics seminar, I need to write a dialogue between two philosophers of set theory who disagree about what Skolem's paradox shows. The first takes Skolem's relativist side: because every consistent set theory with a model has a countable model, and because a countable model can satisfy the sentence asserting that uncountable sets exist, cardinality claims are relative to a model and there is no absolute fact about whether a given set is countable. The second defends the standard reply: the paradox dissolves once we distinguish what holds inside a model from what holds of it, so that a countable model's version of the first uncountable ordinal is genuinely countable and the model simply lacks the bijection that would witness this. Please stage their exchange around a countable transitive model M of ZFC (supposing one exists) and its ω₁^M, with each responding to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past them: let the relativist press that the dissolving reply quietly assumes a privileged background universe that is itself only another model, and let the absolutist press that this conflates a model's failure to define the witnessing bijection with there being no fact of the matter. Neither side should be a strawman, and both should be equally competent.
