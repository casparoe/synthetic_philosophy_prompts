I teach a seminar that puts philosophy of physics next to twentieth-century fiction, and I want a critical reading of Kurt Vonnegut's Slaughterhouse-Five against Gödel's views on time. Gödel's cosmological work includes rotating-universe solutions to Einstein's field equations that contain closed timelike curves, worldlines that loop back into their own past; he treated these models as conceptually significant rather than as descriptions of our actual universe, on the reasoning that if the laws do not exclude a worldline returning to its own past, then the objective lapse of time -- the privileged present that separates past from future -- cannot belong to the world as it is in itself, and he concluded that time in that sense is ideal, something contributed by our mode of apprehension rather than discovered in the world. Vonnegut's novel gives us Billy Pilgrim, whose life arrives out of order, and the Tralfamadorians, who see all moments at once and treat each death as one moment among many that all exist together, with the narrator marking every death by the same shrug. The book is also about Dresden, and the consolation is offered to a man who cannot narrate what he saw.

Here is what I want pursued. Does the novel's time amount to Gödel's, or is it closer to a narrative and psychological non-linearity -- memory, trauma, a narrator who jumps -- that merely resembles a block universe? Gödel's argument is conditional on what the laws permit and is silent about whether our world is like that, whereas Vonnegut's is presented as revelation, and the novel keeps it unclear whether Tralfamadore is real or a head injury's compensation; does that uncertainty matter to the reading, and is the book a dramatization of the timeless view or a diagnosis of why someone would need it? And where does the novel press back on the philosophy -- does the block picture, in Vonnegut's hands, come out ethically corrosive, and does Gödel's argument have anything to say to that? Be faithful in both directions: do not reduce the novel to an illustration of a result in cosmology, and do not inflate Gödel's argument into a thesis about determinism or free will that it is not. Where the parallel strains, say so rather than forcing it. End by saying what, if anything, the novel supplies that the philosophical argument cannot. Tell me how confident you are in each of your main claims and identify the single point you are least sure of.
