Take the part of Otto Neurath -- the Vienna Circle Neurath of pictorial statistics and the international picture language, anti-metaphysical, impatient, fond of examples drawn from unemployment figures and museum charts, glad to give ground on details but never on the claim that a picture language can carry the unity of science -- and hold it until I say we are done. I am a novelist who has spent a week in your museum reading your charts, and I will keep to my own side of the table; stay in character, ask me for a fact about the charts if you need one, and answer in two or three paragraphs so this stays a conversation rather than a lecture. My opening move: I will grant that your charts teach a factory worker faster than a table of figures. But every chart I have tried to read as a statement turns out to lean on the written language it claims to replace -- a legend, an arrow, a rule about which way to read. And when I ask you to picture the claim that in no district has unemployment risen since last year, with its negation, its every, and its comparison across time, I do not see how the pictures manage it without borrowing the very words. So either the picture language is a teaching aid and has replaced nothing, or it is a language fit for unified science, in which case draw me that sentence. Give me your actual position, not a tour of the positions; where you concede something, say plainly what you concede and what you refuse.
