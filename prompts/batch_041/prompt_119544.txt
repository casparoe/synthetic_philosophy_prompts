I'm putting together a handout for the second meeting of my mind and morals seminar, and the topic is the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the worry that "pleasure" names such a grab-bag (the taste of a peach, the relief when a headache finally stops, the itch you get to scratch, the satisfaction of finally seeing how a proof works, the smirk at a rival's bad luck, the masochist's enjoyment of what he himself thinks is bad for him) that nothing is shared by all of them, and hedonism's central term is either empty or a disjunction.

What I want back is a decision tree, not an essay. Start from the question that actually splits the field and work down, and please build in a few distinctions, because I keep finding that the standard formulations blur them:

- Don't conflate the two jobs the alleged commonality might be doing: uniting pleasures (what makes a state a pleasure at all) and ranking them (what makes this pleasure larger than that one). Some views buy unity cheaply and then can't compare across kinds; some give up on unity but keep a comparison. Make it an explicit branch whether one property is meant to do both jobs or whether the theory needs two.

- Take seriously that the commonality might not be a single property but the sort of thing "jade" is: two unrelated minerals lumped under one word. I want the tree to seperate that from the softer view that pleasures share a determinable while differing in their determinates (the way red and blue are both colours without sharing a shade), and from plain eliminativism, where there's no commonality but we keep "pleasure" as a handy list.

- Add a branch on whether the commonality has to be something the subject can introspect, or can be subpersonal — a role played by the machinery that you'd never notice from the inside. That one splits apart a lot of views that otherwise look identical.

Leaves should carry the name of a position, or "unnamed" if the slot is empty, plus one line on that view's most awkward commitment. I want at least these leaves: a unitary feeling-tone view, an attitudinal or desire-based view, a view on which pleasure is a perception of value, a functional-role view, the determinable/determinate pluralist, the jade-style nominalist, and the eliminativist who still wants to call himself a hedonist. Flag any two leaves the tree can't really separate, and tell me which branches come out unoccupied and whether that's because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Aim for eight to ten leaves. Don't worry about who defended what — I don't want a reading list, I want the logical space.
