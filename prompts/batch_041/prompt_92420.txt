hey, im a junior in high school and i run the philosophy club (theres four of us, dont be impressed). for next weeks meeting im supposed to bring a short essay that argues something and then everyone tears it apart, and i want you to tear it apart first. mine is about whether "clutch" is a real thing in sports, but like, philosophically. can you read it and tell me whats wrong with it?

heres the gist. everyone says some players are "clutch" -- they hit the shot when it matters. i want to say being clutch is a real trait of the player, not just a fact about how often they happened to make shots. so i say: a player is clutch if they WOULD make the shot in a close game, under the right circumstances. "would" is doing the work, its a disposition, like glass being fragile or sugar being soluble.

then the obvious objection: how do you know what someone WOULD do, you cant rerun the game. so i bring in the two ways a disposition can be real but not show up when you look:

1. the trait gets destroyed right when you test it. example i made up: a shooter whos amazing in practice, but the second the lights are on and its a real game, something in him tightens up and the ability is basically gone. the test itself removes the thing being tested. i call this a "trap" (i think people who write about this call it a fink, but i dont like that word).

2. the trait is still there, but something gets in the way of it showing. example: same shooter is fine, but the coach runs a play where he never touches the ball. the ability is there, the situation just blocks it. i call this a "blocker" (i think the word is mask?).

my claim: "clutch" should only count as a real trait if we mean the version where nothing is blocking it. and choking in big games is a trap, not proof the player never had it. so a guy who misses every big shot might still be clutch and we'd just never know, which sounds insane but i think its right.

what i want from you:

(a) is this actually accurate to how people who study this stuff think about it, or did i mess up the trap/blocker thing? especially -- is choking really a trap, or is it a blocker, or is that even a real distinction and not just me being clever?

(b) is the essay actually interesting, or am i just renaming something obvious?

(c) where am i just asserting stuff i cant back up?

(d) someone in my club is 100% going to say my definition is circular -- "he'd make it if nothing stopped him" just means "he'd make it if he made it." how do i answer that? is that a real problem or are they just being annoying?
