I'm writing a position paper on the applicability of mathematics and want a consistency audit before I commit to print. Here is where I have landed.

(1) Mathematical truth is discovered, not made. The structures mathematicians investigate have their properties independently of us, of our notation, and of whichever axiom system we happen to be working in.

(2) Our knowledge of those truths is a priori in the relevant sense: what justifies a theorem is a proof, and a proof is not an experiment. Nothing in the epistemology of mathematics resembles observation.

(3) Even so, our mathematical beliefs are fully explained by contingent history — which problems were tractable, which notation caught on, and above all which formalisms paid off in physics. There is no faculty of mathematical perception doing any explanatory work, and I would treat the postulation of one as a naturalistic failure.

(4) The fit between mathematics and the physical world stands in need of explanation, and I do not think the demand is met by saying that we simply retain the mathematics that fits. That story has the right form only if fitting is independent of having been retained; otherwise it explains why we kept certain formalisms, not why they work on problems they were never built for.

(5) I take appeals to unification in theoretical physics to be evidentially serious. When a single formalism absorbs two previously independent domains and no rival does, that is evidence that the formalism is true of the world and not merely a convenient encoding.

(6) There is a case I need to be able to handle: a mathematician develops a structure for purely internal reasons; decades later a physicist finds that structure realized in a physical system; we say the mathematician's work turned out to be about the world. I want to insist that this is a genuine discovery about nature rather than a fortunate coincidence of notation.

I suspect this package does not hang together, but I cannot tell which item is doing the damage. Please do not tell me that everything is in tension with everything else. Identify the pairs that genuinely conflict, order those conflicts by severity, and say explicitly which apparent conflicts dissolve and why. For each real conflict, name the cheapest commitment to surrender and state what surrendering it costs me elsewhere in the package. If a single revision would clear up more than one conflict, say which one and why.
