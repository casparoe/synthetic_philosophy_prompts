I was reading about the Ise Grand Shrine in Japan — the one thats torn down and rebuilt from scratch every twenty years or so, with all new wood, and everyone still calls it the same shrine and acts like the deity is still there. It got me thinking about mind uploading and gradual replacement, because the shrine looks like a case where a thing survives the total replacement of its parts and nobody blinks. So what view of identity does that rebuilding practice actually presuppose? Not just "we decided it's the same shrine" — I want the implicit theory. Does it need identity to live in the form, in the site, in the ritual, in the deity, or in the community that keeps rebuilding? Which of those are load-bearing and which are just decoration? And does that theory carry over to a person whose neurons get swapped out one at a time, or to a mind uploaded to a computer, or does it secretly depend on something — a god, a place, a line of priests — that has no analogue for a mind? Tell me what the practice strictly presupposes versus what it merely fits with, where its underdetermined, and what would have to change in the practice (or in our uploading assumptions) if we swapped in a rival theory of identity. Give it to me as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, no intro, no conclusion.
