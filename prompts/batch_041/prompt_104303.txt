I do causal inference and ML for a living -- DAGs, do-calculus, interventions, counterfactuals, d-separation, mutual information, that whole toolkit is my native language and I think in it alot. I've been poking at epiphenomenalism, the view that phenomenal/conscious states are caused by or supervene on physical brain states but do no causal work themselves, they're like a shadow or a byproduct. And the thing that's supposed to make the view self-undermining is the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if my phenomenal states can't cause anything, they can't cause my judgments or reports about them either, since those reports are themselves physical events. So how are my reports even about them, and how could I know them?
