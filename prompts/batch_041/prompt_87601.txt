I retired from geology a couple of years ago and have been reading philosophy ever since — a lot of it about what you can and can't know from physical traces, which as you can imagine feels like my old day job in a new suit. I've got a bee in my bonnet about archaeologists confidently telling us what prehistoric people belived, so I want you to build me a case to order.

Here's the spec. One site, one set of objects, realistic — a small prehistoric burial ground with grave goods, that sort of thing, nothing sci-fi, no time travel, and no conveniently preserved texts. Also no fair appealing to living descendants or to a neighbouring culture that happens to have left written records; I want those ruled out by the setup itself rather than by my good manners. The material record has to support two rival reconstructions of what these people believed about their dead — say, one where the grave goods are provisions for a journey, and one where they're dangerous things that have to be buried along with the body to keep them away from the living — and both reconstructions have to fit every single observable feature of the objects: what's present, what's absent, wear patterns, placement, the lot.

Now the hard part, and this is the bit I actually care about: make the two beliefs behaviourally inert. The people holding belief A and the people holding belief B do exactly the same things, with exactly the same objects, in exactly the same circumstances — the only difference between the two reconstructions is what's going on inside their heads. I want to see whether the case can be built at all without smuggling in a hidden behavioural difference, because if it can, then the record underdetermines belief by construction, and the whole thing turns into a question about whether "belief" here is even a separate thing from the practice.

Then do the work. State the record, give both reconstructions, and walk through the evidence piece by piece showing it's neutral between them. Tell me which feature of the case is doing the work — I suspect it's the inertness stipulation, but you tell me — and what happens if I drop it: does the case collapse back into ordinary inference from traces, and if so, how far? Then tell me how someone who thinks you can read beliefs straight off objects would attack the case. I want their strongest move, not a strawman, and I want to know whether any of their moves actually lands on this setup.

One more thing. Be careful to seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested. I don't want a confident-sounding answer that quietly turns one school's view into the consensus — if people who actually know this area disagree about whether a belief difference that leaves no behavioural trace is even coherent, or about whether the belief/practice distinction survives in a case like this, say so plainly, and tell me which parts of your answer are load-bearing for the argument and which are just my framing.

Keep the scenario itself to about a page; the analysis can run longer. Plain prose please, no jargon I'd need a dictionary for — I'm a beginner, remember.
