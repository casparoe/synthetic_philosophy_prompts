I host a small departmental podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher who works on the surprise exam paradox. Her position is deliberately non-standard: she holds that the puzzle dissolves once one separates the teacher's announcement as a claim about the students' epistemic state from the announcement as a premise the students are entitled to reason from, and that the backward induction has no grip on agents who merely hold the announcement with some credence rather than assuming its truth. I want eight to ten questions that force her to develop and defend this rather than restate it. No biographical or warm-up questions, and nothing that asks her to recommend reading.

Please include at least one question each on the following, and for each question a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give:

- the final-day case: does her account entail that the students cannot be surprised on the last possible day, and if so, does the announcement itself rule that day out?
- the status of the students' reasoning: is it a valid argument whose conclusion is that the announcement cannot be known, or is it simply a fallacy? She should not be allowed to have it both ways in a single breath.
- the self-referential character of the announcement: its content seems to shift as the students' beliefs shift, and I want to know what she takes that to imply about whether the teacher can assert it at all.
- a variant I have invented and want her to handle cold. The teacher writes the day on a card and seals it; the class is told only that the card will be opened at the end of the week. Then a second version, in which the class is told the exact procedure by which the day is chosen — say a fair die over the five days — so that everyone can see the surprise is guaranteed in advance while nobody can say which day it will fall on.

Close with two questions aimed at the weakest point of her view, one of which should put this to her directly: on the last evening, if the exam has not yet occurred, only one day remains, and that seems to follow from the calendar rather than from trusting the announcement at all.
