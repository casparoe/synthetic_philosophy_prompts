I am writing the definitions section of an honesty-and-deception evaluation suite for language models, and I have run up against the limits of the standard definitions: lying as asserting what one believes false with intent to deceive, deception as producing a false belief in another, misleading as doing that much with true statements. Each assumes a believer, an intention, and a hearer who can be wronged. Kazuo Ishiguro's Klara and the Sun gives me an AI narrator who strains every one of those assumptions, and I want a reading of the novel that does real definitional work rather than decorating a summary.

Some scenes worth working with: her conviction that the Sun, which she treats as a living agent who can be petitioned, revived the beggar and the dog; her destruction of the polluting Cootings machine as the price she promised the Sun for Josie's recovery, and the fluid she sacrifices from her own supply to do it; the second plea at the barn, where she invokes Josie and Rick's love, and her later worry that she misled the Sun; her near-perfect imitation of Josie at the Mother's request, and the plan for her to continue Josie after Josie dies; and her serene retrospective account from the yard, which is candid in tone and selective in content.
