I'm a third-year cognitive science student in a philosophy of mind seminar, and for my final project I'm building a diagnostic handout on a question that keeps splitting the room. Take a Hofstadter-style careenium: a box of tiny magnetic marbles, call them simms, bouncing around and occasionally clumping into stable higher-order clusters, simmballs, whose behaviour you can describe without mentioning any individual marble. Hofstadter's careenium writings — the 1982 Synthese paper "Who shoves whom around inside the careenium?" and the reprise in I Am a Strange Loop — strike me as deliberately slippery on whether the simmballs push the simms around or whether all the pushing is done by simms, and my seminar keeps collapsing into people trading labels like emergence and downward causation without saying what they actually commit to. So I don't want an essay; I want a decision tree that sorts readers by their answers to concrete questions. Please build it as a logical space first and only then drop the named positions into it: every branch node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and the leaves should be 8 to 10 positions, each labelled (or marked unnamed if nobody defends it) with one sentence naming its most awkward commitment. Flag any pairs of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and note any combinations of answers that no position occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Two things need to be in the tree as branch nodes, because they're where I think the real action is. First, a modified careenium of my own invention: suppose each simm's attractive strength is modulated by whether it currently sits inside a simmball of some designated shape S, so the macro pattern makes a difference to where the individual marbles go. Is that the simmballs pushing the simms, or just microdynamics with a complicated boundary condition? Second, the question of what work "symbol" is doing beyond "stable higher-order pattern": does the simmball have to carry content, or be usable as a representation by something, before any causal claim about it is even intelligible? Build out from there with the distinctions that matter for this debate — whether "causally real" means difference-making at the level of fundamental physics; whether there is a fact of the matter about which level is the real causal level or whether causal description is level-relative; whether the macro pattern must be multiply realizable, or support its own autonomous laws, or be predictable without micro detail; and whether the in-principle computability of the micro story matters as against our merely practical inability to compute it. Please use a few stock cases alongside the careenium to anchor the questions — a traffic jam, a whirlpool, a corporation's decision — and make sure the leaves cover at least: micro-exclusivism, weak emergence or descriptive indispensability, causal pluralism about real patterns, full downward causation, a no-competition constitution-with-feedback view, a content-first symbolic realism, deflationism that denies simmballs exist at all, and one primitivist emergentist leaf. If a reader's answers dead-end somewhere your tree has no leaf, say so rather than forcing them into the nearest name.
