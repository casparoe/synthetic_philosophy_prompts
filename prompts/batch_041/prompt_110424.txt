So I'm in a reading group on philosophy of mind and we just did the Dennett stuff on the three stances -- physical, design, intentional -- plus his idea of real patterns, and it turned into a shouting match I'm still annoyed about. My position was that the physical stance is basically the ground truth: underneath it all there's just particles and fields doing their thing, and the design and intentional stances are shortcuts we lay on top because we can't do the math. Which means, I figured, that all the belief-and-desire talk is useful fiction, a compression trick, and Dennett must be some kind of instrumentalist about it. My friend says I've got him exactly backwards, that the real patterns business is supposed to be a realist position. I honestly don't see it, because if a pattern counts as real in virtue of being compressible and predictive, then it sounds like it's real in virtue of being useful, which is what I said in the first place. One of the grad students kept muttering 'but what about the Martian' and nobody would explain.

So what I want to know is: when you've got some system in front of you and you're trying to decide whether the intentional stance is telling you something true about it or just giving you a handy way of talking, how do you actually tell the difference? Is there a fact of the matter about whether a pattern is real, or does it just come down to which description you happen to find convenient? And if the physical stance really is the bottom level, doesn't that make the other two second-class in some way that matters -- like, should I end up a fictionalist about other people's minds after all, treating their beliefs as convenient fictions I adopt for prediction but don't really belive in? I'd like someone to sort out what I'm getting wrong here, because 'well it's all just atoms' isn't going to convince anyone in the group and I'd rather understand the thing than win the arguemnt.
