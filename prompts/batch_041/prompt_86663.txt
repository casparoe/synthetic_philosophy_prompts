Some friends and I came out of the same effective-altruism reading group and have spent the years since arguing in a group chat, and my friend Dana's views on duties of rescue have become a running joke and, lately, a data-collection project. Her verdicts, as I have logged them: shallow pond, child drowning, ruined shoes and phone, she must wade in. A charity letter, two hundred dollars to save the life of someone she will never meet, she must send it. The same charity after its annual target has already been met, so that another two hundred dollars goes into a reserve with no measurable effect on anyone, she needn't, though it would not be wrong. A flooded river, where reaching the child means a swim with a real chance that she drowns, she needn't. A mass emergency appeal in which her two hundred dollars is one of several million and no single gift is pivotal, but the appeal succeeds only if enough people give, she must. And a case in which a phone call would save a stranger's life at the cost of some professional embarrassment, she must, and she is impatient with anyone who treats embarrassment as a cost at all. Her record, I have noticed, already rules out one tempting story, namely that duties attach only to identified and present victims, since the distant charity case gets a must.

Next week's case, which I think breaks something: the pond again, except that four strong swimmers are already in the water, they will certainly reach the child in time, and nothing Dana does will change that; the only cost to her is a soaked outfit and a ruined phone. Does she say she must wade in anyway, or not?

What I want is her verdict on that case, then the rules that fit her record equally well — I can see at least a cost-only reading of Singer's principle, a marginal-impact reading, and a fair-share reading — what each of them predicts about the new case, which one I should treat as the rule she is actually running and on what grounds, and finally the case I should put to her next that would separate whichever two rules my new case leaves standing. Do not paper over the gaps: if her record genuinely underdetermines the answer, or if the question is confused — if, say, the word must is doing two jobs across my cases, or the pond-with-others is not actually the case that splits the readings — say so plainly and tell me what I should be asking instead. I would rather be told that my framing is broken than be handed a confident prediction.
