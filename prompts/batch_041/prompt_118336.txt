my flatmate runs a small reading group and they finished up on Zorn's lemma last week, and she wants to close the semester with a poem instead of a recap. she asked me to ask you. write her one — maybe 16 to 20 lines, no fixed meter, but i want a refrain that comes back at least three times.

the feeling shes after: Zorn's lemma is the one where if every chain in a partial order has an upper bound then the poset has a maximal element, and the sting is that maximal isnt maximum. what you get is something with nothing above it, but not something thats above everything — you can have a whole scattering of these, incomparable, each sitting at the top of its own branch, none of them ever touching. and the proof never hands you one you can point to. existence, no name.

so: the poem is spoken by one of those elements. the refrain should be some version of the promise that every chain gets a ceiling — thats the chain condition, the thing the whole lemma is conditional on. and i'd like the shape to carry it: let each stanza take up the previous stanzas last image and carry it forward, so the poem accretes like a chain under inclusion, and the final stanza is the union of all of it — i want that to be the turn, because thats the actual hinge of the proof, the point where you take the union of a chain of chains and everything depends on that union still being a chain. then the ending should be honest that it isnt above everything: it can see the others across the gap and cant reach them.

keep out: the name of the lemma, the axiom of choice, any maths notation, and any explaining of the statement inside the poem. someone who knows it should recongnise the union step and the maximal/maximum gap without being told. also dont let the chain condition off cheap — its the expensive part, and the case where it fails (a chain of finite sets that just keeps growing, so theres no ceiling anywhere) is exactly the case where no maximal element shows up. maybe that earns a line.
