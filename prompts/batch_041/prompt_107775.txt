So my ethics reading group has been going in circles for like three months on the moral saints question, and I've somehow ended up cast as one of the two poles. My friend Priya is what you'd call a morality-first person: she thinks the more morally good a life is, the better it is as a life, full stop — the saint isn't missing anything, we just have unworthy tastes that make her look dull, and if the morally best life strikes us as empty, so much the worse for us. I'm on the other side: I think there's more to a life going well than its moral score, that a life can be morally maximal and still be missing something, maybe even be worse *as a life* than a life that's a bit less morally pure but has more going on in it. The annoying part is that we agree on almost every particular case — the fight only shows up in the shape of the whole thing, so we just keep trading intuitions about the same four examples and getting nowhere. We've decided to do it properly and write the document we should have produced months ago.

So please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration that the two of us could both actually put our names to. I want the claims we can both sign — about what "moral saint" even means, about whether there's a single scale of moral goodness or several things that can come apart, about the difference (if any) between "morally better" and "better as a life," and about what moral philosophy is even supposed to be doing here — and I want them to be real claims, not "reasonable people disagree" mush. Then the disagreements, stated precisely enough that each of us would accept the wording of her own side. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling one would actually move one of us. I suspect two of them are (a) whether the moral point of view is overriding or just one standpoint among several, and (b) whether the saint's life *lacks a good* or is merely *less appealing to us* — i.e. whether we're claiming the life is worse or just that we wouldn't like it — but find others if they're there, and if part of this turns out to be purely verbal, say which part and why. For each crux, give a case or consideration that we'd both agree bears on it, with each of us writing down in advance what we'd predict it shows.

Also, two examples we got stuck on and I'd like folded in. First: two people give identical amounts to the same effective charity — one does it joyfully, it's the whole organizing center of her identity, and she has basically no other projects or relationships; the other does it more grudgingly and inconsistently, but also writes novels, has a family, travels. Is the first actually morally better, and is either life better as a life? Second: an entity — say a very capable AI, or a person, whatever — that maximizes moral value flawlessly but has no aims, attachments, or projects of its own; Priya says that's the ideal, I say something's missing. Use those, and any others you need. Put a numbered agreed-claims section first and then a table for the disagreements. (Sorry this is so long, I've been stewing on it.)
