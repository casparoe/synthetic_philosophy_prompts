Half the room are engineers who've at least heard of the prisoner's dilemma; the other half are sales, support and design folks who haven't and will tune out instantly if I start lecturing. My boss's contribution to the planning has been to say, twice now, that we just need to tell everyone to cooperate. I think that's the exact wrong move and I want the speech to show why — but I also don't want to be the guy who opens an offsite by telling everyone they're trapped. So I'd like you to write it. Treat this as a game theory problem first and a speech second: you're a game theorist who writes speeches on the side, and I'd rather you spend the first part of your answer thinking through the incentives of the actual situation before you write a word of the actual speech.

Here's what the speech has to do, and this is where I need you to be honest rather than rousing. It has to explain, in plain language and without a payoff matrix on the whiteboard, why telling people to cooperate changes nothing when the structure still rewards hoarding information and shipping your own roadmap on time — and why that isn't a character flaw in the room.
