I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size fintech and through a series of bad decisions I'm now the engineering rep on our Responsible AI review board. I dont have any formal philosophy background. We're 60 days from shipping a rules engine that turns our "responsible AI principles" into machine-checkable policy -- stuff like: if a use case involves "manipulating user emotion" then it needs legal sign-off. Here's my problem. Those principles were written as statements of what we stand for; nobody ever treated them as true-or-false claims about the world. But now they're sitting in the antecedents of conditionals, getting negated in exception clauses, joined by "or" in the disjunction rules. Our contractor philosopher says this is the Frege-Geach problem and that we've accidentally signed up for the principles being truth-apt claims, which she says is a much bigger commitment than it sounds -- if they're really just expressions of attitude, then "if manipulation is wrong, then..." isn't even the kind of thing that can be true or false, and the logic of our whole rulebook falls apart. Head of legal says that's a seminar problem and wants to ship.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate at Thursday's review board. I need a concrete recommendation -- ship as is, hold the conditional rules, or ship with some restriction -- plus a timeline, and I need to survive head of legal reading it, so no hand-waving about modus ponens. And please dont just tell me to "convert them into factual claims"; tell me honestly what that costs us, because I suspect it costs more than legal thinks, and give the strongest version of legal's position rather than a strawman.
