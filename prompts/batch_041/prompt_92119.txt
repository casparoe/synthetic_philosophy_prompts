Ok, I've been trying to name a thing for a while and I keep failing, so maybe you can help me.

Example one. A politician gets asked about a rival and says, "Look, I'm not gonna get into all that. I'm just saying it's funny how nobody's ever seen the long-form certificate." He never actually asserts anything false. He barely asserts anything at all. But everyone walks out of the room thinking the rival might not be eligible, and if you press him he goes "I didn't say that. I said it was funny. You're the one who jumped to a conclusion."

Example two, smaller scale. A coworker who doesn't want a colleague promoted says at the meeting, "It's really impressive how Sarah manages to be at literally every meeting." With a certain look. Everyone hears "she's got no time left for actual work." If Sarah corners her afterwards: "I was paying her a compliment! I can't control what you read into it."

What gets me about both is that the speaker is doing something with the listener's own inference. They set the thought up and then hand the actual thinking over to you, so when it goes badly they can point at you and say the thought was yours, not theirs. It's not lying, since nothing false got said. It's not a threat or a bribe. It's also not just being vague -- the vagueness is doing work, it's engineered.

Example three, and this is definately the one that made me write this down. My mum said of my cousin, "well, she's certainly found a nice boy," and I'm fairly sure she meant it warmly. My aunt heard a whole verdict about the boy being a step down. My mum was genuinely hurt that anyone heard that. So is that the same move or not? In the first two the speaker seems to be in control of the thing; here it's the listener doing something to the speaker.

A friend told me this is just Gricean implicature and I should stop overthinking it. But implicature seems way too broad -- we implicate stuff alot, and usually it's innocent, and usually if you ask the speaker "did you mean X" they'll just say yes. The weird part here is the disavowal, the way the meaning is real enough to land but not real enough to be owned. I've also heard "dog whistle," but that seems to be about a specific in-group catching a coded message, and here the message is meant for everyone in the room.

So: is there a name for this exact move? And I don't just want the label, I want to know where the lines are. What makes the implication stick to the speaker instead of sliding off onto the listener? Are the politician case and the coworker case really one phenomenon or two? And if there's no settled name, can you give me one and define it so I can tell it apart from lying, from bullshitting, from dog whistles, and from plain old implicature?
