For some years now I've followed the rationalist AI-safety literature on corrigibility and the shutdown problem — utility indifference, the off-switch game, the recurring suspicion that corrigibility is "anti-natural" — and I've come to think most of the arguments are downstream of a specification nobody has actually written down. Before I read another proposal, I want the desiderata. Write the full list of requirements that any adequate account of a corrigible agent must meet.

For each requirement, give: the requirement stated as a testable condition on the agent's behavior or policy; the case or consideration that motivates it; whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp (e.g., those who want the agent to defer even to mistaken operators versus those who want it to ignore a shutdown it judges catastrophic); and the class of proposals it rules out.

The list should at least cover: the agent must not prevent its own shutdown; must not cause or incentivize others to prevent it; must not manipulate or deceive its operators to avoid shutdown; must remain useful enough to be worth running in the first place; must handle uncertainty about whether a given shutdown signal is genuine and legitimate; and must not drift into incorrigibility as a side effect of becoming more capable or more confident. I'm also after two distinctions I have not seen made cleanly, so treat them explicitly: corrigibility that holds by construction (the agent simply has no stake in continuing) versus corrigibility that holds by incentive (the agent would prefer to continue but is built to comply anyway), and whether an agent can be required to be corrigible toward a specific operator or only toward whoever currently holds the switch.

Then the part I most want: identify any subset of these desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, and say why — for instance the tension between an agent that never acts on a shutdown decision and an agent that should refuse a shutdown it can foresee would be catastrophic. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive proposals.

Format: a table with columns Requirement | Motivating case | Status (non-negotiable or camp preference) | What it excludes; then a short JSON object with fields "irreconcilable_pairs" (a list, each entry an object with "requirement_a", "requirement_b", "why"), "load_bearing_requirement" (string), and "by_construction_vs_by_incentive" (one or two sentences giving your verdict). Skip the scorecard of existing proposals; I'll do that myself.
