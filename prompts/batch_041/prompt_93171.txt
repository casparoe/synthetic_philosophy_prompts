We probe a large language model's internal states and its verbal reports in order to recover what it "takes to be the case" about a small scenario — a locked box, a shuffled deck — and my PI wants to write the results up as evidence about the model's beliefs. I keep objecting that this runs together two very different claims, and I'd like your actual credence on the sharper one, with the reasoning laid out rather than a tour of the literature.

Take this proposition: for at least one currently existing large language model, the intentional stance is not merely a useful predictive fiction but is literally correct — that is, there is some belief, with determinate content, that the system genuinely holds, rather than only a pattern we can profitably describe as if it did. Give me your credence in that, decomposed. I want at least these terms: the probability that the realist-versus-instrumentalist dispute about the intentional stance is itself a genuine question with a determinate answer, rather than a verbal dispute (my colleague insists it is the latter, and that asking whether the stance is correct is a category mistake); the probability that mental content can be naturalized at all, given the familiar indeterminacy worries; the probability that, conditional on both of those going your way, any current architecture satisfies whatever a correct theory of belief actually demands — a functional role, causal sensitivity to the facts, persistence across paraphrase, and whatever else you think is required; and any further term you believe belongs. For context, the operational criterion the lab has been using — we have been calling it robust attribution — is that the system believes that P just in case there is a projectible pattern under which treating it as believing P predicts its outputs better than any belief-free model of comparable complexity, and that pattern survives paraphrase and small counterfactual perturbations of the scenario; I genuinely cannot tell whether that criterion tracks something real or is just a dressed-up version of the instrumentalist move, so say where you think it lands. Finally, what result from our own probing experiments should move your number most, and in which direction?
