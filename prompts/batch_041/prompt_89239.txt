Ok hobbyist question. I play a lot of poker and read decision-theory stuff for fun, and there's a pattern I keep running into that definately has a name somewhere, but I can't find it.

Four examples, and I half suspect they're not all the same thing:

1) My uncle took a genetic test and found out he has a big elevated risk of something with no treatment and no prevention. He says he'd have been happier not knowing. But before the test it seemed obvious to take it — knowing more can't hurt you, right?

2) Poker. Knowing exactly what my opponent is holding is great, until he knows that I know, at which point I'm just predictable. There are spots where I'd honestly pay to be able to promise that I'll forget it. The problem isn't the information, it's that I can't stop myself from acting on it.

3) A friend in a drug trial worked out from her side effects which arm she was in, and after that the drug seemed to stop working as well for her. The knowing wrecked the thing being known about.

4) Insider trading. The info is worth a fortune while it's mine alone and exactly zero the moment it's public. So whatever its worth is, it isn't a property of the information, it's a property of who else has it.

I have a vague memory of some theorem saying that a rational agent can never be made worse off by free information, that it should always be willing to look. But all four of these look like the information left someone worse off, and I think they're each breaking something different — one is about before versus after, one is about being unable to commit to ignoring it, one is about the information being part of its own subject matter, one is about other people having it too.

So: is there one name for the whole cluster, or seperate names for the parts? And where does that never-hurts result actually draw its line — which assumption is each of my cases violating? I don't really care about the labels, I care about the seams: what the boundaries are, which of my four are genuinely the same phenomenon and which only look alike, and whether there's a fifth thing I'm lumping in here that ought to be kept apart.
