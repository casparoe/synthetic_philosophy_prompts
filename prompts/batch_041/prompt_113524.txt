Bit of an odd ask. I write about science and ideas for a weekend magazine, and my editor has definately decided that the philosophy issue needs a joke in it. So: write me a joke about epiphenomenalism, the view that conscious experience is perfectly real and does absolutely no work — a glow riding along on top of the brain events that actually cause everything.

Please don't do the usual thing (steam whistle on the train, lights on but nobody home). I want the joke to turn on the paradox of phenomenal judgement, which is the genuinely awkward corner of the view: if my experiences never cause anything, then my thoughts, and my sentences, about my experiences can't be caused by the experiences they're about. So either the qualia are doing some causal work after all, or my entire inner life as reported is an unbelievable coincidence — the words match the feelings for no reason whatsoever.

And here's the setting I want, because every version I've seen is two philosophers in armchairs: a courtroom. A witness is on the stand describing the pain they felt at the time of the incident, and the cross-examination goes straight at that corner — if the pain was real but couldn't move a single neuron, then the pain isn't what's testifying, so what is, and how does it know what it's talking about?

Make the punchline actually turn on the argument — someone who's read alot of philosophy of mind should laugh, not just nod at the word 'consciousness'. A second version is welcome if you land on a better one. And please don't get there by making epiphenomenalism look obviously stupid; it's a live position, not a straw man, and the joke should work even for someone who half believes it.
