Next month I'm presenting a paper at our department colloquium and I want a Q&A prep sheet for the question period, not a summary of my own argument.

The paper: I argue that the spectrum arguments for the intransitivity of "better than" don't actually establish intransitivity — they establish that betterness is vague/incomplete. My setup is a trilemma. Take a chain where each item differs from its neighbor by one tiny step (my case is a thousand possible careers for the same person, each one swapping a single month of meaningful work for a month of drudgery, from a great career at one end to a grinding one at the other; the ends are clearly ranked, no adjacent pair seems to differ in value at all). The spectrum argument says that transitivity plus the "no adjacent pair differs" intuition forces the absurd conclusion that the grinding career is at least as good as the great one, so transitivity has to go. I say you have exactly three options: give up tolerance (somewhere there's a sharp line between "better" and "not better"), give up transitivity, or give up bivalence (some adjacent comparisons are neither true nor false). I take the third. The middle of the chain is genuinely indeterminate, so betterness is incomplete rather than intransitive, and the argument's slide from "not better" to "worse or equal" is the hidden premise doing all the work. I close by arguing that the usual exploitability/money-pump objection to incomplete betterness can be met by an agent who commits in advance to refusing to trade her way down an indeterminate chain, and that this is more or less what people do anyway.

The audience: eight or so decision theorists/economists from the theory group, several of whom treat completeness and transitivity as axioms of rationality and will not be shy about saying so. Three population ethicists who live in the repugnant-conclusion literature and are allergic to vagueness moves. One semanticist from upstairs who works on comparatives and degree semantics and thinks every one of these debates is secretly about the meaning of "better". Plus a first-year who has read the whole sorites litrature and will try to show I've just reinvented it.
