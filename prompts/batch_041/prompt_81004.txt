I am a PhD student in algorithmic fairness with a background in microeconomics. I am trying to understand the regulatory implications of the impossibility results for group fairness. Please describe the most important implications of those results for a regulator that must certify a credit-scoring model before deployment, where certification requires satisfying one of three criteria: demographic parity (equal positive classification rates across protected groups), equalized odds (equal true positive and false positive rates across protected groups), or predictive parity (equal precision across protected groups). Assume the lender may retrain the model after certification on the post-certification distribution, applicants may costlessly alter any non-protected feature they report, and the regulator observes only a finite validation sample and cannot inspect the model's internals. Which of the impossibility results remain binding under these dynamic and strategic conditions, which are artifacts of a static setting, and what are the most important implications for the design of a certification regime that remains stable after deployment? In particular, what should the regulator do instead of attempting to satisfy multiple fairness criteria simultaneously, and what does this imply for the feasibility of meaningful post-deployment fairness audits?
