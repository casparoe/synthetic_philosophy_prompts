I direct a small formal-epistemology group — myself and one postdoc, three years of funded time — and we have to commit to a research programme on uncertain evidence. The probabilistic-programming group down the corridor keeps asking which of our open problems they should be tracking, and I would rather answer that with an argument than with a shrug. So I want an agenda, not a survey: rank the open questions in the theory of uncertain evidence by how much depends on them, and defend the ranking with the criteria made explicit — which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable with the tools already on the table, which would change what people outside philosophy do if they were settled, and which look central but would turn out to be idle once settled.

Here, in no particular order, are the candidates I have been circling.

1. Order-dependence. Shift your probabilities on one partition, then on another, and you can land somewhere different from where you would have landed had you taken them in the other order. Is that a defect in the rule, a defect in the way I have posed the question, or a fact about uncertain evidence that ought simply to be accepted?

2. Rigidity. What fixes the partition whose probabilities the rule holds fixed? Nothing internal to the rule appears to, and different choices give different posteriors.

3. A colleague's proposal: take all the constraints the evidence imposes, and select, from among the distributions satisfying them, the one closest to your prior in Kullback–Leibler divergence. This is order-independent by construction. Is it also correct, or does it purchase invariance at a price we should refuse to pay?

4. Reduction. Can every such shift be rewritten as ordinary conditionalization on a proposition about one's own evidence — a proposition to the effect that the evidence is thus and so — and if so, is the rule a convenience rather than a necessity, or does the rewriting smuggle in a certainty about the evidence that we do not have?

5. Foundations. Is there a betting or accuracy argument that singles out one rule, or does the family of admissible updating rules remain underdetermined by any such argument?

6. Representation. Should uncertain evidence be modelled as a shift within a single distribution at all, or rather as a constraint on a set of them?

What I want back is a defended ordering, with reasons for the top two or three, a sentence each on why the ones I have ranked low are low, and — most useful to me — the conditions under which the ranking would change: which result in the next year or two would show that I have mis-ranked the list. Assume I know the standard material and do not need to be told what the rule is. Skip the reading list; I want the reasoning.
