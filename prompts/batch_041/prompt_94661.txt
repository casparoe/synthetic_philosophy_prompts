I share an office with a philosopher who has an opinion about everything and a theory about nothing — she'll give you a verdict on any case you put to her but won't tell you the rule she's using — and I've spent alot of the last month trying to reverse-engineer her view on knowledge and probability in the gaps between my own seminar prep and the pile of grading I keep ignoring. Her verdicts so far, as faithfully as I can jot them down: the holder of a single ticket in a ten-million-ticket lottery doesn't know her ticket will lose, and that goes for every ticket taken one at a time; a woman who thinks the bus that hit her was blue because eighty percent of the buses in town are blue doesn't know it was blue; a doctor who concludes a patient is disease-free because the test is 99.99% accurate and came back negative doesn't know that either; but she'll happily grant that you know the red mug is on the desk, that your friend is in the next room because you just heard him, that the sky is blue because you looked, and whatever a generally reliable person tells you. On the preface kind of case she's untroubled: the author of a book with a hundred carefully supported claims is rational to believe each one and rational to expect that at least one of them is wrong, and she dont think that's a contradiction. I can't tell whether she's running one rule or two, and I can't tell which.

So here's the case I want to put to her next, and I'd like you to predict her answer before I do. She's watching a friend about to flip a fair coin a hundred times, and she says that it won't come up heads every time — does she know it won't? I keep going back and fourth, because I can think of at least two quite different rules that fit every verdict above and I'm fairly sure they come apart here: one about whether a belief that rests on nothing but odds and frequencies can ever count as knowledge, however lopsided the odds, and one about whether knowledge has to be safe, i.e. whether she'd have believed the same thing in the nearby ways the world could have gone. Predict her verdict on the coin case, lay out the candidate rules and what each one predicts, tell me which you'd bet she's actually running and why, and then tell me the single further case you'd put to her next if you wanted to force those two rules apart for good. I'm less interested in who's right about knowledge than in getting her view pinned down, so please don't just tell me what a sensible epistemologist would say.
