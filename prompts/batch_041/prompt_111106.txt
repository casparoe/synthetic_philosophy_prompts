ok so my decision theory reading group is doing a session on the absent-minded driver and i said id bring a poem instead of another handout, which i may regret. heres the setup, roughly, in case its not on your radar: a guy is driving a road with two exits. hes absent-minded in a specific technical way — when he reaches an intersection he has no memory of whether he already passed the first exit, so he cant tell if hes at the first one or the second one. exiting at the first is worthless. exiting at the second is the good outcome, the one he wants. if he keeps going past both he gets a small consolation, not nothing but not the prize. and hes stuck with a single policy he fixed before he set out, the same policy at every intersection, because a policy that said "if its the first, do X; if its the second, do Y" would be useless to him — he cant tell which one hes in.

write me a pantoum from inside his head. four or five quatrains. you know the rule: lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the last stanza brings back the opening stanza's lines so the thing closes in a loop. i want the loop to be doing the philosophical work, not just decorating it. the same repeated line should mean something different each time it lands — early on, stopping sounds like a waste, later it sounds like exactly the right call, and he has no way to know which reading he is currently in. the form should make the reader feel the thing the driver cant do, which is tell where he is.

id also like the split between the self who sets the policy in advance and the self standing at the intersection to be in there somehow, maybe as two "i"s or two tones that keep interrupting each other. the whole headach of the problem is that the planner and the guy at the wheel seem to reason correctly and reach different answers, and i dont want the poem to pretend one of them wins.

dont resolve it. no final stanza where he picks the optimal probability and drives off into the sunset. the last line landing back on the first should feel like the loop is the problem — arriving and leaving are indistinguishable from inside, and the form cant tell them apart either.

accuracy matters: someone who has spent a term on this should recognize their own confusion in it. the payoff structure i described, the single memoryless policy, the disagreement between planning and acting — all of that should be visible in the poem without being explained in a footnote. and it should still be a poem, not a proof in verse.
