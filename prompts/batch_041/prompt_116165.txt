I have spent several months collecting verdicts from a colleague — a metaphysician I have been arguing with about dispositions — on a run of cases that each ask whether a given object is fragile, that is, disposed to break if struck. Her verdicts so far, every one of them given with confidence:

1. An ordinary drinking glass, dropped on a tiled floor: fragile.
2. The same glass enclosed in a device that, the instant the glass is struck, projects a field that holds it rigid, so that it does not break: still fragile.
3. The same glass packed in foam, so that a drop leaves it intact: still fragile.
4. A tough plastic tumbler fitted with a device that shatters it the instant it is struck: not fragile.
5. The same glass sealed in a display case, where it will never be struck or dropped: still fragile.
6. A drinking glass that has been through a toughening treatment and would survive a drop on tile, though it looks and feels exactly like ordinary glass: not fragile.

Her verdicts look to me to be jointly consistent with more than one account of what fragility consists in, and I cannot tell which account she is running; I am interested in the rule she is following, not in whether she is right. The case I mean to put to her next is this: a glass of ordinary composition and appearance, except that its material is one that, at the instant it is struck, undergoes a phase change of its own — triggered by the impact — that renders it tough, so that no crack ever forms and it would not break. There is no device and no field here; the change is the material's own response to being struck.

Please tell me what she will say about that glass; give me an inventory of the rules that fit everything she has said so far, with what each of them predicts in the new case; give me your best guess among those predictions, with your reasons for preferring the rule behind it; and, since I would rather settle the matter than guess, name one further case I could put to her that would separate the rules you have identified, saying what each verdict on it would show. If her record genuinely leaves the question open, say so, and say what she would be committing herself to on either side.
