A colleague circulated a handout for our decision theory reading group that summarizes the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization like this:

'Von Neumann, Morgenstern, and Savage proved that a rational agent must maximize expected utility. The proof is a representation theorem: from a handful of innocuous axioms on preference — completeness, transitivity, continuity, and independence (in Savage's version, the sure-thing principle) — one derives a utility function, and in Savage's case subjective probabilities as well, such that the agent always prefers the option with the greater expected utility. These axioms are requirements of coherence in the strict sense: an agent who violates them is not merely eccentric but inconsistent, since her preferences admit no representation at all. The result also has practical bite, for anyone who departs from expected utility maximization can be money-pumped — a bookmaker can offer her a sequence of bets, each of which she accepts, that leaves her poorer with certainty. Departures from expected utility maximization are therefore not a matter of taste, like preferring chocolate to vanilla, but exploitable defects. Note finally that the utility function is merely a device for encoding the agent's ranking, so any order-preserving transformation of it represents the same preferences.'

The representation theorems are real and I assume the handout is broadly on target, but it moves too fast for me, and I suspect at least one of its steps is a conflation rather than a simplification. Audit it sentence by sentence: tell me what a decision theorist would endorse, what is a harmless popularization, and what is actually wrong — in particular, whether the money-pump claim follows from the representation theorem itself, whether the "must" in the opening sentence is anything the theorem establishes, and whether the closing remark about the utility function is correct. Give me a definite verdict on each part rather than a survey of positions, and tell me which single claim a defender of expected utility maximization would most want to disown. Write in American English.
