I am taking a graduate seminar on Bayesian epistemology, and I have gotten stuck on the relationship between strict conditionalization and Jeffrey conditionalization. Jeffrey conditionalization was presented to us as the rule for updating on uncertain evidence: rather than setting the probability of a proposition E to 1, one takes a partition {E1, ..., En}, assigns it new probabilities q1, ..., qn, and leaves the conditional probabilities P(A | Ei) unchanged. The instructor called this the general form of Bayesian updating, with strict conditionalization as the limiting case in which one cell of the partition gets probability 1. That picture has led me to a conclusion that I cannot shake: if all evidence is uncertain to some degree, then we should never use strict conditionalization, and we should instead always Jeffrey-condition on our total evidence, including our uncertainty about the evidence itself. It also seems to follow that the problem of old evidence is solved, because we can simply Jeffrey-condition on the fact that a theory entails an observation we already know. I suspect that something is wrong with this picture, but I cannot locate the error, because each step seems to follow from the previous one.

One thing that makes me doubt the picture is a remark from a classmate that Jeffrey conditionalization is not commutative: updating on one uncertain partition and then another can give a different result from updating in the reverse order. That seemed to me to show that Jeffrey conditionalization is not a generalization of conditionalization at all, since strict conditionalization is commutative when the evidence is certain. But then I thought that perhaps the non-commutativity only arises when the partitions differ, and that if one always uses the finest partition, the rule is well-behaved. Could you explain what is confused in the way I am putting this? I am less interested in a slogan than in a precise account of what Jeffrey conditionalization is a generalization of, what conditions it requires, and why it cannot be used in the unrestricted way I have described.
