I'm a backend engineer, no formal philosophy background, and for the last few months I've been running a game with some friends that's basically Nomic — every turn someone proposes a change to the rules themselves and we all vote on it — and we've hit a snag I can't get past, so I want to know if the thing I'm circling has a proper name or if it's just us being sloppy.

Here's the pattern, in a few flavors. We had a rule saying amendments pass by simple majority. Someone proposed changing that very rule to two-thirds, it passed with about 60% — a majority but not two-thirds — and now nobody can agree whether the change is valid: the new rule seems to say the vote that created it was too weak, the old rule says it was fine, and we only "resolved" it by declaring, out loud, that the old rule governed its own replacement. That declaration isn't in any rule; we just asserted it. Then a friend proposed a rule reading "this rule may never be amended," and we can't even decide if such a rule can be in force, since the only way to test it is to try to change it. But the thing that really bugs me is the general shape underneath all of this: every time we amend the rule about how amendments work, there's a genuine instant where it's undetermined which version of the rules is "in force" for that single act — old or new — and we always paper over it by whoever's loudest saying "old rules for this vote, new rules after." It feels like every time we touch the meta-rules we have to step outside the rules for a second, and that step is pure fiat, not rule-following, and the rules are totally silent about who gets to make it. I keep thinking of it like a config file that controls how config files get deployed — at some point something has to run outside the pipeline, and nothing in the pipeline says what.

I've heard of "the paradox of self-amendment," and something from legal theory about a basic rule that a legal system just presupposes instead of deriving, but neither seems to be exactly what I mean. The self-amendment thing sounds like it's about a rule contradicting itself, and the legal-theory thing sounds like it's about where a whole legal system's validity bottoms out, whereas mine is narrower and more practical: that whenever a rule-system revises its own revision-procedure, there's an unavoidable instant of raw power the rules can't cover, plus the rules say nothing about who wields it. Does this have a name, and if not, can you name and define it and tell me exactly how it differs from the self-amendment paradox, from that "basic rule" idea, and from plain old entrenchment (a rule that just makes itself hard to change)? Don't just hand me a label — I want the boundries: what counts, what doesn't, where the edges are. And since I might be fooling myself, tell me what would change your verdict — what would make you say "actually this is just the self-amendment paradox after all," or "there's no seperate phenomenon here, you're describing an ordinary gap every rule system has." Keep it under 500 words.
