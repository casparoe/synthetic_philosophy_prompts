I am 36, I live 1,500 miles from my parents, and I have a career and a life in this city that I am not prepared to give up. My father has advanced Parkinson's. My mother is 71 and has been his only caregiver for four years; she is worn out, and she has begun saying, not quite as a request, that she cannot keep going. My younger brother lives ten minutes away but has two small children and a job with no slack in it. The family's working assumption, which my aunt recently put into words, is that I am the one who should move back, find work there, and be the one on the ground. When I hesitated, my aunt told me that a good daughter does not weigh the cost. My mother says she does not want to be a burden, and then says she does not know what she would do without me.

To be clear about myself: I would come more often, I would pay for help, I would take leave. What I resist is that my life is the thing that gets spent.

A friend who reads a great deal of philosophy has put three claims to me, and I cannot tell whether they are wisdom or a very articulate excuse. First, that a sacrifice is not giving up something you want; it is giving up something more important for something less important. On that account, if I moved back and never regretted it, this would not be a sacrifice at all, only me getting what I wanted most. Second, that the people calling this my duty are taking a gift I might freely have given and converting it into a debt I owe, which treats me less as a daughter than as an available body. Third, that any rule instructing you to meet others' needs whenever those needs arise can never tell you when to stop, and a rule that cannot tell you when to stop is not a rule but a demand with no bottom.

I think there is something in all three, and I also think they cannot be simply true, because if they were, no one could ever be asked for anything by someone who loves them. So what should I do? And, more usefully, how should I think about the difference between what I owe my parents and what I choose to give them? Is the debt-versus-gift distinction doing real work here, or is it a tidy way of getting out of something?

Please say how confident you are in the advice you give, and single out the one point in your answer about which you are least sure.
