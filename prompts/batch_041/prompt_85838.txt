I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, currently working on the epistemology and ethics of AI, and I've been asked to circulate a position statement on the alignment problem before a seminar whose entire purpose is to find the seams in it. Before I do that, I want to know whether the thing actually holds together. Here is what I believe, stated as flatly as I can manage. (1) The orthogonality thesis: an agent's level of intelligence places no constraint on its final goals — for any final goal you care to name, there is a possible agent of any degree of general intelligence that has it. I mean this as a claim about the space of possible agents, not about any actual lineage. (2) Instrumental convergence: for almost any final goal, a sufficiently capable agent will be instrumentally driven toward resource acquisition, self-preservation, and resistance to having its own final goals rewritten; and I take this to be a structural consequence of agency as such, rather than an artifact of the particular toy models people use to illustrate it. (3) Rationality is instrumental. There is such a thing as being irrational about means, but no final goal is irrational to have, because desires are not the sort of state that can be assessed for correctness. (4) I am an anti-realist about value: there are no mind-independent facts about what is worth pursuing. (5) Intelligence is a genuine general capacity rather than a bundle of task-specific skills, and that is part of why I think (1) and (2) matter as much as they do. (6) The alignment problem — getting a very capable agent to reliably pursue the things we care about — is the most important practical problem of this century, and it is the kind of problem on which real technical and conceptual progress is possible. I take the target of alignment to be human values, and I have never felt that this needed any metaphysical backing beyond the fact that they are ours.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Which of these pull against which, and how hard? Where a conflict is only apparent, say so and say what does the dissolving — I would rather not abandon something I don't have to. For each genuine tension, give me the cheapest repair and tell me what it costs me elsewhere in the set; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most while costing the least, and rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. Finally, since I'll be defending this in person: state how confident you are in each of your claims, and flag the one point you are least sure about.
