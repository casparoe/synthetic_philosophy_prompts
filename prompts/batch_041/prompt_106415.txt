I'm a first-year PhD student who's somehow ended up working on Wittgenstein's scattered remarks about religious belief — the notes his students took from the lectures, the odd lines scattered through his notebooks, that kind of thing. Everyone keeps telling me the area is basically worked out and I should just pick a corner and write, but I don't believe it.

Heres what got me going. Alot of the secondary stuff seems to split into two camps: people who read him as saying religious belief is a different *kind* of thing — not answerable to evidence, so the believer and the atheist aren't even really disagreeing — and people who read him as saying something weaker, just that religious belief gets *used* differently, with no claim that it's insulated from evidence. I've started to suspect the second camp quietly assumes the first camp's conclusion and then denies it, so the whole is-he-a-fideist industry might be running on a distinction that doesn't carve anything. I also can't tell what happens to disagreement on this view — two people who each treat their belief as a form of life but disagree with each other. If form of life is doing the work, that looks like it can only get settled by conversion rather than argument, and I don't know if that's his actual view or just a bad consequence people have pinned on him. And I want to test all of it against a concrete case: one person who says "I believe in God" and means roughly "I'm committing to treat the world as meaningful," versus someone who says the same words as a straight factual claim. Same belief or different ones on his view, and does the answer even hinge on which of the two readings above is right?

What I actually want from you is a prioritization, not a survey. I've got about three years, I'm a text-and-argument person rather than an empirical one, and I want to spend them on whatever is genuinely upstream — the questions that have to be settled before the fideism fight, or the is-religion-immune-to-criticism fight, even makes sense. So: which open questions here matter most, in what order, and why? Which are tractable for one person doing close reading, which look central but are really downstream noise, and what would move a question up or down my list over the next year or two? I want the reasoning about importance and tractability, not a reading list or citations — I already know the literature.
