For several years now I have been trying to decide, each winter, where to send a modest sum of money, and without having read any of the formal literature on the subject I have settled on a crude rule of thumb. I look for a problem that touches a great many people, that I have some realistic hope of helping, and that almost nobody else seems to be attending to. The first two parts of that rule I trust. The third is where I keep tripping, and I would like to know whether the thing that trips me has a name.

Here is the trouble. I have been treating "almost nobody is attending to it" as straightforwardly good news, but I have come to believe that the phrase conceals two situations which are not alike at all. In the first, a problem sits unattended because it has simply never occurred to anyone to look; it is off the map, and a little attention would go a long way. In the second, a problem sits unattended because people did look, tried, failed, and moved on; the quiet around it is not virgin territory but old battlefield, and the silence is the residue of abandonment. From where I sit the two are indistinguishable: few people, little money, almost nothing written down. If I score a cause by how empty the field looks, I will be drawn to the abandoned one exactly as strongly as to the unexplored one, when the abandoned one is precisely what I should approach with the most caution.

I have felt this twice. I once gave to a rare-disease charity that turned out to be underfunded largely because the disease had defeated several serious research programs, not because it had been overlooked; and I once gave to something that genuinely no one had thought about, where a small grant did real work. The two felt identical when I was choosing between them. I have since noticed what seems to me the same distinction wearing different clothes elsewhere: in the way businesses speak of an untapped market, which may be either a frontier or a graveyard, and in the way a research question can look untouched from a distance while being, up close, a question that people quietly stopped asking.

What I want to know is whether there is an established name for this, for the difference between an emptiness that is empty because it is unexplored and one that is empty because it has been found impassable, and, more than the name, how the line should actually be drawn. Are the two cases as separable as I have made them, or can a cause be both at once, or slide from one to the other over time? What would a careful person look for in order to tell which kind of silence they are standing in? I would also like to know what the nearest existing ideas are, so that I can see how far my distinction sits from them and whether it simply collapses into something already named. A bare label would not satisfy me; I am after the carving.
