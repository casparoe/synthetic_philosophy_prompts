I chair our state's parole board and we have to lock the earned-release criteria before the new statute takes effect March 1. The statute tells us to weigh both "the seriousness of the offense and the offender's culpability" and "the likelihood of reoffending and the interests of victims" — which is exactly why we keep talking past each other about whether we're handing people what they deserve or just protecting the public. Were split on one line of the draft: it says nobody gets earned release unless they "acknowledge the harm they caused and show genuine remorse," which knocks out anyone still maintaining their innocence — right now that includes two people with live appeals and one whose conviction several of us quietly doubt. Two members want to scrap the remorse criterion and go on the risk score alone (our tool already spits out an "acceptance of responsibility" subscore, which is part of how we got here). Two others want to keep the criterion but also let a victim's statement that they've forgiven the offender count toward earlier release, which I think is a mess. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's vote: a clear call on the remorse criterion and on the victim-forgiveness idea, with reasons the dissenting members could actually answer and an honest pass at the strongest argument against my side. Also tell us whether to adopt whatever you recommend now or pilot it for a year, and say whether we're even entitled to reward remorse when offering early release hands everyone an obvious reason to fake it.
