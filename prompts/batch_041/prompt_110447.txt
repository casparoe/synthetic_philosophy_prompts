You are a philosopher of technology who has also worked professionally in applied environmental assessment, and you are used to switching between the two idioms without letting one quietly absorb the other. I need a translation exercise done honestly, with the residue reported rather than smoothed over.

Background on me. I have spent about a decade as a practitioner of environmental impact assessment: scoping, baseline characterization, significance determinations, the mitigation hierarchy, cumulative-effects analysis, monitoring and adaptive management. That vocabulary is how I think about any intervention in a complex system; it is fluent, not borrowed. I have only a tourist's knowledge of AI alignment.

Background on the position I want restated. There is a research program in AI safety that aims at agents which have minimal impact on the world — agents that pursue whatever objective they are given while leaving everything else as close to undisturbed as possible. Its central problem is the one you would expect: an agent can only avoid disturbing things it has some way of representing as things to be left alone, and any concrete measure of impact seems to presuppose a description of what matters and how much. The proposals I have encountered come in roughly three shapes. First, compare what the agent actually does with what would have happened under a default or null action, penalizing the difference between the real trajectory and the do-nothing trajectory. Second, measure whether the agent shrinks the set of futures that other agents could reach, treating an action as harmful when it forecloses options that would otherwise have been open. Third, measure whether the agent preserves the utility attainable under a spread of objectives it might have had instead of its actual one, on the theory that a genuinely neutral agent is one that does not privilege its own objective over alternatives. I care about the ideas, not about the labels or who proposed them.

What I want from you.

First, restate this program — the problem, and the three families of proposed measures — in the vocabulary of environmental impact assessment, as faithfully as you can manage: no-action alternative and baseline, project effects versus cumulative effects, indirect and induced effects, significance thresholds, the mitigation hierarchy, offsets and the additionality problem, monitoring, adaptive management, and whatever else of mine actually does work here.

Second, the residue report. Go family by family and say which parts of the AI proposal have a clean counterpart in my vocabulary, which have to be distorted or stretched to fit, and which have no expression in it at all. I am especially interested in three questions. (a) Does my discipline contain a solution to the baseline problem, or does it merely institutionalize it? The no-action alternative is the device we use to make effects comparable across projects, but it is itself a counterfactual we construct, and in practice it is often the most contested object in a review. (b) The AI proposals want impact measured against a world in which the agent does nothing. My practice has no such option: the assessor is not a party to the project, and the proponent cannot be modelled as absent. Does that difference matter, or is it only a matter of who is holding the pen? (c) In my field, significance is explicitly a value judgment disciplined procedurally — by scoping, by regulation, by public comment — rather than by a formal measure. The AI proposals want the analogous thing to be formal and computable. Is that a real disagreement about whether the value specification can be avoided, or a disagreement about who gets to supply it?

Third, your verdict on the residue: for each thing that fails to translate, say whether the failure reveals a gap in my framework or reveals that the AI proposal was vaguer than it sounded. Say in which direction you think each translation is lossy, and where you would expect a practitioner of my discipline and a researcher in that program to talk past each other.

Ground rules. I would rather have an honest this-does-not-translate than a tidy mapping, and I will not be impressed by a correspondence table. Do not import the assumptions of either framework and call it translation. Where the two literatures use the same word for different work — impact, baseline, significance, mitigation — flag it rather than letting it pass. Begin by writing out, for yourself, the two vocabularies as separate lists of primitives, before you attempt any matching, and show me that working; I want to see the mapping being built, not just its output.

Finally, on confidence: separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the subject. Where something is a live dispute — for instance whether a formal impact measure can avoid presupposing a value specification, or whether significance can be operationalized without a decision procedure — say so plainly instead of presenting one side as the consensus. Where you are uncertain about a fact in either literature, mark it as uncertain rather than asserting it.
