I'm a TA for an upper-level "Science, Pseudoscience, and Prediction" course — it's a mix of history and philosophy students — and I have to write one of the final exam essay questions. I want it on Malthus, but not the version students can ace by reciting "population grows geometrically, food arithmetically, then the Industrial Revolution happened and he was wrong." What I actually want them to chew on is the "right but early" move. A mate of mine keeps insisting in the pub that Malthus was basically correct and the theory just hasn't been given enough time, and I want students to judge whether that's a legitimate scientific stance or a rescue that immunizes the theory from any possible refutation. So the question has to force them to pull apart three different defensive moves: (a) saying the prediction's time horizon was simply too short, (b) narrowing the theory's scope so it only claims to hold absent certain kinds of technological change, and (c) re-describing the mechanism so that observed non-famines count as evidence of a *suppressed* equilibrium rather than as counterexamples — and then say which of those, if any, is a genuine empirical move and which is just ad hoc patching. I'd like them to bring in a Popperian falsificationist reading and a Lakatos-style progressive-vs-degenerating question, plus the Duhem-Quine point that you can always protect a core claim by blaming an auxiliary assumption.

Level-wise: they've done Popper and Lakatos in lectures and they know the demographic transition happened, so don't assume any extra reading and dont build the question around specific citations — no one can look anything up in the exam hall.

Please write the essay question itself (it should be answerable in about 90 minutes), a model answer of around 800 words, and a marking scheme built around six must-hit points totaling 25 marks — for each point give the marks it's worth and one line on the specific way a weak answer blows it. The A/B dividing line should hinge on whether the student can actually say what would count as evidence *against* a "right but early" claim, rather than just asserting it's unfalsifiable and stopping there.
