I'm doing a slow close read of Ghazālī on causation — the bit where fire touches cotton and he says the fire isn't what does the burning, God is, and all the rest of it. I've been keeping a running list of how he calls various cases as I go, because I want to figure out what he'd say about something he obviously never saw. Heres my list:

- fire touches cotton, cotton burns: he says the fire doesn't do it, God creates the burn. But he also says you should still put the fire to the cotton if you want a burn, and that when we say the fire burnt it we're speaking loosely, not describing what's really going on.
- Abraham thrown in the fire and it doesn't burn him: no problem, apparently, because fire never had the burning power to begin with — the ordinary burn and the miracle are the same sort of event, just different decrees.
- watching a thing happen a thousand times: he says all you ever observe is that B was with A, never that B came from A. Experience gives you conjunction, not connection.
- but he also says that once you've seen it enough you get a settled confidence it'll happen again, and that this is how everyone lives and it isn't really a mistake.
- you will your hand to move and it moves: he says the will and the power aren't the cause either, God creates the motion, same as the burn.

Now the new case, which I'm making up but which I think is the kind of thing that would actually split readings. Say a big genetic study comes out: carriers of one variant turn out to be a few percent more likely to vote for one particular party. It replicates in two samples of tens of thousands, p under .001, and there is no mechanism whatsoever — nobody can even sketch how a gene would get at a ballot choice, no pathway, no story, nothing. Two things I want:

1. Given the list above, what's my best guess at what he says — that this is a real connection (it's God's habit, and our job is just to notice the habit, mechanism or no mechanism), or that it's spurious (no nature links a gene to a ballot, so the correlation has to be an artifact of how the study was run)? 
2. And more importantly: which rule is he actually running? Because it seems to me all five items up there fit more than one reading of him, and I want to know which readings they fit, what each one predicts about my gene case, which you'd bet on and why, and then the case that would actually force the issue — ideally something where a genuine power and a mere de facto regularity come apart, so that the two readings have to give different answers.

I don't care much about the verdict itself. I care about the rules, the disagreement, and the discriminating case. Also if you think my list is already inconsistent and no single rule covers all five, say so and tell me which item is the problem.
