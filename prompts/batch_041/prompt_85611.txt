I am a professor of philosophy at a state university and the ethicist member of our state's Public Integrity Commission. The Commission must issue a written opinion in the matter of Governor Marlow, who, on the undisputed record, authorized the state police to run a covert operation against a militia group that was planning an attack. The operation used an undercover officer to supply the group with inert explosives, urge its leaders to accelerate their timetable, and rehearse a plan that the state then interdicted; the plot was stopped, and one militia member was injured during the arrests. The Governor's own counsel advised that the operation exceeded statutory authority and that the tactic of accelerating the plot came close to entrapment. The Governor proceeded anyway, left a contemporaneous note saying the legislature would never authorize what was necessary, and then publicly denied knowing about the operation, letting the state police superintendent absorb the blame until a leak forced the truth out. The Commission must decide whether the Governor violated the ethics code's prohibition on conduct that undermines public trust and, if so, what to recommend: exoneration, a finding of violation without sanction, censure, or referral for impeachment. Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before our vote. You are a political philosopher and an experienced commission counsel, so think carefully and begin by brainstorming the strongest version of each of the four dispositions before choosing one. Be precise about the difference between a genuine dirty-hands case and ordinary official misconduct, and say what work the Governor's motives, the operation's success, and the subsequent cover-up are each doing in your argument; in particular, address whether the operation's success should count in the Governor's favor or whether that is a piece of moral luck the Commission should resist. Address the strongest objection to your recommendation -- whether that the Commission would be whitewashing illegality or that it would be punishing a leader for doing what the law could not -- and explain why it does not defeat your conclusion. End with a concrete recommendation, a specific remedy if you find a violation, and a short statement of what the Commission should say publicly about the moral remainder of the operation. No citations or bibliography; I want the argument.
