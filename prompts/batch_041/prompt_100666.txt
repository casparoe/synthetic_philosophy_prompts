Exam question. Gödel's incompleteness theorems are often glossed as showing that we can never prove that arithmetic is consistent, yet working mathematicians treat the basic axioms of arithmetic, and of set theory, as secure. Write a short essay that (a) explains precisely what Gödel's second incompleteness theorem does and does not show about our prospects for establishing the consistency of arithmetic, and (b) sets out what would follow — both mathematically and epistemically — if it turned out that the basic axioms of arithmetic are in fact inconsistent.

Student's answer. Gödel's second incompleteness theorem states that no consistent, effectively axiomatized theory that is strong enough to represent arithmetic can prove its own consistency. Since Peano Arithmetic is finitely axiomatized and can encode facts about its own proofs, it follows that PA cannot prove Con(PA), the sentence asserting PA's consistency. This is what people mean when they say we cannot prove that arithmetic is consistent, and it is why Hilbert's program was doomed: Gödel's result shows that there can be no consistency proof of arithmetic at all, finitary or otherwise.

All the same, we have strong grounds for thinking arithmetic is consistent. If PA were inconsistent, then by ex falso quodlibet it would prove every sentence in its language, 0 = 1 included. A proof is a finite object, so a sufficiently diligent search through the space of possible derivations would eventually turn one up. Mathematicians have been drawing consequences from the Peano axioms for well over a century, and from ZFC for nearly as long, without ever encountering a contradiction. Since a contradiction would have surfaced by now if one were there, we can be confident — for practical purposes, certain — that the basic axioms are consistent.

Were arithmetic nonetheless inconsistent, the consequences would be catastrophic. Every sentence would become a theorem, so no arithmetical result could be trusted, and any mathematics built on arithmetic would collapse. And because PA is a subtheory of ZFC, an inconsistency in arithmetic would immediately infect set theory, and with it essentially all of mathematics.
