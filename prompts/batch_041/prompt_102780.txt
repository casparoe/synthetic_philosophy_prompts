I'm a novelist — second book, deadline I'm already past — and the whole plot hinges on a hit-and-run: a teenager drives off after clipping a cyclist, and the back half of the book is about whether he 'really' deserves what his family does to him afterward, the mother's sudden coldness, his sister refusing to be in a room with him. So I did the thing you're not supposed to do and started reading philosophy about it, mostly the Strawson essay everyone cites on freedom and resentment, and then I made the mistake of arguing about it at a dinner party, which turned into a four-month email thread with a friend who teaches the stuff, and I honestly can't tell anymore whether we're disagreeing or just missing each other. Her line is the Strawsonian one: the question 'but was he really free?' is confused from the start, because our practices of blame and resentment aren't answerable to some fact about determinism — they're just part of how creatures like us live together — and the only sensible question is whether someone is a fitting target of those attitudes, not whether he could have done otherwise in some deep metaphysical sense. My line is: fine, that describes what we do, but it doesn't tell me whether it's *fair*, whether he actually deserves it. She says 'deserves' is just the name for being the kind of creature we resent in that way. I say that's circular. She says I'm chasing a fact that doesn't exist. Lather, rinse, repeat for four months.

Here's my hunch, and I'd like you to tell me if it's right or if I'm fooling myself: I think we're answering two different questions under the single word 'responsible' — she's asking when our blaming practices are appropriate or warranted, I'm asking whether there's a desert-fact underneath them that would make them *true* rather than just licensed — and that the whole thing feels like an impasse because 'responsible' does double duty and neither of us notices. But maybe it's not verbal at all: maybe there's a real, hidden empirical question doing the work (what would actually happen to us if we stopped holding each other responsible — would trust and morality collapse, or would they just look different?), or maybe we're just sitting on bedrock intuitions, hers that the participant stance is something you can't get outside of, mine that any 'ought' needs a ground, and neither of us can argue for those without already assuming them. One more thing I keep throwing at her and she keeps dodging: I resent characters in novels, hard, even though I *know* they're just the author's creatures and could never have done otherwise — and if that's a real case of blame rather than a fake one, it seems to cut her way, but I can't tell if it's a genuine data point or just me confusing fiction with life.

So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right: is this a substantive disagreement about moral responsibility, a verbal one about the word 'deserve', two groups answering different questions under one heading, or something else entirely? Use what each of us is actually saying — don't just survey the positions — and then tell me, given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction we're both missing, a thought experiment that would settle something, a change of question, or nothing at all, in which case say why the stalemate should be expected. And be honest with me, because I have to get back to the novel and I'd like to know whether this is a wound I should stop picking at.
