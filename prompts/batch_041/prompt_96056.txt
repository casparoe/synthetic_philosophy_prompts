Something about the fast-takeoff vs slow-takeoff fight has bugged me for a while and I want a diagnosis instead of another round of it. Fast side: once systems are roughly human-level across the board the loop closes, better systems build better systems, and you go from there to way past us inside a window short enough that nobody outside the lab can react. Slow side: its just another diffusion curve, spread over years and decades, because hardware, capital, deployment, regulation, and the last-10%-is-the-hard-part all put drag on it. Both sides have heard each other's arguments alot and neither moves.

My hunch is that much of this isn't a disagreement about the world. "How fast does takeoff go" might not have an answer at all, because fast vs slow depends on which axis you plot capability on — stretch the x-axis and a jump turns into a ramp. Or the two sides are answering different questions: one about the fastest physically possible trajectory, the other about the likely one given institutions. Or they mean different things by "general", one side treating it as a single number that goes up and the other as a bundle of abilities with no canonical ordering, so "human-level" doesn't even pick out a point.

Which of those is doing the work, and how much is left over as a genuine disagreement about the world? Give me reasons, not a survey. And if the axis hunch is right — is there a measure that escapes it, an axis where the sharpness of the transition is a fact about the system rather than about my choice of coordinates? If there isn't, say so; if there is, say what privileges it. Then tell me what would actually count as progress: a formal result, a distinction, some evidence, or just everyone agreeing to stop using the word.
