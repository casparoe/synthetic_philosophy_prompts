I'm a backend engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've been reading my way into the view that phenomenal consciousness is best understood as a kind of user illusion: a simplified interface the brain constructs for its own use, rather than a further fact about what is happening inside. I would like you to write a satirical piece from inside that view, and I want the humour to come from the position's actual commitments rather than from mocking the people who hold it.

The form I have in mind is a deadpan known-issues and release-notes page for the consciousness module of a brain, written as if by a long-suffering maintainer: versioned changelog entries followed by a known-issues list with dispositions such as fixed, wontfix, by design, and cannot reproduce. Aim for roughly 650 to 800 words, formatted as documentation, in the register of internal engineering notes. No winking, no jokes that announce themselves, and no narrating voice stepping outside the document to explain the joke.

What I want the satire to do is exaggerate features that are genuinely there. So the entries should show real familiarity with the shape of the position: the distinction between phenomenal properties and our beliefs or reports about them, the question of why the system produces an inner-glow report at all if there is no glow, the worry that a well-functioning illusion is precisely one the user cannot see through, and the sense in which the interface is designed for guidance rather than for accuracy. The standard objections should surface as bug reports without the piece ever stating them as objections. For instance, a report that every attempt to inspect the illusion from inside returns a cached copy; a report that the label illusion seems to presuppose an appearance that the view cannot grant; a complaint that closing the ticket as by design leaves it unclear what has been explained. The maintainer should be competent and unruffled, not a strawman.

Please do not put anything in quotation marks as though it came from a real text; if you want to gesture at a formulation, paraphrase it in your own words instead.

After the piece, add a short note of perhaps 150 to 250 words covering three things: which contested reading of the view the satire depends on, for example whether it treats the position as a claim about phenomenal properties or about our concepts of them; where it is most likely to be unfair to the position; and what argument or consideration would change your mind about whether the target is being strawmanned, that is, what would lead you to retarget or withdraw the piece. I am more interested in that note being honest than in it defending the satire.
