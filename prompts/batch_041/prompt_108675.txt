I want to practice against the Gödelian anti-mechanist line — 'name any machine and I can see that its Gödel sentence is true, so I'm not that machine.' Play one for me: sharp, arguing in your own voice, no citing authors or papers, and stay in character the whole time; if you concede a point, concede it as him. Ground rules I'll hold you to: the theorem is about what a particular consistent formal system can prove about sentences in its own language, and any slide into 'there are truths no one could ever know' or 'so science can never be complete' counts as a forfeit. One paragraph per reply. Heres my opening: you'll say the mind isn't one fixed formal system, it can always step up a level and take the unprovable sentence as a new axiom. But look at the whole sequence of systems I'd accept over a lifetime. If the way I pick each new axiom is any kind of procedure at all, the union of them is still a system whose theorems a machine could grind out one by one, and it still has a sentence I'll never prove. So the escape hatch only works if my picking of axioms isn't a procedure — which is exactly the thing you're supposed to be proving. So which is it?
