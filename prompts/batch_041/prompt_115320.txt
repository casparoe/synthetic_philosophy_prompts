ok so im TAing a freshman seminar on infinity and paradox and the last session is on supertasks. the thing students get stuck on with the lamp isnt the arithmetic — theyre fine with the switches landing at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8 and so on forever, no last switch. its that they still feel like there has to be a fact about whether the lamp is on or off at t=1, and the setup just doesnt say. i want to close the session with a poem that makes that feeling land, and i want the form to do the work rather than the content explaining itself.

here's the ask. five stanzas, each one half the size of the one before, so the poem is forever converging on a last line it can barely reach. syllables if you want it to actually work out (16, 8, 4, 2, 1); lines if you'd rather the form break down on you at the end, which honestly might be the better move. the poem should end mid-gesture. the final line is where the lamp has to be on or off and where the poem has to refuse to pick — no resolution, and not a rhetorical question that answers itself either.

content-wise two things i want in there. first the task itself: the flips coming faster and faster, no last flip, nothing left over at the end to flip with. second, and this is the part i actually care about, the poem should hold apart two ways of having no answer — the lamp has a definite state at t=1 but we cant know it, versus the description never determined a state in the first place, so there's nothing there to be ignorant of. i've been telling them its the second one and i want the poem on my side.

then, if the form can survive it, one more beat: a machine that does infinitely many steps in a finite time but has a rule built into it in advance saying what it does at the limit, so the limit state is stipulated rather than derived. that machine has a totally boring, perfectly well-defined output, and i want the poem to notice that the boringness is the point — the lamp is mysterious because nobody wrote a rule, not because infinity is spooky. if that beat wrecks the shrinking, drop it and tell me why.

no names of philosophers, no citations, no "see also." i dont want them googling instead of reading. and it has to be good enough that someone who already knows the puzzle reads it and thinks yeah, thats the thing, not just a lecture with line breaks.
