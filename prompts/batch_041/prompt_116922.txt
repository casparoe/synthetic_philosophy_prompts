Alright, so I'm a backend engineer, never taken a philsoophy class in my life, and I've somehow fallen into a rabbit hole about G.E. Moore's proof of an external world — you know the one, he holds up one hand and then the other and says that's one hand, that's another, so there are at least two external objects, so the external world is real, take that skeptic. I think its from his essay Proof of an External World. Anyway a guy on a discord server says the whole thing is worthless, and his argument is annoyingly clean. It goes like this:

1. Moore and the skeptic actually agree on the setup: if I know there's a hand in front of me, then I know I'm not a handless brain in a vat being fed fake hand-experiences, since you can't have a real hand and be a handless vat-brain at the same time.
2. The skeptic finishes the thought: but I don't know I'm not a brain in a vat, so I don't know there's a hand.
3. Moore just runs the identical setup backwards: but I do know there's a hand, so I do know I'm not a brain in a vat.
4. It's the same logic spitting out opposite conclusions, so the logic can't be what's doing the work. What breaks the tie for Moore is that he's more sure of the hand than of the vat story — seeing a hand beats some weird argument from a seminar room.
5. But being sure of the hand is the exact thing under dispute. So Moore's tiebreaker is just him repeating his side, only louder.
6. And once that tiebreaker counts, it proves way too much. Anyone who's really confident of something gets to overrule any argument they dislike by declaring themselves more sure of their own thing. The guy who's certain his neighbours are spying on him gets to prove they're spying on him. Somebody certain the earth is flat gets to prove the earth is flat. The method proves whatever you already believed walking in.
7. A method that proves whatever you already believe proves nothing. So Moore's proof proves nothing.

I really don't want to accept 7, because it seems to mean I can't even know my own hand is real, which is nuts. But every line looks fine to me and I can't find the cheat. Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up actually cost me elsewhere? I'd much rather keep the hand than keep 7, so if there's a bill to pay for that, tell me what it is.
