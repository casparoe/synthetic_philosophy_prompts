I teach an upper-level seminar that pairs metaphysics with cognitive science, and I'm drafting the take-home portion of this term's final. Our unit on Dennett's three stances — physical, design, and intentional — runs smoothly until we reach the question of what makes a pattern real rather than merely useful, at which point the room splits into two camps that I find equally unsatisfying: the students who conclude that 'real pattern' just means 'convenient fiction,' and those who think the physical stance obviously reports what is really there while the other two are bookkeeping devices. I want a single essay question that forces a third position, or that forces students to show there isn't one, and that cannot be answered by reciting the stance taxonomy back at me.

I'd like you to build the question around a proposal one of my students made last term, which she called the canonical-language fix. Her suggestion was to take the compression idea seriously: a pattern is real when describing a system in terms of it yields a shorter description of the data than its rivals, and this is an objective, mind-independent matter provided we fix a single maximally expressive description language in advance — she nominated fundamental physics for the job. Her hope was that this rescues realism about patterns from the charge that patterns live in the eye of the beholder, since how well a description compresses is a fact about the data rather than about us. The question should make students work out whether the fix helps Dennett's position or quietly dismantles it. The tension I want them to feel is this: if the criterion needs a canonical language, then the physical stance looks privileged after all, which is precisely what Dennett's pluralism about levels is meant to resist; but if compression is always relative to whatever vocabulary we happen to be using, it is unclear what realist work the criterion is doing beyond 'this vocabulary earns its keep with us.' I'd also like them to engage a case in which the intentional-stance description and the design-stance description of the same system both compress the data well and neither strictly dominates — I usually use an ant colony, whose foraging can be predicted either by treating the colony as deciding where to send foragers or by tracking individual pheromone-following rules — and to say what that case shows about whether there can be a fact of the matter as to which pattern is the real one.

Please write the essay question itself, a model answer of roughly 600 to 700 words, and a marking scheme. The question should be answerable in about ninety minutes and should require students to take a stand on whether Dennett can keep both pluralism and realism about patterns or whether the two trade off. For the marking scheme I want five must-hit points worth twenty marks in total, each with its own mark value and a one-line note on the characteristic way candidates miss it; at least two of those notes should identify a mistake that ought to cap a candidate below the top band even when the prose is otherwise fluent — for instance, redescribing the intentional stance as 'just instrumentalist' and declaring the matter settled, or asserting that physics is fundamental without saying what that does to the compression criterion. A strong answer should also say what would have to be true of a description language for the criterion to be objective without being physicalist, and should be explicit about which premise of the fix it is rejecting.
