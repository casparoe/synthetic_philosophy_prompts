My sister's a hospice nurse and she's spent the last month convinced the killing/letting-die distinction is a fiction. Her argument goes like this.

(1) If killing and letting die really differ morally, the difference has to show up in the causal facts. The killer causes the death; whoever lets die only fails to stop it.
(2) Causation is counterfactual dependence: X caused Y just when Y wouldn't have happened without X.
(3) So put a shooter next to her. If the shooter hadn't fired, the patient lives. If she'd hung the antidote drip, the patient lives. Same dependence either way.
(4) So both lots of conduct caused the death -- her not-hanging as much as his bullet. Same cause, same relation.
(5) Same causally means same morally, by (1). So the law's line between murder and failing to rescue is arbitrary, and since I could've given what it would've took to save somebody, I'm a killer too.
(6) Though obviously a nurse holding a patient's head under water and a nurse who just never hangs the drip aren't the same thing. She also likes to point out that clamping off a drip that's already running is something her hand does, and everyone still files that under letting die.

I want to keep (1) -- I don't want the whole distinction resting on something spooky -- and I can't see how to drop (2) or (3) without dropping causation altogether. So which step goes, and what do I pay for dropping it? If I keep (1), what does the honest version of it come to?
