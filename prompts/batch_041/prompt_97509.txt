I'm in a philosophy of biology reading group that's been going in circles for weeks, and I think the problem is that none of us actually has a clear standard for what a good account of this stuff is supposed to do. The stuff being Dennett's cranes-and-skyhooks image and the related accusation of "greedy reductionism" — the idea that some explanations cheat by pulling the rabbit out of a hat (a skyhook), while legitimate ones build the rabbit up out of smaller cranes all the way down, and that the sin of greedy reductionism is skipping levels to explain something high-level by something absurdly low-level. Fine, I get the vibes. But before we read another paper, I want the actual spec. So write me the desiderata: what does any theory of how complex, apparently designed-looking structure gets produced *without a designer* have to deliver? Give me the full list — the cases it has to get right (origin of life, the eye, minds, and tell me whether culture or markets or ecosystems even belong on the same list or whether that's a category error), the puzzles it has to dissolve or at least pin down, and the distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse (order vs. complexity? a crane vs. merely a small skyhook? selection vs. mere sorting?). With each one, say what consideration forces it onto the list.

Two things I especially want you to chew on, because I suspect they're where our group keeps going wrong. First, a friend proposed that the whole thing reduces to one rule — "no unexplained lifting," i.e. every bit of apparent design has to be paid for by some earlier process — and claimed that's the desideratum and everything else is commentary. Is that a genuine requirement or just the crane/skyhook slogan dressed up? Second, is greedy reductionism really a *distinct* failure mode, or is it just skyhook-thinking with the arrow reversed (i.e. the same crime of not actually doing the lifting, only pointed downward)? Also flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling candidates out. Skip scoring the famous positions, I'll do that in the group — I just want the criteria, and be honest about which ones are load-bearing and which are just camp preferences.
