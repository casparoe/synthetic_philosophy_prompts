Me and my mate Dan spent about three hours last night going round in circles on whether the future is open, and I came away with a list of things I believe that I'm now worried don't fit together. I'd rather find out from you than from him. Here it is:

1. The future is genuinely open. Not "we don't know what's coming" — that there's no fact of the matter yet. No single already-drawn line running through the branches. When I say tomorrow's coin toss or tomorrow's sea battle is unsettled, I mean unsettled in the world, not in my head.

2. Truth doesn't come and go. I can't stomach the idea that a claim starts out with no truth value and then acquires one, or that it was false and then becomes true. If something's true, it's true — truth isn't something that happens to a sentence at a moment in time. I think the right word is that truth is tenseless but I'm not 100% sure I'm using it right.

3. The past is fixed. Once it's done it's done. It's not within anyones power to make what happened not have happened. That asymmetry between regret and anxiety is the whole point of having a past.

4. Classical logic, as far as I can tell, is just correct. Excluded middle, the lot. I've never seen a reason to go three-valued.

5. I'm not a fatalist and I don't want to become one. Some of what happens tomorrow depends on what I decide. If it was all locked in, deliberating would be a joke.

6. All of the above is about reality, not about us. The openness in (1) is ontic, not just ignorance.

Also I'm an atheist, so "deny God's foreknowledge" isn't available to me as a fix. Dan kept offering me that and I kept telling him it's not on the table.

Here's what's bugging me. I can feel two of these grinding but I can't tell which two, and every time I try to fix one I seem to break another. Dan also kept insisting that if I accept excluded middle I've already conceded that "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is true or false right now — and I'm fairly sure that's a conflation, but under pressure I couldn't say what exactly it conflates. He also said it's arbitrary to have a fixed past and an open future, that I'm just carving time at the point where I happen to be standing, and I didn't have a good answer to that either.

So: don't hand me the menu of positions and leave me to pick. Tell me which of these I should actually drop. Rank the tensions by how bad they are, separate the ones that only look like clashes from the real ones, and for the real ones tell me the cheapest fix and exactly what it costs me — not "you could deny X or deny Y" but which one you'd deny and why. If the honest answer is that I have to give up (1) or (5), say it straight, I'd rather know. And if you think the three-valued route is better than I'm giving it credit for, make that case properly, but deal with the thing that put me off it: that if a claim about tomorrow has no truth value today, then tomorrow the past seems to have changed, and I really can't have that.
