I have just been appointed to my university's student conduct board, which decides what sanctions follow a finding of academic dishonesty, and I have begun reading theories of punishment in order to work out what our sanctions should even be aiming at. Before I read further, I want the specification rather than the theories: what must an adequate theory of punishment deliver? Set out the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. I have in mind things of this kind — that it explain why hard treatment is permissible at all, rather than prevention or compensation alone; that it explain why only the guilty may be punished, even where punishing an innocent would produce more good overall; that it give an account of how much punishment is fitting; that it distinguish punishment from quarantine, from taxation, and from the mere price of a rule; that it say what gives an institution the standing to impose hard treatment on this person at all (why the university may sanction a student for cheating but not a non-student who does the same thing); and that it handle the offender already ruined by his own offence, the mala prohibita offender who did nothing independently wrong, the corporation that cannot feel pain, and the dead offender. For each requirement, say why it is a requirement and how far it is negotiable. Then tell me which requirements cannot be satisfied together, and why — that is the part I most want, since a jointly unsatisfiable list is itself the result. End by naming the single requirement that does the most to exclude the most popular theories, and say what it excludes.

Give it in four sections: Desiderata, a sentence or two per requirement with the case behind it; Joint satisfiability, one paragraph; The most exclusionary requirement, one paragraph; and Implications for the board, one paragraph.
