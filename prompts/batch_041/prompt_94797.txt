I host a small philosophy podcast and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends a Schlick-style view of life's meaning—roughly that the question "what is the meaning of life?" is a practical question about how to live, not a theoretical one, and that meaning is found in activity pursued for its own sake (play) rather than in work done for external ends. I want to avoid the usual "so what's the meaning of life?" softball. Draft 8-10 questions that really dig into the position, with a follow-up for each that anticipates the answer she's most likely to give. I'm especially interested in: (a) how she squares this with Schlick's own logical positivism—wasn't he the guy who said metaphysical questions are meaningless, so is the question of life's meaning meaningful or not?; (b) whether the play/work distinction can hold up—isn't all play just work for the sake of pleasure, or isn't some work done for its own sake?; (c) what the view says about people who can't play (depression, chronic pain, extreme poverty). Also include one question that asks her to distinguish her view from the idea that meaning requires both subjective attraction and objective value—but don't name-drop, just describe the idea.
