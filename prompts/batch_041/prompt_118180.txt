I was at our local planning committee last week and a bloke stood up to object to a block of flats with the line "a neighbourhood is basically a small country, and the planning rules are its immigration policy" — delivered like it settled the matter. His point, as far as I could follow it, was that a country obviously gets to decide who comes in, so a street obviously gets to decide who moves in, and objecting to the flats is no more bigoted than having a border. I've run into versions of this before, and also from the other side: people who say zoning is just border control with extra planning permission, and use that to argue that if you're against walls at the national level you should be against them at the street level. Someone at the back of the room tried "well then the whole city is the country and our street is just a gated community inside it" and everyone moved on before that got sorted.

I'd like a proper audit rather than an "it's obviously just snobbery" or an "it's obviously fine" take. So, first, what's supposed to map onto what — who or what is the citizen, the state, the border, the visa, naturalisation, the constitution — and does each of those have a real counterpart, or is the mapping coasting on the word "member"? Then the places it breaks. I'm especially bothered about whether zoning restricts people at all or only buildings (and whether that difference survives when the exclusion happens through price instead), whether a neighbourhood has anything like a demos or a shared political culture rather than just a set of people who happen to have bought nearby, and what the analogy does with the fact that newcomers to a street become full neighbours the day they arrive rather than staying a separate class for ever. Also: the analogy seems to be able to argue both directions, so tell me whether it's actually carrying any conclusion or just smuggling in whatever the speaker already thought about borders. And if the nation is the wrong model, what's the better one — a club, a firm, a homeowners' association, a commons — and what does each of those get right that the nation misses? Don't give me a bibliography, I just want the argument.
