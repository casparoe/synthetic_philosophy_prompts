I am a Year 12 student and I help run a small philosophy and technology podcast at my school. Next week we are interviewing a visiting researcher who works on the ethics of AI assistants. She defends a position she calls autonomy-preserving paternalism: roughly, an AI assistant may refuse a user's request, or override it, when doing so is necessary to protect the user's own long-term autonomy, provided the intervention is transparent, minimal, and open to challenge. She contrasts this with crude paternalism, which simply substitutes the assistant's judgement for the user's, and with a purely neutral assistant that never intervenes. She also insists that the assistant must never lie or manipulate, and I suspect that this commitment is harder to hold together with the rest of the view than she lets on.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give? Please make at least one question each on: (i) how overriding a user's present choice can be justified by the user's own autonomy rather than by the assistant's values; (ii) how the assistant could know the user's long-term values well enough to act on them without becoming paternalistic about the user's own conception of the good; and (iii) what makes a refusal contestable in practice, given that the assistant may be the only source of information the user has. I would also like questions on the difference between refusing a request and overriding it, on whether the view licenses manipulation or nudging as well as refusal, on cases such as a user who asks in advance to be stopped from gambling or compulsive scrolling, and on what evidence would show that the policy is failing. Please avoid generic questions about AI ethics, and do not ask for reading lists or citations. Keep the whole set to no more than 700 words.
