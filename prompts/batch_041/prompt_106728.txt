My old boss Marguerite is retiring after thirty-one years running the press office at the transit authority, and somehow I've ended up giving the toast at her send-off on Friday. Half the room is the press corps she spent three decades stonewalling; the other half is her family, who still aren't totally sure what she did for a living. Here's whats making this hard. Marguerite's entire professional creed was that she never lied to anyone — she only ever misled them. She'd say it straight-faced: "I never said the line was closing. I said it wasn't staying open." Every reporter in the room has a favorite example and every one of them is technically true. And I've been reading alot around this whole question of whether misleading someone is really different from lying — whether it matters that you implied something false rather than asserting it, or just left it out, or said something true knowing exactly what everyone would hear — and i genuinely can't decide whether her creed was admirable or just a way of never being accountable for anything. I want the toast to sit right in that, not skate past it. It has to land for the reporters, who'll catch every implication, and for her sister and grandkids, who won't, and it has to be warm even though it doesn't let her off the hook. Give me the toast as exactly five numbered beats, each a sentence or two, no intro and no outro.
