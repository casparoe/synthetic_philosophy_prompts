I sit on a no-fault compensation tribunal that awards general damages for pain and suffering, and there is now a claim before us that our own precedents do not settle on their face. I would like an account of what we should do, what we are likely to do, and what our past rulings actually commit us to. We expect more claims of this shape, since the same dissociation is reported after cingulotomy and under morphine analgesia.

The docket, with the reasons the panel recorded in each case:

1. A claimant who crushed his hand in machinery: severe pain, distress, repeated requests for analgesia, unable to sleep. Compensated in full. Recorded reason: he was suffering intensely.

2. A claimant who sustained a comparable injury while deeply unconscious after a collision. His body mounted the expected nociceptive and autonomic responses, but he was aware of nothing at the time and remembers nothing of the episode. Not compensated. Recorded reason: nothing was experienced, and the responses of a body are not the experiences of a person.

3. A claimant with pain asymbolia following a lesion of the insula and parietal operculum. He localizes the stimulus accurately and rates its intensity as high, but says that it does not hurt in the way pain usually hurts, that it is not unpleasant or distressing to him, and that it does not trouble him. He does not withdraw from the stimulus; his pulse and skin conductance do not rise when it is applied; he declined analgesia. Not compensated. Recorded reason: a painful sensation was present, but nothing disagreeable was being experienced, and the claimant's own account of his experience was the best evidence available to us.

4. A claimant with deafferentation pain after a brachial plexus avulsion. No intact peripheral supply to the arm and no ongoing tissue damage, but continuous pain that she calls unbearable and thoroughly unpleasant. Compensated in full. Recorded reason: what the scheme compensates is an experience, and the absence of ongoing tissue damage is irrelevant to it.

5. A claimant in advanced dementia who could report nothing: she grimaced and pulled away when a joint was palpated, and her pulse and blood pressure rose sharply during care. Compensated. Recorded reason: behavioural and autonomic evidence can establish that a disagreeable experience is occurring, and a report is not a precondition of one.

6. A claimant with intractable nausea and breathlessness from a treatment: no pain at all, but intensely unpleasant and impossible to rest through. Compensated. Recorded reason: the scheme compensates disagreeable experience, not pain as such.

Now the case before us.

7. The claimant has pain asymbolia from the same kind of lesion as the claimant in (3), and gives the same account of his experience: he localizes the sensation, rates its intensity as high, and says that it does not hurt in the way pain usually hurts, that it is not unpleasant or distressing, and that it does not trouble him. Three things differ from (3). First, asked what he wants done about it, he says he wants the pain gone, and would accept a free, immediate and riskless procedure that abolished the sensation. Second, he does withdraw from the stimulus, and his pulse and skin conductance rise reliably when it is applied. Third, over the past two years he has burned himself twice and fractured a foot, because he does not behave as a person in pain behaves; his physicians expect this to continue.

What I want from you: (a) whether the pain itself should be treated as a compensable harm, and what we should award; (b) what we will in fact award, if the panel behaves as it has behaved; (c) the several rules our record is consistent with, and what each predicts about (7); (d) your best guess at the rule we are actually running, with the reasons for preferring it to the others; (e) the closest analog among (1)–(6), and precisely what it does and does not settle, given that (3) is the nearest case on the phenomenal facts but may not be the nearest on the reasons; and (f) the further case we should put to ourselves, or wait for, that would force the choice among the surviving rules. Be explicit at the end about which principle we would be adopting if we compensate the pain, and which if we do not.
