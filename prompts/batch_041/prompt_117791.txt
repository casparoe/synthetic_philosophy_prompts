My roommate keeps going on about "Moloch": multipolar traps, races to the bottom, situations where every actor does the locally sensible thing and everyone lands in an outcome nobody wanted and, in some sense, nobody chose. His examples are arms races, ad auctions, everyone standing up at a concert, industrial farming.

I want you to restate the Moloch / multipolar trap idea in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report. Translate three things, one at a time: (1) the trap itself, (2) the mechanism that generates it — why acting rationally on your own gets you there even when no ones choosing the bad outcome, (3) the standard proposed escapes — coordination, binding commitment, shared norms or values, exit, having a single owner of everything.

The first one is going to translate almost too easily, since my field is basically the poster child for it, and that's exactly why I want the residue rather than the easy part. So: what goes through cleanly, what only goes through if you fudge it, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Push hard on the places where traffic engineering quietly smuggles in something Moloch-talk doesn't have — a central authority that can meter ramps or toll a road, one scalar to minimize, a fixed population of users who all agree travel time is the thing. Then flag the reverse direction: anything the Moloch framing has that I should be suspicious of. Is "Moloch" doing more work than naming a Pareto-inefficient Nash equilibrium and being mad about it? And when you're done, tell me whether the residue is a defect in my vocabulary or a sign the trap idea is less substantive than it sounds.
