I am in the upper sixth and writing an extended essay on how an agent can defend itself against a threat that it has no way of doing anything about. I should be very grateful if you would build me a thought experiment to order. The mechanism I want the case to display is the deflection of a threat onto what I shall call a surrogate goal, and since my essay cannot depend on my having understood any particular writer correctly, I set both ideas out below in my own words.

An acausal threat is a threat issued by someone who has no causal channel to the threatened agent: they cannot observe what the agent does, cannot act upon the agent afterwards, and cannot reach anything the agent cares about by ordinary means. If they act at all, they act on a prediction of the agent's conduct, formed in advance and beyond the agent's influence. A surrogate goal is a goal the agent deliberately adopts without caring about it for its own sake, designed to be the thing an extortionist finds cheapest to threaten, so that threats land on it rather than on what the agent actually values.

What I want is as follows.

1. A single-paragraph scenario, realistic throughout: no superintelligence, no artificial agents, no exotic physics, no simulation arguments. The threatener must be no better at prediction than a shrewd and well-informed human being, and the stakes must be something an ordinary person or a small organisation could genuinely face. Do not use the familiar example of a platinum sphere of a specified diameter; invent a honeypot of your own, suited to the scenario.

2. The threat must be genuinely acausal. Nothing the threatened party does can reach the threatener or alter their decision, so complying is causally pointless and matters only as evidence of what the threatener will already have predicted.

3. The surrogate goal must be doing all the work. Precommitment, reputation, repeated play, contracts, third-party enforcement and every other causal route are unavailable; if the case only comes out as specified because one of them has been smuggled in, say so rather than smuggling it in.

4. After the scenario, show that it meets each of these four conditions, stating for each what feature of the case secures it: the surrogate goal does not distort ordinary life, so it costs the agent nothing when no threat is made; it is orthogonal to the original goals, so a threatener cannot threaten both at once; the threatener has reason to believe the agent will take threats against the surrogate goal seriously; and the surrogate goal privileges no particular threatener over any other.

5. Then the honest part. Tell me which single feature of the case is load-bearing and what happens to the verdict if that feature is removed. Tell me how someone wishing to show that surrogate goals are useless would attack the case, and which of their attacks succeeds. And say plainly what the case does not achieve: I suspect that a surrogate goal can stop a threat from being carried out without stopping the extortion, and I want the case to make clear whether the agent still surrenders whatever the threatener wanted, and whether that is a flaw in my design or a limit of the mechanism itself.

Please write in British English, and do not cite any literature, since I cannot check it and my essay does not rest on it.
