Compare Chesterton's fence with Burke's presumption in favor of inherited institutions. On the surface the two counsel the same thing: do not dismantle an arrangement whose purpose you have not yet understood. Yet Chesterton's fence seems to presuppose a builder with an intention, and it licenses removal once that intention has been recovered and judged to be bad, whereas Burke's defense of institutions rests on their being the slow, unplanned accumulation of countless minds, so that no inquirer can ever fully recover the reasons embedded in them. Does this make Chesterton's fence a rationalist test dressed in conservative clothing, and does Burke's position leave any room for the fence to come down at all? Say which of the two better captures the conservative attitude, and why.
