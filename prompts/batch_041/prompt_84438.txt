ok so I'm building the first session of a grad seminar on philosophy of social science and I need a case I can put on the board that does one very specific job: it has to show that you can be a full-blown ontological individualist — everything there is is individual people plus relations between them, no group minds, no sui generis social forces — and still not get explanatory individualism for free. So the scenario has to be one where every bit of the causal action is individual action, and yet the explanation that carries the counterfactual weight is pitched at the aggregate level.

Constraints, because this kind of case is easy to fake. Realistic setting (a workplace, a village, a market, a bureaucracy — not a toy model with 'agents A and B' and payoff matrices). No leaning on any actual historical episode, I want it invented so nobody can recite it. The aggregate pattern has to be generated from what the individuals do, not stipulated. Board-writable, so a paragraph or two at most.

The thing I really want engineered in: the same aggregate regularity should hold across cases where the individual-level facts are wildly different from one another, so that no single individual-level generalization survives across the cases while the aggregate one does. But you have to actually walk me through the individual-level mechanism each time, in each case seperately. If the aggregate turns out to be doing causal work that the individuals aren't, the case is cheating and I can't use it.

After the scenario: give me the two readings of what's going on, then what the individualist says to re-describe it (she'll say the aggregate is just a summary, and that the micro facts screen off the macro), then why that re-description costs her something, then the strongest version of her reply, which I take to be the exclusion worry — if the individual facts already fix the outcome then the aggregate fact isn't a difference-maker, so at best we've got explanatory autonomy and not causal autonomy. Finish by telling me which single feature of the case is doing the work, wether removing it collapses the case back into one the individualist wins trivially, and where the case proves too much — if the same structure would also show that macro-level explanation in physics is irreducible, I want to know that before a student asks me.
