I'm a backend engineer (CS degree, no philosophy training) and through a series of bad decisions I'm now on our town's historical commission. In three weeks we vote on the wording of a plaque for the old mill site, and we've split into two camps that seem to be fighting about whether history can be objective at all. Camp one: our job is to state what the sources establish about the mill and stay silent where they're silent -- no spin, no lessons for anyone. Camp two: thats a fantasy, any plaque is a selection of facts arranged into a story with a moral shape, we're writing for the town as it is now, and claiming neutrality just launders whoever benefited. Our chair keeps invoking Ranke, someone else keeps invoking Carr and Hayden White, and the actual dispute is concrete: the mill's owner used convict labor for a stretch in the 1890s, the records are patchy (we can document some of it, not the scale), and the two camps want very different sentences about it.

Write the memo I'll circulate before the vote. I need an actual recommendation -- a specific approach to the wording, or a rule for choosing the wording, plus what I should say at the meeting if it gets challenged -- and I need the case for whichever side I land against stated the way it's own members would recognize it, not as a strawman. One constraint from me: whatever goes up, someone will file a public records request or threaten a lawyer, so tell me what's defensible and where the exposure is.

Sections, in this order: 'Bottom line' (3-4 sentences: what I should vote for, and by when), 'Why' (3-5 paragraphs), 'The strongest case against my recommendation' (1-2 paragraphs), 'What would change my mind' (2-3 sentences). Under 900 words. Also, honestly, half this fight might be the two camps using 'objective' to mean different things, so if part of it is them talking past each other, say which part and why.
