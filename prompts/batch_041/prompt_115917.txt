For six years I worked on access control and incident response, and I still think in that vocabulary: trust boundaries and threat models, privileged processes and root, capabilities as against permissions, delegation and revocation, audit trails, false positives against false negatives. I have been arguing for months with a friend who works in criminal justice reform, and I keep finding that when I restate her position to myself in my own terms it comes out either obviously true or obviously confused, which makes me suspect that the translation is losing something important and that I am blaming her argument for my vocabulary's gaps. I want you to do the translation properly and then tell me what does not survive it. Take the debate over whether policing should be reformed, defunded, or abolished — together with the premise all three positions share, that the state is defined by its monopoly on the legitimate use of force — and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: the police as a privileged process with write access to the enforcement mechanism, reform as hardening that process with logging, rate limits, and mandatory review, defunding as reallocating budget from runtime enforcement to prevention and repair, abolition as removing the privileged process and running enforcement in userland with distributed, overlapping capabilities, private security as userland daemons each carrying their own weapon, and self-help as what you get when there is no kernel at all. Assume I am fluent enough in this vocabulary to notice when a political conclusion is smuggled in under an engineering word; I want the arguments restated, not endorsed, and I want the mapping to be honest about its seams rather than quietly tidying them.

What I want on top of the translation is a residue report. Which parts carry over cleanly — the deterrent effect of enforcement, the incentive structure of budgets, the sheer concentration of coercive capability, the who-watches-the-watchers problem, the tradeoff between false positives and false negatives in stops and searches — and which parts only carry over with distortion, in the sense that I have to fudge the meaning of one of my terms to make the fit? I am especially suspicious of three things. The first is the word "legitimate": my vocabulary has authorization, which is always relative to some system that issues it, and it has acceptance, which is empirical; I do not have a way to say that a use of force is authorized by something standing outside the system that performed it. The second is the worry that a state and a protection racket are the same kind of thing, differing only in whether you can exit and whether the protection is genuine — in my vocabulary that sounds less like a reductio of the state than like a description of two deployments. The third is the idea that the enforcer is bound by the rules it enforces, so that a violation by the enforcer is a categorically different event from the same act performed by anyone else, rather than merely a violation by a process with more privileges.

For each of those three, I want a verdict on the residue: does it mark a genuine defect in my vocabulary, something the political debate can say and mine cannot; or does it suggest that the original debate is trading on a distinction that does no independent work, so that "legitimate" is just "accepted, stable, and widely internalized" wearing a suit, and the difference between a state and a protection racket is a difference in marketing? If it turns out that a good part of the reform-versus-defund-versus-abolish disagreement is really about how much to trust the privileged process and how cheaply it can be replaced — an empirical question about failure modes and recovery — say so plainly rather than dressing it back up in normative language. And where you have to invent a concept that is not in my vocabulary at all in order to carry an argument across, flag it as invention rather than translation.
