I have twice now tried to describe the same structural situation to someone and found that I could not do it without drawing a picture, and I would like to know whether there is an established name for it, or whether I should coin one.

The situation is this. Two things determine each other. A is what it is because of B, and B is what it is because of A, and neither can be specified without already invoking the other. The clearest instance I have is a poster I once saw in a dormitory room: two hands, each holding a pen, each in the act of drawing the other, the left hand drawing the right and the right hand drawing the left, with the cuffs of the sleeves trailing off the edge. A second instance is a company whose policy manual contains the rule that the manual may be revised only by a two-thirds vote of the board, while the board's authority to vote, and its very composition, are fixed by the manual. A third is a wiki on which any page may be edited by anyone, including the page that states the editing policy, so that the rule about who may change the rules is itself changeable by the same procedure it governs. A fourth, which I include because it makes me uneasy, is a program that rewrites its own source code, including the routine that decides how source code is to be rewritten.

In each case the mutual determination is not, I think, the whole story. Something further seems to be required that is not itself one of the two mutually determining items, and the odd part is that this further thing does not present itself as simply one level further up. It is not another item of the same kind standing above the pair; it is the thing the pair is, as it were, drawn on. In the poster, the sheet of paper, which is not inside either hand's drawing. In the manual case, the fact that anyone treats the manual as authoritative at all. In the wiki case, the bare fact that edits happen. Yet I cannot articulate what makes the sheet a different kind of thing rather than merely the next storey, and in some of my cases the candidate for that outside position appears to be within reach of the mutual determination after all: the community can revise the norms that govern revising, and the program can rewrite the routine that governs rewriting. So I cannot tell whether the outside position is a genuine structural necessity or merely wherever I happened to stop the description.

What I want is not a label but a carving. Is there a name for the mutual-determination structure, and a name for the item that occupies the outside position? How does the first differ from ordinary circularity, from a vicious circle, and from an infinite regress, and how does the second differ from a foundation, an axiom, or a first cause? Is the outside item always required, or is its apparent necessity an artifact of the examples I chose? How, in a given case, do I distinguish an item that is genuinely outside the mutual determination from one that is merely a level I have stopped asking about — and what happens to the picture when the two mutually determining items can reach the item that was supposed to stand outside them? A friend who has read some logic tells me it is all just the familiar point about a system that talks about itself, or about a language that cannot contain its own truth predicate, but my cases are about authority, depiction, and revision rather than truth or proof, and I suspect several different things are being run together under that reply. If there is no established term, I would like a proposed one, with a definition and with the cases that fall just outside it, since I am fairly sure the boundary cases are where the interest lies.
