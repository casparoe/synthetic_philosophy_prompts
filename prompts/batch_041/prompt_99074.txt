I'm a philosophy professor, and I'm putting together a small grant proposal with two colleagues -- one in decision theory, one in philosophy of religion -- on whether the simulation argument should dampen fanaticism about the far future. We can fund about three people for two years, so this isn't a general survey project; I need to know which open questions actually deserve the slots. I've been going back and forth with a colleague in the hallway after a seminar on longtermism; she thinks the simulation arguement is a knockout, because if we're probably simulated then our far-future projects are probably not ours to steer, so the huge-stakes longtermist bets lose their force. I think thats too quick. Depending on how you read it, the simulation argument might reduce the expected value of our far-future influence, or it might multiply the expected number of future beings if simulators run many sims, or it might just relocate the locus of moral concern to the simulators' level, or it might make our reasoning about the far future unreliable in a way that cuts against fanaticism and against longtermism generally. I've also been toying with a distinction between a probability-discount reading and a reference-class-shift reading, and with the idea of a simulation shadow on our normative reasoning -- the thought that if we're simulated, our reasoning about the far future is itself part of the simulation and maybe not truth-tracking. And then there's the shutdown versus continuation question: does simulation make abrupt truncation more likely, or does it make continuation under simulators more likely?

What I want from you is a defended agenda, not a survey and definately not a reading list or citations. Rank the five or six open questions or lines of work that matter most here, in the order a small team with two years should take them up. For each, tell me why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers and decision theorists rather than physicists or computer scientists, and what would move it up or down the list -- a new conceptual distinction, a formal result, a change in how we model the simulators' motives, whatever. Also tell me which alleged implications are overrated or don't follow at all. I'm less interested in a list than in the reasoning about what actually deserves attention, so please be explicit about the criteria you're using and where they trade off.
