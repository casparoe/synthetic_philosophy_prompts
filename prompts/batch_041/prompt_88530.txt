ok so i'm a novelist, and i'm in a small nonfiction reading group — the woman who runs it writes essays and she's the one who got me into Plutarch — and she has very firm opinions about which lives are actually worth learning from, morally speaking. her verdicts so far:

- classic saint's life, miracles, not one moment of doubt: no. she says you can't be that, so what are you supposed to do with it.
- doorstop biography of a famous general, battles and decisions, basically no inner life: no.
- memoir by a nobody, dead-end jobs, quietly trying to be less of a jerk to his family: yes, best kind.
- a guy who stood by while something awful happened, spent years telling himself it wasn't his problem, then slowly made himself act — no medals, small scale: yes.
- a saint's life where he's a total wreck first and then turns it around over years of unglamorous effort, no miracles: yes.

next month's book is a woman who drank her way through her twenties, treated everyone like garbage, and then got clean — and the whole thing turned on one night where, as she tells it, God spoke to her. no program, no slow grind, just the one night, and then she was different. what is my friend going to say about it? and what rule is she actually running here — are there other rules that fit her list just as well? and whats the next book we should pick to find out which rule she's really using?
