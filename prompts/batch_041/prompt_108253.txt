I chair the grantmaking committee at a mid-size family foundation and I have to write the short opinion I circulate before we vote on Thursday, and I'm stuck on a fight between two trustees.

Proposal A, from the rural-education people, would substantially lift outcomes in the poorest county in our region — real gains for the kids who are worst off by every measure we track. It also throws off a small spillover benefit to the neighboring county, which is already comfortably better off.

Proposal B, from a trustee I like and respect, is a "fairness" package instead: a set of requirements and reporting costs imposed on the better-off county's programs that would pull its numbers down closer to the poor county's. It does nothing for the poor county. Nothing. It just closes the gap from the top.

Her argument is that a gap that size is itself an injustice, and we shouldn't fund anything that leaves it standing even if the poor county does well in absolute terms. She's been saying I'm just scared of equality; I've been saying she'll happily make the pie smaller so the slices feel fairer. Neither of us have moved, and there's alot riding on this cycle.

Draft me the opinion. It needs to recommend a concrete course of action — fund A, fund B, split it, or defer with conditions — with a timeframe, and it has to take her position seriously rather than just asserting mine. What I actually want out of it is the prioritarian-vs-egalitarian stuff made usable: when is reducing someone's advantage without helping anyone else a genuine improvement, and when is it just shrinking the pie? And where does that line fall when the "advantage" in question is positional — e.g., if the better-off county's edge is partly just its rank in a competition for limited state spots, does pulling it down actually help the poor county in a way that isn't mere leveling down? If part of the poor county's gain is also positional, does that change the verdict?

One wrinkle: our charter says unspent money goes back to the endowment and can't be redirected this cycle, so B genuinely can't free up anything for A. Treat that as fixed.

After the memo, give me a short JSON summary with these fields: "recommendation" (the bottom line in a few words), "timeframe" (when it takes effect / the next step), "strongest_case_for_B" (the best version of her argument, two or three sentences), "genuine_leveling_down" (true/false — is B actually leveling down in the sense that matters, or does it help someone), "positional_turn" (whether the answer changes if the good is positional, and how), and "unresolved" (the part a future committee, not me, has to decide). Keep the memo itself under a page.
