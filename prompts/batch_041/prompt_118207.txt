OK so I've been going back and forth alot on the hiddenness of God thing — the argument that if a loving God wanted a relationship with everyone he'd make himself about as obvious as the sun, and since he doesn't, he probly isn't like that. I don't want a tour of the debate, I want your actual credence in one specific version of it, because I think the usual version is too easy to wave away.

My version: the problem isn't the bare absence of evidence, it's how *uneven* the evidence is. Some sincere, open people seem to get bowled over by religious experience, and other people, just as sincere and just as open, get nothing their whole life. If God were handing out evidence so a relationship was possible, you'd expect it to look more even, or at least to track how open someone is. So: what's your credence that this unevenness counts as real evidence against a loving God? I'm at about 0.6 myself.

Break it down — a rough number and one line for each: (1) that there really are non-resistant nonbelievers (people who'd believe if it were clear) and not just everyone secretly knowing and suppressing it; (2) that a loving God would be *required* to spread the evidence around roughly evenly, rather than being allowed to leave some people in the dark for reasons we can't see; (3) whether the usual outs — free will, soul-making, faith being worth more without proof — actually work or just move the problem around; (4) whether the resistant/non-resistant line is even a real distinction or just something we say after the fact. Then give me the combined number, tell me which of the four it leans on hardest, and what would actually move it. Don't fudge it to a nice round number.
