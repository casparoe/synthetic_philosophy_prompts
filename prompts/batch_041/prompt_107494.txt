ok so i'm in this weird reading group that's nominally about philosophy of mind but keeps spiraling into the color phi phenomenon, and i think i've hit the wall everyone eventually hits. here's the setup as i understand it: you flash a blue dot near the top of a screen, then a red dot near the bottom, maybe a tenth of a second apart, and at the right spacing and timing people report seeing one dot travel down and change color about halfway along the path. the spooky part is that when you ask them, they say the change happened before the red dot (the one that actually carries the new color) even showed up -- as if the experience had information it couldn't possibly have had yet. Dennett used this to set up two ways the brain could pull it off: either your experience really does unfold in roughly the order the stimuli arrived, and then your memory of it gets quietly rewritten to insert the smooth color change (he calls that Orwellian -- history gets edited after the fact), or the brain just sits on the information, waits for both dots, and hands you a single already-edited experience from the start (Stalinesque -- the show-trial version, where the verdict is settled before the trial). and here's the stall: both stories predict the exact same thing out of the subject's mouth, so we've been going back and forth for weeks and nobody budges, because every time someone proposes a test it turns out both models just absorb it.

what i actually want from you isn't a ruling on which model is right -- i want a diagnosis of why this thing stalls. is it a real, substantive disagreement about what the brain does, or a verbal one about the word experience (one side counting the rewritten memory as part of the experience and the other not), or are the two camps answering different questions under one heading -- one about the mechanism in real time, one about the content the system ends up representing? my own hunch, and i'm not sure it survives contact, is that the question when did the color change enter your experience smuggles in a demand for a fact of the matter -- a single timestamped moment -- that the brain simply doesn't have, because experience here is being asked to be both the constructed content and a real-time event, and color phi is precisely the case where those come apart. if that's right, then a lot of the back-and-forth is a category mistake dressed up as an empirical dispute. but maybe i'm being too quick and there really is a way to make the two models diverge. so: tell me what kind of disagreement this is, what each side is actually committed to once you press them, and then what would count as progress -- a cleverer paradigm that forces the models apart (some way of getting at the brain's own timing, temporal-order judgments, whatever), a conceptual distinction that dissolves the question, or a real argument that no progress should be expected and why. and please be concrete about the color phi case itself, because every time i read a general answer it slides back into the usual vague stuff about the specious present and i learn nothing.
