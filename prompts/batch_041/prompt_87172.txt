Ok, here's a thing I keep running into in seminar and I've never found a name for, so this is half a plea for terminology and half a plea for someone to actually carve the joint properly.

We're doing Jefferson or Churchill or Columbus, and someone says you can't hold him to our standards, he was a man of his time. Fine, maybe. But I've started noticing there are two different defences hiding in that one sentance, and people swap them for each other mid-argument.

Defence one is an empirical claim about the era: nobody then thought X was wrong, so he can't be blamed for not knowing better. That one is falsifiable. I say fine, not everyone — plenty of people in that same decade objected — and it's dead, or it has to retreat to 'well, he was an outlier'.

Defence two isn't about what anyone knew at all. It's about whose standards get to count: our moral vocabulary just doesn't apply to him, full stop. And here's what bugs me. When defence one gets knocked down, the person slides into defence two without noticing that the two claims are completely different in kind. Defence two isn't an excuse for the man, it's a claim that the criticism can't even be the sort of thing that's true. Which, if you believed it, would also cover the contemporaries who did object, and would cover us criticising people we happen to dislike. Nobody applies it that consistently.

There's a grading thing too. The same deviation from the era's norms gets called 'ahead of his time' in the figures we like and 'of his time' in the ones we don't.

I know the luck-and-responsibilty literature, and I know the presentism / historian's fallacy / Whig history charges, but those are names for what the accuser is doing, not for the structure of the defence itself. Is there an established term for either of these two, or for the slide between them? If there isn't one, coin something and tell me where its edges are — what it isn't, which of the nearby named things it's closest to and why it still isn't that, and what it predicts in a case that's not one of my three. Don't just hand me the label.

Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, no restating what I asked.
