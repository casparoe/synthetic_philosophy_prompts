You are a philosopher specializing in value theory and the formal structure of comparative value relations. Before you evaluate anything, map out how the claims below logically relate to one another — which entail which, which are merely compatible, and which cannot both hold — and then give me your verdict. I don't need a survey of the literature; I need you to reason about the structure.

Some context: I've spent the past month arguing with a colleague about how to compare whole worlds, and I now have to write a short position statement for a seminar. Here is where I've landed.

(1) "Better than" is transitive: if A is better than B and B is better than C, then A is better than C. I treat this as partly constitutive of what "better" means, not as an optional add-on.
(2) "At least as good as" is a total preorder: any two options can be ranked against each other, and the ranking is transitive.
(3) Take a spectrum from a small population of very high-quality lives to an enormous population of lives barely worth living, where each step adds a few people at a slightly lower quality of life. Every step is at least as good as the one before it; no step is a worsening.
(4) All the same, the enormous population of barely-worth-living lives is not better than the small flourishing one. I am not prepared to accept what's usually called the repugnant conclusion.
(5) Two options can be "on a par" — neither better nor worse than each other, yet not exactly equal either — so "better than" does not exhaust the ways two options can relate in value.
(6) If A and B are on a par, a small improvement in A makes A better than B.

Which of these can I keep together? I expect some pairs only look inconsistent and dissolve on inspection, and I don't want a list that treats every pair as equally bad. Order the genuine tensions by severity, say for each exactly what would have to give and what that costs, and identify the single revision that buys the most consistency for the least. Then commit: tell me which commitment I should actually drop, and defend that choice rather than leaving me the menu.
