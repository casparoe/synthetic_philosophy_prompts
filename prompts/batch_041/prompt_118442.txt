I'm on a committee at my kid's school that hands out one small hardship grant a year, and we're definately deadlocked already. To me it's obvious we give it to whichever family is worst off — that's just what fair means. But another member, who grew up in a different country, says no, the right thing is to give it to the family that's put the most into the school community, and that my neediest-first instinct is just the custom where I'm from. A third guy shrugs and says lets just poll the parents, since there's no real fact of the matter about fairness anyway.

Whats bugging me is that the more I look around, the more it seems like people's gut sense of what's fair really does track where they grew up. I half-remember some experiment where you offer two strangers a pot of money to split, and in some places they'll turn down a lowball offer even though it costs them money, and in other places they just take it — and that whole reject-the-unfair-offer thing might be a quirk of wealthier Western countries rather than something universal. If that's true, does it mean the shrug-and-poll guy is basically right, that there's just no answer here? Or can I still hold onto neediest-first without it being me just asserting my tribe's rule? And setting the philosophy aside for a second, what should we actually do on this committee, like this month?
