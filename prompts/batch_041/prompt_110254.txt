Ok so I'm in a moral psych seminar this term and I keep circling the same thing and I want to know if it already has a name before I try to make it my term paper, because otherwise I'll be re-inventing a wheel for 12 pages.

Background: I did a year in Freiburg and I'm back in the states now. Things I noticed. At 2am with literally no car coming, my German flatmates would stand at the red light and wait, and when I asked why, the answer was always some version of "thats just what you do" or "if you don't, then why would anyone." At home that situation is a joke, nobody waits, and the one person who waits feels like a chump. Different one: there was a sign-in rule at the institute that everyone agreed was pointless, and a colleague did it anyway, and when I asked she said it wasn't her call to decide which rules count. My friends here would say the rule is dumb, do it their own way, and sleep fine.

What I keep noticing isn't who's stricter. It's where the authority is supposed to sit. For them, the fact that it's the rule seems to be the reason, full stop, and following it is a kind of respect you owe the other people who are also following it. For us the rule is one input among many and the real authority is my own judgment, so obeying a rule I think is stupid feels like a small betrayal of myself. And when the two come apart — the rule is bad, or pointless, or everyone's ignoring it — we don't just disagree about what to do, we don't even agree on what counts as a good reason.

Is there a name for this specific contrast? I'm sure somebody has one, but the stuff I've been googling feels like it's circling without landing: the individualism/collectivism thing seems too broad and too much about what people value rather than about where they think authority comes from, and the obedience literature is all about authority figures and I'm talking about rules with nobody standing behind them. Also my seminar keeps implying you're not allowed to ask which side has the better of it — is that actually right, or is there a framework that lets you ask it instead of just describing?

Start with the bottom line in one sentence — is there a name or not, and which one you'd pick — then use the rest on the carving: what the phenomenon exactly is, how it differs from the two or three nearest things, and where its edges are. Under 300 words please.
