Compare two answers to the question of whether an ant colony can literally have a mind. On the functionalist answer, the colony has a mind just in case its states and transitions realize the right causal-functional roles, whatever its parts are like and whether or not any part is conscious. On the unity answer, a mind requires a single subject — one locus at which the colony's states are experienced as a single perspective — so a colony with no such locus has at most a simulation of a mind. Press the comparison with a test case: a colony whose collective behavior tracks and manipulates its environment as well as a person's does, but which contains no central integrator, no global workspace in which signals are bound into one state. For each answer, decide whether the colony has beliefs, whether it has a single perspective, whether it can misrepresent, and whether it bears moral status. Return your comparison as a JSON object with fields 'dimensions' (an array of objects with keys 'dimension', 'functionalist', 'unity', and 'assessment'), 'verdict' (which answer better captures the intuition that Aunt Hillary is a someone rather than a something), and 'decisive_consideration'.
