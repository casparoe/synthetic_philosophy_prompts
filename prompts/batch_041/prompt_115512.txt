ok so I'm putting together a dialogue for a philosophy-of-AI reading group and I want it to actually bite, not be one of those "is the map the territory" things that goes nowhere. Heres the setup I want you to use.

Two people in an interpretability lab, both sharp, both been doing this for years, arguing over a draft. Call them R and S or give them names, whatever. R thinks that when you find a feature in a network — a direction, a sparse-autoencoder latent, an attention-head circuit, whatever — you've found something that's really in there, doing causal work. S thinks "feature" is a story we tell about a function that's already fixed by other means: change the probe, the dictionary size, the random seed, the basis, and you get an equally good story, and the network itself never adjudicates between them.

What I want them to actually fight about is a result from my imaginary lab, so they can't just recite takes at each other. They trained a small transformer on modular addition and got the usual Fourier-y / circular-looking features everyone reports. Then two things. First, a decoy net: they took the trained net and inserted an orthogonal rotation into one layer that the next layer exactly undoes, so it computes the identical function bit for bit, but every direction in that layer is now different, and probing the decoy recovers a completely different-looking set of features. Second, they trained a sparse autoencoder on a randomly initialised net of the same shape and got latents that look just as clean and interpretable to human labelers — the labelers couldn't tell the trained net's dictionary from the random one above chance when the inputs were withheld.

The crux I want: what's left of R's realism once you grant both of those? R shouldn't just dig in. I want them making the best case that the decoy net and the random-net dictionary are both red herrings — e.g. that intervention results (patching, ablation) aren't preserved under the decoy trick the way S claims, or that some features are stable across seeds and architectures in a way the SAE artifacts aren't, or that S's own practice quietly presupposes real features. And S has to answer the obvious comeback: if it's all analyst's choice, why does patching one direction in one seed transfer to a different seed at all, and why does "the model represents X" ever predict anything about inputs nobody probed.

Let them get into the actual machinery — basis-dependence, what patching does and doesn't show, whether "the same feature" across two seeds is one thing or two, whether an SAE dictionary is a claim about the net or about the data — rather than floating above it. Neither should win by being vague or by being the reasonable one; if there's a real stalemate, let them locate exactly where it is and say what would break it. End with each conceding one specific thing the other got right, and one thing they still can't make sense of. Maybe 1200-1800 words.
