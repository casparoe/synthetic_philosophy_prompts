hey, so my seminar group got assigned to run a class session on kavka's toxin puzzle and i'm the one stuck designing the activity. there's about 18 of us, we get 75 min, just a normal seminar room with movable tables. the problem is everyone's already read the puzzle, so if i just do the obvious "here's a reward, now will you drink the poison" thing they'll all instantly give the textbook answer and i'll get nothing out of it. i need them to actually feel the trap, not recite it.

what i want to try is a three-stage version where the reward gets handed over before anyone does anything, so the whole class is sitting there with the goods already in their pockets. rough sketch of what i mean:

stage 1: everyone decides privately whether they're "in" -- they write down that they'll do the unpleasant task. i'm thinking something cheap and harmless like holding a hand in ice water for three minutes, or eating a spoon of something grim, or three minutes of a really grating sound through headphones. anyone who writes "in" gets the reward immediately -- tokens, candy, or points toward getting out of presenting next week, whatever -- and it's theirs to keep no matter what happens after.
stage 2: now that the reward is banked, the actual choice. do the task, or don't.
stage 3: i want to bolt on something extra so it isn't just the plain puzzle again. a betting round where the other students put their tokens on who they think will actually follow through, and anyone who calls it right gets paid. so now everybody has money riding on everybody else's intentions, not just on their own.

then the debrief is where the real work happens.

what i need from you: build this into something runnable. rules, materials list, a payoff scheme that works without real money (tokens converting into some in-class perk is fine), an exact timeline for the 75 min including the debrief, and any roles i have to play (i'll probably be "the machine" that pays out). arrange the stages so the trap happens in what they do, not in what i say afterwards -- no lecture up front, and i don't want to even say "kavka" or "intention" out loud until the debrief.

for the debrief: walk me through how to map each moment onto the pieces of the puzzle -- why the reward has to be for the intention and not the action, why it can feel rational to want to become a certain kind of person, and the bit where you'd have no reason at all to actually do the thing once you're already paid. also, i'm genuinely unsure the puzzle is even coherent -- can you really just decide to intend something you know you'll have zero reason to do later? -- so give me a way to let the class argue that out without me adjudicating, plus the standard moves on each side so i can keep it honest.

two more things. first, what will definately go wrong the first time and how do i handle it (i can already picture someone loudly declaring in, then pocketing the reward and laughing, which would kill the mood alot). second, be straight with me about what this activity can't reproduce -- my hunch is that with stakes this low nobody's actually forming a real intention, they're just performing one for the room, so the thing the puzzle is really about, whether you can cause yourself to intend, might not show up at all. if that's right, say so, and tell me what i have to supply verbally to make up for it.
