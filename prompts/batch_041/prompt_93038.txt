I have a story idea I've been chewing on for months and I can never make it work when I try to write it myself, so I'm handing it over.

It's about coffee tasters, but I don't want it to turn into a cute foodie story. Setup: a small family roastery, father and son, both of them have been tasting the same house blend for years, and then one week they both say the same thing at the same time — this isn't as good as it used to be. But they disagree about whats changed. The father says the coffee changed: cheaper beans, a sloppier roast, something in the process. The son says the coffee is exactly the same and he's the one who changed, his mouth or his nose or whatever it is that makes the stuff taste like anything to him at all. And the part I can't get over is that neither of them can be proved wrong. You send the coffee to a lab and it comes back matching last years batch to the decimal, and the son says fine, but that tells me what's in the cup, not what it tastes like. You line up the other tasters and they all say it's lovely, but then maybe the father and son are the two whose insides shifted, and everyone else is just as unable to check.

Here's the bit I actually want: the son keeps a notebook of every batch he's ever tasted, in his own handwriting, going back to when he was a kid hanging around the roastery. I want the story to turn on him trying to use that notebook to settle who's right, and failing — because his old entries are written in words he can't be sure he means the same way anymore. I keep running into this word qualia in youtube essays and comment fights, half of them saying the whole idea is a mess, and I think this story is secretly about that.

Don't have anybody explain the philosophy at the end, no lecture, no wise old man turning up to spell it out. I want the reader to close it feeling that the question of who changed doesn't have an answer, without any character in the story saying so. Also no scifi gadgets, nothing that reads minds. Maybe 2000 words.
