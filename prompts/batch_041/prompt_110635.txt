I'm a third-year student in a cognitive science and machine learning program, currently taking a seminar on mechanistic explanation in the sciences, and I've been trying to connect the philosophical readings to a research practicum I'm doing on mechanistic anomaly detection in small transformer models. The lab has a triage protocol that I suspect is doing more philosophical work than anyone acknowledges. When two models that differ in architecture and training data both pass the same behavioral test suite but diverge on a held-out probe, we run a path-patching analysis; if we can convert one model's internal circuit into the other's by a sequence of swaps that preserves the downstream logit difference, we log the finding as "the same mechanism" and treat the behavioral divergence as a mere implementation difference. If, however, the same circuit-like structure shows up in a model trained on a different task, we log it as "analogous," not the same, even when the path-patching criterion is met. And if a model passes the behavioral tests by a route that resists path-patching entirely, we call it a mechanistic anomaly and flag it for review.

I want to understand what this protocol presupposes about mechanism identity. Does treating path-patching equivalence as sufficient for sameness commit us to an interventionist or causal-role account of what a mechanism is, and does it quietly rule out accounts on which mechanisms are individuated by their component entities and activities, or by the explanatory interests of the researchers? Is the asymmetry between cross-model and cross-task cases principled, or does it reveal that the protocol is really tracking something like "same mechanism relative to a fixed task description" while officially claiming something stronger? More generally, what would a defender of the protocol have to say about multiple realizability and about cases where two systems share a mechanism in the path-patching sense but not in any compositional sense? I'd like an answer that reconstructs the implicit theory of mechanism individuation, distinguishes what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, and says what would have to change if the lab adopted a rival account.
