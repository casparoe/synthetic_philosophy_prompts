I'm putting together a night-class unit on pseudohistory and I'm trying to get past the usual checklist (cherry-picking, conspiracy, etc.). So I've been messing around with a more specific set of failure modes, and I want you to tier-rank them by how diagnostic they actually are: if a piece of history-writing shows this feature, how much should it move my credence toward this-is-pseudohistory rather than just this-is-bad-or-heterodox-history? Here are the markers I came up with:

1. Chronological compression: treating a long, well-attested sequence as a duplicate or refraction of a much shorter one (Fomenko-style).
2. Hyper-diffusion: explaining multiple independent inventions as borrowings from one lost ur-source.
3. Evidential inversion: treating the absence of expected evidence as positive evidence of suppression.
4. Scale-jumping: moving from a local anomaly or translation error to a global rewrite of a civilization.
5. Moralization of method: letting the political or religious stakes of the conclusion do the work of the evidence.
6. Source-capture: relying on a small set of texts while treating the rest of the corpus as corrupted by the orthodoxy.
7. Falsifiability theater: offering predictions that are only checkable after the theory has already been accepted.
8. Anachronistic coherence: making the past too neat, too legible, too much like a novel.

Give me a tier list (S/A/B/C/D or top/middle/bottom) of these, with a sentence or two each. Then, seperately, rank Atlantis, ancient aliens, and Fomenko's new chronology by how much each is a distinct challenge to normal historical method rather than just a different flavor of the same thing. I'm less interested in debunking than in the structure: which of these markers does each case load most heavily on? And say how confident you are in the whole ranking, and which call you're definately least sure about.
