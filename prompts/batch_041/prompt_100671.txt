Write a joke about the frame problem in artificial intelligence and philosophical logic. It should be funny to someone who actually knows the literature — the humour should turn on the real technical content rather than on the generic spectacle of a confused machine, and no reader should come away suspecting that the teller has only a vague idea of what the problem is.

So that the joke need not depend on any particular source, here is the content it is expected to work with. The frame problem is the difficulty a logical reasoner faces in representing what an action does not change: if the world is given as a set of statements and an action is performed, the reasoner must be able to conclude that the statements the action leaves untouched are still true, and the naive way of securing this — an explicit frame axiom for every fact the action leaves alone — requires an endless supply of axioms and invites the reasoner to check them one by one. Three difficulties are worth keeping apart: the frame problem proper, about what does not change; the qualification problem, about whether the action's preconditions actually obtain; and the ramification problem, about the indirect changes that do follow. The standard remedy is to stop deriving the persistence of every fact and let the reasoner assume by default that things stay as they are unless something defeats the assumption; the trouble is that it must then track the defeaters, which threatens to bring the original problem back in a new form.

To give the joke a spine, build on a term I have been using. Call an action self-contained if the only facts it alters are those its own description names. Almost nothing anyone does is self-contained, and doing nothing is not self-contained either, since time passes while one does it and the world does not hold still.

Invent a scene of your own rather than recycling the well-worn illustrations, keep it brief, and let the punchline fall on the line between the frame problem and its two neighbours, or on the thought that the only self-contained action would have to take no time at all.
