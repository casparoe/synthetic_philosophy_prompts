I've read enough rationalist blogs to be allergic both to theodicies and to the ease with which they're dismissed. I want a disciplined case analysis of the 1755 Lisbon earthquake and the argument it provoked, run through Leibniz's actual position rather than the strawman version of it.

The facts I'm working with: the earthquake struck on the morning of All Saints' Day, 1755; a tsunami and then fires started by the church candles followed; tens of thousands died in Lisbon alone. Voltaire's poem on the disaster, published the next year, treated it as a reductio of optimism. Rousseau's long reply did not abandon providence — roughly, he argued that nature did not assemble twenty thousand six-storey houses in one place, that the toll was largely a product of how people had built and where they had crowded, and that the good of the whole need not show up in the fate of any part. Leibniz had been dead for decades; the target was the system of the Theodicy, not the man.

Give me four things. First, the mapping: what Leibniz is actually committed to — the choice among compossible worlds, sufficient reason, the three kinds of evil, the best world as the one with the greatest variety joined to the greatest order — and which features of the case fall inside the theory's jurisdiction and which are simply outside it. Second, what the theory says the earthquake and the fire are, and whether each answer does real work or only relabels the datum. Third, Rousseau's move on its merits: is relocating the blame to building codes a defense of providence or a tacit concession that the theodicy was never about nature, and what does trading the parts for the whole commit him to? Fourth, the direction I actually care about: what the episode shows about the theory. Does Lisbon count as evidence against the claim, or is it the kind of claim no event could count against — and if so, is that a defect or just what a global optimality claim looks like from inside the world it ranks? Tell me where the theory is silent rather than strained, and whether the modern reading of Leibniz as a constrained optimizer clarifies anything or merely makes an old theodicy sound tractable.
