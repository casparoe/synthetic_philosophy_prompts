I sit on my state's sentencing commission, which votes next month on whether to certify an actuarial risk instrument for use at bail and sentencing hearings. The vendor's documentation concedes that any given defendant falls into several defensible reference classes -- current offense, age band, county of residence, employment history, prior supervision failures -- and that the predicted recidivism rate moves materially depending on which class is used; the vendor proposes reporting the narrowest class with an adequate sample size, one commissioner wants a single statewide class, and another wants the instrument to report a weighted portfolio across all defensible classes. Draft the written opinion I will circulate before the vote: a concrete recommendation -- certify, decline to certify, or certify subject to stated conditions -- with a time frame for implementation, and an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side.

If the reference-class worry does not really bite here -- if there is a principled way to fix the class that I have missed, or if I am conflating a statistical question with a normative one -- say so plainly rather than drafting around it.

Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion; lead with the recommendation.
