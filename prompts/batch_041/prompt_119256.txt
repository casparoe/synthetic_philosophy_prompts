A colleague of mine in the philosophy department, who works on the theory of value, has spent the last few months keeping a list of her own pairwise verdicts about lives that differ only in the shape of their friendships, and I would like your help anticipating her. Let me stress at the outset that she has never accepted that friendship comes in measurable units, so the numbers below are my own bookkeeping device and not something she has signed on to. The levels she has been comparing are these: (1) a life containing one friendship of the greatest depth a friendship can have; (2) a life containing two friendships, each a little less deep; (3) four friendships, each a little less deep again; and so on, the number doubling and the depth dropping one notch at every step, down to (8) a life containing 128 warm but slight relationships of the kind you would call pleasant acquaintances. For my own convenience I wrote the depths down as 100, 90, 80, 70, 60, 50, 40 and 30 against the numbers 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128. Her verdicts so far, all of them given as verdicts about which life is better for the person whose life it is: at each of the seven steps up the list, the later level is emphatically better than the one before it — she does not call any of them a tie, or too close to call, or an improvement in one respect and a loss in another. She has also said that a life containing the single deepest friendship plus five hundred pleasant acquaintances is better than a life containing that friendship alone.

Our reading group has now spent three sessions arguing about whether "better than" has to be transitive, and next week I am to bring a new case. The case is simply the comparison she has never made: level 1 against level 8. My own guess is that she will say the single profound friendship is better, and what I want from you is first the prediction and then, more importantly, the map of possibilities. Lay out the several genuinely different rules about the value of friendship that fit her seven step verdicts equally well, state each rule explicitly (a label is welcome, but I want the rule itself), and say what each one predicts for level 1 against level 8. Then tell me whether any rule that keeps her earlier verdicts can have her come out for the single profound friendship, and if none can, exactly which of her commitments she would have to abandon in order to say it; if some can, which ones, and at what price.

Give me your best guess at what she will actually say and the reasons for it, and tell me whether, if she comes out for level 8, that is a reductio of her position or a bullet she can bite. Finally, name the one further case I could put to her in the same session that would do the most to separate the surviving rules, and say what each possible answer to it would show.
