Hey — I'm putting together a little fiction-and-philosophy reading group and I want to commission a piece, so I'm hoping you can act as a short-story writer with a real feel for satirical speculative fiction — the kind where the joke is structural rather than a punchline, and the reader only figures out the rules of the world halfway through because the narrator never bothers to explain them. Think carefully about craft here, not just the idea.

The seed I want you to run with comes out of that old Hofstadter thing, the Person Paper — the essay where he smuggles an entire argument about sexist language past you by swapping the axis, so that a famous line about all men being created equal suddenly becomes a line about race and everyone can finally hear how strange it sounds. Don't redo that swap (gender-for-race); that one's been done to death and honestly a model like you probably has the whole trick memorized. I want the same machinery aimed somewhere it hasn't been aimed. Pick a fresh axis of default-versus-marked — handedness, birth order, the language your parents spoke at home, whether you were born in the city or came in off the districts, whatever — and build a small invented society around it where the default is invisible precisely because it's the thing you never have to mention. Here's some seeding so you're not just reaching for stock tropes: set it in a midsize river town with an old municipal institution — a licensing board, a ferry authority, a school placement office — where people queue. The story is one ordinary workday for someone inside that institution who is, by the society's lights, an exception, and the whole point is that the story must never say so. No character explains the system, no narrator lectures, no exposition paragraph. The reader should feel the default only through what gets a modifier and what doesn't, who gets a surname and who just gets a job title, which line on the intake form is left blank, who is asked to confirm something the others simply are. Then close with a short author's note in your own voice, two or three sentences, naming the asymmetry you planted and why it's the one that stings.

Before you draft, spend a beat brainstorming — list four or five candidate axes, pick the one least likely to be something I've seen before, and say why — then go. Aim for around a thousand words, keep the register dry and offhand, and don't moralize at the end. One more thing: please don't use the words "default" or "marked" anywhere inside the story itself, that'd blow the whole thing.
