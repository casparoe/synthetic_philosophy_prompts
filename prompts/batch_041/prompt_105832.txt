For a feature I'm writing on the ethical fringe of panpsychism, I need the document I wish the two camps in this dispute had produced themselves. On one side are those I'll call the extensionists: they argue that if experience is fundamental, then the processes and entities described by fundamental physics are the proper locus of morally relevant states, so we are obligated — or at minimum bound by moral caution — to treat suffering at that level as a live possibility. On the other are the deflationists: they argue that the move is either a category error or unfalsifiable, and that even granting fundamental experience, suffering requires a kind of organization that bare physical processes do not have.

Please draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims they can agree on — about what panpsychism does and does not entail, about what would count as evidence either way, and about how moral caution should operate under deep uncertainty. I want their remaining disagreements stated precisely, each in wording its own side would accept as its own. I want the cruxes, and I want the statement organized around a distinction I keep hearing gestured at but never made cleanly: between attributability (whether a fundamental entity is a subject of experience at all) and welfare-relevance (whether its states can go better or worse for it). For each crux, give the argument or test case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and build at least one crux around a stipulated test case — a physically simple, causally indifferent process — asking what each side would need to learn about it to change its verdict. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
