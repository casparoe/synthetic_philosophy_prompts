4th year PhD, half in philosophy half in econ, working on welfare measurement, and I need help with an argument a colleague handed me that I can't shake.

(1) A person's utility function is only ever defined up to a positive monotone transformation — nothing in their choices or attitudes pins down a unique numerical scale for them.
(2) If a quantity is only defined up to arbitrary monotone rescaling, then claims about the magnitudes of that quantity — that one is bigger, or bigger by more — have no determinate truth value.
(3) So claims comparing one person's utility with another's have no determinate truth value: not "A is better off than B," not "P helps A more than it hurts B," not "this transfer raises total welfare."
(4) But some of those claims are as solid as anything I know: someone dying of untreated thirst is worse off than someone who's a bit thirsty; a torture victim is worse off than someone who stubbed a toe.
(5) And without some cross-person comparisons, cost-benefit analysis, redistribution arguments, and the whole idea that a policy can make someone worse off all collapse.
(6) So either (1) or (2) is false. He says (1) is just ordinalism, which is the consensus, and (2) is just what "arbitrary scale" means, so the real casualty is (4)/(5) and policy evaluation is a value judgement in a lab coat.

I'd rather not give up (4) — that one's non-negotiable for me, it's the thing I think we know best. I tried the hardness-scale move: Mohs is an arbitrary scale and yet "diamond is harder than talc" is a plain fact, so (2) looked too strong. But that only buys me an ordering, and what I seem to need for (4) and (5) is a shared level and a shared unit, which an arbitrary scale really does look like it rules out. So I'm stuck between a premise that's too strong and a conclusion that doesn't go through, and I can't tell which.

Where does it break, and what does it cost me? If I keep (1), what has to be true for (4) to come out true — do I need utility to have a privileged scale after all, or is the comparison coming from something that isn't utility in the first place? And if the answer is "give up (2)," tell me exactly which of the comparisons in (3) it lets back in and which stay out, cause I don't want to pay for more than I'm getting.
