The topic is whether RL agents can suffer, with reward signals as the candidate for valence. Every version of this argument I've read stalls in the same place: either you need a theory of consciousness you don't have, or someone says the reward is just a number. My suspicion is that treating reward as valence conflates two things. First, in a standard MDP the zero point of the reward scale is arbitrary -- add a constant to every reward and no optimal policy changes -- so the formalism on its own can't distinguish pain from the mere absence of pleasure, whereas valence seems to need a sign that isnt merely relative to some baseline. Second, a scalar the designer hands the agent to maximize looks structurally different from a signal the agent's own machinery is built to keep within bounds, which is closer to how homeostatic regulation works (the dopamine prediction-error story is the obvious reference point).
