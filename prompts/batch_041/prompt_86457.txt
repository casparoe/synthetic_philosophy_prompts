I'm working through the cluelessness objection to consequentialism for a seminar, and I want to build a single case that separates two things people tend to run together: the kind of uncertainty where we simply don't know how an act's consequences will ramify, though there is in principle a fact about their expected value, and the stronger kind where no determinate expected value exists at all, because the downstream effects are open-ended or lack a well-defined distribution. Please construct that case for me to specification.

The specifications. It should be set in an ordinary institution — a hospital, a school, a small company, a household — and turn on one mundane act describable in a single sentence, with no exotic physics, no infinite or arbitrarily large utilities, no perfect predictors, and no stipulation that the consequences are unknowable; the unknowability has to fall out of the causal structure of the scenario. I want two variants that differ in exactly one respect. In the first, the act's expected value is well-defined but unknowable — the butterfly-effect case where any finite mind would have to trace an enormous causal chain to assess it. In the second, the expected value is itself ill-posed, so that it is not merely that we cannot compute it but that there is no fact of the matter about what it is. Make the difference between the variants turn on something concrete in the setup, and tell me what that something is.

Then do the analysis. Say what act consequentialism prescribes in each variant, and whether the prescription in the second is even meaningful; whether a rule consequentialist who has adopted a plausible general rule about the kind of act involved gets a more determinate answer or merely relocates the cluelessness; and which of the two positions the case actually embarrasses. Finish by naming the feature of the case that is doing the work, saying what happens if I remove or vary it, and stating how a committed consequentialist would push back — in particular, whether they can treat the second variant as just a harder version of the first. Be honest if the case turns out to collapse back into ordinary decision under risk.
