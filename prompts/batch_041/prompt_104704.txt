My reading group has been argueing for a year about gradual replacement and I want to force a document out of it. Camp 1: replace someone's neurons one at a time with functionally identical parts (silicon, whatever) and the person survives — same person, different stuff. Camp 2: all you've done is kill the original and build a replica who sincerely believes they're the original. We keep talking past each other.

Write the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a real adversarial collaboration. Structure it as:

1. Numbered agreed claims. What the thought experiment is actually probing; what we can and can't read off from the outside; where 'survival' and 'identity' might come apart or coincide; what the one-at-a-time gradualness does and doesn't settle; what both sides will admit they can't observe. Nothing vacuous — a claim that someone who thinks the whole question is confused would also happily sign is too weak, cut it.

2. A disagreements table. One row per residual disagreement, each stated in wording the side holding it would actually accept. No strawmen: if the anti-survival side would balk at being described as believing in some soul-substance, don't write it that way, and same in the other direction.

3. The cruxes. The sub-questions where settling it would move at least one side. For each crux, give the case, experiment, or argument both sides would agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction written down in advance. If you decide part of the dispute is purely verbal, say which part and why.

I want one wrinkle folded in, because I think it's the thing that actually breaks the tie and nobody in the group has dealt with it. The replaced parts aren't thrown away. Keep the original biological parts intact and working, and suppose they could in principle be reassembled and restarted. Now:

- run the replacement with the parts discarded vs the parts kept;
- run it backwards (reassemble the originals);
- run a version where the parts of two different subjects get pooled and mixed before reassembly — do you get two claimants, one, or none?
- and a version where each replacement part runs a hair slower, so the finished system is functionally a bit off.

Does either camp have to change its verdict on the plain one-at-a-time case because of any of these? Does the kept-parts version leave you with one claimant or two?

No citations or reading lists — we all know the literature and can't look anything up anyway. Just get the formulations exactly right, in wording each side would own. And at the end, tell me how confident you are in the joint statement and which single point you're least sure about.
