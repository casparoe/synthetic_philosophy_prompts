Please draft one essay question for the PhD comprehensive exam in value theory, answerable in ninety minutes, on lexical threshold negative utilitarianism — understood here as the view that suffering and enjoyment are not weighed on a single scale: there is a threshold of suffering such that reducing suffering below it takes lexical priority over any gain in enjoyment, however large, whereas above the threshold the two are traded off in the ordinary way. The question should be unanswerable by reciting the labels negative utilitarianism or incommensurability. It should compel candidates to argue whether the lexical threshold is a principled commitment or an ad hoc patch, whether positing the threshold presupposes that pleasure and pain are commensurable or denies it, and whether the view can meet the objection that a lexical ordering lets a small reduction of great suffering outweigh any amount of good. Then supply a model answer of roughly 700 words and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points worth four marks each, with a one-line note on the characteristic way candidates miss each point.
