I'm putting together an application for a small independent-research grant — six months, basically me and a laptop, no lab — and I need a two-page research proposal. I'll give you my half-formed idea and I want you to turn it into something with a sharp question, a real method, and an honest account of what would count as it failing.

The idea: people talk about prosaic alignment — getting current methods (RLHF, human-written constitutions, debate-style oversight, interpretability tools, the whole grab bag) to be enough, without waiting for some new insight about agency or about value. And the implicit story is that these are complementary. You get a bit from each and the bits add up. I don't beleive the adding-up part. My suspicion is that the prosaic methods split into two families that trade off against each other. One family targets mis-specification: the system is optimizing for something that isn't what we wanted, and you fix it by feeding the model more of the information we have — our judgements, our stated rules, our ability to check outputs. The other family is supposed to target mis-generalization: the objective is roughly right, but the policy that satisfies it in the training distribution doesn't in deployment. And here's the bit I can't get past: the first family works by handing the model more of what the overseer knows, which is exactly what raises the ceiling on how capable the trained system ends up being, which is exactly what makes mis-generalization worse. So maybe there's a conservation-law-ish thing here — every unit of mis-specification you buy down with a prosaic method buys some mis-generalization back, and the field reads the two curves as one curve going down.

What I want from the proposal: state the question so it has a determinate answer; say why the usual framing of prosaic alignment doesn't settle it; lay out what I'd actually do month by month (my rough plan is to pin the two failure modes down precisely enough to even state a tradeoff, build the smallest toy setting in which a tradeoff could show up, and then see whether it does — or whether it evaporates once I'm precise, which would be a result too); say what a positive and a negative result would look like and which considerations would decide between them; and give an honest account of how the project could fail (say, if the distinction collapses into the ordinary inner/outer alignment split, or if the toy setting is so toy that it proves nothing about the real methods).

Skip the bibliography, I'll handle citations myself. And don't pad it with why the area is important — the reviewers get plenty of that.
