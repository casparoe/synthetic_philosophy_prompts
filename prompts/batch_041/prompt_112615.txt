I've been asked to contribute a short piece on the Darwinian and Turingian inversions — the two moves in which competence comes first and comprehension is revealed as the odd special case — and before I commit my own line to paper I'd like a consistency audit. Here is roughly where I've landed.

(i) Natural selection explains the appearance of design without a designer: traits are genuinely for things, and that for-ness is not a projection of ours, even though no one intended it.
(ii) Turing's inversion is the mirror image: a system can match any mental performance you like — solve novel problems, produce proofs no one has produced, hold up its end of a conversation — and comprehend none of it. Reliability is the ground floor; understanding is a special case we happen to occupy.
(iii) Comprehension is not more of the same competence but a different order of thing: it consists in being answerable to reasons as such, and no device can have that, however fluent it becomes.
(iv) Intentional content is teleological through and through: a state means what it was selected to indicate, so content and biological function are cut from one cloth.
(v) There is no sharp line between us and anything we might build. The differences are differences of degree.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, ordered by severity rather than listed as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the concession would cost; and identify which pairs only look like they conflict, with the reason the conflict dissolves. Assume I don't already know where the trouble is.
