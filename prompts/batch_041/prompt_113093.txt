Is there a name for this? The version of the guilt/shame distinction I got taught was: guilt is about what you did, shame is about who you are, and shame needs an audience -- someone who could see you, real or imagined. What I can't fit is a case I've seen more than once. Someone does something wrong that nobody knows about and nobody ever will. The reaction isn't "I did a bad thing, I should make it right." It's "there's something wrong with me," plus a pull to hide -- except there's no one to hide from, so the hiding ends up being from themselves: they steer around the memory, avoid mirrors, stay busy. And it doesn't stay put. One small private failure bleeds into parts of the self that have nothing to do with it. Making amends doesn't touch it either, and there's nothing to make amends to, since the person they wronged never found out.

So: is there an established name for this exact shape -- shame-like reaction with no witness, aimed at the whole self, immune to repair? Or is it just shame and I'm splitting hairs? I've run into "moral injury," but I think thats about something else, and "self-disgust" sounds adjacent but not quite it. More than the label, I want the edges drawn: what this is, what it isn't, and where it shades into ordinary guilt or ordinary shame.

Second thing, maybe related. People talk about shame cultures versus guilt cultures and imply a society could just choose which one to run on. If the unwitnessed version above is real, then shame doesn't seem like something you can vote out -- it shows up even when nobody's watching. Does that follow, or am I conflating two different questions?

Answer in American English, please.
