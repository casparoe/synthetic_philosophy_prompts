Compose a satirical piece, of roughly 650 words, in the form of an internal customer-support log for a fictional Office of Universal Maintenance. In it, a mortal user files a sequence of bug reports and feature requests against the actual world — a request to delete earthquakes, a complaint about a persistent toothache, a demand that everyone be happy all the time, a proposal that he simply be issued a better world — and a deadpan celestial maintainer closes each ticket by appeal to Leibniz's claim that this is the best of all possible worlds. Format it as a numbered series of tickets, each pairing a short user message with a shorter maintainer resolution; keep the tone corporate, patient, and entirely straight-faced, with no winking asides and no acknowledgement that the doctrine is being mocked.

The comedy must come from the metaphysics being exactly right, so each resolution should turn on a genuine Leibnizian move rather than a generic shrug: the principle of sufficient reason; the principle of the best, on which God's will is constrained by what is best and so is not arbitrary; compossibility, on which individually possible perfections may fail to be jointly possible, so that the best world is the maximal compossible set of them rather than the world with the fewest defects; the measure of the best as maximal variety combined with maximal order, judged of the whole rather than part by part; the claim or striving that possibles are said to have toward existence, which God's choice adjudicates; and the traditional division of evil into metaphysical, moral, and physical. A reader who knows no Leibniz should still follow the complaints and enjoy the resolutions, while a reader who knows him well should find the parody fair rather than a strawman. The maintainer must never break character, never concede the doctrine false, and never import a later theodicy that Leibniz himself could not have given.
