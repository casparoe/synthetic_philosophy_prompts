Im a philosophy professor and I run a decision theory reading group with my colleague Priya, who has strong and sometimes idiosyncratic views. We've been spending the term on variants of the absented-minded driver problem -- the one where a driver on a highway with two exits can't remember whether he's already passed the first exit, so at each intersection he doesn't know if it's the first or the second. The standard payoffs are: exit at the first intersection, 0; exit at the second, 4; continue past the second, 1. Priya has given verdicts on a few earlier variants. In one, the payoffs were exit first: 0, exit second: 1, continue past second: 5, and she said the driver should always continue and never exit. In another, the payoffs were exit first: 10, exit second: 0, continue past second: 0, and she said the driver should always exit at the first intersection. In a third, the driver had full memory (not absent-minded) and the standard 0, 4, 1 payoffs, and she said he should continue at the first and exit at the second. Now I'm trying to predict what she'll say about the standard absent-minded case with the 0, 4, 1 payoffs. I suspect she'll either say "use a mixed strategy, continue with probability 2/3 and exit with probability 1/3" or "always continue." I'm less interested in the answer than in which rule she seems to be running -- is she maximizing ex ante expected value at the policy level, or is she doing something more like causal decision theory at the action level, or something else entirely? And what further case would best tell those rules apart? Give me your best guess, tell me how confident you are in it, and single out the point you're least sure about.
