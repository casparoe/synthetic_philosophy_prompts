I am not a philosopher by training, but I have been building a formal ontology for a knowledge graph, and I keep encountering a structural pattern that appears to be more than an engineering inconvenience. Suppose I wish to represent that two entities stand in a relation. I reify the relation as its own node, with edges to each entity. But the reified relation is itself an entity, and to say that it actually relates those entities I seem to need a further relation between the relation-node and each of its arguments. If I reify that further relation, the same demand arises one level up. If I refuse to reify it and simply assert the edge, I am treating the relating as primitive, which is precisely what I set out to explain. The general shape is this: any attempt to analyze what it is for a plurality of items to be unified into a single fact or thought introduces another item whose unity with the others must then be explained, and so on without end. I have a vague sense that this is an old metaphysical problem, but I do not know whether there is a standard name for the regress, whether it is generally considered vicious or benign, or how the standard responses differ from one another. Could you identify the established concept, distinguish it from other regress arguments such as the third man and the homunculus, and say whether my database case is an instance of the same problem or merely an analogy? If there is no exact established name for the database-specific version, I would like a precise name and definition that marks the boundary between the metaphysical issue and the merely practical one, including what would count as a solution and what would not.
