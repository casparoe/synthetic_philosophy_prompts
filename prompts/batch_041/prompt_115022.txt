ok so I'm a cog-sci PhD student and I'm trying to practice defending a claim that keeps getting shot down in seminar, so I want to rehearse it against someone sharp.

First the setup, in case it isn't fresh for you. Hofstadter's careenium is basically a giant pinball table stuffed with zillions of tiny magnetic balls (he calls them simms) careening around. Once they get magnetized they clump into stable higher-level structures, simmballs, and at the zoomed-out level those simmballs seem to stand for things out in the world -- the walls of the table change their bounciness depending on what's going on outside, so the simmballs end up tracking the world and interacting with each other. His question is who shoves whom: is all the pushing done by the simms, or do the simmballs do some of it too?

I want you to play a hardline simm-level reductionist. Be genuinely sharp, not a strawman -- someone who knows the causal exclusion argument cold and thinks that 'the simmball shoved the sim' is at best a handy shorthand and at worst a category mistake, that only simms push simms. You can definately concede that simmball-talk is indispensable for predicting and explaining, that we'd be lost without it, that it isn't "mere" in any practical sense. But you never concede that a simmball has any causal push of its own. Stay in character till I say stop, one paragraph or so per turn so it feels like a conversation, and don't let me win cheap.

My opening move: I can build a gadget inside the careenium -- think of it as a lock -- that only fires when one particular simmball pattern shows up. Now I can realize that same simmball out of a completely different clump of simms, swap the entire underlying substrate, and the lock still opens, because what it's keyed to is the pattern, not the individual balls. Same goes for predicting and controlling everything downstream: I can do all of it at the simmball level and just ignore which simms happen to be realizing it. So the effect tracks the simmball across all these different micro-realizations. If the simm-level facts already fixed what was going to happen, fine -- but then the simmball facts look like they're doing no work, and yet they're exactly what the effect is tracking. So either the simmball is doing the shoving, or you owe me a story about why the causal story keeps following the simmball around. Which is it?
