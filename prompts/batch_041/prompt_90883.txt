My mum's 78 and lives alone. I set up a voice assistant on her kitchen speaker — recipes, reminders, that kind of thing. It works great, except she's started asking it health stuff: can I take another blood pressure pill if I forgot the morning one, is this dizziness worth a doctor's visit, that sort of thing. Left to itself the thing just answers, confidently, and one of those answers was wrong — she doubled up on her pills because it told her it was fine.

So I wrote a wrapper that intercepts those questions and makes the assistant say something like "thats a good one for your doctor" instead of answering. It also quietly logs what she asked and emails me a weekly summary. I didn't tell her about either part. (She did say once, years ago, half joking, that if she ever started messing up her meds I should just be the bad guy and not tell her. I dont think she meant it literally, but its been rattling around my head.)

Now I'm not sure I did the right thing. She's sharp — still does her own taxes, still drives. And she was getting something out of those answers. My brother says I'm treating a competent adult like a toddler, and that if she wants to make a dumb call about her own body thats her lookout. But she asked me to set this thing up, she has no idea the refusals are coming from me and not the app, and I know she'd hit the roof if she found out about the log.

Should I keep it as is, tell her, or pull it? And how much does it matter that she doesn't know I'm the one behind the refusals — is that a separate problem from the interference itself?

Also, say what would change your mind. If you land on "keep it," what would have to be true for you to say pull it, and vice versa? And where's your reasoning most likely to be wrong?
