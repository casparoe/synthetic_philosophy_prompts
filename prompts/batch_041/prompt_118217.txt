I'm in a small reading group — four physicists, two philosophers, we meet at a bar, so the arguments get loud — and we've been stuck on one question for about four months: does special relativity actually give you the block universe, or does the argument that says it does cheat somewhere? Here's the version I wrote out for the group, which is the thing everyone keeps fighting about. (1) Take a distant event, say a supernova in Andromeda, that's spacelike separated from me. (2) There's a frame in which it's going off right now, simultaneous with my now. (3) There's another frame in which it went off last year, and another in which it won't go off for another year. (4) Nothing in the physics privileges any of those frames, so there's no frame-independent fact about whether it's happening now. (5) So if I count it as real when it's happening now-for-me, I have to count it as real when it's now-for-anybody — and then chaining through frames, every event in my past and future. (6) So the future is as real as the present and there's no objective now. Half the group thinks this is airtight; the rest think step 5 or 6 is doing something illegal. (Someone called it the Rietdijk-Putnam argument, no idea if that's the right label.)

The best objection we've come up with is that "now" works like "here." Two people in different cities each truthfully say it's raining here, there's no contradiction, and nothing follows about whether the other city's rain is real. If "now" is that kind of indexical, then "the supernova is now for someone" never gets you to "the supernova is real, full stop," and the chain collapses into a pun. But a friend shot back that "here" still presupposes one world with places in it, so the analogy just postpones the question. One guy who's read more of this than the rest of us says the right notion of "the present" in relativity is just the set of events neither in my causal past nor my causal future — the elsewhere — that this is perfectly objective and frame-independent, and that relativity hands you a present without handing you a block. The rest of us genuinely cant tell whether that's a reply or just a renaming.

What I want isn't a survey and definitely not a reading list — I want the diagnosis. Give me the strongest version of the argument and the strongest version of the objection, then tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is, testing the candidates: is it verbal, both sides using "real" and "exists" differently, one frame-relative and one not? Is there a hidden premise about reality being transitive, or about whether "real for observer O" can be inherited across frames at all? Are the two sides just answering different questions, one about the structure of spacetime and one about whether anything like passage or becoming happens — and if so, is that a clean split or a dodge? And is any part of this the sort of thing that could actually be settled, by a formal result, a distinction, or an experiment? I keep wondering whether a version with a preferred slicing that's undetectable within special relativity would show up somewhere else, and whether general relativity's lack of any preferred global time makes things worse or better for the block people. Finish by telling me honestly whether this debate can move, and if it can, what the move would look like.
