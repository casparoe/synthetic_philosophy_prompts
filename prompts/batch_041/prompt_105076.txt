I am assembling a specification before reading any particular account of democratic backsliding, and I want the desiderata first. Set out what any adequate account of the phenomenon must deliver: the cases it has to classify correctly (Hungary after 2010, Poland from 2015 to 2023 including its partial reversal, Turkey, the United States around 2020, and, as controls, an ordinary change of government and a case of full authoritarian breakdown); the distinctions it must not collapse (backsliding from autocratization and from breakdown; de jure authority from de facto control; erosion as a matter of degree from a threshold event; a government's intentions from the effects of its actions; lawful and gradual means from extra-legal ones); the facts it must accommodate (that the work is frequently done legally, incrementally, by elected governments with genuine mandates, and is sometimes undone); and the requirement that it say who is entitled to apply the label and on what evidence, and whether the label is descriptive or an act of political alignment. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it and mark it non-negotiable or the preference of one camp. Then identify any pair of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, whether an account can be at once neutral across ideological directions and substantive enough to condemn the standard cases — and say which single requirement does the most to rule out existing accounts. The response should say where its own reasoning is most likely to fail and what would change its verdict.
