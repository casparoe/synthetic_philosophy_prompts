3. The common-prior assumption is a substantive idealization that typically fails in real life. Most disagreement between well-informed people is traceable to different priors, and for that reason the theorem does not show that real people who share their evidence must agree.
4. Permissivism about priors: two agents who are both fully rational and who share exactly the same evidence can rationally hold different credences, and neither of them need be epistemically deficient.
5. There is no such thing as a rationally arbitrary prior. If two agents with the same evidence hold different priors, then at least one of them is epistemically deficient.
