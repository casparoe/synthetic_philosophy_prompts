The episode that keeps being cited is the one from 2022 in which a Google engineer, having published transcripts of his exchanges with LaMDA, publicly concluded that the system was a person; he was dismissed, and the company replied that the claims were unfounded and that the model was doing nothing beyond predicting text. I should like that episode analysed through Dennett's distinction between the intentional, design and physical stances.

Keep the mapping disciplined: what the stance is being applied to, what behaviour it is asked to predict, what would count as evidence for or against the attribution, and where the framework falls silent. Three things interest me in particular. First, is the engineer's move from the predictive success of the intentional stance to the system's actually having beliefs a mistake on Dennett's own terms, or does the instrumentalist reading of the stance make the further question of real belief idle? Second, does the company's design-stance reply defeat the belief attribution, or merely offer a competing explanation at a lower level? Third, can the stance supply any criterion for when it is the right stance rather than merely a useful one, given that the system has no environment of its own and no ends beyond continuing the exchange? Finally, say what the episode shows about the theory — a confirmation, an anomaly, or a case it never anticipated — and what follows for how someone in my position ought to describe what such a system believes.
