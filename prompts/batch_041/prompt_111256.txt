I teach a second-year metaphysics unit and I'm writing the final. The last essay is on persistance, and every year I get the same answer -- endurantists say an object is wholly present at each time, perdurantists say it has temporal parts, and the temporary intrinsics problem is the killer argument for perdurantism -- and then they stop. I want a question that pushes them past that recital. Specifically, I want them to assess whether the temporary intrinsics problem really favors perdurantism, given that the endurantist can just reply that the object is bent-at-t1 and straight-at-t2 (or that the properties are relational), and whether perdurantism actually solves the problem or merely relocates it -- after all, the four-dimensional worm as a whole never changes, only its parts do. Then I want them to say wether the whole endurantism/perdurantism dispute is substantive or merely verbal once both sides are allowed to relativize properties to times. Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme that names the specific moves that separate an A from a B answer (and the specific omissions that should cost marks). No need for citations -- just make the moves. Have the model answer state how confident it is and flag the point it's least sure about. American English, please.
