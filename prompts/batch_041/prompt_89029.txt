I've got to hand in a one-page "here's where I stand" thing for my metaphysics reading group on persistence, and before I circulate it I want someone to tell me whether the package actually holds together, because I've got a nagging feeling it doesn't and I genuinely can't see where the crack is. Here's the pile. (1) I'm an endurantist: when a bike or a cat exists at more than one time, the whole thing is there at each of those times — it doesn't have temporal parts, and it isn't strung out through time the way a road is strung out through space. (2) I'm a universalist about composition: take any things you like and there's a single object that's just those things fused, and composition is never vague or restricted, so yes, there is an object composed of my bike and the moon. (3) I don't believe in coincident entities — you never get two distinct material objects in exactly the same place at the same time; the statue and the clay are one object under two descriptions, not a pair of things sitting inside each other. So if you squash the thing flat, the statue's gone and the clay's still there, and that's not two objects, it's one object that's stopped being a statue. (4) Ordinary objects survive losing and swapping parts: my bike is the same bike after I swap the rear wheel, my cat is the same cat after the vet shaves her. (5) I'm a B-theorist: past, present and future are all equally real, and there's no objective now doing any metaphysical work. (6) I don't think identity or existence can ever be indeterminate — there's always a determinate fact about whether a thing exists and whether a is b. (7) And I think personal identity isn't what actually matters in survival; what matters is psychological connectedness, which comes in degrees.

What I want: go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which ones only look like they do.
