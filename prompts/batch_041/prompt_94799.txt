Act as a philosopher of mind who knows the margins-of-error and self-knowledge literature inside out, and start by brainstorming three or four candidate cases before you commit to one — I want to see the options. Background: I spent 35 years at the bench and have been reading philosophy since I retired, mostly epistemology, and I keep running into the idea that knowing something doesn't guarantee that you know that you know it. The illustrations everyone reaches for are ones I've seen a hundred times — sorites of temperature, barn facades, lottery tickets, brains in vats, clocks that stopped at midnight — so build me a fresh one to spec.

The spec: an ordinary-life scenario, one paragraph, in which someone knows a thing but does not know that they know it, and is not being sloppy — careful, well-informed, using their best method, nothing a sharp colleague could call careless. Stipulate that they are a good witness to their own mind: they can tell you what they believe and what their reasons are, no self-deception, no bad self-assesment. No fake barns, no lotteries, no sceptical scenarios, no gadgets, and don't string it out as a long run of imperceptibly different cases — one step in the margin is enough.

Then do the bookkeeping, and don't just assert it: name the belief, the evidence, the method, the nearby cases, and say why the first-order belief is knowledge while the second-order one isn't. Tell me which single feature is doing the work, what happens if I take it out (does KK come back?), and how someone who reads KK as the claim that if you know then you're in a position to know that you know would try to defuse the case — and whether that defence actually works or just moves the problem along. Last thing, which I genuinely can't work out on my own: if the case works, is it really because she can't tell, at the margins, how strong her own evidence is? And if so, have I just smuggled in a failure of transparency about her own mind, which would be cheating given what I stipulated? If some of my constraints can't all be satisfied at once, tell me which one to drop and why.
