I work on ML infrastructure, no formal philosophy background, and I keep getting dragged into an argument on my team that never resolves. One camp says our models obviously have internal representations: a linear probe can predict a property from activations, ablating a direction changes behavior, and steering along it changes outputs. The other camp says none of that settles it: a probe can decode information the network never uses, an ablation can break something unrelated, and behavioral sensitivity isn't the same as the model representing anything in the way we do. We agree on the experimental results and disagree about what they show, and we've been circling for months. I suspect this is the realism debate in interpretability and that the disagreement isn't empirical: is it verbal, with one side meaning decodable information and the other meaning a causally privileged internal role, or is it substantive, about what counts as evidence, or two different projects—prediction versus causal explanation—sharing one word? If I add a distinction between a representation being recoverable, being used, and being explicitly represented as a representation, does that dissolve the impasse or just relocate it? Diagnose the structure of this dispute and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a formal result, a new experimental paradigm, or a decision to stop asking the question.
