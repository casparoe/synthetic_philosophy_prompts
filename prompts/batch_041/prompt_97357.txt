You are a philosopher of value working on the structure of comparative choice, and I need a case built to order rather than a survey of the literature. Before writing, brainstorm several candidate scenarios and think carefully about which one can actually meet every constraint; then commit to one.

Construct a realistic, ordinary-life choice between two options that stand to each other neither as better, nor as worse, nor as equal, but in the relation sometimes called parity: comparable enough that a rational agent may prefer either, yet not ranked by any common measure. Constraints: the options must differ in kind, not be two quantities of the same good; the choice must be one an ordinary adult could face (a career against a relationship, one city against another, a vocation against a vocation); no science fiction, no stipulated rankings, and no appeal to a special faculty; and the case must not be defused by "it doesn't matter, just pick one" — it should still present itself as a hard choice.

Then run the test that separates parity from equality. Identify a small improvement to one option, along a dimension both options share, so that it cannot be dismissed as irrelevant; design the main case so that this improvement does not make the improved option better. Give a control case, identical except for a single feature, in which the same improvement does tip the balance.

After the two cases, do the following. (1) For each of the four candidate relations — better, worse, equal, on a par — state the verdict it yields and why the first three fail in the main case. (2) Name the feature that renders the small improvement inert, and show that removing it collapses the case either into equality or into a plain ranking. (3) Say which broad view of value would deny that the main case is even coherent, and how its defender would attack it.

This is for a graduate seminar on incommensurability, so what counts as cheating is a case that stipulates the verdicts instead of generating them from the features of the options. Keep the whole response under roughly 700 words.
