I'm putting together a little teaching unit on how to update your beliefs when the evidence itself is shaky — not "I saw the coin land heads" but "I'm pretty sure I saw heads, though I could be wrong." What I want is a dialogue between two Bayesians who both know the technical setup and genuinely disagree about what to do.

One is a strict conditionalizer: she says the only legitimate rule is to conditionalize on a proposition you're certain of, so in the shaky case you conditionalize on a proposition about your own perceptual state — "my visual experience was such-and-such" — and then just get on with it. The other is a Jeffrey conditionalizer: he says you should take your uncertain evidence at face value and update by weighting your old conditional credences by your new credence in each possible evidential state, and that the strict view smuggles in a false certainty about your own mind while making your update depend on how you happen to describe the experience.

Let them really go at each other. I want the strict side to press the Jeffrey side on whether his rule is even well-defined — which partition are you summing over, and why that one rather than another? — and on the fact that his updates dont commute, so updating on A and then B can give a different answer than B and then A. And I want the Jeffrey side to have actual answers rather than folding. Have them land on one concrete case — say, glancing at a thermometer that might be off by a degree, or a somewhat unreliable witness — and show where the two rules give different numbers and why that matters for what you should actually do. Neither one should be a strawman; both are experts, and they should respond to each other's specific points instead of talking past each other.

Wrap up by saying how confident you are in the technical claims you made and flag the one point you're least sure of. American English, please.
