I was having dinner with an old friend of mine, Dana, who teaches at a law school, and we got onto whether everyday conventions — tipping, queueing, jaywalking, holding doors, that whole family of things — are actually moral obligations or just etiquette that people moralize about. What struck me is that Dana has very settled, almost viseral verdicts about these, and they don't obviously all come from one principle. Her track record, as best I could get her to state it: she thinks it's genuinely *wrong* — not just tacky — to leave no tip at a sit-down restaurant where the server is depending on tips and the service was fine; to push ahead of a long, orderly line at airport check-in by pretending you're about to miss your flight; and to take a loud phone call in a designated quiet carriage. She thinks it's *not* wrong at all to jaywalk across an empty street at 2 a.m. with no cars anywhere in sight, or to wear a baseball cap in a restaurant where it's mildly frowned on but bothers no one. And she insists the tip and the queue cases are wrong in the same way and for the same reason.

Here's the case I want you to call for her. A German colleague is spending a semester with us. He's been here two months, speaks fluent English, and knows perfectly well that people queue at the food trucks on campus and that you're expected to tip at the sit-down places. Last week he walked straight past a fifteen-person line at a food truck and ordered at the window. My hunch is that Dana will say he did nothing wrong — something about him not being a party to our little practices, so we can't hold him to them — but I don't see how that squares with the rest of what she says. So: tell me what Dana will actually say about him, what rule she's really running, whether some other rule fits her earlier verdicts just as well, and where the two come apart.

Then tell me which of those rules *I* should accept, and name the further case that would force the choice between them. Don't hedge and don't hand me a menu of positions — give me your best guess about Dana, a definite verdict of your own, and the reasons. Keep the whole thing under 500 words.
