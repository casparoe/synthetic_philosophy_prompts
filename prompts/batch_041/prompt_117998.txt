I am trying to pin down a concept, and I suspect that no single established term covers it. I care much less about the label than about the boundaries, so please do not stop at a name.

I have spent my working life in software and my weekends volunteering with a land trust that manages a few hundred acres of old field and second-growth woodland; a friend who is a conservator supplies my third case. In each setting I keep running into the same shape of thing, and I have never found a word for it.

A plant is a weed in the vegetable beds. There I can be straightforwardly wrong about which plants are weeds, and when I am wrong it is an empirical mistake that someone who understands competition and germination can correct me on. Move the same plant to the pollinator meadow and it is not merely a tolerated non-weed; it is a plant we are trying to establish, and a volunteer who pulls it has done harm. The handbooks are admirably candid here: a weed is defined as a plant in the wrong place. A program behavior is a defect against the specification we shipped and an intended feature against the specification we are now writing; the same unaltered line of code crosses from one to the other. A darkened bronze surface is either accumulated damage to be reversed or the object's history to be preserved; the same atoms, the same measurements, and opposite instructions.

What strikes me is the combination. First, once the purpose is fixed the category is not soft at all: it has a real extension, sharp edges in practice, and expert disputes that evidence can settle. Second, across purposes the category does not merely grow or shrink at the margins; it inverts, so that something can be a central member under one purpose and a central non-member under another. Third, the purpose is not arbitrary either, since it is constrained by the thing itself, by what can be measured, and by what people actually care about, and yet I cannot find a purpose-free fact that settles the matter. Fourth, the purpose is not stable: a modest change in it, or in the earlier state I am comparing the present one against, can flip a great many memberships at once, and I have never seen a non-question-begging place to stop adjusting it. Fifth, people argue about these categories as though they were ordinary empirical ones — the science says this species is invasive — and I do not think that is simply a confusion.

I have tried the obvious replies. Whether these are the kinds of categories the sciences discover, and whether the ones that are not are merely conventions, both seem to me to be the wrong question, because I am happy to grant that these categories are perfectly real. I know that many categories are held together by overlapping similarities rather than by a definition; but overlapping similarity is about fuzzy edges, and my cases have sharp edges once the purpose is fixed. I know that some words are half description and half appraisal, so that applying them already commits you to caring about something; but appraisal alone does not explain why the answer is discoverable by fieldwork. I know that classifying things can change the things classified; but that is a further fact about some of my cases, not the thing itself.

So: does this have a name, or a small family of names that are usually kept apart? If an established concept fits, I want to know which one fits best, which ones are near misses, and exactly where my description departs from each. If nothing fits, coin a term, define it, and tell me where its edges are — cases where the purpose is contested, where two purposes are held at once, or where the purpose seems to be discovered rather than chosen. I do not need a reading list or citations; I would have no way to check them, and it is the carving I am after.
