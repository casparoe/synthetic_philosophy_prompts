I am a second-year PhD student in computational biology, and I keep running into a cluster of claims in the philosophy of biology that I cannot get a grip on in the terms I actually use: that DNA is a code, that genes carry information, that the genome is a program, that it is a language. I know what those words mean in my own technical settings — formal language theory, coding theory, Shannon information, programming languages — and I want to know how much of the biological claim can be rebuilt out of exactly those materials rather than out of an imported metaphor.

Take the strongest defensible version of the claim that genetic material encodes or carries information about the organism's parts and products, and translate it into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Use the resources I have: alphabets and strings; a code as a mapping between two alphabets; redundancy and error-correcting structure; a source, a channel, a receiver, and noise; a grammar and its derivations; a program and its interpreter; a lookup table versus a rule. Make the translation concrete where you can: is the codon table a degenerate mapping, and does its degeneracy do anything a coding theorist would recognize as error-tolerance? Is the reading frame a parsing problem, and if regulation and splicing make the "meaning" of a stretch of sequence depend on cellular context, is that context-sensitivity in the formal-language sense or something else? Is a point mutation a bit flip, and if so what is the channel? If you can get the claim to come out clean in these terms, do that first; I would rather see a translation that nearly works than a verdict that it cannot be done.

Then give me the residue report. Separate three things. First, the parts that translate cleanly, where my vocabulary is enough. Second, the parts that translate only with distortion — where I have to quietly add something my vocabulary does not supply, such as a reader, a purpose, a convention, or a distinction between a signal and a mere cause. Third, the parts that have no counterpart at all: claims that appear to be about meaning, about error and misrepresentation, or about the sequence representing something, which my vocabulary can only restate as further causal and structural facts.

I care most about that third category, and about your judgment on what it shows. Is the residue a defect of my vocabulary — something a better technical framework could capture — or is it evidence that the biological claim is doing work that the word "code" is smuggling in and that would be better stated without it? I want the translation attempted seriously before I am told that information talk in biology is just a metaphor; if that is the conclusion, I want to see it earned by the translation failing in a specific place rather than asserted up front.
