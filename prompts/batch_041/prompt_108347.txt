I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training. Most of my career has been spent reverse-engineering undocumented systems: a black box that consumes and emits bytes over a wire, or a service whose only public interface is its behavior, and I have to recover its wire format, its opcodes, and eventually a semantics for it. My working vocabulary is protocol inference, differential fuzzing, observational equivalence, round-tripping, parser and interpreter construction, latent-state inference, and the occasional bit of model checking. I am fluent in that vocabulary and I trust it considerably more than I trust philosophical prose.

I have been reading about Davidson's account of radical interpretation: an interpreter meets a language with no shared vocabulary and nothing to go on but the speakers' observable behavior in their environment; to get anywhere she must assign meanings to their utterances and attribute beliefs and desires to them at the same time, under some principle of charity; the whole assignment is holistic, so no single utterance can be fixed independently of the rest; there is no standpoint outside the interpretation from which to check it against the facts; and the result is indeterminate in a way that is supposed to be more than a confession of ignorance.

Restate that position, as faithfully as you can, in my vocabulary. The interpreter becomes someone inferring a spec for a black box from I/O traces; meaning becomes a semantics for the language the box implements; belief and desire become the latent state you are forced to posit to make the traces compress; charity becomes a prior or a regularizer; indeterminacy becomes underdetermination of the spec by all possible traces. Use my terms as precisely as they will bear.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the position map cleanly onto inference of a behavioral spec? Which map only with distortion, and where exactly did you have to bend my vocabulary, or bend the philosophy, to make the fit? Which parts have no counterpart at all in how I think about reverse-engineering — name them explicitly. Two things I want you to resist: do not quietly assume the black box has one true spec that my inference is converging on, since I suspect that assumption is precisely one of the things at issue; and do not tell me the residue is just my vocabulary being less expressive unless you can say what specifically it fails to express and why that is different from the original claim being vague.

For each piece of residue, say whether it is a defect of my vocabulary — something an engineering account of meaning ought to have and lacks — or a sign that the philosophical claim was doing less work than it appears to. Then state how confident you are in the overall verdict, and identify the single point you are least sure of. End your response with a final line of exactly the form \answer{...} containing one of: engineer-vocabulary-defect, philosophical-unclarity, mixed.
