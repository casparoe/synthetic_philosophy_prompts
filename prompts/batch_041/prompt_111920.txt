Ok so I'm putting together a zine of fake documents from imaginary political systems, and I need one from you.

Set it in a small midwestern town -- call it Marrowfield, pop. around 12,000 -- that switched over to futarchy about eight years ago. If you dont know it: futarchy is the real proposal where people vote on the values and then bet on the facts. The town has an official welfare score (some weighted mix of median income, life expectancy, a survey, whatever) and every actual policy question gets settled by a prediction market. If the market says passing the school bond will raise the score, it passes. Zoning, the water utility, library hours, all of it.

What I want is the town's advice column. A Dear Abby type thing in the local alt-weekly. Give it a straight name, no winking. Around 600-700 words: three reader letters with the columnist's replies, and if you want, a one-line editor's note at the top.

Rules for the humor:

- The columnist is a true believer and never breaks character. No "of course, the system has its critics" throat-clearing, no explaining the joke, nobody ever says the name of a real critique out loud. The reader should figure out whats wrong from the details alone.
- Every joke has to hang on an actual mechanic of the idea. The stuff I'd love you to mine: conditional markets (the "if this passes, will the score be higher in two years?" kind) and who gets to resolve them; the fact that the score itself is a political choice, so the people who trade on it have a strong interest in how its measured; thin markets and subsidized market makers on boring questions like the fence-height ordinance; the gap between the market's forecast and what actually happens; and the slow pileup of patches the town keeps adding -- a minimum-volume rule before a market is binding, a "common sense" override nobody can quite define, a tiebreak by the mayor.
- One letter should be from somebody who's mad that a market was obviously gamed but the rules say it wasnt cheating. One should be about who decides what the welfare score even measures. The third is up to you.
- It has to be fair. Somebody who actually likes futarchy should read it and think "yeah, those are the real soft spots," not "thats a strawman."

No real people in it -- invented mayor, invented officials, invented town. American English please. And dont let it turn into a list of complaints; it should read like a real advice column, just one where the advice is quietly insane.
