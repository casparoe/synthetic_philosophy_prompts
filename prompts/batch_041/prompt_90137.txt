I am a high school student assembling a philosophy project, and I would like you to construct a thought experiment to specification rather than simply illustrate a familiar topic. I want a realistic case in which a high schooler faces a choice that is transformative in the following sense: before undergoing the experience she cannot know what it is like, and undergoing it will change her preferences, not merely her information. The case must make two decision procedures deliver opposite verdicts. The first is the revelation procedure: because the experience is epistemically transformative, she cannot assign genuine utilities to the outcomes, so the rational response is to choose the option whose value will be revealed in the undergoing, or to treat the choice as a leap rather than a calculation. The second is ordinary expected-utility maximization using her present preferences as authoritative, with uncertainty about the experience treated as uncertainty about a state of the world rather than as an absence of the relevant values. Constraints: realistic and contemporary; no science fiction, no vampires, no mind-uploading, no life-threatening illness, no psychedelics; the scenario should be one paragraph and could happen to an ordinary student; and the transformation must be socially ordinary, such as joining a demanding religious order, enlisting, moving abroad, or committing to a career that will reshape her tastes. I also want the case to turn on a feature that is not part of the standard discussion: the experience is reversible in the sense that she can later return to her previous social role, but returning does not restore her original epistemic position, because afterward she knows what the experience was like and that knowledge cannot be undone. Call this asymmetric reversibility. Build the case so that the two procedures disagree, and make the disagreement depend on asymmetric reversibility rather than on a trick in the payoffs. After the scenario, do the demonstration: state the choice, the options, the relevant states, and what each procedure recommends and why. Then tell me which feature is actually doing the work, what happens if you remove asymmetric reversibility and make the return fully restorative, and how a defender of the expected-utility procedure would argue that the case is rigged or that the revelation procedure is not a genuine alternative. I care more about the structure of the case than about which option is finally recommended. No citations or reading lists; I want the case and the analysis.
