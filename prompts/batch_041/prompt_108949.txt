ok so theres this thing i keep noticing and i dont know if it has a name.

like last month basically our whole grade decided the new taco place by the mall was amazing. i went with my friends and it was fine, honestly kind of mid. but heres the weird part — when i actually asked people, most of them also thought it was just fine. turns out like three people genuinely loved it, and everyone else just assumed everyone else loved it, so they told other people it was great, and now its just a fact that its great. same thing happened when our teacher asked if everyone got the homework and the first two people said yeah — i definately did not get it but i wasnt gonna be the only one, and im pretty sure half the class was in the same boat. and in the class president vote everyone kept saying they were voting for the same girl so i did too, even though if youd asked me alone id probably have picked someone else.

what i cant figure out is the name for this. its not just peer pressure because nobody was forcing anyone, and its not that people are dumb — everyone was actually being pretty reasonable, treating what other people did as evidence for whats true. its also not a fad or trend where people just copy each other for no reason. the thing that bugs me is the group ends up "knowing" something that no single person actually believed, and it seems to get worse the more people join in rather than better.

is there a name for this exact pattern? and i dont just want the label — i want to know how its different from groupthink and conformity and "the wisdom of crowds," like what actually makes it its own thing, and where the line is where it stops being this and turns into something else. if theres no name for it can you make one up and tell me what counts and what doesnt.
