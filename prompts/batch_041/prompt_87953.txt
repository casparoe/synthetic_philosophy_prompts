Okay, I'm a novelist, but I take copywriting gigs between drafts to keep the lights on, and the current one I want to have a bit of fun with. I need you to write a strategy memo — the kind a slick consultancy sends a client to sell them on managing their corporate "heritage" — except the whole thing is secretly Nietzsche's three ways of doing history (monumental, antiquarian, critical) repackaged as three productized service lines. Deadpan throughout, no winking, and never name Nietzsche or say you're doing this; the reader should be able to enjoy it as a real memo and only a philosophy person should slowly realize what's happening.

The joke has to come from the actual logic of each mode and, more importantly, from its built-in disease, so get these right: monumental history is for the person who needs models to act, but it mythologizes, blurs what's different, and can slide into fanaticism; antiquarian history is pious reverence for where you came from that preserves but tends to mummify the present and decay into mere hoarding; critical history judges and breaks up the past, but since we're made of the thing we're condemning, it risks leaving the client rootless. Roughly 600 words, formatted like a memo with headings for each service line, and let the third one get a little out of hand in a way that a careful reader notices. If you can work in a fourth thing they're reluctant to actually sell — the option where you decide nothing genuinely new can ever happen and just contemplate the eternal sameness of it all — that would be the icing.

End on something that reads like a mission statement but is really stating the underlying tension that all this history is only worth anything if it serves life. No citations or reading lists obviously, it's a sales document.
