Im putting together the handout for the first meeting of a reading group on knowledge, and I want to start with the desiderata before we get into any particular theory. The area is the tracking idea — the sensitivity condition, roughly that if the thing had been false you wouldnt have believed it — and the trouble it gets into with closure, i.e. whether knowing P and knowing that P implies Q gives you Q.

Give me the list of what any theory of knowledge in this area has to deliver, each item with the case or consideration behind it. Off the top of my head the list should cover: the Gettier-style luck cases (the stopped clock that happens to read right, the barn facade, the man who is right by accident); the lottery, where you are near-certain your ticket lost before the draw and yet nobody wants to call it knowledge; the difference between knowing and merely getting it right; memory, testimony and induction, where the tracking condition starts to sound strange (if it had been false, would you not have remembered it? would your friend not have told you?); necessary truths, where the tracking condition looks like it comes free because the antecedent can never be met; and, if the theory talks about reliable processes, some requirement that pins down which process a belief came from, since otherwise the theory is underdetermined.

Two things I want in there that are less standard. First: a case where your belief is true, and if it had been false you would not have believed it, but only because a neuroscientist was standing by ready to zap you into believing it anyway. My gut says you still know, the sensitivity condition seems to say you dont, and I want the desideratum that decides which of those reactions to trust. Second: I keep thinking the list should say whether the theory is required to be knowable by its own lights — if the tracking view is right, does anyone get to know that it is right, and is that a bug or just what the view says.

Then the part I actually want most: which of these cannot all be satisfied together. I am fairly sure closure, fallibilism and not sliding into skepticism cant be had at once, and I want that argued rather than asserted, plus any other pair or triple you think is jointly unsatisfiable — that would be the real result of the exercise.

Also tell me which items are genuine requirements and which are just a camp's preference — e.g. is it a real desideratum that knowledge not depend on facts the knower could never get at, or is that internalism sneaking in? And does the list change between single-premise closure (deduce Q from one thing you know) and multi-premise closure (conjoin a hundred things you know), since the lottery and the preface paradox seem to pull those apart?

No scoring of the actual theories, I'll do that in the group, and no citations needed. Bullet points are fine, keep it casual.
