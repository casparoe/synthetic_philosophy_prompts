I was up half the night argueing with my mate Priya about G. E. Moore, of all people — she's doing a master's in ethics, I'd been re-reading the last chapter of Principia Ethica, and we ended up going round in circles about whether Moore can actually have it both ways. The thing that started it was his method of absolute isolation: to find out whether something is intrinsically good, you're supposed to imagine a world containing just that thing and ask whether it's better than nothing. Fine. But Moore also holds the doctrine of organic unities — the value of a whole isn't the sum of the values of its parts — and he uses that to say that the appreciation of a beautiful object, or the love of a person, is a great good. Priya's point was that if the value of the appreciation is organic, then you can't isolate the beautiful object and ask what it's worth on its own, because its contribution is only realised inside the whole. Yet Moore seems to want to say that beauty and friendship are the two great goods, and that the value of each depends on the nature of its object — a beautiful thing, a good person — which sounds like it's the object that's doing the work, not the whole. I want to set this as an essay question for my upper-level seminar, because I think it separates people who've actually thought about Moore's method from people who've just memorised "beauty and friendship are the goods."

Could you write me the exam question — something that forces them to confront the isolation/organic-unity tension as it applies specifically to beauty and to friendship, and to say what Moore would have to give up to make the view consistent — plus a model answer of about 800 words and a marking scheme? For the marking scheme, I want maybe five must-hit points worth 20 marks total, and for each one a one-line note on the mistake that costs students the marks (e.g. treating the isolation test as if it just asks "would you prefer this to nothing?", or treating organic unities as a mere summing rule). Also, don't let them get away with saying "Moore values friendship more than beauty" without saying what in his account licenses that ranking, if anything does. The whole thing should be answerable in 60 minutes, no notes, and please write it in British English — I'm marking enough American spellings as it is.
