Every year six of us rent the same big house for a long weekend in October, and the four bedrooms are definately not equal — one has a balcony, one has the good bathroom, one's over the garage so you hear cars pulling in all night, and one's just a normal room. A few years back my buddy Marcus made up a rule as a joke: if you want somebody else's room, you pay them thirty bucks and you trade for the night. It stuck, now it's a whole tradition, real cash changes hands, and because I'm the responsible one I end up holding the money in a shoebox.

Here's what's been bugging me. By Sunday we almost always end up in exactly the rooms we started in — Marcus back on the balcony, Priya back in the quiet one — except now there's like four hundred dollars in the shoebox and everybody is sure they came out ahead. I have literally watched the same three people do the same loop twice in one weekend: Priya pays Marcus for the balcony, then Marcus pays Dev for the quiet room, then Dev pays Priya to get back over the garage, and around it goes. When I brought it up, Marcus said I'm overthinking it, because every single trade is voluntary and both people say yes at the time, so every trade is a win-win and nothing's broken — if everyone's happy, what's the problem? My cousin who took some econ classes said it reminded her of something from a textbook but couldn't remember what. And then Dev asked me a weird favor: he wants me to stop accepting swaps from him after the first night, and keep his thirty dollars whether he ends up asking for a trade or not, "to protect him from himself."

So — is Marcus right that it's all fine because each swap is voluntary? If he's wrong, is the problem with each person's choices in the moment, or with the weekend as a whole, or with me for running the shoebox? Is Dev's request a reasonable thing for me to go along with, or is it a little creepy that he wants me to hold him to a deal he might beg me to break? And tell me what would change your mind — what would I have to turn out to be wrong about for you to decide it's all actually harmless after all?
