ive been reading rationalist blogs and forums for like a decade and there's one recurring fight that I swear definately never moves, and I want you to diagnose it instead of just taking a side.

It's about the common prior assumption. The setup: Aumann's agreement theorem — if two agents share a common prior and their posteriors about some event become common knowledge, then their posteriors are equal, so they can't agree to disagree. Half the people I read treat this as a huge result: persistent disagreement between two people who respect each other's reasoning must mean they differ in information, full stop. The other half say the common prior is doing all the work and is either false or empty — if two people really disagree you just say they had different priors, and since any disagreement can be re-described that way, the theorem tells you nothing about actual disputes. Then it goes in circles: "you're just denying the Harsanyi doctrine," "no, you're smuggling in a substantive empirical assumption and calling it rationality."

My working hypothesis is that it's not one disagreement at all. One side is making a claim about how to model agents — the common prior as a kind of normalization, a gauge or coordinate choice you're free to make, so the theorem is a consistency constraint on a model rather than a fact about people. The other side is making a claim about rationality — that two ideally rational agents with the same information can't hold different priors, so the theorem is a normative claim about how we ought to converge. Those are different questions wearing one name.

Is that right, or is there a real substantive disagreement underneath (about what counts as "the same information," about whether a prior is a psychological state or a modeling parameter, about what rationality even demands)? And is part of it just verbal — people using "prior" for two different things? Tell me which kind of disagreement each move in the exchange actually is, grounded in what each side is committed to, and then tell me honestly what would count as progress here — some distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or whether this one is just permanently stuck and we should stop expecting it to resolve.

Also, someone on a forum said the common prior is like choosing a coordinate system in general relativity: no physical content by itself, just needed to write anything down. I can't tell if that analogy is illuminating or if it's sneaking in a conclusion. Address that too.
