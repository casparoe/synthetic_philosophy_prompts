ok, so i'm a 4th-year PhD student in HPS (history & philosophy of science, basically) and i need help diagnosing a fight, not winning one. our reading group has now spent six weeks on the demarcation problem and nothing has budged. the shape of it is always the same: someone says falsifiability is the answer; someone else says it can't be, because you can never strictly falsify a statistical hypothesis, and Duhem–Quine means no hypothesis is ever tested on its own, and the falsificationist line has been used to throw out perfectly good science while waving in stuff that's just as bad; then the first person says fine, but then what do we tell the school board about astrology and creationism, and everyone goes quiet; and around week four somebody always rediscovers the arguement that the whole thing is a leftover of logical empiricism, that there is no such thing as 'science' to demarcate, and we should all just stop asking — except we can't stop asking, because we're all reviewing for journals that keep rejecting papers as 'unscientific' and none of us can say what that means, and half the department is now arguing about whether the machine-learning people down the hall are doing science at all.

my working hypothesis — and i want you to actually test it, not nod along — is that this isnt one stalled debate but at least three questions wearing one name: (a) a descriptive/sociological one about how the word 'science' actually gets used, (b) a normative one about what deserves credence or institutional trust, and (c) a political one about who gets to decide what's taught, funded and published. and the reason we never move is that we keep sliding between them mid-sentence.
