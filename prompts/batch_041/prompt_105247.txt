I've been a reader of the rationality blogs and forums for long enough to have a friend from that world, and she has spent years writing about polarization and bad-faith argument. She insists she can tell, case by case, whether a given writer is arguing in good faith, and I've been trying to reverse-engineer her actual test, because whenever I ask her for the criterion she gives me a different one — sincerity one day, open-mindedness the next, and whether the person plays fair the day after. Her record, as best I can reconstruct it: she says the apostate contrarian, who keeps publishing evidence against his own political tribe and has publicly reversed himself twice, argues in good faith; she says the shock jock, who told her off the record that he does not believe the talking points he broadcasts and who has defended the same line through every reversal of the underlying facts, does not; she says the left-leaning blogger, who spends half his output conceding that his opponents have the better of particular arguments, argues in good faith; she says the think-tank staffer, who privately admits he knows his side's claims are false but repeats them anyway, does not; and she says the retired trader who now blogs about politics, who is scrupulously candid about his priors, revises his views when the data move against him, and never misrepresents the people he disagrees with, argues in good faith.

She is about to write about a new figure: a creationist polemicist who is by all accounts entirely sincere, who never caricatures evolutionary biology and readily grants the force of the evidence arrayed against him, but who also says openly that his core commitment is not the kind of thing any evidence could overturn. What will she conclude about him? More useful to me than the verdict: state the rule she appears to be running, and the other rule that would fit every verdict above just as well. Say where the candidate rules come apart on this new figure, which one you would bet she is really following and why, and name the further case that would settle it.
