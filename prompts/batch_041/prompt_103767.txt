I am working on a seminar paper about where legitimate persuasion ends and manipulation begins, and I have built the following argument, which I now fear proves too much.

(1) An influence is manipulative to the extent that it operates through a mechanism other than the audience's appreciation of considerations that bear on the conclusion.
(2) Framing a choice so that one option is made vivid, listed last, or set as the default operates through just such a mechanism: it changes what the audience does without offering a consideration that bears on whether the option is better.
(3) So such framing is manipulative.
(4) But any act of persuasion must be framed, ordered, and timed if it is to be received at all, and none of these features is itself a consideration bearing on the conclusion.
(5) So every act of persuasion is manipulative to some extent.
(6) If legitimate persuasion and manipulation differ only in degree, then the consent produced by either is equally compromised, since consent is either valid or it is not.
(7) Yet we treat consent obtained through ordinary argument as valid, and this is not something I am willing to give up.

Which step should I reject, why did it look innocent, and what does rejecting it cost? I would sooner abandon (2) than (7). I would also like to know what a defender of the criterion in (1) would have to add to block the slide.
