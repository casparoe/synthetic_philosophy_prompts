ok so I'm a junior in high school and we just finished a unit on free will, and there's one thing that's been bugging me.

My teacher says the rule is that you're only morally responsible for something if you could of done otherwise. She calls it the principle of alternative possibilities, PAP for short. Then she gives us the chip thing (she called it a "Frankfurt case," after some philosopher) where a scientist puts a chip in your brain that would force you to do X if you were about to not do X, and you do X anyway on your own and the chip never fires. Then she goes, "See? You couldn't have done otherwise, and you're still responsible, so PAP is dead." Half the class nodded and I was sitting there like... ok, but there's definately a difference between "the chip never fired" and "you literally could not have done anything else," and I don't think the story as she tells it even shows the first thing clearly.

So what I want to know is which versions of this kind of story actually do damage to PAP and which ones are just window dressing. Please act like a philosopher who's spent years working on free will and moral responsibility, think it through carefully before you commit to anything, and start by brainstorming a little so I can see what you're considering.

Assume the chip is 100% reliable unless I say otherwise, and assume the person has no idea the chip is there unless I say otherwise. Don't worry about who said what or what these are called in the literature — I don't need citations or a reading list, I just want your reasoning. Here are the seven:

1. Plain chip. You're deciding whether to help a stranger who collapsed on the sidewalk. A scientist has implanted a chip that would have forced you to help if you'd been about to walk past. You help, sincerely, on your own. The chip never fires.

2. Leaky chip. Same setup, except the scientist tells us the chip only works about half the time, so there was a real chance it would have fizzled and you'd have walked past.

3. Picky chip. Same setup, except the chip would only have fired if you'd been about to walk past for one particular reason (you were late for a movie). If you'd walked past out of laziness or spite, the chip would have stayed off. So only one of the ways of doing otherwise was blocked.

4. Locked door. No chip, no scientist. You choose to stay in your room and study instead of going to the party. Unknown to you, your roommate locked you in from the outside before you even decided, so you couldn't have left anyway. You stayed for your own reasons, and you'd have stayed even if the door had been open.

5. Future-self chip. Exactly like #1, except the chip wasn't put there by a scientist. You somehow sent it back in time from twenty years in the future, because future you wanted to make sure past you helped. Same result: it never fires.

6. Known chip. Like #1, except you know the chip is in there and you know it'll zap you if you're about to walk past. You help anyway and it feels sincere to you, but you're not sure whether you'd have helped if you'd never heard of the chip.

7. No chip at all. Nobody intervened and nothing blocked anything. You helped because of who you are, and who you are is the product of your genes and everything that ever happened to you, all of which was caused by stuff before that, going back forever. There was never any real chance of you walking past.

Rank these from the one that does the MOST damage to PAP down to the one that does basically none. Give each one a damage score out of 10 (10 = PAP is toast, 0 = PAP is totally fine) and put each one in a tier (S, A, B, C, or D).

Format it like this, with these section names in this order:

**Brainstorm** — quick bullets, under 120 words, the obvious stuff you want to get out of the way before deciding anything.

**The ranking** — the seven in order, 1 through 7, each with its name, its tier, its damage score, and one sentence of explanation.

**Why they landed where they did** — a few sentences on each, ~400 words total, and be specific about which part of each story is doing the work (the chip? the reliability? the knowledge? the fact that it never fires?).

**Where I'm probably wrong** — ~100 words on which of your rankings you'd flip most easily and what would have to be true for you to flip them.

**Bottom line** — 2-3 sentences: does PAP survive in any form, and if so, what's the weakened version?
