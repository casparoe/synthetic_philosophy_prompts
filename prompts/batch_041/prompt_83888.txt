I host a fortnightly science-and-ideas interview show, and next week's guest is a philosopher who's spent the last few years defending what she calls the scale arguement about insect sentience. Her move is to say the interesting question isn't whether insects feel pain but what the expected moral cost comes to when you multiply a headcount that dwarfs every vertebrate we already fuss over by a probability you'd have to be a fanatic to call anything other than small. She'll cheerfully grant the probability might be one in a million; she claims the product swamps the vertebrate side anyway, and that this survives a wide range of guesses about both the probability and the per-insect amount of welfare. She's also made the sharper claim that the whole dispute isn't really about insect neurobiology at all, it's about which prior you're allowed to bring to it.

Write me 8-10 questions that get past "so, do bugs feel pain?". I want at least one pressing the arithmetic itself, one on whether the same logic drags in things she'd rather it didn't (bacteria, plants, simulated minds, all the insects we can't do anything about), one on the additivity assumption, and one on what result would actually make her abandon the arguement. For each, a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. Dont ask her to define sentience or to walk through the history of the debate. She's a good sport, so nothing needs softening.
