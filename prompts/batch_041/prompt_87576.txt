I chair the clinical ethics committee at a mid-size hospital network, and I have to write the committee's formal opinion before Wednesday's meeting, becuase we're split and the medical director is pressing for a ruling.

Background: our affiliated health plan covers "medically necessary treatment" and says outright it won't pay for "enhancement." The case in front of us is a 61-year-old woman who works as an air traffic controller — her employer forces retirement at 65 — and who's noticed her reaction times slipping and occasional trouble finding words. She's had full neuropsych testing. Technically she's "within normal limits for age": no diagnosis, no MCI, nothing on paper. But she's well below her own baseline from ten years ago, and she says the gap is already showing up on the job.

She's asking the plan to cover a twelve-week course of an experimental drug, veldoral, plus a transcranial stimulation protocol. A small trial suggests the combination improves reaction time and working memory in people like her, and — this is the part splitting us — it also improves healthy twenty-somethings, and the drug apparently only works if you start it before there's any clinically significant decline. So it reads as prevention of a disease, and also as enhancement of a normal person, depending on where you stand.

The medical director's position: no diagnosis, therefore enhancement, therefore no coverage. Two members say it's treatment, since it's forestalling a real decline that will cost her her job. Two say it's enhancement, since there's no disease to treat. One says the real question is whether the plan exists to keep her employable. I need to land this.

Draft the committee's opinion: recommend a concrete decision — approve, deny, or approve with conditions — with a time frame the plan can actually implement, and give the strongest version of the argument on the side you're ruling against rather than a strawman. Also tell me where your reasoning is weakest, and what would change your mind — which fact or argument would flip the verdict.
