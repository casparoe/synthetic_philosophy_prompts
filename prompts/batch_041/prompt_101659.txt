I'm a philosopher who has been embedded for the past year in a machine-learning lab, and I'm putting together an application for a small internal grant (six to nine months, essentially my own time plus a bit of a research assistant) to work on a problem in the ethics of AI value alignment. My half-formed idea, which I'd like you to turn into a proper proposal: the alignment literature tends to treat "human values" as though there were a single thing to be maximized, and it usually smuggles in two quite different questions under that heading — first, the franchise question of *whose* values or preferences are to be counted, and second, the aggregation question of *how* they are to be combined into a determinate output. I suspect that much of the appeal of liberal neutrality in this context is misplaced: neutrality is normally a constraint on what a state may do with its coercive power, but an aligned model, unlike a state, cannot be neutral by abstaining — it has to emit a token, make a recommendation, take an action — so any neutrality it displays has to be re-described as a constraint on the aggregation rule rather than on the domain of concern. I want to know whether that re-description leaves neutrality with real content, whether it collapses into something trivial, or whether there is a third possibility I haven't seen, and I'd like the proposal to take seriously that the aggregation problem here is not just the familiar social-choice difficulty but also a problem about a common currency, since the values at stake are plausibly incommensurable and the parties are, in the relevant sense, separate.

Please write the proposal itself: a precise statement of the question such that it has a determinate answer, an account of why the existing positions — the standard defenses of neutrality, the standard treatments of interpersonal aggregation, and the way alignment practice actually operationalizes a single scalar objective — leave the question open rather than settling it, and the method by which a philosophical (as opposed to empirical) project makes progress here. I'd like the method to be concrete: say what I would actually do across the months, which distinctions I would try to draw and stress-test, and how I would tell a genuine conceptual result from a verbal rearrangement. Say what a successful outcome would look like as a deliverable, and be honest about what would count as the project failing — including the possibility that the whole framing is confused and the honest conclusion is that neutrality was never doing the work people think it was. Also give a realistic sense of scope for the time available and flag which part is most likely to blow up into a much larger project.

Don't worry about citations or a literature review; I'll add those myself once the argument is fixed. You can refer to positions and arguments by description — the neutrality-of-aim versus neutrality-of-effect distinction, the usual worries about interpersonal comparability, the impossibility results for combining individual rankings, the separateness-of-persons objection to aggregation — without naming works or authors, and please don't invent titles or dates. The conceptual work I need from you is in the framing: a question that is genuinely open, tractable in the time, and angled away from the version of it that everyone already knows how to answer.
