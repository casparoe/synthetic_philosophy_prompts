I've been reading a lot of the quantitative macrohistory stuff, the people who try to turn the past into data and then look for rhythms in it. Not looking for sources here — I just can't shake a methodological worry and I want to know if it has a name, because "that's just noise" is not an answer I can use.

Here's the pattern, and I've now seen it maybe five or six times. Someone claims history runs in a cycle — two hundred years, or fifty, depending. I go back to see how the numbers were made, and the rhythm is never in the numbers as they were collected. It shows up only after two moves. First, all the seperate series get squished into a single index: grain prices, wages, population, wars, whatever, each rescaled and added together, with the wieghts picked by hand. Second, each year gets replaced by the average of that year together with the twenty or so years on either side of it. Do both and out comes a beautiful wave. Widen or narrow the averaging window and the period of the wave changes. And the wave is always somewhere between a third and a half of the length of the whole record, so you never get to see it come around more than two or three times — which is exactly the number of repetitions you'd want before believing it.

I did some stats in college, so I know the reflex is to say overfitting, but that doesn't seem to be it: nobody is fitting a model to anything, the wiggles show up before any model does. A friend who's better at this than me says I'm confused, because the averaged series is just as real as the yearly one, and if there's a cycle in the averaged series then there's a cycle in the averaged series, full stop. Which is true in some trivial sense, but it can't be the whole story, because then a claim about historical cycles would be unfalsifiable — any pile of numbers can be averaged into a wave.

So what I'm after: does this thing have a name? What are the nearest concepts to it, and where exactly do they stop fitting — is it overfitting, is it choosing your window after you've seen the graph, or is it something else that deserves its own label? And is there a principled way to tell a claimed long cycle that's in the data from one that's in the arithmetic? If the honest answer is that a single record can never settle it, fine, I'd like to hear that too — but then I want to know what the difference is supposed to be between a real cycle nobody could detect and an artifact, and whether that difference lives in the world or only in how we describe it. A name alone won't do it for me.
