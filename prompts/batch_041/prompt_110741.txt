A lecturer I had last term gave us her version of the honour culture / dignity culture / victimhood culture distinction and I wrote it all down, but I'm not sure how much of it to trust and I dont want to build an essay on top of it if half of it is her own gloss.

Her version, as I wrote it: in an honour culture your worth is your reputation, so if someone insults you the right response is to hit back yourself, because a man who doesn't gets walked over; this is the culture you get when there's no state worth relying on to protect you, which is why it turns up in herding societies, where losing your herd to a thief ruins you, and in the American South. In a dignity culture your worth sits inside you and doesn't depend on what anyone thinks, so the mature response to a slight is to shrug and get on with your life; this is the culture of modern liberal societies and it's basically Kant's idea that persons have dignity. Victimhood culture is the new one and it's dignity culture gone wrong: people still believe they have inherent worth, but instead of shrugging off slights they run to whoever will listen -- HR, the university, the internet -- to have their status as a victim officially recognised. She said the three form a moral ladder, honour at the bottom because it's violent, dignity in the middle, victimhood as a kind of regression in progressive clothes, since it brings back the obsession with status that dignity culture had supposedly got rid of, except now you climb by being seen as harmed rather than as tough. She also said duelling died out because people became nicer and stopped caring about status.

How much of that is fair? Take it claim by claim and tell me what someone who actually works on this would nod at, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong. Then tell me whether the moral ladder is even the kind of thing the distinction can support, or whether ranking the three needs a seperate argument she never gave. And if I do want to ask which of the three is morally superior, what do I have to settle first -- what would count as being better? I'll admit the dignity-culture bit appeals to me, which is probably exactly why I'm suspicious of it.
