I read alot of history and I keep running into the same argument, and I've never found a name for the thing that's actually going on inside it.

The argument is the one about whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler. One side says of course there would, look at the conditions -- the depression, the humiliation at Versailles, the paramilitaries, the collapse of the center -- the role was sitting there and somebody was going to fill it. The other side says try it: put Hitler out of the way in, say, January 1933, and the whole thing comes apart. Then the first side says, sure, but you only got that answer by holding everything else fixed, which is cheating, because the man was himself a product of the conditions you just froze in place. So the second side says, fine, if I can't hold anything fixed then nobody ever did anything and history is just weather.

What I've slowly figured out is that they aren't really disagreeing about the history. They're disagreeing about what you're allowed to hold fixed while asking 'would it have happened anyway', and neither of them says so out loud. And the answer genuinely flips depending on where you make the cut. Rewind a week and he's indispensable; rewind thirty years and he's a detail. Worse, people seem to do it inconsistently inside a single argument -- holding the economy fixed but not the political culture, say -- and I honestly cant tell whether that's cheating or whether that's just what the question requires. Same shape shows up outside history, too: did the marriage end because of the affair, or because of the ten years before it, and which of those do I get to hold fixed?

Is there a name for this? Not for great-man-versus-structures, I know that one, and I know 'overdetermination' -- several causes each of which alone would have been enough. But this doesn't feel like overdetermination, because you don't need two sufficient causes here, you just need to shift the background, and the same single cause slides from 'necessary' to 'incidental' and back. Nor does it feel like just 'counterfactuals are vague', because the vagueness is doing something specific and patterned.

So what I want is the thing itself, properly carved. What is it that's happening when the answer to 'was this person the cause' turns on a choice nobody announced they were making? What are the nearest established notions and how do they come apart from it -- is this one phenomenon or a family wearing one coat? And where is the line between a legitimate choice of what to hold fixed and an illegitimate one? If it already has a name, fine, but the name is the least of it. If it doesn't, coin one and tell me what it does and doesn't cover.
