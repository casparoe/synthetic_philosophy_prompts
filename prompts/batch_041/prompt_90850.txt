A colleague cited the no-free-lunch theorems in a design review last week, and I have been reading up on them since. I think I have grasped the deeper point and want to check it before I repeat it. The theorems show that, averaged over all possible problems, no learning algorithm generalizes any better than any other, and I read this as a mathematical statement of Hume's problem of induction: our models appear to generalize only because we have quietly assumed something about the world, and since that assumption cannot be justified without circularity, their success is ultimately a matter of luck. So my question is whether the intellectually honest position is to make a uniformity-of-nature assumption an explicit axiom that we simply posit, and to treat the no-free-lunch results not as a technical footnote about optimization but as the fundamental epistemic limit on machine learning.
