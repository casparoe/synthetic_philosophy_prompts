ok this is a bit of a weird ask. my town is running one of those citizens' assemblies for the first time -- forty people picked by lot, six weekends, deciding what to do with the old mill site -- and somehow they've asked me to give the opening five minutes on saturday morning. I want to give it as a Spinozist, because I actually think he's the honest guide for this, but heres my problem: maybe half the room have actually read his political writing and will wince if I say anything he'd have rejected, and the other half are neighbors who came for the coffee and will glaze over the second I say "conatus."

write me that speech. I want Spinoza's politics to do real work in it, not just be decoration -- it should actually constrain what I'm allowed to say up there. a few things I already know I cant do: I cant promise anyone their voice will win, and I cant pretend the assembly's recommendations bind the council (they dont, the council can ignore the whole thing). I also dont want to comfort people with "just showing up makes you powerful," because of what he'd say about right and power. and I want the peace bit to land the right way -- peace as something you have to be strong enough for, not just the absence of noise. probably goes without saying but I dont want to lean on the general will or on people's duty to obey either; if theres an argument for going along with the group, I want it to be the one where agreeing is what a rational person actually wants.

five minutes, spoken, no jargon I'd have to stop and define, and dont quote him at me -- I'd mangle the wording anyway.
