ok so I'm in a 19th-c British philosophy seminar and my final paper is basically one argument, and next week I have to present it to the class plus two guest faculty, and I need a Q&A prep sheet.

My thesis, roughly: everyone treats Sidgwick's psychical research as a slightly embarrassing hobby that's seperate from the real work, The Methods of Ethics. I want to argue it's the reverse — it's continuous with the ethics. Because Methods ends in the dualism of practical reason, i.e. no proof that self-interest and duty actually converge, Sidgwick needed some kind of cosmic guarantee that duty would be backed by the universe, and he couldn't get that out of reason alone, so he went looking for empirical evidence instead — survival, apparitions, the whole SPR business (he was its first president in 1882, and the Census of Hallucinations came out in 1894). I'm calling this move 'borrowed warrant': he outsources to empirical psychical research a cosmological premise his ethics needs but can't derive. So the psychical stuff isn't a lapse or a midlife thing, it's load-bearing for the metaethics.
