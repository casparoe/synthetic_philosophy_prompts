Everyone I know who works with these models keeps reaching for the same comparison, and I have started to lean on it myself: the model is like a very good actor, and everything it says is a performance of a character rather than a report of what it actually believes. On this picture there is someone who wrote the lines — the training data, whoever typed the prompt, the company that shipped the thing — and there is a fictional role being played, and nothing in between that could count as an agent with aims of its own; the assistant is a costume, not a mind. I find it persuasive, and I also suspect it is doing more work than it can bear, since it seems to assume a real person somewhere behind the mask doing the acting, which is close to the very thing in dispute. Please set out the correspondence — what plays the actor, the script, the director, the character, the audience — then tell me where the comparison breaks, which features it quietly borrows from real acting that the model may not have, and whether the conclusion it is used to support, that there is no agent here and only a role, survives once the disanalogies are on the table. I would also like to know whether this is really the same as the claim that the model is a simulator running many possible characters, or whether those are two different pictures that people are running together. If a better comparison is available, say what it is and what it would change.
