I want a real number on quantum immortality, decomposed, not a survey of the debate. Here's the proposition: if I rig a quantum measurement that kills me with probability 1/2 and just keep running it, then on a no-collapse / many-worlds picture of QM I should expect to keep having experiences forever — from the inside I never die. Whats your credence in that? Two things I think are the actual crux and want you to factor in: (a) the branch where I've survived a million rounds has absurdly tiny measure, so the claim seems to ask me to think of myself as sitting in an astronomically atypical branch, which rubs against the usual assumption that I should consider myself a typical observer; (b) does any of it survive if the killing device is a classical coin-flip gun rather than a quantum one, given that branching, or whatever is standing in for it, is what's doing the work?

Format: exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion. Item 1 is your headline credence. Items 2-4 are the three sub-questions the number hinges on most, each with its own probability and one line of why. Item 5 is how those combine into the headline number, which of the three is doing the most work, and what evidence or argument would move you most.
