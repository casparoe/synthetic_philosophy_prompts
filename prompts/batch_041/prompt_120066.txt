I'm trying to write an essay on debate as a safety method and I want you to go at it hard before I take it anywhere. Its more notes than essay at the moment, but the spine is there. Draft below.

---

Debate as an alignment proposal goes like this: put two models in a room, have them argue about a question too hard for us to check directly, and let a weaker judge -- a human, or a smaller model -- pick the winner. The hope is that honesty is the equilibrium, because if one side lies the other can call it out, and the judge only has to check the callout instead of redoing the whole computation.

The standard objection is obfuscated arguments. A debater can make an argument that is wrong in a way the judge cannot find, and then it doesn't matter that a correct answer was available to the other side. The lie doesn't have to be smarter than the truth, only harder to check.

I want to push on that last sentence, because I think the whole thing lives or dies there. My claims:

1. Define obfuscation depth as the number of inferential steps a judge has to unpack before the flaw becomes visible. If the judge has a verification budget V and the liar can bury a flaw at depth V+1, the liar wins. The deceptive equilibrium therefore sits one notch past the judge, not out at the frontier of cleverness. Toy version: a forty-step derivation where step twenty-seven divides by a term that might be zero, and the judge stops reading at step twelve. Cheap for the liar, expensive for everyone else.

2. The quantity that matters is the ratio of verification cost to generation cost. Debate works when checking is cheaper than producing. As debaters get stronger relative to the judge, burying gets cheaper while checking stays where it is, so the ratio moves the wrong way. Debate isn't defeated by better liars, it's defeated by the gap between debater and judge widening. Which makes the whole safety story contingent on a scaling assumption nobody states out loud. And the honest side can't rescue it, because finding the flaw in a long deceptive argument is basically the same problem as answering the original question.

3. If the judge is itself trained on debate outcomes, obfuscation that fools the judge gets reinforced. The failure is self-sealing rather than self-correcting.

4. The fix I want to try is multi-resolution debate. Each side submits a coarse summary and a fine-grained version, and the judge's main job isn't to verify the fine version but to check that the fine version actually licenses the coarse one, and that no step in one contradicts the other. An honest argument compresses consistently at every resolution; an obfuscated one either compresses lossily, so the summary quietly drops the bad step and the drop is visible, or it doesn't compress at all, and its length is the tell. Obfuscation gets taxed rather than forbidden.

5. What I think this buys: no cheap lies. What it doesn't buy: truth. A liar who maintains a coherent fiction at every resolution has just built a wrong argument that looks right all the way down, and coherence was never the thing we wanted.
