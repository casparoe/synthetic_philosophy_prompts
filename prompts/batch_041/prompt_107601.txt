I am a second-year PhD student in philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and I have been trying to turn a seminar worry into a short research proposal for a six-month pilot grant from my department's mind and cognition workshop. The worry concerns the thought experiment often called the careenium, in which a large population of simple mechanical parts -- simmballs -- is arranged so that their local collisions realize a higher-level system that looks for all the world like it manipulates symbols. The standard lesson is that the symbolic level is a real pattern but not an extra causal agent: the simmballs do all the pushing, and the symbols are at best a convenient gloss. I am not satisfied. I suspect the interesting question is not whether symbols are real but whether the macro-level counterfactuals that we use to identify a symbol's content -- if this symbol had been different, that output would have been different -- are autonomous from the micro-level counterfactuals that describe the simmballs, or whether they are merely shorthand for a vastly complex microphysical disjunction. If they are autonomous, then in a clear sense symbols do push the substrate around, even though every individual collision is fully determined by the simmballs; if they are not, then the symbolic level is epiphenomenal in a stronger sense than the usual multiple-realizability story admits.

I want the proposal to state the question so that it has a determinate answer and is not just a verbal dispute about causation. It should explain why the existing answers -- the standard deflationary reading of the careenium, interventionist accounts of higher-level causation, and mechanistic accounts that treat levels as explanatory stances -- leave the autonomy question open. It should describe the method: what I would actually do month by month, including what formal apparatus I would borrow, perhaps a simple causal-model representation of the careenium and a comparison of macro- and micro-interventions, what toy cases I would construct, and how I would tell whether the two levels' counterfactuals come apart in a way that matters. It should say what a positive result would look like, what a negative result would look like, and what would count as the project failing to answer the question rather than merely failing to find autonomy. It should also be honest about scope: whether six months is enough, what part of the careenium literature I can safely bracket, and what would make the project too big for a pilot. I will add citations and literature myself, so please do not include a bibliography or reading list; refer to positions and arguments by description instead. I would also be grateful if you would flag any place where the question as I have stated it collapses into a familiar debate rather than staying in the weeds.
