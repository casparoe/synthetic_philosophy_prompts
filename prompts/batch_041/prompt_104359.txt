I'm on the school board in a district of about 6,000 kids and I have to bring a written opinion to next Tuesday's meeting, so I'm asking you to draft it.

Background, fast: last spring we ran a pilot with an outside company selling a K–2 screening package. The usual stuff — letter sounds, rapid naming, vocabulary — but the selling point is that they also swab the kids and use what they call a "genetic risk panel" plus family history to sort struggling readers into two buckets, which their materials call "neurodevelopmental" and "opportunity-gap." The pitch is that the buckets need different treatment: the neurodevelopmental kids supposedly won't get much out of our Tier 2 tutoring and should go straight to a specialized placement, while the opportunity-gap kids should get more of what we already do. Their write-up says reading disability is "about 60% heritable" and that this is why some kids plateau no matter what we throw at them. They also say their panel "identifies which children are which," and that in their own pilot the tutoring effect was near zero for kids above their cutoff score. Their validation sample, for what it's worth, is mostly of European ancestry and our district isn't.

My daughter was in the pilot and came out "neurodevelopmental." Part of me was furious and part of me thought, well, she's had the same tutoring as everyone else and she's still stuck at the same level, so maybe. I've been reading about this for three weeks and I still cant tell which parts of their argument are real and which parts are vibes with a decimal point on them.

What I need from you: a short written opinion I can circulate before the meeting, no more than 1,000 words, that lands on a concrete recommendation — approve the three-year contract, reject it, or approve it with specific conditions — plus a timeline for whatever I'm recommending. Assume the other six board members are not scientists and won't read anything longer than this. Take the strongest version of the vendor's case seriously; if there's something true in the idea that kids differ in how much they get out of intervention, and that some of that difference runs in families, say so plainly and say what follows from it. And be specific about which of their claims survive and which don't — just telling me heritability isn't destiny is too vague to vote on. No footnotes or citations, we're not publishing this and I'd rather not have half the memo be references I can't check.
