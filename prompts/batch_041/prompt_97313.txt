I fell down a rabbit hole the other night reading about the knowability paradox (the Fitch one, where if every truth *can* be known it seems to follow that every truth *is* known, which is obviously nuts) and now I can't stop picking at it, but in a different way than the articles do. Everyone online jumps straight to proposing a fix -- weaken this premise, restrict that rule, drop a bit of logic -- and I don't trust any of it, because nobody ever says first what a fix is even supposed to accomplish. So before I read one more blog post telling the antirealist to just stop saying "all truths are knowable," I want the spec. What does any account of knowability actually have to deliver to count as a real solution to the paradox rather than a dodge? Fix the purpose for me like this: I want an account good enough that someone could still be an antirealist about truth -- truth not as correspondence to a mind-independent world but as tied to what could in principle be known -- without that view collapsing into the silly claim that everything is already known by somebody.

So give me the requirements list. The cases it has to get right (I care alot about the one where a person says there are truths nobody will ever know -- the antirealist wants to deny that's a real possibility, but they can't just call it false either; and the "p, but I don't know that p" case, which sounds absurd for a reason I want spelled out); the distinctions it can't mush together (knowable-in-principle vs knowable-by-us-now vs knowable-by-a-bigger-mind; unknown vs unknowable; a principled restriction on the knowability claim vs one patched on purely to dodge the proof); and the background results it has to live with -- I keep hearing you can't wriggle out of this by switching to intuitionistic logic, and I want to know if that's right and what else seperately closes doors like that. My friend keeps insisting the clean move is to restrict the knowability claim to truths that don't themselves mention knowledge, and I honestly can't tell if that's brilliant or a cheat, so if it comes up, tell me which requirement it fails. And finally: which of these requirements can't all hold at once? I suspect there's a set that's jointly impossible and something has to give, and I want to know which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the popular fixes. Skip the scorecard of who's right -- I'll do that part myself.
