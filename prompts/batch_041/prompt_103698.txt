I'm writing this up for a grad seminar on epistemology and I've built an argument that I'm pretty sure is airtight, which is exactly the problem, because I hate where it lands. Here it is. (1) If you know something, then it's true -- that's just what "know" means; you can't know something false. (2) Suppose you know P, and then somewhere down the line you run into evidence that suggests not-P. Well, you know P, and knowing is factive, so P is true; which means that evidence has to be misleading, since it's pointing you toward something false. (3) So, right now, you know that any counter-evidence you ever encounter is misleading. (4) If you know a piece of evidence is misleading, you're entitled to set it aside -- you don't have to factor misleading evidence into what you believe. (5) So if you know P, you're entitled to set aside any evidence against P, whenever it shows up. (6) And if you're entitled to set aside all the evidence against P, then no evidence could ever make it rationally required for you to drop P -- you can just go on believing it. (7) So knowledge is self-sealing: the moment you know something, you're rationally licensed to ignore every future objection, forever. (8) But that's obviously wrong. Someone who says "I know it and I don't care what you show me" is the textbook definition of dogmatic, not rational, and any theory on which knowing licenses that has proven way too much.

So something in that chain is broken and I can't find it. The premise I most want to keep is (1) -- I'm not giving up factivity, it seems definitional, and honestly I'd rather keep (2) too, since it just looks like (1) applied. Tell me which step actually fails, then explain why it looked so seperate from the others and so obviously fine, because the whole thing reads to me like one seamless move after another. And then price the repairs for me: for each candidate rejection, what do I have to give up, and is there any way to keep (1) and (2) intact while doing it? Please don't just name the paradox and wave at it -- I know what people call it, I want the actual diagnosis and the bill.
