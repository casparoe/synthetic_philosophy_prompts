I am trying to formulate a coherent anti-realist position and would like a consistency audit before I commit to it. My commitments are these. (1) Truth is not evidence-transcendent: for every truth there is some possible situation in which it is known. (2) I accept the Fitch-style derivation as formally valid, and so I see that (1), together with the standard logic of knowledge, entails that every truth is actually known. (3) To avoid that result I restrict (1) to propositions whose being known is not ruled out by their truth—the Cartesian propositions. (4) I nonetheless believe that some truths are in principle unknowable, for example facts about past events whose evidence has been destroyed. (5) I think the restriction in (3) is principled rather than ad hoc, because it marks a genuine distinction between propositions that can enter knowledge and those that cannot. (6) I treat the knowability principle as a schema rather than a single quantified claim, so I am unsure whether the Fitch derivation may legitimately apply it to a conjunctive proposition such as p and not known p. Do these commitments hang together? Which are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity, and be explicit about which parts of the Fitch debate are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who know the topic; do not present a contested view as the consensus.
