I serve on the advisory council of a charitable foundation that funds youth and family programmes, and at our next meeting we must decide whether to restructure our grant-making around generational cohorts: a Gen Z strand for digital mental-health work, a Millennial strand for housing insecurity, and so forth. Our new director is keen on the idea; two councillors are at loggerheads over it. One argues that generational labels are a marketing fiction with no place in evidence-based philanthropy, and that we should simply fund by age band instead; the other insists that cohorts are real historical kinds, shaped by formative events and technologies, and that our funding streams ought to track them. I am a philosopher by training, and it seems to me they are talking past each other about what it would even be for a generation to be real.

Please draft the written opinion I shall circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a definite course of action for the foundation and supply the council with criteria it can actually apply when the proposal comes to a vote. I should like it to work through, rather than merely gesture at, the following: the difference between a cohort as a demographic index and a generation as a purported culturally unified kind; the age–period–cohort identification problem — the fact that age, period, and cohort are linearly confounded, so that a cohort effect cannot be separated from age and period effects without extra assumptions — and what this implies for any claim about distinctive Gen Z or Millennial traits; the possibility that generational labels have looping or self-fulfilling effects, helping to constitute the very kind they purport only to describe; and the question of what conception of a real kind (whether something like a homeostatic property cluster, a historical kind, or a kind that is real only relative to a purpose) our decision should presuppose. Tell me plainly where the director's proposal can be salvaged and where it cannot, and give the strongest consideration on the losing side its due rather than a straw man.

Please write in British English, and keep the memo to something a busy council can read in a single sitting.
