So I just lost about three hours of my evening to an arguement with my flatmate about heterophenomenology, and I need to put the leftover adrenaline somewhere. Her position: it's behaviourism in a lab coat. All you ever do is write down what the subject says and then officially decline to say whether there was anything it was like to be them, so the method can't even in principle touch the thing it's supposedly about. My position: that's the whole trick. You take the reports seriously, you build the subject a full heterophenomenological world out of everything they say, and you treat that world the way you'd treat the world of a novel — you can describe it in enormous detail without having to believe a word of it happened. The subject is the narrator, you're the reader, and it doesn't matter whether the narrator is reliable. She said that's exactly the problem, then she brought up zombies, I said the zombie thing is a feature and not a bug, and then we went round in circles for another hour. Still annoyed.

So, catharsis. Write me a job advertisement — standard sections, so: about the lab, the role, main duties, person specification, what we offer, how to apply — for a postdoc position in a heterophenomenology lab at a made-up university. 550–650 words. Deadpan the whole way through, no winking, no asides to the reader about how silly it all is.

The jokes have to come out of the method itself and not out of generic academia-is-weird material. The ad should be able to get through its entire recruitment spiel without ever once presupposing that the candidates, the interview panel, or the research subjects are conscious — and it should still read like a normal, functioning job ad that an actual HR department signed off on. The behaviourism charge should be handled the way a real lab would handle it: filed under something like "a point frequently misunderstood," and then not really answered. The zombie point should come across as an advantage the lab is quietly proud of. And the how-to-apply bit has to survive the same neutrality, so think about what it would even mean to ask someone for a personal statement. Invent the lab, the university, and the PI; don't cite anything and don't lean on real institutions.

If it makes her laugh, I'm counting that as winning the arguement.
