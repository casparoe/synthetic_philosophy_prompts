I'm giving a talk next month arguing that the question "why did industrialization happen in Britain and not China?" is basically ill-posed. My line is that why-not questions like this only get a determinate answer once you fix a contrast class and a candidate population, and the Needham question quietly treats "China" as one agent whose failure needs explaining when it's really an aggregation artifact -- once you disaggregate, you're comparing Britain to a few specific regions (the Yangzi delta, Gujarat, the North Sea low countries) and the question collapses into something much more tractable, maybe even trivial. The room will be a bunch of cliometricians who want factor prices and unified growth models, several China historians who already hate the "failure" framing but for their own reasons, and a couple philosophers of social science who care about contrastive explanation and will want to know what makes a why-question well-posed in the first place.
