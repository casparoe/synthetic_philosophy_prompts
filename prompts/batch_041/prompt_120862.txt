My half-formed idea: everyone treats self-amendment in Nomic-style games, where changing the rules is itself a move, as where the paradox lives, but I think the really hard case is what I've been calling parasitic ammendment — a rule that, once passed, purports to invalidate the very procedure that passed it (it declares the old amendment clause void, or retroactively lowers its own threshold). Now the new rule's validity seems to rest on a procedure the new rule says was never valid. Is that a flat contradiction, an unstable-but-liveable equilibrium, or just something rule-systems absorb through convention — and does the answer come apart between a game, where the players themselves have to settle what the rules now say, and a legal order, where courts and entrenched clauses (the constitutional-theory thought that the power to amend isn't the power that constituted the thing) do the settling?
