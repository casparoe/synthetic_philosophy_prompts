ok so I want a short story, 1500-2500 words, built around a fairly specific idea that I'll spell out here so you don't have to guess at it. The idea is metaphysical grounding -- the "in virtue of" relation, fundamentality, that whole area -- but I want it dramatized, not explained.

Here's the setup. A city (or honestly a mid-sized bureaucracy, whatever you find funnier) has an office whose entire job is grounding. Any fact asserted in public life -- in court, in the paper, in a contract -- has to arrive with the facts that make it the case, and those arrive with the facts that make them the case, and so on down. The office calls the bottom of a chain bedrock and treats anything that reaches bedrock as settled and actionable. A junoir auditor gets handed two files that are ruining her week.

File one: a chain that never bottoms out. Every single link is fine on its own -- each fact really does seem to be what makes the next one the case -- but the chain just keeps descending. No bedrock, no loop, just going down. Everyone senior to her insists this is impossible and she must have made an error, because a chain that doesn't terminate can't be doing any grounding work; there'd be nothing for the upper links to inherit their status from.

File two: a pair of facts where each one appears to be what makes the other the case, and the office has a hard rule that a fact can't ground itself, directly or around a circle, so one of the two has to be demoted. The catch is that she can't find any non-arbitary reason to pick which one goes. Every test she runs, the choice comes out looking like a coin flip.

I also want the office to have an internal rule that ends up mattering a lot: being necessary and sufficient for something isn't enough to be its ground. Two facts can track each other perfectly, or even mirror each other, without one being what makes the other so. The auditors have a test for this and it's the source of most of the office's fights, because everyone's intuitions about the test disagree at the edges.

Don't resolve it with the city being a simulation, or a dream, or the auditor waking up. I want the metaphysics actually worked out through what the characters do and argue about. Give me at least one character who thinks the whole demand for bedrock is the real mistake, and one who thinks the auditor is just confusing herself with her own bookkeeping. The auditor shouldn't take a side because it's convenient or because her boss tells her to. End somewhere genuinely unresolved but not a cop-out -- she's found a way to keep working, and it costs her something real.

Feel free to invent the office's jargon and procedures, just keep it internally consistent and keep the philosophy doing actual work in the plot rather than being scenery.
