I recently agreed to coordinate a small volunteer tool-lending library in my neighborhood, and after several months of watching borrowed tools come back late, shifts go uncovered, and reliable members quietly drift away, I have had to work out what I actually believe about cooperation instead of merely intuiting it. Before I put a proposal to the membership, I would like my position audited for internal consistency.

Here is where I have landed:

1. Rationality is a matter of expected payoff; in a genuinely one-shot, anonymous prisoner's dilemma, defecting is the rational move. I am not a moral rationalist, so I do not assume that what is rational and what is right must coincide.

2. In an ongoing relationship, where each side can see what the other did and expects to keep dealing with them, conditional cooperation — open with cooperation, then mirror the other's last move — is both the rational policy and the policy I want members to follow.

3. Even in the one-shot anonymous case, one ought to cooperate: fairness is not hostage to prudence, and the absence of witnesses does not change what is owed.

4. Cooperation among people is explained by reciprocity and by kinship, and by nothing else; appeals to what benefits the group or the species do no explanatory work.

5. What makes cooperation stable in a community is the shadow of the future — repetition, memory, and reputation. Where a setting is genuinely anonymous and terminal, I expect cooperation to decay.

6. In practice nobody in the library is a calculator. They reciprocate and follow internalized norms even when doing so costs them, and that, rather than any calculation about consequences, is what keeps the place running.

7. Our rules give new members a grace period before missed shifts count against them, and I am convinced this is necessary: one cannot demand that someone already have a reputation before permitting them to build one. For those first weeks, then, a newcomer can take without consequence.

Tell me which of these actually conflict, which only appear to, and which one has to give. Rank the genuine conflicts by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and for each say what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the list. Please discriminate rather than flagging everything as a tension.
