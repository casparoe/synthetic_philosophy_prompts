So this came up at a flat dinner and I genuinly couldn't referee it. My friend Nadia has been reading about personal identity and came out swinging with a claim I'd never heard put so bluntly: that you could not have come from a different sperm and egg. Not 'probably wouldn't be you' -- couldn't. Her reasoning was that the particular sperm and the particular egg are part of what you are, the way a particular lump of clay is part of a particular pot, so a world where some other sperm got there first simply doesn't contain you, however nice the resulting person turns out to be. Josh said that's just a picture doing the work. He said the reason I'm me and not you is that my mental life hangs together as one continuous thing -- memories, temperament, the whole chain of experiences -- and if you stipulate a world where a different sperm fertilised the egg but the resulting person still ends up with my whole life, then that person is me, and Nadia's intuition is just squeamishness about not being made of the 'right' stuff.

Then it got more interesting. Nadia said Josh's stipulation is cheating, because the genes come from the sperm, so you can't hold my memories and personality fixed while swapping the sperm around -- change the sperm and you change the person who grows up, so the thought experiment begs the question. Josh said fine, then try it with identical twins: same sperm, same egg, same genome, two seperate people. If the genome can't be what makes me me, he said, it's hard to see why the sperm that delivered it should be. Nadia came back with a distinction I hadn't thought of: that the twinning happens after fertilisation, so her view was never really about the zygote -- your origin is the particular cell you grew from, and each twin grew from a different cell, so twins aren't a counterexample at all, and Josh has been knocking down a crude version of her position. Josh said that move is the problem rather than the fix -- if 'your origin' gets redefined every time a case comes up, the thesis stops predicting anything and becomes unfalsifiable.

Please go through this properly, move by move -- for each of the four or five steps, say whether it's a good point, a bad one, or somewhere in between, and why. Then tell me what each of them is missing, and who's closer to right overall. Write your answer in British English.
