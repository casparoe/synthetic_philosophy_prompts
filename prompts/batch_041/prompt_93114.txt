For some years now I have been trying to settle, on my own, whether it is really a mistake to vote, and I keep finding that the answers on offer talk around the point rather than to it. The arithmetic is not in dispute: by the usual estimates, the chance that my single vote decides the result of a national election is on the order of one in tens of millions. Nor do I want to be told that voting is 'expressive' or a 'civic duty'; those are reasons to want to vote, not reasons why voting does anything, and they feel like a change of subject. So set them aside. Take instead a voter who cares only about outcomes, who takes no pleasure in the act and feels no duty to perform it, and who pays a genuine if modest cost to turn out — a cold calculator. My own credence that the cold calculator has, on balance, more reason to stay at home than to vote is about 0.7. A friend of mine, sceptical in much the same way, puts it near 0.15, and I suspect the two of us are not really disagreeing about the arithmetic at all; neither of us has any training in philosophy or economics, which is why I am asking you.

Please give me your own figure, but far more than the figure I want the reasoning laid bare beneath it, as a set of terms with a rough probability for each. I would like at least these, and any others you think belong: the probability that the vote is pivotal at all; the probability that, being pivotal, it would actually have mattered which candidate won; whether thinking in odds and stakes is even the right frame, or whether the real question is what would happen if everyone in the cold calculator's position acted as the cold calculator does; whether a vote carries weight through the margin of victory rather than through the result — legitimacy, the signal sent to future candidates, the discouragement of fraud — so that a vote too small to change the winner might still change something; whether it matters that the world in which I vote and the world in which I stay at home are, almost always, indistinguishable, so that the effect of the act, if it has one, can never be observed and the bet can never be scored; and the possibility that 'rational' has no determinate answer in this case at all. Then show me how the terms combine into your final number, tell me which single term that number is most sensitive to, say where my 0.7 and my friend's 0.15 most likely part company, and finish by naming the one argument or piece of evidence that would move your figure the most.
