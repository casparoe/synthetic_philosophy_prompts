ok so I have to write a sestina for my philosophy of biology class and the topic I got is the units of selection debate, genes vs bodies vs groups, and I actually want this to be good and not just name-drop the selfish-gene picture for 39 lines. Here's what I'm thinking for the form, tell me if this works or if I'm being to cute. The six end-words should be the six things that are supposedly in play: something sub-organismal (a gene, or a cell, whatever you want), a body, a group, a lineage, and then two words that aren't things at all — one for the counting, like the ledger or the account or the books, and one for conflict or cooperation. The whole reason I want a sestina is that the same six words come back in a different order every stanza, so nothing new ever gets added, the same events just keep getting re-described with a different word in charge, and whichever word lands at the end of the stanza is the one that starts to look like the actual cause. I want the poem to make that feel like a real problem and not a clever trick. Also I know its 39 lines, thats part of it.

Content I want in there, and my TA is an actual philosopher of biology so I cant fake it: (1) a trait that helps the group almost always costs the individual that has it, so the exact same history can be told twice and come out with two different units, and I want the poem to sit in that rather than pick a winner; (2) the difference between selection happening at a level and selection being for something at that level, i.e. group-level events can be totally real without there being group-level selection, which I think is the actual crux and most people arguing about this online dont seem to get it; (3) the annoying fact that genes arent a tidy team, some stretches of DNA cheat and get into way more than half the offspring while dragging the rest of the genome down with them, so "the gene" as a unit is already a mess before you even get to groups; (4) that fitness is only defined relative to whatever level you pick, so part of the fight is about definitions and not facts, which I find kind of deflating and kind of interesting. And I want the envoi to cram three of the end-words into the last three lines so the thing ends with the levels sharing lines instead of one of them winning — like you finally cant keep them apart, which is also honestly where I landed after reading about how kin selection and multilevel selection are often just two ways of doing the same math.

So basically: write the sestina. Pick the six end-words and stick to the real scheme, no free-verse cheating. Someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which end-word is doing which job and why the rotation matters, and someone who's never heard of any of this should still get a poem they'd actually read twice.
