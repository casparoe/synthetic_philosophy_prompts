I'm not a philosopher, just someone who got into an argument with my cousin at Thanksgiving about surrogacy. She's a nurse in California and knows people who've been surrogates, and she says it's just work—you carry a pregnancy for someone else, you get paid, and if a woman signs a contract and consents, what's the problem. But I have a friend from Italy who says surrogacy is banned there, and I think alot of European countries either ban paid surrogacy or only allow it if the surrogate isn't paid beyond expenses, while in the US there's this weird patchwork where some states enforce commercial surrogacy contracts, some won't, and some criminalize them. Meanwhile egg donors in the US often get paid for time, pain, and inconvenience but the contract definately insists it's not payment for the eggs themselves, which sounds like a legal fiction to me. And in a gestational surrogacy the surrogate has no genetic tie to the baby, so the law treats her as not the mother, while the egg donor is the genetic mother but usually has no parental rights. That seems to assume a very particular view of what makes someone a parent—gestation, genetics, intention, or contract. So my question is: what philosophical commitments does each system actually presuppose? The American market one seems to presuppose that women can sell reproductive labor and that a contract can cover pregnancy, but the European bans seem to presuppose that some things shouldn't be for sale even if the woman consents. Are those views consistent with their own other rules? Like, if Europe bans paid surrogacy because it commodities women, why does it often allow compensated egg donation for research, or unpaid altruistic surrogacy? And if the US says it's just a service, why does it get so squeamish about paying for the egg itself rather than the time it took to produce it? I want you to reconstruct the implicit theory behind each practice, tell me which parts are strictly presupposed and which are just convenient, and say where the practice is actually underdetermined so that several theories could license it. Also, if we replaced the presupposed view with a different one—say, treating pregnancy as a form of labor like any other, or treating the body as outside commerce entirely—what would have to change in the actual laws and contracts? Keep it under 800 words, and dont just give me a list of pros and cons; I want the hidden philosophy.
