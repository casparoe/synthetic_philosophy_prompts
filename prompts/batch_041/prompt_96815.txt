Bit of context first, then the ask. I'm the deputy head at a state secondary school in France -- not a philosopher, just someone who reads the rationalist blogs on the train and has somehow become the person who handles every request to be excused from something, to wear something, or to skip an activity on religious grounds. It's maybe two or three a week, the stakes are a kid's grades or a family's trust in the school, and whatever I decide becomes the precedent for the whole building, so I can't just wing it. I need a procedure I can run at my desk in ten minutes: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it terminates in a no, and where it's a values call that gets punted upstairs to the head.

What I keep tripping on is that I've got two instincts fighting, and they're basically the two sides of the laïcité vs multiculturalism thing. The French instinct says the school is neutral ground and nobody gets special treatment. The Anglo instinct says neutrality is a fiction and fairness means accomodating everyone equally. My hunch -- tell me if it's wrong -- is that these two agree on the answer most of the time and disagree only about the reason, and that the genuinely hard cases are the ones where one of them has to stop being honest about what it's actually doing. I also suspect laïcité is really two seperate doctrines stapled together, one about the state not endorsing religion and one about protecting people from religion, and that the second one is doing most of the work and isn't neutral at all. If that's right, I want the procedure to say which doctrine it's leaning on at each step instead of pretending there's a single principle underneath.

So: build the procedure, mark the point where the French and the Anglo frameworks actually fork rather than papering over it, and run it on three cases. A girl who wants to wear a headscarf in PE -- I'm fairly sure that's just illegal here, and I want to know whether the procedure reaches that conclusion honestly or has to cheat to get there. A family asking for a substitute meal in the canteen. And one request that you think I should refuse even though refusing is awkward, because granting it quietly costs some other kid something. If the procedure tells me to grant something I'd instinctively refuse, don't massage it into a yes -- own the no.

Last thing: tell me what the procedure can't do. Which requests will it mis-sort no matter how carefully I fill in the blanks?
