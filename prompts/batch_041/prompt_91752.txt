My friend and colleague of thirty years is retiring at the end of term and I'm giving the toast at the dinner — about forty people, five minutes max, half of them her family, who don't know a theorem from a thimble. She's spent her whole career as a constructivist: constructive analysis, the Bishop sort, no law of excluded middle, no 'there exist infinitely many' unless you can actually hand her one, no proofs that lean on double negation, no actual infinity. Over the years she's said some version of the same rule at me across a lot of dinners: if you can't exhibit it, don't assert it. I'm not one of her. I'm a card-carrying classical guy — I think every sentence she won't let me say is either true or false — and privately I think those scruples cost her a career.

Here's the gift I've decided to give her. I'm going to write the whole toast in sentences she could have asserted herself. Every one. Its turning out to be brutal, because every line a retirement toast actually runs on is one of the banned ones. 'Nobody else could have done it.' 'Her work will outlive all of us.' 'She's irreplacable.' 'You'll be missed' — even that one I can't back up, I don't know what this department will feel in March. And I can't fall back on 'she was either the finest teacher in this building or the finest researcher in it' when I've never once been able to decide which. She'd also have hated me saying she thought some statements are neither true nor false, so keep me well away from that.

One thing I'd like to land, if you can find a way: I believe there are true theorems nobody will ever prove. She'd say that isn't even a sentence worth saying. I want the toast to hold that without me asserting it.

So write me the toast. Warm, a couple of real laughs, 600-700 words, and it has to work for the cousins and for the six people in the room who will be checking every clause. It should be honest that I haven't converted. And it should never feel like a lecture on logic — I want it funny-strange and careful and full of things I actually watched her do, not airy stuff. Also don't end it with 'here's to the next forty years,' for obvious reasons.
