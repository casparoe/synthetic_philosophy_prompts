I am a retired natural scientist who has lately been reading philosophy of science, and I have got stuck on the F-twist — the thesis, associated with Milton Friedman's essay on positive economics, that a theory's assumptions need not be realistic and that the theory is to be judged by the accuracy of its predictions over the range of phenomena it is meant to cover. After forty years of building and defending models, I find this at once familiar and evasive, and I would rather have the specification than another defence of it.

Set out the desiderata any adequate account of unrealistic assumptions in scientific modelling must satisfy: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the features of actual modelling practice it must be consistent with, with the consideration that motivates each requirement. The following should be among them, since they are where the simple story breaks down in my experience: false assumptions whose errors cancel in the aggregate; a false assumption that is precisely the mechanism a proposed intervention would act upon; assumptions that approximate a truth approachable as a limit, as against fictions for which no such limit exists; and the difference between assumptions that merely license a derivation and assumptions that do the explanatory work. Please coin and fix a distinction between falsehoods that are load-bearing, in the sense that the result depends on them, and falsehoods that are scaffolding, in the sense that the result survives their removal, and give me a criterion for telling which is which in a particular model.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are commitments of one camp. If some pair cannot be jointly satisfied — the demand that a model explain rather than merely predict, say, together with the demand that its unrealistic assumptions be epistemically innocuous — say so plainly, as that is the part I most want. Then, briefly, how Friedman's answer and the Galilean-idealisation answer fare against the list; a table is acceptable there, but keep the weight on the criteria.
