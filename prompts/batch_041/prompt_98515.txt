I've been drafting a short essay on Occam's razor and Solomonoff induction and I'd like comments before I inflict it on anyone. My line is roughly this. People sell Solomonoff induction as "it proves Occam's razor": you weight each program by 2^-its-length, short programs dominate, so simplicity is justified. I want to argue this gets it backwards twice over. First, what the convergence result actually needs is just that the prior is universal — puts nonzero weight on every computable sequence — and the specific 2^-length weighting is a convenience that keeps the prior computable and the bounds tidy; any prior that dominates it does the same epistemic job, so the "razor" is bookkeeping, not the engine. Second, the invariance you get on the Kolmogorov side is only up to an additive constant that depends on your universal machine, so the razor has content only asymptotically or in convergence rates, never in a particular finite comparison — which is the only regime anyone actually has data in. Punchline: Occam's razor, read this way, is really a bet that the world is compressible rather than a preference among theories, and the real question is whether that bet is empirical or a precondition of induction at all. I've also got a throwaway paragraph claiming L2 regularization in ML is a different beast, since it's a prior over parameterized predictors rather than over programs, so the two razors don't transfer. Where am I getting this wrong, which bits are actually interesting versus just restating the obvious, and is the bookkeeping point fair or am I ignoring that having a computable prior is what makes the theorem go through in the first place?
