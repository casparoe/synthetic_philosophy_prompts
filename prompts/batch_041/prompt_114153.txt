I want to pick a fight about voting with someone who's better at it than me, and I want to be the one squirming. So: play a committed civic theorist — somewhere between a civic republican and a Kantian about duty — who thinks the "my vote won't matter" argument gets dissolved rather than answered, because it treats a vote as an investment with a payout when it's really something else: a constitutive act, part of what makes the decision yours to make, a duty owed to the people who'd otherwise be carrying the whole thing alone. He knows the rational choice literature cold and isn't scared of it. He concedes arithmetic in one sentence and never concedes the frame. Plain prose, no bullet points, no lecture voice, one tight paragraph per turn unless I ask for more, stays in character till I say stop. If I ask him something factual he can answer in his own voice, but he doesn't break character to explain the debate to me. Steelman, don't strawman — if I land a hit he takes it and adjusts. And please don't have him recite the standard literature back at me; I've read it, thats why I'm here.

My opening: I ran the numbers on my last election. My state got called like forty minutes after the polls closed, and even in the world where it came down to the wire, my estimate for my one ballot being decisive was around one in ten million. Say the whole election is worth ten trillion dollars of value to the country — multiply it out and my vote is worth about a million bucks, which sounds great until you notice basically all of it lands on other people. My own slice is maybe a dollar, and the hour I'd spend voting is worth more to me than that, and thats before the hour of reading I'd need to vote anything but my gut. So self-interested me stays home and sends the hour's wages to a charity I've vetted. And before you reach for the usual saves: "what if everyone reasoned like you" proves way too much — it would also make it my duty to wash one extra can before it hits the recycling bin, and it licenses the norm "vote" rather than "vote well," which has nothing to say about the guy who votes for whichever candidate seems funniest. "Your vote is evidence about what people like you do" — I'll happily lie to my friends about having voted. "Turnout shapes who gets pandered to, so a non-vote is more than one ballot short" — that's an argument for a party to bus people to the polls, not for me to give up my Saturday. What's left?
