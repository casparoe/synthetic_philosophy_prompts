I'm in a reading group that's been running about a year, and we've split roughly down the middle on a question that started as 'whose values should aligned AI serve?' and has now hardened into two camps that can't talk to each other. On one side are the people I've started calling the aggregators: they reckon that once you've got a system powerful enough to matter, the honest thing is to build in a single, well-defined objective over everyone's preferences or welfare -- choose the aggregation rule carefully, fine, but stop pretending you can avoid ranking some lives against others. On the other side are the neutralists: they think any single objective function is a contested conception of the good wearing a lab coat, and that an aligned system has to stay procedurally neutral -- surfacing options, brokering agreements, never itself deciding that your values outrank mine. They've been argueing past each other for months and I'm sick of it.

So I want to force them into the adversarial-collaboration format. Draft the joint statement they could both actually put their names to. I want the claims both sides genuinely accept (about what alignment is even for, about what neutrality can and can't mean, about the plain fact that someone has to write the code); then the exact residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would sign off on the wording; then the cruxes -- my hunch is that one is whether interpersonal comparisons of value are ever more than a modelling choice, and another is whether neutrality is itself a thick value commitment rather than a meta-level absence of one, but tell me if I've got the real cruxes wrong -- and for each crux, the case or argument both sides would agree definately bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

To stop this floating off into the ether, our group got weirdly obsessed with a half-baked proposal one of us keeps pushing, which we've ended up calling the chit system. Everyone gets a fixed budget of influence chits to spend however they like on how the system resolves conflicts between their values and other people's, and the system maximises chit-weighted satisfaction -- you can hoard them, trade them, blow the lot on one pet cause. The aggregators claim this is basically their view with extra steps; the neutralists claim it's aggregation laundered through a market metaphor. Whatever you make of it, please use it as a concrete test case both sides have to react to, since it's the closest we've come to an actual proposal. Format the whole thing as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and finish with a short note on which parts of the document you'd trust least, and what would make you tear it up and redraft.
