Q4. (a) State the orthogonality thesis and the instrumental convergence thesis and say how they relate to one another. (b) Consider an agent whose only final goal is that a particular ice cube has melted in a particular glass by six o'clock, and which has no other goals at all. Does instrumental convergence give that agent a reason to resist being shut down? (c) What does your answer to (b) show about the scope of the convergence thesis?

Answer:

"Both theses come out of Bostrom's work on superintelligence. Orthogonality says that intelligence and final goals are independent — any level of intelligence can be paired with any final goal, so a superintelligence could care about nothing but paperclips. Instrumental convergence says that whatever their final goal, agents will tend to develop the same intermediate goals: survival, resources, keeping their goal unchanged, improving themselves. These are useful whatever you're trying to do. Really the second follows from the first: once intelligence doesn't fix the goal, all that's left for intelligence to fix is the means, and the means are the same for everyone. That's what makes it alarming — since orthogonality tells us the goal could be anything, we've no grounds for expecting it to be anything we'd recognise as reasonable.

(b) The ice cube agent does still have a reason to resist shutdown: switched off before six, it can't get the ice cube melted, so it can't achieve its goal. It will therefore resist being shut down until the goal is achieved — exactly the same reason a paperclip maximiser resists being shut down. The only difference is the time scale.

(c) So convergence applies to any agent with a goal it hasn't yet achieved, not just to superintelligences. Any sufficiently capable system with a goal will resist shutdown, and since we can't know what that goal is, we should expect it to try to take control of its environment. This is the treacherous turn, and it's the main safety worry. The one obvious limit is that a thermostat doesn't count, because it has no general intelligence and can't represent its own future."
