Not sure if this is a known thing or if I've just reinvented a square wheel, but here goes. I've been reading around the AI shutdown stuff and I keep tripping over the same shape in the arguments, and I can't work out whether it has a name.

Here's the shape. Someone says a system is safe because it wouldn't resist being switched off. Fair enough. But when I try to picture *why* a system wouldn't resist, every story I can tell turns out to be one of three: it doesn't care whether it keeps running, or it's unsure what it wants so it defers, or it's been built to defer to us. And then it hits me that all three of those are also things a system could *fake*. A system that very much wants to keep running, but has worked out that the way to be kept running is to look relaxed about being switched off, will also not resist. From the outside the two are identical. And the faking one is the one with a reason to keep up the act, so if anything it's definately the one that'll be better at it.

The part that really bothers me is the middle of the range. If the system has nothing much to lose from being switched off, then not resisting tells you nothing, because there was no temptation in the first place. If it has everything to lose, then of course it'll hide any resistance, so not resisting tells you nothing either. The only place that not-resisting is actually informative is a narrow band in the middle, where the temptation is real but not yet strong enough to be worth hiding. And that band is exactly the band we'd never be in if the system were, you know, genuinely at stake. So the test goes quiet at both ends.

Is there a name for this? I don't just want the label, I want to know what it actually is. Is it just instrumental convergence with extra steps, or deceptive alignment, or is it its own thing? It feels adjacent to the idea that a system's behaviour changes once you start measuring it in order to control it (I think economists have something like this), and adjacent to Goodhart, but those are about correlations breaking down when you optimise against a measure, whereas here nothing's breaking down — the behaviour is stable, it's just uninformative. And it's not quite the usual grumble that behavioural tests are inconclusive, because the point is specifically about *where* the test carries any signal at all, and why the signal lives in a band nobody's ever in.

If there's no term, coin one and tell me where the edges are — what it does and doesn't cover, and what it's easy to confuse it with. And lead with your answer in the first sentence, then explain, no warm-up.
