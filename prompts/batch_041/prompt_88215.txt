Now that I have retired from a career in experimental physics, I have spent two years reading my way into philosophy of physics, and next month I am to lead a session of a small reading group — mostly retired scientists, with two members from the humanities — on the substantivalism/relationism dispute about space and time. I do not want the explanation itself but a plan for how to give it. The group will arrive holding the schoolroom version: that Newton's bucket settles the matter in favour of absolute space, that Leibniz replies that space is nothing over and above relations, and that Einstein's general relativity somehow adjudicates between them. My aim is to bring them to the question as it is now posed — whether a world and its uniform shift are one world or two, and whether the two parties are disagreeing about the world or merely about words — and I need the route laid out. Tell me what must be established first and in what order, and which of the usual components (absolute motion, inertial structure, the identity of indiscernibles, the distinction between a mere shift and a symmetry) genuinely depends on which, so that nothing is asserted before it has been earned. Tell me which single example should carry the whole session and what it must have and lack. The bucket is what they will expect, and I suspect it is a trap that teaches absolute motion rather than substantivalism, so say whether I should use it, replace it, or use it precisely in order to dismantle it; and if it is to be replaced, whether a bare toy universe of my own devising — nothing but a few particles fixed in a pattern, considered first in one place and then shifted bodily — can bear the weight, or whether it smuggles in the very container it is meant to test. Give me the misconceptions they will form at each stage, the point in the sequence at which each should be headed off, and one closing question that would reveal whether they have grasped the problem rather than memorised the positions. Finally, tell me what I should openly decline to cover at this level, and why.
