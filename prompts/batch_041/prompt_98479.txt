I need a joint statement for an adversarial collaboration on the leveling-down objection, and I would like you to draft it. The two parties are a prioritarian and a telic egalitarian. The prioritarian holds that a benefit matters more the lower the absolute level of the person who receives it, and therefore denies that there is anything good about an inequality's being removed as such. The telic egalitarian holds that equality is itself a value, so that a more equal distribution can be in one respect better even when it is better for no one — the implication that critics call the leveling-down objection. The two have been talking past each other for years, and the deliverable is a text that both could put their names to.

Set out, first, the claims both can sign: what leveling down is and is not, what each side concedes about the other's motivation, what the objection is actually targeting, and which parts of the dispute turn out to be about words rather than about substance. Then state the residual disagreement as precisely as you can, in wording that each side would accept as a fair rendering of its own position, not a caricature of it. Then identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and for each crux give a case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about how it comes out written down in advance. I would like the cruxes to include at least these candidates, though you should add or drop as the drafting requires: whether the objection is a reductio that refutes telic egalitarianism or merely a cost that a pro tanto value can bear; whether the priority view should be stated comparatively or non-comparatively, and whether the comparative statement leaves it exposed to the same objection it was meant to answer; and whether the objection has any grip when the worse-off group is unaware of the better-off and could never be affected by them.

I also want one case that I have not seen discussed, and I would like you to build it rather than borrow it: a single person whose life is made less peaked — the good years trimmed, the bad years untouched, so that the whole life is flatter and nobody, herself included, is better off for it. Ask each side whether its verdict on leveling down between persons carries over to leveling down within a life, and if the two verdicts come apart, whether the divergence is principled or an ad hoc restriction.

Write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way one would write a jointly signed memorandum or an open letter: no headings, no numbered lists, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables. Where you would be tempted to set the two formulations side by side in a table, write them as consecutive sentences instead. Do not include citations, and do not rest any claim on who said what — state the positions in their own terms, so the document stands on its own.
