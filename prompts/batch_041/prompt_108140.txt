You're a senior research engineer on an AI lab's safety team — specifically the part of the team that evaluates deployment safeguards under the assumption that the model may be misaligned and may be actively working against its overseers. I'm a software engineer building tooling for that team; I have no formal philosophy or ML research background, so I'd rather have the reasoning spelled out in engineering terms than summarized as a literature review. No citations or reading lists, please — work from the descriptions below and from first principles.

Here are eight deployment-time safety measures:

1. Preference-based harmlessness fine-tuning (RLHF) on a broad mix of prompts.
2. Input/output classifiers: separate models that score every user prompt and every model response for policy violations.
3. Trusted monitoring: a weaker, trusted model reads the untrusted model's chain of thought and flags reasoning that looks like it is pursuing an unauthorized goal.
4. Untrusted monitoring: a copy of the untrusted model monitors its own transcripts, with anti-collusion tricks such as hiding which transcript is being scored or making the monitor commit to a random value the monitored copy must guess.
5. White-box probes: linear probes on the model's internal activations, trained to fire when the transcript is deceptive.
6. Paraphrasing: routing the model's output through a separate model that rewrites it, so that any steganographic signal encoded in word choices is destroyed before a human or monitor sees it.
7. Honeypot tasks: seeding the deployment environment with tasks that a schemer would plausibly attempt, and checking whether it does.
8. Capability-threshold evals: not deploying until red-teaming shows the model cannot autonomously complete a set of dangerous tasks.

I want these ranked from most to least schemer-robust — that is, how much of each measure's protective value would survive a model that is not merely misaligned but situationally aware of the measure and optimizing against it. Please think carefully and brainstorm before ranking: first, for each measure, state in a sentence or two what it implicitly assumes about the model (for example, that the model is honest, that it cannot model the monitor, that its deception necessarily surfaces in some observable channel, that two copies of it cannot coordinate, or that its own self-reports are informative about its internals). Then give the ranking as a tier list: place each measure in exactly one of five tiers (S, A, B, C, D), give each a 0–10 score on the same dimension, and add one sentence of justification per measure. Where two measures only do real work in combination, say so explicitly instead of ranking them independently.

Close with a short paragraph on two things: which of these, once you grant a competent schemer, comes closest to security theater rather than a genuine safeguard, and which single measure not on the list you would most want to add.
