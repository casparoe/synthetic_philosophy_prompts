I've been going back and forth with a friend from my ethics seminar about promises, and she's built an arguement I can't shake but that seems to me to prove way more than it should. I'd like you to tell me where it actually breaks, if it does, and what it costs to break it.

Her setup is that a promise is supposed to be a case of a "normative power" -- a bit of verbal behaviour that changes what you owe, all by itself. She calls these world-changing words and puts on the same list consent, contracts, waivers, vows, resigning, swearing an oath, appointing someone to act for you. Then the argument proper. (1) An obligation binds you only if it's grounded in a reason that holds independently of your will -- you can't manufacture a reason by deciding to have one, or by saying out loud that you have one. (2) When I promise you something, what actually happens in the world is that I make noises, you form an expectation, and my future psychology is slightly altered (guilt, embarrassment). (3) Noises, expectations and feelings are just facts, and facts about someone's expectations don't by themselves make anything owed. (4) So at most I acquire an obligation not to deliberately cultivate expectations I mean to dash -- a duty of honesty or non-maleficence, which is not the duty to do the promised thing and which apology or compensation can discharge. (5) So a promise never generates the obligation it purports to generate; it only triggers obligations I already had. (6) If that's right for promising it's right for everything else on her list, consent included, since all that literally happens when I consent is that I say words and you form an expectation. (7) So nobody can ever change what's owed by an act of will; the normative landscape is fixed by reasons that don't depend on anyone's say-so. (8) But (7) has to be false. If consent changes nothing, then the difference between a surgeon who operates with my permission and one who operates without it is just a difference in how I feel about it, and I don't believe that, and neither does she.

I keep thinking the weak link is somewhere in (1) to (3), but every repair I try either leaves (6) untouched or hands the conclusion back to her. She, for what it's worth, would rather die than give up (1), and thinks (4) is obviously true if you look at it for two seconds. She also keeps saying that if I want promises to bind I have to explain how a mere utterance reaches forward and grabs a future me by the throat, and that the only honest answer is "it can't, we all just pretend it does." So: which step do I reject, why did it look fine until I stared at it, and what do I have to pay to reject it? And if you tell me to keep (1) and give up something else, tell me what happens to the consent case when I do.
