My arithmetic gives an infinite expectation for p ≤ 1/2 and 2p/(2p − 1) for p > 1/2, so that p = 0.5001 yields an expectation of about 5000 while p = 0.4999 yields an infinite one; and if you do not know p and your prior puts any weight on p ≤ 1/2, the expectation is still infinite. Check this, correct it if I am wrong, and then carry on with the corrected version. I take it to show that the familiar reply that real coins are not fair and real casinos go broke does not rescue expected-value maximization.
