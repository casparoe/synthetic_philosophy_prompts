I am attempting to determine whether a pattern I have noticed in practical reasoning has an established name, and if so, how precisely it is drawn. The pattern is as follows. I am one of many agents who are relevantly similar to one another and who are dispersed so widely that no action of mine can causally affect any of them, nor theirs mine. There is no communication, no contract, and no enforcement. Nevertheless, when I deliberate about whether to take an action that would benefit those other agents at some cost to me, I find that my choice is not an isolated act. Because they and I are running the same kind of reasoning over the same kind of situation, my choosing the cooperative option is evidence that they will choose it too. If I choose it, I should expect a world in which the cooperation is reciprocal; if I choose the selfish option, I should expect a world in which it is not. The upshot is that a kind of mutually beneficial cooperation can be rational even though no one can affect anyone else. It is not cooperation in the ordinary causal sense; it is not merely a moral hope, because the reasoning changes what it is rational to do; and it is not simply a coincidence of values, because the agents need not share values in full—they may each be willing to help the other because each expects the other to help them.

It appears to me that this is close to several ideas I have encountered—evidential reasoning, superrationality, team reasoning, Kantian universalizability, acausal trade—but none of them seems exactly right, or perhaps I am missing the one that is. I am particularly unsure about the version in which the agents are not identical copies but are merely drawn from a common distribution of reasoning procedures, and in which their values overlap only partially. Is there an established name for this phenomenon? If there is, I want to know what it is and, more importantly, how it is carved: what exactly the mechanism is; what it requires (identical decision procedures? common knowledge? a common prior? a large world? correlated algorithms? overlapping values?); and how it differs from the nearest alternatives. If there is no single established name, please coin one and define it, with the same attention to boundaries. Do not give me a bibliography or a reading list; I want the concept, not the citations.
