OK, I need to get my own house in order here. I teach Dennett's three stances every year, and I've started noticing that my gut verdicts about when there's a real pattern in something don't add up. So here's me going case by case with no theory attached, and I want you to tell me what principle I'm actually running, if there is one.

- Pi's digits: real pattern. A genuinely random binary string: no pattern. A pseudorandom string out of a seeded generator: my gut says no pattern either, even though a short program spits it out and I can predict every bit of it. I know that verdict is embarrassing for the usual line that being a pattern just is being compressible, so it may be the one that has to go.
- A face in a cloud: not a pattern. A face in the actual crowd: pattern. That one feels solid, and the reason I give is that the crowd face tells me where the eyebrows will be and the cloud face doesn't.
- The centre of mass of my car: real. The average taxpayer: real, boringly. The 'typical Millennial': I want to say that's not a real thing, and I can't articulate what's different about it.
- Species: real. Gene: real. Dog breed: real, I think?? Race: definately not, that's a social construction. Breeds are the one I keep flip-flopping on, because the story I tell about species (deep, causally-loaded clustering, shared history) versus race (shallow, and the clustering is enforced by us rather than by the world) is a difference of degree, and breeds sit right in the middle, with the enforcement done by us again.
- Thermostat: it's 'trying' to hold the room at 20 and I'm fine saying that, but it doesn't believe or want anything. Chess engine: believes its queen is en prise, wants to win. Dog: believes and wants. Bacterium: 'trying' to get to the nutrients, fine; 'wants' the nutrients, and I squirm. Hurricane: not trying anything. The market 'expects' a rate cut: fine. 'The West believes X': fine when I'm being loose, not when I'm being careful.

So: is there one principle behind all this? My guess is its something about how much the higher-stance vocabulary buys you over the lower one in predictive terms, plus something about whether the pattern is there in the thing or only in my projection of it, but that guess doesn't survive contact with the pseudorandom string. And what does your repaired principle say about two cases I haven't listed -- a single thrown dart: is the trajectory a real pattern, or only the distribution over a thousand throws? And a termite colony, where the design stance seems to work beautifully and no termite anywhere is holding the blueprint? If one of my verdicts has to go, tell me which, and be straight with me about what the surviving principle still gets wrong.
