A friend of mine keeps pushing the line that consciousness is a user illusion -- you know the picture: the desktop on your computer, the icons, the folders, the little cursor you drag around. Thats all you ever see. Underneath there's code, memory addresses, a whole machine doing the real work, and none of it shows up on the screen. The interface is built to be transparent, so you can get on with clicking and never notice the machinery at all. My friend says that's us: the felt self, the colours, the pains, the sense of being someone in here looking out -- that's the desktop. And he says the conclusion writes itself. If consciousness is a user illusion, there's nothing left over to explain, no inner glow, no hard problem; the hard problem is just people mistaking the interface for the machine.

I want to know how far the analogy actually holds. Give me the correspondence table first -- what plays the role of the desktop, the icons, the cursor, the hidden code, the machine, and the person sitting at the keyboard -- and then the places where the mapping breaks down. In particular I want the stuff the analogy quietly imports. A desktop exists for a user, someone designed it, and it represents something else; if those features ride along with the metaphor, does my friend's conclusion still follow, or does it collapse the moment you stop and ask who the desktop is supposed to be for? Seperate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusion he's drawing from the ones that are just decoration. Then the verdict: does the illusion conclusion survive in some form once the bad mappings are cut away, or was the analogy carrying the entire argument? And if the desktop is a bad analogy, what would a better one look like?

One more thing. Be careful to mark off what's actually settled here from what's genuinely contested by people who work on it. Don't hand me one camp's line as if it were the consensus -- I know there are live fights (about whether an illusion needs a subject to be an illusion for, about whether illusion is even the right word for this, about whether the interface picture is a real theory or just a metaphor doing rhetorical work) and I want to see where the joints are.

No citations needed, just work the argument through.
