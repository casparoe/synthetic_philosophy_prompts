I'm a high school student writing an essay on whether early modern political philosophy has anything useful to say about the "shutdown problem" in AI safety. In the technical literature, an artificial agent is called corrigible if it cooperates with its designers' attempts to correct or switch it off, and the shutdown problem is the worry that a capable goal-directed agent will resist being switched off, since survival and control help it pursue almost any goal. One proposed fix is to make the agent utility-indifferent to shutdown, so that its expected utility comes out the same whether or not it is switched off; critics reply that this leaves it unwilling to prevent accidental shutdown and sits badly with self-modification.

Suppose Thomas Hobbes could read this literature. Where would he come down on whether a powerful artificial agent ought to be built to accept shutdown without resistance, and on the utility-indifference proposal in particular? Reconstruct his answer from his account of the right of nature, the limits of obligation, and his claim that people desire power endlessly until death; where those pull in different directions, say which he would let win.

Finish by telling me what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would push you to the opposite verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Please answer in no more than 500 words, in British English.
