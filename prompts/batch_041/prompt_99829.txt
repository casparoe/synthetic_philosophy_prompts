Hey — I'm teaching a grad seminar this fall on the moral status of wild nature, the whole uncomfortable question of whether an ecosystem is actually a good thing for the animals living inside it, and I want to kick of week two with a piece of satire. Can you write it for me?

Here's the brief. Write a direct-mail fundraising letter, maybe 600–700 words, from the (invented) executive director of an (invented) wildlife charity. The premise is that the charity has quietly followed animal-welfare biology to its conclusion and is now soliciting donations to destroy habitat. Play it completely straight — no winks, no self-awareness, no moment where the writer lets on that this is a bit. The director is earnest, faintly defensive, and sincerely believes he's the compassionate one in the room. If the reader only realises it's satire when the penny drops on its own, perfect.

The humour has to come from getting the actual content right, not from the absurdity of the premise. So lean on the arithmetic of r-selected life histories — an animal that lays ten thousand eggs and gets two to adulthood is, on the ledger, mostly a machine for producing brief painful deaths — on the gap between a species and the individual animals that constitute it, on why preserving the wetland can mean locking in another few thousand years of exactly the same, on why the quick deaths a bulldozer causes are meant to be a rounding error next to the births that never happen. Have him actually run numbers: give me a made-up but internally consistent figure, call it suffering-years averted per hectare, and then use it consistently through the rest of the letter, including in the donation tiers, so the maths holds together if someone checks it.

Please include: a short objections section where he fields the obvious one (but don't you kill the animals that are there right now?) in a way that exposes the structure of the position rather than papering over it; a paragraph where he gets visibly irritated at donors who only care about pandas and biodiversity; a signature block; and a P.S. that lands one more turn of the screw. Keep it legible to a smart reader who's never heard any of this — define the idea in passing as he goes, don't assume the vocabulary, and don't let it turn into jargon.

No real organisations or real people — invent the charity and its director. Thanks!
