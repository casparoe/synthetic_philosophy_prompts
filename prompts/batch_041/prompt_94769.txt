I manage a small forecasting team at a nonprofit that advises state health agencies, and we keep hitting a recurring problem: two analysts will present different probabilities for the same event, each has read the other's memo, each knows the other has read it, and yet neither moves. My graduate training left me with Aumann's agreement theorem and the uneasy sense that it says this should be impossible under certain conditions, but the conditions are exactly what we never have: a shared prior, common knowledge of rationality, and posteriors that are common knowledge rather than merely public. I need a decision procedure my team can run, in under an hour, when two analysts disagree by more than a stated threshold, say ten percentage points on a binary event, to decide whether to keep reconciling, escalate to me, or formally record the disagreement and move on. The procedure should distinguish among the several ways the theorem's assumptions can fail, because the right response differs depending on whether the failure is a different prior, private information that one analyst cannot disclose, a disagreement about the model class, or a failure of common knowledge rather than mutual knowledge. It should treat common knowledge as a graded thing, what the team knows, what each knows the other knows, and so on, and it should say at what level it is worth spending meeting time to climb, since infinite common knowledge is not available in a Tuesday staff meeting. It should also be honest about cases where the theorem is irrelevant because the disagreement is normative, because the analysts are not Bayesian agents in the relevant sense, or because they are disagreeing about what counts as the event rather than about its probability. Please write the procedure as an internal operating procedure with numbered steps, a short set of test questions, a clear statement of who signs off on escalation, and a final section on what the procedure cannot settle.

Then run the procedure on three cases: first, two analysts disagree about the probability that a new variant will cause a hospital surge, one using a mechanistic model and one using a historical base rate; second, two analysts agree on the model but one has seen a preliminary unpublished dataset that cannot be shared outside the team; third, a case where the procedure tells me to stop reconciling even though I strongly want consensus, and explain why that is the right answer. The output should be in American English, and it should avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need something my team can actually use, not a scholarly essay.
