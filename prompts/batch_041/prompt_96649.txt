I've been reading about Galileo's fight with the church over whether the earth moves, and I tried to write his side out as a clean argument just to see if I actually buy it. Here's what I got.

(1) The bible was written for everybody, including people who can't do math or astronomy, so God had to describe things the way they look to ordinary people rather than the way they actually are.
(2) Nature doesn't do that. The planets don't adjust their behaviour to fit what we expect.
(3) So when a passage of scripture seems to say something about nature that a real demonstration contradicts, the passage has to be read in a way that fits the demonstration.
(4) Something counts as demonstrated when it's shown by sense experience and necessary proof.
(5) The verses about the sun standing still and the earth not moving were being used to refute the earth moving, but by (1) those verses are just describing how things looked.
(6) So they don't refute the earth moving.
(7) Nothing in (1) depends on which passage it is, so the same goes for any scriptural passage that seems to describe nature.
(8) Therefore no passage of scripture can ever settle a question about the natural world.

The trouble is this seems to prove way too much. It makes the meaning of scripture about nature hostage to whatever the scientists of the day can demonstrate, and demonstrations get overturned all the time — Galileo himself got plenty wrong about comets and tides. So the rule would have us reinterpret scripture to fit a science that later turns out to be false, and then reinterpret it again. It also seems to prove the opposite of what Galileo wants: if scripture has nothing to say about nature, then fine, the church was wrong to condem him, but it also means scripture can't be trusted when it looks like it's describing a miracle, and the sun standing still was supposed to be a miracle. So which step do I give up? I'd like to keep (3), because I don't think anyone should be condemned for saying the earth moves, but I don't want to end up at (8). Tell me where the slide is and what it costs to fix.
