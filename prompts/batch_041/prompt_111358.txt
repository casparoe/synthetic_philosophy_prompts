A colleague of mine who works on theory change in physics has been ruling, case by case, on whether each discarded or superseded posit was real or a useful fiction. Phlogiston: fiction. Caloric: fiction. The luminiferous ether as a mechanical medium: fiction, though she insists that the equations of the wave theory were real, since they survived into Maxwell's theory. Newton's absolute space and absolute time: fiction, with the inertial structure alone surviving. The chemical atom of the nineteenth century: real. The electron: real, and what she stresses in that case is that you can trap one and fling it at a screen. So she is neither a pessimist about old theories nor a consistent operationalist: the atom was real to her decades before anyone could observe one, and the ether's equations were real even though nothing about the ether could be handled. Her verdicts seem to fit more than one rule, and I cannot tell which one she is actually running. She will next be asked about dark matter: ineliminable from the structure of our cosmological models, credited with successful predictions, and impossible to isolate or manipulate in any experiment. What will she say, and why? Give me the rule she appears to be running, at least one rival rule that fits her earlier verdicts just as well, what each implies about dark matter, your best guess with your reasons for it, and the case that would separate the rules. Answer in American English.
