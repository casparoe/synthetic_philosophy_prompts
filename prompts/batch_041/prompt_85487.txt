Ok so I'm hosting a session for our philosophy of religion reading group and I want to try something other than the usual each-side-presents-then-we-argue format. We've got two regulars who've been going round in circles for about a year on divine hiddenness. One of them thinks the widespread existence of reasonable, non-resistant non-belief is a serious problem for the idea of a loving God, and honestly for any personal God rather than just the maximally loving one. The other thinks hiddenness is not a bug but a feature: a relationship you have to actually want, and search for, is worth something that a relationship you couldn't avoid wouldn't be.

I want to hand them the document they should have written months ago, the adversarial collaboration. So please draft it. In British English please.

What I want in it:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims they could both actually sign. It should cover what the hiddenness argument is and isn't claiming, what phenomenon is even up for explanation (is it the absence of evidence, or the absence of experience, or the pattern of who ends up believing and who doesn't?), and what would count as the argument succeeding. Dont pad it. If a claim is one both sides would sign only because it's empty, cut it and say why you cut it.

2. A disagreements table. One row per genuine disagreement, with each side's position written in wording that side would accept as fair to it. Then a column for what would move each of them, and whether anything actually would.

3. The cruxes, the sub-questions where settling it would shift at least one side. I think three are in play but you tell me: whether belief is voluntary at all (if it isn't, does withholding evidence even count as something a loving being could do?); how to set up the counterfactual comparison at all, since we can't just imagine God making himself obvious without smuggling in an entirely different world; and whether the theist's it's-good-for-us reply is itself an empirical claim that could be falsified. For each crux give a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

4. Then the bit I care about most. Both of them have to say something about the Evident World: a world where God's existence is as manifest to everyone as the existence of other minds, or of the external world, or whatever you think is the least controversial analogue. Is that world even coherently describable? Would it be better or worse for the goods each side cares about? Each side writes its prediction first, no hedging.

5. Finally, flag anything that's really just verbal. My suspicion is that a chunk of it turns on whether hidden means no evidence as opposed to no experience, and on whether loving is being used as a standing disposition or as something that shows up in experience. If that's right, say so; if it's not, say where I'm wrong.

Please don't just tell me who argued what or point me at literature, I want the actual formulations and I don't have time to chase references anyway. Use a seperate table format for section 2.
