The setup, in case any of it matters: Beauty goes to sleep Sunday, they flip a fair coin, heads means they wake her once on Monday, tails means they wake her Monday, wipe her memory of it, and wake her again on Tuesday; either way it's over Wednesday and she's been told the whole protocol in advance. When she wakes and doesn't know what day it is, what should her credence in heads be? I'm a thirder (1/3), Dana is a halfer (1/2), and we each have the other's arguements memorized — hers is that waking up tells her nothing she wasn't already certain of on Sunday, mine is that you should count yourself as a random draw from the awakenings and two of the three of them are tails-awakenings, plus the betting stuff where she breaks even at even odds if she bets on heads at every waking but not if she only bets once. Four months, zero movement, and everyone in the group is now sick of us.

Here's my hypothesis, and I want you to tell me honestly whether it's any good: I think we're stuck because we aren't answering the same question and we're both using the word 'credence' for it. Dana keeps talking about the coin — about what happened Sunday and what the chances were then — while I keep talking about the epistemic position of whoever is awake now, which is a fact about the structure of the experiment rather than about the coin. Notice that the moment Beauty can settle something with the information she actually has, we agree completely: told it's Monday we both say 1/2, told it's Tuesday we both say 0. The fight only starts when she's deliberately kept in the dark. So maybe one of us is right about chance and the other about what an agent in her situation should think or do, and the setup is just a case where those two things come apart and we've been calling both of them 'credence'. Or maybe that's too cute and the real disagreement is a formal one, about whether a probability has to be defined relative to a partition the agent has actually selected, in which case the verbal/substantive line I'm drawing is in the wrong place.
