I'm not a philosopher, I just read alot at night after my shift (I work in a warehouse) and I've been going back and forth with a freind about antinatalism for like a month now. I finally wrote up my take as a short essay and I want you to poke holes in it. Not a rewrite, just comments — mostly on whether I've actually got the view I'm attacking right, and where my main objection is weakest.

Here's the gist of what I wrote. Benatar says there's an asymmetry between pain and pleasure when it comes to being born. Pain is bad when you have it, and the absence of pain is good even when there's nobody around to enjoy that absence; but pleasure is only good if there's someone there to have it, and the absence of pleasure isn't bad unless there's somebody who's been deprived of it. So existing gets you both the good and the bad, but never existing is never bad and can be good — which is supposed to show that coming into existence is always at least a harm and never a benefit, so we shouldn't do it. My objection: this only works if "the absence of pain" is a real thing that can be good all by itself, floating out there with nobody to have it. But then why can't "the absence of pleasure" be a bad thing all by itself, floating out there the same way? He says no to the second one because there's no subject to be deprived — fine — but then the first one should get the same no for the same reason. It looks like he lets himself have an impersonal good (no pain, even with nobody) but not an impersonal bad (no pleasure, even with nobody), and that just feels bolted on to get the answer he wants. Then in my last paragraph I add that by the same trick it would be good for a happy person to never exist, which seems obviously wrong to me, so I call it a reductio.

So: is my reading of the asymmetry fair, or am I mis-stating one of the four cells? Is the "impersonal good" thing the strongest version of my objection, or is there a cleaner one I'm circling? And what's the reply he'd actually give — I know there's got to be one because people still defend this, but I can't reconstruct it for the life of me. Comments on that last paragraph would be welcome too, I'm not sure the reductio lands or if I'm just restating the first objection with a frown. Also if there's an obvious reason the four cells don't have to be symmetric, spell it out slowly, I'm definately not good with the formal stuff.
