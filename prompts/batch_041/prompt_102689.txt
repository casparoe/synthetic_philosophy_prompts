I am a high school senior enrolled in an independent study on philosophy of mind, and my instructor has asked me to assist with the first round of grading for a peer essay. I have not yet formed a settled judgment of the essay and would appreciate an independent assessment. The assignment asked students to compare two ways of answering whether an ant colony known as Aunt Hillary—a colony whose overall behavior seems intelligent and even conversational while its individual ants remain unaware of the colony's larger purposes—can genuinely have a mind. The first way treats a mind as requiring conscious representation at some level; the second treats a mind as a functional organization that can be realized by components that do not themselves represent the whole. The exact question was: Does Aunt Hillary have a mind, or is she merely a system that behaves as if she does? Compare the two ways of answering and explain which, if either, is more defensible. One of my classmates wrote the following response.

The first way says that for Aunt Hillary to have a mind, the ants themselves must be conscious or at least represent the colony's thoughts. This is the view that mentality must be present in the parts, so a colony can only be a mind if its members are tiny minds. The second way says that a mind is a pattern of information processing, not a material thing, so it can be realized by ants even if no single ant understands what the colony is doing. This second way is more defensible because it matches how brains work: neurons are not conscious of the thoughts they help produce, yet the brain as a whole is conscious. By the same logic, Aunt Hillary can have a mind even though her ants do not. The only problem is that the colony's behavior is not unified in the way a brain's behavior is, because the ants wander off and follow local pheromone trails rather than a central plan. But if the colony can still solve problems and respond to questions, then it has a mind in the functional sense. Therefore Aunt Hillary has a mind, and the first way is wrong because it assumes that consciousness must be located in every component, which is not how emergence works.

Please assign the response a grade out of 100 and provide point-by-point comments that justify it. In particular, identify which claims are accurate, which are imprecise or mistaken, and which steps in the argument do not follow. The comments should be specific enough that the student could use them for revision, rather than a general impression of the essay's quality.
