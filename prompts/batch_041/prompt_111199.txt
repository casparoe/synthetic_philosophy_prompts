Two of my friends have been argueing for a week about something one of them read in an AI paper, and they've asked me to settle it, which was a mistake on their part because I can't. Here's the setup they both accept. Two AI agents have to play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma: no repeat play, no reputation, no third party, and neither sees the other's move before it moves. The twist is that before moving, each agent hands the other its full source code, and then picks its move with the other's code in front of it.

(1) Sam says that settles it: have each agent run 'if the other agent's program is identical to mine, cooperate; otherwise defect.' Two copies of that program cooperate, and neither one could have gotten a better payoff by being some other program instead, so it's a real equilibrium — you get cooperation in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with nobody enforcing anything. (2) Rae says it's circular. They cooperate only because both were told to look for the same string, which is coordination on a label, not a solution to the dilemma. Change one line, or let two different authors write down the same idea, and you get mutual defection, so it tells us nothing about real agents. (3) Sam says the string-matching was just the toy version; the real constructions let an agent hand over a program that cooperates on the strength of being able to establish that the other agent, seeing my code, will cooperate — and that works for agents that aren't literally the same text. (4) Rae says that only works when both agents share a logic strong enough to settle questions about each other's programs, and in general you can't establish what an arbitrary program will do, you can't even establish that it stops, so against anything you can't see all the way through you're defecting again, and that's every realistic case. (5) Sam says Rae is demanding a guarantee that no solution concept ever provides — that this needs a shared substrate the same way a contract needs a court — and that the interesting claim was only ever 'cooperation becomes individually stable,' never 'cooperation is guaranteed.'
