I'm hoping this has a name, and if it doesnt I'd like one with a sharp definition.

I ran into a contest once where a fixed pot gets split evenly among everyone who enters, and you can enter as many times as you want. Nothing stops you from entering a hundred times, and no single person gets hurt in an obvious way if you do. But if everyone filled it out the way you might, the pot would be sliced down to nothing — so the move that seems right is to enter once, or maybe not at all, and the reason isn't any deal or any enforcement, just the fact that you and the other entrants are all doing the same arithmetic.

There are two things here I can't name. One is the assumption that other people, with no agreement and no causal link to me, will land where I land — so that "if everyone did this" is a real constraint on me and not a platitude. The other is the boundary. I keep seeing the same move made where it looks like a trap: same kind of shared pot, but most of the other entrants have never heard of any of this and just grab as much as they can, so my restraint buys me nothing but a smaller share. So there's one family of cases where choosing as if everyone in your position chose as you do is right, and another where it's self-harm, and I don't have words for the move or for the line between them. Someone told me it's just the prisoner's dilemma, but there's no defection payoff and nobody can punish me, so that's not it. Whats the established term, if there is one, and more importantly, what has to be true about the other players for the move to be legitimate?
