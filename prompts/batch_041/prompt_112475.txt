I am a novelist who has been asked to grade the written work in a graduate seminar on moral philosophy and narrative, and I would like a second opinion on one script before I return it. The question and the student's answer are below.

Question. A woman publicly condemns a colleague for leaking confidential material to a journalist. It is common knowledge that she herself leaked comparable material some years ago. Does her hypocrisy deprive her of the standing to blame him, or is it irrelevant to whether her criticism is apt? In answering, distinguish standing from the other conditions on warranted blame, and say what follows for how the colleague should respond.

Student's answer. "The condemner's own leak and the colleague's leak are two separate matters, and the first does not settle the second. That she once did the same thing does not make his leak any less a breach of confidence; her criticism can be perfectly accurate, so hypocrisy is not a refutation of it and no tu quoque is available. What hypocrisy does is disqualify her from the practice of holding others to the norm she has flouted. Blame is a second-personal address: it asks the wrongdoer to answer to the blamer, and it presupposes that the two of them stand on the same footing with respect to the norm. Having broken that norm, she has put herself below that footing, and so her reproach has no grip on him. It follows that he owes her no answer and has no reason to feel any reproach at all. If she wanted her standing back, she would first have to make amends for her own leak; that is why the restoration of standing is a matter of repair rather than apology. I would add that her hypocrisy also shows her moral judgment about this kind of conduct to be unreliable, which is part of why her criticism should carry no weight. None of this excuses the colleague: the leak remains wrong, and anyone who has kept faith with the norm may still take it up with him."

Please give the answer a grade and the point-by-point comments that justify it: what it gets right, what it gets wrong, and which of its errors a hurried grader would be most likely to let slide. I want a specific assessment of each substantive claim rather than a general impression of the script. Please also say what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would move the grade in either direction — and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
