I've been rereading the whole conscious-will-is-an-illusion thing -- Libet's readiness-potential stuff, the split-brain confabulation results, Wegner, all the pop sci that fed off it -- and what bugs me is that the philosophical muscle behind the conclusion is a seperate argument entirely, one that almost nobody in that literature actually runs. I mean the causal exclusion argument: causal closure of the physical, plus supervenience-without-identity, so the mental is either a redundant overdeterminer or not a cause at all, and nobody wants overdetermination, so you get exclusion. I've been in enough comment threads about this over the years to know the standard methodological gripes about Libet's setup, and I don't need them. What I want is the episode run through the argument properly.

Do the mapping first: in the Libet case, what's the candidate cause, what's the effect, what plays the role of the sufficient physical cause, and where does supervenience actually do any work -- because I suspect the pop version is smuggling in something the argument doesn't say. Then tell me whether the argument even needs the data: does it predict the results, are the results evidence for it, and is the timing claim -- conscious intention isn't the trigger of the movement -- really the same claim as the metaphysical one, the mental is excluded, or a weaker one wearing the same coat. Then run it the other way: what does this episode show about the argument? Confirmation, a case it never anticipated, or the beginnings of a reductio, since it seems to eat biology and economics too and I've never seen anyone who runs it accept that. And if you're defending the experiment, say what you have to concede if the argument goes through.

Be honest about where the argument is just silent -- causal selection, token vs type, whatever you think the real gap is -- instead of making it speak, and if one of the standard escapes (same-cause, determinable/determinate, whatever) survives this framing, say which one and why. Skip the citation parade, I don't care who said what.
