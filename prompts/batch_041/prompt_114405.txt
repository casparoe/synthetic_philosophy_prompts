Taking a value theory seminar and we've hit hedonism, and the lecturer gave us his take on the heterogeneity problem, which I wrote down pretty much word for word because it sounded so clean, and now I'm worried it's too clean. Roughly:

Hedonism says pleasure is the only thing thats good for you. The heterogeneity problem is that pleasures have nothing in common: the taste of a cold beer, the relief of finally sitting down, laughing at a bad pun, the nasty little thrill of hearing your rival messed up. No single feeling runs through all of them. So he had us run what he called a substitution test. If they really were one thing, you could lift the pleasant-feeling part out of one experience and drop it into another, like swapping a battery between two phones, and you'd get a pleasure aimed at a different object. You can't do it, so pleasure isn't a natural kind, its just a label we slap on a ragbag. And from there hedonism falls, because it needs there to be one thing, pleasure, that's uniformly good, and there isn't one. He said the only version left standing is the attitudinal one, where a pleasure is just your taking pleasure in something, and at that point hedonism has quietly turned into a theory of desire wearing a costume. He also said that once you have to write the pleasures out as a list, you've stopped having a theory and started having a list.

So two things. First, go through this and tell me what a careful hedonist would accept as a fair statement of the objection, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong — I have a feeling the substitution test is begging something but I cant put my finger on it. Second, does his conclusion survive the fixes? If the heterogeneity stuff is right, whats actually left of hedonism as a position, and is the attitudinal move a rescue or a change of subject? I'm writing on this and I don't want to build an essay on a summary that quietly snuck a non sequitur in.
