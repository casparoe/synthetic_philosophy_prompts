Write a short story, in exactly five numbered scenes and no more than two sentences per scene, that dramatizes the paradox of self-amending rules. The setting is a small deliberative assembly whose founding charter contains one master rule: any rule, including this one, may be amended by a simple majority. In scene one, a reform faction proposes to replace the master rule with a two-thirds requirement for all future amendments. In scene two, the proposal passes under the old simple-majority rule. In scene three, a later faction invokes the new two-thirds rule to try to restore the old simple-majority rule, arguing that the entrenchment was illegitimate because the old rule did not authorize its own entrenchment. In scene four, a proceduralist objects that the assembly is now trapped: if it uses the two-thirds rule to revert, it concedes that the two-thirds rule is valid; if it uses the old rule, it concedes that the old rule survived. In scene five, the assembly reaches a decision, but the story should leave the reader unsure whether the decision was an amendment, a revolution, or an application of a rule that was never validly repealed. Do not have a narrator step out to explain the paradox; let the five scenes carry it. No introduction or conclusion.
