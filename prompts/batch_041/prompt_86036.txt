I want the specification before I look at any particular account of free-floating rationales — reasons that explain why a trait or a behavior has the shape it does even though nothing in the system represents those reasons. Write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory in this area has to deliver. Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, each at most two sentences, with no preamble and no closing summary; mark each requirement in a parenthesis as non-negotiable or as the preference of one camp, and use one of the five entries to name the pair of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied together with the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidates.

Each requirement has to be forced by a case, and these are the cases I want the theory to answer to: an engineered device or craft technique that still does its job after everyone who understood it is dead; a trait that persists for reasons unrelated to the benefit its rationale names, so that the rationale is true about what would be favored and false about why the thing is here; an imputed rationale that no selection process ever tracked, where the story is a fact about the explainer's model rather than about the lineage; a mimic or a parasite whose behavior is explained by another organism's rationale, so the reason is real but sits in someone else's economy; and the same behavioral profile realized both in an evolved controller and in a machine we designed, where I want the theory to say whether the two share a rationale or merely resemble each other.

I don't want the generic checklist that any discussion of biological teleology produces — each requirement should be one whose loss would make the theory say something false about one of those cases. Skip the scorecard of existing views; I'll run that myself once the list is in hand.
