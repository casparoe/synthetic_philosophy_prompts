I am a novelist, and the book I am finishing turns on a translator who is trying to render a short, tightly rhymed Renaissance lyric into English without surrendering a single one of its formal features — the metre, the rhyme, the refrain, and the letters that spell a name down the left-hand margin. I want a poem that could stand as her finest attempt and her honest defeat at once. Write it as a sestina, in her voice: let the six end-words be the six things she refuses to lose, and let the form's inexorable recycling of them enact the predicament rather than describe it, so that the reader feels the constraint bearing down on the sense and cannot quite say whether it is the constraint that defeats her or the constraint that makes any translation possible at all. It should be exact enough that anyone who has tried to translate under constraint will recognise the bind, and lucid enough that anyone who has not will still find it a poem.
