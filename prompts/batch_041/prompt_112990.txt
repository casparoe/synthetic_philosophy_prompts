My reading group keeps getting stuck on an argument a friend of mine built, and I think it proves way too much, but every time I try to say where it goes wrong I just end up repeating the argument back at him. Here it is.

(1) A signal is honest only if it's hard to fake, and it's hard to fake only if producing it costs a low-quality signaller more than it costs a high-quality one. The cost has to be differential; if it costs everyone the same, the low-quality types just pay it and lie.
(2) So a cost that's just a cost, identical for every signaller, buys you nothing. Anyone can pay it.
(3) What makes a display credible, then, is exactly the part of it that's pure waste, spent on nothing but the display itself. If the money bought something useful, a cheater would just buy the useful thing instead.
(4) And the bigger the waste, the harder it is to fake, since the differential burden grows with the size of the display.
(5) So the most credible display is the one that wastes the most.
(6) Nothing in the logic stops there either. The signaller who destroys the most of its own fitness is the one we should believe most. The peacock should grow the longest tail it can still survive with, the gazelle should stot until it drops, and the human cases are worse: the person who gives everything away, burns their boats, wrecks themselves, is the most honest person in the room.
(7) But that's absurd. Peacocks don't have infinitely long tails, and someone who signals until they're dead can't collect on the thing the signal was for.

So one of these steps has to be wrong. I'd rather not give up (1) or (2), because without them I genuinely don't see what a signal even is. Which step do I drop, and what do I loose by dropping it? The bit that feels off to me is somewhere in the middle and I can't say why, so if it turns out the problem is elsewhere I'd like to know that too.
