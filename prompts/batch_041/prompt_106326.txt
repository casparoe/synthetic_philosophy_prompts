ok so I have to write a position paper for a grad seminar on the Holocaust and modernity, and I want to make sure my views don't fall apart under questioning before I commit them to paper. here's roughly where I've landed:

1. The Holocaust was a distinctively modern event. Not a relapse into pre-modern savagery or a sudden eruption of ancient hatreds, but something that needed modern bureaucracy, modern tech, and a state with the reach to plan and administer a whole society. The killers were mostly ordinary functioning people, not monsters.

2. It isn't unique in any way that makes it incomparable. Comparing it to Armenia, Cambodia, Rwanda isn't just allowed, it's necessary if we want to understand it at all.

3. The historians who relativized it — well, other people did awful things too, so let's put the German crimes in proportion and stop treating them as special — were doing something intellectually and politically indefensible.

4. If 1 is right then modernity isn't the cure for this kind of thing; the same planning and rationalization that built the welfare state built the camps. I like the image of the modern state as a gardener, designing a perfect society and weeding out whatever doesn't fit. So it's a warning about modern societies in general, not a story about a uniquely German pathology.

5. At the same time I don't want to give up the uniqueness rhetoric. Saying it was unique, unprecedented, is what makes "never again" mean anything politically; I don't think you can ground a real commitment to prevent genocide without it, even though 2 denies the theoretical claim.

6. And the individual testimony has to stay in view. If you turn it into a structural abstraction you lose the thing itself.

I honestly can't tell if these hang together or if I've just stacked up things that sound like they might contradict. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it? Rank the real tensions by how serious they are, tell me for each what I'd have to give up or amend, and if there's one single change that cleans up the most, say what it is.
