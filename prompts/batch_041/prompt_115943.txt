Here's where I've landed on the whole are-generations-real question:

1. I'm a realist about social kinds generally — race, money, marriage are all constructed, but constructed doesn't mean fake or arbitrary. Mind-dependent can still be real.
2. My test for whether something is a *robust* real kind is basically interventionist: the kind has to do real work in counterfactuals and generalizations, not just sit there in correlations. If you can't in principle wiggle the kind and watch the effect change, it's closer to a convenient label than a kind.
3. So I think generations (Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z) are real kinds in that robust sense. Being born into a cohort is a genuine cause of differences in politics, media habits, whatever.
4. I'm also a methodological individualist — no group mind, no sui generis social entity. Whatever's true of generations has to bottom out in facts about individual people.
5. But 'Gen Z' as the marketers use it is basically made up. The birth-year cutoffs (1997? 2012?) are arbitrary and get redrawn every time someone writes a trend piece.
6. I do think there are real generational differences in values, and that they need an actual explanation, not just "young people have always been like that."
7. My proposed mechanism is shared formative experience: a cohort is the set of people who went through their impressionable years during the same stretch of history, and that leaves a durable stamp.

So — does this hang together or am I tripping over myself? I want to know which of these genuinely pull against each other, which ones only look like they conflict, and which single revision buys me the most consistency. Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, say for each one what it would force me to give up, and don't just tell me everything's fine or that I should "just define my terms."
