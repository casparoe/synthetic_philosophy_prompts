I'm three months into my first term on the town council of a town of about 7,000 (Marston Falls, if that matters) and I've got a vote on Tuesday that I cannot get my head around — I ran on fixing the storm drains, so this is not what I signed up for. The item is a proposed bylaw banning any business whose 'primary purpose is to facilitate nudity among adults'. It's aimed at a couple from out of state who bought the old gravel pit on Route 9 and want to run a small members-only naturist retreat out of it: 40 members, fenced, no signage, no liquor, nothing visible from the road, and our town attorney says the existing indecency bylaw probably doesn't reach it. But the wording would also kill the clothing-optional end of the town beach, which has been that way since the 70's, which nobody has complained about in my lifetime, and which is where the whole town goes on the 4th of July. So there's two questions tangled up here and I can't pull them apart: whether we should be doing this at all, and whether it matters that the bylaw sweeps up the beach too.

What bothers me is that my two smartest colleagues make arguments that sound alot better than my vague feeling that 'gross isn't a crime'. The first, a deacon at the church on Route 9, says a town is held together by a shared moral code and you can't pull one thread out without the whole thing coming apart — allow this and next year it's something else, and eventually there's no 'us' left to make laws for. He says morality is more like a language than a list of rules, and that the majority is entitled to keep the town the kind of town it is. The second, a retired judge, says that's hand-waving: noone has ever shown that towns with nudist retreats have more of anything bad, and the only legitimate reason to make something illegal is that it hurts someone, and if nobody is hurt then 60% of my neighbors finding it icky is just not a reason. Then there's the third one, who honestly worries me the most: she says the law teaches, that by permitting something we're saying it's fine, and that the message itself is the harm — to the kids especially — and that the judge's test can't see that kind of harm precisely because it never shows up in a crime stat. She calls it the town's moral climate and says you can't measure it but you can feel it.

So what do I do on Tuesday, and what do I say when I'm called on? I'd like to understand the actual shape of the disagreement rather than vote my gut, and I have a strong feeling this exact fight has a long history and a name — so tell me what it's usually called, what the strongest version of each side really is, and where each one is soft. And specifically: if the deacon is right that a community is entitled to protect its shared morality, does that get us the retreat, or the beach, or both? Is there a principled line between a fenced gravel pit you'd have to go looking for and a public beach in the middle of town, or is that just squeamishness dressed up as principle? And is the judge really right that consenting adults in a private club are simply none of the law's business — that seems too clean to me but I can't say why.
