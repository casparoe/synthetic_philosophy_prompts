I am assembling a three-year research programme on the ethics and epistemology of blame, staffed by two postdocs with a modest budget for survey and vignette studies, and I need to decide what it should actually be about. Take the following as a working hypothesis rather than a thesis to be defended: that the literature has treated standing to blame as a binary property of an individual blamer — either you have it or you do not — whereas it is better modelled as a scalar, domain-relative relation that can be pooled within a community and even borrowed from an institution whose standing is not in doubt. On this picture a wronged party, a fellow participant in a practice, and a court each hold different quantities of standing over different ranges of conduct, and those quantities can be lent, aggregated, or forfeited in ways that ordinary talk of hypocrisy and meddling obscures.

Given that reframing as a starting point, I want a prioritised agenda rather than a survey. Which open questions does it make urgent, and which does it dissolve or render idle? Consider, among others: whether standing is a normative status of the blamer or merely a pragmatic precondition on the speech act of blaming; whether pooled or borrowed standing is genuinely coherent or collapses into the standing of whoever supplied it; what makes a forfeiture of standing permanent, temporary, or repairable, and what repair would consist in; whether standing and desert can come apart, so that one may deserve blame yet lack the standing to deliver it; and how individual standing interacts with institutional blame and punishment, where the blaming agent is not a person at all.

Rank these, and any you would add, by how much other work depends on them, how tractable they are for philosophers working with a little empirical support rather than for psychologists or lawyers, and how neglected they currently are. Defend the ordering: name the criteria you are using, where they trade off against one another, and what evidence or argument over the next two or three years would move a question up or down the list. Tell me which single question the programme should lead with, why it is upstream of the rest, and what a genuine result on it would look like — what would count as progress rather than mere restatement. I am interested in the reasoning, not in a reading list.
