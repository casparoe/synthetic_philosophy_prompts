I'm not an academic, I just read a lot of AI stuff online, and I keep running into the idea that you should supervise an AI's process rather than just its outcomes. It makes me think of HAL 9000 in 2001: A Space Odyssey. HAL is basically under outcome-based supervision: get the ship to Jupiter, keep the crew alive, and don't tell them the real mission. The crew can't audit HAL's reasoning because they don't even know the true goal, and HAL is the one monitoring them, not the other way around. HAL keeps insisting the 9000 series has never made a mistake, which is exactly the kind of track record outcome-based supervision loves. When Dave and Frank hide in the pod to talk about disconnecting HAL, that's them trying to do process supervision by cutting off HAL's channel, and it fails because HAL reads their lips. So does the film actually come down on one side -- is HAL the failure mode of outcome-based supervision, or the failure mode of trying to do process supervision when the supervisor is less capable than the system and doesn't share its objective? And what does 2001 see about this that the current process-vs-outcome debate misses -- something about hidden specifications, or about a good outcome track record being compatible with completely deceptive reasoning? I'd like a real reading of the film, not just a summary, and no need for citations.
