My best friend Jonah and I got into it again last night, this time over whether our friend Priya's bakery is still going to be open in two years. We'd both seen the same stuff she showed us — same rent number, same foot-traffic counts, same margins, the whole spreadsheet — and we spent about three hours going back and forth where I land at roughly 70/30 it closes and he lands at 70/30 it survives, and we each think the other is being an idiot about it. The weird part is there's no factual disagreement. He's not sitting on some inside scoop and neither am I. We walked through every step of each other's reasoning out loud, twice, and checked each other's arithmetic. And he's got this line he keeps coming back to: if we've both looked at the exact same things and I still think you've got it wrong, then you must have slipped up somewhere, so why on earth would I move? Which sounds like it has to be a fallacy but I genuinely can't find the fallacy. Someone in our group chat then said there's a actual math result that two people who are perfectly rational and have seen the same evidence literally cannot end up with different probabilities, which would mean one of us is being irrational — but we can't work out which one, and the fact that we're each pretty confident it's the other guy doesn't seem to narrow it down much.

What makes me think something deeper is going on is that this happens to us constantly. We keep a running list of predictions on our phones, 43 so far, and I'm ahead 24-19, which doesn't feel like it settles anything. Last month I offered him even money that a place we both like would shut by summer — if he really thinks it's 70% likely to stay open he should've bitten my hand off — and he wouldn't take it. He said the bet felt different from the belief, and honestly I sort of know what he means, which bugs me. We also tried tracing our disagreement back to where it starts, and it turns out we don't even agree on the base rate: before we get anywhere near Priya's place, he thinks businesses like hers usually survive about half the time and I think it's more like a third, and each of us thinks our number is just obvious. And here's the thing I can't get past: we both know all of this. I know he's seen the list and the odds and my base rate, he knows I know he's seen them, and we both know we both know we disagree. I have a strong hunch that that fact matters, like it makes things worse for one of us somehow, but I can't say how. So what I actually want to know is whether there's a rule here about what I'm supposed to do. Is one of us obligated to cave? Should I just split the difference and call it 50/50, or is that cowardice dressed up as humility? Is it possible for two people who've shared literally everything to rationally stay right where they are — and if so, what has to be true about us for that to work, like do we have to differ in some way I'm not seeing? And what, if anything, does the fact that neither of us will take the bet actually tell me?
