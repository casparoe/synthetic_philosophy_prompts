Write a satirical in-universe document of about 600 words: Form IN-4, "Application for Determination of Intrinsic Nature," from a municipal Office of Essential Properties — the form's instructions, the applicant's handwritten answers and marginal notes, and the clerk's adjudication. The office requires each applicant to state what the designated item is in itself, apart from its parts, its causes, and any convention of naming. The applicant, who has been reading Madhyamaka, answers every question with the four positions the tradition examines and sets aside — that it is so, that it is not so, that it both is and is not so, that it neither is nor is not so — and the clerk, obliged to tick exactly one box, rejects all four. The humour must come from the philosophy being right: the demand for a determinate own-nature (svabhāva) is what those four positions are designed to dissolve, not a menu to choose from; the form would be perfectly completable under the conventional/ultimate distinction, which is exactly the move the office cannot process; and the four-member appeals panel, one seat per position, should land as a fair reductio of turning a therapeutic method into a licensing procedure. Let the clerk voice the standard misreading that emptiness means nothing exists, and let the applicant's marginal notes quietly betray that a teaching aimed at ending conceptual proliferation has generated a filing system of its own. Deadpan throughout, no winking; the reader should finish unsure which of the two is in the grip of a view.
