I am a retired natural scientist who has recently begun reading philosophy, and I have encountered an ancient argument that I cannot accept. It is reconstructed as follows: (1) The word horse names a shape; the word white names a color. (2) What names a color is not what names a shape. (3) Therefore white horse is not horse. (4) Therefore a white horse is not a horse. (5) But a white horse is a horse. (6) The argument proves too much, since by the same form a red apple is not an apple and a tall man is not a man. Which premise or step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would rather keep (5) than (1), but I want to know what I am paying.
