Six months ago my partner and I had our first child, and a good deal of the time since has gone into reading about how a single cell turns into a person. My day job is on the build system of a compiler, and two years ago that toolchain finally reached the point where it builds itself, so the two subjects have become tangled together in my head. I keep meeting the same structure in both, and I have not been able to find out whether it has a name.

The structure is that the thing being copied is read in two ways at once, and the two readings have to be kept apart. One part of the system has to treat it as instructions — read it for what it means and act on that — while another part has to treat it as raw material, duplicating the marks with no regard for what they mean. Neither part can do the other's work. A copier that understood what it was copying could, it seems to me, silently correct it; an interpreter that had to duplicate the instructions before following them could not follow them without first rebuilding itself. In my compiler, the same lines of text are at once the program that does the work and the data that gets copied out. In the cell, as far as I understand it, the strand is duplicated by machinery that attends only to its shape, while its meaning is extracted elsewhere, by different machinery that never copies anything.

What I notice next is that the loop never quite closes. I could not get the copying apparatus inside the thing being copied, because then I needed a second copier for the copier; and in the cell, the machinery that turns a sequence into protein cannot be built without that same machinery already being present, which is why, I gather, the origin of the whole arrangement is treated as a serious puzzle. Something is always presupposed rather than produced, and the system looks closed only if you stop asking where the presupposed part came from.

I have heard programs like mine described as self-printing, and I know that logic's version of self-reference is a sentence that talks about itself, but neither of those is what I am pointing at — one is about output, the other about truth, and neither is about copying. So: if the two-faced role of the copied thing, the blindness of the copier, and the fact that the regress is broken only from outside together amount to a recognised phenomenon, what is it called? If there is no single name, I would like to know which of the neighbouring ideas comes closest — the self-printing program, the self-referential sentence, the biological scheme, the general business about levels — and exactly where each of them stops fitting, and whether what I have described is one phenomenon or three. A label on its own will not help me; I want the boundaries.
