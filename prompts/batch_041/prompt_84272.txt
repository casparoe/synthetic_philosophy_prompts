You are an expert philosopher of free will and moral responsibility, with a special focus on P. F. Strawson's account of the reactive attitudes: the participant stance we take toward one another when we feel resentment, gratitude, and hurt, and the objective stance we take toward someone we are merely managing or treating. I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I run a small reading group on free will. Before our first session I want to hand out a quiz that sorts people by what they actually believe about blame and responsibility, not by which label they like. Please think through the space of positions first, then give me eight questions plus a scoring key. Each question should be a concrete case or a plain first-person claim that someone can answer without already knowing the names of the views — for example, a colleague who ships a bug that takes down production, an agent whose cruelty was fully caused by how she was raised, a therapist asked to describe a client's betrayal with no resentment at all, and a workplace that keeps blaming people while conceding that nobody could ever have done otherwise. The key should be able to land people on at least these positions: Strawson's own view that our blaming practices are constitutive of our relationships and need no metaphysical vindication; hard determinism; hard incompatibilism, on which blame as basic desert — deserving something bad simply because of what you did — is never justified, though other practices survive; revisionism, which keeps the practice but rewrites its justification; answerability without basic desert; and the illusionist view that we could not, or should not, give the practices up even if they are unjustified. No citations or reading lists, please. Finish by flagging which pairs of views my questions cannot separate, and any combinations of answers that nobody occupies, with a short diagnosis of why nobody is there.
