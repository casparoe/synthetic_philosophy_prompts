I cover philosophy for a general-reader outlet and the standing-to-blame fight has been bugging me for years, and I cant put my finger on why it never resolves. Roughly: one side says a hypocrite who blames you for something he does himself has forfeited something — call it standing — so his blame doesn't land the way it would from someone clean; the other side says that's either incoherent or beside the point, since whether you did the wrong thing doesn't depend on who's pointing it out; and a third line says standing is real but only kicks in when the blame is addressed to you as a demand, not when it's just a private judgment. New papers with new counterexamples come out every couple of years and the camps don't budge an inch. My hypothesis — just a hypothesis — is that this isn't one disagreement but three wearing the same coat: people slide between standing as a permission to blame, standing as a power to hold someone accountable, and a plain moral objection to the blamer's own conduct. Diagnose it for me, using what each side actually argues, and tell me whether there's a real substantive question buried under the wordplay — say, whether blame is fundamentally an act of address or a verdict — and what would actually count as progress here. Skip the bibliography, I just want the diagnosis and the reasons for it.
