Two of my colleagues got into it after a seminar last week and I still can't decide who had the better of it, so I'd like you to referee. I'm reporting it as a bystander and I genuinely don't know which of them is closer to right.

(1) She said laws of nature aren't things sitting in the world over and above the pattern of non-modal facts — they're just the true generalizations that make it into the best systematization of everything that happens, the ones that buy the most simplicity and strength. "Governing," she said, is a metaphor we'd be better off dropping.

(2) He said that can't be right, because laws explain thier instances and tell you what would have happened in ways a mere summary doesn't. Take "all the uranium spheres there ever are are under a mile across" — even if it's true and exceptionless, it doesn't explain why any particular sphere is small, and it doesn't ground the counterfactual about a bigger one. So laws have to be something more: relations of necessitation between universals, or dispositions, some real modal glue.

(3) She said the difference he's pointing at is either pragmatic or can be earned. Once you've got the best system you can derive the counterfactuals: to evaluate one you hold the axioms fixed and vary the antecedent, and the "necessity" he's feeling is just our practice of holding the laws fixed while other facts vary. And his necessitation relations either do no work — the regularity plus the glue explains nothing the regularity didn't already explain — or they're occult.

(4) He said the best-system story has its own problem. What counts as "best" depends on our taste for simplicity and strength, so either the laws come out mind-dependent, or she's smuggling in an objective fact about which system is best that she can't cash out without the very modality she's trying to avoid. And the counterfactual move is circular: you can only "hold the laws fixed" if you already know which generalizations are the laws, and on her view what makes them laws is that they're in the best system, which is picked by us. He added that on her view a law can't explain why the mosaic is the way it is, since the law is just a summary of the mosaic, and you can't explain the summarized by the summary.

(5) She said he's conflating the epistemology with the metaphysics. The best system is a fact about which deductive system best balances those virtues against the mosaic, and that we're the ones applying the standards no more makes the result mind-dependent than "shortest path" is mind-dependent because we care about distance. As for "you can't explain the summarized by the summary" — why not? We explain particular facts by deriving them from general ones all the time, and the general facts aren't over and above the particulars. If he wants laws to be reasons the mosaic is as it is, she said, he owes us an account of how one fact necessitating another isn't just relabeling the regularity.
