I am trying to get my epistemological house in order before a graduate seminar on disagreement and higher-order evidence, and what I want from you is a consistency audit rather than a tour of the literature. Here is where I have landed.

(1) Higher-order evidence is genuine evidence. When I acquire information bearing on the reliability of the reasoning that produced some belief of mine — that my reasoning was impaired, that I have erred in this way before, that a competent judge would have reasoned differently — that information is itself evidence relevant to the proposition the reasoning was about, and it can rationally move my credence in that proposition.

(2) Conciliationism about peer disagreement. When I learn that someone I regard as an epistemic peer has reached a different view on a question, and I have no independent reason to regard either of us as the better judge on this occasion, I should move my credence substantially toward hers — in the symmetric case, roughly to the midpoint.

(3) No rational epistemic akrasia. It is never rationally permissible to hold a belief while also believing that holding it is epistemically irrational, or while believing that I epistemically ought to give it up. The rational mind is transparent to itself in this sense: the first-order and higher-order levels cannot rationally pull in opposite directions.

(4) Steadfastness in the face of misleading defeaters. When the first-order evidence for a proposition is decisive, and the higher-order evidence suggesting that I have botched the reasoning is itself misleading — a drug that makes me doubt my arithmetic without corrupting it, for instance — I may rationally retain the belief at full confidence, and I am not required to lower it merely because the misleading higher-order evidence exists.

(5) Entitlement to self-trust. I am entitled to rely on my own faculties and to treat my present considered judgments as defeasibly trustworthy, even though I know those faculties are fallible and that I frequently err.

My question is which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly. I suspect that not every apparent conflict is real, and I would rather you tell me which ones are genuine than flag each pair as a tension. Order the genuine tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what it would cost me to give it, identify any apparent conflict that dissolves on closer inspection and explain why it dissolves, and then tell me which single revision would resolve the most while conceding the least. Please write your answer in American English.
