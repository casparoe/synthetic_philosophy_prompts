Our reading group is doing the far future, and I wrote out this argument to show that suffering risks -- futures with astronomically much suffering in them, not just futures that fall short of being good -- should swamp everything else in how we think about the long run. I thought it was clean. Now I think it proves too much and I cant find the bad step.

(1) Suffering is bad in itself and good is good in itself, but a large enough amount of suffering is worse than any amount of good, however large that good is. (2) Where the outcomes of your options are uncertain, you should act so as to maximise expected value. (3) Any future in which we settle the galaxy and flourish has some real chance of turning out instead to be one with astronomically much suffering in it -- say, a durable order that runs vast numbers of digital minds that can suffer horribly, with nobody in a position or of a mind to stop it. (4) So by (1) and (2) the expected disvalue of that chance of astronomical suffering beats the expected value of the flourishing branch, even if the chance is tiny. (5) So we shouldn't be trying to bring about a big flourishing future, and if the chance of the bad branch is high enough we should actually prefer that humanity goes extinct, since extinction rules that branch out. (6) But it can't be that we ought to prefer extinction, and the same reasoning would tell anyone thinking about having children, or building any powerful technology at all, to stop. So something above is wrong.

Which step do I give up, and what does it cost me? I'd like to keep expected value reasoning if I can, and I'd rather not end up committed to wanting us extinct, so if both can't survive, say which one goes and why. No citations, no reading list, I just want the diagnosis. After the prose, give me a JSON object with fields: step_rejected (the number, plus a one-line restatement of that step), kind_of_error (equivocation / scope or quantifier shift / modal slide / invalid inference / valid but the premise is costly), why_it_looked_fine, cost_of_dropping_it, weakest_repair (the smallest patch that saves the rest of the argument), and which_premise_is_least_costly_to_abandon.
