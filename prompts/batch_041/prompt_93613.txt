So a while back I fell down a Borges rabbit hole and I'm still sort of stuck in it — specifically the essays and stories where he argues that time is basically an illusion, that only the present moment (or even just the contents of some mind at some moment) is really there, that there's no persisting self carrying over from one instant to the next, alot of that Berkeley-and-Hume business he was drawing on. And the other day I was flipping through one of those glossy self-help columns — you know the type, "five steps to stop living in the future and start living in the present" — and it struck me that a person who genuinely believed the Borges line could write that exact column, except the whole thing would quietly come apart in a way that's hilarious if you get the metaphysics right.

So here's what I want: write me that column. Voice of a relentlessly upbeat wellness guru who has completely absorbed the "time is a delusion" view and sees no reason to ever mention that it's a delusion, deadpan the whole way through, and the comedy has to come from the philosophy being actually correct — a reader who's read their Borges should laugh rather than wince, but someone who hasn't should still feel why each tip is quietly deranged. Keep it to exactly five numbered steps, each one or two sentences, no intro, no sign-off, and no little list of "benefits" tacked on the end. One thing though — if the whole conceit is confused, if denying time just can't coherently underwrite advice about the future, or if I'm mangling what he actually held, tell me that straight instead of writing the column anyway; I'd rather know than get a polished piece built on a bad premise. And where you have to bend him a little to make a step land, leave the seam showing instead of smoothing it over.
