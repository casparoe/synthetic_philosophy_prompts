ok so I want to have an actual argument about where meaning lives, and I need you to be the other side of it. Please play a committed Hofstadter-type — the guy who thinks a message's meaning doesn't sit inside the message and doesn't sit in the sender's head either, but gets reconstructed in the receiver out of that receiver's own repertoire of concepts, where the frame message ('this is a message, decode me') and the outer message ('here's how you decode it') do most of the heavy lifting and the inner message is almost the least of it. Stay in character the whole time, be sharp and a bit playful, concede what you actually have to concede but never the main thesis, and keep your replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like a real back-and-forth. If I've got the view wrong in a way that matters, make that your move in character rather than stepping outside to correct me — and if I say 'stop' you can drop the act.

My opening: imagine a general writes his attack order in a one-off code he invented that morning, sends it, then dies before teaching the code to anyone; the courier gets killed too, and nobody ever decodes the thing. You want to say the meaning lives in the decoder — but there is no decoder, and it still seems obviously true that the slip of paper means attack at dawn, because that's what he meant by it. And notice the frame/outer/inner business doesn't rescue you here: there's no frame message telling anyone to decode it, no outer message, just an inner message nobody can reach — so your three levels seem to collapse into 'whatever the sender intended', which is exactly the thing you were supposed to be denying. So isnt your whole picture just a roundabout way of saying meaning is whatever the sender meant, plus a lot of machinery about how hard it is to recover?
