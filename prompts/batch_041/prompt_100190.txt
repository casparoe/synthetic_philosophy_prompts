Two camps in the philosophy of social science have argued past each other for decades. The methodological individualist holds that social phenomena — institutions, norms, aggregate patterns — are properly explained by the actions, beliefs, and dispositions of individual persons, and that an explanation which stops at the social level is a placeholder awaiting individual-level microfoundations. The holist holds that social structures and macro-properties possess explanatory autonomy: some social facts explain other social facts without any detour through individual psychology, and the social level may exert a kind of downward causation on the individuals who occupy it. Suppose the two camps have agreed to a good-faith adversarial collaboration and want the joint statement such a collaboration would produce.

Draft that statement. It should carry out the following tasks, in order.

1. Separate the dispute into its distinct strands and keep them apart: the ontological question of what exists, the explanatory question of what may figure in a satisfactory explanation, and the semantic question of whether social predicates are definable in individual terms. Flag any place where a disagreement in one strand has been mistaken for a disagreement in another.

2. Distinguish thin individualism (only individual persons act) from thick individualism (every social kind is definable, without residue, in individual terms), and say which of the two the debate is usually really about.

3. List the claims both sides can sign — for instance, that social predicates are multiply realizable across different individual-level mechanisms, or that social facts supervene on individual facts — and be candid about which of these are too thin to settle anything.

4. Give the residual disagreements in neutral wording, each formulation phrased so that the side it describes would accept the way its own position is put.

5. Identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side, and say which side each would move.

6. For each crux, supply a concrete case or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Include at least the following two test cases, and reason about them rather than reciting prior verdicts on them: (a) a norm that has outlived the conditions that plausibly gave rise to it, still transmitted and enforced although no living person endorses it for its original reason; and (b) an aggregate pattern of the Schelling kind, in which mild and individually innocuous preferences generate a stark macro-outcome that no one intended.

7. State whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, and if so which part and why.

Write the statement as polished prose in American English, then append a structured summary as a single JSON object with these fields: agreed_claims, an array of objects each with claim and significance; disagreements, an array of objects each with strand (one of ontological, explanatory, semantic), individualist_wording, and holist_wording; cruxes, an array of objects each with crux, test_case, individualist_prediction, and holist_prediction; verbal_disputes, an array of objects each with issue and why_verbal; and unresolved, an array of strings naming what the collaboration leaves open.
