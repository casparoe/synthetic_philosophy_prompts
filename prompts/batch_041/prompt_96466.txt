Okay so I'm running a reading group on Dennett's Quining Qualia next month and I want a handout that does more than just list realism vs eliminativism for the hundredth time. Can you build me a decision tree or taxonomy of the positions on qualia that a student could actually use to figure out where they land? The gimmick: every branch node has to be a concrete case, not a label. Start them at the coffee-taster case (Chase says his coffee tastes exactly the same as always but he's stopped liking it; Sanborn says the coffee tastes totally different now but he likes it just as much as ever) and ask what they think is going on — did Chase's qualia change, did Sanborn's, both, neither, is there even a fact of the matter? From there branch through at least: the inverted spectrum, the wine-tasting machine (a device that can sort wines but presumably has no inner life), the superspartan (a creature that behaves just like us but allegedly has no qualia), and the gradual neuron-replacement case. Each leaf should be a named position (or unnamed if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I especially want the tree to seperate the views that Dennett is trying to collapse — the ones that treat qualia as intrinsic, private, ineffable, incorrigible — from views that keep some notion of phenomenal character but drop one or more of those four features. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really separate, and note any branch combinations that look unoccupied and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep the whole thing under 900 words, and make the questions answerable by someone who hasn't read the paper yet — no jargon in the branch nodes.
