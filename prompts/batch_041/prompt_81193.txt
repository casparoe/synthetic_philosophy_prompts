I am trying to find the line between legitimate persuasion and manipulation, and I have a set of verdicts I trust more than any theory I have tried. I want the principle, or the smallest set of principles, that captures them, together with an honest account of which verdicts it cannot save.

Here is what I think, case by case. A nurse who tells a frightened patient that most people come through this fine, knowing that the patient's particular fear is in fact the likeliest complication, manipulates her. A private seller who calls a street usually quiet, knowing jets pass overhead on weekends, does not manipulate the buyer. A friend who says she thought I was someone who finishes what I start, to keep me at the gym, is not manipulating me; my boss who says he thought I was a team player, to get my weekend, is. A salesperson who opens at an absurd price so the real one feels like a favor manipulates me; a doctor who describes the worst course of an illness before giving me the milder actual diagnosis does not. A novelist who makes me want to see a city I had never considered is not manipulating me; a marketer who manufactures a want I did not have and then satisfies it is. My partner, working on my guilt until I eat better, manipulates me even though the end is one I share. A model tuned to agree with me and keep me talking manipulates me, though nothing in it intends anything at all. My paying a service to hide my own distracting apps from me is not manipulation. A teacher who tells her class that electrons orbit like planets, intending to correct it next lesson, is not manipulating them; a science writer who uses the same false picture and never corrects it is.

The case I keep circling is the negotiator who never argues, but restates the other side's own stated reasons back to them, reordered and slightly sharpened, so that the concession feels self-reached. Is that manipulation or just good listening?

My one candidate principle: manipulation is influence that works by giving the target a distorted picture of her situation, or by operating through a channel she would disavow if she noticed it. Test it against the cases, repair it if it can be repaired, and tell me which verdicts it forces me to abandon. Then say what the repaired principle predicts about a case I have not listed, and whether it treats the negotiator as continuous with the nurse or with the novelist.
