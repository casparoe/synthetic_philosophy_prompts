So I run this little philosophy thing in the back room of a pub, every other Thursday. The regulars are a retired civil engineer, two theology undergrads, a nurse, and my brother who mostly comes for the beer and to announce that all of this is made up. Next month I want to do the modal argument for God, the one where you get from it being possible that God exists to God actually existing, the version with the boxes and the S5 machinery. I'm not asking you to explain it to me, I roughly get it. I want you to plan how I explain it to them, forty minutes, whiteboard, no handouts.

What I need:

The order the pieces have to go in and why each one can't come earlier. My own guess is: first what the word possible is even doing here (not just I can picture it), then the idea of a being that couldn't have failed to exist, then the move from possibly-necessary to necessary, then the payoff. Tell me if that's wrong, and where.

The one example that has to carry the whole thing. Here's my idea, tell me if it's bad: I want to run it with a lump of matter that couldn't possibly not exist, instead of with God, because the second I say God half the room starts arguing about church and the other half stops listening. Does swapping God out for something bland keep the engine running, or does it kill the thing it's supposed to show? If it kills it, what should carry it instead, and what does that example need to have and need to not have?

The misconceptions they'll walk in with or pick up along the way, and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off. I'm expecting: possible means imaginable; possible worlds are parallel universes; this is just defining God into existence; if it works it proves any old thing; and the one that worries me most, that by the end absolutely everything turns out to be necessary and nothing could have gone otherwise. Also my brother will say the whole thing is a word game and I don't want to just wave that away.

Where I put the parody. I've made up my own, becuase the classic ones feel too cute: the best possible hangover, a hangover than which no worse can be conceived. Is that a fair parody or does it cheat? And where in the forty minutes does it go, early to innoculate them, or late so it doesn't derail everything?

The one question I ask at the end that tells me whether they actually got the arguement rather than just enjoyed the fight.

And what I should openly tell them I'm skipping, and why, so they don't think I'm hiding the hard part.

Don't give me a reading list, I'm not assigning anything and half of them wouldn't read it anyway. And please don't write the explanation out for me, I want the plan.
