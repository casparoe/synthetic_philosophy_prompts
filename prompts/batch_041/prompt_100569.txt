I'm in a phil of science course and my prof keeps giving us his verdicts on whether theories count as confirmed, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the rule he's running. So far: a theory that predicted a new particle and then the particle was found — confirmed. A theory that unifies two areas of physics but makes no new predictions and has no free parameters — not confirmed, though he says its still scientific. A theory that predicts a new effect but only after you tune a parameter to fit the data — not confirmed. A theory that explains why the constants have the values they do by positing a multiverse, and says we should observe a certain value if we're typical observers, which we do — not confirmed. Next week he wants us to predict his verdict on a theory that was invented after a known anomaly, retrodicts that anomaly with no free parameters, is the only theory that explains it, and makes no new prediction. Will he say confirmed or not? Which rule is he actually running — novel predictive success, inference to the best explanation, or a Bayesian likelihood view? What other rules fit his earlier verdicts just as well, and what case would tell them apart?
