I think I got the shape of it: dogma one is the analytic/synthetic split, dogma two is the reductionist idea that every meaningful statement can be cashed out in terms of immediate experience, and what he puts in their place is holism — the whole body of belief faces experience together, at the edges, and anything, even a law of logic, can be given up if the rest of the system gets adjusted enough to compensate.

But I honestly cant tell whether I actually understand it or whether I'm just nodding along because it sounds like things I already believed for other reasons. So do this for me.

Restate Quine's position — the attack on analyticity and the holistic picture that replaces it — in the vocabulary I actually think in. Assume I'm completely fluent in all that, don't explain my own field back to me, and I'd rather you be ugly and precise than smooth and vague. I want a real translation, not a metaphor.

Two specific things I keep getting stuck on, use them as test cases. First: the metre used to be a metal bar in a vault outside Paris, then it was a wavelength of krypton light, and now it's fixed by the speed of light.
