I'm a science writer working on a long piece about the awkward fact that when people say AI should be aligned to human values, nobody can quite say whose. I've been asked to run a session at a ideas festival next month — mixed room, about 18 people, 75 minutes, half of them technical, half not — and I want it to be something they do rather than something I talk about.

What I want them to actually feel: that adding up different people's values has no neutral answer, and that every attempt to stay neutral — walling certain commitments off from the adding-up — is itself a value choice, made by some procedure that had to be picked first.

The setup I have in mind: a fictional city is switching on a municipal AI that has to decide one thing, like how a shared weekly pool gets divvied up (time on a public supercomputer, noise allowance in a shared courtyard, whatever's easy to stage). Each person gets a stakeholder card with a bundle of wants, and here's the twist I want to try — every want comes with a budget of chips they spend to show how much they care, and a few of their wants are marked as commitments (tied to their religion, their kid, their identity) which are meant to be untradeable, off the market. Round one: plain majority on one question, everyone feels it's fair. Then I add a second question that cuts the room a different way and I want the majority to start cycling — no stable winner — so they watch the same fair rule give different answers depending on the order things come up. Then I nudge someone to propose protecting the commitments from the vote, and the room has to decide which commitments count as real ones and who decides that, and it goes turtles all the way down.

Can you write the whole thing for me — the cards, the chip and trading rules, the three rounds, the exact prompts for the moments where I want them to turn on each other, and a debrief that maps what just happened onto the real menu (summing welfare, majority rule, giving priority to the worst off, walling off whole spheres) without me having said any of those names up front. Also tell me what predictably goes sideways the first time I run it, and be straight with me about which part of the actual problem the game can't reproduce and that I'll have to just say out loud at the end.
