I'm in a metaphysics seminar working on the statue and the clay, and I've become convinced that the standard debate is framed by a false dichotomy: either the statue and the clay are numerically identical (and statue is just a phase sortal of the same object) or they are two numerically distinct material objects that coincide. I want to float a third option and get your honest credence in it. The option: constitution is a relation of explanatory dependence between two ways of counting the same portion of matter, and the question how many objects are on the pedestal has no sortal-free answer; there is one object under the count lump and one object under the count statue, but these are not two objects in any sense that should make an ontological inventory. Call this the one-world, two-counts view.

My question: what is your credence that this view can do all the work a theory of material constitution needs to do — preserve the truth of the claim that the statue is not the clay, explain why the statue depends on the clay but not vice versa, make sense of counting and identity statements, and avoid the problem of the many — without collapsing into either eliminativism about statues or a two-object coincidentalism? Treat that as asking whether there is a coherent, non-ad hoc package of semantics and metaphysics on which the view satisfies all four desiderata. Please give me a number, and decompose it into at least the following sub-questions, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each: (a) the probability that ordinary sortal-relative counting is the right semantics for counting sentences; (b) the probability that explanatory dependence can be non-objectual, i.e., can relate counts rather than entities; (c) the probability that the view can preserve the modal difference between the statue and the clay, since the clay can survive being squashed while the statue cannot; (d) the probability that the view avoids the problem of the many; and (e) the probability that the view is not just a notational variant of one of the standard positions. Then combine them in whatever way you think is appropriate, state the final credence, and tell me which term is doing the most work and what argument or case would move it most. Please keep the whole response under 650 words, and lead with the number.
