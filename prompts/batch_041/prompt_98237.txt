I read the rationalist blogs and forums fairly obsessively, and I've been circling a specific worry about coherent extrapolated volition that I'd like to turn into a proposal for a funded six-month project. The standard framing presents CEV as a single limit: take a person's present preferences and idealize them along two axes at once — giving them more true information about the world, and making their preferences more internally coherent — then ask what they would converge on. My half-formed suspicion is that those two idealizations do not commute. Idealize for information first and then impose coherence, and you may converge somewhere different from idealizing for coherence first and then adding information; if so, the word coherent in the name is doing path-dependent work that the slogan conceals, and the appeal to an idealized endpoint loses some of its claim to be the person's own will rather than an artifact of the order in which we tidy them up. Write me the proposal: state the question so that it has a determinate answer, say why the usual defenses of CEV leave this order-of-idealization issue open rather than settled, describe the method — what exactly I would do over six months, and whether the disagreement is formal, conceptual, or both — explain what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing, and give an honest assessment of scope for the time available.
