Ok so I've been chewing on this puzzle — the one where every rule you've ever actually followed is compatible with a whole swarm of other rules that agree on everything you've done so far but disagree the moment a new case shows up. And then the nasty bit: is there even a fact of the matter about which rule you were "really" following, or does the whole idea of meaning something determinate just fall apart?

I want a short story that dramatizes that, but please not the worn-out arithmetic version (you know, the "+" vs the weird operation that matches on every sum you've ever done and then goes sideways). Put it somewhere the rule is practical and the stakes are real. Here's a seed, feel free to bend it: a woman runs a tiny mushroom-foraging club and teaches her nephew a rule for sorting edibles from poisonous ones by their features. Over two seasons he's seen maybe a hundred specimens and never once disagreed with her call. Then a new mushroom turns up that fits both her rule and a slightly different rule the nephew's quietly been running, and the two rules give opposite verdicts on it. She says eat it, he says don't. And neither of them can point to a single thing she ever said or did that rules his rule out — nor he to anything that rules hers in.

I want the story to make me feel the vertigo of that, but I also want it to stage at least two tempting ways out, without tipping its hand about which (if any) is right. Like: meaning gets fixed by what the community of foragers would do; or by her training and dispositions, whatever those would have produced; or maybe there's simply no fact here and the two of them are just going to have to settle it by fiat. Have a scene where somebody tries to "check" the past to see which rule was meant and finds the past useless, and another where a third club member insists the community settles it.

Third person, somewhere around 1000-1500 words, and let the mushroom itself actually matter — don't just make it a lecture wearing characters as costumes. End on a concrete image, not a summary of the debate.
