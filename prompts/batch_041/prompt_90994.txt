I just finished my EMT cert and I've been doing ride-alongs, and a couple weeks back we had a call with two patients and only one ambulance free, so I had to pick who went first. I've been chewing on it alot since. My roommate is a philosophy PhD student and her dissertaion is on whether the numbers count when you're deciding who to save — the whole Taurek thing — so this has turned into our dinner argument basically every night.

Here's where she's landed on the cases I keep throwing at her:

- One stranger vs. five strangers, all of them certain to die without help, and I can save either group with the same effort. She says she'd save the five. But she always adds that this is not because five is more than one — she says the lone stranger's claim on me is exactly as strong as any single one of the five's, and she could never look him in the eye and tell him his life counted for a fifth of theirs. When I asked what she'd do if it were one vs. one, she said flip a coin. When I asked why she doesn't flip in the one-vs-five, she said "honestly I don't know, maybe I'm a coward."
- Same setup, except the one is her sister. She saves her sister, instantly, and says that's not even the same question.
- One person who'll die vs. a hundred people who'll each have a bad headache for an afternoon. She saves the one, obviously — she says that's how I know she isn't just counting heads.
- One 25-year-old who'll die vs. five 90-year-olds with terminal diagnoses and a few weeks left. She still saves the five, and says age and life expectancy don't enter into it at all. She got genuinely annoyed when I pushed on that one.
- One person who'll die vs. one person who'll die, otherwise identical: coin flip, every time.

So here's the case I actually want help with, because I think it's the first one where her stated rule and her gut point in opposite directions. Chemical spill, six people exposed. Without treatment, one of them (call her A) will be in severe pain for four weeks, and the other five will each be in the same severe pain for one week. I have one dose, and I can either treat A or treat the five.

What's she going to say? And I care way more about the second half: what rule is she actually running? Because I can't tell if she's really an "each person's claim is separate, so compare A's claim to each of theirs and hers is the bigger one" person, or a "just help as many people as you can" person who keeps dressing it up in Taurek language. Are there other rules that fit everything she's said so far just as well? Which one would you bet on, and what should I ask her next that would actually settle it?

One thing though — don't hand me a confident "here's what philosophers think" if that isn't true. Tell me which parts of this are settled among people who actually work on it and which are live disagreements, and don't sell me a contested view as the consensus.
