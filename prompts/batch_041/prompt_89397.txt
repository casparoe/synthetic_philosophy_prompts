I'm wrapping up a unit on the epistemology of testimony and I want to end with a poem the students can pull apart. Write a short pantoum -- three or four quatrains, with the usual repeating lines -- from the point of view of a reductionist about testimony. The reductionist keeps asking for non-testimonial evidence that anyone is reliable, and the anti-reductionist keeps saying that trust is the precondition rather than the conclusion. Use the kid-learning-the-word-red case: the kid is told that red is the color of that cup, but to know the parent is reliable the kid would need evidence, and the only evidence around is more of the parent's say-so. I want the form to do alot of the work: the repeated lines should make the regress feel like either a vicious circle or a benign spiral depending on which voice you hear them in, and the last time the opening line comes back it should mean something different. Let the anti-reductionist's line be one of the refrains, so the poem can't decide whether repetition is proof or just repetition. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the debate recognizes both positions without me having to name-drop. And no citations, obviously -- it's a poem, not a reading list.
