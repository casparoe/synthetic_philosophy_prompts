Next Thursday I'm giving a WIP seminar and I want a Q&A prep sheet. The thesis I'm defending: the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism — the Rawls/Nozick line about society not being a single person writ large — is really two claims bundled together, (a) that persons are distinct loci of value, and (b) that persons are non-substitutable, i.e. that no amount of one person's good can stand in for another's. Utilitarianism, I argue, only ever needed to deny (b), not (a); it's perfectly happy to say persons are distinct loci of value, and the objection's entire force rests on (b), which is a seperate and much weaker claim than it looks. If I'm right, the standard horror cases — the transplant, sacrifice one to save five — don't actually follow from the criterion itself, they follow from a decision procedure I'm happy to replace. Give me the five objections I'll actually get, ordered by how much damage they do, each phrased the way this lot would phrase it — in their vocabulary, not mine — and for each one a couple of sentences: my reply, and where the reply is thinnest.
