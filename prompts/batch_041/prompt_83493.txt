I sit on the research-ethics board of a spaceflight company — we review the human side of a mission before it flies. Next Thursday we vote on the first settlement proposal: twelve adults, no return leg, and a charter the founding twelve can lock in for good — who decides, who does which work, and a rule that some of them have to keep having children so the population stays above the genetic floor — with any later change needing every surviving founder's yes. My friend and I went at this for two hours last night and got nowhere. She says the founders consented to the deal and any kids are just born into a polity, same as the rest of us; I say a society you can never leave, whose constitution is nailed down by people who won't have to live under most of it, is a different moral animal. I have to circulate something before the meeting. Draft me the written opinion: one clear bottom line — approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a time frame — the reasoning for it, and a fair-minded pass at the strongest version of the side I don't take. Argue it; don't just point me at books.
