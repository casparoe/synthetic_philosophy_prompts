I write novels, and I keep running into a kind of line I don't have a word for. I'd like to know if there's a name for it, because it bothers me that I can't talk about it precisely.

Some sentences don't just say something, they do something — saying them is the act. Sure, everyone knows that. But the ones I keep writing are the ones where the doing happens with nobody on the other end. A woman stands at her father's grave and tells him she forgives him; he's been dead for years, he can't hear her, there's nobody else there, and yet I'd say she has actually forgiven him, not merely said the words — it's the same act she could have done while he was alive, except now it can't be recieved by anyone. Or a man alone in his kitchen at two in the morning says out loud, to the empty room, that he's quitting tomorrow — and that feels like the moment he quits, or at least commits, even though nobody heard and nothing was signed. Or the letter someone writes and never sends, telling a person exactly what they think of them. The person never reads it, so it did nothing to them, and yet the writer did something.

So what I'm after: is there a name for the class of utterances that are fully carried out but have no addressee who can take them up — no listener at all, or a listener who can't recieve them? I have a feeling the usual story about these sentences is that they need to be heard or accepted to work, and my examples look like counterexamples to that, unless I'm misreading the usual story. And I want to know where the edges are. Does the forgiveness at the grave belong with the unsent letter, or are those two different things? Is this just talking to yourself? Does it matter whether the speaker believes anyone could ever hear it — my man in the kitchen may be performing for a God he doesn't believe in, and that seems to change something.

Don't just slap a label on it. Tell me what the nearest existing notions are, how each one falls short, and whether I'm describing one phenomenon or three. If there's no name, coin one and define it so I can tell what's in and what's out.
