Okay so I write novels for a living and I keep noticing that my characters end up in fights about who's allowed to be angry at who, and I've started keeping a list of my own gut reactions to real-life versions of these scenes, and the list doesnt hang together. Help me out.

Case 1: My uncle cheated on his wife for years. Now he's constantly going after his brother for cheating. My gut: he has no standing, he should shut up, and it makes me furious.

Case 2: A friend of mine is a recovering alcoholic, five years sober with a couple slips. She told another friend she's worried about his drinking. My gut: totally fine, more than fine, she of all people gets to say it.

Case 3: A tenant is behind on rent. The landlord is also months behind on his own mortgage and has been for years. Landlord lectures the tenant. My gut: he should still get his rent! But as a moral lecture it's worthless, I don't feel the tenant owes him an apology, just money.

Case 2 and case 3 look alike to me and I feel them differently. Why does the alcoholic get standing and the landlord not?

Case 4: Someone who lies constantly for fun calls out a politician for lying. My gut: no standing, and yet I think the politician's lie is still bad, and if the liar were the only witness I'd want him to speak up anyway. So the no-standing thing isnt about whether the claim is true.

Case 5: A woman who was assaulted confronts her attacker. Obviously standing. (She's also cruel to her own sister, but that seems irrelevant to the standing question, which is itself weird.)

Case 6: My tentative rule was 'you can only blame someone for a rule you're keeping yourself.' Then I thought about my ex-smoker friend who quit and is now insufferable about other people smoking -- I want to say she has standing even though she's a bore, and the rule agrees, fine. But then case 1: my uncle has technically stopped cheating (got caught, stopped). By my rule he'd have standing now, and I still say he doesn't. So the rule is wrong.

What principle actually gets cases 1-5? And what does it say about the one I keep flip flopping on: a guy who is genuinely trying to be better, in therapy, but keeps relapsing into the same cruelty toward his partner, and he tells his friend she's being cruel to her partner. Does he get to say it? Also tell me which of my verdicts has to go, because I suspect at least one does.
