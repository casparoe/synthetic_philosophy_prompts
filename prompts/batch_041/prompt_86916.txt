I'm putting together a handout for a grad reading group on "consciousness as a user illusion" — we've read a pile of stuff that all seems to agree with itself until someone asks a follow-up question, at which point it turns out everyone in the room means something totally different by "illusion." So I want a decision tree / flowchart that takes a reader from a fairly neutral starting point to a definite position, so people can see where they actually land rather than where they think they land. Could you act as a philosopher of mind who works on the metaphysics of consciousness and write that tree for me? Please think it through carefully first — brainstorm the distinctions that actually matter before you commit to a structure, and don't pad it out with positions nobody holds.

Here's what I want in it. The entry point should be a commitment almost everyone in the room already shares, something like: introspection doesn't tell me what experience is made of, only what it seems to be made of — we're granting that the way consciousness presents itself isn't automatically evidence about its underlying nature. From there, every branch node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing which label it leads to — a concrete case, or a claim they can agree or disagree with — never "are you an illusionist?" Two things I specifically want the tree to force apart, because our group keeps collapsing them: first, the claim that consciousness is an illusion, the claim that the self is an illusion, and the claim that conscious will is an illusion, since alot of people who assert the first one only really mean the second or third; second, the claim that phenomenal properties don't exist at all, versus the claim that they exist but are nothing like what introspection reports, versus the claim that the concept of a phenomenal property is just confused and there's nothing there to be right or wrong about. That three-way split needs to show up as separate leaves, not get lumped into one "illusionism" box.

Leaves should be named positions wherever there's a standard name (illusionism, eliminativism, higher-order theories, global workspace, self-model theory, panpsychism, whatever else earns its place), and each leaf should carry one sentence on its most awkward commitment — the thing its defenders have to bite. Also: note any combinations of answers that nobody occupies, and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; and flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate with the questions you've got, instead of pretending the questions do the work. I don't need citations or a reading list — just the positions themselves. Aim for something like 8-12 branch nodes and 8-10 leaves, and keep the questions in plain language even where the labels are jargon.
