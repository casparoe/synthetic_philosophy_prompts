ok here's a Parfit's-hitchhiker variant I can't settle, and I want your actual credence plus the decomposition, not a survey of positions. Setup: you're stranded in the desert, one driver comes by, he'll only take you if you sincerely promise to pay him $100 once you reach town, and you both know there's no enforcement and you'll never meet again. Normal Parfit verdict: promise and pay. My twist: the driver is a decent amatuer psychologist and can tell whether your promise is sincere, so you can't just mouth the words -- to get the ride you have to actually intend, right then, to pay. And of course once you're in town you won't want to. So: credence that, given you really did intend it back in the desert, it's rational to pay when you arrive. Decompose it -- how much rides on whether a promise your desert-self made binds your town-self at all, how much on whether the question is about the act of paying or about the act of getting yourself to form that intention in the first place, how much on whether there's any reason to pay once the ride is already got, and anything else you think belongs. Number and one line for each term, how they combine, then which term you're least sure of and what would move it.
