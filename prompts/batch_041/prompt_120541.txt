ok, background first because this wont make sense otherwise. im in a reading group thats spent like three months on whether it can ever be rational (or ethical) to care less about a persons welfare just because it happens later. two of us have basically stopped speaking. lets call them N and T.

N: pure time preference is indefensible, full stop. once youve held fixed the size of the benefit, whos getting it, and how confident you are itll happen, temporal distance should get zero weight -- same as spatial distance gets zero weight. the fact that the good lands in 2200 rather than 2026 isnt a feature of the good.

T: thats too quick. a valuer is located somewhere in time, and the stretch of the world they can still act on isnt just an arbitrary subset of it. he'll admit uncertainty discounting and diminishing marginal utility are doing most of the work in practice, but he thinks something is left over after you strip those out, and its not merely bias.

the two things we came up with that neither of them can answer, and i think these are the actual cruxes:

1. anticipation asymmetry. some goods are partly consumed before they arrive -- you enjoy the trip for months beforehand, you suffer a surgery in advance. so prefering a nearer benefit can be a preference about the benefit, not about its date. N says fine, then value it where its felt, whenever that is. T says no, it shows goods arent date-neutral in the first place.

2. the revision case. compare a policy that pays off in 30 years with an identical one that pays off in 300, where we know the 300-year beneficiaries will hold values we'd reject and might not endorse the transfer. N: irrelevant, welfare is welfare. T: thats exactly where the settledness of the future starts mattering, and its not the same thing as uncertainty.

and 3, the one that makes it personal: if T is right that a valuers temporal position has normative force, N says hes licensed to care more about his own kids than about strangers kids, which she thinks is a reductio. T says thats a different kind of partiality and the analogy is cheap.
