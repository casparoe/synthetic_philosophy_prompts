I am a PhD student in philosophy working on self-locating belief, and I am drafting a chapter on the halfer–thirder disagreement. I want a constructed case rather than another run-through of the standard example, and I want it built inside a framework I state myself, so that nothing in the construction turns on anyone's published setup.

Framework. An experiment is fixed by three things: a randomizer with finitely many outcomes b, each with probability p(b) > 0; for each outcome, a finite number n(b) ≥ 1 of waking episodes for the subject, the whole protocol being disclosed to her in advance; and a reset that erases memory of earlier episodes, so that her total evidence at every episode is exactly the same, namely the protocol plus the fact that she is awake. She cannot tell the day, the episode number, or anything else about her situation. Two credence rules are on the table. Rule A: her credence that the outcome was b is p(b), and conditional on b she takes herself to be uniformly distributed over that outcome's n(b) episodes. Rule B: she takes herself to be uniformly distributed over the multiset of all episodes that occur, each episode weighted by the probability of the outcome that produces it. In the standard two-outcome example these rules deliver the halfer's and the thirder's answers respectively. I am not asking you to settle which rule is right.

What I need. A protocol such that (1) the two rules agree on her credence that the outcome was one designated outcome X; (2) they disagree on her credence that the outcome was some other outcome Y, and also on a self-locating proposition about her own position, such as that this is her first episode; (3) the probabilities and counts are simple fractions and small integers, and the randomizer is an ordinary physical object; it should not be a coin, and I expect you to show why a two-outcome device cannot meet the specification at all; (4) the disagreement must not be manufactured by giving her different evidence at different episodes.

Deliverables. (a) The protocol, in one paragraph, in enough detail that the subject could be walked through it. (b) A table of the two rules' credences in every outcome and in the self-locating proposition, with the arithmetic visible. (c) The general condition on the counts and probabilities under which the two rules agree about an outcome, with a one-line proof, plus an account of what makes X special and whether the agreement there is a coincidence or a structural feature of your construction. (d) What becomes of the case when that condition is removed, and when it is strengthened to equal counts throughout. (e) A bet the two rules price differently, with both prices. (f) The strongest objection a defender of Rule A would press against the case, the strongest a defender of Rule B would press, and your replies. (g) Confirmation that the standard two-outcome example fails your construction and that your case is not a relabelled version of it. (h) In a sentence or two, whether the case makes the underlying disagreement look substantive or like an artefact of the reference class each rule fixes. I do not need references; the construction and the verification are the whole point.
