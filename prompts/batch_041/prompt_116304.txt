I'm in an animal ethics seminar and we just finished a unit on aquaculture, and there's something I can't stop chewing on. The reading basically said the fish welfare movement is following the chicken playbook — that a farmed salmon or tilapia is, in the respects that matter for welfare, like a broiler chicken, so the reforms people already won in poultry should transfer more or less wholesale: lower stocking densities, some kind of enrichment, no routine mutilation, proper stunning before slaughter, breeding for health instead of just for growth. My study group took this as obvious. One guy said if you care about a chicken you have to care about a fish, same structure, end of discussion. But another friend pushed back hard and said the analogy is doing all the arguing, and that a fish is'nt just a chicken in water.

Can you audit it properly? I'd like the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the chicken, the stocking density, the beak trimming, the enriched barn, the gas stunning, the slow-growing breed — and then where the mapping actually breaks. My friend's list was: fish are ectotherms, so everything scales with water temperature; the medium they live in is also what they breathe and what poisons them (ammonia, dissolved oxygen); the numbers are insane, one farm can hold millions of individuals and most of the lifecycle is eggs and larvae that die anyway; and the "individual animal with a life" unit that the whole chicken framework assumes maybe doesn't survive contact with a species that sheds a million eggs. Also, alot of what fish welfare people actually talk about is water quality, which isn't a welfare issue for a chicken at all. So which of those are real disanalogies and which are just differences that don't touch the argument? And does the chicken-playbook conclusion — import the poultry reform package — survive once all that's on the table, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time?

One more thing, does any of this stretch to crabs and lobsters, or do they need an entirely different analogy? I've heard "a crab is like an insect" floated in the same seminar, which also sounds like it's doing a suspicious amount of work. I don't need citations or a reading list, just the mapping and your honest read on what each analogy can and can't carry.
