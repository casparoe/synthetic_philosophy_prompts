Not an academic, just someone who argues with family a lot, and there's a thing I keep running into that I don't have a word for.

Cleanest case first. My brother-in-law Mike is a film snob who hates everything. I tell my sister "even Mike liked it." She says "so you're saying you expected him to hate it?" and I say "no, I never said that" — but I sort of did, and I'd feel like a liar insisting I didn't. Try to take it back and it goes wrong in a funny way: "even Mike liked it, and I'd have expected him to hate it" just sounds like I've said the same thing twice, and "even Mike liked it, though I had no particular expectation either way" sounds like I'm contradicting myself. There's a "but" version too: "the flat's cheap but it's a long way from the station." Swap "but" for "and" and the little jab disappears even though both halves are still sitting there. "It's cheap and it's a long way from the station" and nobody thinks I'm warning them about anything.

Now here's what's bugging me. This isn't the ordinary business of hinting. If someone asks whether there's more cake and I answer that I've got a dentist appointment in the morning, I've hinted there's no cake — but I can take that back perfectly well ("...though that's not why I mentioned it, help yourself"), and I could have got the same hint across a dozen other ways, like "save room for dinner." And plainly that hint isn't part of what my sentence means.

With "even" and "but" I can't do either of those things. So is there a name for this? And I don't just want the label. Is the extra bit part of what the sentence means, or is it something I'm doing, or is that split not even the right one here — because if I only ever *said* the one thing, how come my sister is entitled to be annoyed at me for the other thing? Whose is the jab, mine or the word's? What is this not — is it like "bachelor" meaning unmarried, or like "the king of France" assuming there's a king, or neither? What happens if the whole thing goes inside a question or an if — does the jab survive? And where do the edges fall: are "still", "yet", "so", "therefore", "managed to" all the same animal, or does this split into two or three different things that I've been lumping together?

Also please tell me what would change your mind. If you decide it is a real distinct thing, what would have to turn out true for you to say it isn't, and where's your reasoning most likely to be weak? I'd rather have the caveats than a tidy answer, honestly.
