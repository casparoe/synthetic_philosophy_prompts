I am setting the final examination for an upper-level seminar on agency and mechanism, and I want a single essay question that tests whether students can think with the notion of sphexishness rather than rehearse the stock example and its usual moral. To keep the question from collapsing into recall, I have built it around a distinction of my own and a pair of cases, and I want the students to adjudicate among these rather than reproduce a familiar reading. Here is the material I intend to hand them alongside the question.

The distinction: a charge of sphexishness can be pressed in three registers, and they need not coincide. In the counterfactual register, a behavior is sphexish when it is insensitive to goal-relevant changes in the situation — disturb the pattern mid-routine and the agent simply restarts the routine from the top, as the insect in the stock example is said to do. In the provenance register, a behavior is sphexish when, whatever it looks like from outside, it is generated by a fixed and non-revisable mechanism, so that even apparent flexibility is only rigidity at a higher order — rigid through and through. In the ownership register, a behavior is sphexish when its insensitivity is not the agent's own: something that happens to the agent rather than something the agent does or endorses.

The cases, chosen to pull the registers apart: Case A, the settled virtuoso — a performer whose execution of a difficult passage is completely stereotyped, identical under every variation of the surrounding conditions, and impossible for her to vary on request; the rigidity is the product of long deliberate practice and is, by her own lights, exactly what she wants to do. Case B, the restless improviser — an agent whose surface behavior is endlessly variable and exquisitely sensitive to circumstance, but whose variability turns out to be produced by a fixed lookup table over inputs, with no capacity to revise the table in light of anything.

What I want from the question is that students be forced to do four things at once: take a stand on which register, if any, is the one that bears on freedom and responsibility, rather than sliding between them; show how the two cases separate the registers; confront the suspicion that the charge of sphexishness is unfalsifiable, since any finite behavioral repertoire can presumably be redescribed as the output of some fixed program; and say what would have to be true of an agent for the charge to be both substantive and avoidable. The question must be unanswerable by narrating the stock example and concluding that insects are unfree while we are not.

Please draft the essay question itself, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves a top answer must make and the specific mistakes that should cost marks. Because the material is partly my own invention, the model answer should reason from the distinction and the cases as given rather than leaning on attributions or citations it cannot support — no reading lists, and no gestures toward what some named author is supposed to have argued. Finally, I want the model answer to separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this topic, and in particular not to present a contested verdict about the freedom of any of these agents as though it were the consensus; where the answer depends on my three-register distinction, I would rather it flag the places where that distinction is doing contestable work than smooth them over.
