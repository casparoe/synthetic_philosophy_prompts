Ok, so I cover science and ideas for a magazine and I've been circling this one for weeks and honestly I keep going in circles. Here's the position I've basically landed on, and I want to write it up as the decisive version of the objection: consequentialism is dead as a practical guide to action because of cluelessness. Not the boring "we can't predict the far future" version everyone trots out, but the sharper one. The sharper one is that for anything that's even mildly chaotic, our knowledge of initial conditions is always finite, so tiny errors blow up, so any long-run expected-value calculation is garbage-in-garbage-out — and once you see that, the claim that there's a fact of the matter about which option is better in the long run just evaporates. It's not that we don't know the answer, it's that there isn't a well-defined answer to know. I think thats much stronger than the usual complaint and I'm about to say so in print.
