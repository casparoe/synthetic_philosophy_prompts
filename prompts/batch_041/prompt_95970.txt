A friend of mine has gotten really into the whole "reducing suffering is the only thing that actually matters" thing (he calls himself a negative utilitarian) and he always explains it with accounting. Pleasure and pain are credits and debits on the same ledger, he says, the point is to keep the balance from going into the red, and a huge pile of credits doesnt cancel out one big debit — a broken leg isn't fixed by a good meal, you can't pay off someone's misery with someone else's ice cream. When I tell him that's not how ledgers work he switches to a differnt one: suffering is like a fire, the whole aim is to put fires out, and nobody would start one just so there'd be more to put out.

I'm a software engineer, so analogies are how I think, and both of these feel like they're smuggling the conclusion in. Can you audit each one: what's supposed to map onto what, where it actually breaks down, what it quietly imports, and whether either of them really gets him to "the bad side is the side that counts"? I don't need the usual "you can't measure happiness" stuff, I want the analogies themselves taken apart. And if neither one can carry it, what picture would?
