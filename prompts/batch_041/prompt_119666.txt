I did a project for my ethics class on Bentham and I've ended up basically agreeing with him about sex. My version of it: the law has no business in what consenting grown ups do in private, the idea that certain acts are against nature is just made up, and most of the outrage about it is people enjoying being disgusted by other people. The only thing that really counts is how much pleasure and pain comes out of a thing.

I don't want to just sit here agreeing with myself, so what would count against this? Real defeaters, not ones I can wave away. Three things I keep getting stuck on. First, if being disgusted counts as pain, then the pain of everyone who's grossed out is on the ledger too, and the math might come out the other way — so am I just cheating by not counting it? And if I need a rule for why it doesn't count, does that rule cost me the whole view? Second, I keep saying consent matters but I'm not sure it's doing any real work for me; is it just a shortcut for nobody got hurt? Because I can think of cases where nobody gets hurt and it still seems bad (someone lies to get someone into bed by pretending to be in love with them? I dunno). Third, if it turned out that some of the old rules actually do hold together stuff that makes alot of people happy overall, would the view have to go along with the rules? That feels like it would sink it but maybe I'm being dramatic.
