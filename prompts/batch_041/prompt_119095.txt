Analyze the Mytilenean debate (427 BCE) as a test case for theories of collective intentionality and joint action. The assembly, in anger, voted to put the adult men of Mytilene to death and enslave the rest; the following day it reversed itself, and the second ship overtook the first in time to stop the killing. Do the mapping explicitly. On a reductionist account of shared intention, on an account that treats a collective intention as a joint commitment binding the parties to one another, and on an account that treats the we-intention as primitive and irreducible, what exactly did Athens intend at the first vote, and who or what is the intending subject? Then press the episode against each theory: is the reversal evidence that no genuine collective intention was ever formed — that there was only a decision, or a mere aggregation of individual votes — or that the assembly that voted the second time was not the same agent as the one that voted the first, or that attributing a single intention to the group was a fiction all along? Say which features of the episode each theory explains, where it falls silent, and what the episode shows about it in turn — a confirmation, an anomaly, or a case it never anticipated. Be explicit about which of these claims are settled among people who work on collective intentionality and which are genuinely contested, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
