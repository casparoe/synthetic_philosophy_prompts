My two flatmates got into a really long arguement at breakfast yesterday and I've been chewing on it ever since, because I honestly couldn't tell who was winning and I don't want to just pick whichever side I happen to like. It started when one of them, Rosa, said she'd been reading Hume's essay on taste for a course and was surprised to find that Hume, who you'd think is the ultimate everything-is-in-the-eye-of-the-beholder guy, actually goes looking for a standard of taste -- some rule that could settle which of two people has the better judgment. Theo said that's just Hume being inconsistent with himself, because Hume also says beauty isn't a property of the object at all, it's something that happens in the mind of the person looking, and that when two people feel differently about the same thing neither of them has made a mistake. So by Theo's lights there can't be a standard; Hume just wants one. Rosa said no, those fit together fine: Hume's point is that taste is a sentiment, but not every sentiment is equally trustworthy, because some people have a finer, better-trained palate, and the standard is the verdict of the people who've got that -- she listed the traits, the delicacy, the practice, the comparison, the freedom from prejudice, and she brought up the Don Quixote bit Hume retells, where two men taste the wine, say it tastes of leather and of iron, get laughed at, and then a key with a leather thong gets fished out of the cask. Theo said that's circular: you can't use the good critics to define good taste if the only way to recognize a good critic is that their taste is good. Rosa said the traits aren't circular, because you can check for them independently of any particular verdict -- you can test a palate, you can see whether somebody's actually put in the practice. Theo said fine, but the traits are still loaded: Hume's own sense of good taste is just what a certain educated European man in the 1700s happened to like, and every era has its anointed best judges who turn out to be flat wrong about whole genres. Rosa said that's an attack on Hume's examples, not his framework, and anyway calling a whole era wrong is exactly the kind of thing you can only do if you've already got some standard in mind. Theo said maybe, but then the standard is doing no independent work, it just launders whatever the current consensus is. Then they both looked at me, and I had nothing.

And if there's something both of them are missing about what a "standard" is even supposed to mean here -- because I think that's the part I'm most confused about, whether it's supposed to be a fact about the artwork or just a rule for comparing judges -- please spell that out too.
