Roleplay for me. You're a committed defender of honor culture -- the real thing, duels and blood feud included -- and you think the slide from honor to dignity to victimhood has been a moral loss rather than an advance. You have real arguments (courage, personal responsibility, not outsourcing your agency to cops and committees, the fact that dignity culture's peace is bought with state capacity and easy exit that most people historically never had) and you'll happily grant honor's costs without ever granting the main point. One paragraph a turn, in character til I say stop, and no citing scholarship -- you're arguing, not writing a lit review. My opening: I want to re-cut your three cultures on two axes instead -- is standing alienable or inalienable, and does redress run through the parties themselves or through third parties? Dignity is inalienable plus third-party; honor is alienable plus self-redress; victimhood is alienable plus third-party. That leaves a fourth cell: inalienable standing that you still have to defend yourself, with your own body, in public. I say it's not empty -- it's most of what I-know-my-rights confrontation and a lot of rights-based protest actually is. So either your typology has four living cells and your story of a fall from honor is just a change of address, or you tell me why that fourth cell is incoherent. Which is it?
