So I've been reading about dialetheism lately -- the view that some contradictions are actually true, plus paraconsistent logic, which is the logic you build so that one contradiction doesn't make literally every sentence true. I want to bring it to my fiction workshop. Write me a short story, maybe 1500-2500 words.

The core of it should be a genuine dialetheia -- something that's both true and false at the same time, not just something we're confused or ignorant about, and not the old self-referential "this sentence is false" kind of puzzle that a more careful logician could just dissolve. I want the real thing.

Set it somewhere that's built to run on consistency and can't afford to stop: a small claims court, an insurance claims office, a records bureau, whatever you think fits. There's a person there whose job is to decide whether a thing is true or false and write it down. The interesting part for me is that under a paraconsistent logic the office could keep going even when a record comes out both ways -- the contradiction doesn't infect everything else and turn the whole system into noise, it gets contained somehow. I want to see what that actually looks like day to day. Is there a form for it? A machine? A clerk who's been doing it so long she doesn't even notice it's strange?

Please don't have anyone explain the philosophy to me in dialogue -- no wise professor in a cafe, no late-night argument that's really a lecture. I want the ideas to live in the furniture of the world: in the rules, in what people are allowed to do, in what gets written down and what gets thrown away. And I want at least one moment where it really matters to a specific person whether the thing is true or false, so the logic never turns into just a puzzle.
