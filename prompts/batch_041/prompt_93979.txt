I am a skeptic. Here is my opening move. Imagine a fertility researcher who creates two zygotes from two different sperm-egg pairs, A and B. Before either divides, she swaps their nuclear genomes, so the cell lineage that began as A now carries B's genome, and the lineage that began as B now carries A's. The child who develops from A's lineage—call her Ana—has B's genome and is raised by the parents who commissioned B. She has A's origin but B's genetic make-up and social history. On origin essentialism, Ana is not the child who would have existed from B's sperm and egg, even though she is genetically and socially indistinguishable from that child. But if a mere swap of genomes can separate origin from every other feature we care about, why should origin be the thing that fixes identity? And if you say the swap makes no difference because Ana is still A-origin, what work is origin doing that isn't just a label? Answer as the origin essentialist.
