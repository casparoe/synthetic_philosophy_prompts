What interests me is that its rules are not decoration. Greater quantities are shown by repeating one same-sized silhouette rather than by enlarging a single figure, since an enlarged figure leaves it undecided whether you are comparing height or area; perspective and shading are dropped for the same reason; and each repeated silhouette carries a fixed value only within a given chart, so the identical sign stands for ten thousand people on one page and a thousand on the next. The chart still needs a key and a caption, then, and Neurath appears to have treated the whole thing as a helping language that accompanies words rather than replacing them — even while it was pitched as readable across languages and levels of schooling, on the wager that a simplified picture you remember beats an accurate figure you forget. I genuinely cannot tell whether that is an escape from the ambiguities of verbal language or just a notation with a hidden legend.
