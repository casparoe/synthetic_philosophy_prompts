Ok so I cover science and ideas for a living, which means every election season some editor asks me for the same piece — is direct democracy good for us or is it a disaster — and every version I write could've been written thirty years ago. This year I want to pull a fast one: instead of arguing about ballot measures, I want to write the official voter guide entry for one that doesn't exist yet, and let the format do the arguing for me.

Here's the measure I made up. It's a state constitutional amendment requiring every voter to pass a short civics-and-reading test, given right there at the polling place, before any vote on a proposition gets counted. The test would be written by a panel appointed by the attorney general's office. And yes, the amendment itself would go on the ballot and be voted on by everyone who hasn't passed the test yet.

That last bit is the joke, but I dont want it to read like one.

So write it straight. All the usual furniture: the yes/no question at the top, the attorney general's title and summary, the analyst's fiscal-impact paragraph, the arguments for and against (signed by invented committees), and the rebuttal to each. Maybe 700-800 words. No narrator popping in to say this is absurd, no stage directions, no winks.

What I'm actually trying to skewer is the pretense that the guide is deliberation — that you hand somebody a title, a cost estimate and two short arguments and now they're informed. So the humor has to come out of getting the machinary right: who drafts the title and who gets to fight over it, the single-subject rule, the arguments written by the campaigns themselves, the guide getting mailed to every household and going straight into the recycling, the fiscal note having to price out something nobody can price, and the part where the measure's own supporters would mostly flunk their own test. Someone who's worked a ballot campaign should wince and laugh. Someone who hasn't should just find it weirdly plausible.

Dont end on a tidy moral about whether the vote should be filtered through competence. Let the reader get there on their own.
