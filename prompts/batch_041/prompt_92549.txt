I sit on the ethics review board of a mid-sized AI company, and we keep banging our heads against the same awkward question: when we actually ship a system, whose values should it serve? At this point our own past decisions are the nearest thing we have to a policy, so I'd like you to help me work out what we'll probably do next -- and whether we could even defend it.

Here's the track record. (1) We approved a language-model tutor for a private religious school, set up to teach that school's own moral outlook to its own pupils, with parents opting in. (2) We rejected a plan to gently nudge users of our general-purpose assistant towards the personal political views of our two founders, even though the founders felt strongly. (3) We approved an assistant for a large firm, configured to enforce that firm's code of conduct on its own staff during working hours, since the staff had signed up to the code. (4) We approved a 'neutral' setting that, on contested moral and political questions, lays out the main positions without endorsing any of them. (5) We rejected a recommender that would have raised engagement by playing on users' insecurities, because nobody had asked for that and most users wouldn't have wanted it.

Now here's the one on my desk. It's a consumer gadget for families: the parents buy it, set it up at home, and everyone in the house uses it, including teenage kids. The parents want it, by default, to reflect their own religious and moral values when the kids ask it things -- about sex, about faith, about what makes a good life -- and to gently steer the kids that way. The kids don't share all of those values. What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own earlier calls? And where the precedents really don't settle it, what principle would we be adopting by going each way?

What I'm after is: the two or three different rules our record could be read as following, what each one predicts for the family gadget, your best guess and your reasons, and the single further case that would tell those rules apart. Please write it in British English, and dont bother with citations -- I just want the argument.
