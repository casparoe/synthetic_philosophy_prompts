I am drafting the theory chapter of a dissertation on the wrong kind of reasons, and before I commit to a framework I want the adequacy conditions for the problem set out. Work from the framing below rather than from any canonical presentation of the problem, and describe positions rather than naming or citing authors — I do not want a bibliography, and I would rather not be handed attributions I cannot check.

The framing. Take the family of views on which value is analyzed in terms of fitting attitudes: something is good just in case it is a fitting object of a pro-attitude, and a consideration is a reason for an attitude just in case it bears on that attitude's fittingness. The difficulty is that some considerations appear to give reasons for an attitude while having nothing to do with its fittingness. The familiar case: a powerful and malicious being will punish you severely unless you admire him, and the threat cannot be satisfied by deciding to admire. The threat gives you reason to want to admire, or to bring the admiration about, but it does not make admiration fitting; yet it is not simply false that you have a reason to admire in that situation. The question is what such reasons are, and whether their existence shows that the fitting-attitude analysis conflates two different notions of a reason for an attitude.

Two features of my framing, since they will shape what you produce. First, I want the account to cover attitudes beyond desire and belief — admiration, amusement, gratitude, hope, intention, blame — and I want the same machinery to handle reasons to believe, where the pressure is usually thought to be strongest. Second, I am introducing a third category alongside the object-given/state-given contrast I take to be standard: reasons that arise from the agent's position or commitments, which I will call positional reasons. A promise to feel grateful, a parent's standing to feel pride in a child, a soldier's obligation to feel shame for a unit's conduct, a mourner's role at a funeral — these look like reasons for an attitude grounded neither in the attitude's object nor in the attitude's effects. I want to know whether that category is real, whether it is exhaustive alongside the other two, and whether it generates a version of the problem the two-way taxonomy cannot even state.

What I need from you: the desiderata for any theory that claims to solve or dissolve this problem. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Include at least these: the classification requirement (the theory must sort a given reason for a given attitude as object-given, state-given, or positional, and must do so without circularity); the transmission requirement (an account of why reasons of the wrong kind do not render attitudes fitting, without merely stipulating the distinction); the bootstrapping requirement (the theory must not let an agent make any attitude fitting by manipulating the circumstances that generate state-given reasons); the neutrality requirement (the theory must not presuppose that the problem is genuine, so that a deflationist who holds that talk of reasons for attitudes is ambiguous between two predicates also has to satisfy the list); and the constraint that a fitting-attitude analysis not make the fittingness of an attitude depend on facts about the attitude's existence or consequences. Add whatever else you think belongs.

Then mark any subset of the requirements you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement eliminates the most otherwise plausible theories. If the positional category collapses into one of the other two, say so — that would be a result about my framing rather than a failure to answer, and if the list I have started is itself biased toward the fitting-attitude camp, identify which requirements carry that bias. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and the cases behind them; if you want to note briefly, at the end, where a fitting-attitude analysis is most vulnerable on your list and where a view that denies value should be analyzed in terms of fitting attitudes at all is most vulnerable, do it in a few sentences.
