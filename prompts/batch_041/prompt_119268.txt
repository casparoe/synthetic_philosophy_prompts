My friend in my ethics reading group says moral uncertainty is nothing special: its just like not knowing whether it will rain. You dont know the weather, so you take the umbrella with the best expected payoff; you dont know whether utilitarianism or Kant is right, so you do whatever comes out best once you weight each theory by how likely you think it is. As far as shes concerned thats the whole story, and anyone going on about hedging or voting between theories is just overcomplicating it. Last week she used it on us: she says shes 50% utilitarian, 30% Kantian, 20% virtue ethicist, and on those numbers the good done comes out ahead of keeping a promise, so the three of us should blow off an afternoon with a friend who just got out of hospital and go do something more useful instead. How far does the analogy actually hold? Give me the correspondence table first -- what plays the role of the states of nature, the probabilities, the payoffs, the umbrella -- then where the mapping breaks, and what it quietly assumes that the rain case doesnt. Does her conclusion survive, or was the analogy carrying it the whole way? And if it doesnt hold, what would be a better analogy?
