ok so I've spent the last two weeks going down the satisficing / bounded rationality rabbit hole and I think I've argued myself into a stupid corner. The story I keep running into is: Herbert Simon showed that maximizing expected utility is computationally intractable, you can't actually compute the optimum for a real decision, so agents are forced to satisfice, i.e. stop at the first option that clears some aspiration level. Tidy. But then:

(a) If everyone is forced into it, how is satisficing ever supposed to be wrong about anything? Whatever I end up choosing, you can just say my aspiration level was whatever it happened to be. That's not a theory of choice, thats a relabeling of people doing stuff.

(b) A friend claims the threshold doesn't matter anyway, because payoff functions are flat near the top: land anywhere in the neighborhood of the best option and you're capturing essentially all the value, so optimize-vs-satisfice is a fight over a horizontal stretch of the curve. That smells wrong to me and I can't say why.

(c) Another friend says the whole thing dissolves, just optimize over how much thinking to do — keep deliberating until another minute of deliberation costs more than its worth. Then satisficing is just optimization with a compute budget.

So which of these is confused, and is there a real question left at the bottom of it? I'd like to actually know how you'd set an aspiration level in a concrete case, not just be told it's adaptive and sent on my way.
