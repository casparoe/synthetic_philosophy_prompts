I have been reading popular cosmology and philosophy of science for a few years, mostly as an amateur, and I keep running into a shape of argument that I have no name for. I brought it up in a reading group recently and nobody could tell me whether it has one, so I am asking you, and I would be just as happy with a well-made new term as with an old one. Here is the shape, with the instances that made me notice it. There is a picture of the universe on which it expands forever and cools, so that over an enormous span of time the occasional random fluctuation assembles something briefly minded, a few seconds of confused experience before it disperses. The unsettling part is not the fluctuation minds themselves; it is what follows for me. If minds of that kind vastly outnumber minds that arose the ordinary way, through evolution and upbringing, then, conditional on the picture being right, my present experience is overwhelmingly likely to be one of the fleeting ones, which means the memories I have of reading books, the people I think I remember, and the whole evidential story I would use to argue for the picture are probably confabulated. But I cannot simply conclude that the picture must be wrong, because the picture has just told me that my evidence against it is likely to be junk, and any argument I make from that evidence is being made by a system that, on the hypothesis under discussion, is probably not tracking anything. I notice the same shape elsewhere: in the thought that we might be simulated beings, where most minds like ours are simulated and so my apparent surroundings are suspect; and in the evolutionary debunking of our moral or mathematical faculties, where, if those faculties were shaped for usefulness rather than accuracy, most of the beliefs they produce are probably off the mark, including the belief that they are off the mark. The common structure, as I see it, is a hypothesis that predicts that most of the beings who could entertain it are in no position to entertain anything, so that its acceptance by anyone is a fluke, and the fluke does not refute it, because the refutation would be produced by exactly the kind of being the hypothesis says is unreliable.

What I am after is the carving, not the label. Is there an established name for this general shape, as distinct from the specific cosmological version, which I know is discussed under its own heading? I am aware that there is a whole literature about reasoning from one's own place among observers, and I have read the popular versions of it, but what I cannot find is a name for this particular pattern of a hypothesis sabotaging the evidence for itself. How does it relate to, and how does it differ from, the old skeptical scenarios about demons and vats; from a claim that is simply self-refuting, like a relativism that cannot be stated without exception; and from reasoning about being a random member of some larger class of observers? Is the feature a defect that ought to count against a theory, or can a theory live with it, and if so under what conditions? In particular, does it matter how much probability the hypothesis assigns to my being a deluded observer, so that a view on which such observers are only a small minority is untouched while one on which they are the vast majority is not? I want a definition with visible edges. What should we say about a case where the hypothesis says most observers are deceived but the deception does not touch the evidence for the hypothesis itself; or where the unreliable beings outnumber us but none of them ever forms a belief about the question; or where the hypothesis is itself about how beliefs get formed; or where the beings in question are not deceived but merely rare? And if there is no established term, coin one, and tell me what it should and should not cover, so that I can tell whether a new case belongs in the family or only looks like it does.

Please answer in continuous prose, the way you would write an email to someone who asked you this, with no headings, no bullet points, no numbered lists, no bold or italic, and no tables. I do not need a reading list, citations, or suggestions about where to look; I want your own account of the phenomenon, the nearest concepts it might be confused with, and where its boundaries fall.
