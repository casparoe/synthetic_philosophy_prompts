I am setting the final examination for a master's-level module in game theory and need a single long essay question on iterated elimination of dominated strategies when mixed strategies are allowed to serve as the eliminating strategies. It should be answerable in about fifty minutes, carry twenty-five marks, and be solvable from first principles alone: assume the students have no texts or references to hand.

The crux I want the question to force is twofold. First, a pure strategy can be strictly dominated by a mixed strategy even when no pure strategy dominates it; students must see this and connect it to the notion of a strategy that is never a best response. Second, and more demanding, the set of strategies surviving iterated elimination need not be the same when mixtures may eliminate as when only pure strategies may: students must exhibit a concrete game in which the two procedures reach different fixed points, identify which surviving set is the defensible one, and reconcile this with the familiar claim that iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies is independent of the order of elimination. The question should close by asking them to contrast all this with weak dominance, where elimination is order-dependent and can discard Nash equilibria.

Please give me (a) the examination question; (b) a model answer of roughly 850 words, with explicit payoff matrices for every construction it uses; and (c) a marking scheme that spreads the twenty-five marks over the specific moves a top answer must make and names the particular errors that should cost marks — for instance, assuming that a strategy must be dominated by a pure strategy to be eliminated, or supposing that order-independence carries over to weak dominance. Write in British English, and end the whole response with a final line beginning "Verdict:" that states in one sentence the correct resolution of the crux.
