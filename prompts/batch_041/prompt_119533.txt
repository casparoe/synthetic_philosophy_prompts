I'm in a third-year phil sci seminar and we just wrapped the Lakatos unit, which I honestly loved, and now our seminar leader has set us a curveball for next week. All term she's been handing down verdicts on the historical cases in class, and they're pretty consistent: Prout's hypothesis about atomic weights being whole-number multiples of hydrogen — progressive at the start, since it staked a definite claim about numbers nobody had measured yet and alot of them came out whole, then degenerating once the fractional weights piled up and the defenders just kept invoking impure samples, then progressive again once isotopes showed the rule held for each one separately; the Uranus orbit anomaly, patched by postulating an unseen planet in the belt that then got found, progressive; the Mercury perihelion anomaly, same move, planet never found, and eventually a rival programme accounted for it, degenerating; the 1905 aether case, where one programme saved the null result by bolting a contraction hypothesis onto its belt, degenerating, and the rival programme that got the same result out of a couple of postulates and then predicted effects nobody had gone looking for, progressive; and the psychoanalysis-style case, where every apparent counterexample gets absorbed by a bespoke story about that particular case and nothing checkable ever gets predicted, degenerating. (My friend in the seminar reckons the whole thing is just about whether some rival programme is doing better than yours, which might be part of it, though it doesn't look like the whole story.)

The case she's set us is a made-up one — I'm fairly sure her and the TA stitched it together — about a programme in developmental neuroscience, call it Sorensen's programme. Core: one particular class of glial cells is the only source of a certain signal, and that signal is what drives cortical maturation. Protective belt: a dozen or so auxiliary hypotheses about timing, receptor subtypes, and assay reliability. From 1998 to 2008 it made three novel predictions that were then confirmed, so everyone agreed it looked progressive. Then in 2008 a lab found the signal in animals with those cells ablated, and the programme's answer was to add a "sequestration plus alternative source" clause to the belt; no new predictions came out of that, and none have since. What has happened instead is that the belt has grown into a tight interconnected scheme that now makes sense of a pile of already-published findings nobody had managed to connect — odd recovery effects after injury, a strange result in the receptor knockouts — and the scheme has spun off an assay that other labs now use, plus a load of new questions (does the signal do a second job in adults, and so on), though none of its distinctive claims has been tested prospectively. Meanwhile a rival programme, which says the signal comes from the vasculature, made a novel prediction two years ago that held up, and it's picking up people fast.

So what's she going to say about Sorensen's, and what rule is she actually running? I want the rule, not just the verdict, plus whatever other rule fits her record just as well, what each of them predicts here, and which further case would settle it. Exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion: (1) your predicted verdict and the rule you think she's running; (2) the other rule(s) that fit her record equally well, and whether there's a rule you'd have expected her to be running but that her record rules out; (3) what each surviving rule predicts about Sorensen's; (4) the one further case that would tell them apart, and what each would say about it; (5) which way you'd bet, and what principle we'd be buying into if we go that way.
