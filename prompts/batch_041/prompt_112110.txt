I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends extending the idea of free-floating rationales — the thought that a trait or process can embody a rationale that no one ever entertains — from its original home in evolutionary biology to domains such as bacterial chemotaxis and the apparent reasoning of large language models. His own contribution is a distinction between thin free-floating rationales, which he treats as mere functional descriptions, and thick ones, which he claims are genuinely normative because they are sustained by a homeostatic feedback loop organized around a set-point. Draft eight to ten interview questions that do more than let him restate this distinction. I want at least one question on what makes a thick rationale normative when there is no subject to whom the norm is addressed; one on why the homeostatic-loop criterion does not simply relabel biological function and thereby collapse the position into teleosemantics; and one on whether extending free-floating rationales to language models commits him to realism rather than the Dennett-style instrumentalism about the intentional stance that his critics will invoke. For each of those three, add a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give, and close with a single question about what would count as evidence against the existence of any thick rationale.
