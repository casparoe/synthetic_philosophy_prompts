I'm a third-year philosophy student writing a short position paper for my philosophy of science seminar, and I'd like a consistency audit of my view before I commit to it in writing. Here is roughly where I've landed.

(1) I'm a scientific realist: our best current theories are at least approximately true, and their central theoretical terms genuinely refer to unobservable entities and properties.

(2) I take the historical record seriously. Many theories that were empirically successful in their day turned out to be false, and their central terms turned out not to refer to anything. This should substantially lower our confidence in the theoretical furniture of our own theories.

(3) What tends to survive theory change is the mathematical or structural content. So the realist commitment should be aimed at structure: we can know the structure of the world, but not the intrinsic nature of whatever it is that has that structure.

(4) I don't think structure is merely a feature of our representations; it's a feature of the world. I'm drawn to the further view that relations are more fundamental than the objects that stand in them, so that talk about objects is really talk about nodes in a structure.

(5) The best argument for realism is that it would be a miracle for our theories to be as predictively successful as they are if they weren't latching onto something real about the world.

(6) I think approximate truth is best spelled out as a kind of structural similarity between theory and world, rather than as the theory's entities having the right properties.

I'm aware that some of these are in tension, but I can't tell which, and I'd rather not abandon anything without knowing what it costs me. Please go through the set and tell me: which pairs are genuinely inconsistent rather than merely in tension; which pairs look inconsistent but come apart on inspection, and why; and, for the genuine ones, how severe each is and what the cheapest repair would be, including what I would lose by making it. Then tell me which single change would resolve the most at once, and whether the position that results is still recognizably scientific realism or has quietly become a form of instrumentalism. Rank the problems by severity rather than treating everything as equally bad, and be specific about which commitment has to give rather than offering general remarks about holism.
