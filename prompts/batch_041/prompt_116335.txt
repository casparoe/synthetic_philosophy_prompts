Give me the desiderata -- the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the background claims it can't contradict -- with the case or consideration behind each one, and mark which are non-negotiable vs which are just one camps preference. Cases I keep tripping on: a parent who sacrifices for their child (that's required, not heroic); a stranger who donates a kidney; the tiny favour that's optional but too small to be heroic; someone who does far more than duty out of vanity; and someone who pulls off the heroic act only by neglecting their own family. I also want to know whether supererogation is a property of acts or of agents -- does the same act stop being supererogatory if it's done from grim habit rather than love? And does a theory of supererogation have to leave room for the mirror image, acts that are permitted but lousy? Flag any subset of the desiderata that can't all be satisfied at once -- my guess is that "you ought to do the morally best thing available" and "supererogation exists" can't both stand, but tell me if I've picked the wrong culprit, and which single requirement does the most excluding work.
