Second-year PhD in philosophy, mostly language and formal epistemology, and I keep running into the same one-liner: you can't put a probability on an indicative conditional. Last week in a reading group someone deployed it twice in ten minutes — first to argue that any betting-style story about conditionals is a non-starter, then to say that a paper I actually like, which assigns credences to conditionals, is just confused about what conditionals are. My gut says the line is doing sevral different jobs at once and that at least one of them is being done badly, but every time I try to say what's wrong I end up gesturing at Lewis's triviality proofs and trailing off.

What I want from you is a menu rather than a verdict: the distinct precise theses that sentence could be expressing, ordered roughly from the boring/terminological up to the ambitious, with a verdict on each — trivially true, true but weaker than it sounds, genuinely contentious, or false. Is it (a) that indicative conditionals have no truth conditions at all, so there's simply nothing for a probability to attach to; (b) that they do have truth conditions, but the probability of one just isn't the conditional probability of the consequent given the antecedent, so the tidy equation everyone reaches for fails; (c) that you can have credences in them, but those credences aren't probabilities in the standard sense — they don't obey the axioms, or they come out undefined or 0/1 exactly in the interesting cases; (d) something purely about assertion or rational degrees of belief, i.e. the old test of hypothetically adding the antecedent to your beliefs and asking whether you'd then accept the consequent; (e) something about the semantics of the language rather than about what a particular agent happens to believe? And then tell me which of those readings would actually license the two uses from the reading group, and which one I should have in mind when I cite the triviality results back at them — because if the proofs show something narrower than "conditionals have no probabilities," I'd like to know exactly how narrow. If some of these readings are really just different vocabularies for the same underlying claim, say so too, since half my confusion is that people seem to switch between them mid-sentance.
