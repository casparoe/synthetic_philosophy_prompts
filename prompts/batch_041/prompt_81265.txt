I'm a second-year PhD student in formal epistemology, and I've been asked to run the anthropics sessions for our department's formal methods reading group this term. Rather than circulate a reading list that half the group won't read and the other half will read tendentiously, I want to build a decision tree that sorts a participant into a position on self-locating belief by asking them questions about cases rather than by asking them which assumption they endorse. The tree should begin from the one commitment I'm willing to attribute to everyone in the room, namely that in the setups below there is, before you learn anything further, something genuine for you to be uncertain about concerning which observer you are, and it should end in leaves that are named positions (or marked unnamed where the position has no standard label). I want six to nine branch nodes and seven to nine leaves, written for people who know the Sleeping Beauty debate well enough to be tired of it but who have not read anything on anthropics proper, so assume nobody has the standard labels memorized and nothing may be cited, titled, or attributed; the whole instrument has to stand on its own in about two pages. Two constraints on the nodes: each one must be phrased as a question a participant can answer without already knowing which position it leads to (no node may ask "do you accept the self-indication assumption?" or anything of that shape), and the tree must be built from the logical space first, so that you first exhibit the combinations of answers the nodes allow and only then locate the actual positions in that space, noting which combinations are unoccupied and diagnosing each one as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

I want the tree organized around two distinctions in particular, and I'd like you to use two cases I've been working with in my own writing, stated in full here so that nothing needs to be looked up. The first is the Two Labs case: a fair coin is flipped; on heads, Lab A creates exactly one observer, in a single room, wearing a red hat, who is told the whole setup; on tails, Lab B creates one observer in a red hat who is told the whole setup, and, in a separate soundproofed room, a second observer in a blue hat who is told nothing and is never released; you now find yourself wearing a red hat, having been told the whole setup. This should be the case at the node that separates the assumption that your credence tracks the number of observers (or observer-moments) anywhere who share your evidence from the assumption that it tracks the fraction of observers in your world who share your evidence, and I expect it to leave the count-based position and the full-non-indexical-conditioning position inseparable; if you agree, say so explicitly and say what further case would separate them, and if you disagree, show the split. The second is the Long Life variant, which is the same coin but a different pair of labs: on heads, Lab A creates one observer with a single moment of experience, which is the moment of being told the setup, after which that observer ceases; on tails, Lab B creates one observer who will live through three moments of experience, of which only the first is the moment of being told the setup, the other two being blank staring at a wall with no further information arriving; you are now at the moment of being told the setup. This is the case for the node that asks whether the sampling unit is the observer or the observer-moment, and it should be the node on which the two fraction-based positions come apart.

The leaves I need represented, described by content rather than by label wherever the label would give the game away, are: the fraction-based position with the observer as unit; the fraction-based position with the observer-moment as unit; the count-based position together with its uncomfortable consequence that you should be nearly certain that you are one of a very large number of observers, which I want to appear as its own bite-the-bullet leaf rather than as a reductio; a count-based position that blocks that consequence by restricting which extra observers count, which I suspect is either unnamed or empty and which you should diagnose if so; full non-indexical conditioning, described as conditioning on the fact that some observer has your evidence rather than on which observer you are; the line that self-locating uncertainty is an artifact to be dissolved rather than quantified; the view that there is no fact of the matter about the correct reference class, so the question is ill-posed; and the view that replaces the credence question with a decision question about which policy to adopt, so that the indexical credences drop out. Where two of these leaves cannot be separated by any node you have, flag the pair rather than papering over it, and where the tree forces a participant into a leaf that combines answers nobody has actually held, say so and give one sentence on what makes that combination awkward.
