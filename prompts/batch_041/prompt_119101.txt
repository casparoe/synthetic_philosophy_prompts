My colleague Dana and I both sit on the science committee of a mid-sized land trust, and in the weeks before a funding round we walk each other through hypothetical restoration plans, mostly to find out where our instincts part company. Every scenario is stated the same way: a plan either succeeds, securing a stated number of acres, or fails and secures nothing, and every probability is given outright. Her verdicts from last week's session were as follows. (1) Plan A: 1,500 acres guaranteed; Plan B: an 89% chance of 1,500 acres, a 10% chance of 2,000 acres, and a 1% chance of nothing. Dana chose A. (2) Plan C: an 11% chance of 1,500 acres and an 89% chance of nothing; Plan D: a 10% chance of 2,000 acres and a 90% chance of nothing. Dana chose D. (3) Plan E: a 60% chance of 2,600 acres, a 35% chance of 300 acres, and a 5% chance of nothing; Plan F: 1,000 acres guaranteed. Dana chose E. (4) Plan G: a 97% chance of 2,000 acres and a 3% chance of nothing; Plan H: 1,600 acres guaranteed. Dana chose G.

Two remarks before the question. First, she has said in other contexts that she would rather take a plan whose odds are fully known than one whose odds are merely known to lie between the same two figures, though the probabilities in all four scenarios above were specified outright. Second, I have a persistent but unarticulated sense that her answers to (1) and (2) cannot both be right in the way she takes them to be.

Next week's scenario is the one I cannot call. Plan X: a 1% chance of 24,000 acres and a 99% chance of nothing. Plan Y: 400 acres guaranteed. Predict which plan she will choose, but do not stop at the prediction. Set out the candidate decision rules that fit all four of her earlier verdicts, say what each of those rules implies about X against Y, tell me which rule you would bet she is running and why, and name the further scenario that would best separate the two rules you cannot otherwise tell apart. If some rule's verdict on X against Y turns on something the four earlier answers leave undetermined, say what that something is and how far the earlier answers constrain it.
