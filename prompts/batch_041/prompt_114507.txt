Two philosophers have been going at each other about the statue and the clay for years. One says the statue just is the lump of clay -- one object, two ways of picking it out -- and the other says there are genuinely two things in the same place at the same time, the lump and the statue, related by constitution rather than identity. They've never managed to write anything together. Draft the joint statement they could both sign.

I want: a numbered list of claims both accept -- what the case is, what's uncontroversial, and what the fight is *not* about (neither denies that squashing the lump ends the statue while the clay survives, and both think that needs explaining); the disagreement stated twice, once in wording the identity theorist would put her name to and once in wording the constitution theorist would put his to; then the cruxes. I'm fairly sure one crux is whether counting objects is relative to a sortal or count-noun -- whether "how many things are on the pedestal?" is even a well-formed question without saying how many *whats* -- and another is what our judgments about "the same statue" track when a lump is squashed and later re-formed into a matching shape, so include those two if you think they're live, plus any you think are more central. For each crux give a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance, and dont let the predictions be weaselly. A table is fine for the disagreements and the cruxes. If part of their fight turns out to be purely verbal, say which part and why.
