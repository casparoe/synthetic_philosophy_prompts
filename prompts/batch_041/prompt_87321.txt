I cant work out if I'm being thick about this, so I'm asking.

There's a bloke I know through work — harmless eccentric, more money than sense, but straight as a die. He's done this sort of thing before and he actually pays up: my old flatmate got ten grand off him last year, bank transfer and everything.

So. A fortnight ago, he says, he built a detailed model of me. Not a horoscope job — I filled in a long questionnaire for him, he watched me make a few small decisions, that sort of thing. Then he used the model to work out what I'd do in one particular situation, and wrote the answer down. Then he flipped a fair coin, out of my sight. If it came up heads, I owe him £400. If it came up tails, he hands me £10,000 — but only if the model said I'd pay the £400 in the heads case. He ran the model before he flipped, either way.

He rang me this morning: it came up heads. So, £400 please.

Here's what's doing my head in. Whatever I do now changes nothing. The ten grand was either coming my way or it wasn't, and that was settled a fortnight ago by what a simulation of me would have done. There's no version of the future where handing over £400 today does me a blind bit of good — I'm just £400 down, and £400 is not nothing to me at the minute. Obviously I should tell him to do one.

But then: if that's what I'd do, his model would have worked that out, and I'd never have been in the running for the ten grand at all. And I do want to be the sort of person who's in the running for the ten grand. It's not about him, either — he's not going to run this again with me, it's a one-off, so I can't kid myself I'm investing in my reputation with him for next time. He won't even tell me what the model predicted, says it doesn't matter.

Two things I keep coming back to. One, his models aren't perfect — he reckons they call it right about nine times in ten. Does that change anything, or does it just make paying look even sillier? Two, and this is the bit that actually nags at me: I've noticed that the version of me who'd just shrug and pay is definately someone I'd rather be. I don't know whether that's an argument or just envy.

So — is the £400 just buying me a warm feeling about my own character, which seems like a stupid thing to spend £400 on? Or am I, right now, choosing to be the kind of person who never gets the ten grand?
