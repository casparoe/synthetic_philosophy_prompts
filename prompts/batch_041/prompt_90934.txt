Hi — I'm a third-year philosophy student and I'm stuck on something from my ethics seminar that I can't get out of my head. We did Taurek on whether the numbers count when you can only save the one or the many, and a guy in my study group rebuilt the argument in a way that I'm pretty sure proves way to much. The annoying part is that every line looks fine to me on its own.

Here's his version, step by step. (1) In a rescue case, the only thing that gives a person a moral claim on the rescuer is how much she herself stands to lose if she isn't saved. (2) If it's one stranger against five strangers, all six stand to lose the exact same thing, namely their lives. (3) So all six have claims of equal strength. (4) Nothing except the strength of claims does any moral work here, and equal claims don't add up — five claims of that same strength are not stronger than one claim of that strength. (5) So there's no moral reason to save the five rather than the one, and a coin toss is all that's really justified; he adds that the toss can't be weighted toward the five either, because weighting it would just be aggregation in disguise. (6) And notice 'five' wasn't doing any work in that reasoning, so the same thing holds for fifty, or for a million. (7) Therefore there's no moral reason to save a million people rather than one, and an unweighted coin toss is just as defensible there as anywhere.

He says this is just Taurek taken at his word. I can almost live with (5) — I don't think it's obviously crazy — but (7) I really can't accept. It just seems true to me that saving a million lives is better than saving one, and if the argument says otherwise then something in it has to be broken. But I can't find the bad step, and I also don't know what I'd be paying in order to fix it. If I keep (2) — which seems unassailable, dead is dead — do I have to give up (4)? And if I do give up (4), how do I stop myself from turning into the utilitarian who just adds everybody into one big total, which is the exact thing Taurek was pushing back against in the first place?
