"Special relativity tells us that simultaneity is relative to a frame of reference. Any observer at rest in some inertial frame has a plane of simultaneity — the set of events she counts as happening now — and if she moves relative to another observer, her plane of simultaneity tilts relative to his. So two people passing each other in the street, moving at different velocities, will not agree about which distant events are happening right now. Putnam's Andromeda example makes this vivid: if one of them is walking towards the Andromeda galaxy and the other away from it, then an event in Andromeda that lies in the past for one of them lies in the future for the other, even though the two are standing in the same place at the same instant. Putnam used this to argue that presentism is untenable. Presentism says that only the present moment is real. But whose present? There is no privileged frame, so there is no frame-independent fact about which events are present. Therefore the present cannot be a real, objective feature of the world, and the four-dimensional block — all events equally real — is what we're left with. Rietdijk's own paper made the same point in terms of determinism: he argued that if an event that is future for one observer is already past for another, then the future is already fixed and there is no genuine openness.

Does the argument smuggle in a premise? I don't think it needs to. Everything I've said follows from the special theory of relativity itself, and the special theory is extremely well confirmed. The only assumption in play is that the laws of physics take the same form in all inertial frames, which is just the principle of relativity and is not remotely controversial. You might object that the block universe is a strange metaphysics, but strangeness is not an argument. And if the argument works, it tells against the growing block view and the moving spotlight too, since both of those still need a single objective present; so it isn't only presentism that's in trouble. The block universe is the only view left standing."
