The thing thats been bugging me, and I dont think I've seen anyone put it this way, is that the analogy seems to get the error profile backwards. In perception, when you're wrong it's usually because something got in the way between you and the thing — fog, bad light, a mirror, a clever stage set — while the thing itself was still sitting there being whatever it was. But the textbook cases of modal error seem to come from the object end rather than the medium end: you think you can conceive of a world where the watery stuff isn't what it actually is, and the mistake turns out to be located in what water is, not in any interference on the way in. So the analogy predicts one shape of error and the actual errors have another shape.
