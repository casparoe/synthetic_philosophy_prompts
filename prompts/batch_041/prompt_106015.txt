I have been arguing with a friend about what Keynes would say about the good life today, and I would like a reasoned extrapolation rather than a summary of his known views. My friend's starting point is Keynes's 1930 essay on economic possibilities for our grandchildren, where he predicted that within a century the economic problem would be solved and the real challenge would be how to use leisure wisely; my friend thinks that prediction has largely come true for the developed world, but that we have failed the challenge, replacing the art of living with the attention economy, bullshit jobs, and a culture that treats work as identity. On that basis my friend proposes a radical policy package: a tax on automation to fund a universal basic income, plus a public ministry of leisure that funds the arts, friendship infrastructure, and contemplative practices, on the grounds that the good life requires teaching people to enjoy leisure rather than merely giving them time. I am not so sure. Keynes was a Bloomsbury aesthete who treated love, friendship, and beauty as intrinsic goods, but he was also a pragmatic policy-maker who wanted to save capitalism and who thought economists should be humble and competent, like dentists. I suspect he would have been more ambivalent about state-engineered leisure, and more worried about the morbid love of money than about the distribution of work.

What I want from you is a prediction of Keynes's views on a specific contemporary question: in a world of AI-driven abundance, should the state actively cultivate the art of living, through funding for the arts, public spaces for friendship, and even a leisure curriculum in schools, or should it stick to ensuring the material conditions and leave the good life to individuals? How would he adjudicate between the aesthetic and the paternalist strands in his own thought? And what would he say about the rise of virtual communities and AI companions as substitutes for the face-to-face love and friendship he prized? I am less interested in whether he would endorse my friend's package than in the rules or priorities from his earlier commitments that would drive his answers, the places where those commitments pull in opposite directions, and the further question that would best tell which strand wins out.
