I am putting together a seminar on situated and extended cognition, and I want to use Christopher Nolan's Memento as the centrepiece for one session. Read the film through the extended mind thesis. Leonard Shelby's Polaroids, tattoos, and scraps of handwriting appear to meet the thesis's own conditions: the system is always to hand and he endorses its entries without question, so that on the parity principle his beliefs about John G. would seem to be stored as much in his skin and pockets as in his head. But I want the reading to press hardest on the closing sequence, where Leonard destroys the evidence he has accumulated and deliberately commits a false entry to his own system in order to give himself a reason to keep going. Does that ending damage the thesis, or does it merely expose what the thesis quietly assumes about the external resource -- that it is stable, transparent, and not authored by the very subject whose beliefs it is supposed to carry? Say, too, where the film dramatizes the objection that being reliably coupled to a notebook is not the same as the notebook's being part of one's cognition, and whether it suggests the real question is less metaphysical than ethical: not whether the notes are part of the mind, but who is answerable for what a self-scaffolding agent writes into them. Keep the reading honest in both directions -- do not reduce the film to an illustration of a paper, and do not soften the thesis to save it -- and close by saying what the film adds to the debate that the arguments alone do not.
