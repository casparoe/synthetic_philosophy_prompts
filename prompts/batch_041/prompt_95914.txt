Quick background: I'm on the board of a small neighborhood association in my town, like 40 households, we manage a shared community garden and a little park. We have this rule that only households that have been members for 10+ years can vote on how the budget gets spent. A founder wrote it into the bylaws back in the 90s, and honestly there's no good reason for it, it was just to keep things from getting "too complicated" I guess.

Now my neighbor Dana moved in about five years ago. She's by far the most active person in the association, she organizes the garden workdays, she fixed the irrigation, she basically runs the place. She wants to vote on the budget, and she's right that it's unfair she can't. But here's the thing: if we try to change the rule, the older members, who are the ones who show up and pay dues, are going to be furious. Two of them already told me they'd quit the board if we "let newcomers take over." If they quit, the association probably dissolves and the garden goes back to weeds.

So I'm stuck. Dana is clearly in the right, but she has no power to change anything, and the people with the power to change it are the ones who benefit from the rule staying as it is. And if I side with Dana and push the vote, I might destroy the whole thing. But if I side with the old-timers and keep my mouth shut, I feel like I'm just going along with an injustice because it's convenient and because it "keeps the peace." One of them even said to me, "we've always done it this way, and it's worked, why mess with it," and I couldn't really argue with that, which bugged me.

I keep going back and forth. Part of me thinks: the rule is arbitrary, everyone knows it's arbitrary, but arbitrary rules that everyone follows are what hold a community together, you don't get to opt out just because you personally think a rule is dumb. Another part of me thinks: that's exactly what tyrants say. And a third part thinks the real problem is that Dana is right but weak, and the board is wrong but strong, and there's no version of this where the right thing actually wins unless someone with power decides to give some of it up.

What should I actually do here, and how should I think about the tradeoff between doing what's fair and doing what keeps things from falling apart? I'm not looking for a generic "communicate better" answer, I want to know what's actually going on in this kind of situation. Please answer in American English.
