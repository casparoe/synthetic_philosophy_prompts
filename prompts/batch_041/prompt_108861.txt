My mate and I had a ridiculous four-hour arguement last night about whether you can really compare how well off two people are, and I'd rather see it as fiction than go round in circles again. So: write me a short story, 1500 words or so, about a hospital committee deciding which of two patients gets the last slot on a dialysis programme, using a scoring system that's meant to put both patients' welfare on one common scale.

What I want it to actually dramatise, not just mention. First, the gap between ranking two people (she's worse off than him) and saying by how much — all the committee's adding up needs the second, and nobody can say where the unit comes from. Second, that the comparison can feel like a measurement while really being a decision about which scale to use: change the calibration and the ranking flips, so the obvious answer is only obvious on one of them. Third, that the empathy fix — imagine living both their lives and pick the one you'd rather — doesn't settle it either, because whoever's doing the imagining brings their own scale along with them.

Don't give it a tidy moral. I'd rather the decision goes through on a comparison everyone can see only holds on some scales, and the member who keeps objecting slowly realises she can't say what the right call is, only what the question actually was. And give me a scene where two members agree completely about who's worse off and still disagree about what to do — that's the bit my mate couldn't get his head round.

Invented people and cases only, and no philosophy lecture in the dialogue. British English please.
