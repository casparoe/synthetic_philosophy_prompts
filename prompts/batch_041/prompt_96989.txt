So I've been following the whole Dennett vs Caruso thing on free will and punishment for a while now — the book they did together, the podcasts, the endless back-and-forths — and I keep getting this nagging feeling that I'm watching two really smart people argue past each other while secretly agreeing about almost everything checkable. Dennett: compatibilism, moral responsibility is real, we should keep our practices of holding people responsible, it isn't just a useful fiction. Caruso: basic desert is a myth, ditch retributivism, go with the public-health/quarantine model. Fine. But on the ground they basically converge — no eye-for-an-eye, focus on reform and deterrence and prevention, don't hate the offender. So where's the actual beef?

I've been marinating in rationalist blogs for like fifteen years, so my first instinct is always "taboo the words and see what's left," and here's my hypothesis, which I want you to stress-test rather than just nod at: the disagreement isn't really about facts or even policy, it's a verbal dispute that only looks substantive because both sides keep using the same two words, "free will" and "desert," to point at different things. Taboo both words and I suspect Dennett is answering one question ("can our responsibility practices be justified, and how?") while Caruso is answering a different one ("is there basic desert of the kind that would ground retributive punishment?"), and they're both filing it under "the free will problem." If that's right, the stall is an illusion and there's nothing to resolve except terminology.

But maybe thats too cheap and there's a real crux under there. Candidates I can come up with: whether the reactive attitudes are justified versus merely instrumentally useful; whether "ought implies can" has any real bite against determinism; whether moral responsibility needs ultimate self-authorship or just reasons-responsiveness; and whether a purely forward-looking justification of blame can ever be stable or whether it always smuggles in something backward-looking.

So: diagnose it. Is this a verbal dispute, a substantive one, two different questions wearing one name, or a bedrock clash of intuitions neither side can argue for? Ground it in what each side actually says, not a survey of the free will literature. Then tell me what would count as progress — a distinction, a thought experiment, a formal result, a changed question — or argue honestly that nothing will move it and say why.

And one concrete thing I want you to chew on: imagine a desert-o-meter that reads out, for any action, whether the agent was the ultimate source of it in the strong libertarian sense — and it always reads zero. Dennett shrugs: so what, compatibilist sourcehood is the thing we cared about all along. Caruso says: exactly, so basic desert is dead. Is Dennett here just redefining "source" to dodge the result, or is he correctly identifying that we never needed the libertarian reading in the first place? Taboo the word and tell me whether they actually disagree or just don't like each other's tone.
