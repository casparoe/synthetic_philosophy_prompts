I host a departmental interview series, and my next guest is a political theorist whose book argues that historical analogies are best understood not as premises in an inference — predictions, warnings, items of evidence — but as instruments of description: an analogy supplies the vocabulary in which a present situation is characterised, so its characteristic failure is not that it predicts badly but that it renders certain features of the present case unsayable. She calls this description capture, and she holds that the long quarrel between those who defend the lessons of history and those who dismiss them as rhetoric is a stalemate because both sides treat the analogy as a premise.

Please draft eight to ten questions for me, none of them biographical or warm-up, that press the position rather than invite a restatement of it. I want at least one question on how description capture is supposed to differ from ordinary framing effects or confirmation bias, since a sceptic will say the view collapses into one of those; one on whether the account can accommodate cases where an analogy seems genuinely to have carried information, such as the non-use of nuclear weapons since 1945 or the decline of great-power conquest; one on the reflexive difficulty that her own diagnosis is offered in a vocabulary that is itself selective; one on whether the view has any practical upshot for someone advising a decision-maker, or whether it amounts to a counsel of quietism; and one on what would count as evidence against the account. Do not ask her for citations or for a survey of the literature.

Return the result as a JSON object with two fields. "guest_thesis" is a one-sentence restatement of her position in my own words, as a check that I have understood it. "questions" is an array of eight to ten objects, each with the fields: "order" (integer, its position in the sequence), "question" (the question as I would actually put it aloud), "targets" (which component of her position it is meant to test), "likely_reply" (the answer she is most likely to give), and "follow_up" (what I should ask if she gives that reply). At least two of the follow-ups must be designed to close off the deflection that I have misunderstood the thesis.
