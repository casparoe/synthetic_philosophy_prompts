I'm putting together a handout for a reading group that's doing Ayer on the self and survival -- the verificationist line that a claim like I will survive the death of my body is either meaningless or at best a hypothesis no one could ever check, plus the bit where he had his own near-death expereince late in life and admitted it shook him even though he never came round to believing. I want a decision tree that sorts a reader into a position on whether survival is even a coherent thing to hope for and, if it is, what it would consist in. Branch nodes should be questions someone can answer without already knowing the label: is the claim truth-apt at all or just an expression of attitude; if it's truth-apt, does survival need a persisting subject or only a future person psychologically continuous with you; must that future person be numerically you, or is a faithful replica enough; does the continuity have to be gapless, or can you be out of existence for a while and come back; and if two replicas both wake up claiming your memories, does that sink the survival claim or just make it a branching one. Leaves: Cartesian soul; a reconstitution or replica view; the reductionist line that survival without identity is enough; no-self or Buddhist reductionism; the verificationist not-even-false option; Ayer's own post-near-death wobble; and one for nobody survives, the causal order just carries on -- give it a name if it hasn't got one. Six or seven nodes, and tell me which combinations come out empty and why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any two leaves the tree can't really separate.
