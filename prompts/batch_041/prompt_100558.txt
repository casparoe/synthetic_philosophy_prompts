Christopher Nolan's Memento concerns a man who cannot form new memories and who rebuilds himself every few minutes out of tattoos, Polaroids, and handwritten notes, until the film's end reveals that he has been editing that record to give himself a purpose. Read it through Daniel Dennett's account of the self as a "center of narrative gravity" — the view that the self is not an inner thing but an abstract posit, the protagonist of the story the organism spins about itself, as real as a center of gravity and no more. Where does the film bear the theory out, and where does it strain it? Leonard's identity really does seem to reside in his notes, and the ending makes him an author revising his own narrative; yet Dennett presents the narrative self as a harmless fiction that earns its keep, while the film treats its constructedness as something closer to a curse. Is that a disagreement with the theory or only about the price of living with it? And does Leonard's case show that the narrative self requires a narrator who remembers spinning it — a demand the theory is meant to dissolve? Identify the scenes where the film makes concrete what the theory handles abstractly, where it resists the reading, and say what, if anything, the film contributes to the debate that argument alone does not.
