Here is an argument I have been working on and cannot decide whether it is sound. It concerns the Judy Benjamin problem. An agent has a prior over four possibilities: Red Headquarters, Red Second Company, Blue Headquarters, Blue Second Company. She receives a radio message: I cannot tell whether you are in the red or blue territory, but if you are in red, the probability that you are in headquarters is one half. The standard response is to update by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler divergence from the prior subject to the constraint that the posterior conditional probability of headquarters given red is one half. Call this rule Infomin.

(1) When an agent receives a report that gives her a conditional probability P(A|B) = q, and the report is her only new information, her posterior should be the prior distribution that minimizes the Kullback-Leibler divergence from her prior subject to the constraint P'(A|B) = q.

(2) This rule generalizes conditionalization: when the report tells her that B is true, minimizing KL divergence subject to P'(B) = 1 yields the conditionalized distribution.

(3) Now consider an agent who receives two reports, one giving P(A|B) = q and the other giving P(A|C) = r, where B and C are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, and q and r are consistent. Infomin applied to both constraints simultaneously yields one posterior.

(4) Suppose the same agent receives the same two pieces of information sequentially: first P(A|B) = q, then P(A|C) = r. Applying Infomin twice can yield a different posterior.

(5) Therefore Infomin makes the agent's posterior depend on the order in which she receives information.

(6) But the order in which information is received is irrelevant to what one should believe: if two agents have the same prior and receive the same total information, they should have the same posterior.

(7) Therefore Infomin is not a correct rule for updating on conditionals.

(8) Any rule that satisfies (1) and (2) is subject to the same problem.

(9) Therefore there is no correct rule for updating on conditionals.

(10) But that conclusion is absurd: there must be a correct rule for updating on conditionals.

(11) So one of the premises is false.

Which premise should be rejected, and what does rejecting it cost? I am particularly interested in whether the problem lies in (1), in (6), or in (8). If I reject (1), what rule should replace it? If I reject (6), what could justify order-dependence? If I reject (8), what other rule satisfies (1) and (2) but avoids the problem?
