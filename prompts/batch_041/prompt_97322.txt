I'm assembling a short reading to open a seminar on weakness of will and time inconsistency, and I'd like a poem rather than another excerpt from the literature. Please write a pantoum of five quatrains, twenty lines, each quatrain headed by a bare time-marker giving how far off the choice is — a year out, a month out, a week out, tonight, the morning after — so that the headers carry the speaker from a distance to the moment of choice and one step past it. Let the pantoum's rule do the argument: lines 2 and 4 of each quatrain return as lines 1 and 3 of the next, so the same sentence is said again from closer up, and the closing quatrain should bring back the poem's opening lines so that the form ends where it began.

The subject is a preference reversal of the ordinary kind. At every distance the speaker faces the same two options, a smaller good available now and a larger one available later; at long range she prefers the larger one, and as the nearer one approaches the same unchanged pair gets re-ranked. The line that sounds like resolve from a year out should sound like a rationalization a week out, and the recurrence of the lines should read as the naive pattern — each return arriving as though it were a fresh resolution.

I want the poem to hold two things in tension without settling them. First, that this is a genuine reversal of what she wants, so that on a discounting picture each time-slice simply does what it most prefers and there is no weakness of will in the story at all. Second, that she lives it as the classic thing: knowing the better and doing the worse, with the further good going under. Let the reader feel that the discount is steeper close in than far out — that distant intentions are stable and cheap to make, and that the ranking only starts to move as the deadline does. The two options must remain the same two options throughout; nothing new is offered to her.

Constraints: no term from the literature may appear in the poem — no "discount," "hyperbolic," "utility," "akrasia," "precommitment," "time-slice." The concepts have to be enacted by the form and the images rather than named. No moralizing close and no tidy resolution; the pantoum's loop is the point. Rhyme and meter are yours to choose, but keep it a real pantoum. A specialist in intertemporal choice should recognize the phenomenon without being told its name, and someone who has never heard of it should find a good poem.
