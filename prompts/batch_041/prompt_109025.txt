I do evaluation work for a health charity and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a name for, and I'm not sure its even one thing.

Here's the setup. We pick a number because it's a decent stand-in for something we actually care about — say, the share of patients still showing up to clinic a year later, used as a proxy for "the care stuck." Early on it behaves like a proxy should: it tracks the thing we care about, and where the two come apart, that gap is where the interesting information lives.

Then the number becomes the thing we manage. And here's the part I can't explain: it doesn't stop tracking the thing we care about, it starts tracking it *better*. Our validation checks come out cleaner than they ever did, the correlation is higher, the noise is lower, and nobody can point to a case where the number looks good and the underlying thing looks bad. But I've stopped trusting those checks and I can't say why in a way that isn't hand-wavey. Best I can do: the number tracks the thing better because the clinic has reorganized itself around the number, and the reorganizing has eaten exactly the variation that made the original correlation mean anything. The gap between proxy and goal is where the information lived, and the gap is now closed — not because the proxy got more accurate, but because the thing we care about got quietly redefined as whatever the proxy can see.

So: a measure that gets more predictive of its target at the exact moment it stops being evidence about it.

Is there a name for this? Goodhart's law is the nearest thing I know but it looks like the opposite — Goodhart is the number drifting away from the goal, mine is the number cosying up to it. Self-fulfilling prophecy is also close but backwards: there the belief makes itself true, here the belief was already true and it's the evidence that rots. I don't just want a label — I want to know what the thing actually is, which nearby concepts it's nearest to and how it differs from each, and where it stops applying. (I've got a second case where a charity's "cost per person housed" went down and got more predictive after it landed in a grant agreement, but that one might be a different animal — the easy-to-house got served first, so the number got better at measuring a different population. Tell me if that's the same pattern or not.)
