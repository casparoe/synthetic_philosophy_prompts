I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, though I am comfortable with probability and expected values from performance modelling. I have been sitting in on a reading group about decision theory, and a friend there presented an argument that risk-weighted expected utility cannot be a correct theory of rational choice. I cannot tell whether the argument is a trick or a genuine refutation. As I understand it, risk-weighted expected utility evaluates a lottery by sorting its outcomes from worst to best, taking the worst outcome as a baseline, and adding the gaps between successive outcomes weighted not by the probability of that outcome alone but by a risk function applied to the probability of getting that outcome or better. Formally, if the outcomes are x1 ≤ x2 ≤ ... ≤ xn with probabilities p1, ..., pn, the value is x1 + Σ_{i=2}^n (x_i − x_{i−1}) r(p_i + ... + p_n), where r is increasing, r(0)=0 and r(1)=1; if r is convex the agent is risk averse. The argument runs as follows. (1) A rational agent may be risk averse, and risk-weighted expected utility with a convex risk function represents such an agent. (2) For any non-degenerate lottery L, a sufficiently risk-averse agent prefers the expected value of L for sure to L itself. (3) Consider a lottery L that pays +1 with probability 0.6 and −1 with probability 0.4; its expected value is +0.2, but a sufficiently risk-averse agent assigns it a negative risk-weighted value and rejects it. (4) Now suppose the agent is offered N independent copies of L one after another, each time choosing whether to accept or reject that copy before seeing the next offer. At each stage the choice is exactly L plus whatever wealth they already have, so by the same reasoning they reject every copy and end with their initial wealth. (5) But the compound lottery consisting of all N copies has expected value +0.2N, and by the law of large numbers the probability of any loss tends to zero as N grows; for large N it is almost a sure gain of +0.2N. (6) A rational agent cannot prefer a sure zero to a lottery that is arbitrarily close to a sure gain of +0.2N. (7) Therefore risk-weighted expected utility with a fixed convex risk function permits an irrational pattern of choices, so it is not a correct theory of rational choice under risk.

I would rather keep the dominance principle in step (6) than the claim that a rational agent must be risk averse in step (1), but I want to know what I am paying if I do.
