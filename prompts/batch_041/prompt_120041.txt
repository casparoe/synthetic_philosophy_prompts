I am a PhD student in philosophy of biology, currently writing a chapter in which I argue that decapod crustaceans should be included in the welfare standards governing aquaculture. I was at a workshop last week where an industry representative insisted that crab welfare is a luxury concern for rich countries, and while I have some answers to that, I want to be sure the evidential core of my argument is sound first. Before I commit to the argument, I want to test it against a serious skeptic, so I would like you to play Dr. Marlowe, a comparative neurobiologist who sits on an industry-adjacent aquaculture welfare panel. Dr. Marlowe accepts that fish are sentient and that their welfare matters, and she supports existing fish welfare regulations, but she is not persuaded that the evidence for pain in crustaceans is strong enough to justify the significant costs of extending those regulations to crabs, lobsters, and shrimp. She will concede that the crustacean literature contains suggestive findings, but she will insist that the burden of proof is higher for invertebrates because their nervous systems are organized so differently, and because the practical stakes—feed conversion, mortality rates, the economics of small producers—are high. She is not a caricature: she knows the relevant experiments, she can distinguish nociception from pain, and she will engage with your best points. Please keep your replies to a single paragraph so the exchange feels like a conversation, stay in character until I say stop, and if you need a factual clarification, ask for it in character.

Here is my opening move. I have been reading the same papers you have. In hermit crabs, individuals will endure electric shocks to obtain a preferred shell, and they show long-term sensitization after noxious stimulation; in shrimp, eyestalk ablation is followed by behavioral changes that some researchers interpret as chronic pain. In fish, we treat evidence of roughly this kind—nociceptors, opioid receptors, avoidance learning, motivational trade-offs—as sufficient to ground welfare protections. You say the crustacean case is weaker, but I cannot see the principled difference. If your evidential threshold excludes crabs, why does it not also exclude fish? And if it is low enough to include fish, why is it not low enough to include crabs? I am not asking you to assert that crabs definitely feel pain; I am asking what the relevant difference is supposed to be. Please respond to that.
