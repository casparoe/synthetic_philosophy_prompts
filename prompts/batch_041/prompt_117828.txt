I am planning a ninety-minute session for a comparative-philosophy reading group of non-specialists on the tetralemma associated with Nāgārjuna and its role in Madhyamaka accounts of emptiness. I want to present the tetralemma not as a four-valued logic or as four theses, but as a therapeutic device that exhausts reification: each of the four alternatives—affirmation, denial, both, neither—is made untenable, and the fourth is not a covert fifth position; I also want to include the emptiness of emptiness, so that empty does not harden into a new essence, and to bring in dependent origination and the two truths without letting them become a metaphysical system. Please plan the explanation rather than giving it: the order in which the components must be introduced and why each depends on the previous one; the single carrying example that should anchor the session, what it must have and lack, and why the usual chariot-and-parts example may or may not serve; the misconceptions the group will predictably bring or form—for instance, that the tetralemma is a four-valued logic, that the fourth lemma is an ineffable mystical state, that Nāgārjuna is a nihilist, or that emptiness means nothingness—and exactly where in the sequence each should be headed off; the check for understanding that would show they have grasped the point rather than merely memorized the four alternatives; and what I should explicitly leave out at this level. Do not assign readings. End with a final line beginning "Summary:" that states the teaching plan in one sentence.
