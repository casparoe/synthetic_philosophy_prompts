I never took a philosophy class but I read alot of it on my own, and theres a pattern I keep running into that I don't have a word for. It shows up whenever people argue about whether somebody is to blame for something.

Heres the thing. Say a guy does something terrible. Someone tells me "well, he had a brain tumor," or "he was raised in a house where that was treated as normal," and instantly I stop blaming him -- not blame him less, stop. The cause seems to erase the blame. But now flip it. Say a woman does something wonderful. Someone tells me "she was raised to be generous," or "she just has a warm temperament," and I don't stop crediting her at all. If anything I credit her more. Same kind of cause, opposite effect depending on whether the act was good or bad.

And it gets weirder. If you tell me he did it "because that's the kind of person he is" -- which is still a cause, right, a story about where the act came from -- I blame him more, not less. But "because of the way he was raised" and I blame him less. Two explanations of the same act, both pointing at something in his past or makeup, and one drains the blame while the other piles it on.

So the pattern I'm seeing is roughly: causes seem to cancel blame but not praise, and on top of that some causes (character, "that's just who he is") feed blame while other causes (history, biology, circumstance) drain it. Is there a name for this? I don't think it's just the old free will argument, because nobody in that argument seems to be pointing at this exact thing -- everyone's busy with whether determinism is true, and I'm asking something narrower about which explanations excuse and which ones don't.

I'm not really after a label anyway. I want to know what the thing actually is: what the rule is that decides which causes excuse and which ones condemn, whether blame and credit are really lopsided the way I'm describing or whether I'm secretly mixing up two different questions, and where the edges of it are. If theres no name, give me one and define it well enough that I can tell it apart from just saying "determinism threatens free will." Please answer in American English.
