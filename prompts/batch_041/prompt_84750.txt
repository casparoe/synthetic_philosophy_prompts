I'm a retired toxicologist who got into philosphy after I hung up the lab coat, and I've been stuck on spectrum arguments and whether better than is transative. The setup: better than seems transitive, but a long enough chain of tiny steps -- each one so small you'd never call it worse -- can carry you from something clearly better to something clearly worse, so either transitivity fails, or one of the tiny steps isn't really an improvement, or the endpoints aren't as far apart as they look. I want a poem that makes that structure felt. Write a terza rima, maybe 15-18 lines, running a dose-response spectrum from a barely detectable trace to a lethal dose; each tercet should take one imperceptible step. Let the interlocking rhyme scheme (ABA BCB CDC...) do the work of the transitive chain, and end in a way that either closes the rhyme back to the beginning or breaks it -- I don't want the poem to tell me which. Someone who knows the debate should recognize it, and someone who doesn't should still feel the chain pull and then the snap.
