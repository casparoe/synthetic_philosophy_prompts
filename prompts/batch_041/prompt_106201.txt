It was well written and seemed to know its stuff, and the gist was roughly this:

Turchin's big idea is that history runs on a clock. Societies pass through roughly 250-year rhythms he calls secular cycles, moving through growth, stagnation, crisis, and collapse, and the pattern shows up again and again — Rome, medieval France, imperial China, and the US. The engine is pretty simple: population growth outruns the food supply, wages fall, ordinary people get immiserated, and meanwhile the elites grow too numerous for the number of positions available, which he calls "elite overproduction." That competition turns nasty, factions form, and eventually the state can't collect enough taxes to hold itself together, so you get a revolution or a civil war, and then everything resets and the wheel starts over. He's even boiled it down to a single number — a Political Stress Index — and he says the index predicts political violence, so it's basically a mathematical law of history. The critics say it's just curve-fitting and that you can't predict human societies the way you predict the weather, but Turchin says his model made a real prediction: back in 2010 he said US instability would peak in the 2020s, and here we are. The takeaway is that America is in trouble for the same reason the late Roman Republic was — too much inequality, plus too many overquallified and frustrated elites — and the fix is to reduce inequality and stop minting so many overeducated elites who have nowhere to go.
