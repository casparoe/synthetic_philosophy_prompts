My uncle and I keep going in circles about the definition of death. He even pushes it further and says it's like someone on a heart-lung machine: if the bypass patient's own heart and lungs aren't doing the work, then the vent patient's own brain isn't doing the work either, so what's the difference. I'm more of a circulatory-death person and I think the analogy is quietly doing all the work for him. Can you lay out the correspondence properly -- what maps onto what -- then show me where it actually breaks (I suspect it's the slide from information to life, and the assumption that an organism is one machine rather than a whole ecology of them), then tell me which of those breaks actually matter for the conclusion he wants, namely that the patient is already dead, and whether that conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. A table of correspondences would be fine. And don't just lay out both sides and leave it to me -- come down on which analogy is doing honest work here, which one is smuggling in a picture of what a person is, and where you think I'm wrong.
