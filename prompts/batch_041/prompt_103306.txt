Ok so I've been lurking in these corners of the internet for like a decade now and I keep watching the same fight about Moloch / multipolar traps / race-to-the-bottom stuff go around in circles, and I want to force it into an actual adversarial collaboration document instead of another comment thread.

The two sides I have in mind: (1) the lumpers, who think multipolar traps are one real phenomenon worth having a single word for — that arms races, tragedies of the commons, regulatory races to the bottom, status arms races and so on are all instances of the same underlying dynamic, and that naming it Moloch is doing real cognitive work; and (2) the splitters, who think "Moloch" is basically a vibe that mashes together game structures with completely different escape routes, and that the label mostly makes people worse at fixing anything.

I want the document they could both put their names on after a genuinely good-faith collaboration. Specifically:

- a numbered list of claims both can actually sign — about what a multipolar trap is, about when a race is bad vs. actually fine or even good, and about what we do and don't know re escapes;
- the residual disagreements, each phrased so that the side that holds it would accept the wording as fair to its own view;
- the cruxes, i.e. the subquestions where settling them would move at least one side;
- and for each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Some things I want them to have to react to, cause I think they break the usual slogans: (a) split "defection" into unconditional defection — individually rational no matter what everyone else does — vs. conditional defection, which is only rational once you expect others to defect; I suspect the lumpers are quietly sliding between these two; (b) a waste test: does the burned resource buy the parties anything they'd separately value, or is it pure positional burn, and should that change whether we call it Moloch at all; (c) the trap-inversion point — whether any escape from a trap has to create a new trap somewhere (a cartel of restraint that is itself a race to defect from), so that "solved it" is never quite the right description; and (d) whether "Moloch" is a claim about the world or a claim about how we're modeling it, and how you'd tell the difference.

For the concrete cases I'd want at least antibiotic overuse, an AI capabilities race, an academic credential arms race, and overfishing — and for each one, which of the signatures above it has and whether that changes the verdict.

Format: agreed-claims section first, numbered; then a disagreements table; then a cruxes table with each side's predictions. If some chunk of this whole fight is just verbal, say which chunk and why. And please don't send me a syllabus or a reading list, I want the actual document.
