You are a political philosopher and economist with expertise in mechanism design, democratic theory, and the political economy of prediction markets. I want you to think carefully and at length about a historical episode that is usually discussed as a cautionary tale about government prediction markets, and to analyze it through the lens of futarchy. Please begin by brainstorming the relevant features of both the episode and the theory — jot down the mapping, the candidate disanalogies, and the questions you will need to resolve — and then write a polished analysis.

The episode: the Policy Analysis Market (PAM), a DARPA-funded project within the FutureMAP program, developed beginning in 2001. PAM was to be a futures exchange on political and military instability in the Middle East, allowing traders to bet on outcomes such as economic growth, military activity, and terrorist casualties. In late July 2003, Senators Ron Wyden and Byron Dorgan publicly condemned the project as a federal betting parlor on terrorism and assassination; within a day the Pentagon cancelled it, and John Poindexter, who headed the DARPA office that housed the project, resigned shortly afterward. The controversy turned partly on a few sample screens that listed, among miscellaneous items, the assassination of Yasser Arafat and a North Korean missile attack.

The theory: futarchy, the proposal associated with Robin Hanson, in which democratic elections or votes fix a welfare metric — a measure of national wellbeing — and conditional prediction markets then estimate whether a given policy would raise or lower that metric. Policies with higher conditional market prices are implemented. The slogan is to vote on values but bet on beliefs. In futarchy, the markets are not supposed to be unconditional bets on world events; they are supposed to be decision markets that price the difference a policy would make.

Analyze PAM through futarchy. I want a disciplined case analysis that runs in both directions. Specifically:

1. Map the episode onto the theory. Who or what plays the role of the values-setting electorate, the welfare metric, the conditional market, and the policy proposal? Was PAM an instance of futarchy, a degenerate cousin, or a category error? Explain the distinction between betting on whether an assassination will occur and betting on whether a policy will reduce political instability, and say why that distinction might have been invisible to PAM's critics.

2. Say what futarchy would have prescribed in PAM's place. What would a futarchy-compliant version of the project have looked like — what would the welfare metric have been, what conditional contracts would have been offered, who would have traded, and how would the results have bound policy? Be concrete enough that the design is testable.

3. Identify where futarchy is silent or fails to fit. Does the theory have anything to say about the yuck factor — the visceral revulsion that killed PAM? Does it have resources for the problem of thin markets, manipulation, or the political economy of funding? What does the episode reveal about the objection that values and beliefs cannot be cleanly separated, since the choice of a welfare metric is itself a contested political act? Is PAM an anomaly for futarchy, a confirmation, or a case the theory never anticipated?

4. Finally, tell me what the episode shows about futarchy's feasibility as a real institution, not as a thought experiment. Does the PAM debacle show that the theory is politically impossible, or only that it was badly packaged? What would futarchy have to assume about public reason, expertise, and the legitimacy of market outcomes for the PAM episode to count as evidence against it?

Write a well-structured analysis of roughly 1,500–2,000 words. Use a table for the central mapping if that helps. Be precise about the mechanism-design details and honest about where the analogy between PAM and futarchy breaks. Do not give me a bibliography or reading list, and do not rely on external sources or citations; reason from the facts given and from the structure of the theory. I want the analysis itself.
