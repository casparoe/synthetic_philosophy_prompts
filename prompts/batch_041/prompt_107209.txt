ok so im writing up my position on moral uncertainty for a workshop and i want a consistency check before i present, because i suspect im helping myself to something somewhere but i honestly cant see where.

here's roughly where ive landed:

(1) im a moral realist — theres a mind-independent fact of the matter about whats right, its not settled by our attitudes.
(2) since im unsure which theory is true, i think the thing to do is maximize expected moral value: put a credence on each theory and act so as to maximize the credence-weighted value.
(3) i dont think value is intertheoretically comparable — theres no deep fact about whether one unit of value under theory A equals one under theory B. so i dont think you can just add values up across theories; instead i lean on a bargaining picture, where each theory gets a seat at the table and can veto any outcome that would be catastrophic by its own lights.
(4) im really worried about fanaticism. a one-in-a-googol chance of infinite bliss shouldnt be allowed to dictate everything.
(5) i think partiality is objectively right, not merely permitted: a parent who saves their own child instead of five strangers is doing the right thing, not just the excusable thing, and i dont think the impartial agent who saved the five is morally better.

so: wich of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and whats the cheapest fix for each real tension — and what do i give up by taking it? id rather you rank the real tensions by how bad they are than just dump everything in one list as equally problematic. and tell me if theres a single change that would clean up the most.
