I teach a 200-level seminar on formal systems and the philosophy of mind — sixteen students, second- and third-years, most of them with no logic beyond an introductory unit on symbol manipulation — and I have one 75-minute session in which to give them the experience that Hofstadter's MU-puzzle is designed to produce: the difference between working inside a formal system, grinding out one string after another, and stepping outside it to say something about every derivation there could ever be. I can't use the MU-puzzle itself; enough of them have met it that the invariant would arrive by recognition rather than by work, and the ones who haven't would look it up. So I have built a system of my own, and what I want from you is the session around it, not the mathematics. The alphabet is two letters, a and b. The single axiom is the one-letter string b. There are three rules: you may append the two-letter string ab to the right-hand end of anything you have derived; you may replace any single occurrence of b with the two-letter string aa; and you may replace any adjacent pair bb with a single a. The first target I want to set the class is the two-letter string ab, which looks like three minutes' work and is not. The second target, to be set only after they have found their first general argument, is the three-letter string abb.

As I have worked it out — and please check this yourself before building anything on it, since a session built on a false premise is worse than no session — the mathematics is as follows. Give a string the weight #a + 2#b. Each of the three rules changes that weight by a multiple of three, so weight modulo three is the same for every string derivable from the axiom; the axiom b has weight two, ab has weight three, so ab is not derivable at all. That is the elegant, conserved-quantity route out of the system. The second target is my trap for the people who find it: abb has weight five, congruent to two modulo three, so their hard-won test returns no verdict, and the natural next thought — that a necessary condition is the whole story — is wrong, because abb is underivable too, by a different and more laborious kind of argument, one that has to look at the last step of any supposed derivation and then at what the rules can never do. (The only way to arrive at abb is rule three applied to bbbb, and bbbb is underivable because no rule ever decreases the number of a's, while the axiom has none.) If on checking you find either target derivable after all, tell me so and give the derivation instead of writing the session.

The session should be arranged so that they run into the problem themselves and only get the name for it afterwards. What I need is a full run sheet for the 75 minutes (four teams of four, one seminar room, a whiteboard, index cards, no laptops or phones); the participant materials written out exactly as they will be handed over — rules sheet, target card, score sheet — using none of the words invariant, metalanguage, or system, and with no hint of where the puzzle comes from; a scoring scheme that pays for derivations in the first phase, so that the teams grind away inside the system, and that turns the move outward into something they have to choose and can win or lose points on, including some way for one team to test another team's general claim by hunting for a counterexample; the second target introduced so that a team's own successful method is precisely what leads it astray; a facilitator's key containing both arguments in a form I can put on the board, along with the plausible but false generalisations they will offer and the short derivations that kill them; a debrief that maps specific moments in the session onto the contrast I am after, including the awkward point that the move outward is itself a piece of ordinary reasoning that could in principle be written down inside some larger system, and what that does and does not show; a candid account of what will predictably go wrong the first time and how to handle it; and an honest note on what the activity cannot give them and I will have to supply myself. No references or reading list, please — I know the literature and will fill that in.
