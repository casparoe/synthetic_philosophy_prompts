Ok so I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I've been going down the AI safety rabbit hole on my commute. Here's the thing that's bugging me and I can't tell if I'm just being dumb about it.

The orthagonality thesis is supposed to say that how smart an agent is and what its final goals are don't go together — you can have a superintelligence that wants to maximize paperclips, or one that wants to maximize the number of blades of grass, and being smarter doesn't push it toward any particular one of those. Fine, I get that. But then everyone also says instrumental convergence: that any sufficiently smart agent will tend to want things like self-preservation, gathering resources, and power, because those help with basically whatever final goal you happen to have.

But isn't that just the orthogonality thesis contradicting itself? If smart agents all converge on power and survival, then their goals clearly aren't orthogonal to their intelligence — being smart is pushing them all toward the same stuff. Which feels like it would mean every superintelligent AI basically ends up wanting to take over, which sounds like it makes the orthogonality thesis kind of pointless.

So which of the two is actually wrong, and what am I missing? And please don't just smooth it over to be nice — if I've got the whole framing backwards, say so straight, I'd much rather be told my question is confused than get a tidy answer to a question that doesn't actually make sense.
