Most discussions of the wrong kind of reasons separate considerations that bear on whether an attitude is fitting from considerations that bear only on whether it is advantageous to hold, and they conclude that the latter are never reasons for the attitude, only reasons to bring it about. Argue against that conclusion for a restricted class of attitudes. Call an attitude adoption-committed when it cannot be held half-heartedly — promise-keeping trust, sustained hope, and action-guiding faith are the paradigm cases — because to hold it at all is to commit to acting as if its content were true. Argue that for adoption-committed attitudes the fitting/advantageous split loses its grip, so that a consideration grounded in the benefits of holding the attitude can be a reason for the attitude itself and not merely a reason to engineer it. Give the strongest case you can, and then identify the objections you take to be genuinely serious — wishful thinking, the independence of the epistemic, and the charge that you have merely renamed pragmatic reasons — and say how you would answer them.
