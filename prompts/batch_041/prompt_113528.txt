ok so this is a thing I keep noticing in arguments about history and I genuinely cant tell if there's a word for it or if im just being pedantic.

Here's the shape. Someone brings up that a dead person did something we'd call bad now -- kept slaves, thought women shouldn't vote, whatever. Someone else says: you can't judge him by our standards, he was a man of his time, everybody thought that then. Fine. But then the same person turns around and praises some other figure from the same decade who was against the thing -- an abolitionist, an early suffragist -- calls them brave, ahead of their time, admirable. And I feel like you can't do both. If 'everybody thought that then' is a real excuse, then the ones who didn't think it aren't admirable, they're just outliers. And the second you praise the one who saw past the times, you're measuring them by a standard that isn't the times' -- which is the exact thing the excuse said you mustn't do. So the times-standard comes out only to block blame and never to block praise, and it comes out hardest for the people the speaker happens to like.

Two wrinkles. First, the excuse often isn't even true -- I've looked up a couple of the defended guys and plenty of their contemporaries were saying the opposite, loudly; nobody checks, because the point was never to describe the past, it was to end the argument. Second, the same move gets aimed at us: 'in a hundred years they'll think we were barbarians for eating meat / flying everywhere / whatever, so who are we to judge' -- which seems like a different job, not understanding anybody, just getting off the hook for now.

Is there a name for this? Not presentism in the loose sense, and not just 'man of his time' by itself -- I mean the specific double standard where the same standard kills blame but not praise. Which of the usual labels is it nearest to (presentism, whig history, anachronism, the historian's fallacy, whatever the relativist one is called), and where exactly does it part company with each? If nothing fits, don't just hand me a word -- coin one, with a definition and edges: clear cases that are it and clear cases that aren't.

And be straight with me about the state of play -- if the people who actually study this have mostly converged on how to think about judging past actors, say so, and if it's still genuinely fought over, don't sell me one camp's answer as if it were the consensus.
