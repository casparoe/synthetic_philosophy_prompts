I am preparing a short paper for a graduate metaphysics seminar and would like a referee-style read before I commit to the argument. The paper concerns the modal argument for the essentiality of origin, the argument that you could not have come from a different sperm and egg, and my aim is to show that the argument establishes less than it is ordinarily thought to. I give the text below and then say what I want from you.

The case that does the work. Consider the familiar pair of questions. Could you have been conceived from a different pair of gametes, one that in fact produced someone else, your sibling say? Most people find it easy to answer no and hard to say why. And could you have grown from your actual gametes into someone utterly unlike yourself in character, memory, and history? Most people find it easier to answer yes, or at least to feel that the question is not absurd. The asymmetry is striking: a difference in origin appears to be a difference in who, whereas a total difference in the resulting life appears to be a difference in how things went for whoever it is. Origin essentialism takes this asymmetry at face value and treats it as a datum about metaphysical necessity.

I want to propose two cases that put pressure on that reading. First, the Gamete Lottery. Before you are conceived, you are offered a choice. Option A: you are conceived from the gametes that in fact produced you. Option B: you are conceived from a different pair of gametes, but the resulting person has your entire subsequent history, your body, your memories, your attachments, the whole qualitative career, with the difference confined to the first instant. Call B the transplant world. Then consider the reverse, Option C, in which your actual gametes unite but a cosmic ray rewrites the genome, so that the resulting person shares almost nothing qualitative with you. If origin fixes identity, B contains a stranger who merely resembles you and C contains you, badly off. That is precisely the asymmetry above, and the Gamete Lottery only makes its cost explicit: identity comes apart from everything we care about when we ask who someone is. A defender will say this is a virtue rather than a cost, since identity was never supposed to track what we care about. Granted. But notice what is doing the work in B. The question of which one is you has no answer available except the one we supply by rule, because everything that could be inspected, body, memory, dispositions, is stipulated to be the same on both sides. Origin answers the question because we have decided that it answers the question.

Second, the Sibling Swap. Suppose the gametes that produced you produce your sibling, and the gametes that produced your sibling produce you, with the resulting lives swapped along with them: your sibling's life unfolds from your gametes and yours from theirs. Who is who? Two tracking policies are available. Follow the gametes, and you are the person living your sibling's life; follow the life, and you are the person living your life, conceived from different gametes. The origin essentialist must take the first policy. But the first policy is now plainly a policy: a rule for answering a question that the facts underdetermine, chosen because it is determinate rather than because the world forces it. That, I claim, is the whole content of the necessity that the modal argument reports. We keep track of persons across worlds by origin because origin is the only tracking device that returns an answer in every case. The necessity is a projection of the determinacy requirement internal to our practice, not a feature of persons.

Why this matters, and the reply I expect. I do not conclude that origin essentialism is false. I conclude that the modal argument cannot establish it, because its premise concerns our individuative practice and its conclusion concerns the world, and the principle that licenses the move, namely that our tracking rules answer to joints in nature, is assumed rather than defended. An objector will say this is verificationism in modal dress. I am not denying that there is a fact of the matter about identity across worlds; I am denying that the argument gives us a reason to affirm one side of it. A second objector will say that I have shown only that the case is underdetermined, and that underdetermination of evidence is not indeterminacy of fact. Granted. But then the argument needs an independent reason to think that origin fixes identity, and the intuitions it relies on are exactly the practice under dispute, so the argument is circular. A third worry is twinning: on the zygote version of origin essentialism, two twins share an origin and so would be one person, which is absurd; on the gamete version, the origin is incomplete until the fission is included, and once we include the fission we are including the developmental path, which raises the question of why we stop there rather than at the whole causal history back to the beginning. I would like help seeing how far this third worry can be pressed.

What I want from you. First, an assessment of whether the distinction I draw between what the case shows and what our practice supplies is stable, or whether it collapses into a distinction without a difference. Second, the weakest step in the argument and the strongest objection I have not considered. Third, whether the Sibling Swap genuinely generates a dilemma or has an obvious resolution I have missed, and if so whether that resolution costs the origin essentialist anything. Fourth, a judgment on whether the paper is interesting or merely a restatement of a familiar deflationary line, and if the latter, what would have to be added for it to be a contribution. Fifth, whether my opening intuition pump begs the question by describing the asymmetry in terms that already presuppose the tracking policy I later criticize.

Please write as a referee would, telling me what to cut, what to strengthen, and where I have mistaken a verbal point for a substantive one. Do not supply references, citations, or suggestions for further reading; I am interested in the argument rather than the literature.
