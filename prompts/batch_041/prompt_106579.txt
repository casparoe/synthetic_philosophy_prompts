An encyclopedia that anyone can edit instructs its contributors that the test for including a claim is verifiability — whether a published, reliable source states it — rather than truth. Contributors are told not to add what they personally know to be the case unless a source says it, and not to delete a sourced claim merely because they believe it to be false; yet the project still describes its articles as aiming at accuracy and treats some sourced claims as unacceptable.

I want to understand the conception of truth that this practice presupposes, and I am struck by an apparent inconsistency: the practice can require the inclusion of a claim its editors believe false and the exclusion of one they know to be true, while still treating accuracy as a goal. Reconstruct the implicit theory. Does the practice require a substantive, correspondence-style notion of truth that it merely declines to make operative, or does it treat truth as nothing more than warranted assertibility by reliable sources? Consider a deflationist account, on which truth is not a substantive property but a device for endorsing or generalizing over propositions, so that there is nothing more to a claim's being true than its being the case. If that account is correct, is a rule that privileges verifiability over truth even intelligible, or is it a category mistake, given that verifiability is a property of our evidence while truth is a property of the claim? Does the practice presuppose that truth and justification are separable — so that a claim may be true yet unsourced, or sourced yet false — or does it collapse the one into the other?

Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and mark the points at which it is philosophically underdetermined, where a deflationary theory and a robust correspondence theory would license the same practice. Then say what would have to change in the practice under deflationism, and what would have to change under a correspondence theory that treats truth as a substantive relation between claims and the world.
