In my philosophy of language seminar we have been working through Frege's distinction between sense and reference, and my lecturer used the telescope analogy to motivate the claim that senses are objective rather than private psychological items: the moon is the reference, the real image formed by the objective lens is the sense, and the image on the retina is the idea. The point, as she put it, is that the sense is something public and shareable in the way the real image is, whereas the idea is private and varies from observer to observer; the analogy is what licenses us to treat a sense as the sort of thing two people can grasp in common rather than as a mental particular belonging to each of them.

Please audit that analogy. I would like a table of correspondences first — moon, real image, retinal image, observer, telescope, and whatever else you think belongs on either side — and then an account of where the mapping breaks down. I am especially interested in three candidate disanalogies, and I would like you to say of each whether it is real and whether it matters for the use the lecturer made of the analogy: first, that a real image is a spatio-temporal, causally produced particular while a sense is supposed to be neither; second, that a real image is about the moon only if something interprets it, so it may be a poor model of a mode of presentation at all; third, that the analogy pictures the sense as sitting in a causal chain between the subject and the object, which invites reading it as an intermediary entity rather than as a way the object is given.

Then tell me whether the conclusion — that senses are objective and shareable — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time. If a better analogy is available, offer one and say what it loses. If you think the question as I have posed it rests on a misreading of what the analogy is for, or if the analogy is not in fact used in the way I describe, say so plainly rather than working around it; I would rather be told that my framing is confused than receive a tidy answer to the wrong question.

Maximum 800 words.
