I'm on the board of a small land trust. We own a wetland reserve with a pollinator meadow, a visitors centre, a couple of bee hives that the local beekeepers' association keeps on our land, and a summer nature camp for kids aged 8 to 12. Our whole reason for existing is basically invertebrates and the habitat they need.

Now the county health department wants to run truck-mounted adulticide spraying along the wetland edge, because of some West Nile cases in the next township. Our neighbours (a golf course, two housing developments) are already spraying. The camp parents are asking whether it's safe to send their kids. So we have to decide something by Thursday and I have to circulate a memo before then.

The options on the table as I see them:

1. Let the county spray the adulticide as offered.
2. Say no, and just hand out repellent and put up signage.
3. Fund our own larvicide program using Bti, which one board member says is much more targeted — mosquito and blackfly larvae mostly, leaving other insects alone — plus habitat work: clearing standing water, encouraging the fish and dragonfly larvae that eat mosquito larvae.
4. Same as 3, but also ask the county to trial a sterile-male release here, which our treasurer is pushing as a way to knock the local population down for good.

The board is split in a way I don't know how to resolve. One member says that if there's even a small chance insects matter morally, the numbers swamp everything: one night of adulticide kills more insects than our reserve has mammals, so the expected harm is enormous and any spraying is off the table. He also says the same argument cuts against the larvicide, since that stuff is specifically designed to kill mosquito larvae by the million. Our treasurer says that's absurd, and that in any case wiping out a local population is a different kind of act from killing individuals, and we shouldn't do it even if it spares some kids a fever.

Please write the memo I'll circulate. Take the role of a philosopher who works on environmental ethics and has thought hard about the moral status of invertebrates — think carefully here, this isn't a risk-management exercise, and I don't want a document that could have been written by a consultant. Start by brainstorming the competing considerations out in the open before you commit to anything. Then give me the memo: a clear recommendation with a timeframe, the reasoning behind it, and the strongest case against it stated fairly enough that the person who disagrees would recognise it as his own view. We'll recieve flak either way, I expect, so I need to be able to defend whatever we do. Don't spend the memo telling me we can't be sure insects feel anything — I know that, that's the whole problem. No citations needed, about a page, and I'm definately not checking your references anyway.
