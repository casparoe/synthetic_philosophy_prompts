ok so for my phil of mind seminar we just finished the unit on the eastern stuff and our professor spent basically a whole lecture on Schrödinger's side hobby, where he argues that consciousness is only ever found in the singular and that a bunch of seperate minds is somehow a paradox (I think he calls it an arithmetical paradox? I'm paraphrasing) and that this lines up with the Vedanta idea that there's really only one consciousness and the many-ness is a kind of appearance. For our creative response assignment I have to write something and I want to do a fake advice column, so could you write it for me?

Here's what I'm picturing: an agony-aunt page in an invented glossy magazine, deadpan the whole way through, in that warm confident voice these columns always have. Call the magazine The Witness and the column "Ask the Witness", and sign it "— Maya". Around 650 words. Format: an editor's note at the top, then three readers' letters with the columnist's replies, then one short editor's note at the bottom (you can decide what that one deadpans about — I had the idea that the magazine has merged its letters desk with its answers desk because they were always the same department, but do whatever lands).

The bit I actually care about: the jokes have to come from getting the Advaita right, not from a vague we-are-all-one-man vibe. A reader who actually knows this stuff should definately be nodding, not wincing. So the columnist should be using the real machinery correctly, without ever lecturing about it: the witness-consciousness versus the ego (so the view isn't solipsism, and she should be able to say why in about one line), the rope mistaken for a snake, the two levels of truth — everyday/empirical versus absolute — which is how she gets to absorb any objection by redescribing it, and the deep-sleep state where there's no plurality at all (maybe someone asks what happens to the one when you're unconscious). Have the three letters each press a genuine objection that the doctrine has to field, and let the columnist field them in character:

1. Someone asking, basically, isn't this just solipsism with extra steps, and if so why should I care about anyone.
2. The sharper one: if there's only one consciousness and it's by its very nature awareness and bliss, then whose confusion is my confusion? Who exactly is ignorant here? (She should have an answer, and it should be the kind of answer that makes you notice the doctrine is now describing the problem instead of solving it — but she never notices that.)
3. A reader who's really taken it to heart and now can't tell whether helping someone else is generosity or just self-care, and whether the difference was ever real. Let the reply be serene about this. A reader should feel that something got quietly lost.

Keep her breezy and certain, never wink, never break character, no scare quotes around her own doctrine, and don't put the target of the joke in her mouth — I want the reader to be able to tell what's being poked at without being told. The target is the genre of metaphysical comfort writing that can dissolve any objection by re-describing it, and the way we're-all-one gets used as a mood rather than a claim — not the tradition itself, and not any real person. One of the letters should be about something totally mundane so it doesn't float off (a roommate who won't do the dishes, a parking ticket, whatever) and the columnist should answer that one with the exact same cosmic calm as the others. No real names of scholars or books, the magazine and everyone in it is made up.
