Here's what set it off. My supervisor told me to randomize the animals into the two conditions, and when I asked why that's enough, she said: once you've randomized, you can just go ahead and treat the groups as the same in every respect except the treatment. Which obviously isn't true — with eight animals a side the groups will differ in a hundred measured and unmeasured ways. What the randomization actually buys you isn't equality, it's a licence to reason *as if* the thing I didn't measure is independent of the thing I did assign. And once I saw it that way I started noticing the same move everywhere. In my undergrad physics labs we just assumed the errors on repeated measurements were independent and nobody ever argued for it. When I shuffle a deck I treat the deal as independent of the shuffle, as if the order were unrelated to anything about me or the table. Someone in my reading group said that a suspicious-looking coincidence in a dataset isn't evidence of fraud, because you'd have been equally suspicious of a thousand other coincidences. Same shape every time: I have no reason to think these two things are related, therefore I'll treat them as unrelated, and then build everything else on top of that default.

What I can't figure out is what that default actually *is*, and whether it has a name of its own. I've come across the principle of indifference and the maximum-entropy thing, and they feel adjacent but not the same — indifference is about spreading probability over possibilities I've already listed, whereas this is about a relation between two variables, and the entropy rule seems to want me to already know what the constraints are before it can say anything. I've also seen people shrug and call it just an independence assumption, but that's a description, not a theory: it doesn't tell me when the assumption is earned and when it's a computational shortcut that happens to be convenient. Randomizing the animals feels earned in a way that "eh, the errors are probably independent" doesn't, and I can't articulate why, and that bugs me.

So here's what I'd like, and please don't just hand me a label and stop. Is there any formalisation in which it comes out as a theorem rather than a heuristic, and if so what does the theorem actually say, and what does it assume?
