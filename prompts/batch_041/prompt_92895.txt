I'm a novelist, and the last third of my current book turns on an argument four characters keep having about whether historical atrocities can be ranked — and at the moment all four of them sound like they are saying the same thing in different tempers. You are a philosopher of value who has worked on comparability, incommensurability, and the ethics of comparison; think carefully before you answer, and begin by brainstorming the logical space rather than listing famous names.

Build me a decision tree of the positions on whether historical atrocities can be ranked. Each branch node must be a question my characters could answer without already knowing which position it commits them to, and each branch a claim rather than a label. Grow the tree from the space itself, not from a roster: first whether "worse than" is a determinate relation between evils at all; then, if it fails, what kind of failure it is — in our knowledge of the values, in the values themselves, or in there being a third relation on which the two are comparable yet neither better, worse, nor equal; then whether the obscenity of ranking is a distinct reason not to rank (a constraint on what it is fitting to do) or merely a report on what ranking expresses, or on who has standing to speak; then whether the unit ranked is the event, the suffering, or what the event means to a community; then whether any surviving scale is cardinal, merely ordinal, or permits one evil to outweigh any number of lesser ones.

Give me ten to fourteen leaves. For each: a name (or "unnamed" if nobody holds it), one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and one line of dialogue it would put in a character's mouth. Where the plot forces a ranking — dividing a finite memorial fund, say — note which leaves can accommodate that and which have to bite a bullet. Flag any pairs of leaves your tree cannot separate, and diagnose the combinations of answers that no position occupies: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Don't cite anything; I want the space, not the literature.
