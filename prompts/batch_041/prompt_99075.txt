I am a retired experimental biologist — four decades at the bench, mostly in developmental genetics — and since retiring I have been reading philosophy with more enthusiasm than discipline. I have just worked my way through Daniel Dennett's contrast between cranes and skyhooks, and his related charge of greedy reductionism, and I would like your help turning it into a piece of sustained satire. Please approach this as a professional philosopher of biology with a talent for deadpan parody: think the distinction through carefully before you write, and sketch your plan and the angles you intend to use before you begin drafting.

The target is a style of explanation rather than any person — the assumption that once the lowest-level facts have been named, everything has been explained, and that each intervening level (chemistry between physics and biology, cell biology between molecules and organisms) is mere scaffolding to be kicked away. Write a deadpan internal document from a fictional research institute that has taken this assumption to its logical conclusion and is accordingly dismantling its middle-management divisions: a reorganization memorandum, with a subject line and numbered sections, roughly 600 to 700 words. The humor must arise from the actual content of the ideas rather than from invented absurdities — the difference between a reduction that genuinely does explanatory work and one that skips the very processes doing the work, and what an institution is then compelled to posit when the gaps it has opened cannot be closed from the bottom up. Never wink at the reader; the memorandum should proceed as if entirely reasonable, and it should be legible to someone who knows the literature without ever becoming cute about it.

Do not cite, allude to, or depend upon any real publications, dates, or specific texts. Finish with a short, sober note addressed to me, outside the document, stating where the piece takes liberties with these concepts or leans on a contested reading of them — and be definite about the interpretive choices you made, giving your reasons for them, rather than laying out alternatives and leaving me to choose.
