Ok so I'm a novelist — mid-career, three books, none of them medical — and I'm about eighty pages into a new one set inside a hospital ethics comittee, and I keep hitting the same wall: every character talks about "disease" and "health" as if we all already agreed what those words mean, and we really don't. My cardiologist character thinks disease is just biology gone wrong; the disability-studies consultant on the panel thinks it's all politics; and my protagonist (a palliative care nurse, if it matters) keeps drifting toward some in-between thing I can't articulate. I don't want to read a textbook. I want a tool.

So: could you build me a decision tree / taxonomy of the actual positions on what makes something a disease? The deliverable is a branching instrument, not an essay. Every branch node should be a question my characters could answer about a concrete case without already knowing which camp it drops them into — no node that just asks "so are you a naturalist or a normativist?". Every leaf gets a plain-English name for the position plus one sentence on the commitment that's hardest to swallow.

A few things I need it to do:

- Sort on cases, not labels. Please build it around cases like: a condition that's extremely common in older people yet still gets treated as an illness (male-pattern baldness, high cholesterol); a condition that was called a disease and then stopped being one with no change to the biology (I'm thinking of how homosexuality was declassified, and older examples like drapetomania); a state that's statistically unusual but causes no harm whatsoever; a perfectly healthy person who merely carries a pathogen or a risk gene; ordinary grief versus the newer prolonged-grief diagnosis; whether aging itself counts as a disease; and someone whose body is atypical who flatly refuses to be "treated" for it.

- Make the branches split on the disagreements that actually matter: whether dysfunction is measured against a species design or against mere statistics, whether harm has to enter the definition at all, whether the concept is descriptive or evaluative or both, and whether there's one concept or several.

- I want the leaves to include at least: a hard naturalist view where disease is value-free biology; a hybrid view that needs both a biological malfunction and a harm; a hard normativist view where "disease" is nothing but a value judgment; the view that health is really about the organism's capacity to adapt to its own environment and set its own norms; a pluralist "there are several legitimate concepts for different jobs" position; and an eliminativist "let's scrap the word" position. If some position on your tree is one nobody actually defends, leave the branch in anyway and tell me it's empty and why — unattractive, incoherent, or just unexplored.

- Also flag any two leaves your tree can't really seperate, because I'd rather know than have a fake branch papering over it.

Keep it small enough to hold in my head — maybe 10-14 nodes and 6-8 leaves. Audience is me: a novelist with a decent liberal-arts background and zero medical training. And no citations, no reading list — I'm on a deadline and I won't chase them down.
