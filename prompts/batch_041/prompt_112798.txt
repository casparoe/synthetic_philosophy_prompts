Consider a mathematical practice in which researchers posit entities called bridge-numbers. Bridge-numbers are introduced only to solve certain equations; they are never observed, and the theory treats any permutation of them as yielding the same empirical predictions. Yet the equations they solve are indispensable for deriving those predictions. A nominalist says bridge-numbers are useful fictions; a platonist says they are abstract objects. Give an example of a conceptual issue that this disagreement raises about mathematical explanation, and explain why the issue is not settled by the fact that the practice works.
