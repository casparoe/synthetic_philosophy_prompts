You are grading a midterm in an undergraduate comparative-politics course. The exam question and one student's answer appear below. Grade the answer and give the comments that justify the grade.

Exam question: The fictional Republic of Valdoria has the following constitutional provisions. (a) A president is directly elected for a fixed five-year term and appoints and dismisses the prime minister. (b) The prime minister and cabinet must retain the confidence of the unicameral Assembly; the Assembly can remove the government only by a constructive vote of no confidence that names a successor. (c) The president may dissolve the Assembly only after two failed constructive votes within a year. Classify the regime and assess its expected stability relative to a textbook presidential system and a textbook parliamentary system. Use the course handout's three functions — authorization (how office is conferred), sustainment (what keeps an officeholder in place between elections), and removal (how an officeholder is ousted early) — and state which function does the most work in distinguishing the two textbook types. The handout argued that the textbook contrast turns primarily on whether sustainment and removal are fused with the legislative majority.

Student answer: Valdoria is best classified as a presidential system. The directly elected president with a fixed five-year term is the defining feature of presidentialism, since the president's mandate comes straight from the voters rather than from the Assembly. Because the president both appoints and dismisses the prime minister, the government is accountable to the president and not to the legislature, so the sustainment function operates vertically rather than through parliamentary confidence. The constructive vote of no confidence is the one parliamentary holdover, but it makes removal easier than in a textbook parliamentary system: requiring the Assembly to name a successor simply means it can replace the government whenever a majority prefers any alternative, so the government is more vulnerable, not less. This is presumably why the constitution adds the dissolution check after two failed votes — the framers worried that an unstable Assembly would churn through governments. Relative to a textbook parliamentary system, Valdoria trades legislative responsiveness for executive stability; relative to a textbook presidential system, it is less stable because the prime minister can be removed mid-term without the president leaving office. On the handout's three functions, removal does the most work in distinguishing the two textbook types, since the fixed presidential term is just a special case of how office is authorized.
