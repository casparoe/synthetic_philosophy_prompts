I'm not an academic, I just read a lot on my own and I've been in a tenants' rights group for about six years, which is where most of my thinking about this comes from. A friend lent me a short piece by Simone Weil, a French writer who died in 1943 and who spent a year working in car factories on purpose so she'd understand what workers actually lived like. In it she argues that political parties should be abolished outright. Her case is basically that a party is a machine for whipping up collective emotion, that it's built to put pressure on how its own members think, and that its real aim, whatever the banner says, is always its own growth — so a party can never really serve truth or justice, and being in one quietly wrecks your ability to see the people on the other side as anything but enemies.

I nodded along the whole way through, but then I went back over groups I've been in that have nothing to do with parties, and now I'm not sure what her point is exactly. Our tenants' group burned an entire meeting on the wording of one leaflet when the real fight was about who gets to be the one who decides. The housing co-op I lived in had a member who kept asking annoying factual questions, and after a few months people just stopped answering her emails. Even a community choir I sang in — a person quit because the one who picked the songs kept picking hers. Weil writes all this up as a disease of parties, but I've watched it happen in alot of groups that just have to keep making decisions together and have some shared identity to protect. So either she's wrong that there's anything special about parties, or she put her finger on something real and then aimed it at the wrong target.

I'd like an essay on this that actually lands somewhere. If the rot is generic to groups rather than specific to parties, is her diagnosis just too narrow, or is it right about the mechanism and wrong about where it lives? And what about the way out she seems to be gesturing at — she wants people to stop organizing around a program and a side and to keep their attention on what's actually true and on particular wrongs and particular people instead — does that survive once you've decided the disease is generic, or is it just the same thing with fewer people and more self-satisfaction? Dont end on 'both sides have a point.' Tell me what you actually think is going on, and what it means for someone like me who wants to keep doing the work without turning into the exact thing she's warning about.
