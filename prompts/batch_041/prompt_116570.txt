Last week in a product review I proposed surveying our users about which feature they want next, and a colleague shut the idea down with a line I have now heard three times: watch what they do, not what they say. He added that this is just revealed preference and that it is settled economics. I have spent enough time in dashboards to feel the pull of it, but I want to know exactly what I would be conceding if I accepted it. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making, from the trivial to the strong, and for each say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or simply false? I am particularly interested in which readings are definitional rather than empirical, whether any reading actually rules out asking users what they want as opposed to merely ranking it below observed behavior, whether the slogan presupposes that each user has a single stable ranking of wants that their clicks express, and which reading my colleague is most likely relying on. Please end by saying what follows for the decision actually in front of us: should we run the survey, and if the slogan does not settle that, what would?
