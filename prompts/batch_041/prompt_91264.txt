I host a podcast on social theory, and my next guest is a social scientist who argues that "generations" — boomers, millennials, Gen Z — are real social kinds rather than journalistic packaging of ordinary age differences. Draft eight to ten interview questions that make her defend the ontology rather than restate the demographic story. I want at least one question on each of the following, kept distinct rather than folded into one vague prompt: what she means by a "kind" here, and what would be lost if generational labels were replaced by a continuous birth-year variable plus period effects; the identification problem — since cohort is period minus age, no dataset separates the three without an added assumption — and whether she regards that as a technical nuisance or as a reason to doubt that "the cohort effect" is a well-defined quantity at all; the contrast between a generation as a shared location and a generation as an actuality that has taken shape around formative events, and whether the contemporary labels track the latter or only the first, with a follow-up in case she answers that the labels start as administrative conveniences and then become real because people act on them, asking what that concedes to her critics; the fact that differences between cohorts are normally much smaller than differences within them, and what explanatory payoff a category earns if it predicts less than it appears to; and the political stakes, given that the generational frame is now used to assign blame over housing, debt and climate. Give a follow-up for each question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Don't hedge: if one of these lines is a dead end, drop it and say why, and commit to the single question you think she cannot answer.
