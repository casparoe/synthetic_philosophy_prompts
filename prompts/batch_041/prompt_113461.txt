I've been thinking on my own about whether an ant colony can have a mind, and I've settled on the view that it can't: when we say a colony "decides" to move or "recognizes" an intruder, that's a useful shorthand and there is nothing it is like to be the colony. What I don't count as an objection is that a colony is made of mindless parts, since I'm made of cells that aren't conscious either; what I lean on instead is unity -- a mind is a single point of view, and the colony seems to have none. Tell me what would count against this, ranked from the considerations that should genuinely move me to the ones that would be merely awkward. I want to know, in particular, what it would take for a colony to meet the unity test I'm relying on -- some overall state running through all the ants the way a mood runs through a person -- and whether that's a real possibility or just a manner of speaking; what my view implies about a colony being split into two that each carry on, or two colonies being put together into one, given that I take it a person can't divide or fuse; and whether I'm quietly denying minds to anything with replaceable parts, myself included. Tell me which of these my own side would have to concede rather than explain away, and whether my view risks anything at all or has been insulated by how I've put it.
