Ok, Ive been chewing on a view that gets called tranquilism and I want an essay on it. Here's the view, roughly: the only things that matter intrinsically are the absence of craving and the absence of suffering. Pleasure, knowledge, love, achievement, beauty -- none of it is good in itself; it's good only insofar as it doesn't stir up craving, and a lot of it is just craving in a nicer outfit. Strip out the craving and the suffering and you've got everything worth having. Nothing else.

What I want the essay on is the spot where I think the view either gets interesting or falls apart. Craving is the thing to be eliminated. But wanting to be free of craving is itself a craving -- the purest one, since it's aimed at your own inner state. So the view seems to recommend a state you can only get to by doing the thing it says is bad, and every step toward it is powered by the very stuff you're trying to get rid of. And it gets worse: if craving-absence is what's good, why does it matter how the absence came about? Someone tranquil because they've given up on everything, someone asleep, someone dead -- all of them have craving-absence, and the dead one has it permanently and can't relapse. So can the view say the thirty years of practice were worth anything? Or is working toward tranquility incoherent on the view's own terms?

Second thread, and I want it in there: if the good is an absence, and absences don't need anyone around to be true, what stops the view from saying a universe with no sentient life in it is the best universe there is, or that the endlessly many people who never exist are all doing beautifully? Push on that too.

I want you to land somewhere. Don't list four readings of craving and say much depends on which one you pick. Tell me whether tranquilism survives these two, and if it does, exactly what it has to give up to survive -- a patch, not a vibe. If it doesn't, tell me which of the two kills it and whether anything nearby lives (say, a version where craving only matters when it's actually felt as bad, or where the good is the process of unwinding rather than the absence at the end). Take a real swing. I'd rather have a wrong verdict than a survey.
