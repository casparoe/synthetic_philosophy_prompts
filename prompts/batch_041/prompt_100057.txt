Write the deadpan official instructions for Form DS-1, the petition by which a field of inquiry is certified as science or reclassified as pseudoscience, for a fictional national standards bureau. The piece should read like a real government form drafted by a mid-level civil servant -- numbered sections, tick-box criteria, a field where the petitioner lists the theory's testable predictions, and a notes area for the reviewing officer -- roughly 600 words, with the comedy coming entirely from the substantive criteria and never from jokes about bureaucracy or winks to the reader. Let the form's questions quietly enact the actual demarcation debate, so that a philosopher of science recognises a bureau that has absorbed the literature and still cannot complete its own paperwork: the falsifiability criterion, together with the twin embarrassments that a horoscope issues checkable predictions while an untestable mathematical theory of everything is still physics; the progressiveness or degeneracy of a research programme; the difference between abandoning a refuted theory and rescuing it with an ad hoc patch; and what, if anything, a community's consensus adds to a claim's status. State none of the objections outright. Let the reviewing officer's marginal notes on four sample petitions carry the reductio: an astrologer, a group defending Fomenko's New Chronology (the claim that the conventional ancient and medieval timeline is largely fabricated and internally duplicated), a cosmologist defending an untestable multiverse, and a folklorist petitioning to have Atlantis formally demoted.
