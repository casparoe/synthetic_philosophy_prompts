ok so I'm a third-year phil PhD working on formal ethics / decision theory, and my reading group has this ritual where someone writes up their whole philosophical profile and everyone else gets to take a swing at it, and it's my turn in a couple weeks. Here's roughly where I've landed, in no particular order:

(1) Impersonal totalism about value: the value of an outcome is just the sum of the welfare in it, welfare is cardinal and interpersonally comparable, and adding a life worth living always makes things better. No diminishing marginal value in numbers of people -- a billion happy lives is exactly a billion times as good as one.
(2) I accept the VNM axioms and I don't think that's up for negotiation: I want my preferences representable by an expected value, and I don't want to be Dutch-bookable. So rational choice just is expected value maximization. And I don't think risk aversion is a seperate demand of rationality; it's just concavity in your utility function.
(3) Strong verdict on a case: offered a sure decent life versus a lottery with a 1-in-10^30 chance of a life 10^100 times better and nothing otherwise, I take the sure thing, and I don't think that's a failure of nerve or a limitation I should apologize for. I think preferring the sure thing there is what a rational agent does.
(4) I'm a longtermist: the expected value of the far future is astronomically large and that's basically the whole reason to care about it, and it's why I give to x-risk stuff rather than, say, bednets.
(5) I'm allergic to the obvious escapes from (3). Capping the value of outcomes at some finite ceiling feels arbitrary (and I have a nagging feeling you can always sweeten a lottery enough to break any cap anyway), and I don't much like probability weighting or maximin-style rules either -- they seem to throw away information I'd rather keep.
(6) In ordinary life I take the sure fifty bucks over a coin flip for 0 or 120, I buy insurance, I don't walk home through the sketchy park at 2am to save ten minutes.
(7) I'm a totalist but honestly the repugnant conclusion strikes me as repugnant, and I'm not happy about having to swallow it.
