Write me a dialogue about this puzzle I keep chewing on. Flash a dot on the left of a screen in red, then a fraction of a second later flash a dot on the right in green. People don't report seeing red-then-green — they report a single dot that travels across the gap and turns green somewhere in the middle, which means they seem to have experienced the colour change at a moment before the second dot was even on the screen. Two people I argue with about this have different fixes. One says the brain just edits the memory after the fact: at the time you really did see red, then green, and the travelling-and-changing version gets written in afterwards. The other says no — the brain holds the first dot in a buffer, waits for the second to arrive, then plays you the finished film, so the travelling dot was genuinely experienced, just a little late. (Apparently the effect depends on the timing: leave a long enough gap and people just see two seperate dots, which each of them treats as evidence for their own side.) Write the dialogue between the two. Neither should be a pushover — I want them responding to each other's actual moves rather than talking past each other — and I especially want them to fight it out over whether any experiment could ever tell the two stories apart, or whether they're just the same claim said twice.
