Draft the joint statement that a committed declinist about violence — someone who holds that the world has become substantially less violent and that the rate per capita is the right measure — and a committed conceptual skeptic — someone who holds that the trend claim is an artifact of how violence is defined and counted, and that the question may lack a determinate answer — could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Proceed in three parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: what any long-run trend claim presupposes (a harm-equivalence function mapping killings, injuries, enslavement and preventable deprivation onto a common scale; a denominator, persons-at-risk or person-years; a baseline, all deaths or excess deaths), and what both accept about the record — that it is unevenly preserved, and that rising state capacity to record can raise measured violence while actual violence falls, so a flat measured trend is compatible with a decline. Second, a parametric formulation both can sign: the family of harm-weightings, denominators and baselines each side regards as reasonable; the region of that space in which the sign of the trend flips; each side's assessment of whether that region is large and whether it contains options it considers defensible; and what happens to the sign if the scale is extended to include omissions and preventable deprivation. Third, a disagreements table: one row per dispute, each position stated in wording its own side would accept, the cruxes listed with each side's prediction about what the evidence will show recorded in advance, and for the crux both agree would actually be decisive, a concrete case or comparison both accept as bearing on it. State explicitly which parts of the dispute are verbal — in particular whether the quarrel over whether there is a fact of the matter reduces to whether violence is functioning as a natural-kind term or as a stipulative scale — and which are substantive.
