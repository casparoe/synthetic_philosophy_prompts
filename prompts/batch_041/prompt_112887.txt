I don't have any academic training in this area, but I've spent a fair amount of time thinking about generations and I'd like to write down where I've landed, mostly to find out whether it holds together. Here is my position as clearly as I can state it.

1. Generations are real. When people speak of Baby Boomers or Gen Z, they aren't just reaching for marketing labels; they're picking out genuine groups of people who really are alike in ways that matter.
2. What makes a generation is a shared stretch of growing-up years — roughly the events and conditions someone lived through between about ten and twenty-five. That's the glue.
3. Because of that shared upbringing, members of a generation should resemble one another more than they resemble members of other generations, at least in outlook and habits.
4. Age and stage of life explain more of people's behavior than generation does. A twenty-five-year-old today and a twenty-five-year-old in 1985 have a great deal in common simply by virtue of both being twenty-five.
5. The boundaries between generations are soft. Nobody becomes a different person on their birthday, and someone born right at a boundary can plausibly belong to either side.
6. When I say something like "Gen Z is more anxious," I mean it as a claim about those people — that they are a certain way — and not merely a claim about the times we are all currently living through.
7. My rough test for whether a generation is real is this: if two strangers of about the same age met and compared notes on what growing up was like, they would recognize each other as having come from the same world. If they wouldn't, there's no generation there.

I'd like a careful audit rather than reassurance. Which of these commitments actually pull against one another, which only appear to, and how serious is each genuine conflict? For each real one, say what would have to be given up in order to fix it and what that concession costs me. Please rank the conflicts from most to least serious rather than treating them all as equally bad, and finish by telling me the single change that would resolve the most at once.
