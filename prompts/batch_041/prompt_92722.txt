Okay so I've been lurking LessWrong and adjacent blogs for years, and I'm now doing that thing where you reverse-engineer your own gut into a rule — this time about iterated elimination of dominated strategies in games. I have alot of verdicts on specific little cases and I want the principle that ties them together, or proof there isn't one. Here goes.

Case 1: a strategy that's strictly worse than some other pure strategy no matter what anyone else does. Obviously it's out. Case 2 is the one that started all this. Row picks Top / Middle / Bottom, Column picks Left / Right. Top pays 1 against Left and 0 against Right; Middle pays 0 against Left and 1 against Right; Bottom pays 0.4 against either. Bottom is not beaten by any single pure strategy (it beats Top vs Right and beats Middle vs Left), but the 50/50 coin flip between Top and Middle pays 0.5 no matter what, which beats Bottom's 0.4 no matter what. My gut says Bottom is out — a lottery that always does better than you means you're gone. But notice my gut is asking for *mixed*-strategy domination, since no pure strategy kills Bottom. Case 3: back in that same game, Top is the best reply to Left and Middle is the best reply to Right, so I want both kept, obviously, even though each is terrible against the other move. Case 4, the one I keep flip-flopping on: I've read that if you allow elimination by weak domination (only ever at-least-as-good, sometimes strictly better) the order you eliminate in can change what survives and can even wipe out everything — which definately feels like a bug in the procedure rather than a fact about the game — whereas for strict domination I've read the order doesn't matter and the surviving set is unique. Am I right to want to restrict the principle to strict domination for that reason? Case 5, which I can't verify and don't fully understand: I've seen the claim that with three or more players a strategy can be "never a best response to anything the others might independantly do" and yet still not be dominated by any mixed strategy, because the others' play could be correlated in a way no product of independent mixes captures. If that's real, then "throw out everything that's never a best response" would discard more than "throw out everything that's dominated," and Cases 1–3 alone wouldn't tell me which of those two I actually want.

So: give me the cleanest principle that fits Cases 1–3 (and 4, if you agree with me), stated precisely, and say whether it should be phrased as "strictly dominated by a mixed strategy" or as "never a best response," and whether those two come apart in two-player games or only with 3+. Tell me what it predicts for a game I haven't listed, and which of my verdicts, if any, it forces me to give up. My tentative stab is: eliminate a strategy just when some mixed strategy over the *remaining* strategies does strictly better against every pure strategy the others can pick — I want to know whether "every pure strategy" should really be "every mixed strategy / belief," and whether restricting the eliminator to the remaining strategies even matters. And please don't just validate me: if Case 4 or Case 5 rests on something I'm misremembering, or if the whole "find the principle" framing is off, say so plainly. I'd rather get corrected than get a tidy-sounding rule that quietly drops a case.
