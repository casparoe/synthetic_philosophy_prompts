You are a philosopher of mind who is also an unusually gifted teacher, and I would like your help planning an explanation rather than receiving one. I am a journalist who writes about science and ideas, and once a month I host a ninety-minute salon for a room of curious non-specialists: no philosophical training, but widely read and quick to object. Next month I have committed to the distinction between original and derived intentionality, using Dennett's two-bitser as one pole and Searle's contention that only genuine minds possess intrinsic intentionality while everything else merely borrows it as the other. Please assume I already understand the distinction; do not explain it to me. Plan how I should explain it.

Begin by brainstorming: before you commit to anything, set out the candidate entry points into the topic and the candidate examples that might carry it, and reason about which of them fit an audience that has no prior vocabulary yet bristles at being condescended to. Only then give me the plan itself.

I want the order in which the components have to be introduced, and why — what must be in place before the question even makes sense to them. Does the notion of aboutness come first, or the idea that a mental state can be correct or incorrect about something, or the contrast between representing and merely functioning? For each piece, say what it depends on and what depends on it.

I want the single example or case that should carry the whole session, and I want you to think hard about which it ought to be. Should it be the two-bitser itself, a thermostat, a chess program, a photograph, a parrot, a bee's waggle dance? For whichever you choose, tell me what it must have and what it must conspicuously lack — and tell me why the obvious candidates, the Chinese Room and the bat and the Martian whose inner states we cannot interpret, are the wrong carriers for this room at this stage, if in fact they are.

I want the misconceptions my audience will predictably bring or form — my guess is that they will collapse intentionality into information or into representation in the computational sense, and that they will hear derived as meaning fake — along with the precise point in the sequence at which each should be headed off and how I should head it off there.

I want one question I can put to the room near the end that would reveal whether the distinction has actually landed, rather than whether they can repeat a slogan of Dennett's or Searle's.

And I want to know what I should openly refuse to explain at this level — the material that would only muddy the water — and to say so out loud.

Please think carefully and work through this step by step before you answer.
