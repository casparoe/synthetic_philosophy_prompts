I retired last year after the better part of forty years as a marine biologist, and I've spent most of the time since trying to teach myself philosophy — badly, and mostly in bed with a pencil, driving my poor wife up the wall. Here's what I need. My oldest friend and scientific sparring partner is retiring at the end of the month and I've been asked to give the toast at his dinner. We spent the best part of three decades arguing about essentially the same question — in print, at conferences, and very loudly in the pub — and neither of us ever shifted an inch. I've just stumbled on Aumman's agreement theorem and it's unsettled me a bit. As I understand it (and I may have this wrong), if two people are both rational, start from the same prior beliefs, and each knows what the other knows, then they can't end up disagreeing — their views have to converge. Which would seem to mean that either one of us has been an idiot for thirty years, or we never actully started from the same place. I'd like the toast to turn on this. It needs to be warm and funny and honest, not a lecture, five minutes at most. The room will be roughly a third scientists who know the theorem inside out, a third his family who've never heard of it, and the rest old friends who'll be listening to see whether I slip in anything barbed — so it has to land for all three without losing anyone. And there's one thing I read somewhere that I'd love it to make something of, if it can without bogging down: apparently the theorem only really bites once the disagreement is *common knowledge* — i.e. when we both know we disagree, and know the other knows, and so on up. In all those years we never quite had that; I just assumed he was being obstinate, and I suspect he assumed the same of me. The honest reading, I think, is that we had different priors and there was no fact of the matter, so neither of us was being irrational — I'd like that to come through without me having to say it outright. Can you write it?
