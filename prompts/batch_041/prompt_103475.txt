ok so before i read any more takes on whether AI art is "creative," i want the full spec first. everyone just grabs their favorite example and argues from it. i want the checklist that any adequate theory of creativity has to pass -- the cases it must classify right, the puzzles it has to dissolve, the distinctions it cant collapse, the facts about how making actually works that it can't contradict -- with the case behind each item. then the part i really care about: flag any two requirements you think cant both be met at once, i.e. where buying one forces you to give up another. and tell me which single requirement does the most work, in the sense of knocking out the most otherwise-popular answers. dont score the actual theories, i'll do that myself.

here's the pile of cases i keep tripping over, use them:

- a diffusion model spits out an image no one has ever made and it's genuinely good. rerun it with a different seed and you get a different good image. is the first output creative? is the seed? is the model? is the person who typed the prompt?
- a forger paints a flawless fake. technically superb, zero novelty, and yet calling it uncreative feels wrong somehow.
- a compiler finds an optimization nobody wrote into it. new, useful, nobody intended it.
- a chess engine plays a move that's never appeared in recorded play and wins with it.
- natural selection arrives at the eye. no agent anywhere in the loop.
- two people independently write the same song the same week.
- a four-year-old's drawing that's a copy of a copy, but it means something to her.
- a model writes a poem that feels fresh to me even though it's basically a statistical average of everything it read.
- a random mutation of a known painting, kept only because it happened to look nice.
- someone has a genuinely novel dream.
- a system that can only make things it was trained on, but recombines them in combinations no one has seen. combinatorial novelty -- does that count?

so: what does a theory of creativity have to get right? think cases it must classify correctly, distinctions it can't collapse (novel vs valuable, product vs process, agent vs event, intended vs lucky, degree vs kind), and the empirical facts about how humans actually make stuff that the theory can't contradict -- skill, iteration, tradition, collaboration, the fact that most real creators work inside a genre and borrow constantly. with the case behind each requirement. the bar is a theory good enough to actually settle the AI-art fights, not just a vibe. a numbered list is fine, and keep the weight on the criteria and their cases rather than on any theory you happen to like.
