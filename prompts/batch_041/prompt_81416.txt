Here is my opening move. Consider a choice between two options, A and B, and suppose that I refuse to trade A for any lottery that gives me a nonzero chance of ending up with B, and I refuse, symmetrically, to trade B for any lottery that gives me a nonzero chance of ending up with A. I insist that neither option is worthless and that neither is infinitely valuable. Your view says a trade rate exists, so it must class me as preferring A, preferring B, or being indifferent. If I prefer A to B, then some small chance of losing A ought to be worth a large chance of gaining B, and I will not accept that. If I am indifferent, I ought to be willing to swap one for the other outright, and I will not. So tell me which of the three I am, and what exactly my refusals are a mistake about.
