Right, so I've got a question I'd like to run past someone who actually knows this literature, because I've been reading a lot of Rorty since I retired and I've ended up somewhere I can't tell is stable or if I'm just fooling myself. I spent forty years as a geologist — mostly basin modeling, some field mapping — so my instincts are pretty stubbornly realist (rocks are rocks whether or not anyone's got a theory about them), but the Rorty stuff has gotten its hooks into me. Here's what I'd currently say I beleive. (1) Rorty is basically right that we should stop thinking of true sentences as things that "correspond" to a non-human world, or of knowledge as a mirror of nature; our vocabularies are tools for coping, and there's no God's-eye standpoint from which to check them against the world as it is in itself. (2) Truth is just what survives inquiry — what we can't be argued out of under as free and open a conversation as we can manage — not a property that floats free of our practices. (3) I'm also a deflationist about truth: calling a claim true is just endorsing it and saying ditto, it doesn't tack a further property onto the claim. (4) All that said, I still think the earth-science story is a case of getting closer to how things actually are — the drift people were onto something real even when they had no mechanism and everyone thought they were cranks, and plate tectonics isn't just a more useful vocabulary than the geosyncline business, it's more nearly right about a world that was already doing its thing back in the Jurassic. (5) And I think the fact that the whole edifice works — that we can find oil, sometimes forecast quakes, land the InSight probe — is best explained by those theories latching onto real structure. (6) I don't think the world is just whatever we say it is; it pushes back. You can't make a well produce by adopting a nicer vocabulary about the reservoir. (7) Finally, and this is probably the one that doesn't fit, I think moral progress is real — abolition, say, wasn't just a change in what people were willing to say, it was getting something right.

I've read alot of secondary stuff and suspect some of these pull against each other, but I honestly can't see which. Could you audit it for me? Which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are — I have a hunch (6) is fine and the trouble is somewhere else — and for the real ones, how bad is the damage and what's the cheapest way out, plus what I'd be giving up to take it. Rank them by severity instead of just telling me everything is in tension with everything. And if there's one single thing I should drop to clean up the most mess, say which.
