I sit on the naming and public art commission of a mid-sized city. Every time we propose to rename a street or relocate a monument, someone at the hearing says that we are erasing history. I have never been able to tell whether that is a prediction about what will happen, a claim about what a monument or a street name essentially is, or a way of saying that they dislike the decision. Please set out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making, ordered from the nearly trivial to the ones that would need real defense, and say of each whether it is true, false, or indeterminate, and whether it would actually give the commission a reason to refuse. I want a menu I can bring to the next meeting: which reading is in play when the phrase does genuine argumentative work, which is doing nothing but signaling opposition, and whether what we are proposing is best described as erasing a historical record, overwriting it, or simply ceasing to maintain one particular commemoration.
