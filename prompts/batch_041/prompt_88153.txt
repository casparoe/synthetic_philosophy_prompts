I am preparing a term paper for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of mathematics, and the paper is to be on Skolem's paradox. Before I commit to the long version, I would like your comments on the following compressed sketch of my argument, which is the whole of what I have so far. The sketch runs: (i) by the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem every consistent theory in a countable language has a countable model, and ZFC is such a theory, so ZFC has a countable model M; (ii) ZFC proves that the power set of the natural numbers is uncountable, so M contains a set which M takes to be that power set and which M believes to be uncountable; (iii) from the outside, M is countable, and so is that set; (iv) the received resolution denies that there is any contradiction here, on the ground that when M says uncountable it means only that no bijection onto the natural numbers of M is itself an element of M, and the external bijection is simply not in M; (v) I want to press further, that the resolution is correct but unstable, since it treats M's domain as a determinate collection whose size we may report from outside, when the outside is only some further model to which the same relativity applies — so either we halt the regress by fiat, helping ourselves to an absolute universe we cannot describe in the very language we are using, or we concede that countability is not a property of collections but of collections-as-described-by-a-theory, and I mean to take the second horn; (vi) as a sharpening of (v) I add that the external bijection is not merely absent from M but not definable over M either, since otherwise replacement inside M would make M's power set of the natural numbers countable in M's own eyes, and this seems to me to show that the resolution's witness is one that no one, inside or out, can actually point to; (vii) I concede that ascending to full second-order logic blocks the paradox, since the downward theorem fails there and the natural numbers can be pinned down up to isomorphism, but I regard that as a change of subject rather than a solution, since we lose a consequence relation we can control; and (viii) I note that transitivity is no help, since a countable transitive model has only countably many reals in it.

What I want from you is comment on that sketch, and I am equally interested in being told that a step is confused as in being told that it is sound. Please lead with a one- or two-sentence verdict on whether the argument survives as it stands, and then organise your remarks under three headings: first, accuracy, meaning anything in (i) through (viii) that is false, misleading, or stated in a way that invites an obvious objection, with the correction supplied; second, the instability thesis, meaning whether (v) is a real philosophical point about the received resolution or a verbal illusion, and if it is real, what its strongest formulation would be, and whether I am conflating the relativity of cardinality with something else — with the indeterminacy of the domain, or with the indeterminacy of reference of the sort that Putnam's model-theoretic argument turns on; third, what I have left out, meaning the replies available to someone who thinks the received resolution is complete and needs no instability thesis at all.

Keep the whole reply under eight hundred words. I would rather have sharpness on two or three points than coverage of everything.
