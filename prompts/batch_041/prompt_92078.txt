An acquaintance who works on the foundations of mathematics has over the years given the following verdicts on appeals to Gödel's incompleteness theorems outside their original setting. She assents to the claim that no consistent, effectively axiomatized formal system can prove every truth of arithmetic. She denies that the theorems show the human mind cannot be a machine. She denies that they show a complete axiomatization of physics to be impossible. She denies that every formal system is incomplete, since some are complete. She denies that an inconsistent system must be incomplete. She denies that a computer cannot model the mind because it cannot grasp the truth of its own Gödel sentence. She is now asked about a fresh case: a consistent, effectively axiomatized formal system whose theorems are exactly the truths about the natural numbers under addition and order, but which has no way of defining multiplication. Predict her verdict on whether the theorems force this system to contain an undecidable sentence, reconstruct the rule she seems to be running, set out the rival rules that fit her earlier verdicts just as well, say what each predicts about the new case, give your best guess with your reasons for it, and name the further case that would most cleanly separate the rivals.
