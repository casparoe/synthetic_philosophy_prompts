Right, I've definately talked myself into a view about escalation of commitment and I want to know whether it's actually any good or whether I'm just fooling myself.

Here's the view. When people keep pouring money into something that's clearly going badly — bidding past the value of the prize in a dollar auction, a government shovelling cash at a doomed project — we normally call it the sunk cost fallacy and treat it as a bug in the brain. I don't think that's right. In the cases that actually matter, the person is responding to the incentives right in front of them. In the dollar auction the runner-up still has to pay, so once you're in, raising your bid really is the lesser evil, and I've read that the whole thing is basically a war of attrition, where the sensible play is exactly the thing that looks like madness. So the trap is in the rules, not in the head. Sunk cost stuff is real, but I reckon it's a much smaller part of the story than everyone says.

So: what would count against this?
