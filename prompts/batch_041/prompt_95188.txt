Ok so every fall I teach this grad seminar on non-constructive existence proofs, and every fall Zorn's lemma is where I lose them. They can all recite it back — every chain in a non-empty poset has an upper bound inside the poset, therefore the poset has a maximal element — and not one of them feels how wierd that is. It hands you a thing it never builds, and it isn't even the top of the pile; it's just somewhere nothing else in the pile beats. There's that old line about choice being obviously true and well-ordering obviously false and nobody having any intuition for Zorn, and that's exactly the gap I want the poem to fill. I'm going to close the session with a poem, and I've definately decided it has to be terza rima, because the form is the lemma.

Here's what I mean. The rhyme scheme is the chain. Every tercet's middle rhyme drags the next tercet out of you, on and on, which is exactly what the proof does: suppose there's no maximal element, then for anything you're holding onto there's something strictly bigger, and off you go again. And the form can't run forever any more than the proof can — end it the way Dante ends a canto, with that one last line that ties off the whole rhyme-chain. That closing line is your upper bound, and the poem should feel the relief of it. Around 20 lines.

Three things I actually care about:

1. Don't name or describe a particular maximal element. The lemma gives you existence with no witness, and if the poem produces a definite object sitting at the top it's lying about the theorem. The end should be a place the chain stops, not a summit.
2. Somewhere in there, register that the whole thing is empty if you start from nothing — no elements, no chains, no bounds, no conclusion. A poem that's all ascent and no floor is a poem about the empty set.
3. If it fits without strain, one tercet can nod at the fact that this is the axiom of choice in different clothes (choice, well-ordering, Zorn — one content, three outfits). Don't belabor it.

And please don't just versify the statement of the lemma. I want a poem, not a mnemonic. The person in the seminar who already knows the lemma should find it right, and my partner, who does philosophy of art and has never seen a poset, should find it a good poem. Avoid the words poset and maximal element if you can manage it — make the language do the work.
