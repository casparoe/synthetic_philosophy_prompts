My friend is finishing a dissertation on Kant and has somehow become the person everyone asks about animal ethics. Here's the track record I've assembled from arguements with her:

- kicking a dog that's tied up because you're in a bad mood: wrong, and she doesn't care that the dog is somebody's property
- shooting a stray dog that's going for your kid: fine
- a farmer who keeps chickens in decent conditions and kills them quickly: fine
- the same farmer cutting the chickens' beaks off to save money: not fine
- bullfighting, canned lion hunts, that kind of thing: not fine
- a kid who's caught a mouse and is poking it with a stick for fun: not fine
- poisoning mice in your kitchen wall: fine
- a classroom demo where a live mouse is cut open without anesthetic so students can see what a heart looks like: not fine
- the same procedure with anesthetic, in a study that might actually help someone: fine
- smashing up a gravestone when there's no family left to care: not fine, though she says it's a different kind of not fine

Next week she's on a panel about a company that wants to sell a robot pet. Its a plush dog with motors in it, but it yelps and cringes when you hit it, and it's convincing enough that people wince. The panel has to decide whether it's OK to sell the thing, and whether a guy who buys one specifically to kick around is doing anything wrong.

I also want to know what she'd say about a case I made up to annoy her: a man tortures a cat, once, and it's independently verified that it leaves his character exactly as it was and that nobody will ever find out.

So: what does she say about the robot dog, and what about the cat? I'm less interested in her answers than in the rule she's running. I can see at least two that fit everything above -- the one where cruelty is bad because of what it does to us (our sympathy, our habits, the way we get used to treating living things as tools), and the one where something is owed to the animal itself even though it can't claim it. Tell me what each of those predicts about the robot and about the quarantined cat, which one she's probably running given the list, and which of the two cases would actually settle it. And if neither rule fits and she's running something else, say what that is.
