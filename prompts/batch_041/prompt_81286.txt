Ok, I'm building a small collection of satirical bits and I need one written. Premise: a mid-size data analytics firm in Columbus, Kestrel Data Services, ran an internal pay audit two years ago (they call it the Fairweather Review) and found that salaries tracked a whole lot of stuff nobody chose — your parents, your school district, whether you happened to be standing near the good project when it got handed out. Instead of burying the report like a normal company, they went the other way and scrapped merit pay entirely in favor of desert-based compensation. Your quarterly pay is now just your ablation score: they re-run the quarter with you taken out (or with a stand-in hired from the applicant pool, because the baseline you pick changes the number, but that's what the Baseline Committee is for and their minutes are sealed) and pay you the revenue difference. There's also a Luck Ledger every employee has to keep, an Effort Surcharge, and a Residual Escrow account that's been quietly growing for eight quarters and is now bigger than the firm's annual revenue, which nobody wants to talk about.

What I want is the new-hire FAQ for the scheme — about 700 words, formatted as Q&A, written by the Head of People Operations, relentlessly upbeat, sincerely proud of the firm, deadpan, no winking at the reader. The jokes have to come from the arguments being right, so someone who's read the meritocracy/desert literature laughs instead of wincing, and someone who hasn't still follows every clause. So the answers should really engage with: the regress where your effort turns out to be something you didn't choose either, the difference between deserving a thing and being entitled to it under a rule, the fact that your marginal contribution depends on the rest of the team and on what the market will pay for the product, the worry that once you strip out everything unchosen there's nobody left to pay, and the objection that the scheme punishes exactly the people it's supposed to be fair to. Don't let it resolve any of them — every answer delivered with total confidence, and the reader does the recoiling. Work in a question about the Residual Escrow, since every new hire asks about it, and a seperate one about the Luck Ledger.

Two more things. It should include at least one clause that eats itself (something recursive about the effort surcharge) and one covering the employee whose ablation score comes out negative and who therefore owes the firm money. And every position the FAQ takes should be one a sincere desert skeptic or luck egalitarian could actually endorse — the absurdity should come from following the logic, not from the company being staffed by idiots. End with a one-line footnote from Legal that gives the game away, and describe any philosophical view in plain terms rather than naming philosophers or works; I don't want it citing anyone, real or made up. I'm intrested to see whether the upbeat tone holds all the way to the footnote.
