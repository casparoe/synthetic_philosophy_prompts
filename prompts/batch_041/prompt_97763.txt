For most of my adult life my views about abortion have been settled, and I believed they were coherent. I am now four months into an unplanned but welcome pregnancy, and the coherence has not survived contact with the situation. I have been trying to write down what I actually believe, and the list is longer and less tidy than I expected. What I want from you is an audit, not a defense, so please be blunt.

Here is where I have landed.

1. I take bodily autonomy to be the ground of the permissibility of abortion, and I take it to be decisive even on the assumption that the fetus has the same moral standing as a born child. A person is not required to lend her body to sustain another's life against her will; that is what Thomson's violinist is meant to establish, and I accept the conclusion.

2. I also accept the future-like-ours account of why killing is wrong: what makes killing a person wrong is that it deprives her of a valuable future. On this account the fetus is the sort of thing that can be wronged by abortion from quite early on, because it too has such a future ahead of it.

3. I think the moral status of the developing human being increases continuously from conception onward, and that there is no sharp moral threshold anywhere in the process — not at implantation, not at viability, not at birth. Birth strikes me as morally unremarkable in itself.

4. I think infanticide is exactly as wrong as murdering an older child, and I would reject any view that treats birth as a morally transformative event.

5. Nevertheless my practical judgments are graded. First-trimester abortion I would defend without hesitation. Late abortion — say after twenty-four weeks, absent a serious threat to the woman's health or a grave fetal diagnosis — I find very hard to justify, and I am not uncomfortable with laws that restrict it.

6. I do think it matters morally that a pregnancy resulted from voluntary sex. I would not criminalize abortion on that basis, but I think a woman who became pregnant through a choice she knowingly made owes something that a woman who was raped does not.

7. I am not religious and do not believe in souls. Whatever status the fetus has, it has in virtue of the kind of thing it is and will become, not in virtue of anything infused into it.

What I want to know is whether this set hangs together. Some of these look to me as though they might be pulling against each other and some may only look that way; I cannot tell which is which, and I would rather not be told that everything is fine. Please sort the list: which pairs are in genuine tension and how severe each tension is, which apparent conflicts dissolve on inspection and why, and, for each genuine tension, what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. Order the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if I abandoned it, would clean up the most — and do not assume in advance that it is one of the ones I stated most confidently.
