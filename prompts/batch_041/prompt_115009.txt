the setup i'm using, paraphrased: Judy is dropped into territory she can't see, divided into Red and Blue, and each half is divided again into a Second Company area and a Headquarters area. Four cells, she starts uniform over them. She gets the officer on the radio and he tells her that if she's in Red territory, the odds are three to one that she's in the Headquarters area. So the message constrains a conditional credence of hers: given Red, Headquarters is three times as likely as Second Company. The message isn't an event in her algebra. There's no cell of her partition corresponding to the officer having said that, and conditionalizing on anything she already has credences in won't reproduce the change, because what she learned is a fact about a ratio inside one half of the partition rather than a proposition she had a prior for.

my main line: i think the puzzle is an artifact of an underspecified likelihood model, and specifically of a mismatch between the officer's map and Judy's map. Concretely, the message is phrased in terms of Red and Headquarters as his map draws them, and her map is coarser or just different at the edges — there's a strip that she files under Blue and he files under Red, or under Second Company where he'd say Headquarters. If that's right, the constraint she's been handed doesn't even live in her algebra. It lives in a finer space, and any rule that minimizes the distance from her prior subject to the constraint is silently choosing a projection from the fine space down onto her coarse one. Different ways of representing the fine-grained possibilities give different answers, and i don't think anything in the story privileges one. So the relative-entropy rule isn't a rule of inference here, it's the shadow of an implicit likelihood model nobody wrote down, and once you write it down the case is just conditionalization on a richer event.

second thing i want to argue: the fact that the message arrived at all is evidence. Judy's radio presumably only works in some places, or the officer only transmits when he's got something to say, or she only gets reception in range of the Red relay. If reception is correlated with territory then the event of receiving this message is itself informative about Red vs Blue, and any treatment that takes the content as the only thing learned is going to be wrong in a way that has nothing to do with conditionals. i'd like to make the stronger claim: every version of the case where the update looks non-conditional is a version where we've smuggled in a reception model and then refused to conditionalize on it. tell me if that's too strong. i suspect it is and i want to know exactly where it breaks.
