I've got a pattern I keep running into and I don't have a word for it, so I'm hoping you can tell me if there is one.

Setup first. A friend runs a betting game with two urns. Urn one has a hundred balls in it, fifty red fifty black, and we all watched him count them in. Urn two also has a hundred red and black balls, but nobodys counted them and he won't say what the mix is. You put money on a colour and draw.

With both urns sitting there, I'll take urn one every time. And if he then offers me the same bet on the other colour, I'll still take urn one. I can see perfectly well that both of those can't be the smart play if urn two has some actual fixed proportion of red in it, but that's what I do, and I do it every time.

Now take urn one off the table. Put only urn two in front of me, and I'll bet on it without a second thought. The squirminess is basically gone. Its not that I've gotten braver, it's that there's nothing to compare it against.

Second thing. If instead he lets me choose which colour I'm backing *after* I've picked the urn, I flip, and now I'd rather have urn two. And if he's smiling because he knows whats in it, my stomach drops and I won't touch it — same with a mechanic who says he knows exactly what's wrong and won't say, versus one who shrugs and says he'll find out when he opens it up. But if nobody could know, if the balls were scooped blind out of a bin that's since been thrown away, I'm back to being fine with it.

So the thing I'm chasing isn't just that I dislike not knowing the odds, because that doesn't explain why the dislike comes and goes. What seems to set it off is (a) having a nice clean gamble sitting right next to the murky one, and (b) the possibility that someone else is holding the information I'm missing.

Is there a name for either half of that, or for the whole cluster? People keep saying ambiguity aversion at me and I nod, but that seems to cover the first half and not the second, and it says nothing about why the murkiness stops bothering me once the clean urn leaves the room. What are the nearest actual concepts — I'll take two if it takes two — and where exactly does my description not line up with them?
