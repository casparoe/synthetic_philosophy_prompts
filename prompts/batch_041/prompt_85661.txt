I've been lurking on rationalist and EA blogs for years and I keep circling back to wild animal suffering, so I figured I'd write down where I've actually landed and see if it hangs together, because I have a nagging feeling it doesnt and I cant see where. Here's the package: (1) I'm a welfare consequentialist — the only thing that matters intrinsically is sentient welfare, positive and negative; species, ecosystems, biodiversity have only instrumental value if they have any at all. (2) I think wild animal suffering is plausibly the biggest problem in the world: the sheer number of animals and the way most of them die (starvation, predation, parasites, exposure) dwarfs anything humans suffer. (3) I think it's quite likely that the average wild animal has a net negative life — more suffering than wellbeing — though I know that's contested and I dont have a knockdown argument for it. (4) I love nature and I think habitat destruction is a tragedy; I donate to conservation groups and I'd be gutted if the rainforests went. (5) I think we should be extremely cautious about intervening in ecosystems to reduce suffering, because they're complex and top-down interventions usually backfire in ways we dont foresee. (6) I accept a total welfare view: the value of a population is the sum of the welfare of its members, so more happy lives is better and more suffering lives is worse.

Now when I follow this out I get some uncomfortable results. I have a vague sense that my conservationism in (4) and my caution in (5) might not sit well with (1)-(3) — if habitat destruction reduces the number of net-negative lives, isnt it a welfare improvement? — and the world-exploder thought experiment really bothers me: if I take (6) seriously and (3) is right, then a device that instantly and painlessly ended all sentient life would be a good thing, or at least not bad, which is horrifying and seems to clash with (4). But I also suspect some of this is only apparent — e.g., maybe (1) and (5) arent really in conflict because consequentialism under uncertainty just is caution. Can you audit this? Which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only look tense, rank the genuine tensions by how bad they are, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. I dont know which one I'd give up, so dont assume.
