ok so I want a short story built around the no-trade theorem, and I want the theorem itself to be the engine of the plot, not just decoration.

Quick version of the idea so we're on the same page: if a market is in equilibrium, there are no noise traders or other irrational types mucking things up, and the whole apparatus by which traders get their information is common knowledge, then nobody can actually profit from private information — even though people really do hold private information. The reason is that the act of trying to trade leaks it. You bid, and I know you're rational and that you know I'm rational, so I read your bid as evidence, and it's priced in before I even accept. Anyone offering me a trade must know something I don't, so why take the other side and be the mark? Everyone reasons the same way, so no trade happens. It's a deliberately idealized result, but take it seriously as a premise.

The story: a world where this result has become common knowledge in the strong sense — everyone knows it, everyone knows everyone knows it, it's in the textbooks, it's on posters. And the unintended consequence is that the price system quietly stops doing the one thing it's supposed to be great at: aggragating the scattered, local knowledge of millions of people who each know some little thing nobody else does. Why bother acting on your private scrap of info if the second you try, the price absorbs it and you get nothing? So people stop, prices go stale, capital gets allocated worse and worse, but you can't point at any single bad decision beacuse locally every single actor is behaving perfectly rationally. The failure is systemic and invisible.

Give me a protagonist inside this — maybe a market maker, maybe someone at a regulator or an exchange, maybe just a person whose life depends on prices being informative — who works out that the only fix is to un-common-knowledge the theorem. To make the market work again you'd have to restore the belief that trade is actually possible, or convince people that not everyone has read the memo, i.e. you'd have to make people somewhat wrong about the world in order for the world to get tracked correctly. The story should turn on that decision.

Constraints: under ~2500 words, one or two scenes, roughly contemporary setting, no need for sci-fi. Don't have anyone explain the theorem out loud to the reader — let the reader piece it together from what happens. No lecture at the end, and no paper titles or author names. The dilemma should be genuinely hard; don't hand the protagonist an easy out or a clean moral.
