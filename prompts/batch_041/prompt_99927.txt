Having spent a career in physics and come to philosophy only in retirement, I have been trying to make sense of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and I think I follow it well enough to be troubled by where it leaves us. As I understand it, the relativity of simultaneity shows that there is no frame-independent fact about which distant events are happening now, and this refutes presentism and establishes that the whole of spacetime is equally real, so that the block universe is simply what special relativity was telling us all along. What I cannot work out is the practical upshot: if my future actions are already as real and settled as my past ones, is deliberation anything more than an epiphenomenon, a ritual I perform while the outcome sits fixed in the block? And since the argument seems to turn entirely on the fact that simultaneity is relative to a frame of reference, why should that be thought to bear on what exists rather than merely on how we choose to slice a four-dimensional whole? I should be grateful if you could straighten me out on this.
