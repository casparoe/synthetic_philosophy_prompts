Ok so I've been reading moral-trade stuff for years now — the basic idea that two people with different values can both come out ahead if each does something the other cares about — and I keep hitting the same wall: everyone agrees the core move is neat, and then the conversation just dies at the meetup level. I've got a small pot of money (roughly two theorists for three years, or a reading group plus a couple of workshops, whichever looks better) and I want to aim it at the open questions that actually matter here, not the ones thats fun to argue about over beer.

So what are the main open questions, and which two or three would you prioritize? Stuff I keep bumping into and can't tell if its deep or trivia: whether a trade even counts as moral if the concession is something you were already obligated to do anyway (you can't trade what you already owe); how either side verifies the other actually did the thing, given both are promising to do something they'd otherwise never do, which smells alot like the additionality problem in carbon offsets; whether you can trade over the rules of future trades, like a standing pact about how you'll resolve your next disagreement; what it means to trade with someone whose values you think are monstrous — complicity or the whole point?; whether a third party could run a clearinghouse matching trades at scale and take a cut, and whether that changes the legitimacy of the trades underneath; and whether any of it survives when one side is a future generation, an AI, or some hypothetical agent that can't sign anything.

Rank them: which is upstream of which, which are tractable for a couple of philosophy people with no lab and no field experiments, and which would actually change what people do if we settled them — versus which are just neat puzzles where the answer wouldn't move anything. And tell me what evidence over the next couple years would make you reorder the list. I want a defended agenda, not a survey.
