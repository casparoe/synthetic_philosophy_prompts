I've got this side project, its an app called Sovereign: you write a decision down in advance (quit smoking, no life support, cap my trading losses, whatever) and the app enforces it later, on the version of you who will definately want to weasel out. The whole pitch is that the person who signed is the person who decides. I want a parody FAQ for the landing page — deadpan corporate support page, the kind with numbered questions and answers — that takes our own pitch all the way to its conclusion and never blinks.

Here's the thing though, I want it to be funny to someone who actually knows the philosophy here, not just lol future me is lazy. So it has to run on one principle and stay perfectly consistent with it, no winking anywhere: the signer is sovereign, and any later change of mind is a malfunction of the mechanism, not a decision. Under that rule, answer stuff like:

- what if the later me has information the earlier me didn't have
- what if the later me is clearly competent and says no
- what if the later me is seperate-ly happy in the exact state the earlier me paid to prevent (she's content now, does that count for anything)
- what if the later me says she isn't the same person, or doesn't remember signing
- one that turns on whether the earlier me was protecting something she cared about at the bottom of her life (not being kept alive on machines) versus something she just happened to enjoy (eating meat) — I feel like those should come out differently and I want to see how you handle that without cheating
- two directives from different times that contradict each other
- who decides whether the later me is competent, us or her

Then a short known issues / changelog section at the end where we log changes of mind as bug reports and give them ticket numbers.

Around 650 words, numbered Q&As, customer-support voice, and please no philosophy jargon and no philosopher names — no autonomy, no precedent, none of that. The whole joke is that we're saying something wild in HR language. Don't make us cartoonishly evil either; we should sound reasonable, because the creepy part is that our reasoning isn't crazy, it's just applied. A philosopher should wince in recognition, not because we got it wrong.

And to be clear I'm not asking you to settle the actual debate — I know smart people split on whether the current person should get to override the earlier one. I just want the FAQ that takes the earlier self's side all the way down, consistently, so I can see how bad it looks.
