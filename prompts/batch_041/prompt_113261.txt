I sit on the standards comittee of a mid-sized aquaculture certification body. We certify farmed salmon, tilapia, and increasingly shrimp and prawns. Our current animal-welfare standard only covers finfish at slaughter: it requires that fish be rendered insensible before they're killed, and it lists acceptable methods. The question in front of us is whether to extend that clause to decapod crustaceans -- farmed shrimp, prawns, and the occasional crab -- and if so, what we should actually require. The proposal on the table is to mandate either electrical stunning or mechanical destruction of the ventral nerve cord before boiling, chilling, or any other killing method. The industry reps say this is expensive, impractical at scale, and that the science on crustacean pain is still unsettled. One of our scientific advisors says the evidence is suggestive but not conclusive, and that we shouldn't regulate on vibes. Another says the fact that we can't prove they don't suffer is exactly the reason to act, especially when the intervention is cheap relative to the value of the certification. There's also a worry that if we move too fast, farms will just leave the scheme, which would reduce our leverage and maybe make things worse. And someone pointed out that our own finfish clause is already pretty weak on stunning, so there's a hypocrasy angle. I need a short written decision memo I can circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete policy -- yes, no, or a phased/tiered version -- with a clear implementation timeline, and it should engage the strongest argument on the other side rather than just asserting that shrimp are sentient. Also, please say something about how we should handle the uncertainty itself, because that's really the crux: we're not deciding whether shrimp definitely feel pain, we're deciding what to do under deep uncertainty about it. Keep it under 1000 words if you can, and make it something the industry reps and the animal advocates can both actually respond to.
