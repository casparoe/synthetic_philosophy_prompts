I'm writing a novel where one of my characters is a political scientist who's gone sour on local politics, and I'm trying to read up so I don't embarrass myself on the page. I came across a piece — I think it was a magazine explainer, or maybe the intro chapter of a pop-econ book — that summed up the paradox of voting like this:

"The paradox of voting was discovered by Condorcet, and it shows that majority rule is logically self-contradictory: a majority can prefer A to B, another majority can prefer B to C, and yet another can prefer C to A, so there's really no such thing as the 'will of the people' at all. This is why rational-choice theorists have shown that a selfish person has no reason to vote — your one vote will never decide anything. And Arrow later proved, with his impossibility theorem, that no democratic system can ever be made to work. The only real mystery is why anyone bothers to show up."

I'd like my character to be able to push back if another character says this, but honestly I can't tell where the truth is. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me what a political scientist or economist who actually works on this would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's just plain wrong? And does the punchline — voting is pointless, democracy is impossible — actually follow from any of the parts that are true?
