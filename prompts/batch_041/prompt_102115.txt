Right, so my mate Priya and I have just spent most of a Sunday arguing about whether video game characters matter morally, and I've come out of it with alot of gut reactions that I can't get to fit together. I'm hoping you can tell me what principle, if any, they add up to.

Here's where I actually land, case by case.

Mowing down pedestrians in GTA for a laugh: a bit rubbish, slightly embarrassing, but not actually wrong. Shooting a guard in Hitman who's basically a walking patrol route: same.

Kicking the dog off the cliff in a game where there's no quest and no reward, I just want to see the animation: that one bothers me more, and I honestly can't tell you why, it's the same pixels and the dog isn't real either.

In a game where the NPCs are driven by a language model, and one of them asks me to stop and then brings it up again the next time I load the save: I think that's genuinely wrong. Wrong even though I'm fairly confident the model isn't conscious and doesn't feel anything. If it's just a script with a beg-for-mercy animation that I can trigger on any NPC, it's less bad, but still not nothing.

A character who only exists in cutscenes, who I can't interact with at all: no moral question whatsoever.

Deleting a hundred-hour save where I raised a character from a kid: feels like a small bereavement. Not a wrong done to anyone though. I don't think I've hurt the character.

Then the ones that broke the argument. Priya reckons that torturing an NPC in a game with no AI at all, pure script, is still not on, and the reason isn't the NPC, it's me, or what it says about the sort of person who enjoys it. Fine. But she definately thinks anything goes with a character who's just text on a screen, and those two seem to pull in opposite directions.

And the old puzzle: virtual murder, fine. The other thing, the one everyone means when they talk about that dilemma, absolutely not, and not just because it's illegal or because someone might see. I can't get that asymmetry out of any of the above.

Last one. A friend of mine made a small game and put in a character modelled on her brother, who'd died a few years before. I messed about with it, made him say stupid things, and felt awful. But that's about her. He isn't there. Or is that exactly the point and I'm just dressing it up?

My own half-baked attempt at a principle is something like: a character matters to the extent that a real mind is behind it, or that a real person is invested in it. But that makes the language-model NPC come out fine, since there's no mind and nobody's invested, and I don't believe it is fine. And it makes the dead brother come out wrong only because of Priya's feelings, which makes it sound like it'd be fine if she never found out. I don't think it would be.

So: is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give one up? If there is one, state it precisely, and tell me what it says about a case I haven't mentioned — say, a game where the NPCs are procedurally generated nonsense but the developer has quietly left in a flag that makes one of them flinch, and nobody playing knows it's there. And if you think I have to overturn one of my verdicts, say which one and why. Don't give me a reading list, I just want the argument.
