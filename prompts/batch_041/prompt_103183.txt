My brother in law sent me one of those moral foundations theory videos over thanksgiving and now every family dinner turns into a two hour fight about whether the psychology of left and right tells us anything about who is actually right, and honestly I'd like to just get the shape of the disagreement straight instead of rehashing it. So: build me a decision tree or taxonomy of the positions on that question — whether the empirical psychology of moral difference has any bearing on the normative question of which side is right.

Two things I care about. First, build it out of the logical space, not out of a list of famous names: start from the questions that actually split the answers, then hang the positions on them, and tell me which combinations of answers nobody occupies, and whether that's because the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Second, I don't want a tree that sorts people by what they call themselves, so no node that just asks "are you a moral realist" or "are you a conservative." Every branch should be a question you could answer without already knowing where it leads — ideally about a concrete claim, e.g. whether the left/right difference in which foundations get used survives controls for education or group membership, or whether the fact that a moral view is widely held could ever count as evidence for it.

The leaves I need to see: (1) the separate-compartments view, where the psychology is interesting but has zero bearing on the normative question; (2) the wider-is-better view, where a morality that honors more of what people actually care about is richer, so the conservative has more of the moral truth; (3) the parochialism reply, where the extra foundations are exactly the morally suspicious ones, so breadth counts for nothing; (4) a relativist/anti-realist leaf where, if morality just is what our foundations spit out, then neither side is right and the question dissolves; (5) a leaf where the descriptive claim itself is shaky enough that the normative question lapses; and (6) some kind of constructivist leaf where the foundations are raw material for normative theorising rather than either a verdict or a debunk. If you think one of those is really two views, split it. If there's an obvious seventh I've missed, add it.

Audience is me, my brother in law and two cousins — none of us are philosophers, we all read alot of pop science, so plain phrasing please, but don't dumb down the distinctions.

And tell me where your map is most likely to be wrong. If two of my leaves are actually the same view wearing different hats, say so; if one of the cells I'd call empty is actually occupied by something real, name it. What would change your mind about the layout — a case you haven't considered, a distinction that collapses two branches, or something in the empirical literature?
