I'm a geophysicist; I do seismic and potential-field inversion for subsurface imaging, which means my working life consists of problems where the data admit infinitely many models and I have to pick one and defend it to a client. My vocabulary, in case it matters: a forward operator that maps a model of the subsurface to predicted data; a misfit functional; a fixed parametrization of the model space; the sensitivity matrix (Jacobian) and its null space; regularization — Tikhonov, smoothness, sparsity — and the regularization weight that trades misfit against roughness; the classical criteria for a well-posed problem (a solution exists, is unique, and depends continuously on the data) together with the recognition that most of my problems fail the second and third; and the practical art of designing a new survey specifically to shrink the null space. Our house rule is that if a feature of the model lies in the null space — if no experiment we can run would change the predicted data when we change that feature — then we say the feature does not exist: we do not report it, we do not argue about it, we do not build a career on it. A colleague in the philosophy department tells me I am living inside the underdetermination of theory by evidence, that philosophers have argued about it for a century without settling it, and that my house rule is a philosophical position rather than an engineering convention. I am not convinced, and I would like the translation done rigorously rather than as a dinner-table analogy.

Please restate the underdetermination debate in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, position by position. The positions as they have been explained to me: that the gap between evidence and theory is always in principle closable with more or better data; that for some rivalries no conceivable evidence could decide, so theory choice outruns what any possible observation could license; that a failed prediction never tells you which of your assumptions to blame, since the hypothesis, the auxiliaries, the instrument model, and the background theory all go down together; and that our confidence in current theories is undermined less by known rivals than by the ones nobody has thought of yet. For each, give me the translation and then an honest accounting of what did not survive it. Where my framework has no counterpart, say so plainly rather than manufacturing one.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually want. I have four specific suspicions to be tested. First, is the philosophical dispute essentially a disagreement about whether the null space of the universe is finite and shrinkable — that is, whether all non-uniqueness is transient rather than permanent — and if so, is that a disagreement that could ever be settled by argument rather than by data? Second, my house rule that null-space features do not exist: does the translation expose it as a substantive epistemic commitment, something like a verificationism about unobservables, rather than the harmless reporting convention I take it for? Third, the holism point — in my practice a bad fit can be blamed on the physics, the discretization, the noise statistics, or the regularization weight, and I resolve it by convention, experience, and trial. Does the philosophical version of holism tell me anything my practice does not already embody, or is my practice simply the whole content of the claim, dressed up? Fourth, and most important: when philosophers insist that two empirically equivalent theories can be genuinely different hypotheses about unobservable structure, is that a claim my framework can even state, given that in my world there is always a true model and the only question is how much of it the data constrain? If it cannot be stated, tell me whether the failure is a defect of my framework — it cannot see something real — or a defect of the claim, which asserts a difference for which no criterion is offered. Throughout, separate what is settled among people who actually work on underdetermination from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus; I would rather be told that the field is split, and how, than receive a tidy answer that turns out to be one camp's line.
