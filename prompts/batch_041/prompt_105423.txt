Ok so I'm putting together a handout for my phil of science students and I want to close the unit on confirmation with a poem instead of yet another problem set, because I think the form can do something the prose can't.

Write me a pantoum about Hempel's raven paradox. You know the drill with the form: lines 2 and 4 of each quatrain come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next one, and the last stanza loops back to pick up lines from the first, so the whole thing ends near where it started. Give me five quatrains, as five numbered stanzas so I can drop them straight into the handout. Rhyme if you can, but dont let the rhyme drag the logic around by the nose.

Here's what I want the form to actually do. I want two lines that keep coming back: one that's a straight positive instance (a raven that turns out black) and one that's the annoying one (a green apple, a red herring, whatever -- some non-black non-raven sitting there on the desk). In a pantoum those lines have to reappear in new positions and new company, and I want the reader to feel the equivalence condition doing its work: the apple line keeps turning up where you'd expect the raven line, and it keeps being, formally, every bit as good. The hypothesis is that all ravens are black; the apple is evidence for it only because it's evidence for the contrapositive, and the poem should make that feel at once airtight and absurd.

Two guardrails. Dont let it slide into Goodman's grue -- that's a different problem (projectibility, not instance confirmation) and if green things need to be weird in the poem I want them weird about the right thing. And dont let the repetition get so dreamy that the logic goes soft: someone who knows the paradox should be able to point at which line is the positive instance, which is the contrapositive's instance, and why they end up side by side.

For the last stanza, land on the deflationary reading rather than a spooky one -- the thought that the apple really does confirm, and the only thing wrong with saying so is how little it confirms -- so the loop back to the opening feels like a shrug. Actually, hmm, leave a bit of sting in it too; a shrug alone is a cop-out and my students will smell it.

Voice: someone alone in a room doing an inventory, counting birds, counting fruit, writing it all down, casually. No preamble from you and no closing note, just the five numbered stanzas.
