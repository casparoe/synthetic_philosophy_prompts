I'm putting together a joint statement for a workhop on two-dimensional semantics and I need help drafting it properly. Set it up as an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been going round in circles for years: someone who takes the framework epistemically -- the diagonal as a genuine space of epistemic possibilities, the a priori knowable stuff, conceivability doing real work -- and someone who takes it metasemantically, for whom the diagonal is just a semantic value that falls out of how the reference of a term gets fixed, and has got nothing much to do with what anyone could know a priori. Chalmers-ish vs Stalnaker-ish, if that helps fix the camps.

What I want:

1. An agreed-claims section, numbered, of things both can actually sign: about the formalism (contexts and worlds, character and content, what the diagonal is supposed to be), about which puzzles the machinery was built to handle (the contingent a priori, the necessary a posteriori, the apparent failure of substitution in attitude reports), and about what it would take for one reading to beat the other. Nothing vacuous, please.

2. A disagreements table: the exact points of difference, each side's view written in wording that side would accept, and a note on which differences are genuine and which are verbal -- I suspect at least one is just the two of them using "a priori" for different things, and I'd like that called out if so.

3. The cruxes, with, for each, something both sides agree would bear on it and what each side predicts. I want these to bite on cases neither can wave away, so use these three, which are definately the ones I keep getting stuck on:
   (a) a term introduced for whatever causes a certain observable effect, where the effect later turns out to have two unrelated causes and no single underlying substance -- what's the intension, what did the introducers know a priori, and was their earlier belief that there was one cause empirical or a priori?
   (b) an empty theoretical term of the phlogiston sort, where the community's stipulation presupposes something that isn't there at all;
   (c) a theorem that's provable but nobody's proved it and it would take a thousand pages of work -- is that a priori knowable, and does the diagonal care?

Also, tell me straight whether the two readings are actually rivals or just two different jobs for one bit of machinery -- that's the bit I most want an answer on.

Don't bother with citations or a reading list, I know the literature. Write the whole thing in British English please.
