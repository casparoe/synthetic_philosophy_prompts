I'm a novelist and I've got a story I can't crack, so I'd rather you just write it and let me watch how you do it. Two people, each keeping a diary of a recurring feeling they insist has no name, each using their own invented symbol for it. They meet, compare diaries, and after a while they manage to agree on one shared symbol — and the thing that actually lets them do it is noticing that they both get the feeling an hour or so before a thunderstorm, so the weather ends up being their check on whether they're using the symbol right. The story has to turn on that: is the word doing any work because of the private feeling at all, or only because of the public occasion, and what does it mean that neither of them can audit their own old diary entries except by memory. The scene I care about most is the one where the occasion is missing — clear blue sky, one of them gets the feeling, the other doesn't — and they have to figure out whether their disagreement is even a disagreement, especially after one of them tries to settle it with a heart rate monitor, wich just hands them a public stand-in instead of a check. Please don't let this turn into a beetle-in-a-box lecture or a tidy summary of the arguments about private languages; keep the philosophy inside the scene. 1200 words or so, and leave it unresolved in a way that feels honest rather than coy.
