I want your credence that deflationism about truth can be made fully adequate, and I want the number decomposed rather than asserted. To keep us from arguing about a moving target, here is the exact position I have in mind. Truth has no substantive nature; the T-schema is the whole conceptual core; general talk of what is true is handled by substitutional quantification over sentences; and wherever truth appears to be doing explanatory or normative work — as in the claim that a community whose beliefs are mostly true tends to succeed, or the injunction to believe what is true — the work is really done by the specific beliefs and their reliability, with the truth predicate merely abbreviating a conjunction of instances. Call this the abbreviation package.

The proposition I want a number for is this: the abbreviation package meets every legitimate demand we place on a theory of truth, so that no explanatory or normative role remains which only a substantive property of truth could fill.

Give me your credence in that, then take it apart. How much rides on whether success-explanations are genuine explanations or mere compressions of instances; on whether substitutional quantification can carry the required generalizations without smuggling a property back in; on whether the paraphrase of the norms of assertion and belief stays deflationary rather than reintroducing a thick notion of correctness; on whether the inflationist alternatives are independently well-motivated; and on whether the package copes with truth-attributions that come in degrees rather than all-or-nothing — for instance when we call a probabilistic forecast accurate. Assign each sub-question a rough probability with one line of justification, say how they combine into the headline figure, tell me which term you are least confident in, and identify the single argument or piece of evidence that would move it most.
