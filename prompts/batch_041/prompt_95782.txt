Mine is that the "looks like the country" bit is doing none of the real work and what matters is that everyone had an equal chance of being picked. We couldn't work out whether we were disagreeing about politics or just about what the word "representation" means, and eventually we just went to bed annoyed with each other.

Here's my half-formed idea, see what you can do with it: I think the literature on sortition runs two justifications together that should be kept apart. On one view, selecting by lot is legitimate because it's a sample — it mirrors the population, so the body's decisions can be read as the population's decisions. On the other, it's legitimate because it's a fair lottery — everyone had an equal probability, so no one was excluded or advantaged, and it doesn't matter in the slightest whether the resulting body resembles the population. These two justifications, I think, pull in different directions on basically every design question: how big the body should be, whether you stratify or quota it, whether you can swap in alternates, whether a tiny body is a defect or is actually fine. And they definately give different answers to the objection that lottocracy has no re-election mechanism to discipline it.
