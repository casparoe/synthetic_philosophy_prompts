I have been asked to give the keynote at the dedication of an immersion preschool for a community whose language is being revived. The room will be divided in a way I have to hold together: the elders and parents are certain that the language carries a distinctive way of seeing the world, and they will hear any hedge as a denial that the language matters; the board's funders and their applied-linguist advisers, meanwhile, regard the strong form of linguistic relativity — the idea that a language determines what its speakers can think — as long refuted, and will hear any overclaim as grounds to cut the grant. I want a single address, about eight minutes long, that makes the case for the school without resting on the strong hypothesis and without treating the community's conviction that the language is valuable in its own right as mere sentiment. Write the address. Then add a short note, clearly marked as separate from the address, saying how confident you are in the address's central claim, where it leans on a contested reading of the evidence, and which single claim in it you are least sure of.
