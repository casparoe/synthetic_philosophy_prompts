I chair the house-rules committee at a 24-flat co-op. Our charter gives every household sole say over what happens inside its own walls — the committee only writes rules for shared space — and that clause is the one thing everyone here actually agress on. Right now two flats are locked in a noise fight and I have to bring a decision to Thursdays meeting.

Flat 5 is Nadia, a cellist who has stopped playing. Flat 9, above her, is Priya, who plays trumpet. I talked to them seperately. Nadia's order of preference is: silence, then her own practising, then Priya's. Priya's is: Nadia practising, then her own, then silence — she says she'd quit tomorrow if Nadia would take it up. So Nadia won't play, because she'd rather have quiet; Priya will, because she'd rather make noise than sit in it; and we've landed on the one arrangement both of them rank below the other. Getting to the arrangement they both prefer means getting Nadia to play in her own flat, which our charter says is none of our business and which no amount of committee goodwill will get her to do.

I suspect this isn't really a neighbour problem, that there's no house rule at all which keeps both of our promises — but the memo has to be something we can vote on, not an essay. Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no opening or closing paragraph: the recommendation and a date by which it should happen, the reasoning, and the strongest thing to be said for the two members who'll argue we should just leave both flats alone.
