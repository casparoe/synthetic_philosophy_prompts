My ethics reading group keeps hitting the same move on advance directives and I want it audited properly. Someone says: a directive is just a Ulysses contract, so of course it binds the later self — Odysseus had himself lashed to the mast and told the crew to ignore him when he begged, and nobody thinks the crew should have untied him. I think the anology is doing more work than it can carry, but I'd rather have the mapping laid out than just assert that.

So give me the correspondence first. What plays Odysseus, what plays the mast and the ropes, who are the crew, what are the Sirens, what's the sea? Then where it breaks, and I care about four things in particular. Odysseus is the same man before, during and after, and he's awake and shouting, so the crew refusing to untie him is him overruling himself with his own endorsement at both ends — whereas with dementia there may be no return, and the person who now wants the telly and the ice cream isn't obviously the person who signed. Second, Odysseus knew exactly what the Sirens did and met the song in real time; the woman signing at 60 is choosing blind about what it'll be like to be 80 and confused, on the strength of other people's descriptions. Third, his crew had wax in their ears — they weren't judging anything, they were executing; my sister and the ward staff have to listen to the later self and decide whether it's really her talking, so the analogy quietly imports an enforcer who never has to make a call. Fourth, the crew are his own sailors under his instructions, while clinicians have duties of their own plus a legal department.

The version I actually need to resolve: the directive says in terms that any later request to the contrary is to be ignored, and the later self isn't distressed at all — cheerful, settled, asking for the very treatment the directive refuses. Can the analogy license overriding that, or did it only ever license the shouting case?

Don't give me a both-sides survey. Take a position: say which disanalogy is the one that does the real damage, whether the conclusion the analogy was brought in to support — the earlier self's directive binds — survives it, and if it doesn't, what you'd put in the analogy's place.
