Write me a short story, 800-1000 words, about someone who takes a pill that makes them logically omniscient in the boring technical sense: everything that follows from what they believe, they believe too, instantly and for free. No computation happens, that's the part I care about -- closure isnt the same thing as seeing. She believes every theorem of arithmetic and can't prove a single one; she can answer any question correctly and can't say why; and she can't tell which of her beliefs are new, so "I just worked it out" is a sentence she can never say truthfully. She isn't smarter or faster or more creative, she just has no gap at all between her premises and everything that follows from them. Two things I want the story to actually do. First, make her fragility vivid: she believes all the consequences of all her beliefs at once, so the moment two of them conflict she believes everything, contradictions and all their consequences included, which makes her the most breakable epistemic agent alive. Second, at the end leave open whether she knows anything at all, given that knowing something is supposed to take more than having a true belief -- you're meant to have some grip on it and she has none. First person, present tense, no philosophers named, and dont have the narrator stop to explain the paradox. Let the scenes carry it.
