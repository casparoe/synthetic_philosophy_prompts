Right, so I'm putting together my 'position' for a reading group next week — mixed bunch, a few philosophers, a couple of social scientists — and the topic is the gender-equality paradox. Could you act as a philosopher of social science who's read widely on the nature/nurture/justice side of this, think carefully, and ideally brainstorm a bit to yourself before you commit to anything?

Here's roughly where I've landed.

(1) I think the gender-equality paradox is real: the richer and more egalitarian a country is, the larger the average sex differences in things like preference for people-versus-things and risk appetite, and the more segregated the occupations get. I don't think that's definately just noise or an artefact.
(2) I think a decent chunk of the average difference in those preferences is biologically grounded — not the whole story, but a real chunk.
(3) But I'm basically a social constructionist about who actually ends up in which job: what sorts people into occupations is socialisation, institutions and expectations, and biology is at most a nudge.
(4) And I think it's plainly illegitimate to wave biology around to justify unequal pay or to tell an individual woman what she's cut out for — justice is about removing barriers, not reading deserts off nature.
(5) So I'm in favour of fairly interventionist stuff — quotas in some places, reshaping how work is structured, early-years measures — whilst also thinking people should be free to choose.
(6) One nagging thing someone put to me: some of the indices that rank countries as more or less 'egalitarian' already fold women's labour-market participation or the sex mix of occupations into the score itself, so if that's right the paradox might be partly baked into the ruler rather than found in the world.

What I want isn't a lecture on the literature. I want to know which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for each real clash how bad it is and what the cheapest way to fix it would be and what that fix costs me. Rank them by severity rather than telling me they're all in tension, and finish by telling me which single thing I should give up or weaken to clean up the most mess. Don't be polite about it.
