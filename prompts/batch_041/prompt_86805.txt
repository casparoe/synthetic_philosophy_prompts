Ok so I've somehow ended up de facto running a small reading group — four of us, all philosophy / decision-theory people, no funding and no compute, meeting for a year — and we've landed on the foundations of Kelly betting and "geometric rationality" as our topic. I definately want us to spend the year on the two or three open questions that are actually load-bearing, not on the stuff thats been rehashed to death (we all know the basic maximize-expected-log-wealth story, the don't-bet-too-big intuition, fractional Kelly as a practical hedge). So: what are the main open questions here, and which should we prioritize, given it's a year of four people's attention and the deliverable is conceptual clarity rather than new theorems?

The things we keep circling, in case it helps you see the shape of our confusion:

- The whole framework seems to assume you know the probabilities. What does growth-optimal betting even mean when you don't — when the distribution itself is uncertain, or you've only got a set of candidate distributions? Is there a principled growth criterion under ambiguity, or does every candidate either collapse back into picking one distribution or stop being growth-optimal in any useful sense? And is that the same problem as Ellsberg-style ambiguity aversion or a different one wearing the same clothes?

- The ergodicity angle: the argument that log-wealth-maximization is correct because it maximizes the time-average growth rate of one long-lived agent, whereas expected-utility maximization optimizes an ensemble average no individual ever experiences. Is that a real foundation or just the same claim redescribed? And does it survive once the wealth process stops being multiplicative — once you can spend, or the horizon is finite, or there's a floor you can't go below, or it's a single bet with no repetition?

- What happens when you're not alone. Growth-optimal agents in a market with non-growth-optimal agents: is the Kelly bettor exploitable, or does the presence of non-optimizers change what the optimizer should do? Is geometric rationality an individual norm or only a market/aggregate one?

- The annoying one: there seem to be about five different things called "the Kelly criterion" (the gambler's version, the investor's version, the multi-asset version, the continuous-time version, the version where your own bet moves the price). Are the open questions in one of these actually open in the others, or is most of the apparent disagreement just people talking about different objects?

What I want back is a defended ordering, not a survey. For each candidate: why it's upstream of the others (what gets easier, or breaks, if it's settled), how tractable it is for four philosophers over a year with no new math, and what evidence or argument would move it up or down the list. And tell me which of those four bullets is a red herring — I'd rather drop one early than spend a semester on it. Also, if theres a question I haven't listed that you think dominates all of these, say so and defend it.
