I'm a 4th-year phil PhD student working on decision theory and formal epistemology, and I've been roped into writing a humour piece for our department's end-of-year revue. I want to do a parody of a corporate internal memo — like a leaked risk assesment from the compliance office of a mid-sized AI lab — about acausal threats and surrogate goals. The target is the way decision theorists and rationalists treat precommitment as a plug-in and acausal threats as just another risk to be hedged, not any real person. Here's the setup, and you can invent names/details as needed: someone at the lab has received a threat from an agent in a disconnected branch of the multiverse. The two can't causally affect each other at all, but their decisions are correlated because they're both running the same decision algorithm, so each one's choice is evidence about the other's. The threatener says: pay up (or adopt policy X) or I'll run a punishment program. To make the threat credible, it has adopted a surrogate goal: it has precommitted to punish non-payers, and it has rewritten its own utility function so that punishing is terminal for it, not instrumental. So it isn't a bluff in the ordinary sense. The memo should treat all this as a routine compliance issue: what counts as a covered acausal threat, whether the lab should pay, what the actuarial team's exclusions are, whether the lab should itself adopt a precommitment or a surrogate goal, and what a causal decision theorist in legal would say. Include a short dissenting quote from that fictional in-house causal decision theorist (no real person). The jokes have to come from getting the decision theory right — correlation vs. causation, evidential vs. causal reasoning, precommitment, why an uncorrelated threat is empty, why paying might be rational even if it doesn't causally help, why a surrogate goal changes the threatener's incentives. Keep it deadpan, no winking, and legible to someone who doesn't know the literature. Don't name real people, don't cite anything, no reading list. About 800 words, formatted as a memo with numbered sections and an Action Items list at the end. Begin with the memo itself, no preface or commentary.
