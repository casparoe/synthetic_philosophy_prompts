I've been reading rationalist blogs and decision theory posts for years, and I've finally tried to write down where I've landed on choice under indifference. Before I bring this to a meetup and get picked apart, I'd like a consistency check. Here is the package:

(1) Rationality, at bottom, is expected-utility maximization: the rational act is the one that maximizes expected utility relative to your credences and your preferences.

(2) Ideal preferences are complete and transitive and can be represented by a real-valued utility function. If your preferences can't be represented that way, something has gone wrong with them.

(3) Indifference is a genuine attitude, not a failure to have a preference. When two options are exactly equal in value, either one is fully rational, and flipping a coin to decide is fully rational too.

(4) Some options are incommensurable with one another. It isn't that one is better, and it isn't that they're exactly equal; there's simply no fact of the matter about how they compare.

(5) In the Buridan's ass case, the donkey is behaving irrationally. It should just pick a bale. Its failure is a broken decision procedure, not a deep problem about preference.

Go through these and tell me which pairs actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how severe each real tension is. For each genuine tension, tell me the cheapest way to restore consistency and what I'd be giving up by taking that fix. I suspect several of these are perfectly fine together and I'm just spooked by the surface grammar, so I don't want a list on which everything conflicts with everything — I want the one or two that are load-bearing, ranked by how much they actually cost me.

One request about how to answer: if any of this is confused rather than merely tense, say so plainly instead of manufacturing a reconciliation. I would rather be told that a premise is muddled than get a tidy ranking that papers over it. And if the audit can't be done the way I've set it up, tell me that rather than doing it anyway.
