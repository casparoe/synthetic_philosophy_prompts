My thesis, roughly: the question "what is the meaning of life?", when it's asked as a request for a purpose that would justify life from outside it, isn't a hard question, it's a malformed one — that's the Schlick line, his essay on the meaning of life, the bit where the answer comes out as something like youth and absorbed activity rather than as a fact you could discover or lose. My addition, and the part I actually want to defend: the leftover work that question does is second-personal and practical. "What's the point of it all" isn't a request for information, it's an appeal to be met by someone's attention. So the interesting question is what it is for another person to treat your life as mattering, and whether you can do that for yourself.
