I've been doing detection engineering and blue-team work for the better part of eight years now — I write the rules, I own the alert queue, I've sat through more post-incident reviews than I care to count — and I've lately fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole after a colleague told me that my whole attitude to alerts was, without knowing it, a theory of knowledge. I've got as far as this: there's a guy called Nozick who says knowing is a matter of your belief tracking the truth — the thing has to be true, you have to actually believe it, and then there are two counterfactual conditions, roughly that if it had been false you wouldn't have believed it, and that if it had been true you would have. And I gather he uses that to give up closure: you can know one thing, know perfectly well that it implies a second thing, and still not know the second. That sounds mad to me, and it also sounds like something we do every day in the SOC, where a correlation rule fires and the resulting ticket asserts something considerably stronger than whatever the sensor actually saw. I know the Gettier material and I know roughly what the tracking conditions are supposed to say, and that is honestly the extent of my philosophy.
