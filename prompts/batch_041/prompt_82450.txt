Put me down as holding a moralized account of coercion: a threat voids consent only when the threat is itself wrongful — when the threatener proposes to do what he has no right to do, or uses it to extract what he has no right to demand. On this account, the severity of the pressure and the badness of the consenter's alternatives do not by themselves settle whether she consented; the wrongness of the pressure is what does. I want the case against the view laid out. Which observations and arguments should move me — a threat that is plainly wrongful yet leaves consent intact, a threat that is not wrongful yet voids consent, manipulation that voids consent with no wronging at all, the fact that people routinely consent under unjust background conditions — and which of these are decisive rather than merely awkward? What does the account predict that its purely causal or informational rivals do not? What would a defender of it concede as counting against it, rather than dismissing as a critic's talking point? And is there a circularity risk in cashing out 'wrongful' in terms that already presuppose valid consent? Take a definite position: say which defeaters you think actually bite and whether the account survives them, rather than laying out the options and leaving the verdict to me.
