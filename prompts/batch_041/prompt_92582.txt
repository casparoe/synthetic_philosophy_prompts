So I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas — I do a monthly column and I'm trying to write a feature about autonomous systems and the whole "who's responsible when the robot kills someone" mess. I want to build the piece around a flowchart that readers can actually follow, like one of those "which position do you actually hold?" sidebar graphics. Can you write it for me?

Here's the setup I want. Start from a commitment I think basically everyone now accepts: an autonomous system — a self-driving car, an autonomous weapon, a trading algorithm that flash-crashes the market, an LLM that hands someone lethal medical advice — can cause serious harm even when no single human being satisfies the ordinary conditions for being to blame (they didn't know, couldn't have known, couldn't have stopped it). Thats the starting node.

Then every branch should be one question that actually splits the positions, phrased so a reader can answer it without already knowing the label of the view they're heading toward. The first cut I want is between people who think blame/desert is the whole game and people who think responsibility is a forward-looking social practice of answerability — that one seems to do alot of work. After that: is the failure of the control-and-knowledge conditions something new that autonomous systems introduce, or is it just the old "problem of many hands" you get in any bureaucracy? Then: can responsibility be traced back to some human who set the thing up — designed it, trained it, deployed it — even if they couldn't foresee this exact harm? And for the people who say no to that: do you restrict deployment until a human can always satisfy the conditions (some kind of "meaningful human control" requirement), or keep deploying and swap desert for a no-fault / strict-liability / insurance scheme, or spread the responsibility across a collective?

Leaves I need to see: the gap thesis (there's a real hole and it's bad); the "no new gap, this is just many hands" continuity view; tracing / responsibility-by-design; meaningful human control; strict liability + insurance / no-fault compensation; collective or distributed responsibility; and the one that says the gap is actually fine or even good because it exposes how much we're still in love with retributive blame. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really pull apart — I'd rather admit two views collapse than fake a distinction. And tell me which combinations of answers nobody actually occupies, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Keep the node questions as concrete as you can — use the car, the weapon, the flash crash, the bad medical advice as the cases. Maybe 6 or 7 branch nodes and 7 or 8 leaves. Dont bother with reading lists or who-said-what, I just want the instrument itself.
