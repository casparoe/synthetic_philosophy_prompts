Write a satirical employee handbook for a fictional state department of corrections that has adopted a policy called Desert-Free Accountability. The handbook should run about 800 words, in deadpan bureaucratic prose, with numbered sections, headings, and a closing FAQ. Its target is the practice of justifying punishment by just deserts, not any living person or real institution. The satire should come from the actual conceptual difficulties rather than from winking asides, and the humor should be fair to the positions rather than a caricature of them. Distinguish basic desert from consequentialist responsibility; show how a compatibilist position can justify confinement, deterrence, and reform without appealing to cosmic desert; and show how a skeptic about free will might treat criminal conduct as a public-health problem, with quarantine, treatment, and risk management replacing blame. Invent a fictional commissioner and a fictional staff philosopher, and let their prose reveal the tensions. Include a section on the desert ledger, in which the department tries to assign different terms to two offenders with identical future risk because one is judged more blameworthy, and a section on the reasons-responsiveness audit, in which officials try to measure whether an offender's will was responsive to reasons. The handbook must be internally consistent: every policy should follow from the department's official theory of responsibility, even where that leads to absurd or unsettling results. A specialist should recognize that the piece understands the compatibilist and free-will-skeptic positions; a general reader should be able to follow it. Do not mention any living philosophers by name, do not cite sources, and do not include a bibliography. End with a FAQ entry on whether the department still believes in punishment, and do not resolve the tension too neatly.
