I run a monthly discussion group at my local library, and next month I am doing Maxwell's demon for an audience whose physics stops at high school. I do not need the demon explained to me; I need the session planned. I have read several accounts of why the demon fails and they do not all point to the same place — some put the trouble in getting the information, others in holding on to it or in clearing it away — and I doubt they can all be the real answer, so the plan has to make plain which one carries the weight and where in the sequence the others are set aside. Give me the order the pieces must come in for the failure to feel like a result the audience reaches rather than a rule handed down to them; the one example the whole forty-five minutes should hang on, whether that is the trapdoor demon, the one-molecule engine, or something else, and what it must include and must not include if they are to be able to run the argument themselves afterwards; the misconceptions they will bring in or form along the way and the exact point at which each has to be headed off, including the two I expect to be hardest to dislodge, that the demon is really about a conscious observer and that the whole business shows entropy to be nothing but our ignorance; the one question I should close on to see whether they have understood the idea rather than merely followed me; and what I should openly decline to go into at this level.
