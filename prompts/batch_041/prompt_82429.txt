Over the past year, a colleague in my department's ethics group has defended a consistent set of verdicts. She holds that a treatment which relieves chronic pain should be developed and offered even though one of its side effects is a marked dulling of the capacity for pleasure; that bringing new happy beings into existence is, by itself, no reason to do anything; that when resources are scarce the agony of a few should be addressed before the discomfort of many; and that it is a genuine improvement to prevent the existence of beings whose lives would be barely worth living, even where those lives would contain slightly more pleasure than pain. Next week we are to discuss a proposal for a device that would, at the touch of a button, instantaneously and painlessly bring about the end of all sentient life, so that no being ever suffers again. What rules do her earlier verdicts leave open, what does each predict about the button, which do you think she is actually running and on what grounds, and what further case would best tell them apart?
