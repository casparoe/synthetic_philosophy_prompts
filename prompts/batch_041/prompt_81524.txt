Im writing the final for a fourth-year seminar on Adam Smith and the Scottish Enlightenment -- 90-minute essay, 20 marks, and about half the class are PPE students with one intro econ course behind them. Last year nearly everyone wrote some version of "Smith was a free trader, so obviously he opposed empire", recited the invisible hand, and stopped. I want a single essay question that can't be answered that way, plus a model answer (650 words or so) and a marking scheme of five must-hit points worth 4 marks each, with a one-line note on the mistake that usually costs each mark and a final line on what separates a 16 from a 20.

Three things the question has to make them do. First, be concrete about the mechanism by which the colonial trade monopoly hurts Britain rather than the colonies -- Smith's story about what the monopoly does to the rate of profit in the colony trade, where capital gets pulled from, and who ends up paying for it. Second, explain why his praise of the Navigation Acts on grounds of defence is a principled exception rather than a hole he papers over, and what he gives up by conceding that much. Third, say what he actually proposes Britain should do about the colonies; I keep getting "he wanted to abolish the empire", which isn't it, and I want them to see why more than one remedy follows from his premises. If it helps, hang the whole thing on a one-line provocation for them to argue against -- something like the claim that Smith's only objection to empire was that Britain was running it badly. No quotes needed, and don't ask them to cite anything; I'm marking the argument.
