I recently resigned from a political organization after watching a close friend be treated as disloyal for raising a procedural objection, and I have been reading Simone Weil’s critique of political parties. I want to run a 90-minute session for our departmental reading group, about twelve graduate students, that makes her critique felt rather than merely understood. Design an activity in which participants must form a party, adopt a platform, and then respond to a dissenter, with rules that force them to experience how collective belonging and the party’s need to reproduce itself can override the principles it was founded to serve. Include a mechanism that produces a loyalty cascade: each participant privately disagrees with part of the platform but sees no public dissent, infers that they are alone, and the party converges on a harder line than any individual wanted. Provide roles, a timeline, a penalty or payoff scheme that does not require money, and a debrief that maps what happened onto Weil’s claims about parties as engines of collective passion and as structures that turn thought into a test of allegiance. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and be honest about which part of Weil’s view the activity cannot reproduce.
