I retired last year after forty years in a physics department, most of it on nonlinear dynamics and control theory, and I've been using my new free time to teach myself philosophy — mostly political stuff lately. I keep running into this three-way split over liberty: negative liberty (nobody interferes with you), positive liberty (you're your own master), and then the republican idea that you're unfree if you're merely dominated — someone *could* interfere arbitrarily even if they never actually do. Pettit and Skinner are the republican ones, I think, and the negative/positive split I associate with Berlin.

Here's what I'd like you to do. Take all three and restate them as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually think in: state spaces and degrees of freedom, constraints and actuators, controllability and observability, open-loop versus closed-loop, setpoints and reference signals, disturbances, gain, saturation, the reachable set. I'm fluent in that — I could teach a graduate course in it — so dont dumb it down and don't turn it into loose metaphors. Use the real formalism and tell me which piece of the philosophy maps onto which piece of the machinery.

Then give me the residue report. For each of the three, what translates cleanly, what only translates if you fudge it (say what you had to distort), and what has no counterpart in control at all. The bit I'm most suspicious of is "arbitrary" in the republican phrase "arbitrary interference." Control theory has no notion of a legitimate versus illegitimate input — an input is an input. So is that a genuine gap in my vocabulary, or is the republican smuggling in a normative premise that the control language usefully exposes as extra? And I have a hunch that the republican "mere capacity to interfere" is close to controllability by another agent plus a controller that preemptively shrinks its own reachable set to avoid provoking the actuator — tell me if that's right or if I'm forcing it.

Be honest about where the translation does violence and where it reveals something. Where is your reasoning weakest? Definately don't hide that at the end.
