I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has been working my way through philosophy with more enthusiasm than system. Next month I am to interview, for a small seminar series, a philosopher whose recent work offers a revisionist reading of Nietzsche’s essay on history and its uses and disadvantages for life. Her central claim is that the familiar trio—monumental, antiquarian, and critical history—is not a taxonomy of historical writing but a set of therapeutic strategies for a culture suffering from what she calls historical overload. She also argues that Nietzsche’s account is incomplete: all three modes are retrospective, and she proposes a fourth, prospective history, in which a community deliberately constructs a usable past in order to open a new future. I should be grateful if you would draft eight to ten interview questions for this conversation. They should go beyond asking her to summarise the three modes. I want at least one question on how she distinguishes monumental from antiquarian history when both seem to serve the needs of a scientific research programme; one on what life can mean when the entity whose life is at stake is not an individual but a discipline; one on whether the critical mode can be used against the very past that a prospective history is trying to construct; and one on the charge that her fourth mode is just monumental history under another name. For each of those four questions, please supply a follow-up that responds to the answer she is most likely to give. Please also include a closing question that asks what would count as a misuse of history, rather than merely a use she happens to dislike. The questions should be in British English, and they should be phrased as questions I could actually ask aloud, not as essay prompts.
