1. Basically all my evidence is uncertain. Nobody ever hands me a proposition at probability 1 — I look at the thermometer and it's "about 20", I skim the paper and it "probably supports" the hypothesis, my advisor says something and I'm 80% sure I heard it right. So the general rule has to be Jeffrey conditionalization: take whatever partition I'm re-weighting, move the cell credences to their new values, and leave the conditional probabilities inside each cell where they were.

2. Conditioning on E is just the limiting case where the evidence proposition goes to 1. So I don't think anything is lost by treating Jeffrey's rule as the general story and plain conditionalization as a special case of it.

3. The thing that makes a diachronic update rule correct is that violating it leaves you open to a sure loss — the Dutch book, or the accuracy-flavoured version where the alternative is expected-accuracy-dominated. I don't think there's anything else to appeal to here. "It's just what rationality requires" isn't an argument.

4. Rational credences are path-independent. If I get two pieces of evidence in one order and then in the reverse order, and I land in two different places, something has gone wrong. Same content in, same credences out.

5. When I re-weight a partition, the conditionals P(A|Ei) don't move. That's part of what makes it *the* evidence — if the within-cell conditionals are drifting too then I've changed the subject.

Does this hang together? Which of these are actually pulling against each other, and which just look like they do?
