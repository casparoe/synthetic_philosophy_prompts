Write an essay on what matters in survival. I have been reading about Parfit's teletransporter — the machine that scans you, destroys your body, and builds an exact copy of you at the destination — and about the opposing intuition that a copy, however exact, is not you, so that the trip kills you. I want an essay that weighs the strongest arguments on both sides and then commits to a verdict, rather than summarizing the debate and leaving it open.

To keep the essay from turning into a recitation of the standard examples, please work with a modified machine. Call it the drifting teletransporter. It reproduces you faithfully at the destination except for one small, random alteration per trip — a single trivial memory, say, or a momentary lapse in some minor habit — so that each arrival is psychologically almost, but not exactly, identical to the person who entered. Imagine someone who uses this machine a thousand times across a long life. Does the person who steps out at the end stand to the person who first stepped in in the relation that matters for survival, and if the answer is not quite, is there any principled place to draw a line?

The two familiar criteria seem to pull in opposite directions here. A criterion that locates what matters in psychological continuity and connectedness — which, by its own lights, come in degrees — must either say that the thousandth arrival is fully you, in which case the alterations seem to be doing no work at all, or say that survival itself admits of degrees, which I find strange. A criterion that locates what matters in the persistence of the same living organism has the reverse trouble: it makes the drifts irrelevant, but it also makes the very first trip fatal, which many people find hard to believe.

Take a side and defend it. If you side with the psychological view, say precisely what role the drifts play and whether survival comes in degrees; if you side with the bodily or animal view, explain why the first trip is already fatal and why so many people's intuitions are mistaken. Address the strongest objection to your own position — for the psychological view, that a mere copy is not you however perfect; for the bodily view, that if you do not survive the trip then the person at the destination is a stranger, and yet we would not treat a stranger as the rightful inheritor of your projects, promises, and grief. Do not end on a fence.
