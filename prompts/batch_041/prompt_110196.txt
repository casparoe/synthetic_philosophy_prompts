I would like an essay on a controversy in the ethics of space colonization, and I want it to end with a verdict rather than a survey of considerations.

The case is this. A consortium proposes a permanent settlement on another world, with two features that matter. The founding charter is unamendable: no future assembly, referendum, or court within the settlement can alter it. And return to Earth is impossible — not because the technology is unavailable, but because the consortium chose to spend its mass budget on capacity to grow rather than on a return vehicle, and because the life-support architecture itself embodies the charter, so that changing the rules would mean rebuilding the machinery that keeps everyone alive. The first generation goes knowingly and voluntarily. Their children are born there, cannot leave, and had no say in any of it.

Defend a verdict on whether founding such a settlement is permissible, and be explicit about what the verdict turns on. Work through at least the following, taking positions rather than laying out options: whether the second generation is wronged at all, given the puzzle about whether someone can be wronged by the very conditions that made them exist; whether the difference between a technological impossibility and a deliberate choice matters morally, and if so whether it should; whether the entrenchment can be defended as protection against catastrophic risk taken by people who do not yet understand the environment, or whether it is indefensible because it hands permanent authority to the generation that knows the least about conditions on the ground; and whether putting the charter into the walls rather than into law changes anything, including whether it forecloses the usual answer that a determined generation can always reclaim an entrenched clause.

Say what would have to be true for your verdict to flip, and whether you would still hold it if the settlement had no contact with Earth at all. Give the strongest version of the position you reject. I am not asking whether we should colonize space, or whether the money would be better spent on Earth; set those questions aside and treat the settlement as given.

Write it as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — the way you would write a long letter.
