Write a keynote speech for the 150th anniversary of the founding of a fictional women's college, St. Jerome's College for Women, in a fictional country. The college was founded by a religious order but is now secular. The audience includes a conservative bishop, secular feminists, and alumnae. The speaker is a philosopher who has been asked to present Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's views on women's education, but not as a simple plea for equality. The speaker's thesis is that Sor Juana, in her reply to the Bishop of Puebla, defends women's study primarily on theological grounds: ignorance is a form of culpable negligence, and the defence of the faith requires the intellectual labour of women as much as of men. The speaker also wants to introduce a new argument, not made explicitly by Sor Juana but consistent with her position: that excluding women from formal theological education is a form of theft from the Church, because it deprives the Church of half its intellectual resources and leaves it unable to answer its critics. This new argument must be the pivot on which the speech turns. The speech must reconcile that argument with Sor Juana's own emphasis on humility and obedience, and it must treat the cultivation of the intellect as a duty owed to God and to the Church rather than as a right owed to the self. The speech must not use the vocabulary of rights or equality, because that would alienate the bishop; nor the vocabulary of obedience or self-abnegation, because that would alienate the feminists. It must find a third vocabulary. The speech should include a direct address to the bishop and a direct address to the feminists, with the new argument serving as the bridge between them. Write the speech in British English, around 900 words. Do not quote directly from Sor Juana; paraphrase her views. Do not mention sources, citations, or reading lists.
