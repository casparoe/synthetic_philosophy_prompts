I'm setting the final for my upper-level seminar on Berkeley -- first half of the term is the Principles and the Dialogues, then four weeks on the Irish econmic writings. I want one essay question that a student can't answer by reciting 'money is just a ticket, wealth is really labour.' They can all do that by week 10 and it tells me nothing.

Here's the framing I use in lecture, take it as given: in the Querist he asks whether the true idea of money be not 'that of a ticket or counter', whether gold, silver and paper aren't just counters for 'reckoning, recording, and transferring', whether 'the four elements, and man's labour therein' be not the true source of wealth, and whether there has ever been an industrious nation poor or an idle one rich. He wants paper credit and a national bank for Ireland to get idle hands working. Good. What I want the exam to force is whether he's *entitled* to any of it, given the rest of him -- the critique of abstract ideas, and esse est percipi. Because saying a note is 'worth a bushel of wheat' looks like it needs a general idea of value, or a sortal; and if its worth is nothing but everyone's expectation that everyone else will take it, then what's its esse? Is a banknote's value percipi? I want them to have to decide whether his nominalism is what saves his account of money or what wrecks it, and they don't get to escape with 'he's just inconsistent' unless they pay for it. Give them one concrete case to bite on: a note issued against nothing but a public promise, circulating only because each holder expects the next holder to accept it -- invent it, or just use his Ireland.

Closed book, 50 minutes, ~700 words, no citations (they won't have the texts).

Then, in this order:

1. The question, as it would appear on the paper (150 words max).
2. Model answer (~700 words, written the way a strong student would write it, not the way I'd write it).
3. Marking scheme -- five numbered must-hit points, marks summing to 20, each with a one-line 'how they usually miss this'.
4. The A/B line (2-3 sentences: the specific moves that separate them).
