The absurdity is semantic: the two clauses contradict one another, because asserting that it is raining is just another way of asserting that one believes it is raining. So the paradox shows that first-person present-tense belief reports are not descriptions of an inner state but expressions or avowals of that state, and that belief is therefore not a mental state at all but a kind of committed performance. It also shows that we cannot be mistaken about what we currently believe, since 'I believe that p, but I do not believe that p' is not merely odd but impossible."
