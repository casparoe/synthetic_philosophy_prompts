I have been trying to pin down a structural feature that keeps surfacing in global histories, and I would like to know whether it already has a name — and if it does not, what it ought to be called, precisely enough that I can tell when it is present and when it is not.

The feature shows up most clearly in the way such narratives decide who is acting and who is acted upon. Take an account of Indian Ocean commerce, or of Chinese technological achievement, or of a West African kingdom. Up to the point of sustained contact with Europeans, the account explains the society through its own dynamics — its own ecological pressures, its own rivalries, its own inventions — or else dismisses it as static and inward-looking; either way, the causal story is internal. After contact, the same account reorganizes. The society's later trajectory is now explained by what Europeans did to it, and its own internal developments are demoted to background or to local color. The hinge is always the encounter. The same asymmetry runs the other way as well: European developments are granted internal causes — a fiscal crisis, a religious upheaval, a turn in natural philosophy — however local those causes may have been, while developments elsewhere become significant mainly as responses to Europe, anticipations of it, or obstacles to it.

Three things about this leave me unsatisfied with the obvious answers. First, it is not a matter of sympathy. Some of the accounts that display the pattern most completely are severe about European expansion; a narrative that blames Europe for everything still leaves Europe as the only party that acts. Second, it is not merely a question of which events are included: an account can be admirably inclusive in its selection of episodes and still carry the asymmetry in its connective tissue, in what is treated as the explanation of what. Third, I cannot decide whether the right level of description is narrative form or something closer to a claim about where agency resides.

So: is there an established term for this specific thing — the uneven attribution of causal weight, anchored to the moment of contact, that can survive in accounts that are not Eurocentric in their sympathies? I know Eurocentrism as a charge about standpoint or bias, and that is not what I am after; what I want is a formal property of how causal weight is distributed across a narrative, which may be present or absent independently of whose perspective is privileged. If a term exists, I would like it named, defined, and distinguished from the nearest concepts it might be confused with. If none exists, I would like one proposed, defined, and bounded — with the cases that resemble it but are not it, and the cases that do not resemble it but are. And if the pattern I have described is really two or three distinct things bundled together, I would rather be told that, and have them separated, than be handed a single label that papers over the difference.
