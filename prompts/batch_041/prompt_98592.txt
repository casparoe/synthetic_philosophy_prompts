So I've been going down a rabbit hole on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — Frankish, Dennett, the whole crew — and also some pop-sci perception stuff (that Hoffman guy who says perception is just an interface we evolved), and I keep hitting the same analogy everywhere: consciousness is like the desktop GUI on your computer. You get icons, windows, a tidy little workspace you can drag files around in, but under the hood it's just transistors and voltages; the desktop is a convenient fiction that hides the machinery. The people using it say: same with qualia. The redness of red, the sting of a pain — that's the desktop. It's a representation the brain spins up for its own convenience, and there's no further fact about what it's like, no inner glow that the GUI is secretly a picture of. Honestly it's a seductive picture and it's doing a ton of arguementative work for them, but my gut says it's carrying more than it can bear and I can't quite pin down where it breaks.
