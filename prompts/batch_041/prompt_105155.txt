ok so this is kinda random but my philosophy club did a unit on rational choice and the thing that broke my brain was the money pump. the setup is you have three things, call them A B and C, and you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, which is a cycle so it cant be ranked in a line. you start with C. since you like B more than C you'll happily pay a penny to swap C for B. now you have B, and since you like A more than B you'll pay a penny to swap B for A. now you have A, and since you like C more than A you'll pay a penny to swap A for C. your back to C but down three pennies, and the guy can just run it again forever. our teacher said this is supposed to show that intransitive preferences are irrational, but some kid in the back said no, it shows theyre exploitable, and i honestly dont know if thats the same thing.

anyway i want to write a parody of this. the idea is a company that does the pumping but as a legit subscription business. write it as one long email from their customer support to an angry customer who just realised they've been pumped about forty times. deadpan, no winking, the company is genuinely polite and genuinely believes its in the right. the joke has to come from the actual structure of the pump, not from the company being cartoonishly evil, because the scary part is that they never do anything the customer didnt prefer at the moment they did it.

stuff i want it to get right without ever spelling it out: that the pump only works if the customer keeps re-deciding at each step instead of binding themselves in advance; that the difference between just having a cycle and actually getting pumped for money is basically the fees and the offers, which is why the rep keeps saying they never charged for anything the customer wouldnt have paid anyway; that you can also pump someone who's just indifferent to a tiny fee, not only someone with a full cycle; and that the obvious fix, refuse every trade, is what the company sells as a premium add on, some kind of commitment device package where you pre-authorise them to lock you out, which is funny because it means the cure is also a product they own. maybe the rep also mentions, very calmly, that if the customer's preferences werent cyclic they wouldnt be a customer at all, and offers a free consultation to help them fix that, which should sound helpful and be horrifying.

one more thing, the teacher said there's a real debate about whether the person who just says no, i refuse the whole sequence is being rational or is being irrational by ignoring their own preferences, and i'd like the email to be consistent with the company's position on that, whatever it is, without the rep ever using the words rational or irrational. like the rep should be answering that question by accident. keep it around 700 words and please no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just continuous prose the way you'd write an actual email, paragraphs and all. also maybe have them throw in a line about how the customer agreed to every single trade, so legally and morally theyre definately in the clear, which i think is the most annoying part.
