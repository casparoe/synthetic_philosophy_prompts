I run a small quiz site with some mates and I want one on whether everyday conventions — queueing, jaywalking, escalator standing, tipping, shoes off indoors — are actually obligations. Write me 9 questions, each a concrete situation with 2-4 answer options, plus a scoring key that sorts readers onto positions: (a) it's just etiquette, no obligation at all; (b) the norms bind, but only socially or prudentially, not morally; (c) they're real moral obligations because breaking them wrongs someone in particular; (d) the fair-play line — you're bound only if you've taken the benefits of the practice; (e) the territorial line — locals' rules bind you by sheer presence, and visitors are bound too; (f) the convention-independent line — you should conform because of the consequences, not because it's the norm, so if the consequences don't change, neither does your duty; (g) the expressivist line, where "that's wrong" about jaywalking is really just disapproval dressed up; (h) the particularist line, where there's no general answer and it's case-by-case all the way down. No question may just ask "do you think queue-jumping is wrong" — every one has to be a case where these come apart. Please include: the 4am empty-street jaywalk; the 10-items-or-fewer lane with 11 items when nobody is behind you; tipping as a tourist somewhere servers actually depend on it and you didn't know; tipping where the norm is that tipping insults the host; the visitor who shows up on time to a dinner where everyone else arrives an hour late; and one case where the local convention is itself morally bad, so I can see who treats convention as defeasible. A few questions should also probe whether it matters that the norm is a bare coordination equilibrium (which side of the escalator) versus one that moves money or status around (tipping), and whether it matters that the norm's beneficiary can be picked out. Then give me the key: which answer patterns land on which position, which combinations are unoccupied and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody's thought of it, and which of my eight positions your quiz can't seperate. Finish by telling me where the instrument is most likely to be mis-sorting people — which question is carrying too much weight — and what you'd have to see to decide a position on my list isn't really distinct after all.
