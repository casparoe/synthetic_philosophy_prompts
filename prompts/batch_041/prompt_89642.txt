I have no medical or philosophical training, but I have been thinking about pain asymbolia and it seems to settle a question for me. As I understand it, people with this condition still feel pain—they can locate it and rate its intensity—but they are not bothered by it and may even smile during a painful stimulus. People with congenital insensitivity to pain, by contrast, feel nothing and get injured for lack of a warning signal. Since the asymbolia patients have the sensation without the suffering, and the congenital patients lack both and are worse off, doesn't this show that the badness of pain lies entirely in the emotional reaction, not in the sensation? If so, shouldn't the ideal painkiller induce asymbolia—preserving the useful sensory signal while removing the suffering—rather than dulling the sensation itself? And doesn't this mean that pain is not intrinsically bad, only the suffering reaction is?
