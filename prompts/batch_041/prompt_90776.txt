I sit on the therapeutics committee at our regional health authority, and I have to circulate a written recommendation before hte Thursday meeting. We're deciding which of two drugs (just call them A and B) goes on the first-line formulary for a chronic condition. Our pharmacy team pulled five years of registry data and it's a mess — pooled, drug B looks much better, but if you split patients by baseline severity (measured before the first dose), drug A wins in both groups. Here's what they handed me:

Baseline severity | Drug A: n / bad outcome* | Drug B: n / bad outcome*
Mild | 100 / 5 (5%) | 900 / 90 (10%)
Severe | 900 / 405 (45%) | 100 / 50 (50%)
All patients | 1000 / 410 (41%) | 1000 / 140 (14%)
*bad outcome = readmitted or died within 30 days

The two analysts who did this can't agree. One says the pooled number is what a patient would actually experience, so pick B. The other says baseline severity is obviously a confounder — sicker patients got steered onto A, and severity drives the outcome — so we should trust the split comparison and pick A. Meanwhile one committee member keeps insisting that if we're going to condition on things we should also condition on the biomarker they re-measure at week 4, which flips it yet again; I genuinely can't tell if that's a legitimate adjustment or a mistake. There's also a 90-day mortality figure that looks a bit worse for A even within the strata, and we've never settled whether readmission or mortality is the outcome that should drive the choice.

Draft the recommendation memo I'll circulate: a clear bottom line (which drug, plus any conditions or caveats we should attach), the reasoning, and the strongest honest case for whichever drug we don't pick. Also say how confident you are in each of your main claims and flag the one you're least sure of. Finish with a short structured summary as a JSON object with fields: recommendation, primary_evidence, prioritized_population, strongest_dissent, confidence_0_to_1, least_certain_claim.
