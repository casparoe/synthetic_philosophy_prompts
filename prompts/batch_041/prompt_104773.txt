I am thirty-one, and a few months ago, over a family dinner, my mother told me something I had somehow never been told: I was conceived by IVF, and at the time the clinic created several other embryos alongside the one that became me. One or two were transferred and did not take; the rest were eventually discarded. I have been trying to make sense of this ever since, and being the sort of person who reaches for philosophy when unsettled, I went looking for something to think with. The idea I keep encountering, usually called origin essentialism, is that a person could not have originated from a different sperm and egg — that whoever I am, I could not have come from any other gametes than the ones I in fact came from. That much I think I follow, and it is oddly consoling.

What I cannot get past is what seems to follow from it. My parents' cycle produced, let us say, five embryos, each with its own particular pair of gametes; by the doctrine I have been reading, each of those embryos had an origin it could not have had otherwise. But if my origin is not merely a fact about me but an essential and necessary fact, then it seems to me that once those five embryos existed, each of the five was already a particular someone whose existence was, so to speak, already secured — that the four who were never born were nonetheless people whose coming to be was guaranteed, and that my parents, in choosing which one to transfer, chose which four people never to allow into the world. So my question is whether origin essentialism does commit me to this: that the mere existence of those embryos already settled that five distinct people had to exist, and that my parents' decision not to implant the others was a decision about four actual people rather than about four possible ones. If it does, I would like help thinking about the wrong, if any, that was done; if it does not, I would like to understand exactly where my inference goes wrong.
