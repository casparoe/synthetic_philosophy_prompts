I've been reading rationalist-adjacent debates for years, and the internalism/externalism fight over reasons is one where I keep feeling the two sides talk past each other, so I'd like to see an actual exchange rather than two monologues. Write a dialogue between an internalist — who holds that an agent has a reason to act only if it is anchored somehow in her own motivational set, so a consideration that could never move her is not a reason she has — and an externalist, who holds that some facts count in favor of acting regardless of what the agent is or could be moved by. Neither should be a pushover: each should press the other's weakest point and concede where the pressure is real.

Center the exchange on one case I'll stipulate, because the stock examples have gone stale. Sana is fully informed, deliberates without error, reflectively endorses the whole of her motivational set, and is entirely content; no element of what moves her would be served by her becoming more responsive to the suffering of strangers. The thing the two of them should actually fight over is whether Sana has any reason to revise her motivational set — that is, whether there can be a reason to acquire new motivations when nothing she already cares about would be served by acquiring them. The internalist has to say where such a reason could possibly lodge without smuggling in an external one; the externalist has to say what makes his "reason" more than an idle ought with no grip on her; and each should be made to confront whether his position quietly collapses into the other's once you push on the counterfactuals (what she *would* be moved by, under conditions that never obtain).

Give it to me in three labeled parts. First, 'Framing' — two or three sentences setting the scene and the case. Then 'The Exchange' — the dialogue itself, roughly 700 to 900 words, in which each speaker answers the other's actual last move rather than restating a position. Then 'Standing' — four or five sentences saying which point each side won, what each had to retreat on, and what the case leaves genuinely unresolved.
