ok so I'm the student rep on my town's school board (I'm a senior, and yes I actually get a vote on this stuff, which I definately did not think through when I ran). Tuesday we vote on what to do about our board chair, and I have to write the opinion memo the rest of the board reads before we walk in. I need help.

Here's the situation. Two years ago the bond to fix the high school roof and the middle school boiler lost by about forty votes. Before the revote, the chair — retired teacher, twenty years on the board, wrote my college rec, everybody's favorite person in town — told a packed auditorium that the state had warned the district the high school might lose accreditation if the roof wasn't fixed. There was no warning. She made it up. The revote passed by a bigger margin, the roof and the boiler got fixed, and my little brother's classroom stopped flooding. Then a parent filed a records request with the state, got told no such warning ever existed, and now a reporter is running the story Friday — two days after our vote. The chair isn't denying any of it. She says she'd do it again.

Options on the table: censure her plus a public statement; refer it to the state ethics commission, which can void the bond vote, in which case the money is gone and we're back to buckets in the hallways; accept the resignation she's offered; or say nothing and hope Friday is survivable.

Where I'm stuck isn't the lying. I know lying is bad. It's this. My friend on the board says if the lie was for a good enough reason and it worked, then it was the right call, and censuring her is punishing her for doing the right thing. I don't buy that but I can't say why. I think even when the call was right, something is still owed — the wrong doesn't get cancelled by the good outcome, it just gets carried by somebody. So I want the memo to say who carries it. And I'm suspicious of the resignation, because if she's the only one who pays while the rest of us keep the fixed roof and our clean consciences, aren't we just using her as a trash can? Also I'd be voting too, so I don't get to act like my hands are clean, and I'd like the memo to deal with that honestly instead of flattering me.

What I want in it: a clear recommendation — which option, the exact motion to make Tuesday, what happens to her and when — plus the real reasoning, not a civics lecture. Give the strongest honest case for whatever option you're rejecting; I have to sit next to these people and I don't want to be the kid who beat up a strawman. And if you think my instinct is wrong — that sometimes the price of a justified lie has to be paid by whoever told it, and sometimes it's better to just let the whole thing go — say so plainly.

One more thing I can't figure out. My dad says this is the old dirty hands thing from political theory, and he says the whole point is that the person with dirty hands has to pay a price, otherwise they're not carrying anything, they're just getting away with it. But our vice chair said something that keeps bugging me: if we make the price automatic, then nobody will ever tell the useful lie, and the roof leaks and the kids get sick, so a rule that always punishes the noble liar is a rule that guarantees the worst outcome. Is that right? And if it is, how do we have the price without making it irrational to pay it? I don't know if there's an answer, but I want the memo to try, and to admit it if there isn't one.

Keep it to like a page and a half, we're all busy.
