I am a high school student who runs a small interview series for our philosophy club, and our next guest is a philosopher who argues that omissions — failures to act — can be genuine causes of harm and not merely grounds for blame. I would like eight to ten questions that press her view rather than let her restate it, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. At least one question should ask what an omission is supposed to be, given that it looks like the absence of an event rather than an event; another should test the charge that her account makes everyone who ever failed to prevent a harm its cause, and ask where she draws the line between a cause and a mere background condition. I want a question built on a case where two people each failed to act and only one seems responsible — a supervisor who never logs a fault and a night worker who never reads the log — forcing her to say whether the difference is causal or only moral; a question about a failure that does its damage by blocking someone else's rescue rather than by letting a threat run on; and a question about a harm that emerges from a whole team's uncoordinated inaction, where no single person omitted anything that made the difference. One question should ask whether calling an omission a cause is really just picking out whatever strikes us as abnormal, so that the debate is about norms rather than causation. Close by asking what would make her give up the view.
