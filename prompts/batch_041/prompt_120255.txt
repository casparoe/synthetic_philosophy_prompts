I'm on the critical care triage committee at a big NHS trust, and I have to circulate a written opinion before Thursday's meeting. We're revising our pandemic ventilator allocation protocol. The current draft has three parts: a life-years score; a fair-innings adjustment that gives a modest boost to younger patients on the idea that everyone should get a chance at a normal lifespan; and a reciprocity weight for frontline staff who've taken on extra risk. A rival proposal would replace all that with a lottery among patients expected to survive to discharge, on the grounds that anything else is discriminatory.

The chair, Dr A, says Harsanyi's aggregation theorem settles it. His version: imagine yourself behind a veil of ignorance, equally likely to be any patient, and if you're rational in the expected-utility sense, the only impartial social rule is the one that maximises average expected utility. So we should maximise expected life-years with equal weights. Dr B says that's a misreading. Harsanyi's theorem assumes individual and social preferences satisfy expected-utility axioms and a Pareto indifference condition (if every individual is indifferent between two options, so is the group); it yields a weighted sum of individual utilities, not equal weights, and not necessarily life-years. She says the veil-of-ignorance move adds risk-neutrality, which is a substantive ethical choice, not a theorem. We also ran an internal exercise where staff chose behind a simulated veil of ignorance; most picked a rule giving extra weight to the worst-off, not raw life-years. Dr A says they were just being irrational or risk-averse.

Please draft the opinion. Recommend a concrete decision -- keep the current draft, switch to the lottery, or adopt a modified protocol -- with a time frame for implementation and a review trigger. Take Dr A's Harsanyi argument seriously rather than just labelling it utilitarianism. Also say what weight the committee should give the veil-of-ignorance exercise: is it evidence about our values, or irrelevant to what's rational? I don't want to recieve a bibliography or citations, just the reasoning. British English, please. I'm not a philosopher, so I definately need the theory bits usable.

After the memo, give me a JSON summary with these fields: recommendation (one of adopt_current, adopt_lottery, adopt_modified); protocol_name (short name for what you recommend); weights (an object with numeric values for life_years, fair_innings, and reciprocity, or null if not applicable); harsanyi_verdict (one of sound, partly_sound, unsound); strongest_objection (the best argument against your recommendation, in one or two sentences); implementation_deadline_days (integer); review_trigger (a short condition that would force the committee to revisit).
