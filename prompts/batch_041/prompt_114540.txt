Hi — I'm a third-year undergrad taking a personal identity / philosophy of mind unit, and our revison group has somehow volunteered me to write the practice paper this term. Could you write me one essay question on mind uploading and gradual replacement, plus a model answer and a marking scheme I can use to mark the group's attempts? It's aimed at our level (we've done the standard stuff on personal identity), should be doable in 90 minutes, and I want it worth 20 marks.

My problem is that every mock question I try collapses into "is the teletransported person still you?" and then everyone just recites the same three paragraphs. I want something where they have to commit to an actual principle and then watch it crack under a case they haven't rehearsed.

Here's the case I'd like it built round. A woman called Priya has a degenerative brain disease. A clinic offers "piecemeal migration": each day they replace one small cluster of her neurons with a silicon chip that is input-output identical to the cluster it replaces, sits in exactly the same causal role, and is wired into the same surrounding tissue. She's awake and behaving normally the whole time; over about a year the entire brain is swapped out. Crucially, the clinic keeps every scrap of the original biological tissue in cold storage, and at the end they can stitch it back together and reanimate it. So at the end of the year there are two candidates walking around claiming to be Priya. Sil-Priya has the all-chip brain and has been continuously awake, active and planning her life for the whole year. Bio-Priya is the reassembled original meat, dormant for the year, now switched on — and she also says she's Priya, remembers the whole life, has the same personality and the same plans.

Two extra wrinkles I want in there, because they're the bits that actually trip people up in our group:

- The chips need a power cycle once a week, so Sil-Priya gets switched off for ten seconds every Sunday. Is that gap relevant to whether she survives, or not — and how does it compare with Bio-Priya having been frozen for a year?
- Suppose the clinic had instead done it in one go: scan the brain, destroy it, build the chip brain from the scan. I think most people say that's a copy, but the piecemeal version is "the same person" — and I want the question to force them to say what the actual difference is supposed to be, if anything.

What I'd really like the question to make them do: (a) say whether either, both or neither of the two continues Priya, and defend it; (b) decide whether the answer is allowed to depend on the *other* one existing (my instinct is it shouldn't — if Bio-Priya had never been reassembled, we'd all happily say Sil-Priya is Priya, and it seems mad that reassembling the old meat should retroactively change who Sil-Priya is — but I can't articulate why cleanly and I suspect that's the whole crux); (c) say what follows for the "what matters in survival" side of things as opposed to the identity side; and (d) say something about the sleep/power-cycle gap and whether continuity is really about being awake the whole time.

Could you write the question so it names the marks per part, and make the model answer around 700 words? Please don't let the model answer lean on who-said-what — we haven't memorised the names and I want it to stand on the arguments themselves, not on attributions. And the marking scheme should spell out the specific moves that separate a first from a 2:1, i.e. what a top answer has to actually do versus what a decent-but-summary answer will do, and the specific errors that should cost marks (I'm thinking things like treating "continuity" as just "no gap in consciousness", or letting the existence of the second candidate decide the first one's identity without noticing they've done it). Also — everything in British English please, the whole unit is marked in it. Cheers!
