I'm a grad student helping run a small interview series for our conciousness reading group, and next month's guest is a philosopher who defends what she calls the diagnostic view of the meta-problem. Her basic line is that the hard problem of consciousness is really a problem about why we have the intuition of a hard problem, and she thinks that intuition can be explained by a combination of introspective opacity, the way attention models itself as a spotlight, and our tendency to treat the first-person perspective as a kind of inner theater. She's not a flat-out eliminativist; she says she's a realist about phenomenal experience but a deflationist about its mystery. I need to prep eight to ten interview questions that go beyond letting her restate that thesis. I want at least one question on what exactly is supposed to be opaque to introspection and why that opacity would generate the sense of a non-physical residue, one on the difference between explaining the intuition and explaining it away, one on whether her account can make sense of the meta-problem feeling like a discovery rather than a cultural construction, and one on how she'd respond to someone who says she's just swapped the hard problem for a harder problem about self-knowledge. For each question, give me a likely follow-up she might give and then a follow-up to that, so I'm not caught flat-footed.

I'm also trying to avoid the usual canned questions about zombies and Mary's room, since everyone in the group has definately heard those a million times. If you can, work in some pressure about whether her view is empirically testable or whether it's really a piece of conceptual therapy, and about what would count as a rival explanation of the meta-problem that she'd have to take seriously. Keep the wording conversational and don't make it sound like a job talk.
