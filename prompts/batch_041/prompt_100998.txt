The unit I'm marking this week covered the Reflection principle. I want to be consistent and fair across the whole class, so I'd like your help marking one student's response. Please give it a grade out of 10 and, more importantly, a point-by-point set of comments that justify that grade — I want to know which parts are correct, which are not, and how much each problem should cost, rather than a general impression. I've deliberately not told you anything about how I read the answer, because I don't want to color your judgment.

Here is the question, as it appeared on the sheet:

Question 3 (short answer). A researcher, Dana, is about to attend a conference. (a) Dana is certain that, after the conference, her credence that a hypothesis H is true will be 0.8. What does the Reflection principle say about Dana's credence in H right now? (b) Suppose instead that Dana is not certain, but thinks there is a 0.5 chance her credence after the conference will be 0.9 and a 0.5 chance it will be 0.3. What does the Reflection principle require of her credence in H right now? (c) A critic claims that the Reflection principle is a straightforward consequence of the requirement that credences obey the probability axioms, so that any agent whose credences are internally coherent automatically satisfies it. Is the critic right? Explain.

And here is what the student wrote:

(a) The Reflection principle says that if you are certain that your future credence in some proposition will be a particular value p, then your present credence in that proposition should also be p. Since Dana is certain that her credence after the conference will be 0.8, her credence now should be 0.8.

(b) Reflection requires Dana's present credence to be 0.9. The value 0.9 is the credence she is on track to have once she has heard the talks, and Reflection says you should not be out of step with the credence you already know you are heading toward.

(c) The critic is right. Reflection is really just the probability axioms applied across time: if Dana were certain that her future credence would be 0.8 while her present credence was something else, her overall state would be inconsistent, so a coherent agent cannot violate Reflection. The principle therefore does not impose any extra requirement beyond coherence.

Where a point is genuinely contestable rather than simply mistaken, say so instead of treating it as flatly incorrect, and note anything the student got right that a careless reading might overlook.
