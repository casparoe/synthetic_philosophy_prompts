I'm a third-year sociology student and I'm taking this course on the philosophy of social science, and last week we did the whole realism-vs-constructionism thing about race, class and gender, and ever since I cant stop thinking about generations because I honestly can't tell where I land on them. Heres whats bugging me. Theres this very real, very well-funded practice built entirely around generations. My university's career centre runs a "communicating across generations" workshop for staff, my mum's company made everyone sit through a generational-diversity training, marketing firms chop the whole population into Boomers / Gen X / Millennials / Gen Z and sell reports about what each one "values", and the birth-year cutoffs everyone uses (Gen Z starting around 1997, Millennials around 1981) get treated like they carve nature at the joints. The workshop slides say stuff like "Gen Z needs constant feedback, Millennials need purpose" -- claims about deep psychological differences that are supposed to have been caused by whatever was going on when you were a teenager.

But in my stats class we did the age-period-cohort identification problem, where apparently you literally cannot separate the effect of being a certain age from the effect of being born in a certain year from the effect of the current moment, because all three move together in a straight line. So when someone says "young people today are more anxious", that could be an age effect, a period effect, or a genuine cohort effect and the data often can't tell you which. And the same uni that runs the generational workshop has a demography prof who insists "cohort" and "generation" are not the same thing at all -- a cohort is just whoever was born in some window, whereas a generation is supposed to be a group that went through something formative *together* and knows it, so they develop a shared outlook. Meanwhile some of my friends say it doesn't matter whether generations are "real", because if everyone believes in them and treats people accordingly then the belief makes them real in a self-fulfilling, feedback-loop kind of way.

So my question is: what does the generational-training / generational-marketing practice actually presuppose about social kinds? Does it require generations to be real kinds with essences, or is it enough that they're useful predictive buckets, or that people believe in them and act on the belief? Which rival views does the practice quietly rule out -- does it need a cohort to have actually shared some formative experience, or would a bare birth-year bin do the job? And are the parts of the practice even consistent with each other, the marketing segmentation vs. the HR training vs. what the demographers mean by "cohort"? If you swapped the presupposed view for something deflationary, like "generations are just a handy label for age groups", what would concretely have to change in how these workshops and reports get run?
