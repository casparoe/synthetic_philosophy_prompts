In my school's philosophy club we have spent the past few weeks on the mind–body problem, and last meeting the conversation about whether a machine could ever genuinely think was brought to an abrupt halt when one of the older members said, as though it settled the matter, that the brain is simply a computer — that the mind stands to the brain as software stands to hardware, so that there is nothing left over to be mysterious about. I was not satisfied, partly because I could not tell whether he was making a modest claim about the usefulness of a certain kind of model or an enormous claim about what we fundamentally are, and those two things seemed to be doing very different work in the argument that followed. He then used the line a second time to brush aside a question about whether a detailed simulation of a brain would be conscious, which struck me as a much stronger conclusion than the first use could possibly license.

Could you lay out for me the distinct, precise theses that the sentence "the brain is a computer" could actually be expressing, arranged from the nearly trivial to the radical, and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or something closer to false? I would like the readings presented as a menu rather than a single preferred interpretation, with some note of what kind of evidence or argument would genuinely bear on each one — for instance, I suspect one reading is merely that computational models are often predictively useful, another is that neural activity literally consists in computation over representations, and another is the multiple-realizability claim that mental states relate to brain states the way software relates to hardware; but I am not confident I have separated them correctly, and I may be conflating computation in the strict formal sense with any physical process that can loosely be described as information processing. Finally, please tell me which of these readings would actually support the two uses he put the slogan to — the modest one and the one about the simulated brain — and which reading the people who repeat this line most often seem to have in mind when they deploy it to end a discussion.
