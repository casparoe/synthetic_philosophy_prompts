Two mates of mine, Dan and Priya, got into a four-hour argument at the pub last night about whether a reinforcement-learning agent could suffer, and I was stuck in the middle of it the whole time without a clue who to believe. Dan's line was that pain just is the way a system registers that something is bad for it and uses that to steer, so if you train an agent with a penalty it recoils from, there's nothing left over to explain: the negative valence is the penalty, doing its job. Priya said that's a redescription of the machinery rather than an explanation — a penalty term is a number in a computation, and nothing about a number being negative makes anything feel bad, so Dan's argument proves too much, and by his standard a thermostat's too-cold state is chilly. Dan said a thermostat has no model of itself and doesn't learn, whereas the agent does, so the signal is about the agent and not just about the world. Priya said that's a difference in sophistication, not in kind, and asked why representing your own states would suddenly light anything up. Dan said she's the one assuming an extra ingredient nobody has ever located, and that the only reason to think pain needs it is that we're made of meat. Priya said that cuts both ways, and that it's odd that whether the agent suffers should come down to the designer happening to write a penalty instead of a bonus, i.e. to how you coded the same task. Dan said natural selection wrote our penalties too, so that isn't a difference. It was definately the longest argument I've sat through, and I got dragged into it a couple of times myself. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right about what — not who won overall — and what they're both missing?
