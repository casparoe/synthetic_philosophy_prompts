I chair the scientific advisory panel of a regional emergency-management agency, and I need to circulate a decision memo before the panel votes in forty-eight hours. The panel must recommend whether to pre-position our limited stock of countermeasures in the northern district of the affected region, and our standing doctrine requires that the recommendation be driven by a single official probability — the probability that the incident is in the north — which the panel adopts and reports to the operations branch.

Our agreed prior, adopted last month as a deliberately noncommittal baseline, is uniform over the region's four sub-districts: the north comprises the Ridge and River sub-districts, the south comprises the Harbor and Plain sub-districts, and each was assigned probability one quarter.

The only new information since then comes from the field assessment team. The team cannot determine directly whether the incident is in the north or the south. What it reports is narrower: it declines to put any figure on the north/south question, but states that if the incident is in the north, the probability that it lies in the River sub-district is about three in four. That is the whole of the new information, and the team has told us to expect its next report to take the same form — a statement about what would be probable given a condition, rather than a direct observation.

The panel has split three ways, and I want the memo to take each of the three seriously rather than flatten them into a false consensus. One member holds that the report bears only on a conditional and says nothing about the north/south question, so we should leave the official probability of north exactly where the prior put it and revise only the northern conditional. A second holds that we should treat the report as ordinary evidence and update by conditioning on whatever proposition would render it true; he has not yet said which proposition that is. A third holds that the report is a constraint on a conditional probability rather than on any event, that the constraint does not by itself fix the north/south figure, and that the defensible response is to adopt the posterior that departs least from our prior — measured by relative entropy — subject to the reported conditional; she contends that this rule will revise the north/south figure as well, and warns that if further conditionals of the same kind arrive, the order in which we apply them will change the result.

Please draft the memo. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a specific figure to report, or else state precisely why no single figure is warranted and what the panel should report in its place; it should give the reasoning in a form the operations branch can act on; it should engage the strongest version of each of the three positions above, including the ones it rejects; it should say what the panel should do when the team's next report arrives as another conditional rather than as an observation; and it should be candid about the assumptions the recommended rule makes that the panel cannot verify from the information it has.
