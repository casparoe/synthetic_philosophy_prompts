I spent forty years as a chemist and have lately been doing philosophy in my retirment, mostly by reading and arguing with myself. I've got stuck on a little puzzle I can't let go of. In the toy game I keep playing, you take a short string like abc and change it to abd, then ask what ijk should turn into. There's no single right answer, obviously — ijd keeps the letters, ijl keeps the rule, and people wobble between them. I've been inventing my own variants too, e.g. one where the letters sit on a ring so after z comes a again, and the wobble gets worse, which is what got me interested.

Before I read any more grand theories of concepts and analogy, tell me what any theory of this sort of thing would actually have to deliver. Give me the desiderata: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't flatten (like between an answer being wrong and an answer being merely less apt), and the things it must explain about how the shift from one reading of the string to another even happens, with the case behind each requirement. Flag any pair you suspect can't be satisfied together — that's the bit I most want. And tell me which single requirement does the most to rule candidates out.

Write it in British English please.
