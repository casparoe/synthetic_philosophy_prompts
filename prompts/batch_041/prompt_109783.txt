Before we start arguing about whether qualia are even a coherent thing to posit, I want the spec on the table — the desiderata any account of them has to meet, with the case or consideration behind each one, and a note on which are non-negotiable versus which are just commitments of one camp. So: the cases it has to get right (inverted spectrum, the two coffee tasters, whatever else earns its place), the distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse, the results it can't contradict. Two things I want baked in from the start. One, don't let the list presuppose that qualia exist — one live option is that the posit is incoherent, so the list needs a requirement about what would count as showing that, and none of the other items can smuggle the thing's existence in as a premise. Two, here's a case I keep chewing on that I've never seen anyone handle properly: someone learns a vocabulary for a smell, or a wine, or a chord, and afterwards sincerely reports that the experience itself now has parts and structure it didn't have before, and she isn't lying or confabulating — her earlier reports were equally sincere. The account has to say whether the experience changed or only the description did, and if only the description, what the difference between those two comes to. It should also come down on whether "a quale" is a countable thing at all or just shorthand for a discriminable difference. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to rule candidates out, and whether any two of them can't be satisfied together — thats the part I actually care about. No headings, bullets or tables, just continuous prose like you'd write in an email, and dont bother with citations, I'll sort those out.
