I serve on the Referendum Franchise Commission of the Republic, the standing body that fixes, before each referendum, which classes of persons are entitled to vote. We are now docketing a case whose governing rule we cannot extract from our own record, and I would like your help in extracting it.

Our rulings to date are these.

1998, a referendum on replacing the national pension formula: we enfranchised every adult citizen and every non-citizen habitually resident for five years or more, and excluded temporary permit holders and citizens who had left the country before their eighteenth birthday and never returned. Our stated ground was that the scheme should be settled by those who would live under it.

2004, the referendum on Highmoor's secession: we enfranchised the whole national electorate rather than the Highmoorers alone, since the constitution of the whole Republic was to be remade.

2009, the merger of two municipalities: we enfranchised only the residents of those two municipalities, on the ground that the reorganization altered no one else's legal position.

2015, the siting of a waste repository whose hazard will outlast every living person: we created a single fiduciary vote for persons not yet born, cast by a trustee we appoint.

2019, the statute implementing a joint river-basin authority with our neighbour: we enfranchised riparian residents on both sides of the border.

2022, the licensing of a coastal fishery worked by stateless boat people holding no citizenship and no fixed residence: we enfranchised them.

The case now before us is a referendum required by the constitution on the Watershed Reorganization Act, a statute of the national parliament that transfers three districts from the province of Ardenne to the province of Bellmark and reassigns their water rights. The districts' residents, both provinces, and farmers downstream in a third province who draw on the same river all stand to gain or lose; the rest of the country is bound by the statute as law but is materially untouched by it.

Tell me how we should rule to remain consistent with ourselves: what rules our record is compatible with, what each would predict here, which you would adopt and on what grounds, and which further case would discriminate between the surviving rules. Keep the whole answer under 600 words.
