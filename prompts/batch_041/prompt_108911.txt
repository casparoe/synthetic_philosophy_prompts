The thing I care about: there's a bunch of agents, each of whom can unilaterally take some action, each of whom is uncertain how many of the others will also take it, and the action is good if only a few people do it and bad if lots do. So each agent reasons privately "if I don't do it, there's a decent chance nobody will, so I should" — and because that exact reasonning is running independently in every head, the action gets taken way more often than anyone wanted.

So please don't hand me a generic "too many cooks" joke, or a joke about coordination failure or the tragedy of the commons, or "lol everyone thinks they're the main character." Those would fit any collective-action problem and so they're not about this one. I want the punchline to hinge on the gap between each agent's individually reasonable "someone's gotta do it, and it might have to be me" and the aggregate mess that produces when everyone runs it at once — or on the weird corollary that the action tends to happen even though, ex ante, everyone would definately have preferred that it not.
