I'm a third-year PhD student in political theory — my dissertation is on how contested political concepts get operationalized into measures, which is probably why this is bugging me — and I've gotten stuck on something in the long-running fight over whether violence has declined since the Middle Ages. I don't think I'm confused about the empirics. I think I've noticed a shape that I don't have a word for, and it keeps wanting to be cited and I keep not being able to cite it. Here's the shape. Any long-run series has to fix two things up front: what counts as a violent death (homicide? war? famine? suicide and overdose?) and what you're dividing by (total population, adults, person-years, just the people actually at risk?). Now over those same centuries the word has stretched — plenty of things nobody would have tallied in 1650 (a husband beating his wife, a beating in a schoolyard, deaths from a policy everybody could see coming) are tallied now — and the stretching looks like it was itself part of the moral progress, not a bug in it. So the category in the numerator is expanding at the same time as the underlying behaviour is (maybe) shrinking, and the denominator is a population whose size and composition are also moving. The upshot is that "less violent" can mean at least four different things — fewer acts total, lower chance per person, less severe acts, or a narrower class of acts counting as violent at all — and they come apart, sometimes in opposite directions. And I can't just say "well, then use the old, narrow, fixed definition," because the old definition is exactly what people now point to as part of the problem: it quietly decided whose suffering didn't count.
