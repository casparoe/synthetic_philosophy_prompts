I am preparing a short statement of theoretical commitments for my qualifying exam in archaeological theory, and I would like it audited before I submit it. This is roughly where I have landed.

(1) The material record is the only evidence there is, and it is the residue of human action. It follows that every inference I make about the past runs through an inference about what someone was doing, and why.

(2) To understand an action is to grasp the reasoning behind it: I have to be able to see how it could have been a thing for that agent to do. Where I cannot reconstruct the practical reasoning, I have described the remains but not explained them.

(3) The conceptual worlds of past people may be radically unlike ours. I do not assume that they sorted experience into the practical and the ritual, the natural and the supernatural, or the individual and the collective as we do. That a mask was alive for them, and that they believed a mask was alive, are different claims, and the first may be the better description.

(4) Nevertheless I take beliefs to be inferable from objects: grave goods, for instance, license some claim that the dead were thought to go on somewhere. I am equally willing to infer institutions from material regularities, reading differential house size and storage as evidence that some people had standing to decide things for others.

(5) Where a functional or ecological explanation is available, I prefer it, because it accounts for the deposit without committing me to anyone's mental states.

(6) I hold claims about past beliefs to a high evidentiary standard, since the record underdetermines almost everything, and I do not want modern categories — religion, art, economy, the individual — read into the past.

I would like to know which of these commitments genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and how severe each real conflict is. Please order the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. For each, say which commitment is doing the damage, what the cheapest repair would cost me, and whether the cost falls on something I would notice. Tell me also which apparent tensions dissolve once the relevant distinctions are drawn, and which single revision would resolve the most at the least expense. I would rather accept one sharp revision than a list of hedges.
