So I'm putting together a little thing and I want a piece of satire out of you, and I've decided Moore's paradox is criminally underused as a comedy premise. The bit I mean: saying that it's raining and that I don't believe it's raining. Weird to say, but not a contradiction — I could be wrong about the weather or wrong about my own head, and either way both halves can be true at once. What's broken is the saying, not the sentence. And saying it's raining while believing it isn't is a different animal again, because that's just two beliefs that can't both hold. Also worth noticing that saying it rained but I didn't believe it is completely fine, and so is saying it's raining but she doesn't believe it, so whatever's wrong has to do with me, now, asserting it out loud.

Here's what I want. A review post for a small consumer gadget that listens to you talk and buzzes when you say something in that shape — plus a couple of other customers' takes pasted in by the reviewer, the way people do on forums. Deadpan the whole way, no winking, no asides about what philosophers have said. Let the humor come from the gadget and its users banging into the actual structure of the problem: it fires on sentences that are perfectly true; it's useless against lying, since a liar who says it's raining while believing otherwise isn't producing a paradox, he's just lying; it has to do something about tense and third person or it buzzes all day; and there's a firmware update that claims to catch the person who believes it's raining and doesn't believe they believe it. One or two disappointed owners would be great, and so would one true believer who insists the buzz is just the machine being honest about something we all do and don't say.

Around 600 words please, and continuous prose only — no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables, write it like you're typing an email. Dont bother citing anyone, I don't care about the literature, I care whether someone who's thought about this laughs instead of rolling their eyes.
