So here's my thesis, roughly. The standard way hinge epistemologists frame things is that hinges are a distinctive class of propositions that enjoy a distinctive epistemic status — you can't justify them, you can't doubt them, they just sit at the foundation doing their thing. I want to argue that this whole framing is a mistake. On my reading, a hinge isn't a proposition at all; it's the constitutive rule of a particular practice of inquiry — it's what makes certain moves in that practice count as moves in the first place. So the regress worry (if you can't justify the hinge, what justifies the practice that rests on it?) is a category mistake, like asking what justifies the rules of chess. The rules of chess aren't true or false, they constitute the game. I think alot of the handwringing about the Münchhausen trilemma dissolves once you see hinges this way, because the trilemma only bites if you insist on treating the hinge as one more claim in need of grounds.
