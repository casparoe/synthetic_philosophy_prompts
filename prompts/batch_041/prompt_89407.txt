I'm an undergraduate currently taking a module in the philosophy of physics, and I've been asked to lead the seminar discussion next week. I'd like you to act as a philosopher of physics with a deep working knowledge of Everettian quantum mechanics, the Copenhagen approach, and de Broglie–Bohm theory, and to build me a thought experiment to order. Please think carefully before you write, and begin by setting out in a few lines each what you take to be the core commitments of the three interpretations, so that I can see whether your design really does the work I want. Since Copenhagen covers a family of views, say at the outset which version you are working with and hold to it throughout.

What I need is a single experimental scenario — an interferometer of some kind, with whatever detectors and choices you require — engineered so that the three interpretations come apart in two specific and opposite ways. First, Copenhagen and Bohm should agree with each other, and Everett should dissent, on whether the experiment issues in exactly one actual outcome. Second, Everett and Bohm should agree with each other, and Copenhagen should dissent, on whether the whole experiment can be described without invoking any measurement postulate or observer. Both patterns must fall out of the interpretations themselves rather than being stipulated by me or by you; if getting a pattern requires adding an axiom to one of the interpretations, say so plainly and mark it as an addition.

Constraints on the design:

- one experiment only, described in at most a paragraph;
- no appeal to consciousness, to observers' minds, or to an undefined notion of measurement;
- nothing that could not in principle be built from an interferometer and ordinary detectors — no infinite apparatus, no exotic new physics;
- the retention or erasure of which-path information should be the single variable that does the work, with everything else held fixed;
- no Wigner's-friend-style nested observers; the case should stand on its own;
- I would prefer something other than the standard double slit with a which-path detector. If you do use it, you must show that it satisfies every constraint above.

After the scenario, please walk through the reasoning: for each interpretation, and for each of the two questions, state the verdict and the premise that drives it. Then tell me honestly which feature of the case is doing the work, what becomes of both patterns if that feature is removed — say, if the record is made permanent, or if erasure is made impossible — and how a committed Bohmian would try to resist the case, in particular whether they can retain their agreement with Everett on the second question while still insisting on a definite trajectory.

This is for a tutorial presentation, so I am not trying to refute any interpretation; I know they are empirically equivalent. What I want is the smallest experiment that makes all three sets of commitments visible in the same breath. If the two patterns cannot both be obtained from one experiment, please say so and show exactly where the obstruction lies rather than fudging it. Please structure your answer with short headed sections, and write in British English.
