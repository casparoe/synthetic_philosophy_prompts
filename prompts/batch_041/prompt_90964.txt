I retired from the bench three years ago — thirty-two years as a chemist, mostly kinetics — and my wife will tell you I've simply swapped one set of obsessions for another. Lately it's been rules that change themselves. It started with a game a friend showed me called Nomic, where the players take turns amending the rules, and the rules include the rules about how to amend the rules, so that even the rule saying you have to obey the rules is up for grabs; I gather the ruleset is split into ones you can change and ones you basically can't, and there's a whole little industry of turning the unchangeable ones into changeable ones so you can get at them. What gets me is the ground-floor question underneath it: if a rule authorizes its own revision, what authorized that rule, and can the system ever really lock its own core away from its own procedure? I keep trying to write a poem about it and it keeps coming out as an essay, which isnt what I want.

So that's the ask. I'd like a short poem — call it fifteen to twenty lines — laid out like a numbered constitution, as a list of articles or clauses, in the voice of the amendment clause itself, the provision whose whole job is rewriting the others. Partway down, the poem has to actually amend itself: one article should revise, overrule, or quietly contradict an earlier one, and I want that to happen in the poem's own body — a line that gets rewritten, a rhyme that held for two stanzas and then breaks, an article that reaches up and cancels the one above it — not merely be announced. And please don't tidy up the regress at the end. The part I actually find vertiginous is that the power to change the rules has to come from somewhere, and if it comes from the rules then it's the system pulling itself up by its own bootstraps; I'd much rather the poem leave that hanging than resolve it into some neat closing image of order. Someone who's chewed on self-referential legal systems should find it honest about the problem, and someone who hasn't should still find it a good, slightly unsettling poem. If it can also smuggle in the trick of making an entrenched rule amendable by first amending the rule that entrenches it, all the better.
