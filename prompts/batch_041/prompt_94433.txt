I'm in a reading group thats been having the same fight for a year about the "outside view" for states and empires. The Turchin-ish half says you can put collapse on a curve, that there are base rates, that the whole point of the outside view is to stop treating your favourite empire like a unique snowflake. The other half says every collapse is a singular pile-up of contingent events and any "base rate" is just a number you got by lumping incomparable things together. Nobody has moved an inch, and at this point I suspect we're not even disagreeing about history. My hunch, and I want you to treat it as just a hunch, is that the real fault lines are two things upstream: a refernce class problem in disguise, plus something like Galton's problem -- our "sample" of empires were mostly trading partners, rivals and imitators of one another, so they may not be independent draws at all, and the base rate might be diffusion/autocorrelation wearing a toga. Is that the right diagnosis, or is the real disagreement conceptual -- what even counts as an empire, or as a collapse, in the first place? Diagnose the structure of this disagreement for me (not a survey of who said what), and then tell me honestly what would count as progress: a better reference class, a diffusion-adjusted statistic, a new distinction, or just admitting the question is ill-posed and moving on.
