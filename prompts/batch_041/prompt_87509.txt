Okay, this has been bugging me for a while and I cant tell if it's a real problem or just me.

Take constitutions. Germany's Basic Law has what people call an eternity clause — Article 79(3) — which says certain core things (human dignity, democracy, the federal state) can never be amended, not even through the special amendment procedure the constitution itself sets up. India ends up in a similar place but not because the text says so: the Supreme Court, starting with the Kesavananda Bharati case in the 1970s, held that parliament can amend the constitution but can't destroy its basic structure. And then there's the boring everyday case that I think is actually the weird one: basically every constitution has an amendment clause, and that clause is itself usually amendable using the very procedure it defines. So a legislature can lower its own threshold for future amendments, and then use the lowered threshold to do things the original rules would never have permitted. Rules that rewrite themselves.

What I can't wrap my head around is what this whole family of practices is assuming about where legal authority comes from. If the constitution is valid because the people, or the constituent power, willed it, then how does it bind that same power — who says the willer can't change its mind? If instead the validity comes from the amendment procedure, then what makes the procedure valid, and why is it immune to being used on itself? It feels like the practice needs a source of authority that is somehow both inside and outside the legal order at once, and I want to know if that's actually a coherent thing to believe or if I'm just confusing myself and one of the blunt alternatives — everything is revisable, or nothing is — is the more defensible line. Also, honestly, I suspect the German clause and the Indian doctrine are not doing the same work even though they get lumped together, and I'd like to know which of the two, if either, survives the paradox.

Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no conclusion — reconstruct what the practice presupposes, tell me what it rules out, and say what would have to give if the presupposition were wrong.
