A colleague of mine works on the intentional stance — the strategy of predicting a system's behaviour by attributing to it beliefs and desires that would make its behaviour reasonable. She has given verdicts on a run of cases, and I am trying to reverse-engineer the rule she is actually applying.

Her verdicts so far:

- A greenhouse thermostat: the intentional stance is available, but it tells us nothing we could not already read off the circuit diagram; she denies that the thermostat has any beliefs.
- A chess engine that plays above grandmaster level: no beliefs, she says, because the design stance already gives a complete account of its play, so the intentional stance adds nothing.
- A bacterium chemotaxing up a nutrient gradient: no beliefs.
- A honeybee that navigates to a flower and dances out its location for the hive: beliefs and desires, and she insists that this is compatible with everything it does being mechanically subpersonal.
- A border collie: beliefs.
- A reinforcement-learning agent that has learned to play a single Atari game: no beliefs, although she concedes that the intentional stance predicts its play perfectly.

The new case is a current large language model, fine-tuned on a very large corpus of first-person writing, which reliably produces utterances such as 'I think' and 'I was wrong about that', and whose next-token behaviour is predicted far better by treating it as having beliefs and desires than by any attempt to read its answers off its architecture. She has not yet ruled, and we are arguing about what she is committed to.

Predict her verdict. More importantly, tell me the rule she is implicitly running, and set out the rival rules that fit all six earlier verdicts just as well. I take it the track record is compatible both with a thoroughly pragmatic rule — belief-talk is warranted exactly when the intentional stance earns its predictive keep over the design and physical stances, and there is no further fact of the matter — and with a rule that instead demands internal states with representational content in the right functional role, or a capacity for the system to be mistaken from its own point of view. For each candidate rule, say what it predicts about the language model, which of the six earlier verdicts it fits least comfortably, and which rule you think she is really running, with your reasons and a confidence level. Finally, name the further case that would separate the candidate rules and explain why it does. Answer in British English.
