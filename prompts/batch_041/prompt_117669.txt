I'm a fourth-year PhD student in early modern philosophy and I co-host a pretty informal interview podcast that a few of us in the department run — it's basically a seminar room, a bad USB mic, and whoever happens to be visiting the department that week. Next week's guest is someone who's been defending a quite specific reading of Berkeley's master argument, the one where he says we can't so much as think of a sensible thing existing without its being perceived, since thinking of it just is framing an idea of it. Her line is that the argument works as it stands, and that the standard objection — that you can't get from a fact about what I can conceive to a claim about what can possibly exist — is a category mistake, because on her reading Berkeley isn't making a modal claim at all. He's reporting what it's like to try to frame the thought, and the argument is really a reductio on the idea that being aware of a sensible thing is a two-place relation between a mind and an object that could in principle hold with no mind around. She also thinks the argument doesn't need God at all, and that the God stuff is doing seperate work elsewhere in Berkeley's system, which I'm suspicious of.

Could you draft me eight to ten questions for the interview? I don't want her just restating the view — my cohost can get that out of her in two minutes and then we're stuck improvising.
