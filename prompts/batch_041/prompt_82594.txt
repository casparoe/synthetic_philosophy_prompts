Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the philosophy of influence could both sign. The first camp holds that manipulation is a unified and distinctive wrong: it operates by bypassing or subverting the target's rational deliberation rather than engaging with it. The second holds that there is no such unified category — that what we call manipulation is a miscellany of deception, exploitation of cognitive quirks and ordinary pressure, with no principled line separating it from legitimate persuasion. I want the claims both sides accept about how influence works and what any adequate account of it must explain; the precise statement of each side's position in wording its own side would endorse; the cruxes — including whether the persuasion/manipulation boundary is descriptive or irreducibly normative, and whether exploiting a known cognitive bias by means of entirely true information is manipulation; and, for each crux, the case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use, among others, the case of a salesperson who presents only true facts but in an order that exploits anchoring, and the case of a speaker who rouses fear with accurate statistics. If you judge part of the dispute to be merely verbal, say which part and why. Format the document as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
