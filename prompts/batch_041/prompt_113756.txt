Below are eight hypothetical transactions that a legislature might be asked to permit or forbid. Assume for each that the parties are competent adults, the terms are fully disclosed, and no one outside the transaction is affected, so that the familiar objections from coercion, fraud, and external harm are set aside.

1. Deathbed statement brokerage: a firm buys, from a dying person, the exclusive right to compose and publish that person's final public statement, and resells the text to the highest bidder.
2. Mourning understudies: an agency rents out professional strangers who attend a funeral and comport themselves as though they had been close to the deceased, so that the family appears more widely grieved.
3. Procreative offsets: a person pledges to remain childless and sells the resulting averted emissions as tradable credits.
4. Naming rights: a corporation pays a parent for the exclusive right to choose the child's legal given name.
5. Proxy forgiveness: someone who has wronged another pays an unrelated third party to perform, on the victim's behalf, a formal ceremony of forgiveness.
6. Diary futures: a publisher acquires the right to publish a person's private diaries, unredacted, after the diarist's death.
7. Attention leases: an advertiser pays a person for the right to occupy a fixed portion of that person's visual field, during waking hours, through an always-on headset.
8. Queue-standing: a person is paid to wait overnight in line for a concert ticket that the buyer will collect in the morning.

Call the expressive residue of a transaction whatever wrongfulness remains once the idealizations above are granted, i.e. wrongfulness attaching to the character of the act itself rather than to its causes, its side effects, or the way it would erode institutions if widely adopted.

Rank all eight by the size of their expressive residue, from largest to smallest, and assign each a score from 0 (no residue whatsoever) to 10 (a grave wrong even under the idealizations). Give one sentence of justification per item. Then identify the adjacent pair in your ordering that was hardest to separate and say what would have to be true for you to reverse it, and name the single item for which the idealizations do the most work, the one that looks worst before they are granted and much better after, explaining your choice in two or three sentences.
