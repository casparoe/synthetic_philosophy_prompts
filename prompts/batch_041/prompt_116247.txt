I am preparing a position statement for a workshop on laws of nature and would like to know whether it will survive scrutiny before I circulate it. Here is what I hold.

1. Lawhood is not primitive. The laws are the axioms of the best systematisation of the whole mosaic of non-nomic fact — the system that strikes the best balance of strength against simplicity — and once that system is fixed there is no further fact of the matter about which generalisations are laws. As I understand it, the mosaic is a distribution of local, intrinsic features over spacetime. Nomic necessity is not primitive either, but it is not for that reason unreal: it is the necessity those axioms induce, namely truth in every world that satisfies them.

2. Which generalisations are laws is a substantive, mind-independent matter. Physicists discover laws; they do not legislate them. That a generalisation belongs to a theory we find elegant, unifying, or easy to work with cannot be part of what makes it a law.

3. The laws are contingent. They could have been otherwise, and no law of nature is a metaphysical necessity.

4. Laws sustain counterfactuals in a way that accidental generalisations do not. That every gold sphere happens to be less than a mile across tells us nothing about what a sphere of a mile and a half would do; a law tells us what would have happened under conditions that do not obtain.

5. The special sciences state laws too. The hedged generalisations of biology and economics are not merely convenient summaries or rules of thumb; they are laws of nature if anything is.

6. Entanglement is a fundamental and irreducible feature of the world. The state of an entangled pair is not fixed by the states of its members, and no enrichment of the members' local states reproduces the joint statistics. I take this to be a result of physics rather than a metaphysical imposition.

I would rather not surrender any of these, and I do not know which of them, if any, is doing the damage. Which of them are genuinely in tension, and how serious is each tension? Please order the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, separate the conflicts that are real from the ones that dissolve once the commitments are stated more carefully, and for each real one say what would have to give and what that costs. If a single revision would clear up more than one of the problems at once, say which it is.
