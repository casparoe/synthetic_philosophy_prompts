I'm assembling a short position statement for a graduate seminar on mind and cognition, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Rather than have you tell me which views are popular, I want you to take the set below as my actual commitments and tell me which subsets cannot be held together. Here is where I have landed. (1) Propositional-attitude eliminativism: beliefs, desires, and intentions are not real states of anything; folk psychology is a degenerating predictive scheme whose posits will be (and ought to be) abandoned. (2) Scientific realism: our mature scientific theories are approximately true, and we should be realists about the entities they posit — but this realism is earned by a theory's track record, so we should withhold belief from entities that only a theory under constant ad hoc repair posits. (3) The special sciences are autonomous: psychology, economics, and linguistics are legitimate sciences whose generalizations are irreducible to physics, and I do not think they are merely placeholders for a future neuroscience. (4) Intentionality is real: the aboutness of thought — that a state can be directed at an object or content — is a genuine feature of the world, not a projection. (5) I am a realist about phenomenal consciousness; experiences have a qualitative character that is part of the world's furniture. (6) We are genuinely morally responsible for what we do, and responsibility requires that an agent have acted for reasons. (7) Introspection is a fallible but real source of knowledge about the contents of my own mind.

Some of these I take to be obviously compatible and some I suspect are not, but I have been unable to say which is which without begging the question in favor of the ones I like. What I want from you is an audit rather than a lecture: sort the pairs (or larger clusters) into those that are genuinely in tension, those that only look incompatible, and those that are mutually supporting; rank the genuine tensions by severity; and for each, say exactly what would have to be given up to restore consistency and what that concession costs. Please be explicit about which tensions are real and which dissolve, since I would rather hear that most of my set is fine than have every pair flagged as a problem. Finally, tell me whether there is a single revision that would repair the most damage at the least cost, or whether the honest conclusion is that the package is not one position but two.
