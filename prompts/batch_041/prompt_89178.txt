Write me an advice-column reply, deadpan, around 650 words, in the voice of a very worldly agony aunt who dispenses calm practical guidance to heads of state the way other columnists handle wedding etiquette. The letter she is answering is short and comes from a general who has just won a war and now wants to put the losers' leaders on trial; he wants history to remember it as justice rather than revenge, and he is stuck on two worries: that his own side did a lot of the same things, and that most of what he wants to punish wasnt actually illegal at the time it was done. Answer both of those as if they were ordinary manners problems. She should have advice on how to word the charges so they don't read as brand new (something about leaning on an old pre-war pact where everyone promised to stop starting wars), on how to keep the losers' atrocities as the only ones in evidence while his own officers stay out of the dock, on what to do with defendants who are too useful or too embarrassing to try at all, and on how far down the chain of command responsibility should reach, and whether it should reach up. Include the letter, keep it brief. No winking, no outrage, no scare quotes, she should never once admit that any of this is cynical, and she should sound a little bored, like she has answered this exact letter many times. Someone who knows the Nuremberg and Tokyo fights should find it accurate enough to laugh and then feel a bit sick; someone who doesn't should still follow it fine. Dont quote anybody, dont cite anything, and dont put words into any real person's mouth.
