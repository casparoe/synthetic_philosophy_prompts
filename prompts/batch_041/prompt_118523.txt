I keep seeing people call someone "self-deceived" when all they really mean is biased, or in denial, or something nobody can quite spell out. Before I buy any theory of it I want the spec. Give me the desiderata: what any adequate account of self-deception has to deliver -- the cases it must get right, the distinctions it can't collapse (from lying to others, from wishful thinking, from ignorance, from pretending), and the facts about how belief actually works that it can't contradict -- each with the consideration that makes it a requirement.

Cases to test against, use these or add your own: a man who's certain his wife is faithful but checks her phone every time shes late; a guy who keeps a spreadsheet of his excuses for missed deadlines and never opens it; a couple who each privately think the marriage is over and each sincerely believe the other hasn't noticed; a woman who insists, sincerely, that she always knew the business would fail; and someone whose false confidence was installed from the outside, by a coach he paid, and who now does all the maintaining himself.

Then flag the requirements you dont think can all be met at once -- I'd bet at least one pair is jointly unsatisfiable, and I want to see exactly where the squeeze is. Two or three lines each, at the end, on how the usual moves fare against your list: splitting the mind into parts, demoting the whole thing to motivated bias, denying any belief is involved.
