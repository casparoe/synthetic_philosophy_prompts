I've been arguing with a friend about whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible — cases in which a person is genuinely required to do each of two things and cannot do both. He holds that the very idea is incoherent, and his argument runs roughly as follows. Since 'ought' implies 'can', you can only be required to do what you are able to do. In the supposed dilemma you are unable to do both things. So you cannot be required to do both. So at least one of the two apparent requirements is not a real requirement at all, and what we call a dilemma is just a hard choice. He adds that if both requirements somehow stayed in force, morality would be contradicting itself, and a self-contradictory morality could not guide anyone — which is absurd. I couldn't fault the individual steps, but the conclusion feels far too strong, and I suspect the argument is quietly treating two requirements on me as though they added up to one requirement. Can you locate the mistake, if there is one, and say what it shows about what a genuine moral dilemma would actually have to be?
