Setup: I'm a Bayesian, roughly — learning something is conditonalizing on it, that's the whole game as far as I understand it. Now here's the argument:

(1) For a bit of evidence E to confirm a hypothesis H, learning E has to raise my credence in H: P(H|E) > P(H).
(2) Once I've learned E, my credence in E is 1. That's just what it is to have learned it.
(3) If P(E) = 1 then P(H|E) = P(H) — conditioning on something you're already certain of changes nothing.
(4) So once E is in hand it can't confirm H.
(5) And there's no hidden moment where the confirming gets done instead: it would have to be the instant I go from not having E to having it, which is a jump, not a stretch of reasoning I can do anything inside of.
(6) But all the evidence I ever have is evidence I've already got. I don't reason from stuff I haven't learned yet.
(7) So no evidence I possess ever confirms anything.
(8) Which means when I say Mercury's perihelion confirmed general relativity, or that the muddy footprints confirmed the butler, I'm saying something my own theory says can't be true.
(9) It gets worse. Proofs go the same way: by the time I've followed the proof I already had the axioms, so the conclusion was already there to be conditionalized on, so a proof can't confirm its theorem either.
(10) And I can never rationally change my mind about anything, because the evidence for the new view is, by the time I hold it, old.

I've been calling this spent evidence in my notes — evidence that's already been cashed in and so can't buy anything anymore. My worry is that by that logic *all* evidence is spent the moment you have it, so the phrase is doing no work and the whole notion of being convinced by evidence collapses.
