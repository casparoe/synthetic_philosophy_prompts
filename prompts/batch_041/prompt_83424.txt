A close friend and I have argued for the better part of a year about what makes a promise binding, and I would rather settle it by drafting the joint statement we should have written long ago than by repeating ourselves again. I hold that a promise binds only through the damage its breaking does — to the person who was counting on it, or to the general practice of taking people at their word — so that a promise broken in complete secrecy, where no one is harmed and no one's plans are disturbed, leaves nothing to answer for. My friend holds that the act of promising itself places you under an obligation, so that breaking such a promise wrongs the person you made it to even if she never learns of it and loses nothing by it.

Write the document we could both sign. I want the claims we can accept together about what promises are for and about when a broken promise calls for an answer; then our remaining disagreement stated twice, once in the words my friend would accept and once in mine; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us — and for each crux a concrete case that we both agree bears on it, with each of our predictions written down in advance. Please work through at least these three cases: a promise made to someone who has since died, where no living person knows the promise was made; a promise made to a friend who has since lost all memory of it; and a promise whose keeping turns out to do clear harm to a third party. If part of our dispute is really only about the word "obligation," or about whether someone can be wronged without being harmed, say so and say which part. Format the result as a numbered list of agreed claims followed by a table of disagreements.
