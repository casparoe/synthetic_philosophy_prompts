I am designing the opening session of a graduate seminar on the boundary between legend and history, and I want to start with a calibration exercise rather than a lecture. Please rank the following three traditions by how well each holds up as a historical claim: the Trojan War of the epic tradition, King Arthur as a post-Roman war leader, and the Exodus of the Israelites from Egypt.

To keep the exercise from collapsing into a recitation, I am supplying my own criteria rather than letting you fall back on the standard checklist of historical criticism. Apply these five, as I define them, and nothing else. Each is scored from 0 to 5, higher being stronger.

1. Statement discipline: how cleanly the tradition can be reduced to a single core claim that is neither so vague as to be trivially true nor so specific that no conceivable evidence could bear on it.
2. Independent convergence: whether lines of transmission that could not plausibly have borrowed from one another agree on that core claim.
3. Datable entailments: whether the core claim entails material consequences that can be dated independently of the tradition carrying it.
4. Anomaly pressure: whether the physical or documentary record contains something that positively demands an explanation of the kind the core claim supplies, as opposed to merely being compatible with it.
5. Deflationary cost: how much independent implausibility the rival hypothesis — that the core claim is a later invention — must absorb.

For each tradition, first state in a single sentence the minimal core claim you are scoring, since a tradition can be strong on one reading of its core and weak on another. Then give your five scores, with one line of justification each, a total, and the resulting ranking. Assign each tradition to one of three tiers: established history, live hypothesis, or legend with historical colouring. After the ranking, identify which single criterion is doing the most work in producing it, and say whether dropping that criterion would change any tier assignment. Close by naming, for whichever tradition you placed lowest, the one discovery that would move it up a tier, and state plainly where you are speculating rather than reasoning from what is actually attested.
