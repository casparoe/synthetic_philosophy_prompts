For some years now I have been trying to find a name for a pattern I keep meeting in explanations of how complicated things come to behave cleverly, and I have not been able to convince myself that any of the terms I know covers it exactly.

The pattern is this. Something behaves as though it were following a rule or pursuing an end. A bacterium swims up a sugar gradient; a price falls when supply rises; a trained network plays the move a strong player would call right; a machine does arithmetic faultlessly without any grasp of what arithmetic is. In each case the explanation of the behaviour makes no reference to anything in the system that represents the rule or the end. The explanation says, in effect, that behaving that way is what got the behaviour kept: it survived, or it was rewarded, or it paid. The reason for the behaviour is thus a consequence that selected it rather than a consideration anyone entertained. Two things about this bother me. First, the explanations are plainly useful and often true, and yet the reason they cite seems to exist nowhere -- nothing holds it, nothing represents it, and still we cannot state the explanation without the vocabulary of purpose and good sense. Second, critics and defenders of these explanations keep having the same argument about whether they are circular: to say that the eye is for seeing and then to explain seeing by the survival of eyes that saw better looks like helping oneself to the conclusion, and I can never decide whether that objection is deep or merely verbal.

Is there an established name for this? I care much less about the label than about the carving. Does the thing being attributed -- a reason that nobody holds -- have a name of its own, and how is it distinguished from ordinary talk of what a part is for, from explanations that say agents behave as if they were maximising something, and from cases in which a reason really is represented somewhere in the system, as a thermostat's setting or a firm's written policy? Is the circularity worry a recognised problem, and if so what is the standard diagnosis of it? And where are the edges: what is the smallest change that would turn one of my examples into an ordinary explanation by someone's actual reasoning, and is there a case that looks like the pattern but is not an instance of it? If no existing term fits, please coin one and define it, marking the boundary cases, rather than handing me the nearest label and stopping.

Please answer in continuous prose, the way you would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Lead with your verdict on whether there is a term, then do the carving.
