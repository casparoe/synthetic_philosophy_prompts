A colleague of mine has been using an analogy in a seminar to argue that the hard problem of consciousness is a pseudo-problem, and I would like it audited rather than dismissed. His reasoning runs roughly as follows. Liquidity is a genuine feature of water even though no individual molecule of H2O is liquid; liquidity emerges from how the molecules are arranged and interact, and no one treats this as evidence that physics leaves a residue unexplained. In the same way, he says, phenomenal consciousness is a real higher-level feature of certain neural systems rather than an extra ingredient added to them, and the felt mysteriousness of experience is an artefact of our concepts rather than a gap in the world. He extends the analogy further: on his view, properties such as being in pain are scale-relative in just the way liquidity is, so that asking which neuron is in pain is a category mistake of the same kind as asking which molecule is wet.

Please lay out the correspondence explicitly, a table being welcome: what plays the role of the molecules, their arrangement and interaction, the phase transition, the macro-level property, and the observer or instrument that detects it. Then tell me where the mapping breaks. I would like the disanalogies sorted into those that are incidental to his argument and those that are load-bearing for the conclusion that the hard problem dissolves, and in particular I want to know whether the analogy can carry the stronger scale-relativity claim or only the weaker claim that higher-level properties are real without being additional ingredients.

Two further questions. First, what does the analogy quietly import from the water case that the consciousness case may lack, and which of those imports is doing the persuasive work? Second, what does the analogy imply about a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person that lacks experience: does it render that possibility incoherent, as a request for water that is not H2O would be, and if so, is that a consequence of the analogy or a presupposition of it? Close by saying what a better comparison would be, if there is one — something that preserves what is genuinely right in the emergence picture while marking the respect in which experience is unlike other macro-properties — or else by arguing that no analogy of this kind can settle the matter, and why not.
