Since I began a long writing project last year I have become uncomfortably familiar with the gap between judging that I ought to be working and not working, and in trying to understand it I have ended up in two literatures that describe the same behaviour and cannot speak to each other. The philosophical discussion treats weakness of will as a problem about practical reason: an agent judges, all things considered, that one course of action is best, and then freely and intentionally does another; the puzzle is how that is so much as possible, given what a judgment of that kind is supposed to be. The economic discussion redescribes the same behaviour as present bias — a discount function steeper in the near term than the far term, the hyperbolic form, so that rankings reverse as options approach — and asks whether the agent anticipates the reversal or is naive about it, and what commitment devices she will buy. Since preferences there are read off choices, an agent who chooses against her stated judgment is not a puzzle but simply an agent whose preferences were not what she said they were; that deflationary move is what the philosophers will not accept. I suspect the impasse is not one disagreement but two questions wearing one name, with the word will hiding the seam, but I want that tested rather than endorsed. Consider two cases. Can akrasia occur at a single moment, when someone judges now that she should stop now and does not? And can the preference framework accommodate the akratic agent's retrospective verdict on what she did, or does the standpoint from which she condemns herself as weak-willed have no place in it? If the economic apparatus cannot handle either case, the two sides do not have the same phenomenon in view, and the disagreement is more than verbal. Diagnose it — verbal dispute, disagreement about which cases count as data, or two different questions under one heading — and say what would count as progress, or whether none should be expected.
