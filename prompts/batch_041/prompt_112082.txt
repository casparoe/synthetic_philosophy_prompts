ok, I'm trying to get my views on democratic epistemology straight and I want a consistency check before I say any of this out loud in a seminar. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. On factual questions, the Condorcet jury theorem is the strongest epistemic argument for democracy going. Bigger groups of better-than-chance voters get more reliable, and that's the deepest reason to trust a crowd over a clever individual.
2. But the independence assumption in the theorem isn't a technicality, it's the whole ballgame, and real electorates violate it constantly. People share the same newspapers, the same teachers, the same priors, the same feeds.
3. I've got this picture I call the one-newspaper town: if every voter in town gets their facts from the same source, then adding more voters doesn't add any accuracy at all. The crowd is exactly as reliable as that one source, no matter how big it gets. And I think a lot of real politics is a one-newspaper town.
4. I also think deliberation before voting is good. It tends to make people individually more accurate, and more accurate voters is exactly what the theorem rewards.
5. I'm convinced by the diversity-trumps-ability stuff: a bunch of diverse, mediocre reasoners will beat a bunch of individually sharper but similar ones.
6. And on any given factual question I think the right move is to defer to whoever actually knows, even if that's a small number of experts.

Do these hang together? I suspect at least one pair is rotten but I honestly can't tell which, and I'd rather you tell me which one has to go than reassure me it's all fine. Order the tensions by how bad they are, tell me which ones are only apparent and why they dissolve, and for the real ones say what the cheapest fix is and what I lose by making it. Be specific about which commitment is doing the damage.

Also be upfront about where your own reasoning here is most likely to be wrong. What would have to be true for you to give me a different verdict, or which of my six you're least sure how to read and why that changes your answer?
