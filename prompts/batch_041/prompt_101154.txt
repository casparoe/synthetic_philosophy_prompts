I am a philosophy student sitting in on a decision theory reading group that has been working through puzzles of imperfect recall, and we keep returning to the absent-minded driver. The story, as it was set up for us, is this: a driver has to get home by continuing along a road that passes through two intersections; at each intersection she may either continue or turn off; she cannot remember, at any point, whether she has already passed the first; turning off at the first intersection leaves her stranded far from home, turning off at the second is exactly what she wants, and continuing past both carries her beyond her turning and is a mild loss. At our last meeting one of us defended her continuing by saying that a rational agent maximises expected utility, and then, barely ten minutes later, someone else used the very same phrase to argue that she must turn off at the first intersection, since she cannot tell where she is and so must treat the decision as a gamble and take the branch that is not catastrophic. Both of them seemed to think the slogan settled the matter, and I could not tell whether it favoured one of them, both of them, or neither.

Could you lay out, as a menu I can take back to the group, the distinct precise claims that 'maximise expected utility' might be making here — separating, in particular, what exactly is being maximised (the particular act before her now, a policy fixed in advance, a standing disposition or rule for choosing, the expected value of a whole course of action), whose utility is in question, and the time and the information relative to which the maximisation is performed — and then say of each reading whether it is a harmless platitude, a substantive and defensible principle, or simply false? For each reading I should also like to know whether it is the one that would actually support my first friend's conclusion, my second friend's, or neither, and whether there is any reading under which the slogan genuinely settles the driver's case rather than merely restating the answer in different words.
