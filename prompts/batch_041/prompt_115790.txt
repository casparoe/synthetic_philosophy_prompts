Two friends of mine, Nadia and her brother Sam, went at each other at dinner last night about a guy we all know who blew his savings on crypto, got wiped out, and then needed an expensive course of treatment that the public system only partly covered, so the state ended up topping him up. I got asked to referee and honestly couldn't, so I'm handing it to you. Four rounds:

(1) Nadia: he's exactly what the luck egalitarians are willing to live with. His bad position traces to choices rather than brute luck, so it's option luck, so equality isn't offended and he's owed nothing beyond whatever minimum we all agree on anyway. Sam: look at how the loss is actually distributed. Two guys make the identical bet, one wins and one loses. The loser's position relative to the winner's is entirely unchosen — neither of them picked the coin. So declining to compensate the loser means permitting an inequality that, at the level of the comparison, is brute luck all the way down. Nadia: the comparison isn't the unit of assessment. The question is whether his position is worse than it would have been had he not bet, and that gap is one he chose to risk.

(2) Sam: then everything hangs on how you individuate the choice, and "he chose to bet" isn't the only description available. Did he choose to take a risk, or did he choose to take a risk *with the risk tolerance he happens to have*, which he didn't pick? If it's the second, then option luck only ever applies to people lucky enough to be prudent, which is a funny place for a theory about luck to end up. Nadia: take that seriously and nobody is ever responsible for anything, since the self behind every choice is unchosen — at which point you've just got plain outcome egalitarianism with extra steps. She said the regress stops at "could he have known better at the time," and that not choosing your dispositions doesn't make your choices not yours.

(3) Sam: fine, but own the cost. On your view we can leave a man to die of a treatable illness becuase he made bad bets. Nadia: no version of it actually says that; the standard fix is a floor, where the luck principle governs everything above a decent minimum and nobody falls below it whatever they've done. Sam: then you've quietly switched to sufficiency and the luck part is just decoration. Nadia: a side-constraint isn't the same as abandoning a view, every theory has them.

(4) Sam tacked on one more consideration at the end and Nadia said it was beside the point: not compensating him isn't leaving him where he was, since someone pays either way — the hospital, his sister, or him by dying — and those are costs landing on people who never chose his bet.

Go through this move by move — rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right and say briefly why — then tell me what the two of them are both missing and who has the better of it overall.
