I have read the blog-level version of this debate for years, and I want the specification rather than the survey: what any theory of imprecise credence has to deliver before I would trust it in an agent that must act on its uncertainty, update on evidence, and hand a human a single number when asked. Fix the purpose that way, then write the desiderata — the cases it must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the results it cannot contradict — each with the consideration that motivates it, marked as non-negotiable or as the taste of one camp. Include at least these: the Ellsberg choice, where a range rather than a point looks like what the evidence supports; dilation, where an observation you take to be irrelevant to X widens your interval for X, and the theory must say whether that is a discovery or a bug; the demand that the width of an interval answer to something, so that imprecision represents evidence rather than being a free parameter; a long run of evidence, where I want to know whether convergence to a point is required, permitted, or forbidden; and the moment of action, where acting on the worst case, on anything undominated, and on the best case come apart and the theory must still yield a choice. The part I most want is the incompatibility: I suspect that conditioning on the evidence, treating the interval as genuine evidential support, and never dilating cannot all hold, and I want that flagged as a result rather than smoothed over. Skip the scorecard for particular proposals; the criteria are the deliverable.
