Security engineer here, protocol design mostly, and I've been re-reading the Location of Meaning chapter of GEB on and off for years. Hofstadter's three layers — frame message, outer message, inner message (I can never keep the first two straight; roughly it's this-is-a-message-at-all, here's-how-to-decode-it, here's-the-content) — are supposed to settle where the meaning of a thing actually lives: in the DNA, in the ribosome, in the receiver's prior knowledge. I want the whole argument restated in the vocabulary I actually think in — plaintext and ciphertext, keys, out-of-band setup, handshakes and protocol negotiation, headers and content-type, magic bytes, MACs, known-plaintext attacks, forward secrecy, traffic analysis, and the difference between information in the Shannon sense and actual content. Do it honestly; don't just relabel his biology as crypto and call that a translation.

Then the part I actually care about: what doesn't survive the trip. Which of his claims are really just protocol agreement in disguise (I suspect most of them, and fine), which ones only come through if you fudge — my guess is anything about the decoder needing the right isomorphism, since in my world the decoder's structure is fixed and public and the only secret is the key — and which have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary. There's also something crypto has that I don't think he does: an adversary, who sees the frame and the outer message and still can't get at the inner one. Tell me if that's a genuinely new axis or just a reciever who lacks the key, which he'd say is a receiver without the right isomorphism.

And then the verdict: is the leftover a sign my vocabulary is missing something, or a sign his way of putting it was less clear than it sounds? I'd rather hear the second, if it's true.

No reading list please, I'm not going to go off and read the literature — I just want the translation and the residue.
