I have been trying to understand why political philosophers treat republican liberty as a third concept, distinct from the familiar negative and positive ones. As I reconstruct the three: negative liberty is non-interference; positive liberty is self-mastery or self-realization; and republican liberty is "non-domination." But I cannot see what the third adds. If no one is actually interfering with me, I am free; that someone merely could interfere — a landlord with the legal right to evict me at will, an employer who could dismiss me tomorrow — seems to me a matter of security or prudence, not a further kind of unfreedom. On this reading, republicans have not discovered a new concept of liberty; they have simply redescribed a standing threat as a loss of freedom. Please explain whether this reconstruction is mistaken, and, if it is, what the republican idea of domination captures that the absence of interference does not. End with a single line beginning "Misconception:" that names the false presupposition in at most fifteen words.
