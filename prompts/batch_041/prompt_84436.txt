A friend of mine who teaches young children argues that the long dispute between Mencius and Xunzi over human nature is settled by a single analogy: moral development is like a seed growing into a plant. You cannot make a seedling flower by pulling on it, she says; you can only supply soil, water, and light and then let it grow. She concludes that moral cultivation is a matter of provision rather than reshaping, and that Xunzi's programme of deliberately remaking our nature is a mistake. Audit the analogy. Set out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the seed, the soil, the gardener, the mature plant — then mark where the mapping breaks and say which breaks matter for her conclusion and which do not. Address in particular whether the analogy can bear the weight she puts on it against Xunzi or whether it quietly assumes what is in dispute; whether a plant model leaves room for the agent's own effort, given that Mencius's own image of a farmer who tried to hurry his crop by pulling on it warns against force but also against neglect; and how Xunzi's rival image of moral formation as raw material worked into shape by deliberate effort bears on the comparison. Close by saying whether any version of her conclusion survives the disanalogies, and what analogy, if any, would do the job better.
