I'm a software engineer by training, and this term I've been co-teaching a graduate seminar on explanation in cognitive science with a philosopher colleague. She's away for the final, so the exam is mine to write, and I'd like your help drafting one essay question with a model answer and a marking scheme. The exam is closed-book and offline: students can't look anything up, so everything a student needs must be contained in the question itself. I also don't want marks to turn on recall of names, papers, or dates, so please don't put any real authors or works into the question, the model answer, or the marking scheme.

The unit being examined uses two pairs of labels. A skyhook is a posited capacity or entity that does explanatory work but is not itself the product of any lower-level process — it lifts without having been lifted. A crane is a device assembled out of materials already available at the lower level; it lifts, but it is itself lifted into place by prior processes. Separately, greedy reductionism is the attempt to explain a higher-level regularity directly in terms of the lowest-level facts while leaving the intervening levels unaccounted for. Students have read the standard material on all of this, so assume they can produce a summary; I want the question to separate the ones who can actually operate the distinctions from the ones who can only recite them.

Here is the stimulus I've written for the question, invented for this exam and shown to students as part of it:

A lab studies how people come to follow arbitrary conventions, such as which side of a sidewalk to walk on. In its preprint, the lab argues that conformity is 'reduced' to reward learning: people conform because the brain treats social approval as a prediction-error signal, and the lab reports that individual differences in that signal track how quickly subjects adopt a group's convention. The preprint also posits a 'conformity drive' — a disposition to align with whatever the local majority does — and treats that drive as a primitive the theory does not decompose. A commentator calls the whole thing a textbook case of greedy reductionism.

Please write the essay question around this stimulus. I want it to require three things: first, that students state a crane/skyhook test and a greedy-reductionism test as short procedures that someone hostile to the proposal could still apply, each saying what evidence would flip the verdict; second, that they apply both tests to the stimulus and say whether the commentator's charge lands and on which part of the preprint, distinguishing what the tests condemn from what they vindicate; and third, that they say what the lab would have to do to convert the 'conformity drive' into a crane, and take a side on whether the whole framework survives if the levels picture is only a convention of exposition. Then give me a model answer of roughly 650 words — what a strong student could actually produce in about fifty-five minutes — and a marking scheme worth 20 marks, built from five must-hit points at 4 marks each, with a one-line note for each point on the mistake that typically costs students the marks.

Open your response with the exam question itself, with no preface and no description of what you're about to do, and don't restate the question anywhere afterward. Within the model answer, put the bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning.
