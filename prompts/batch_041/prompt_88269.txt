I run user research at a mid-size software company: think-aloud sessions, five or six a week, and each one has to turn into a one-page readout that a product director signs off on and that actually moves the roadmap. What I can never do is decide how much weight to put on what a participant says about their own head. I know the standard moves — people have access to what they're thinking but not to why, so a fluent account of a choice is usually a story built afterward; confidence doesnt track accuracy, and the slicker the explanation the more I should suspect it; and there's the classic result where people give confident reasons for a choice that was really determined by something they never noticed — but I can't put a paragraph of theory next to every quote, and "self-report is unreliable" is useless when I still have to write the doc.

So give me a procedure I can run on one line of transcript in under a minute: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops, and where it hands the call back to me because it's a judgment and not a rule. I want it to seperate a report about what someone is experiencing right now ("this text is too small to read") from a report about why they did something, and to do something useful with the third kind, the verdict — "it was pretty easy, I'd use it" — which I suspect is nearly contentless but I keep putting in the doc anyway. One test I half-believe and want in there: if the participant could have worked out what they say from the same stuff I can see — the screen, their clicks, the task — then it isn't privileged access, it's just them guessing about themselves the way I'd guess about them.

Then run it on three from last week: a guy who says, unprompted and very smoothly, "I clicked the third result because I always trust official-looking sources"; a woman who insists she never saw the cookie banner, which was on screen the whole time; and "honestly the whole thing felt pretty easy." Flag the places where it can't help and I just have to decide, and if it says something about my own instincts I won't like, say it anyway. No follow-up studies and no more participants — this has to work on what's already in the transcript.
