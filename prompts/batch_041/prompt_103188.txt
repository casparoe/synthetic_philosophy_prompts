Im giving a talk at our consciousness reading group in a few weeks and I need to get ready for the Q&A, so I want you to write me a prep sheet of the objections I'll actually get from this room.

Heres what I'm defending. Illusionism, but a somewhat non-standard version. The usual line is that phenomenal consciousness as normally conceived doesnt exist -- no qualia, no intrinsic ineffable feels -- and that the belief in it is a cognitive illusion, introspection failing to represent its own workings. I accept that much, but I want to push two extra claims. First, that the illusion isnt just cognitive but metacognitive in a specific way: introspection doesnt merely fail to represent the experience, it positively misattributes the *content* of the experience to the experience itself, so the redness I see gets coded as a property of my seeing it. Second, and this is the part I think is more original, that the illusion is pragmatically sustained -- we keep it going partly for social and communicative reasons, because treating experiences as private ineffable inner objects is useful for certain kinds of coordination and for assigning blame. So its not just a bug, its a bug we keep paying for.

My argument sketch: the metacognitive misattribution predicts that when you disrupt introspection you should get reports of pure awareness or contentless consciousness; and I want to argue the contemplative reports are actually evidence FOR illusionism rather than against it, because theyre reports of the introspection machinery running with nothing to grab, which the subject then misdescribes as encountering consciousness itself.

The audience: its a mixed reading group. Mostly analytic philosophers of mind, and a lot of them are unapologetic qualia realists -- they think of qualia as intrinsic, ineffable, private, and theyll be irritated by the whole project. But theres also two people from the meditation / contemplative-science lab across the hall who work on no-self and on so-called pure consciousness experiences, and they think Im both stealing their thunder and getting it exactly backwards. And theres one hard eliminativist in the dept who thinks illusionism is a fence-sitting cop-out and will say so loudly.

Give me the prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from *this* room, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would put it, with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Include the one question Im hoping nobody asks. And flag any objection where I should just concede up front instead of fighting it.
