My dads retiring next month after 34 years as a city traffic engineer — signals, crosswalks, the whole thing — and I'm giving the toast at the dinner. Thirty-odd people: my mom's side, who'll cry at anything, and a solid block of his old colleagues from the department, engineers to the bone, who will visibly wince if it gets sentimental or if it sounds like I'm accusing him of anything. Here's what I actually want the toast built on. For the last few years before he retired he fought over one crossing on Fourth Street: whether you have to press a button for the walk signal, or whether it just comes around every cycle on its own. He thinks that's an engineering question about warrants and pedestrian volumes. I think its a question about whether the street treats you as a user or as a guest. And the version I'd really like the speech to land is this: when a design makes an option unavailable, the argument about that option just stops happening, and nobody in the room experiences it as having lost an argument. That's how something gets settled without leaving fingerprints. But don't let it turn into the standard design-is-political sermon — half the room has heard the low-bridge example a hundred times, and the other half will hear 'my dad was a villain', which is the opposite of what I want. Give me the steelman of his side somewhere in the middle so it doesn't read as a hit job. Also please don't use the word politics at all, it will kill the room. Four minutes, warm, and it should honor what he actually did all day, which was decide thousands of times who waits. He gets a reply, so leave him something to say. No jargon, and don't quote anyone — he'd hate that.
