I am a literary translator under contract to render a collection of formally constrained poems into English: lipograms, acrostics, syllable-counted and rhyme-locked forms, and at least one text whose governing rule is invisible to a casual reader. The decisions come one after another for months, the deadline is fixed, I work alone, and my editor — not I — has the final say on any loss I flag. Give me a decision procedure I can run on a single poem in under an hour.

Ground it in the lesson of Le Ton beau de Marot, where dozens of defensible English versions of one short Renaissance French poem show that what has to survive translation is not the words but the work the words do. Build the procedure on a classification of what a constraint is for: ornamental (felt rather than decoded), constitutive (the text is about its own rule), mnemonic (aids recitation), covert (carries a message to insiders), or ironic (performed while mocked). Then give the ordered tests, what counts as passing each, and the point where the procedure stops and hands the call to my editor because the question is aesthetic rather than analytic. Distinguish translating the constraint, substituting a constraint native to English, compensating elsewhere in the text, and dropping it, and say what each costs and when it is defensible. Add a note on constraint drift — the same rule producing a different effect in the new language — and on visibility: whether the English reader is meant to perceive the rule at all.

Run it on three cases: a lipogram forbidding the commonest letter in English, in a poem about absence; an acrostic spelling a name that will not fit the English line; and a metrically strict poem whose metre is invisible to contemporary readers of English. Include one ruling I will not like, and say plainly what the procedure cannot decide.

End each case with a line beginning Ruling: followed by one of the labels preserve, substitute, compensate, drop, or refer to editor. End the whole response with a single line \answer{...} giving the default rule in at most twelve words.
