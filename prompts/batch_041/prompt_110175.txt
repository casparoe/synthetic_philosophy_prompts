I've been trying to get my head round epiphenomenalism — the idea that the feeling of pain, or the redness of red, is like the steam off a kettle: real, but not doing any pushing. What trips me up is that I can talk about it. I say 'ow', I compare shades, I argue with people about whether my red is their red. If the feeling isn't causing any of that, what am I actually talking about? Please argue for a side: either epiphenomenalism can make sense of how we come to report and reason about our own experiences, or it can't, and the view undermines itself.

If you defend it, I want the actual machinery, not just 'the brain state does the work'. I think the move has to be that the feeling is what my report is about even though it isn't what makes me say it — and I dont see how something can be about a thing without being hooked up to it in some way. Test whatever you come up with on this case: someone wired (hypnotised, whatever) so they'll announce 'I see red' no matter what's in front of them, and they happen to be looking at something blue when the words come out. Normally I'd call that a lie or a glitch. But if the feeling and the saying were never connected in the first place, what has actually gone wrong there, and is there any fact of the matter about what they saw? Take a position and stick to it.
