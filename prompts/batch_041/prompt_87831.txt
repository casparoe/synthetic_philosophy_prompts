In AP U.S. History we spent a week on Reconstruction, and our class visited the county historical society, where the archivist showed us how thin the surviving record is for the Black families who farmed in our county in the 1870s: a few tax entries, a handful of newspaper notices, almost nothing in their own words. When I asked how a historian could ever write about those families, a classmate said, "Well, history is written by the victors," and the discussion more or less ended there. Our teacher nodded, and I nodded too, but the more I think about it the less I know what that sentence is supposed to mean, and I would like to know precisely which version of it my classmate was relying on when he used it to close the question.

Please lay out the distinct claims that "history is written by the victors" could be making, ordered from the weakest to the strongest, and for each one say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false. I am especially interested in the readings that sound alike but are not: the difference between who produces the documents and who decides what gets preserved and catalogued; the difference between being recorded and being represented; the difference between the claim that the surviving record is skewed and the claim that there is no fact of the matter about the past at all; and the difference between a claim about the archive and a claim about what gets taught, memorialized, or repeated. For each reading, tell me whether it would actually support my classmate's conclusion — that we simply cannot know anything about those families — or whether it cuts the other way, and if it cuts the other way, explain how. It also seems to me that the losing side often has the most reason to write, since it has a defeat to explain, which makes me suspect the slogan is at least partly false as it is usually stated; I would like you to say whether that observation damages all of the readings or only some of them, and which ones.

I would also like you to say which reading you think most people intend when they say it, and to be honest about how confident you can be in that diagnosis, since you cannot survey anyone.

Finally, please tell me what would change your mind: which argument, example, or piece of evidence would move you off your verdict on whichever reading you think is most defensible, and where your reasoning on this is most likely to fail.
