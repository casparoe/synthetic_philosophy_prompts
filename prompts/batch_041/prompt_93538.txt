ok so I'm a novelist and I've been trying to get my head straight on identity — partly for the book I'm drafting, partly just for me — and I've realised I believe a handful of things that might not all fit together. I'd like a consistency check before I build a whole novel on top of them. Here's where I've landed:

1. What survives when a person survives is psychological continuity. The memories, the character, the ongoing intentions and projects carry over, and that's what makes tomorrow's person me. The atoms don't matter, the particular brain tissue doesn't matter — only the psychology.

2. But the deeper thing, and honestly the one I actually care about, is that a person is the story they're living. Who someone is, is the narrative that makes their life hang together — the past as they hold it, the future as they project it, the whole thing as a shape. Give me a person with an identical psychology but a completely different history and I want to say it isn't them. Amnesia doesn't erase the person either, because a story can absorb a break.

3. For my books: a novel is its text. Change enough of the words and it's a different novel, full stop. I don't think there's a ghostly 'the same book' hovering above the sentences. Which means a translation, however faithful, is a different novel.

4. Characters are a different case though. A character is the same character across drafts as long as they're doing the same work in the story — same role, same function — even if I change every fact about them: their name, their past, their personality, all of it.

5. And on the old ship puzzle — planks swapped out one by one, then the originals reassembled from the scrap — I've always thought there's no fact of the matter. 'Same ship' is something we settle by picking a way of counting, not something we discover.

I'm fairly sure some of these are pulling against each other and I can't tell which ones, and I'm also fairly sure one of the clashes is only apparent and I'm inventing it. So: which pairs genuinely conflict, how bad is each, and what's the cheapest fix — and what does that fix cost me? Rank them by severity, don't tell me everything's in tension, and skip the reading list (I'm offline on a residency for a month, no library).
