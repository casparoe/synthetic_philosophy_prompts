His summary: In counterfactual mugging, Omega, a perfect predictor, flips a coin. If it lands tails, Omega asks you for $100. If it lands heads, Omega hands you $10,000, provided it predicted beforehand that you would have paid in the tails case. Once you are actually being asked for the money, the coin has already landed tails, so paying buys you nothing, and the whole question is whether your decision can reach across into the other branch of the coin flip. Causal and evidential decision theories both tell you to refuse, which is why people say the standard theories are simply irrational on this case; the theorists who tell you to pay do so because they think your choice exerts a kind of backwards or acausal influence on what happened in the heads branch. Since everyone agrees that you should pre-commit to paying if you can, the only sensible answer is to pay. XOR blackmail is the same puzzle with a blackmailer in place of Omega, and the name refers to the fact that you cannot tell whether the letter arrived because of the bomb or because of you.
