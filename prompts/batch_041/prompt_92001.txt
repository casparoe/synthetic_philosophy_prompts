Ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole on the gender-equality paradox and I want to actually pin down a credence instead of just vibing about it. Here's the claim I'm trying to put a number on:

(C) The cross-national pattern in which richer, more gender-egalitarian countries show bigger sex differences in field-of-study and occupational sorting (fewer women in engineering/CS, more in care and education work) is real, and is better explained by evolved sex differences in interests than by institutions, labor-market structure, or how the equality index itself is built.

I'm sitting at about 0.35. Give me your own number, but mostly I want the guts of it: break C into the sub-questions it actually rests on, give each a rough probability, show me how they combine, and tell me which one my 0.35 is most sensitive to.

Sub-questions I'd want covered (add any you think I'm missing):

- Is the pattern even robust, or is it mostly GDP per capita / general development wearing a gender-equality costume? The usual equality indices are heavily collinear with wealth, and some of them are dominated by stuff like share of parliamentary seats, which has nothing obvious to do with how men and women sort into jobs.
- Is the outcome measure even measuring 'choice'? If a country has a big public sector and a compressed wage structure, the STEM wage premium shrinks and everyone sorts out of it, men and women both — so a low share of women in STEM might be about the shape of the labor market, not about women's preferences.
- Conditional on the pattern being real, how much of it is 'equality removes constraints so underlying preferences show through' vs. 'the preferences themselves are culturally shaped, and egalitarian countries just shape them differently'? I keep feeling these two get run together and I want to seperate them.
- The inference itself: even if less constraint lets a sex difference show more, does a *bigger* expressed difference count as evidence that the difference is innate?
- And the justice bit: suppose C is true — how much should that move someone who thinks we should push for equal representation as an outcome? I want a rough number on that too, and why it's not bigger or smaller than people assume.

I'd call the pattern 'real' if it survives swapping the equality index for something more directly about labor-market equality (pay gap, female labor-force participation) while controlling for income level. And I'd call the nature reading supported if the same ordering shows up in pre-verbal kids across very different cultures, or in our close primate relatives — though I realize thats its own inferential leap and might be doing more work than it should.

Also, tell me how confident you actually are in all this, and single out the one sub-claim you're least sure about.
