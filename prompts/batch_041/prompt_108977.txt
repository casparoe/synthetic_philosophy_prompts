Okay, so — I'm a freelance journalist who writes long features on philosophy and the sciences, and I've got a shot at a small reporting-and-research grant (about three months, enough for one 8,000-word piece plus a short public talk). I want to spend it on the suffering-focused ethics crowd — the negative utilitarians, the effective-altruism-adjacent people who think the reduction of suffering is basically the whole ballgame — but not another "is negative utilitarianism too demanding?" piece, that's been done to death.

My half-formed idea, and I'd like you to turn it into a proper proposal: I keep noticing that the whole debate seems to run on one unexamined assumption, that suffering is a single kind of thing that can be compared, aggregated, and given lexical priority over everything else. But I think there are actually two things people mean, and they keep sliding between them without noticing. One is suffering as a feeling-state — a bad experience happening now, in a creature, full stop. The other is suffering as a life going badly — a relation between a moment and a whole trajectory, where how bad it is depends on how the moment fits into a story, what it's for, whether it was chosen, whether it's part of something the person endorses. And I suspect most of the hard cases in this literature (depression, chronic pain, the suffering of creatures with no narrative self to speak of, the question of whether a long life of mild contentment beats a short life of agony) come out differently depending on which one you're using — and that a lot of the disagreement between the camps is really just people using different ones without realizing it.

So what I want from you is a proposal for the reporting-and-analysis piece: state the question precisely enough that it has a real answer, say why the existing camps (the negative utilitarians, the suffering-focused EAs, the folks who push back with "but pleasure matters too") don't already settle it, and give me a method — what I'd actually do over the three months, who I'd try to talk to, what I'd read, and how I'd test the distinction against cases rather than just asserting it. Also tell me what would count as the piece failing, and whether this is realistically doable in three months or if I'm kidding myself. Don't give me a bibliography or a reading list, I'll chase the sources myself, but you can name positions and arguments. And be honest about the biggest risk: that my two-kinds-of-suffering distinction is just a restatement of something alot of people already say with a different label, in which case I need to know that now and not in month two.
