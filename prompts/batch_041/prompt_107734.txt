my reading group is finishing a unit on testimony next week -- the old fight about whether you need evidence for each particular thing a person tells you before you're entitled to believe it, or whether taking people at their word is the default and the burden runs the other way. i want to open the session with a pantoum, because the form hands lines forward from stanza to stanza so they come back meaning something different each time, and thats the phenomenon in miniature: a claim travels and its credit goes up or down depending on who's carrying it.

can you write it so the chain of who-heard-it-from-whom actually gets longer as it goes, and by the last stanza nobody in the poem is in a position to check the original? make the thing being passed along small and boring (a neighbours remark about a well, whether a road was ever built) so that the demand to verify every link looks like what it is in ordinary life, which is slightly mad -- and then give one stanza to a kid repeating a phrase they only half understand, since thats the case where earning it from scratch seems to have no first step. i also want the sceptic in the poem to be someone who only ever heard the doubt from somebody else, so their own demand is inherited too. end on the same line you open with so the whole thing loops -- a reader should feel the loop as the problem, not the resolution. four or five quatrains is fine.

don't use the words testimony, justification, warrant or epistemic; i want it shown, not labeled. someone who knows the debate should be able to point at the stanza thats the challenge and the stanza thats the reply, with neither side winning. start with the poem itself, no preamble.
