Amit says of course she knows it, that's just how testimony works, he knows the capital of Peru without having been there. Then they went back and forth, roughly like this:

(1) Debs: aesthetic knowledge is different. To know a film is good you have to have seen it. Believing it's good on someone's say-so is like believing a joke is funny because someone told you it's funny.

(2) Amit: that's just a special-pleading claim about art. If the essay is reliable, and the film really is a masterpiece, and Rosa believes it because of the essay, then she knows it. Full stop. The Peru case is the same structure, he says, not a disanalogy.

(3) Debs: no, the difference is what "masterpiece" is doing in the sentence. When you call a film a masterpiece you're not just reporting a fact about the film, you're claiming everyone ought to agree with you, and you're only entitled to that claim if it's grounded in your own response to the film. Rosa isn't entitled to it. She's relaying someone else's entitlement.

(4) Amit: even granting that, it's a point about manners, not about knowledge — being pushy about a belief you aren't entitled to doesn't stop the belief being knowledge. But he doesn't grant it anyway. He thinks Rosa's judgment *is* grounded in her own response, just not to the film — she responded to the essay's reasons. And he offered a distinction: testimony can be either the source of an aesthetic belief or a pointer to it, and Rosa's case is really the pointer case, because being genuinely convinced by an essay means you saw *why* the reasons work.

(5) Debs: that makes "pointer" swallow "source" — every belief acquired from testimony can be redescribed as "well, you appreciated the reasons," so the distinction stops doing any work. And she added: appreciating a reason for thinking a film is good isn't the same as appreciating the film. The essay can tell you the film is formally daring; it can't make you feel the daring.

(6) Amit, last word: the clincher is that Rosa finally saw it last month and agreed with everything, so she was right all along, and being right from a reliable source is knowledge.
