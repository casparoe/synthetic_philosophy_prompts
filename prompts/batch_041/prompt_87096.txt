I am a PhD student in political theory working on Dewey's experimentalism about democracy and inquiry, and I need a thought experiment built to a fairly precise specification. The aim is to isolate one variable: whether an inquiry is conducted as a shared, experimental practice by those affected, or as an expert-led problem-solving exercise that arrives at the same substantive decision. The case should make Dewey's position recommend the shared practice even though the expert-led procedure is faster, cheaper, and no less accurate with respect to the decision. Constraints: the setting must be a realistic local institution—a school board, a municipal water authority, a union local, or the like; there should be no villains, no manipulation, and no suggestion that the experts are incompetent or self-interested; and the case must not turn on the superior wisdom of crowds or on the intrinsic value of participation in the abstract. The work should be done by Dewey's conception of inquiry as the directed transformation of an indeterminate situation into a determinate one, and by his conception of democracy as a mode of associated living. The case is meant to isolate the difference between outcome-oriented and process-oriented justifications of democratic inquiry, so it fails if the Deweyan verdict is merely stipulated, or if the shared practice is made to produce a better decision. After presenting the scenario—which should be compact, no more than a paragraph or two—reconstruct the Deweyan reasoning that generates the verdict: explain the roles of inquiry, democracy, and the public in the case, and why the shared practice is recommended even when the outcome is identical. Then identify the single feature of the case doing the most work, say what happens to the verdict if that feature is removed, and state the strongest objection from a realist or epistemic democrat, together with how a Deweyan should reply. I am not asking for citations, textual exegesis, or a literature review; I want the argument reconstructed in your own terms.
