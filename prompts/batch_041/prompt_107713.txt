I'm a grad student in philosophy, originally from Kolkata, and I've been a pretty committed Millian since undergrad. I just found out, embarrassingly recently, that Mill spent most of his working life at the East India Company and wrote a lot about India and colonial rule. Now I'm trying to figure out if my own Millian package actually hangs together or if I've just been cherry-picking. Here's what I hold:

(1) Utilitarianism: right action is whatever maximizes overall happiness, no exceptions for rights.
(2) The harm principle: the only justification for interfering with someone's liberty is to prevent harm to others.
(3) Free speech absolutism: even false and offensive opinions should be protected, because we need them to test our own.
(4) Representative government is the best form of government, because it educates people into public reason and protects their interests.
(5) Some colonial rule can be justified as temporary tutelage, if it's genuinely preparing a society for self-government and is administered in the subjects' interests.
(6) Generally no intervention in other countries' internal affairs.

I also have a concrete verdict I want tested: I think a British colonial governor in the 1860s would have been wrong to shut down a native-language newspaper that was advocating Indian self-rule, even if he thought it was stirring up dangerous discontent. But I think he would have been within his rights to require that newspaper to publish a government reply alongside its editorials.

Can these hang together? I suspect some of them can't, but I don't know which. Please don't just list all the possible tensions. Give me your actual best audit. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, how severe is each, and what's the cheapest revision that would fix the worst one? I'd rather you take a definate position on what I should give up than give me a menu. Also, if you think my concrete verdict is the thing that doesn't fit, say so directly.
