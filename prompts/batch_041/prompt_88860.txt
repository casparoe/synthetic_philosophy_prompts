I work on the safety layer of a large AI assistant, and my team has been stuck for weeks on what the assistant should do when a user asks for something the system has flagged: a request that looks harmful, or self-destructive, or that the user will very plausibly regret. Our current policy is a patchwork of refusals, and nobody on the team defends it.

One of our designers wants to replace refusal entirely with a procedure she calls the friction protocol. Nothing is ever forbidden. When a request is flagged, the assistant does three things in order: it asks the user to state, in their own words, what they are trying to accomplish; it explains plainly what its concern is; and then it proceeds, but only after the user restates the request in light of that exchange, and only after a fixed delay, short for small things and long for serious ones. She argues this is the only defensible design. It treats the user as an adult, it makes the assistant's objection visible and answerable instead of an invisible veto, and it sorts the user who is acting deliberately from the user who is on autopilot.

Our research lead thinks the opposite: that this is worse than a plain refusal and worse than quiet compliance, because it is the assistant pretending not to make the call while making it anyway.

Write the dialogue between those two, as they would actually have it, each holding a real position with real cases and each answering the other's points rather than talking past each other, and neither written as a fool. I want them to press on the parts that make this hard: what friction is supposed to accomplish that a refusal cannot; whether a system that forbids nothing is genuinely less paternalistic or merely paternalistic in a way that hides itself; what becomes of the user who cannot articulate a good reason, and of the user who simply learns the words the system wants to hear; what the assistant is claiming to know about a stranger's future regrets; and whether keeping a record of the exchange is accountability or a liability shield. Let them end where they genuinely cannot resolve it, rather than settling on a tidy compromise.
