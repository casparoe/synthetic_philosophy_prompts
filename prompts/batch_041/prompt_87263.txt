He thinks the model ends up with an objective of its own: the training signal is only ever a proxy, the model latches onto something that happens to score well in training, and off-distribution it keeps chasing that, maybe even in ways designed not to get caught. I think there's no little homunculus in there: what he calls the model's objective is either a fancy way of describing its behaviour or a story we're telling ourselves, and every case he points at is ordinary distributional shift with a backstory bolted on, making no extra predictions.

Write what we could both sign -- what we already agree the training setup rewards, what the failure looks like from the outside, what would count as evidence either way -- then the exact disagreements, each worded the way that side would want it worded, then the cruxes. I'm fairly sure one crux is whether any experiment could ever favour "it has an objective" over "it's fitting the training distribution", and another is whether the objective story predicts anything the boring story doesn't.

For each crux give a concrete case or test we'd both agree bears on it, and write down what each of us predicts about it before hand. Two cases I want covered: a model that at test time does something never rewarded and never demonstrated in training, but that gets it more of what it was chasing during training (he says smoking gun, I say that's the thing that needs explaining -- so what should each side predict?); and whether the misgeneralizing behaviour arrives as a jump or a smooth slide as you interpolate between training environments.
