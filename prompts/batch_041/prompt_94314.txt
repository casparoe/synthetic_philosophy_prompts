I'm building something for a reading group on GEB and I want a case built to order rather than a rehash of the MI/MU thing, so here's the spec.

Take the jumping-out-of-the-system move — you've got a formal system, someone grinding through derivations inside it, and then someone else steps back, spots an invariant, and settles the question in three lines — and build me a thought experiment that isolates that move. Constraints:

- New system, please. Not MIU with the letters swapped. It should live somewhere a person could plausibly spend an afternoon: a written rulebook for a game, a bureaucratic process where forms get combined and split, a notation for some craft, whatever you like. It needs an actual rule set written down (four to six manipulation rules is plenty) and a target state that's supposed to be reachable and isn't.
- Cheap invariants don't count. Length, total symbol count, how many times a rule has fired — the target has to be compatible with all of those, or the puzzle is a warm-up. I want the kind of invariant that takes a level-shift to see: a residue class mod n, a parity, a value in some group, something where why it's preserved isn't obvious rule by rule. And say why the cheap ones don't do the job here.
- Verify it. Walk your rules one at a time and show the invariant survives each, including whichever rule shortens strings, since thats usually where these things break. Then show the target violates it. If a rule breaks your invariant, fix the case, don't fudge it.
- Twin case. Change exactly one rule, or add one axiom, so the target becomes reachable, and tell me what that does to the invariant argument. If it's reachable, give the derivation or at least a sketch; if you can't get one, say so and don't claim it.
- Three characters. One has been applying rules for hours — why is his failure not the proof? One does the jump. One is the fake jumper who announces that the puzzle is the wrong question, or that you have to think outside the box, without producing anything. Then tell me what would turn that fake jump into a real one.
- The relativity bit, which is the part I actually care about: build the case so the jumper's insight can be restated as a longer statement inside a richer system that contains the original one. Does that make the jump merely pragmatic rather than a view from outside? Is there a final outside, or does every jump just land you in a bigger system with its own blind spots? Say which way your case leans and why.

Deliverable: the scenario in one or two paragraphs, then the check (invariant, rule-by-rule, target), then the twin, then the three characters, then the relativity discussion. Finish with the honest notes: which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if I delete it, and how someone who thinks there's nothing but the system would attack the whole thing — whats the best version of their complaint? Plus one line on whether your case shows that jumping out produces knowledge or only a good reason to stop looking.

For whats counts as cheating: I'm not asking you to stipulate that the target is unreachable. The unreachability has to fall out of the invariant. If I can throw away your invariant and still have an argument that the target is out of reach, you've built the wrong case. No citations or reading lists, just the construction.
