For several years I have kept a notebook of what I consider my own best ideas, and a pattern has emerged in it that I can describe but cannot name. Nearly every entry is a variation on something that already existed. One is a musical idea: a rhythmic figure lifted from one instrument and given to another while the melody is held fixed. Another is a proof: a technique that is routine in one branch of mathematics applied to a structure where nobody had thought to apply it. Another is a joke whose entire force comes from substituting one word in an otherwise familiar phrase. In each case the raw material is old, and whatever is new lies in a small, deliberate change along a single dimension that I chose to vary while holding everything else constant.

Lately I have come to suspect that the change itself is not where the creativity lives, and that the interesting act is the choice of dimension — the moment when a property that was previously invisible becomes a knob one can turn at all. Treating "which instrument" or "which branch of mathematics" as a free variable is a different kind of move from turning a knob that everyone already recognized as turnable, and I think the second is what people are gesturing at when they speak of creative insight. But I may be conflating two things that are really one, and I do not trust my own carving here.

Is there an established concept for this? I am less interested in a label than in the shape of the thing, so tell me the nearest concepts you know of and exactly how each falls short. I have a vague sense that there is work on creativity as recombination, and on analogy or transfer between domains, but neither seems to be about the deliberate turning of a knob inside a representational structure, and neither appears to distinguish turning an existing knob from adding a new one. If nothing fits, coin the concept yourself, define it precisely, and mark its edges: what counts as a variation, and what is instead a replacement or an invention from nothing. I am not asking for a reading list; I want the carving.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. And tell me what would change your mind about the carving — which case, if any, would show that the two things I want to keep apart are in fact a single phenomenon.
