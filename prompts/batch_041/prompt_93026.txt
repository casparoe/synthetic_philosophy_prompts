I chair the school board in a mid-sized district, and next Tuesday we vote on how to meet the strategic plan's pledge to "cut our achievement gap by a fifth within three years." Staff have priced three routes: (A) expand the advanced track and add tutoring at the bottom, which lifts the top and leaves the bottom roughly where it is; (B) dissolve the advanced track, fold its budget into general instruction, and reassign the strongest teachers across all classrooms, which our own data say would leave the bottom group's scores statistically unchanged while pulling the top group down enough to hit the target; and (C) target everything at the bottom quartile alone, accepting a wider gap. Route B is the one the equity office favors and the one I find hardest to vote against, partly because my own daughter is in the advanced track. Draft the written opinion I'll read into the record: recommend one route with a clear rationale, say candidly what the district gives up under your recommendation, and give a genuinely fair hearing to the position that a narrower gap is worth achieving even when it leaves no one better off and some students worse off. It should be usable as the board's reasoning, not a survey of the literature.
