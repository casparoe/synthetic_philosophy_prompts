I'm putting together an interactive flowchart for a long-form piece on the "hinge of history" hypothesis, and I want it to sort readers into the positions that are genuinely on the table rather than the ones with the loudest advocates. Please design that flowchart. Begin from commitments a reader can assess without already knowing any of the labels: whether history contains moments whose influence dwarfs all others, whether such a moment is a property of an era or of particular acts within it, whether we could ever know we were inside one, whether a hinge can recur or must be terminal, and whether living at one changes what we owe to the future. Make each internal node one of those questions, phrased so that answering it doesn't tip off which position it leads to, and give each leaf the name of a position — or mark it "unnamed" — with one sentence on its least comfortable commitment. I'd like the branches to follow the logical space rather than a roster of famous names, so please note which combinations of answers nobody occupies and say briefly whether that is because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two positions the tree cannot tell apart. Keep it to roughly six to eight branch nodes, and write in American English.
