I write about science and philosophy for a general-interest magazine, and I keep running into a move in arguments that I don't have a name for, and its starting to bother me. Here's the shape of it. Two people are arguing about a big harm and a small one — a death, say, versus a side effect nobody would call serious. One of them says: fine, take the small one, it's trivial, now imagine it happening to an enormous number of people, or happening to the same person over and over for years, and tell me it still doesn't outweigh the big one. And they say it like the arithmetic has settled the matter. I watched this play out in real time at a hospital ethics meeting last year: the fight was over a drug that saves a small number of lives but gives a much larger number of patients a genuinely annoying, non-dangerous symptom, and one person kept scaling the patient numbers up until the room felt stupid for caring about the deaths, while another person kept insisting that you don't get to add up a million itches and come out with a funeral. The strange part is that they agreed on every fact. They agreed the symptom was trivial, the deaths were awful, and the counts were what they were. They split entirely on whether the little things are even the sort of thing that can be added together into a big thing — and at some point, once the numbers get big enough that nobody can actually picture them, the whole thing stops being about anything you can hold in your head and turns into a bare claim about arithmetic.

I've seen the same shape in a completely unrelated place, a city budget fight over whether to keep funding a program that spares a few hundred people a miserable-but-survivable condition versus moving the money to something that saves a few lives outright, and everybody in the room drew the line for when a pile of small goods becomes one big good in a different spot, and nobody could say why their spot was the right one.

So what I want from you: is there a name for this? I don't mean the general one-big-thing-versus-many-small-things comparison, I can find plenty about that. I mean the specific disagreement, where both sides accept all the same premises and split only over whether the small harms get summed at all. If there's an established term, tell me and tell me what it actually covers; if there isn't, coin me one and define it, and honestly I care alot more about the definition than the word. I want to know how it sits next to the obvious neighbors — the position that says some harms are so small they can never be traded against bigger ones no matter how many of them there are, and the position that says nothing is special and everything adds up — and where the line is between this and just ordinary cost-benefit weighing. Is it a fallacy, a paradox, a clash of two defensible moral views, or a sign that the question was underspecified to begin with? And give me at least one case where it's genuinely unclear which side has it right, so I can see the edges of the thing. American English, please — my copy editor is American and she'll flag it otherwise.
