Tier list request, Liar paradox edition. Here's the usual menu of escape routes, in my own words: (1) level the language, so nothing can call a sentence true at its own level or higher; (2) keep self-reference but allow truth-value gaps, building truth up from ground-level facts and letting some sentences simply never get a value; (3) keep the contradiction and go paraconsistent, so a contradiction doesn't blow up everything else; (4) deflate the whole thing — "true" is just a device for endorsing a claim, so nothing is really being predicated and the puzzle is grammar; (5) make "true" context-sensitive, so the liar sentence keeps shifting what it says as the conversation moves.

Now rank those five against a case that isn't the boring one-liner. Take n sentences in a ring, each one saying that the next one isn't true. If n is odd it's a flat-out paradox, no assignment works at all. If n is even you get two different assignments that both work, which is wierd but not contradictory. So parity matters here, and a decent account should say why. Sort the five into tiers by how well they handle that, a sentence of justification per item, and flag any that give the odd and even ring the same verdict — then tell me whether you'd call that a bug or a feature, and which one you'd actually put money on.
