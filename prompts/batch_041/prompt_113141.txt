A friend and I have been argueing for about a year now about uncertain evidence and Bayesian updating, and at this point I genuinely can't tell whether we're disagreeing or just talking past each other, so I want a diagnosis rather than a defense of either side. Here's the terrain. Plain conditionalizing is for when you come to learn a proposition outright. The Jeffrey rule is for when nothing is learned outright but your credences shift across a partition, you go to .7 that the light was red and .3 green, and you renormalize within each cell. He thinks that's the whole story: that's what a rational agent does with uncertain evidence, and anyone who says otherwise is confused about what evidence is or in the grip of a bad picture of experience. I think the rule is fine in the toy cases and falls apart under pressure. Feed two uncertain inputs in different orders and the answer changes. When the input isn't a clean shift over a partition, when what you learn is something about a conditional say, the rule doesn't even apply, and every proposal for extending it disagrees with the others. And there are inputs where nothing tells you which posterior to have, so there's no fact about the rational response for a rule to capture. We loop through this and neither of us moves.

My own suspicion is that the real fight is about what the input is. For him an evidential input is a fact about the world you're less than certain about. For me it's more like a constraint on your credence function, and if that's right then half of what we say to each other isn't addressed to the same question. But I also think there's a third thing neither of us will say out loud, which is that noone here can say what makes an experience a candidate for being an evidence item at all, as opposed to just a cause of a new credence function.

Here's a case I made up, and I want you to tell me what it shows. Suppose the uncertainty is about the partition itself. I'm .7/.3 between red and green, but I'm also .1 that there's a third setting on the machine I've forgotten about, so the space of possibilities I'm supposed to be updating over is itself uncertain. The rule needs a fixed partition to renormalize within. What does a rational agent do there, and does the answer generalize, or is the rule only ever well defined because the examples were chosen to make it well defined?

So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? A verbal one about the word evidence, a substantive one about the objects of credence, or a disguised formal question about whether the constraint determines a unique posterior, the sort of thing a theorem could settle? Which parts of the impasse are real and which are artifacts of how we've posed the question? And what would count as progress here, a representation theorem, an impossibility result, a distinction, or just conceding that how you should update on uncertain evidence was never well posed? If the honest answer is that no progress should be expected, tell me that, and why.
