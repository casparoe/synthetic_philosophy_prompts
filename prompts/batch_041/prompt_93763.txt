I'm a distributed-systems engineer — I work daily with live process migration, checkpoint and restore, event-sourced state, forking, and observational equivalence, so I'm fluent in the vocabulary of processes and threads, address spaces, serialisation, referential versus structural equality, event logs and folds, bisimulation, and garbage collection — but I have no philosophical training.

Restate the main positions in the debate over whether mind uploading preserves the person, as faithfully as you can, in that vocabulary. Use two scenarios as test cases: gradual replacement, where the parts of a brain are swapped one at a time for functionally equivalent substitutes, and sudden scanning-and-copying. Make sure you cover at least (i) the view that what is required is the continued life of one and the same biological organism, (ii) the view that what matters is psychological continuity, which comes in degrees and can branch, and (iii) the view that there is no further fact of the matter about which of two successors is the original. For each position, say what translates intact, what translates only with distortion and in what way, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then give the residue report: for each item that failed to translate, say whether the failure is a defect of my vocabulary or an artefact of the philosophical debate — and separately, flag any place where my vocabulary makes something look easier than it is, or smuggles in an assumption the philosophers would reject. My own suspicion is that the branching case looks to me like a routine fork, and I want to know whether that intuition is doing illegitimate work.

Answer in British English, and give it as a JSON object with these fields: translations, an array in which each element has position (the position in plain terms), restatement (the position in my vocabulary), carries_over, distorted, and no_counterpart (strings; the last three may be empty if there is nothing to report); false_friends, an array of objects with engineering_term, philosophical_claim, and why_it_misleads, for terms of mine that appear to translate but do not — including, if you think it belongs there, my fork comparison; residue, an array of objects with untranslatable_item, why_it_resists, and verdict, where verdict is one of defect of the target vocabulary, artefact of the source debate, or genuine feature of the subject matter; and bottom_line, a single short string naming the most important thing that did not survive the translation.
