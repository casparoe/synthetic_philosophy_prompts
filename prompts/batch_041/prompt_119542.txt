Two members of our housing co-operative have filed complaints about the same person, and on Thursday the member conduct committee — of which I am one of five — has to decide what to do with them. I would like your help drafting the short written opinion I will circulate beforehand, since I am the one who has to put our reasoning in writing and I do not want it to fall apart the moment M.'s friends read it aloud.

The complaints are about a member we will call M. R. says M. has treated her with open contempt for months: sneering at her in the laundry room, mocking her in front of others at meetings, and once following her to her door to continue an argument. T., a third member, has filed a separate complaint about the same incidents. T. was not the target, but she saw most of them and says she is tired of watching it happen.

The wrinkle is R. herself. There is an unresolved complaint against R. from another member alleging very similar conduct — cutting remarks, a habit of holding forth at meetings, an inability to let anything drop. She has not denied it; she says only that she was provoked. Two people on the committee think that settles the matter: R. is a hypocrite, so her complaint should be dismissed without a hearing, and T. should be told this is not her fight.

I do not think it is that simple, but I cannot say precisely why, and I have no training in this kind of reasoning. It seems to me that whether R. gets to be heard is a different question from whether she is the right person to hold M. to account, and that a bystander's complaint might be stronger than a victim's for reasons I cannot sort out, or weaker. There is also the awkward fact that this committee has let this sort of behaviour slide for three years, and I have said things at meetings I would not want quoted back to me. The co-op has just adopted a restorative process, so M. will get a chance to raise her own concerns about R. before any finding is made — I mention it because I do not know whether that clarifies the standing question or just muddies it.

Please draft the opinion: a recommendation we can act on, with a timeline; a clear position on R.'s complaint, on T.'s, and on what finding, if any, we make about M.; and an honest account of the case for dismissing both complaints outright, which should not caricature the people making it. Where standing is doing real work, say so plainly; where it is being used as a shield, say that too.
