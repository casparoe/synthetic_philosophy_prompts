Two friends of mine got into a long argument after we all read Hofstadter on the self as a strange loop, and I honestly couldnt referee it. Sam says: if the I is just a self-referential pattern rather than some physical atom, then when a big chunk of your pattern gets recreated in someone else's brain -- they knew you deeply, absorbed your habits of thought, can predict you, carry your voice around in their head -- that isn't just a metaphor for remembering, it's an actual partial survival of you. He insists Hofstadter means this literally about his late wife living on in him. Alex says that's a category error: a copy of a pattern is a numerically different pattern, resemblance isn't identity, and what makes me me is the ongoing causal history of this particular brain, not the shape of the information. He says: copy my song onto your iPod and the song isn't now in two places doing two things -- you've got a duplicate, not a survivor. Sam comes back: but the whole point is that there's no further fact beyond the pattern; if the self is the self-model, then a faithfully running self-model just is a self, and anyway it's obviously graded -- we already treat people as continuous through massive change, so why not continuous, a little bit, into other people? Alex says that move makes the whole thing vacuous: by that standard I contain everyone I've ever met, and surviving stops meaning anything you'd care about. Sam says Alex is smuggling in a soul-like demand for strict identity that Hofstadter is precisely rejecting. They want me to settle it.
