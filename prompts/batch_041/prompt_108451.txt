I am a final-year philosophy student currently taking a module on philosophy of mind and personal identity. For my assessed podcast, I am to interview a visiting philosopher who defends the view that the self is a centre of narrative gravity. I want the interview to press the view rather than let the guest restate the familiar analogy with a centre of mass. Please draft eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give. Order them so that the standard reply -- that the self is an abstraction rather than an entity -- becomes progressively harder to use as a shield.

I would like at least one question on each of the following, though you may add others:
1. Whether the view is intended as a metaphysical claim about what the self really is, or as a pragmatic redescription of how we talk and think about selves.
2. How the view handles narratives that are socially scaffolded or co-authored. For instance, imagine a person whose autobiography is maintained by a court-appointed biographer who edits it each night; every morning the person reads the latest version and treats it as their own. Is there still a single centre of narrative gravity, and if so, whose is it?
3. What the view implies for cases where the narrative is fragmented, imposed, or erased -- for example, a person with severe anterograde amnesia who must read a script of their life each day, or someone subjected to coercive re-education until they recite a false autobiography as their own.
4. Whether the centre of narrative gravity can be realised in non-human animals, in groups, or in artificial systems that generate and revise self-descriptions.
5. Whether the view can distinguish between a self-narrative that is merely avowed and one that is enacted in behaviour, and whether that distinction does any explanatory work.
6. What would count as evidence against the view, and whether the view is falsifiable or purely interpretive.

For each question, make the follow-up anticipate the most likely defensive reply and push past it. I do not need citations or reading lists; I need questions that force the guest to commit on the metaphysics, the social dimension, and the normative consequences of the view.
