So I host this small annual workshop on the arrow of time — mostly physicists, one or two philosophers of physics, and then at the closing dinner a bunch of non-specialists, including my in-laws, who are lovely but dont care about entropy. I give a five-minute speech at that dinner and I want it to actually say something, not just "wasn't that a great workshop." Here's the position I want you to build the whole speech around: I think it's a mistake to say the arrow of time comes from entropy increasing. The fundamental laws run just as well backwards, so all the asymmetry has to live in the boundary conditions — specifically that the early universe started out absurdly low-entropy and nothing seems to pin down the other end. And the part I actually care about: I want to claim that "the past" is nothing over and above the direction in which records pile up — memories, fossils, coffee stains, all of it — so that the reason we remember yesterday and not tomorrow isn't some separate psychological fact, it just falls out of that one lopsided condition. Write the speech so a physicist won't wince and my in-laws still follow it, use one everyday image for the low-entropy start, and land on why this deserves a workshop rather than being just a fun paradox. No quotes, no citations, keep it to what someone could plausibly say out loud after two glasses of wine.
