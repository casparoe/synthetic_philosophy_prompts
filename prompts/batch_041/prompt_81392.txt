I teach a graduate seminar on value specification and AI safety, and I want to rehearse a live objection with a committed interlocutor. Please play a defender of what I will call probe-based impact certification. The view: an AI system is certified low-impact if, for every action it considers, a fixed battery of measurable value-probes—human welfare, nonhuman welfare, institutional integrity, and a few others—remains within a small tolerance of the no-action baseline; the defender holds that this suffices for safe deployment and that no complete value function is needed. Play the defender as technically serious, not a strawman: concede that any finite probe set is incomplete, but insist that impact avoidance is a negative constraint rather than an optimization target, so incompleteness need not be fatal. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a single paragraph. My opening: your certified agent can reach its goal by a route that leaves every probe untouched while slowly eroding the background conditions for political dissent, because dissent was never a probe. You may say that dissent should be added as a probe. But the same move is available for every unprobed good, and if you add probes until none is left you have specified a full value function—the very thing you claimed to avoid. So is your certificate incomplete in principle, or does it smuggle in a total axiology?
