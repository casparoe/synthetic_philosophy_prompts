I'm in a reading group thats been working through the vagueness stuff all term — we've done the usual tour (epistemicism, supervaluationism, degree theory, the "no sharp boundaries" intuition) and next week it's my turn to bring something, and I've decided I want to bring a poem instead of a handout. Thing is, I don't want a poem *about* the sorites, I want one that *is* one — reading it should do to the reader what the paradox does when you actually walk the steps and find you've agreed your way into nonsense. So: a chain of lines, twelve to sixteen, where every line is the same sentence as the one before it except that exactly one word has changed — one notch, one degree, one hair weaker or stronger — and the change is always small enough that nobody would flag that particular line as the turn. First line nobody would dispute; last line nobody would accept; and no single step between them that a reader can point to as the place it broke. If it reads like a proof in verse it's failed, and if someone who's never heard of the sorites just thinks it's a good poem, that's a win.

The subject needs a little sting to it rather than sand in a heap. I keep circling a hospital's line for "still alive," or a rumour that's quietly becoming the sort of thing we'd call knowledge, or the way my grandmother's word for a colour is sliding into its neighbour and leaving a gap where nothing quite fits — pick whichever one you can actually pull off and commit to it, dont hand me a menu. And I want one dissenting voice in there, an epistemicist heckler who insists there *must* be a line, a determinate one, and that our not seeing it is just our own failure of eyesight — let him walk back through the lines hunting for it and come up empty, and don't let the poem settle whether that's because the boundary isn't there or because he's looking wrong. Somewhere in it I'd also like it to go vague about whether we're still in the clearly-true stretch or already out in the borderline, since I think the edge between "borderline" and "clear" is itself blurry, and I'd rather feel that than be told it.

Finish with a short note, a paragraph or so, on where the poem takes liberties with the ideas or leans on a reading that's contested — where it's quietly picking a side on something people still fight about. I'd rather it cheat in the open than pretend to be neutral.
