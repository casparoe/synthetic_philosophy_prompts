I'm writing a paper for my value theory seminar on incommensurable values and hard choices, and before I work through the rival accounts I want the specification they are being measured against. Fix the purpose as guidance rather than metaphysics: I want a theory that a person who actually has to choose, and has to choose by Friday, can use — not merely a map of which values are and aren't comparable. Set out the requirements such a theory has to meet: the cases it must get right (two options where neither is better and neither is worse nor exactly equal; the case where one option is improved by something trivial and still does not become better; a choice that is made and then revisable, where the agent could cheaply switch back; a choice that must be made while no resolution is in sight), the distinctions it must not collapse (between the values being incommensurable and the options being incomparable, and between an agent's values being incommensurable and her simply not knowing what she values), and the results it cannot contradict (that a choice can be rational without a best option, and that regret and residue survive the choice). Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable given the purpose I have fixed and which are commitments of particular camps, and flag the pairs that cannot be jointly satisfied — that is the part I most want, along with which single requirement does the most to rule out the standard positions.
