In Germany, and I think Austria, you can go to prison for saying the Holocaust never happened, and nobody there treats that as a scandal. Both places call themselves liberal democracies with free speech. And neither is absolutist, America still jails people for credible threats or for inciting a riot, so its not like one side thinks speech can never harm anyone. So I keep feeling like the fight isnt really about how much speech to allow, its about something deeper that neither side says out loud.

What I want from you is what each system has to believe for its own rules to hang together. Does the American version presuppose that speech by itself cant harm anyone, that harm only starts once somebody acts on it? Or is it more cynical, like the government can never be trusted to decide which ideas are the dangerous ones, so the rule is really about distrust of power rather than about harm? And Europes version, does it presuppose that words really do wound, that telling a whole group they dont belong here is itself an injury the state has to stop? And if thats the theory, is it about protecting the vulnerable group, or about protecting the democracy itself from being eaten from the inside? I read somewhere that after the war Germany deliberately built a democracy that is allowed to fight back against its enemies, and I wonder if thats the actual engine here and the dignity talk is just the nicer sounding version.

Then the part that really trips me up. Some of these same European countries also have laws against insulting the president or the head of state, which seems like the exact opposite instinct, protecting the powerful instead of the weak. So does the practice actually have one consistent theory behind it or is it two theories wearing the same coat? America has its own weird spots too, like it protects burning the flag but not a threat, which I dont know how to fit together either.

Also please tell me where the rules are just underdetermined, like which parts are doing real philosophical work and which parts could be swapped out without anything changing. And if you had to take one of the two systems and rebuild it on the other ones foundations, what would actually have to change on the ground, the actual laws, or how the courts read them, or just the publics gut feelings?
