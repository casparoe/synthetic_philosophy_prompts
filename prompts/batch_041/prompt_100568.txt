My colleague in the logic group is obsessed with non-constructive existence proofs, and her favourite specimin is the strategy-stealing argument. Her scorecard so far, on the question "does strategy stealing show the first player has a winning strategy?": Hex, yes. Chomp, yes. Chess, no — zugzwang means an extra move can actually hurt you, so the whole trick falls apart. Go, no — komi breaks the symmetry. Poker, no — hidden information, obviously. Now a student has built a game for our seminar and we can't agree on what she'll say about it. It's a Hex-style connection game: you place stones, first to link your two sides wins, an extra stone never hurts you, and it's perfectly symmetric between the players — but unlike Hex we've convinced ourselves a draw is possible here, because both players can end up permanently walling each other off. Predict her verdict on it. Which rule is she really running — and is there a rival rule that fits her scorecard just as well? Tell me what each rule predicts about this game, which one you'd bet on, and what game we'd have to put in front of her to tell the two rules apart. I care about the rule more than the verdict.
