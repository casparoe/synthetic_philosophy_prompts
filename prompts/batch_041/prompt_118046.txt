I write RTL for a living -- chip design, mostly accelerator work -- and I've spent an embarrassing amount of my career arguing about when two pieces of hardware count as "the same thing". Lately I keep hearing a claim from the AI ethics side that I want translated into my language: that the morally relevant thing about a mind isnt the silicon or the neurons or the meat, it's the computation, and that the interesting question is which computations matter rather than which physical stuff runs them. Restate that whole position -- the version where sentience tracks something about the computation itself -- in my vocabulary. And I mean actually translated, not dumbed down. Specs vs implementations. Behavioural equivalence and how you scope it. Cycle-accurate emulation vs a functional model vs a simulation that only looks right from outside. What a virtual machine is as opposed to the thing hosting it. Coarse-graining, don't-care inputs, metastability, glitchy combinational logic -- all the cases where "the same function" isnt even well defined and you have to say same-with-respect-to-what. I know the hardware end cold and I've read maybe three papers in philosophy of mind, so dont make me learn the jargon but dont give me baby talk either.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which of their claims come through clean? Which only survive if you fudge the translation -- e.g. if you say "same computation" and quietly slide from same input-output map to same causal process, that's a fudge and I want it flagged. And which claims have no counterpart at all in how I think about hardware? Is there anything on my side that answers to there being something it's like to be the system, or to the difference between a machine and the machine's model of itself? For each residue item tell me which side is at fault: is my framework missing a distinction they actually have, or was the original claim vague and my framework just made the vagueness visible? A translation that carries everything is one I'll trust less, so dont pad it.

One more thing I want you to take seriously instead of waving at. A colleague of mine says any lump of matter implements every finite state machine you like, so if computation is what matters morally then rocks are in the club, which is absurd, so the position has to be restricted somehow. In my world the standard move is that everything realises everything at some level of abstraction, which is exactly why we insist on a spec, an interface, and a tolerance for what counts as conforming. Does that move actually answer him, or does it just relocate the fudge? I want the translation, the residue, and then your honest read on whether the rock objection is fatal, tolerable, or evidence that the question was never about computation in the first place.
