I hung up the lab coat last spring — forty-one years in analytical chemistry, mostly spectroscopy — and since then I've been sitting in on a philosophy of mind reading group at the local uni to keep the old brain from rusting. Last week the whole two hours went on higher-order theories of consciousness, and I came away with a practical itch: I want to bring a joke to the next session, and I want it to be a good one. So please write me a joke about higher-order theories of consciousness.

Here's the catch. The only jokes that actually land with this crowd turn on the real machinery — the idea that what makes a mental state conscious isn't anything intrinsic to the state itself but the fact that some further, higher-order state represents it. If the joke would work just as well about "the mind" or "consciousness" in the abstract, it'll get a polite cough and nothing else. And here's where we got bogged down, which I think is where the gold is: nothing in the theory seems to force that higher-order representation to be accurate. You could have a higher-order state that represents me as having a first-order state I dont actually have, and on some versions that alone is still enough for it to feel like something — I gather that's one of the standard moves for explaining dreams and phantom pains. So a punchline that turns on the higher-order state being able to lie, or on there being nobody left to represent the representation (someone said that way lies an infinite regress, little men all the way up), would be right up our alley.

Last thing and I'll shut up: I don't want a "higher-order thought walks into a bar" job where the characters are just labels wearing hats. It should make some sort of sense; nobody in the group should walk away thinking I've confused a higher-order thought with an inner sense, or that I think the whole theory is just "being aware that you're aware". Set it up over a paragraph or two if you need the room, then land it.
