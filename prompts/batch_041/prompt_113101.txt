So cooperation is stable. Dev says thats not a solution, its just a commitment device. He says the same setup also makes mutual defection stable: if both submit 'always defect,' neither can gain by switching to the cooperative program, because the other program will still defect. So you have two equilibria and no reason to pick one. Nadia says the cooperative one is the only one that survives if the programs are written by rational agents who know theyre in this setup and can reason about each other's reasoning—the defection equilibrium is just a failure of coordination. Dev says thats circular: you're assuming the agents already solved the coordination problem to get to the cooperative equilibrium.
