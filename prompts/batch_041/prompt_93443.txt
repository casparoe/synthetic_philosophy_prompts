My friend and I have been going around in circles about the absent-minded driver for months and I want an outside read on what we're even disagreeing about. The setup, quickly: a driver on a road with two intersections, he should go straight through the first and turn off at the second to get home, but he's absent-minded and forgets he's passed the first, so at either intersection he can't tell which one he's at. Turning at the first is a waste, sailing past the second is the worst outcome, turning at the second gets him home. Step-by-step reasoning tells him to keep going (the odds seem to favor it), yet if he always keeps going he ends up with the worst result, and the obvious fix -- decide in advance to turn at the second -- gives the best result. He says that just shows you have to pick a plan up front and that act-based decision theory is broken; I say a plan is cheating, because at the moment of choosing the guy really doesn't know which intersection he's at and you can't choose from nowhere. Stalemate.

My hunch is that neither of us is really arguing about plans vs acts -- I think the fight is about how you individuate the agent and his information: is the chooser the guy standing at the intersection or the whole trip, and is his amnesia even a well-formed evidential state? I also can't shake the feeling that the 50/50 credence the step-by-step reasoning leans on is fishy, since under any fixed policy he's at the first intersection with probability 1. Here's a case I built to test it: suppose that before setting off he can cheaply weld the first exit shut, so at the first intersection there's simply no turn available and at the second there is -- now the two spots are distinguishable and everyone agrees he goes straight then turns. Does that show the original was never acts-vs-plans but about making the nodes distinguishable (or about that bogus credence), or does my fix just smuggle a plan in through the back door?

So: what kind of disagreement is this really, is it verbal or substantive, and what would count as progress -- a distinction, a formal result, a better case -- or is it just going to sit there? Dont tell me which of us is right, tell me what we're actually disagreeing about.
