my friend's reading group is finishing a unit on self-deception and she asked me to write a poem to open the last session. I said yes before I worked out that I had no idea what to write, so now it's my problem. I write backend code for a living, so I'm going off vibes here.

Here's the thing that bugs me. Every explanation I've read splits the person in two — a part that knows and a part that doesn't — and the second you do that it isn't self-deception anymore, it's one person lying to a different person, they just happen to share a skull. But if you refuse to split her, you're left saying she knows it's not true and she believes it, which is a contradiction, not a description of anything that happens to anybody.

So write me a pantoum. Four quatrains, the usual rule — lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next — and have the last stanza fold back into the opening so the poem ends where it started. The loop should be the point: she can't get out of the poem. And every time a line comes back around it has to mean something different, so the same sentence reads as a reassurance the first time and as the confession the last time. If it helps, make the returning line something she says out loud to someone, so the poem has to show her believing it while she says it. One self, though — no knower and dupe sharing a body, no "some part of her knew." I'd rather the poem leave the contradiction standing than buy its way out by inventing a second person in there. Someone in the group who's actually read the literature should be able to point at the line where the poem does the standard move and the line where it refuses.
