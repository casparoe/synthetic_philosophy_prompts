I'm a first-year master's student in history and I keep ending up in these long pub arguments with a friend from my theory seminar -- call her Mira -- about whether historical periods are actually real or just things we made up later, and I genuinely cant tell who's making sense, so I'm not going to tell you which side I lean toward. Could you take the role of a philosopher of history who's thought a lot about social ontology and historiography, think carefully, and start by laying out the space of possible positions before you judge anything? Then go through the exchange move by move, rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say who's right about which specific point, and finish with what both of them are missing about the underlying question. Please dont give me a survey of what famous people said -- I want an actual adjudication of what these two said, and I want the criteria you're using made explicit.

Here's roughly how it went. (1) Mira said the Renaissance isn't a thing, it's a later invention, a label that some nineteenth-century people stuck onto the past to flatter themselves, and that 'Middle Ages' is the same trick in reverse -- a put-down coined by people who wanted to be on the far side of it. (2) Dev said she's conflating the label with the thing labeled: sure, the word comes later, but there was a real cluster of changes -- the recovery and re-editing of ancient texts, new techniques in painting, printing, a shift in what educated people wrote in -- and a word being coined afterwards doesn't make the changes evaporate, any more than 'planet' being a Greek coinage makes Jupiter not exist. (3) Mira said fine, but the boundaries are the whole problem: nothing in nature says 1450 is medieval and 1500 isn't, and if you can't say where a thing begins and ends you can't say it's a thing. (4) Dev said vagueness isn't unreality -- there's no sharp line between bald and not-bald either -- and offered a picture: periods are like watersheds on a map. The ridges between river basins are real features of the terrain, but at the flat saddle between two basins the exact line you draw is up to you; real divide, arbitrary dot on it. (5) Mira said that's the wrong analogy, because a watershed is defined by water actually flowing downhill, and the 'flow' in history is exactly what's in dispute. She proposed what she called a grain test: if you re-drew the periods using only whatever would best compress the surviving source evidence, with no story you're trying to tell, would the same breaks show up in the same places? She said no -- the breaks we use were picked by which features we cared to count, and you could instead count continuity in farming, guilds, gender, or village religion and find nothing much happening around 1400-1500. (6) Dev said any description at any scale can be made to look continuous if you choose the variables that way, including the ones physicists use, so that can't be the test; the test is whether the label predicts things you didn't put into it. (7) Mira said it doesn't, once you leave the art and literature departments, and anyway a lot of historians of science have gotten uneasy with 'the Scientific Revolution' precisely because the changes were spread over centuries and 'revolution' was smuggling in a story about suddenness -- and the phrase is a lot newer than the events it names, which she thinks is itself evidence it was doing rhetorical work. (8) Dev said that's an argument about a word's usefulness in a subfield, not about whether anything happened, and if a fuzzy hundred-year smear is the price of a label that organizes a lot of teaching and research, that's a bargain.

So: who's right at each step? I'd especially like a verdict on three things -- whether the "the label came later" point has any force at all against realism about periods, or whether it's a non sequitur like Dev says; whether Mira's grain test is a good test or whether Dev's reply actually sinks it; and whether the watershed analogy is doing honest work or is just a comforting picture. Also tell me which single move in that exchange is doing the most unexamined work, because I have a suspicion that one of them is cheating somewhere and I can't see where.
