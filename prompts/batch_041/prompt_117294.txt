You are a philosopher of physics specializing in quantum foundations, with a particular interest in the ontology of the quantum state. I would like you to think carefully and systematically before answering.

In a reading group on the measurement problem, a colleague keeps saying, "The wavefunction is just a bookkeeping device." They deploy the line to shut down any suggestion that the wavefunction is a real physical entity, and they treat psi-ontic readings as confused. Yet the same colleague says that the wavefunction is real when explaining why Bell inequality violations cannot be explained by local hidden variables, and they agree that the wavefunction's phase has physical consequences in interference experiments. They also sometimes say that the wavefunction is real but not physical, and that this is the only defensible form of realism about it. I suspect the slogan is doing several different jobs at once, and that some of those jobs are trivial, some are substantive, and some may be incompatible with what they say elsewhere.

I want a menu of the precise claims that "the wavefunction is just a bookkeeping device" could be making. Do not give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want the conceptual disambiguation. Before laying out the menu, brainstorm the different senses of "real" and "bookkeeping" that might be in play. Then, for each reading, provide: (a) a precise statement of the thesis; (b) what it commits you to about the relationship between the wavefunction and physical reality; (c) whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false; (d) whether it supports the colleague's use of the slogan to dismiss psi-ontic views; and (e) what other commitments it brings with it—for example, about hidden variables, the completeness of quantum mechanics, the role of the agent, the status of the Born rule, or the meaning of a beable. Make sure the menu includes readings on which the wavefunction is: (i) a representation of an agent's information; (ii) a statistical summary of an ensemble; (iii) a nomological or law-like object; (iv) a structural or relational feature of the world; (v) a physical field or concrete entity; and (vi) any further reading you think is important, including the "real but not physical" reading the colleague sometimes invokes. For each, say whether it is psi-ontic, psi-epistemic, or neither/other, and explain how it would handle the colleague's two claims—the dismissal of psi-ontic views and the appeal to the wavefunction's reality in Bell contexts. If you invoke a no-go result, explain the constraint it imposes in plain terms rather than relying on its name.

Finally, tell me which reading the colleague most likely intends, which reading would actually license the dismissal, and whether there is a stable position that combines the slogan with the reality of the wavefunction in Bell contexts. Lay this out as a numbered list I can bring to the next meeting, with a short verdict at the end. Aim for precision and avoid reciting textbook summaries; I want the distinctions that matter for the argument.
