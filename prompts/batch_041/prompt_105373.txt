For a philosophy project, I need a thought experiment built to order, and I would like you to construct it. The topic is the special composition question, that is, the question of when some things add up to a further thing. The case has to be engineered so that four views deliver a specified pattern of verdicts. The four views are these: universalism, on which any things whatsoever compose a further thing; nihilism, on which nothing ever composes a further thing; the life-based view sometimes called organicism, on which things compose a further thing exactly when they constitute a single life; and a view I have invented for this project, which I will call the static-arrangement view, on which things compose a further thing exactly when their arrangement would persist on its own, meaning that nothing about their being arranged as they are depends on any ongoing process, inside them or outside them, that would have to keep running.

The constraints are as follows. The case must be mundane: an ordinary scene, with no science fiction, no exotic physics, and no hypothetical machinery. It must be one paragraph long. It must centre on something that is uncontroversially alive, so that I am not cheating by choosing a borderline case such as a virus or a consortium of cooperating organisms. And the verdicts must come out in this pattern: universalism says there is a further thing, the life-based view says there is a further thing, nihilism says there is not, and the static-arrangement view says there is not. In other words, I need the static-arrangement view to deny composition exactly where the life-based view affirms it, which I take to be the harder direction to build. Do not fall back on the stock examples: no cat, no pile of sand, no candle flame, no whirlpool.

Once you have the scenario, do the following. State each of the four verdicts together with the reasoning that produces it. Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work. Tell me what happens if that feature is removed, and whether the case then collapses into agreement. Finally, explain how someone who accepts the static-arrangement view would argue that the case cheats, and say whether that objection succeeds. If you conclude that the required pattern cannot be produced without stacking the deck, say so instead and explain precisely why.
