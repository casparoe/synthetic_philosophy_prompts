ok this is gonna sound random but in physics we watched a video about the nuclear waste dump in New Mexico that has to stay lethal for like 10,000 years, and how in the 80s they got a whole task force together to figure out how to warn people in the year 12,000 not to dig there. They came up with giant spike fields, and black house-sized blocks arranged like streets that lead nowhere, and one guy apparently seriously proposed breeding cats that change color near radiation, and someone else wanted a skull and crossbones. Nobody knows if any language will still be readable then, so basically they were trying to say "danger, stay away" to people who dont exist yet.

What I want to know is what a project like that presupposes. Your spending real money and effort on strangers 500 generations out, so you must think their getting poisoned matters even though they aren't here yet and we'll never meet them. But is that all it needs? A friend told me there's this idea that tons of what we care about only makes sense if we believe people will still be around after we die, like our projects would go hollow if we knew humanity was ending soon. Does the waste site presuppose that stronger thing, or only the weaker "future people getting hurt is bad"? And if the weaker one is enough, is all the creepy architecture doing any philosophical work or is it just vibes? Also it seems inconsistent that we'd plan 10,000 years ahead for this but basically nothing comparable for climate. Do those two practices presuppose different views about what we owe the future?
