I want to compare two trade-offs that are usually discussed as separate curiosities, and I suspect they may be the same problem wearing different clothes.

The first is the standard dust-specks case: one person is tortured for fifty years, or some enormous number of people each get a single dust speck in the eye. The second is the structure usually called Pascal's mugging: a small but certain cost, against an astronomically small probability of an astronomically large harm.

I am not interested in a rehash of the stock arguments for and against aggregating in either case. What I want is a structural comparison. Here is the hypothesis I would like you to assess. The reason the dust-specks case presents itself as a genuine dilemma, while the mugging presents itself as a trap to be avoided, is not the magnitude of the harms involved but the axis along which the aggregation runs. The dust-specks case aggregates across persons: each individual bears a harm too small to matter on its own, and only the sum over people makes it matter. The mugging aggregates across states of the world: one tiny chance of a huge harm, however many people are exposed to it. If that is the real difference, then a person-affecting restriction should dissolve the dust-specks case while leaving the mugging untouched, and any principle that handles both — a lexical floor below which no number of small harms registers, a value function that is bounded or concave in the number of harms, or something else entirely — must be doing its work along the second axis only.

Three things, then. First, compare the two cases along whichever axes you think actually matter, and tell me whether my hypothesis has located the real difference or a superficial one. Second, say whether a single principle can handle both; if it can, state it as precisely as you can, including what it implies about how many dust specks would outweigh one torture. Third, consider a case that pulls the two axes apart: some large number of people, each of whom independently faces a one-in-that-number chance of being tortured, against the same number of people each certainly receiving one dust speck. The expected number of tortured people is one, and the number of specks is unchanged; say whether the anti-aggregation intuition survives the move to probabilities spread across persons, whether it survives moving the many tiny harms inside a single life (one speck a day for that many days), and what those two verdicts together tell us about which candidate principle was doing the work.
