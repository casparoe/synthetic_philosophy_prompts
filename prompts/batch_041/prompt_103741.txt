I am trying to formulate a coherent position on Molyneux's problem for a seminar, and I would like a consistency audit before I present it. My commitments are: (1) a person born blind who can distinguish a sphere from a cube by touch and then gains sight would not, at first sight, be able to identify which is the sphere and which the cube; (2) this is not because vision lacks shape content, since visual and tactile experiences represent the same property, shape; (3) the content of early visual experience is nonconceptual, so one can see a sphere as spherical without possessing the concept sphere; (4) recognition, as opposed to mere discrimination, requires applying a concept to the experience, and cross-modal recognition therefore requires a learned calibration between visual appearances and tactile appearances; (5) concepts are amodal, so the concept sphere acquired through touch is the same concept that vision would deploy; and (6) the newly sighted subject can nevertheless visually discriminate a sphere from a cube, seeing that they differ, even if he cannot identify which is which. Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which are independent? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, identify the cheapest revision for each and what that revision would cost elsewhere in the package, and say which single revision would resolve the most.
