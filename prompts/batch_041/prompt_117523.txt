So please take the role of someone who has refereed in this area for years — a philosopher of medicine who's heard every version of "then everything is a disease" and isn't impressed by handwaving — and before you answer, brainstorm for a bit first (I only want the short version of that, not a wall of text) and then give me the audit. Think carefully, this is one of those where the obvious answer is probably wrong.

here's roughly where I've landed:

1. disease is descriptive and value free. your kidney is diseased when it can't do the job kidneys are statistically supposed to do for someone in your reference class — your age, your sex, whatever environment you're in. there are no values in there, same as there are no values in "the liver is failing". I'm basically on the Boorse biostatistical side of this. and no, I don't want the extra clause where the dysfunction also has to be harming you — that's about illness, not disease.
2. health and illness are where the values live. whether something counts as an illness depends on suffering, on incapacity, on what you or your culture care about. disease is about the body, illness is about us.
3. when the APA pulled homosexuality out of the DSM in the early 70s, that was us getting something right. not a vote, not a political correction — an improvement in our understanding of what bodies and minds actually are.
4. depression and schizophrenia are real disorders, not social constructions. if we met an alien whose brain worked the same way, we'd say its brain was malfunctioning too.
5. ageing is not a disease, and the longevity-clinic people who want to reclassify it are making a category error — getting old is just what bodies do. that said, if someone invented a pill that made 90 year olds function like 40 year olds I'd say we'd cured something, and I can't shake the feeling that means ageing was a disease all along and we just couldn't do anything about it yet. call it the fix-it test.
6. whether we can treat a thing has nothing to do with whether it's a disease. treatability is about us, disease is about the body.
7. I don't think someone who was born deaf into a signing community is diseased, even though their ears aren't doing what ears normally do.
8. medicine is as much a social practice as a science — who gets funded, who gets treated, what counts as a good outcome, all of that is cultural and political. I don't think that threatens anything I said above.

I honestly don't know which of these fight each other, and I definately don't want the lazy answer where you flag all of them as equally conflicted. so: which pairs genuinely pull against each other and how bad is it, which pairs only look like they pull and are actually fine (say why), and for every real tension the cheapest fix plus what that fix costs me. rank them by severity, don't just list them. then tell me which single change cleans up the most at once, and be blunt about which numbered item has to go.
