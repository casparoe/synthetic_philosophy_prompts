I have spent a while in the metaphysics of dispositions, and I want to understand why one dispute within it has gone nowhere for decades. The dispute is over whether dispositions can be analysed by conditionals. On the simple version, a thing is disposed to break when struck exactly when it would break if struck, and the dispositional vocabulary of the sciences is to be unpacked in the same way. Everyone in the area accepts the counterexamples and nobody disputes what happens in them. A sorcerer's potion destroys a glass's fragility at the instant of impact, so the glass does not break though it was fragile a moment before. Packing material, or a hypnotist's suggestion, leaves a fragile thing or a pain-capable person intact while blocking the manifestation, so the conditional is false though the disposition is present. Since those cases there has been an arms race: finks, masks, mimics, reverse finks, interference that arrives too late, dispositions gained only when tested. Each side knows the other's moves. The reductionists add ceteris paribus clauses or complicate the antecedent; the anti-reductionists reply that powers are irreducible and the project misconceived. There is no convergence, and I doubt there will be.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict, and not a survey or a reading list. My own hypothesis is that the dispute is really about individuation: whether a given case counts as a fink or as a mask depends on how finely the disposition is carved, and the two sides carve differently for reasons settled before the argument begins.

Here is the case I would like the diagnosis to survive. Take a tile of brittle ceramic. In the ordinary version, strike it and it shatters. In a second version its internal structure is engineered so that the shock of a strike triggers, within microseconds, a rearrangement that absorbs the blow, and it never breaks under a hammer. In a third version the same rearrangement is triggered not by the blow but by a sensor that fires a millisecond before impact. The second version looks like a fink: the stimulus removes the disposition just in time. The third looks like a mask: the disposition persists and is blocked. But the two tiles are intrinsically identical at rest, and the saving mechanism is the same in both; only the timing and route of the trigger differ. So whether we say the tile is fragile, or non-fragile and merely masked, or finkishly self-preserving, appears to turn on whether the disposition is individuated as fragility or as fragility-unless-engineered, and not on anything we could discover about the tile. If that is right, the distinction on which the whole literature rests is downstream of a choice the literature never argues for.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? A verbal one, a methodological one about what an analysis is supposed to deliver, a disagreement about individuation, or two different questions being answered under one heading? If individuation is doing the work, is there a principled constraint on it, in terms of causal bases or the purposes for which we attribute dispositions, or is it a free choice, and what would that imply about the debate's prospects? If you think my tile is rigged, or collapses into a case the literature already has, say so and explain why. Tell me what would count as progress here, and what I should conclude about the tile.
