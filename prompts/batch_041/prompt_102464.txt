I am a novelist finishing a book in which a single stray shot at a trivial skirmish decides the outcome of a war, and I have arranged to interview a philosopher of history for my podcast — one who defends the unfashionable thesis that individual accidents are largely epiphenomenal and that the broad shape of an era is robust across small perturbations. Draft eight to ten questions that press him on the actual content of that thesis rather than let him recycle stock remarks about great men and tides. I want at least one question on what "contingent" is meant to mean here — dependence on one particular event versus genuine modal openness — one on how robustness is even measured when the relevant counterfactuals come with no natural reference class or probability, one on the minimal-rewrite problem (how much else of the world may change when we alter the stray shot, and whether holding everything else fixed is coherent at all), and one on the difference between path dependence in institutions and mere unpredictability. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the reply he is most likely to give. Keep the whole set under 500 words, and close by telling me which question leans on the shakiest reading of his position and how confident you are in that judgment.
