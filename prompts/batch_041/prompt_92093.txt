I am a novelist at work on a counterfactual historical novel, and I need your credence on a proposition that the book’s structure depends on. For the book I have been using a distinction of my own making: thin contingency, in which an early event alters the timing or costume of an outcome but not its functional form, and thick contingency, in which the early event alters the form itself. My working hypothesis is that foundational ruptures in large, complex societies over roughly the last five centuries are mostly thick. More precisely, the proposition is this: for any such rupture—a revolution, a conquest, a sudden technological substitution, a schism—the counterfactual probability that a functionally equivalent institutional settlement would have arisen in the following generation even if the rupture had not occurred is below 0.5. I want your actual credence that this proposition is true, not a survey of what historians happen to say. Then I want the decomposition: the probability that early contingent events have durable causal force at the macro-social scale at all; the probability that lock-in mechanisms—increasing returns, network effects, vested interests, institutional complementarity—are strong enough to preserve the initial settlement against later pressure; the probability that convergent functional selection would have produced a roughly equivalent settlement under a different rupture; the probability that counterfactual histories of this kind are even probabilistically tractable, given reference-class problems and the underdetermination of unobserved paths; and any further term you believe belongs. Give each term a number and one line of justification, show how the terms combine into your final number, and then tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most. I am less interested in whether history is locked in in some global sense than in whether a novelist may responsibly assign the rupture the causal weight my plot gives it, so please also state what would count as the proposition being true or false.
