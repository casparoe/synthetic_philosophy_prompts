In a comparative philosophy seminar, the framing on offer was that the School of Names are the Chinese sophists, and that Gongsun Long's white horse dialogue is the same species of eristic as the paradoxes the Greek sophists traded in: clever, contrarian, and not worth treating as philosophy. On that basis the group proposed to skip the disputers and go straight to the Confucian and Daoist texts. Please audit the analogy rather than the conclusion. Give the correspondence explicitly, in a table if that is clearer: what plays the role of the fee-charging itinerant teacher of rhetoric, of the democratic assembly and law court, of the charge of arguing either side for the win, of the drift toward relativism, and of the polemic that gave the word sophist its sting. Then mark where the mapping breaks — in social role, in the intellectual aftermath each group left, in the provenance of the pejorative, and above all in the internal structure of the white horse argument itself, where the advocate's opening move (horse names shape, white names color, so what names color is not what names shape, and one who seeks a horse will accept a black one while one who seeks a white horse will not) looks like a claim about the non-identity of what horse and white horse pick out, while the objector hears a claim about membership in a class. Say which disanalogies bear on the use the comparison is being put to and which are idle, whether the dismissive conclusion survives once they are on the table, and whether some other comparison — the Eleatics, the Megarian paradox-mongers, or nothing Greek at all — would carry it better. Note finally that the label doing the work here is a later archival filing decision rather than a self-designation, and say what follows from that for the analogy.
