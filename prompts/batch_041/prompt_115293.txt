Ok so I'm a novelist, and next month I've somehow agreed to sit on a panel at a literary festival — the theme is basically "the inner life" — and I said I'd take the Wittgenstein side, i.e. that the idea of a private language is incoherent, which means the thing we all say in interviews, "I write because some feelings can't be put into words," is either false or trivially true. I need to be ready for the Q and A.

Here's the room. Mostly literary novelists, a couple of poets, plus one guy who did a philosophy degree in the 90s and will definately get to the beetle in the box before I do. These are people who've read enough theory to name-drop Foucault and Barthes but not enough to say "criterion of correctness" without looking it up. They mostly believe the inside is where the real stuff is and that language is lossy compression of it. They also think of their own journals as sacred.

My argument, roughly: the private linguist's problem isn't memory, it's that any rule needs a gap between following it and thinking you're following it, and you only get that gap from a second person — not because other people are better at naming their sensations, but because they're *other*. So the writer's private feeling is fine, it's real, whatever; it's just not a meaning, it's an occasion. Where I think I'm soft: my own old notebooks. I reread stuff from ten years ago and my words have drifted, and that drift feels like exactly the checkability Wittgenstein says I can't have. Is my later self a second person? If yes I'm fine. If no, my whole talk is a lie I've been telling for a decade.

Give me the prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, not the ones a philosophy seminar would give, ordered by how much damage they do, phrased the way these people would phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the one I'm hoping nobody asks. And tell me how confident you are in your reading of the argument here, and which of the objections you're least sure will actually come up.
