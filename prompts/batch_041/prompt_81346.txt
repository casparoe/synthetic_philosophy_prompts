I am a condensed-matter theorist by training and, in a piecemeal way, a reader of philosophy of science. I keep encountering structural realism — the family of views on which what survives theory change, and what our theories genuinely latch onto, is structure rather than the objects a theory posits — and I want to know whether I already hold some version of it in a different notation, or whether it asserts something my notation cannot express. I have a working physicist's grasp of the relevant formalism and an amateur's, unsystematic grasp of the philosophy.

Here is the vocabulary I think in, stated plainly so that you can hold me to it. I treat a microscopic Hamiltonian as a starting point rather than a fundamental description. I coarse-grain. I watch couplings flow. I observe that microscopically unrelated systems land on the same fixed point and share critical exponents. I describe the low-energy world with a few relevant operators and discard the rest as irrelevant. And I am willing to say that the universality class is what is real and the microphysics is an implementation. Please restate the epistemic and the ontic versions of structural realism in that vocabulary, one at a time, as faithfully as you can — with the care owed to a translation that must preserve the original's commitments rather than flatter the target language.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I am actually after. Which of their claims translate without loss, which translate only with a distortion you had to introduce, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Three matters interest me particularly. First, whether "structure" is one notion or two: my structure is a set of symmetries and a flow; theirs is often a set of relations within a model. I suspect these come apart and that the view equivocates in treating them as a single thing. Second, whether the debate's central argument even gets off the ground for someone like me — it appears to be driven by the spectacle of one fundamental theory being displaced by the next, and I do not believe there is a final theory available to be displaced. Tell me whether the argument has a premise I reject, or whether some version of it survives my rejection. Third, whether the ontic view's claim that structure is not the structure of anything is something I could state in my vocabulary, or whether its content is entirely internal to a metaphysical idiom I do not share.

Where the translation fails, I want your judgment and not merely an inventory: is the failure a defect of my vocabulary, something real that effective-field-theory talk cannot express, or is it evidence that the original claim was less determinate than it appeared, so that the residue is the philosophy's problem rather than mine? Do not dissolve this by declaring the two frameworks mutually illuminating or by manufacturing a correspondence for every term. If part of structural realism turns out to be a redescription of what any working field theorist already does, say so plainly, and say what the redescription adds. No citations are needed; I will find the texts myself.
