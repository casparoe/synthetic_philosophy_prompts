Ok so a while back I stumbled on this idea called safe Pareto improvements and its been rattling around in my head ever since. Here's the version I've got, which is probably a bit rough. Normally when people say two agents reach a Pareto improvement they mean both end up better off in the world as described. The interesting case is when nobody's sure which world they're in -- the payoffs are fuzzy, or an AI doesnt know the full extent of what its principal actually wants, or the two sides dont even agree on what game they're playing. Then you can sometimes get an agreement that's better for everyone under every way of resolving that uncertainty. Safe, because it cant backfire no matter which version of the situation turns out to be true, whereas an ordinary Pareto improvement might only be an improvement under one guess about the payoffs and quietly be awful under another. I think it came out of the AI safety world, something about agents negotiating on behalf of humans whose values they're unsure about, but it seems like it should generalize to any two parties who are fuzzy on the details.
