Everyone's heard the line that RL agents can't really suffer because a reward signal is just a number, not a feeling. I want to go after a more interesting version of the idea, the one where valence is reward prediction error rather than reward itself: you feel good when things beat your expectation, bad when they fall short, and the raw reward level barely matters. Argue that this is a reductio. Take a fully converged agent that predicts its own reward stream perfectly — under this account it has zero valence all the time, so an agent parked on a big constant reward with no surprises is hedonically blank, which seems flatly wrong, and the same machinery makes the worst sufferer just the agent thats worst at predicting. Defend that line against the obvious reply that a converged agent is neutral rather than blank, and finish by saying what you'd put in place of the prediction-error account — something that handles surprises without making valence disappear for the well calibrated. I'd rather you commit than hedge.
