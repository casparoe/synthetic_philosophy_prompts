I am preparing a graduate seminar on the philosophy of time, and I intend to use the Rietdijk–Putnam argument as a case study in how an argument can become canonical while remaining deeply contestable. As I shall present it, the argument runs roughly as follows. Special relativity denies absolute simultaneity: for any pair of spacelike-separated events there are inertial frames in which they are simultaneous, frames in which the one precedes the other, and frames in which the other precedes the one. Take an event in my present, and an event that lies outside my present but is simultaneous with it in some other admissible frame. If the first is real, and if whatever is simultaneous with a real event in any admissible frame is itself real, then the second is real too; iterating the step, every event in the manifold turns out to be real, and there is no objective present dividing the real into past, present and future. My question concerns the objections rather than the exposition.

I have assembled the following six objections. Please arrange them into four tiers according to how much damage they do to the argument, where tier 1 contains the objections that are fatal or nearly so and tier 4 those that merely clarify or restate the argument's own commitments. Give each objection a short justification for its placement, and do not force a spread if you think several belong together.

1. The inference trades on a notion of simultaneity that relativity itself has shown to be frame-relative; 'simultaneous with a real event' therefore does not entail 'real', and the transitivity step is unwarranted.
2. The argument proves too much: the same reasoning would establish that all places are equally real, which no one denies, and which shows only that 'here' is an indexical — not that space is a static block.
3. The argument needs 'real' to be a one-place predicate in its conclusion but a two-place predicate ('real relative to a frame') in its premises; once it is made consistently two-place, the conclusion no longer follows.
4. The premise that the present is a simultaneity slice is optional: a causal notion of the present, given by the past light cone, is Lorentz-invariant, and on that reading the argument's first step fails.
5. My own objection: even granting that every event is tenselessly real, that is eternalism, not yet the static block, since becoming may survive as a Lorentz-invariant feature if it is relativised to a worldline rather than to a global present. The argument therefore does not establish the absence of becoming, which is what the block universe is usually taken to assert.
6. My own objection: the argument is dialectically inert. Its pivotal premise is intelligible only to someone who already accepts a tenseless reading of 'real', so it cannot move a presentist; soundness aside, it has no force in the dispute it is meant to settle.

After the tiering, give me an overall verdict: does the argument succeed, and if not, which single objection is doing the decisive work? Finally, rate on a scale from 0 to 1 how confident you are in that verdict, where 0 is a guess and 1 is certainty.
