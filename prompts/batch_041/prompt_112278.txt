ok so in my phil of mind seminar we spent basicly the whole week on the claim that the self is a center of narrative gravity. the prof's setup goes like this: the self stands to your brain and your history of behavior roughly the way the center of gravity of a plank stands to the plank. it's a real property -- you can compute it, you can use it to predict where the thing will balance and how it'll tip -- but if you go hunting for it under a microscope you'll only ever find wood. so the self is real but not a thing, and the usual worry about whether the self exists or is just an illusion is confused.

can you audit the analogy for me? give me the mapping first (a table is fine -- what plays the plank, the mass distribution, the balance point, the computation), then where the mapping actually breaks, then tell me which breaks matter for what he wants it to do and which are just noise. the thing i really want to know: does the no-fact-of-the-matter / can-be-rewritten conclusion survive the disanalogies, or was it riding on the center-of-gravity picture the whole time? and if the picture is bad, what's a better comparison that keeps the part that works -- real but not a thing -- without smuggling in the rest?
