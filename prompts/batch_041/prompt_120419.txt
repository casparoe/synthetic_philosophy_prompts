Next month I am recording a podcast conversation with a philosopher of mind who holds that we are warranted in attributing beliefs and desires to large language models, and that the warrant comes not from predictive success as such but from counterfactual robustness: an attribution earns its keep only if it supports predictions about how the system would behave under interventions on inputs it has never been sampled on. He also holds that the proper unit of attribution is the deployed system — weights, sampling procedure, and prompt distribution taken together — rather than the bare trained model. Draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply he is most likely to give. I want at least one question each on (a) why counterfactual robustness rather than plain predictive success is the criterion, and whether that makes his position empirical or stipulative; (b) what his unit-of-attribution claim implies for the ordinary dispute about what a model believes apart from any deployment, and what is supposed to individuate the unit; and (c) how he blocks the charge of permissiveness — that the same reasoning licenses belief talk about markets, termite colonies, or a training corpus. Add a question on systems whose behavior is best predicted by attributing mutually inconsistent beliefs, and one on the models' fluent self-reports: evidence for the stance, or merely more behavior for it to predict? Close with what would count against his view. Nothing about how he came to the problem.
