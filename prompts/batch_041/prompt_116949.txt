Ok so I'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy training, and I've fallen down a rabbit hole on property rights after a dumb argument with my roommate about our landlord. Someone pointed me at Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain thing — fans voluntarily hand him a quarter each and he ends up with a pile of money, and the claim is that if the starting point was just then the end point is just, no matter how lopsided it looks. I see the appeal but my gut doesn't line up with it in a bunch of cases, and I want to know what I actually believe. Here's where I land on a bunch of scenarios; I'd like you to tell me what principle (if any) generates all of it, and where my gut is just inconsistent.

- Chamberlain: totally fine, he earned it, nobody was forced. Even if he ends up with more than everyone else combined.
- My landlord bought our building in 2014 at a fair price, no fraud, and now charges market rent that's triple what it was. Fine. It's his. I can move.
- A guy in a desert town quietly buys up every well over 20 years, all voluntary sales, then charges whatever he wants for water. Not fine, and I think the town should be able to take the wells or cap the price. This feels genuinely different from my landlord and I can't articulate why.
- A squatter lives on a vacant lot nobody has touched for 30 years, farms it, builds a house. I think he should get title to it eventually.
- A guy inherits a company his grandfather built partly on land the family got when a court case was rigged against the previous owners. He personally did nothing wrong. I still think he owes something, but I don't know to whom, since everyone involved is dead.
- A museum holds a painting looted in the 40s and resold through three innocent buyers since. I think it goes back to the family. I feel strongly about this one.
- Someone wins the lottery. Fine. Obviously.
- A company patents something, charges 100x cost, refuses to license. I want to say fine — but if it's a drug people die without, I don't want to say fine, and that annoys me.
- My tentative principle: you own a thing if you came by it without force or fraud, and anything you get from voluntary deals with informed people is yours. That handles Chamberlain, the landlord, the lottery, and the patent. It definately fails on the wells, and maybe on the looted painting.

So is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give some of my gut calls up? If I have to give some up, which ones, and why those rather than the others? State your version precisely — what each clause is doing and which case forces you to add it — and then tell me what it says about a case I didn't list: I write a song, it's a hit, someone samples four seconds of it without asking, who owes what to whom.
