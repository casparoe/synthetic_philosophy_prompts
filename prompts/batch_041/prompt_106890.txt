At a causal-inference reading group I go to, a colleague who works on medieval Islamic philosophy said something that has been nagging at me: that al-Ghazālī's attack on the necessity of natural causation is, when you strip the theology, more or less where a careful structural-equations person ought to end up, and that everything since is commentary on how comfortable you are with that. I know the causal-inference vocabulary cold — directed graphs, structural equations, exogenous variables, interventions and the do-operator, potential outcomes, identifiability, the difference between a mechanism and a mere regularity, the whole apparatus — but I have essentially no medieval philosophy, so I can't tell whether my colleague is right or just pattern-matching. Please restate the occasionalist position as faithfully as you can, entirely in my vocabulary: what plays the role of the causal mechanism, what plays the role of God, what corresponds to an intervention, what it means on this view for a structural equation to hold only "by habit" rather than by necessity, what is supposed to happen at each successive instant of time, and how the view treats an ordinary event like a flame consuming something as against a miracle. Do not let the framework quietly supply assumptions the original does not have, and do not soften the theological commitments into a general scepticism about induction.

Then I want the part I actually care about, the residue report. Which components of the position survive the translation intact, which survive only in distorted form, and which have no counterpart at all in the language of graphs and interventions? Specifically: is the claim that the fire does not burn the cotton but that God does anything over and above the claim that the functional form relating contact to combustion is contingent and epistemically opaque to us — and if it is more than that, what exactly is the extra, and is the extra doing any work? Is a statistician who says "so you're just a Humean, and the rest is decoration" missing something, or has he correctly identified that the metaphysical remainder is idle? And when something fails to survive the translation, tell me which way the failure runs: is it a defect of my vocabulary, something about causation that interventions and structural equations genuinely cannot express, or is it a sign that the original claim was less determinate than it sounds and the residue is the sound of that indeterminacy?
