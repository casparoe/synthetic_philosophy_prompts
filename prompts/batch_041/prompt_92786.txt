You are a political philosopher specializing in the contemporary analytic debate over the concept of liberty — the contrast between negative liberty (non-interference), positive liberty (self-mastery or autonomy), and republican liberty (non-domination, understood as the absence of arbitrary power over one, whether or not that power is actually exercised). Please think carefully before answering. Begin with a short paragraph of brainstorming in which you sketch, in your own words, how each of the three accounts would likely approach the cases below; then give your considered answer.

I am drafting a municipal ordinance around what I am calling captive exit: arrangements in which a person holds a formal right to leave but the costs of exercising it are high enough that the right does no real work. The draft covers three settings: (1) a company town in which the employer owns most of the housing and the only grocery store; (2) a gig platform whose workers are nominally independent contractors but whose access to work depends on a rating the platform alone controls; and (3) a homeowners association that can levy escalating fines and place liens, where selling the home is the only genuine exit but requires clearing the lien first.

For each setting, I want the most important implications of each of the three accounts of liberty — not a summary of the theories, but what each account implies about whether the arrangement renders the person unfree, and what follows for how the ordinance should be drafted. Then tell me which of these implications matter most overall for the ordinance, and which are overrated, question-begging, or simply collapse into one another once spelled out. I am less interested in a survey of the literature than in the argument, so please do not pad the answer with reading lists or citations.

Return your answer as a table with these columns: Setting; Account (negative / positive / republican); Does this count as unfreedom under this account, and why (one or two sentences); The single most important drafting implication; Confidence level, and what would change your mind. After the table, give a short ranked list of the three or four implications that should most shape the ordinance, each with a sentence of justification, and note which account you think the ordinance should ultimately rest on.
