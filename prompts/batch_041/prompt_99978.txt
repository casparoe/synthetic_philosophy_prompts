I was reading an ethics blog post the other day and the author breezed through the is-ought gap and the naturalistic fallacy as if they were obviously the same discovery, and now I can't tell whether that's just sloppy popularising or actually right. Here's the gist of what they claimed, more or less in their words:

Hume discovered the is-ought gap, and the gap just is Moore's naturalistic fallacy. The point is that you can never get an ought out of an is -- any arguement that tries to pull a moral conclusion from purely factual premises commits a logical error, full stop. Hume proved that good can't be defined in natural terms at all; it's a simple, non-natural property that you either intuit or you don't. That's also why he held that reason is only ever a servant of the passions, since reason can only tell you what's true, never what to do. The upshot, the author said, is that science can never tell us anything about morality, that nobody has ever actually produced a valid derivation of an ought from an is, and that the whole naturalistic project in ethics -- Aristotle, the evolutionary types, the moral psychologists -- is dead on arrival. The only way out is to start from some basic moral premise you accept on faith or intuition and build upward from there.

I'm not after a reading list or citations -- I can't look anything up, so please don't send me off to check sources. I just want a fair audit. Take it sentence by sentence and tell me which bits a Hume specialist and a Moore specialist would both sign off on, which are the usual simplifications that quietly lose something important, and which are plainly wrong. In particular: is it fair to either philosopher to say the two fallacies are the same thing, or does that flatten a real distinction? Does the non-naturalism about good actually belong to Hume at all, or has the author smuggled Moore in under Hume's name? And does the closing conclusion -- that naturalistic ethics is dead and that the gap means facts are simply irrelevant to morality -- really follow from whatever is true in the summary, or is that a leap the summary doesn't earn? I've always sort of taken the gap for granted myself, so I'd also like to know where I've probably been oversimplifying, not just where the blog author has. Be honest about the parts that are genuinely contested rather than settled. American English, please.
