I work in quantitative risk at a mid-size insurer — my day job is mostly tail modelling, VaR and expected shortfall, ruin theory, and the occasional argument with the capital team about whether our copula is doing anything or just laundering ignorance. So I'm comfortable with heavy tails, ergodicity vs. non-ergodicity, Knightian uncertainty, decision-making under model uncertainty, all that stuff. A friend recently dragged me into a conversation about "suffering risks" — the idea that the worst possible futures aren't the ones where humanity goes extinct, but ones where something keeps going and there's an enormous, maybe unbounded, amount of suffering, such that the outcome could be worse than extinction. I have basically no background in the philosophy or the effective-altruism side of this, so before I form an opinion I want to see the thing done properly in the language I actually think in. A colleague of mine keeps saying it's "just a fat tail" and that we should be able to price it like anything else, and I suspect he's wrong, but I can't yet say exactly why.

So here's the ask. Put on the hat of someone who is both a serious philosopher of risk and a working quant — think carefully before you write, and start by just brainstorming and listing out the core claims people make in the s-risk discussion, so I can see the raw material before you start translating. Then, for each of those claims, find its nearest counterpart in quantitative risk management — portfolio risk, loss distributions, tail risk, expected shortfall, ruin and risk of ruin, reinsurance and capital allocation, stress testing, hedging and diversification, probability vs. ambiguity — and restate the s-risk case as faithfully as you can in that vocabulary. Please don't smuggle in assumptions from my field and call it a translation; if the s-risk people mean something my machinery genuinely can't state, say so instead of fudging it into something it isn't.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about: which claims translate cleanly, which only translate with distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Be especially honest about where the expected-value and expected-shortfall apparatus breaks down — the ergodicity assumptions, the idea that losses are bounded by the portfolio, the assumption that there's always someone left to bear the loss or be compensated, the treatment of unbounded or infinite disvalue — and finish by telling me what you think the residue means: is my framework missing something real, or is it exposing that some of the s-risk talk was vaguer than it sounded? And give me a verdict on my colleague's "just a fat tail" line. Answer in American English and feel free to be blunt; I'd rather be told my tools are the wrong tools than get a polite nothing.
