I'm the research editor at a small popular-history magazine. Part of my job is triaging pitches and manuscripts that make big unorthadox claims -- a lost Ice Age civilization, the pyramids being poured concrete, Fomenko's re-dating of basically all of ancient history, ancient astronauts hidden in the Sumerian texts. Right now its mostly vibes: if it feels crankish we pass. That's burned us twice this year, once by publishing something we shouldn't have and once by passing on something that later turned out to be respectable. I want a procedure I can actually run on a manuscript in half an hour, and that my two interns could run without me. It should end in one of three verdicts: treat as ordinary scholarship, publish but flag as contested, or decline as pseudohistory. Ground it in whatever the demarcation literature actually settled -- I gather not much, so be honest about that -- and keep it usable by someone who hasn't read any of it. Order the questions, say what counts as a good enough answer to each, and mark the spots where it stops being a factual question and becomes an editorial call I have to sign off on myself. Then run it on three cases: Fomenko, the ancient-astronaut reading of Sumerian texts, and the pre-Clovis migration fight, where the fringe side basically won. Tell me where it hands me an answer I won't like, and name one boundary it must not cross -- I don't want a rubric that quietly classifies religious or political conviction as pseudohistory just because it makes empirical claims.
