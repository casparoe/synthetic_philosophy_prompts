I teach a graduate seminar on personal identity and would like a specification to hand the students before they work through the literature. Write the desiderata for a theory of personal identity — what any adequate account of who survives what, and of what matters in survival, has to deliver. Each requirement should be stated so that a theory could visibly fail it, should name the case that motivates it, and should come with a sentence on why it is a genuine requirement rather than the preference of one camp; note which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable. I am more interested in the list itself than in your verdict on the familiar contenders, so leave the scorecard for class.

Treat the following as data the theory must accommodate, and let them shape the list rather than merely decorate it:

(i) The arrangement Dennett presents in "Where Am I?": the brain is kept alive in a vat in Houston while the body is sent off on its errand, the two joined by radio links, and the subject finds that his point of view does not follow the brain into the vat.

(ii) A stuttering transplant: the brain is removed and kept functioning in isolation for a stretch of time during which the subject remains conscious, and is then implanted in a second body. The theory must say where the subject is during the interval and whether anything of him persists across it.

(iii) Duplication: the brain is copied rather than moved, so that an original and a duplicate coexist, each carrying the same memories and each claiming the first-person perspective.

(iv) Gradual merger: two subjects' psychological profiles are interleaved over a period of months until a single stream of experience remains; the theory must say whether identity, or what-matters, comes in degrees, or whether the outcome is simply a new subject with two ancestors.

Finally, flag any pair or subset of the desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement eliminates the most otherwise plausible theories. Keep the whole response under 600 words and write in American English.
