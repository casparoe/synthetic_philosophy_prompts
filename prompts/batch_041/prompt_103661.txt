Alright, I need a bit of satire for a philosophy blog I write for. The target is the way consequentialists — the EA-adjacent kind — keep trying to do rigorous impact accounting even though they basically can't know what their actions will cause. Format: a mock internal grant-review scoring sheet from a made-up foundation, the kind of rubric reviewers fill out, except the whole thing has quietly gone off the rails because they've taken cluelessness completely seriously. Roughly 600 words, deadpan bureaucratic voice, numbered criteria with point values.

The jokes have to come from getting the philosophy right, so that someone who actually works on cluelessness nods along instead of wincing: the gap between not knowing the probabilities and not even knowing what the possible outcomes are; why tiny perturbations in the causal chain swamp whatever the grant actually does; why an intervention can look incredible ex ante and turn out worthless ex post; why 'just maximize expected value' stops meaning anything once the expectation is undefined or dominated by effects billions of years out. Give them a made-up metric like a 'Sign Stability Index' that nobody can really compute, and let the rubric reward flashy high-variance bets while docking the boring interventions that are actually robust to cluelessness — that inversion is the whole point. Maybe a footnote from an in-house economist who thinks the exercise is unfalsifiable. Dont strawman anyone; the absurdity should follow from the logic, not from making the reviewers dumb.
