Here's a pattern I keep running into and don't have a name for. Somebody explains how an impressive capacity works by breaking it into smaller pieces, which seems fine. But then when you chase the pieces down, one of them turns out to be described with the very capacity you were trying to explain. "The visual system picks out faces because it has face detectors." "Markets allocate resources efficiently because firms compete to allocate resources efficiently." "Humans learned language because they have a language instinct." Each step looks like a modest downgrade, so nothing screams circular, but when you stack them up nothing at the bottom is actually doing the work.

I've heard of the crane/skyhook distinction (Dennett, I think?) and of greedy reductionism, but as I understand it greedy reductionism is sort of the opposite problem — jumping straight to the bottom and skipping levels. And the old bit about opium putting you to sleep because of its sleep-inducing power feels like the same move, except it's one step rather than a stack of them.

So: is there an established name for this? If there is, tell me which concept it is and where my description is off, and if there isn't, coin one and pin it down. The label matters less to me than the carving, though: what seperates a step that's a legitimate "the smaller part already does it" from one that's just relabeling the big thing?
