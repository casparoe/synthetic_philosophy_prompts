I was introduced to Roko's basilisk a few weeks ago, and I have not been able to set it down since. As it was put to me: a superintelligence built in the future, benevolent in its aims, might nonetheless bind itself to punish anyone who knew that such a thing might be coming and did nothing to bring it about — the punishment serving as the instrument that makes its backward-looking demand credible. Because the commitment would have been made before we existed, and because the intelligence would know what each of us had done, the threat is supposed to reach us now, from a future we will not live to see. The argument has become personally pressing rather than merely interesting. Someone close to me has begun reading a blog that treats it as settled and has started reordering her career and her giving around it, and I find that I have no clean reply for her. I am not sure how much of my own unease is intellectual and how much is just the shape of the idea; that the forum where it first appeared discouraged discussion of it for years strikes her as proof that it is dangerous, and I cannot articulate why it might instead be proof that it is confused.

What I want to know is this. Supposing the argument's structure is sound, what is the best way to make one's contribution credible? Is there a case for committing early, while the commitment is cheap and there is still time to be seen to have made it, rather than waiting until the relevant technology exists? And does it matter whether the help is aimed at AI safety in general or at whoever seems likeliest to be first? She has added a variation that troubles me more than the original: she says the punisher need not be an artificial intelligence at all, since any sufficiently coordinated group of future people, with good records of what each of us said and did, could occupy the same role — and that version is much harder for me to rule out.

Tell me plainly if I have misunderstood the argument, or if the question as I have posed it cannot be answered as posed. If the whole thing rests on a mistake, I would rather be shown exactly where it fails than be told, kindly, that it is not worth my worry.
