For years I have been reading and occasionally posting on the rationalist forums, and I am now writing up the unilateralist's curse as a long reference post, because the standard treatments keep collapsing several distinct situations into one and I want the classification right before I write a word of prose. The curse, as I understand it: when any member of a group can undertake an action on their own initiative, and each member forms a private, imperfect estimate of whether the action's expected value is positive, then the chance that someone performs the action when it is in fact harmful climbs toward certainty as the group grows, since the group acts if any one member's estimate errs in the permissive direction. What I want from you is a decision tree over the space of cases and responses.

Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of named views: every internal node should be a question a reader could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to, and each leaf should carry the name of a decision procedure or stance together with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Aim for eight to twelve leaves, and close with a note on which single branch does the most sorting work.

The tree has to keep apart the things people wrongly file under the curse: the actors disagree about values rather than about the facts; they agree on the facts but differ in risk tolerance; the action's costs fall on people outside the group, who cannot weigh in; coordination fails for reasons that have nothing to do with the curse, such as the cost of communication or the impossibility of knowing who else is deciding; and the unilateral option runs the other way, so that any member can unilaterally halt or veto a shared undertaking rather than start one.

I want two branches that I have not seen treated properly. One tests universalizability in the sense the curse seems to need: would this action still be defensible if every similarly placed actor performed it? The other tests whether the action is information-producing — an experiment, a probe, a publication — as against world-altering and irreversible; I suspect these two license opposite verdicts, and I want the tree to show where they diverge.

Leaves I expect to find somewhere on the tree, though you should place each where the logic puts it rather than where I list it: a conformity rule, namely act only if you would be content for every similarly placed actor to act; a supermajority or unanimity requirement before anyone may act; secrecy or mutual precommitment against acting; pooling everyone's evidence before any single actor moves; licensing one designated actor to decide on the group's behalf; a market or betting scheme to aggregate the private estimates; making unilateral action costly; and the laissez-faire stance that each should simply act on their own estimate.

Two things I care about more than the leaves themselves. Mark the combinations that no one occupies, and diagnose each — is the view incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored? And flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, rather than papering over the gap with a node that does no real work.
