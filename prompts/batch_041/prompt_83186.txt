I've been covering consciousness research for about a decade and there's one argument I keep walking into that never moves an inch, and what I want from you isn't a verdict on who's right — it's a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement it actually is.

Here's the setup, briefly, because the details matter. Two dots get flashed in different spots on a screen, a fraction of a second apart, and they're different colours, red then green. Subjects don't report two dots. They report one dot travelling across the gap and changing colour partway, in mid flight. The odd bit is the timing: the change seems to be experienced before the second dot was even shown. So either the brain retroactively rewrote the first slice of the experience itself — the past of the experience got edited, which is the option usually labelled Orwellian — or the experience was perfectly ordinary and what got rewritten is the memory or the report, the Stalinesque option. Both stories produce identical behaviour, identical words out of the subject's mouth, identical later recollections. The labels are usually credited to Daniel Dennett.

My hunch, and please treat it as just a hunch, is that this isn't a real disagreement about the brain; it's two camps answering different questions under the single word "experience", one asking when some content got into the system, the other asking when the person became the sort of thing that could report it. Or it's worse than that and the question is ill-posed, because there's no one moment at which the content gets "shown" to anybody. But I've had researchers insist there's a genuine empirical fact of the matter here, that some measure — priming, no-report paradigms, the timing of the neural response — could in principle settle whether the revision is Orwellian or Stalinesque.

So: diagnose it. Lay out what each side is actually claiming, say whether the dispute is verbal, substantive, or ill-posed, and if it's substantive tell me what would count as progress — an experiment, a distinction, a formal result showing there can't be one. I also want an honest answer on whether "when did it enter experience" is even a well-formed question. I dont need a literature survey.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and dont restate my question back to me.
