I am a novelist, and I am writing a book in which two of the principal characters are philosophers of action who have spent their careers on opposite sides of a single question. One holds that an agent is morally responsible for what she does only if she could have done otherwise — that the ability to do otherwise is a genuine condition on responsibility rather than a symptom of it. The other holds that this condition is false, and that the right kind of example shows it: an agent who acts for her own reasons while a counterfactual intervener stands ready to guarantee that very action should she waver, and who never has to lift a finger. Their quarrel is the substance of my book, and I have concluded that I cannot write their scenes honestly unless I first possess the document they themselves should have produced years ago — the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration. I would like you to draft it.

I do not want a verdict, and I do not want a dialogue. I want the constructive artifact, the text both of them could put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact enough that a reader can see precisely where two serious people part. Please treat the following case as the collaboration's common test object. I invented it for the book, and it is deliberately not one of the familiar examples.

The Regent case. A woman intends to do something she takes to be wrong — she means to betray a friend — and she does it, out of her own resentment and her own reasons, without hesitation. Unknown to her, a device (call it the Regent) is monitoring her. Had she been about to refrain, the Regent would not have forced her hand; it would have done something subtler. It would have replaced her reason for acting, seeding in her a fabricated grievance, so that she would have acted all the same, but from a reason that was not hers. In the actual case the Regent remains inert and she acts from reasons that are entirely her own. The alternative that was foreclosed was therefore not the alternative of not acting; it was the alternative of acting from a different will.

Now please produce the document.

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign — about what the debate is and is not about; about the structure of counterfactual-intervener cases and what they do and do not establish on their own; about which conditions on responsibility (some form of control, ownership, or sourcehood) both accept; about what the Regent case does and does not settle; and about whether the mere presence of an inert intervener can by itself defeat responsibility.

2. A disagreements table, one row per sub-question still in dispute. In one column, state the defender's position in wording she would accept as her own; in the next, the proponent's, likewise in his own wording; in a third, name the crux that row turns on. Include at least: whether the woman in the Regent case could have done otherwise; whether what matters is an alternative action or an alternative will; whether an action that was in fact the agent's own can be blamed if it was not robustly avoidable, so that the only nearby worlds in which she acts differently are worlds in which she acts differently for reasons that are not hers; and whether the case covertly assumes determinism — the reply that if the case requires determinism the agent's responsibility is already in doubt, while if it permits indeterminism the agent may have had an alternative after all.

3. For each crux, identify a case or argument both sides agree would bear on it — not settle it, merely bear on it — and record each side's prediction about how it would come out. Where the two sides would accept a shared statement of the form "if this, then I lose ground," include it. Where either side would count something as evidence against its own position, say so.

4. A short section on how much of the dispute is verbal: if some part of the disagreement is really about how "could have done otherwise" is to be analyzed rather than about any fact, say which part and why, and say what would remain disputed even after the verbal part is set aside.

Please write it in the register of a formal co-authored statement — measured, first-person plural where both are speaking, and scrupulous about not smuggling one side's idiom into the agreed claims.
