Draft the joint statement that a committed reasons internalist and a committed reasons externalist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The internalist holds, roughly, that a true statement of the form "A has a reason to φ" is analytically or conceptually tied to the possibility of A's being motivated to φ under some appropriate deliberative condition; the externalist holds, roughly, that such a statement can be true independently of any motivational possibility in A, and that its truth is a matter of normative fact rather than psychological availability. I want the document they should have produced: a numbered section of agreed claims -- about what a reason statement is doing, about the explanatory and advisory functions of reason-talk, about the status of the amoralist, about what "fully rational" can and cannot mean, and about which of their disagreements are substantive rather than verbal; then a disagreements table with each position's formulation written so its own side would accept the wording; then a crux section, where each crux is stated as a sub-question whose settlement would move at least one side; and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

To force the collaboration into the weeds, use the following stipulated case and taxonomy rather than the usual stock examples. Consider an agent, M, who recognizes a consideration C as counting in favor of an action φ. Distinguish four motivational profiles: (1) M is occurrently motivated to φ; (2) M is not occurrently motivated, but is disposed to become motivated by ordinary deliberation that does not add new non-normative information; (3) M is not motivated and would not become motivated by such deliberation, but could be brought to care by an external intervention -- a pill, a novel, a threat -- that does not change what C is; (4) M is not motivated and cannot be brought to care by any such intervention, though M continues to recognize C. Ask the internalist and the externalist to say, for each profile, whether M has a reason to φ, whether M's reason statement is true, false, or indeterminate, and what the statement is doing when uttered. Then have them record where they predict the other side will concede and where they predict it will not.

Also include a section on the advice test: what an advisor must be able to say to M, in each profile, for the advice to be sincere, correct, and action-guiding. And a section on whether the internalist's "fully rational" clause is doing normative work or merely procedural work, and whether the externalist can explain the explanatory force of reason statements without collapsing into a mere list of normative truths.

End with a candid section in which each side states what would change its mind -- which argument, case, or result would lead it to abandon or substantially revise its position -- and a note on where this joint statement is most likely to be tendentious, unstable, or to have papered over a genuine disagreement. Do not include citations, reading lists, or a bibliography; the document should stand on its own argument.
