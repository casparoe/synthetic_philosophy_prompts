I'm writing a research proposal for a one-semester grad seminar term paper (about 6000 words) in formal epistemology, and I have a half-formed idea I want you to help me turn into something I can actually execute. The topic is the Judy Benjamin problem. Quick version of the setup so we're on the same page, since I dont want the proposal to depend on me reciting it right: Judy is dropped into a dark field she knows is split into Red territory and Blue territory, and each territory is itself split into an HQ zone and a non-HQ zone, so four cells, and she starts with a uniform credence of 1/4 on each. Her radio tells her that if she's in Red territory, she's three-quarters likely to be in the Red HQ. The problem is that this doesn't fix a unique posterior. A bunch of different credence functions are consistent with the conditional credence in HQ given Red being 3/4, and the textbook fix, which is to take the posterior that's closest to her prior in the sense of minimizing relative entropy (the KL divergence), is one answer among several and not obviously the rationally required one. So people end up arguing about which posterior is "correct," and I've never found that arguement convincing, because it feels like quarrelling about a convention.

My half-formed thought is that the whole dispute may be a symptom of an ambiguity nobody has been careful about: when she "learns the conditional," is she learning a fact about a conditional probability, namely that the credence in HQ given Red is 3/4, or is she learning that a conditional proposition (something like: if I'm in Red then I'm in Red HQ) is true, or 3/4 probable? Those are different things, they impose different constraints on her posterior, and I suspect the reason the standard solutions disagree is that they're quietly answering different questions. If that's right, the puzzle might dissolve once the reading is fixed, or better, it might turn into a genuine question about which reading the situation actually supports. So I want the proposal to take a question roughly like: does the underdetermination in the Judy Benjamin problem come from an ambiguity between conditional-probability information and probability-of-a-conditional information, and once that ambiguity is removed (plus some principle about what else has to be held fixed), is there a single motivated update, or does the ambiguity turn out to be a red herring?
