In my phil of mind seminar we've just done Dennett's colour phi argument and I want a number out of you rather than another summary of it. Quick setup so we're on the same page: you flash a dot in one colour at one spot and a dot in a different colour at another spot a fraction of a second later, and people report a single dot travelling across and changing colour partway along the path — the colour change seems to happen before the second dot is even on the screen. Dennett's move is that any story about this has to be either Orwellian (the experience gets assembled after the second dot arrives and then written into memory as if it had happened earlier) or Stalinesque (the experience itself is held back until the second dot is in), and that since the two agree about everything downstream — same input, same report, same neural goings-on — and only disagree about whether some point in the processing counts as before or after 'experience', there's no fact of the matter about which one is true.

So: what's your credence that there IS a fact of the matter about whether the colour phi case is Orwellian or Stalinesque, i.e. that one of those descriptions is correct and the other wrong in a non-trivial way? I want the decomposition, not just the number. At minimum: the probability that conscious experience has a determinate temporal location and order at all, such that questions like 'did this experience occur before or after that neural event' have answers; given that it does, the probability that the phi case comes out Stalinesque rather than Orwellian; the probability that some conceivable experiment could in principle tell the two apart; and the probability that the distinction is verbal rather than two competing hypotheses. Add whatever terms you think are missing, one line of justification each, then how they combine.
