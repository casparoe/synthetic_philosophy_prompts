My brother-in-law has an argument he brings out every Thanksgiving and I've never been able to shoot it down, and it bugs me alot. It goes like this.

(1) The whole point of taxing inheritances is that nobody should get a head start off pure luck -- which womb you happened to fall out of.
(2) But money is the smallest part of what actually gets handed down. My niece got her first internship because her mom plays tennis with a partner at the firm. My nephew has a huge vocabulary because nobody at his dinner table ever told him to shut up and listen. Both of them got braces, therapy, a quiet room to do homework in, and the general sense that grown-ups take them seriously.
(3) You can't tax a vocabulary. You can't confiscate a phone call from a family friend, or the feeling that adults are interested in what you think. And if you tried, the state would be sitting in the living room deciding how parents talk to their kids, which is worse than the thing you were trying to fix.
(4) So if the money head start is unfair, the vocabulary head start is unfair too, and there's nothing anyone can do about that one. Which leaves two options: let the state into the living room, or admit the money head start isn't unfair either and the inheritance tax is just envy with paperwork.

He thinks the second horn is the honest one -- he says the family is just the last aristocracy we're still sentimental about. I'd rather keep (1) and (3) if I possibly can, but maybe that's just me wanting my cake and eating it. So which step is doing the damage, and what does it cost me to patch it? And if the fix is something like "the state can tax money but not love," tell me why that isn't just picking whichever version of fairness happens to be convenient for people like us.
