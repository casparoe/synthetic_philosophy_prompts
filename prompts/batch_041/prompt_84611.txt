long shot, but i need to know if this thing has a name or if ive just invented a category.

Case one: we got a shift-booking system, mostly to stop people double-booking the rota. no hidden agenda, everyone voted for it. two years later nobody argues about who deserves the saturday shift any more, they argue about what the system will let you request. someone says 'it wont let me swap' and thats the end of the conversation — not because anyone thinks the rule is fair, but because nobody can point to the moment it was decided. to object you first have to reconstruct a decision that was never actually taken.

Case two, same shape: the loading bay got a keycard reader, officially just for security. now 'can you let me in after six' has stopped being a thing anyone can ask for. nobody forbade it.

what interests me is that the machine isnt forcing anyone, the people who set it up had no politics, and you can still technically do it the old way. its that the tools way of doing the thing slowly becomes the definition of doing the thing, and then objecting sounds like objecting to arithmetic.

ive read enough to know this isnt technological determinism (thats machines driving history) and isnt the medium-is-the-message line (thats about how media reshape us). i also half remember an essay called do artifacts have politics, but that seemed to be about someone deliberately building a politics into a bridge or a tomato harvester, and here there was no deliberation at all, just drift.

so: is there a name? if there is one i want to know exactly what it catches that those three dont, and if there isnt, coin me one — and then tell me where it stops. does a speed bump count? a dress code? a standard screw thread? because i can feel that some of those are the same thing and some arent, and i cant say which.
