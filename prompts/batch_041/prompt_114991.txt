I am a graduate student in a historiography seminar, and we have spent the term examining the claim that the standard division of European history into the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution is not a neutral description of the past but a set of retrospective constructions, each coined and consolidated by later scholars, institutions, and ideological projects. I accept much of this: I can see that period labels are selective, that they impose boundaries on continuous processes, and that they often tell us more about the people who use them than about the people who lived under them. But the argument has pushed me toward a conclusion that I suspect is too strong, and I would like help diagnosing where the inference goes wrong.

If the Middle Ages were invented by Renaissance humanists, and if the Scientific Revolution was invented by twentieth-century historians, then it seems to follow that medieval people could not have experienced themselves as medieval, that early modern natural philosophers could not have been living through a revolution, and that the changes we associate with these periods—new institutions, new methods of inquiry, new ways of marking time—are merely projections backward onto an undifferentiated flow. On that reading, periodization is nothing but a power move, and it would be misleading to say that anything genuinely happened between, say, 1500 and 1700. Yet the same historians who debunk these categories continue to write books about the Renaissance and the Scientific Revolution, and they do not seem to think they are writing fiction. So my question is: how should I distinguish the claim that a period label is a construct from the claim that the events, experiences, and transformations it labels are unreal? Is there a principled way to say that the Renaissance is an invention while still saying that something changed in fifteenth-century Italy, or that the Scientific Revolution is a historiographical artifact while still saying that something happened in seventeenth-century Europe? Please answer as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. In one of the five points, state what would change your mind about the correct way to draw the distinction, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
