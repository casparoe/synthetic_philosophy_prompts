I am a retired experimental physicist — most of a career in condensed matter — who has spent the last several years reading philosophy of science for the first time, largely without a teacher, and I have arrived at a question I cannot settle for myself. I would like two things from you: a single number, and the machinery that produces it.

The proposition I want a credence for, stated as precisely as I can manage:

(M) The multiverse hypothesis — in any of the forms that arise in inflationary cosmology and in the string-theoretic landscape — is a genuinely scientific hypothesis, in the sense that it can be rationally accepted or rejected on evidential grounds even if no observation could in principle distinguish it from a single-universe alternative.

Please give me your credence in (M). But I care much more about the decomposition than the number, so structure the answer as follows. First, list the sub-questions on which your credence in (M) actually depends, and give each a rough probability. I expect these to include at least: whether the relevant multiverse hypotheses are well-defined enough to bear a truth value at all (I gather there is trouble assigning probabilities across branches, and I want to know how much that matters); whether a correct theory of confirmation requires that a hypothesis make some distinguishing prediction, so that a strict likelihood-based or falsificationist standard is the right one; whether non-empirical virtues — unification, explanatory reach, fertility — can carry genuine confirmatory weight rather than merely pragmatic weight; whether the fine-tuning or anthropic argument supplies any real evidential boost or is circular; and whether the distinction between the empirical and the non-empirical is itself sharp enough for (M) to be a determinate claim. Add any term you think belongs and drop any I have listed that you think is doing no work, but say why.

Second, tell me how the terms combine into your final number, and be explicit about any term that is doing most of the lifting.

Third — and this is the part I most want — keep two credences strictly apart, because I keep conflating them: your credence in (M) as I have defined it, and your credence that some multiverse actually exists. If they diverge, say exactly where the divergence comes from, and whether a rational person could be high on one and low on the other.

Finally, name the single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and tell me what evidence or argument — of any kind, including philosophical rather than empirical — would move it most, and in which direction.
