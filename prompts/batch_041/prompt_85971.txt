I'm writing the specification for a theory of life before committing to any particular account of it. The theory's job, for my purposes, is to license defensible yes/no verdicts on whether a given system is alive, issued by someone with limited data and no prospect of gathering more — a biosignature officer reading a distant instrument, say. Give me the desiderata such a theory has to meet: the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must not collapse, the findings from biology and chemistry it must not contradict, each with the consideration that makes it a requirement. Two cases I want handled explicitly, since I think they are where the usual checklists strain: first, a chemically active, self-repairing system sealed in a stable environment that neither reproduces nor evolves; second, a lineage of self-replicating molecules that does evolve under selection but has no metabolism of its own, its copying driven by apparatus I can switch off. For each desideratum, mark whether it is non-negotiable or the preference of one camp, and flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable; note also which single requirement does the most to rule out otherwise plausible accounts. Close by naming the requirement you are least confident belongs on the list, and what you would have to be shown to drop it.
