Then in their late 20s they get surgery and sight comes on. Three days later a researcher puts a cube and a sphere on a table in front of them, same colour, same material, tells them the two objects are a cube and a sphere, and asks them to point at the cube. No touching, no lifting, no sounds, no sniffing. One shot. My claim, the thing I want a credence on: they point at the cube correctly, better than chance, on the strength of vision alone. Resolution is obvious enough — run the test.

What I want isn't a vibe, it's the number plus the parts it's made of. How much rests on whether someone 3 days out of surgery can even parse the retinal image well enough for the task to say anything about the philosophy, as opposed to just measuring post-surgical visual chaos. How much on the test leaking — two objects, both already named, forced choice, maybe they can tell them apart by size or brightness or which one throws a shadow. And how much is just plain stipulation about what 'by sight alone' is supposed to mean once the person has had two decades of visual-ish experience through another channel.
