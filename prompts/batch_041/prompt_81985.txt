I'm in a phil-of-mind / cog sci reading group and I've ended up as the one defending something like this, so I'd like to know what it's actually exposed to. The claim: talking about real patterns isn't just a polite way of talking about useful fictions. A pattern is real to exactly the degree that it earns its keep as a description -- and I want to sharpen that, because I think the hand-wavy version is what makes people roll their eyes. So, my three conditions: a macro-pattern P over a system S is real iff (i) there's a many-to-one map from S's micro-states onto P-states, so P is a genuine coarse-graining and not just a label we slap on; (ii) P-states are stable, i.e. small perturbations of the micro-state leave you in the same P-state, so the pattern isn't an accident of one trajectory; and (iii) the P-level dynamics are autonomous, meaning you can predict or explain what P does next without going back to micro-facts you've already thrown away. Given that, I want to say the intentional stance is a stance in the same sense as the physical and design stances, and that beliefs and desires are real in the same currency as centers of gravity, temperature, species, and the arrow of time -- real because the coarse-graining holds up, not because there's some little inner sentence doing the work. My friend in the group says this is instrumentalism with extra steps, and another one says its circular: you use the intentional stance to pick which descriptions count as good, then use the good descriptions to prove the intentional stance is pulling its weight. Also he says if my criterion ends up making folk psychology a real pattern then its either the best thing about the view or a reductio, and I honestly can't tell which.

So what would count against it? I don't want a list of criticisms of the view, I want the actual defeaters, sorted by how decisive they'd be, including ones that have arguably already shown up empirically or in the surrounding debates. Things I keep chewing on and don't know what to do with: does the whole thing collapse if (i)-(iii) turn out to be language-relative, i.e. if whether P is stable depends on which class of perturbations you chose, and that choice is something you make rather than a fact about S? Is there a version of the view where that's fine, or does it just reduce pattern-realism to saying I like this description? And what happens in the case where a design-stance or subpersonal description of some behavior turns out to be at least as short and at least as predictive as the intentional one -- by my own criterion, does that show the beliefs aren't real patterns, and is that a live possibility or a scarecrow? I also worry that on this account a real pattern can't do any causal work (its the compressibility doing the explaining, and compressibility isn't a mechanism), so does the view predict anything that a Fodor-style realism about inner states, or a flat eliminativism, doesn't?

I'd also like to know which of these a committed defender would themselves concede counts against them, versus which they'd wave off as a misunderstanding, and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- degrees of reality, real enough, real relative to a project -- and if so whether that's fatal or just the honest cost of doing metaphysics. If you think its well insulated, say so, and say whether that's a bug. And if I want one thing that could actually change my mind, what should it be: a formal result, a possible case, a bit of empirical work, or a case where my criterion gives an answer I'd have to accept and can't stomach (the self? race? the market? a hurricanes eye?).
