So I've been down a sphexishness rabbit hole for the last few weeks and I need someone to help me get my head straight. The gist, as I'm sure you know: Hofstadter's coinage for the digger wasp that supposedly just re-runs its little subroutine — drag the cricket to the burrow, drop it, inspect the burrow, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches while its inside, it comes back out, finds the cricket, drags it over, and then inspects the burrow all over again, on and on, apparently forever. Dennett loves it as the picture of the mindless automaton we supposedly aren't, the thing free will is the negation of. What threw me is that the anecdote itself looks shaky — I gather Keijzer went back through the experiments and found the wasps do eventually adapt, and that the repeated nest-checking might be perfectly adaptive on its own terms rather than some robotic tic. So now I can't tell if sphexishness is a genuine phenomenon, a description-level artifact, or just a really sticky story that survived on rhetorical merit. Which, honestly, might be the most interesting thing about it.

Anyway, here's what I actually want from you. I'm taking a semester of leave this spring — no lab, no fieldwork, just me, a library card, and way too much coffee — and I'd like to walk out with one solid paper and a much cleaner sense of the terrain around sphexishness, behavioral flexibility, and whatever any of this has to do with free will. So give me an agenda: what are the live open questions in this neck of the woods, and which two or three would you actually put at the top of my list, in order? I care way less about the list than the reasoning — for each one, why is it upstream of the other questions rather than downstream, how tractable is it for a philosopher with no bug lab and no funding, how neglected is it, and what kind of argument or finding over the next year would move it up or down the ranking? Please defend the ordering and be honest about the trade-offs between your criteria, because I suspect neglectedness and tractability point in opposite directions here.

A few specific things I'd love you to take a position on, since I keep going in circles. First, one reframing I can't shake: maybe sphexishness isn't a property of agents at all but of descriptions — every system is sphexish under some coarse enough description, so the real question isn't who's sphexish but what makes one description the right one for ascribing agency, and that question might be where the whole debate actually lives. Is that a paper, or is it a dodge?
