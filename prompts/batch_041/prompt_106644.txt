I'm on the board of a small cooperative of freelance illustrators — about 200 of us, mostly people doing children's books, textbook diagrams, that kind of thing. We're meeting next week to vote on a minimum-rate pledge. The idea is that members agree not to take a job below a posted floor, with an exception you can apply for if you're in a tight spot, decided case by case by the board. Half the board is convinced this is the only way out of what's happening to us: everyone keeps undercutting everyone else, rates are down maybe a third in four years, and individually none of us can stop, because the client just goes to whoever bids lowest. The other half says we're just a cartel, that publishers will hire non-members anyway, and that the hardship exception will swallow the rule since the people undercutting are undercutting because they need the money.

Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action — adopt, don't adopt, or adopt with specific conditions and a time frame — with reasons the other side could actually respond to. And be honest about the strongest thing they've got; don't just tell me what I want to hear.

A few things I want you to take seriously rather than hand-wave:

- Our membership isn't uniform. Some of us have agents and steady series work; some are just starting out and would take anything. A floor that binds the second group but not the first might just push the entry-level people out of the market entirely, which is the opposite of the point.
- Whatever we do about the floor, we still have to sell work to publishers who don't give a damn about our pledge. So say plainly whether a pledge like this can do anything at all when the people we're negotiating against aren't in the room — and if it can, what exactly is supposed to be doing the work.
- One member has proposed that the penalty for undercutting be a cut of the fee, paid into a hardship fund that pays out the exceptions. That struck me as clever and also possibly a way of making the exception permanent. Tell me what you think of it.
- Someone else wants the floor indexed so it can only ever go up. I have a bad feeling about that but can't articulate why.

Also flag anything about the legal exposure I should know about before I put my name on this. And one thing I keep circling: is what's happening to us actually a trap, or is it just a market where some of us are losing, and the difference matters for what we should do?
