I work as a statistician — Bayesian, mostly; I think in priors, likelihoods, sampling distributions, reference classes, exchangeability, averaging over hypotheses about how big the population is. The flashpoint is that the second view seems to let a sliver of a chance of an astronomically large population dominate a big chance of a small one, so you're supposed to bet on the huge-population branch — the "presumptuous philosopher" thing. Restate both assumptions and the fight between them in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly onto sampling-distribution talk, what only fits if you fudge it, and what in the philosophers' disagreement has no counterpart in my framework at all. And tell me whether that leftover means my framework is missing something or that the debate is less substantive than it sounds. American English, please.
