I've been turning over the idea that one theory can be closer to the truth than another even when both are false, and I want the standard of adequacy pinned down before I go further with it. What I need it to be good enough for is judging, without appeal to taste, which of two false accounts of the same subject is the better one. So write me the requirements list for any account of "closer to the truth": what such an account has to deliver, not what any particular attempt happens to say.

The cases it has to get right: two rival false theories where one is plainly nearer the mark; the complete truth, which must be the maximum, and a true but empty statement, which must sit nowhere near it; the cautious theory that risks little and must not be rewarded for its caution; and the theory that carries a great deal of true content along with one glaring error. The distinctions it cannot collapse: between being closer to the truth and being more probable, more informative, or more useful. The results it cannot contradict: if a proposal has the consequence that no false theory is ever closer to the truth than any other false theory, the requirement it violates is the one I most need stated sharply.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and say whether it binds every account or is only a preference of one style of account. Two things I especially want flagged: whether the demand for more content and the demand for more accuracy can pull in opposite directions, and whether the verdict on which of two theories is closer may flip when the same two theories are described in different but equally good vocabulary. If some pair of requirements cannot be jointly met, say so, say which one you would give up, and tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out otherwise attractive accounts. No citations or reading lists — I cannot look anything up, and the list itself is what I want.
