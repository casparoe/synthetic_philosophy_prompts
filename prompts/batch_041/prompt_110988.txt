ok so I've been chewing on this for months and I want to write down where I've landed before I tie myself in knots again. It's about lying versus just misleading people, and I've got a feeling I'm helping myself to something I shouldn't be, but I can't see where.

Here's my picture:

1. A lie is when you say something you think is false in order to get someone to believe it.
2. When lying is actually wrong, it's wrong because of the deceiving. Somebody ends up with a false picture of things. No deception, no harm, nothing to be upset about.
3. And yet the sneaky true-but-slanted thing — steering someone to the wrong conclusion without ever saying anything false — has always struck me as at least as bad as a straight lie, maybe worse. I've definately done the "well technically I never said that" move and felt grimier about it than I would of felt about just lying.
4. But I also think there's something extra wrong with the lie itself, on top of whatever deception it pulls off. Language is a shared tool and we're all leaning on each other to use it straight; a lie is basically bait. You're taking the very thing we all depend on being trustworthy and turning it into a trap. Even if I could trick you just as well with a slanted truth, the flat lie adds a wrong that the slanted truth doesn't, because the slanted truth at least kept to the letter of the deal.
5. Honestly I don't think intent counts for much. What counts is where the other person ends up. If they end up misled, that's the wrong. If they don't, no harm.
6. Still, I'd say you can lie to someone you know won't believe you — or to a wall, or just to make a point — and that still feels like a lie to me.

What bugs me most is 3 against 4: sometimes the misleading looks worse, sometimes the lie looks worse, and I can't tell if I'm just flip-flopping or tracking two different things. But I suspect the real trouble is somewhere I'm not looking. So: does this hold together? Which bits actually pull against each other and how badly — rank them — which ones only look like they clash but don't, and what's the cheapest thing I'd have to give up to make the rest fit? Be specific about which piece has to go, and don't just tell me it's all about framing.
