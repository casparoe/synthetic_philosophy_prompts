Consider the MIU string-rewriting system: begin with MI, and repeatedly apply any of four rules—append U to any string ending in I; after the leading M, double whatever follows it; replace any occurrence of III with U; and delete any occurrence of UU. It is a fact that MU can never be produced, and the usual proof of this abandons the rules in order to exhibit an invariant. Now distinguish two ways of abandoning a formal system: a weak jump, in which the reasoning from outside could in principle be re-encoded as reasoning inside a stronger system that takes the original system as its object, and a strong jump, in which no such re-encoding is possible. Which kind of jump is required to prove that MU is underivable, and what are the most important implications of your answer for the claim that the recognition of such invariants—the exercise of mathematical insight—is itself a computational process?
