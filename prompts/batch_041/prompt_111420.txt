Ok, I want to preasure-test a view about reasons that Ive ended up with, and I want the honest version of what counts against it, not a survey of the debate.

The view: all reasons are internal. A consideration is a reason for you only if a sound bit of deliberation from where you already are would get you to it. The part I actually care about is that I dont think this is a separate claim about action. I think it's the same claim as internalism about justification on the epistemic side, just pointed at different subject matter: in both cases, what you have reason to believe or do is fixed by whats within reach of your current perspective — your evidence in one case, your motivations in the other — and not by facts that are true of you from the outside. So the two debates are one house, and they stand or fall together. I havent seen it put quite that way, which is part of why I want it tested.

So what would count against it? Rank the defeaters, fatal down to merely awkward. A few things I'm already worried about and want taken seriously rather than waved off. First, the sound-deliberation test is a black box: if I idealize the agent's motivations enough to get the verdicts we want, have I smuggled externalism back in, and if I don't, do I have to say the person who genuinely doesn't care has no reason at all, which sounds awful? Second, belief isn't under voluntary control and action is, so maybe the parallel only looks tight because the two sides mean different things by reachable. Third, the amoralist: if I explain away our impulse to say he had reason to help as a claim about what we may demand of him, is the view now unfalsifiable, and is that a problem for a view of this shape or just its price?

Also tell me what internalists themselves would concede counts against them, not just what the critics say. And where would the one-house claim actually break — is there a result or case on the epistemic side with no practical analogue, or the reverse? If the two-house view is right, what should we expect to see that mine wouldn't predict?

What would move me: a case where we're all confident someone has a reason, theres no route to it from their psychology even under generous idealization, and it can't be redescribed as a belief about what they'd be moved by. If thats out there, I'm done.
