What I'm after is a joke whose punchline turns on the actual mechanics of the problem: that any relation supposedly responsible for unifying the constituents of a proposition into a single thinkable content — or for holding between two terms — seems to need a further relation to attach it to those constituents, and then another to attach that relation, and so on without end, so that the very thing meant to explain unity either never gets off the ground or presupposes what it was meant to explain. Someone who has actually worked through the debate should be able to recognize that the humor comes from the structure of the regress rather than from the mere mention of Bradley's name.

A seed, if it's useful: I keep picturing a support group for relations that are worn out by the demand that they account for their own attachment to their relata, with one of them protesting that it was hired to relate two things and has somehow been made responsible for relating itself to them as well. Keep it short enough to deliver out loud, and make sure it lands for an audience that has thought seriously about how a proposition differs from a mere list of its components.
