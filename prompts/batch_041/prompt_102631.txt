ok so im putting together a little zine for my reading group and i want to do a poem about Turing's weird bit on telepathy. you know the one, at the end of the 1950 paper where he's listing objections to the imitation game and the last one is ESP, and he says the evidence for telepathy is actually pretty strong, and that if it's real then the whole test is compromised because the interrogator might be reading the machine's mind or the machine might be reading the interrogator's. his fix is to put them in a telepathy-proof room, which is a great phrase but also kinda a cheat because we don't know how to make a room like that or even what telepathy would need to get through. also he thinks a machine might be better at telepathy than a person, which is a fun inversion.

i want a sestina. the end-words should be the six things the test needs to hold still: the room, the wall, the answer, the guess, the mind, the machine. the whole point of the form is that the end-words migrate, they show up in the wrong stanza, in the wrong order, and by the end you can't keep track of who started with which word. thats the telepathy worry in miniature: the boundary between the two minds doesn't hold. the envoi should be the moment where the interrogator's word and the machine's word are the same word and you can't tell which is which. dont make it a debunk, Turing wasn't debunking, he was genuinely spooked. it should feel like the test is only fair if the room is telepathy-proof, and we don't know how to make the room.
