I keep going back and forth on where manipulation ends and legitimate persuasion begins, and I've come up with a test I want you to argue for and then try to break. The test: an influence is manipulation exactly when it would stop working if the person on the receiving end understood precisely how its working — call it the transparency test — and persuasion is influence that survives being seen through, or at least doesn't depend on staying hidden. On this view a lot of what we casually call "emotional manipulation" is really persuasion (the emotion is doing honest work the target would endorse), and a lot of smooth, reason-giving "persuasion" is really manipulation (the reasons are there mainly to stop you looking at the mechanism). I want the strongest case that this test draws the line in the right place: say what "would stop working" has to mean so that the test isn't circular, deal with the obvious objection that almost any influence weakens under the spotlight, and follow it out to at least one conclusion that'll annoy people on both sides. Then give me the single case that hurts it most and say honestly whether the test survives it. Write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, or bold text, the way you'd write an email.
