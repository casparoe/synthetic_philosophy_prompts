i just moved abroad for a job and honestly its the small stuff thats throwing me, not the big cultural stuff. where i'm from, tipping at a restaurant was basically mandatory, like 20% or you're a bad person, and here you don't tip at all and people act like it would be weird if you did. where i'm from you could cross against the light if nothing was coming and nobody blinked; here everyone stands at an empty crossing at 6am waiting for the little green man, and i've gotten actual looks for stepping off. and the queueing: the bakery by my flat has no sign anywhere, but the rule everyone follows is you take a paper ticket when you come in, except if you only want bread and the counter is free you just walk up, and if someone who was obviously ahead of you gets served first somebody will cough meaningfully. same people, at the kiosk in the station, will absolutely shove past you.

what i want to know is what these practices presuppose. does the no-tipping thing presuppose that the price on the menu is the whole price and that service isn't something the customer has any business paying for separately, i.e. a view about what a wage is and whose obligation it is? or is it just a convention with no theory behind it that i'm overreading? same with the bakery: does the ticket rule presuppose that your place in line is a kind of property you acquire by arriving, or that queues are fair only because they're blind to who you are? those come apart the second the person who grabbed a ticket first was just chatting with a friend and i only want one roll. and the red light: is the practice assuming that a law serving no purpose at 6am generates no reason at all, or that it's a coordination device whose whole value is that everyone does the same thing even when its pointless?

don't just lay the options out and leave it to me. tell me which reading you actually think is right for each one, give your reasons, and say clearly where you're just guessing. and flag it if a practice is doing no philosophical work at all and i'm just reading theory into it.
