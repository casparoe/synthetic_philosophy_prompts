Write a short story about a soilder named Judy who gets dropped into a territory thats split into Red and Blue zones, and also into Second Company and Headquarters zones. She has no clue where she is, so she figures each of the four quadrants is equally likely. Then she gets a radio message from a source she trusts completely. The message says that if she is in Red, she is in Second Company. Thats it. Now she has to update her beliefs. The problem is that theres no single obvious way to do it. If she treats the message as a material implication, she gets one posterior. If she tries to minimize how much she changes her credences, she gets another. Different reasonable rules give different answers for how likely it is that shes in Red, and that matters because she has to decide whether to head east or west. Your story should show her wrestling with this, maybe trying out different ways of updating, and maybe realizing that the right answer depends on philosophical choices she didnt know she was making. Make it a story, not a philosophy paper. Dont just have her plug into Bayes rule; the whole point is that Bayes rule doesnt directly apply. You can have her be a philosophy grad student who got drafted, or just a soldier whos surprisingly good at math. Be concrete and vivid. Also, dont quote any real papers or use any citations. And try to make it in the weeds: really get into the details of why the conditional is tricky.
