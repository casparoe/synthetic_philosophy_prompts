Last night I lost an argument about Popper and Darwinism, and I would like to lose it more precisely, because I think my friend won it with an analogy that does not deserve to win. His case runs roughly like this. People keep saying Darwinism is unfalsifiable and they attribute the charge to Popper, on the strength of his remark that natural selection is very nearly a tautology and his willingness to call Darwinism a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable theory. My friend says this whole line of attack misidentifies what the analogy is an analogy between. Natural selection, he says, is not a first-order empirical hypothesis of the kind that competes with a rival within biology; it is a schema — variation, selection, retention — and it is the very same schema that the growth of knowledge itself instantiates, the one Popper himself set out as the movement from a problem to tentative theories to error-elimination to a new problem. Darwinism, on his reading, is that form applied to the living world, and the theory of natural selection is therefore on a par with the logic of the situation rather than with a conjecture inside it. From this he concludes that the demand for falsifiability, applied to Darwinism, is a category mistake: you cannot ask the form of the process to be falsified, any more than you can ask modus ponens to be falsified. The accusation dissolves, and the adverse verdict Popper once rendered — that Darwinism is a programme rather than a theory, a judgement he is widely reported to have softened later — was, my friend says, a confusion Popper eventually came to see through himself. I think this is too quick, but I could not say where it breaks, and I suspect the analogy is quietly carrying the whole argument.

What I want from you is an audit of the analogy, not a verdict on Popper's reputation. First, lay the correspondence out explicitly: what plays the role of the conjecture, the refutation, the retention, the problem situation, the selective environment, the scientist's aim or intention, and the truth or progress that the process is supposed to approach — on both the scientific and the biological sides. Then tell me where the mapping breaks, and, more usefully, which of the breaks matter for the specific conclusion my friend draws and which are decorative. In particular I want to know whether the analogy, taken seriously, actually supports his conclusion that the demarcation worry dissolves; whether it supports the opposite, that a schema compatible with every outcome confers no testability on anything that instantiates it; or whether it is simply silent on the question. Say what turns on the disanalogies I can already half see — that variation in science is conjectural and directed at a problem while variation under selection is supposed to be blind, that refutation in science is informative and non-lethal whereas the elimination of a variant is neither, and that the schema is silent about what the selection is for, if anything. If there is a better analogy for what Darwinism is, or a cleaner way to state the point my friend is groping toward that does not lean on this one, give me that as well.

Throughout, keep separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the material, and do not hand me one camp's reading of Popper as though it were the consensus — where his considered view, or the question whether he had a settled one, is disputed, say so, and say which parts of the dispute are about the texts and which are about what the texts should have said. I do not need citations, a reading list, or a survey of the literature; I need the conceptual work done.
