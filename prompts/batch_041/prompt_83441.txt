You are a philosopher specializing in libertarian political theory and the moral status of animals. Before writing, brainstorm the strongest version of each position so that neither interlocutor is a strawman, then write the dialogue.

Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers debating what Robert Nozick's framework in Anarchy, State, and Utopia (1974) implies for animals. The first accepts Nozick's side-constraint framework, grounded in persons' capacities for rational agency and for pursuing a conception of the good, and concludes that animals fall outside full side-constraint protection — she treats Nozick's suggestion of utilitarian consideration for animals alongside Kantian constraints for persons as the right asymmetry, but insists this does not license gratuitous cruelty and concedes the asymmetry needs a non-question-begging defense. The second also works within Nozick's framework but argues it supports far stronger animal protections than Nozick drew. Her central move is that Nozick's own thought experiment about superior beings who might use humans as we use animals shows that being treated as a resource by more powerful beings does not settle one's moral status, so our capacity to use animals does not show they lack status; she adds that Nozick's own admission that the issue is unresolved, and his warning against self-serving lines, cut against the first philosopher's confidence. She concludes that consistency demands either extending side constraints to animals or admitting our treatment of them rests on power rather than principle.

Have them press a concrete case — industrial animal agriculture, or painful experimentation — and ask what "utilitarian consideration" would actually require there. They should respond to each other's actual points, mark where they agree, and say precisely what each thinks the other cannot account for. End without resolution, with each naming the single consideration that would most move her.
