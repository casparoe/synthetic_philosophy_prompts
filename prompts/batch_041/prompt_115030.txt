Alright, I've got a reading group Friday on whether the wavefunction is real and I've been told to bring an actual position instead of just vibes, so here's my attempt. I'd like to know whether it hangs together before I get taken apart in front of everyone.

(1) I'm on the psi-epistemic side. The quantum state is a summary of what an agent knows / their degrees of belief about what they'll see — it isn't a physical object sitting out there in configuration space. So no, I'm not a wavefunction realist.

(2) I don't think there's a hidden layer of stuff underneath the quantum state. QM is complete as far as I'm concerned; there's nothing deeper waiting to be found.

(3) Measurement outcomes are objective. When a detector clicks, that's a fact about the world, not a fact "relative to me" or "relative to my lab". Any two observers who compare notes will agree about what happened.

(4) The probabilities that come out of the Born rule are objective chances. There's a fact of the matter about how likely a given decay is, and it isn't just a fact about my personal betting odds.

(5) I think the PBR theorem is basically right — the proof looks solid to me — and I still want to be a psi-epistemicist.

(6) There's no collapse. The dynamics is unitary, all the way, always. And I take this to follow pretty much straight from (1): if the state is just my information, then "collapse" is obviously just me updating my beliefs, nothing physical is going on.

(7) I'm neither a many-worlds person nor a Bohmian. One world, one outcome, no pilot wave.

I've also got a take on Wigner's friend that I'm fond of: when the friend measures the spin and sees "up", that outcome is just plain true; Wigner's later superposition description of the whole lab is only Wigner's information, not a rival fact that makes the friend's result un-happen. So the friend case isn't a paradox at all, it's a point in favor of my view.
