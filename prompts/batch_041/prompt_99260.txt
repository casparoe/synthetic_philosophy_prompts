I'm a science journalist and I'm putting together a two-page proposal for a small public-ideas fellowship (six months, one long essay plus a workshop). My half-formed angle: the whole naturalism-vs-normativism fight over the concept of health — is health just statistically normal biological functioning, in the Boorse-style "biostatistical" line, or does it secretly smuggle in values, the normativist line — quietly assumes there's a well-bounded organism with a species-typical design to be normal about. But a lot of biology now treats the real unit as the host plus its resident microbiome, a composite (some people call the combined thing a "holobiont") whose "normal" state depends on which microbial partners it happened to pick up, and which can differ hugely between two people with near-identical genes and environments. So what I want out of the proposal: state the question so it actually has a determinate answer, say why the existing naturalist and normativist positions don't settle the microbiome case (or maybe why both can, but end up disagreeing), lay out the method — I'm not running experiments, I'm doing conceptual work on cases — and tell me honestly what would count as the project failing, and whether six months is even realistic for someone who isn't a trained philosopher. Don't bother with citations or a lit review, I'll add those myself. Write it in American English and keep it tight — no filler, this is a proposal not an essay.
