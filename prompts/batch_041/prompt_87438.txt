I'm a second-year PhD student — philosophy, but my project has drifted into game studies and I'm definatly in over my head — and theres a pattern I keep running into that I can't make sense of, so let me just ask it straight: what does the way virtual worlds actually handle virtual stuff presuppose about whether any of it is real?

Here's the pattern. Take any big online game. The terms of service are adamant that you own nothing — your account, your character, the sword you ground for forty hours, all of it is only licensed to you, the company can delete it or switch the servers off tomorrow and you have no claim, and if someone hacks your account and empties it the official line is basically tough luck, that stuff was never yours. So far so deflationary: virtual objects are just data, virtual losses aren't real losses. But the same company then bans gold farmers for wrecking the game economy, sells you the very same kinds of items for real money in its own shop, and treats a duping bug that floods the market as a genuine crisis. Players get punished by the platform for griefing and harassment that involve no bodies at all, and I'm fairly sure there have been actual criminal convictions in Europe for stealing items inside a game, which only makes sense if the items belonged to someone. And at least one big platform does the opposite and says you do own what you make, so the licensing thing looks like a choice, not a necessity.

So: what theory of what virtual objects and virtual harms are does this whole mess presuppose? Is the practice secretly committed to them being real — real enough to steal, real enough to harm — or to them being mere representations whose value is always borrowed from the offline world? Can one consistent view cover the EULA and the theft cases and the harassment bans, or is the thing just incoherent, and if it's incoherent, which part is doing the real work? Which rival views does it rule out? And if you took the deflationary line seriously, or the realist line seriously, what would actually have to change in the terms of service, in the law, and in how platforms moderate?
