I've been sitting in on a reading group about time perception, and there is a fork in the explanations that I keep running into and don't have a word for. Two of the cases we looked at. In the first, a red dot is flashed on one side of a screen and a green dot on the other side a fraction of a second later; people don't report two dots, they report a single dot that slides across and turns green about halfway. The colour change is reported as happening before the second dot was ever shown, which is the part that bothers me. In the second case, a word is flashed and then a pattern follows it almost immediately; depending on the gap, people say either that they saw nothing but a blur or that they saw the word perfectly clearly, and the change between those two reports is abrupt. What interests me is that both cases get explained in one of two ways, and the two ways are always presented as rivals. The first says the brain held the earlier event in storage, waited for the later one, and then assembled a single experience out of both, delivering it late — so what you consciously get is a construction that post-dates the second stimulus. The second says the experience was delivered immediately and accurately, that you really did see the dot turn green when it seemed to, and that what happened later is that the memory, and hence the report, was quietly rewritten, so that a tidied-up version replaced the original. The wait-and-assemble story and the rewrite-the-record story agree, as far as I can tell, about what content is present in the brain at each moment; they disagree only about which of those content-bearing states deserves to be called the experience and which deserves to be called the memory of it. They seem to predict the same reports, the same response times, and to be consistent with the same recordings. Someone in the group said that this makes the question of when the person experienced the change not merely hard but malformed, and everyone nodded.

What I want is the name of the fork, if it has one, and — more than the name — the carving. I keep feeling the two stories are less symmetric than they are made to sound. The wait-and-assemble story buys its result with machinery that sounds extravagant, since it has the brain idling with material in hand until a later event arrives. The rewrite-the-record story buys its result more cheaply in machinery but at a different price: if the record is routinely edited, then the reports I am using as evidence are exactly the thing the story tells me not to trust, and the explanation starts to look as though it eats its own data. Is there a name for a fork of this shape — two explanations that agree on the mechanism and differ only over where to attach a term — and is there a name for the further move of declaring the fork a non-question? I know the general idea that evidence can fail to decide between two theories, but that usually means not enough evidence has been gathered yet, whereas here it feels as though no evidence of the ordinary kind could ever separate them. And I know people talk about disputes that are merely verbal, but that usually means the parties mean different things by a word and talk past each other, whereas here they agree about everything except which state deserves the word.

I don't need citations or a reading list. I want to know what the nearest established concepts are, which of them fits this best and why the others don't, what would have to be true for the two stories to be genuinely different rather than two ways of describing one thing, and — if nothing in the literature covers this exact shape — a coined term with a definition and a couple of boundary cases so I can tell it apart from ordinary underdetermination on one side and plain equivocation on the other.
