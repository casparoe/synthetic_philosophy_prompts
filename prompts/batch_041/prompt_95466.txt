I sit on the rules committee of the tribunal we set up after the war, and I have to circulate a written opinion before Friday's plenary, so I could really use a hand drafting it. Here's the mess. Our founding statute — which we wrote ourselves, about six weeks after the fighting stopped — has four counts: genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and, over the objection of two of the convening powers, a count of crimes against peace, meaning planning and waging an aggressive war. The defence has filed a preliminary motion saying aggression wasn't a crime under anything our state had signed when the invasion happened, that the statute is therefore retroactive, and that this is just the winners judging the losers. They've also pointed out, and I'm afraid they're right, that one of the convening powers' own generals ordered a pretty comparable incursion into a neighbouring state a few years earlier and nobody is drafting an indictment for him.

Half the committee wants to strike the aggression count and keep the other three, on the theory that reaching for the most politically charged charge discredits everything else we do. The other half says if we drop it we've conceded that might makes right — the only reason it wasn't a crime is that nobody had the power to criminalize it beforehand. Then our legal adviser floated something she calls a reciprocity trigger: keep the count, but add a clause saying it lapses automatically unless every convening power agrees within twelve months to let the tribunal's jurisdiction reach its own commanders for the same conduct. Nobody can decide whether that's a principled safeguard or a poison pill that lets the guilty walk, and there's alot riding on getting this right.

So please draft the opinion I'll recieve and circulate: a clear recommendation — strike the count, keep it, or keep it with the trigger — plus the reasoning, plus an honest run at the strongest thing the losing side has. I don't think the retroactivity objection is as silly as some of my colleagues do, so don't just wave it away. I also don't need a history of Nuremberg or Tokyo, I know the broad strokes; what I actually need is whether the analogies everyone keeps drawing to them settle anything here or are just doing rhetorical work. Give me a concrete procedural next step with a deadline, and keep it to roughly a page.
