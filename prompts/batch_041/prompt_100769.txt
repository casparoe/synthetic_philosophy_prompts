I am trying to work out what compulsory military service, as it is actually practised in Switzerland and Finland, presupposes. Switzerland conscripts able-bodied men only, allows women to volunteer, and charges men declared unfit an extra income tax in lieu of service; Finland conscripts all men, exempts the inhabitants of the demilitarised Åland Islands, and exempted Jehovah's Witnesses from service until 2019; Sweden made its conscription formally gender-neutral in the very year it suspended it, then revived it a few years later. Please reconstruct the theory of political obligation this practice presupposes — whether it is a fair-play or reciprocity view on which service is a debt owed for benefits received, a natural duty of justice, a claim about the state's authority over the body, or a view of defence as a collective responsibility — and say which rival views it quietly rules out. Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, tell me whether its parts presuppose consistent things (a universal duty that can be discharged by paying money, that falls on men alone, or that lapses for the inhabitants of an archipelago), and say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined. What does pairing universal conscription with a declared policy of armed neutrality presuppose about the difference between defence and aggression? And what would have to change if the presupposed view were replaced by one on which a person's labour and bodily risk are alienable only by consent — would conscription itself have to go, or only its present shape? Please answer in British English.
