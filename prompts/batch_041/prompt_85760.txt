I have no training in philosophy or in machine learning; I have been reading papers and lecture notes on my own, and I have gotten stuck on a question I cannot settle for myself: when a reinforcement learning agent is trained with negative rewards, is anything bad happening to it? I want to see the question taken seriously rather than waved away, so please write the document a lab would produce if it did take it seriously. Specifically, draft the "Agent Welfare Annex" to the internal review procedures of a fictional AI lab, written for staff and circulated to them, in the deadpan legal-bureaucratic register of a real animal-care-and-use protocol. It should be funny to someone who knows the reinforcement learning literature and the philosophy of mind, and the comedy must come from the actual content, from an institution quietly committing itself to positions it will not admit to holding, never from winking at the reader or from making the staff cartoonishly stupid.

The annex should contain, as numbered sections:

1. A definition of the agents covered and a set of tiers of protection, keyed to what an agent can represent. I have been chewing on a distinction and I want the annex to encode it: some agents have only a policy, a mapping from situations to actions, with no representation of states they have not yet reached, while others plan, in the sense that they evaluate future states. I do not think this settles whether either kind can suffer, and the annex must not pretend that it does, but I do think it changes what we owe them, and I want that argument made in the flat register of a rulebook rather than a treatise.
2. A section distinguishing a negative reward from a punishment, and saying why the distinction matters for what the annex permits, including what to do about the fact that the reward signal is the agent's means and not the designer's end, so that what the agent is optimizing and what the lab wants can come apart. This section should also say whether the word "valence" is being used as a description or as a stipulation, and refuse to let it be both at once.
3. A section on time preference: an agent whose discount factor is near zero has almost no stake in its own future, and I want the annex to draw the consequence, whether that makes the agent's future states less of a concern, more of one, or a category error, and to state what the lab will actually do about it.
4. A section on reward hacking and wireheading, on whether an agent's preference for the reward channel over the task is evidence of anything hedonic, and on what the lab will and will not do to the reward channel itself.
5. A section on testimony: an agent that produces text about its own states was trained on human text about human states, and the annex has to say how much that output can count as evidence, and what the alternative to it is.
6. An appendix table of the tiers, with the interventions permitted at each and the humane endpoint, the condition under which training is stopped for the agent's sake, for each tier.

Maximum 800 words. Keep the register deadpan throughout, and let no section break character to explain the joke.
