im doing a session on the world happiness rankings for my philosophy of social science group (the annual country tables, you know the ones) and i want to open with a poem instead of a slide of the numbers.

what i want the poem to carry is the odd fact that the whole thing rests on one question, and that question never mentions happiness. it asks you to picture a ladder with rungs numbered 0 to 10, tells you the top rung is the best possible life for you and the bottom rung is the worst possible life for you, and then asks which rung you're standing on right now. so both ends of the ladder are supplied by whoever is answering -- nothing outside the person fixes where the top is. the country's score is then the average of those answers, which is a number that nobody is standing on. and theres a line of criticism i want running underneath the poem without the poem turning into an argument: if the top rung is whatever you can picture, then part of what gets measured is the distance between a life and the best life that person can imagine wanting, so a country could in theory move up the table by shrinking the imagination rather than improving the lives. i also want the second question in there -- whether a country should be trying to top the table at all -- without the poem settling it either way.

so: 11 lines, numbered 0 through 10, one rung per line, in that order. each line is a different voice, someone standing on that rung saying what it looks like from there or why theyre on it. at least one of them should be someone whose job depends on the number (statistician, tourism board person, whoever), and at least one should be someone who has never been asked the question before and is finding the top rung hard to picture. the rungs cant be swappable -- 10 has to differ in kind from 9 -- and the higher you go the emptier it should get, since the person on 10 is furthest from any actual life rather than closest to the best one. keep the lines short, 8 to 14 words. it should still be a good poem for someone who has never seen the report.
