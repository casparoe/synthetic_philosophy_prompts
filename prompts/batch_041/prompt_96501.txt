2. efficient: given what people actually want, it picks the best option, and when there's a cost, it only does the thing when the total benefit actually covers the cost.

4. budget-balanced and individually rational: any transfers net to zero (no surplus we can't explain, no outside subsidy), and nobody ends up worse off than if they'd just opted out of the whole thing.
5. deterministic: I really don't want to decide anything by coin flip. randomization feels like refusing to answer the question.
6. responsive: it should be able to output any of the options, i.e. no built-in favorite and no degenerate range that only ever spits out the same thing.
