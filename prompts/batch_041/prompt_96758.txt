I am preparing a two-page proposal for a one-semester research project in medieval political thought, and I would like you to turn a half-formed idea into a proper proposal. My starting point is the apparent tension in Aquinas's scattered remarks about killing a tyrant. In the Sentences commentary he appears to permit the killing of a tyrant in at least some circumstances, and even to treat it as praiseworthy when done to free the community; in the short work On Kingship he emphasizes that the removal of a tyrant belongs to public authority rather than to private initiative; and in the Summa's treatment of sedition he argues that disturbing a tyrant does not count as sedition, since tyranny is not ordered to the common good. The usual reconciliation distinguishes a tyrant by title from a tyrant by exercise, or distinguishes private action from public action. I want to test a different organizing hypothesis: that what actually controls Aquinas's verdicts across these texts is not the tyrant's legal status but the location of the office of judgment. On this reading, the permissibility of lethal resistance depends on whether the act is an exercise of a public authority charged with the common good or a private assumption of punitive jurisdiction, and the tyrant-by-title/tyrant-by-exercise distinction is doing less work than it is usually asked to do.

Please write the proposal. State the question so that it has a determinate answer, explain why the standard readings leave it genuinely open and what the strongest alternative readings are, describe the method — which texts and conceptual distinctions you would work through, and in what order, over roughly a semester — say what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure or as evidence that the hypothesis should be abandoned, and give an honest assessment of scope: what parts of the topic would have to be left out. I will supply the editions and citations myself, so do not give me a reading list or a literature review by citation. I am especially interested in whether the hypothesis can be made to do independent work — for instance, whether it predicts anything about a modern case such as a military officer or a private citizen contemplating lethal force against someone who is seizing power — or whether it quietly collapses back into the standard distinction.
