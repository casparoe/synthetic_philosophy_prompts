Just got home from a five-hour arguement with my friend Priya about competence without comprehension — the Darwin/Turing flip where you stop treating understanding as the thing that produces the doing and start treating the doing as the thing that produces the understanding, or at least the illusion of it. Her line, which I have not been able to shake: comprehension is just another competence. A system that can answer questions about why it did what it did isn't standing outside its own machinery, it's running one more subroutine on top of it — a subroutine for narrating, which is itself uncomprehended. She says reasons are free-floating, out there in the world doing their work whether or not anyone is home to hold them, and that "being home" is a story the machinery tells about itself. I said that's just a fancy way of changing the subject from consciousness, she said consciousness is the change of subject, and then we just went around in circles for two hours.

Write me a short story that takes her side seriously — say 1,500 words, no more than 2,000. Setting is your call, but I want the POV to be the thing that has all the competence and none of the comprehension: a termite colony, a market, a model, a bureaucracy, a forest, whatever grabs you. The hard part, and honestly the reason I'm asking: it has to be gripping from the inside without ever having an inner life to report, and it can't end with the reveal that it was secretly conscious or feeling all along. That ending is exactly what the arguement was about, and I'll consider the story a failure if it lands there.

If you can, work in the regress I couldn't get past: if every competence needs a comprehender behind it, and that comprehender's competence needs its own comprehender, where does it stop — and if it stops at something dumb like a reflex or a habit, is that an answer or just a place where we quit asking? Don't worry about who said what, I don't need the history or any sources, just the story.
