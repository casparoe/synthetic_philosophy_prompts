I retired from the geology department four years ago and I've been teaching myself philosophy ever since — mostly the decision theory and rationality stuff, which turns out to be closer to my old interests than I expected. I've hit a pattern I can't find a name for and it's bugging me. Let me give you two cases, because I have a suspicion they're not the same thing even though everyone I ask treats them as one.

Case one: a fellow whose preferences run in a circle. He'll swap his record player for a bike plus a small fee, because he'd rather have the bike; he'll swap the bike for a camera plus a small fee; and he'll swap the camera back for the record player plus a small fee. Nobody lies to him, nobody cheats. Three trades later he's holding exactly what he started with and less money. Every trade left him better off by his own lights at the moment he made it. I gather this is what people call a money pump, and I can see why it's supposed to show that something is wrong with the preferences.

Case two is the one I actually care about, and it has no circle in it. This person's preferences are perfectly tidy at any given moment — sit him down, hand him a list, he'll give you a clean ranking with no loops, every time. But the ranking drifts. Not because he learns anything, just because he's a person: he gets hungrier, he gets older, he gets used to things. So now imagine a patient trader. He doesn't need a loop, he needs a calendar. Sell the bike while the man wants a bike, wait eight months, buy it back when he's bored of it, sell him the camera when he's into photography, wait, and so on, taking a fee at each turn. The man is never confused, never weak-willed, never inconsistent *at a time*. He's just walked down a path, and the path ends somewhere he doesn't like, and there's no single moment where you can point and say, there, that's where he went wrong.

So: is there a name for case two specifically, or does "money pump" cover it and I'm being fussy? If it does cover it, why does every telling I run into use the circular version, when the drifting version seems to me far more common and far more troubling? I don't know anyone with loops in their preferences. I know plenty of people who've drifted.

What I want more than a label: is the badness in case two a property of him, or a property of the path? In my old line of work we had a clean distinction between quantities that depend only on the state of the system and quantities that depend on the route it took to get there, and I genuinely can't tell which kind of quantity "worse off" is supposed to be here. And if there's no trader anywhere in the world, is the drifting man still irrational, or merely unlucky that someone could have exploited him? When exactly is he irrational — I keep wanting to ask which time-slice of him made the mistake, and every candidate slice looks blameless. I also notice that telling him about the trader doesn't help; he just gets miserable, because he knows what's coming and still wants what he wants. And if he tries to lock himself out of it — money in a term deposit he can't touch, cigarettes in the bin — then the later him resents the earlier him, and I can't tell whether the earlier him had any business doing that. Is that a third thing, or the same thing wearing a hat?

Please don't just hand me a term and a reading list; I can't look things up at the moment anyway, and the carving is the point — what the concept covers, what it leaves out, which of my cases are definately the same and which only look alike. And if there's no name for case two, coin one and tell me what it excludes.
