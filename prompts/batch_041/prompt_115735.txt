The argument about the color phi phenomenon never seems to end, and I want to know whether it can end and what kind of disagreement it actually is. The phenomenon: two dots are flashed in different places and different colors, and with the right timing observers report that the second dot's color is already present at the intermediate positions, before the second dot appears — as though a later color had been written backwards into the earlier stretch of the experience. The two familiar readings are Stalinesque, on which the visual system inserts the color before the experience is delivered, and Orwellian, on which the experience is delivered unedited and the record of it is revised before it is retrieved or reported.

My hypothesis, which I would like examined rather than endorsed, is that the two readings are claims about the causal order of subpersonal events while the disagreement they purport to be about concerns the personal-level content of experience, and that no experiment can settle it because every measure — verbal report, reaction time, any neural correlate — lies downstream of whichever process does the editing; so the dispute would be ill-posed rather than merely hard. Two things keep me from resting on that diagnosis. First, one can distinguish the time at which a state is tokened from the time its content is about: a representation tokened late can represent an earlier moment, and it is unclear whether that distinction dissolves the puzzle or only renames it. Second, if the readings differ over whether the retrospectively inserted color is genuinely experienced or merely judged, we seem to need a notion of experience independent of what can be reported, and I cannot tell whether that is a substantive commitment or a verbal stipulation.

Diagnose it, then: is this a verbal disagreement, a substantive but empirically underdetermined one, or two different questions — one about mechanism, one about content — sharing a single name? Say what each side is actually committed to, what would count as progress (a distinction, an experiment, a formal result), and whether any progress should be expected.
