Ok so I'm a third-year PhD student working on work and time — the ethics of the 35-hour week, karoshi, that whole area — and there's a saying I keep tripping over and I cant pin down what it's actually claiming.

The saying is 'work is its own reward'. It turns up with cousins ('idle hands are the devil's workshop', 'a fair day's work for a fair day's wage') but the core one is the first, and the problem is I've watched it do totally different jobs in different mouths.

A libertarian-ish guy in my dept uses it to argue that a 35-hour-week law is patronising: it treats work as a cost to be minimised, when for most people work is where the meaning and the reward actually live, so capping it takes something away. Then a union organiser I interviewed used almost the same sentence to argue the reverse — if work is its own reward then the wage is secondary, and bosses lean on that to keep pay low and hours long. And in the karoshi literature people describe workplaces where overwork is sold as its own reward, and the saying turns into something that definately kills people. I've also got a half-memory of some political theorist distinguishing labour (endless cyclical toil, never finished) from work (making durable stuff that outlasts you), and I suspect the slogan quietly equivocates between them — labour is rarely its own reward, but work maybe is.

So what I want: don't pick a reading and explain it, give me the menu. What are all the precise claims 'work is its own reward' could be making — from the trivial to the radical? For each one, say whether it's a platitude, substantive and defensible, or just false, and say what kind of 'reward' it needs (intrinsic pleasure? meaning? a wage? character?) and what sense of 'work' it needs to even be plausible. Then tell me which reading each of my three people is probably invoking, whether any of the readings actually supports a right to work, a right to leisure, or that newer right to disconnect, or whether the slogan is just doing rhetorical work in all of these fights and settling nothing. No sources needed, I'll chase the references myself.
