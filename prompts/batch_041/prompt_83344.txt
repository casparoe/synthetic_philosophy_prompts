I'm TAing a metaphysics course and we've just finished the statue-and-the-clay week, and the whole literature is driving me a bit up the wall — everyone seems to just assert the thing they need for their own solution and then wave at somebody else's counterexample. Before I read any more of it I want the spec on paper. So: write me the desiderata for a theory of material constitution. What does any adequate account of what it is for a lump of clay and the statue made out of it to be the things they are have to deliver? I don't want a survey of positions, I want the checklist — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results from elsewhere it can't contradict (the bronze is just bronze through and through, nothing extra shows up in the chemistry), and the questions it has to at least answer: how many things are sitting on the pedestal, whether the statue and the lump are identical, what makes the difference between them if they aren't, what happens when you squash one and not the other. For every requirement give me the case or consideration behind it in a line or two, because I want to be able to see which of these are genuine constraints and which are just somebody's pet intuition dressed up as a datum.

Two cases I keep running into that I want the list to cover, since they don't come up much in what we've read. First, a bronze horse in a museum where the conservator has the tail cut off and a new tail cast from fresh bronze and attached — same form, same place on the plinth, but part of the matter isn't the original matter; is that the same statue, and is it the same lump? Second, the same lump of clay shaped into exactly the same shape twice, once by a sculptor who means it as a statue and once by a toddler who's just squashing it about for fun — do we have a statue both times, one time, or is the question malformed? And the usual ones need to be in there too (the clay that survives squashing while the statue can't, the ship rebuilt plank by plank with the old planks reassembled elsewhere, the cat that loses its tail).

Then, since the point of a spec is to see what fails it: tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable, flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied — I have a suspiscion that the demand for constitution facts to be fully grounded in the physical facts is going to fight with the demand that the statue and the lump differ in their modal properties, but tell me if that's wrong — and say which single requirement does the most work in ruling out candidates. If it's cheap, a line or two each on how the usual four fare against the list (constitution as distinct-but-coincident objects, four-dimensionalism, mereological nihilism, and the eliminativist line that says there simply are no statues), but keep the weight on the list itself, that's what I actually want.
