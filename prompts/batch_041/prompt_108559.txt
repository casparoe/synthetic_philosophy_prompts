ok so my reading group is wrapping up two weeks on the paradox of deontology and i want to end the last session with a poem instead of another hour of everyone doing the "you just care about your own clean hands" bit at each other. can you write me a pantoum about it. 5 quatrains, 20 lines, standard pantoum recycling, last line is the first line.

the two lines that keep coming back should be the two moves that collide: the constraint's reason (doing harm is a different kind of thing from letting harm happen, and its you who does it) and that same reason read backwards (if harm matters that much, how are you sitting here while five of it happen). i want the recycling to be the argument, not decoration -- each time a line comes back it should mean a slightly different thing, and i want both recycled lines to turn on the same word being used two ways (i'd pick "worse", but your call), so a reader can watch the pivot happen. by the end the constraint's own justification should be the strongest thing against it, and the return to line one should feel like being stuck in the loop, not like a resolution.

dont strawman the reply that agency matters -- half the group thinks that reply is cope and i dont want the poem to take a side on it, or on whether theres a threshold where you may do the one to stop the million. voice: a deontologist arguing with herself at 2am, not a lecture.

then a section called "Notes on the choices", 200-250 words: which reading of the paradox the poem is betting on, where that reading is most likely to be unfair or just wrong, and what would change your mind about it -- what a good deontologist could say that would make you write the loop differently. no citations or reading lists please, i want the poem to stand on its own.
