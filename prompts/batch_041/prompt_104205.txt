I am a philosophy undergraduate in a seminar on action theory, and I am building a study aid for our unit on Kavka's toxin puzzle. The puzzle, as we have been using it, is the case where an eccentric billionaire offers you a million dollars if you intend tonight to drink a harmless-tasting but violently nauseating toxin tomorrow, with the money paid tonight and the toxin available tomorrow, and with the understanding that you may change your mind after the money is paid. Our seminar has spent two weeks disagreeing about whether the offer can be accepted, whether the intention can be rational, and what the case shows about the relation between intending and acting. I want a decision tree that sorts the positions on this question by the commitments that actually separate them, not by labels.

Please write the tree as a JSON object with the following schema. title: a short title for the tree. starting_commitment: the claim the user must accept to enter the tree. nodes: an array of objects, each with id (string), question (string), and branches (an array of objects, each with answer (string), next (string, either a node id or a leaf id), and why_this_splits (string)). leaves: an array of objects, each with id (string), position_label (string), core_claim (string), what_it_denies (string), most_awkward_commitment (string), and explanation_of_later_freedom_to_change_mind (string). unoccupied_combinations: an array of objects, each with combination (string) and diagnosis (string, saying whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). unseparable_pairs: an array of objects, each with leaf_a (string), leaf_b (string), and why_the_tree_cannot_separate_them (string). Return only the JSON object, with no prose outside it.

The tree should begin from a commitment that is weaker than the conclusion, so that the first questions are about whether intending is under voluntary control, whether an intention can be caused by an incentive that does not rationalize the intended action, whether the agent must believe she will act, and whether the reward is for the intention or for the action. Phrase every question as a concrete, first-person or case-based question that a student could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to. Include at least one branch that uses the variant where the billionaire pays only after a brain scan detects the intention, and a branch that uses the variant where you can take a memory-erasing pill after the scan. The leaves should include positions such as: the intention is impossible; the intention is possible but non-rational; the intention is rational as a form of self-manipulation; the puzzle rests on a confusion between intending and predicting; the puzzle shows a two-tier structure of reasons for intending and reasons for acting; the puzzle is dissolved by distinguishing deciding from intending; and at least one position that treats the agent as a temporal series of selves rather than a single continuous agent. Keep the tree to between eight and twelve nodes and at least seven leaves. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate. Do not include citations, reading lists, or names of philosophers; I need the logical geography, not a bibliography. Write in American English.
