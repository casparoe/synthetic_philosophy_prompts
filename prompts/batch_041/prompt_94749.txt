So I need a decision tree / taxonomy of the views on the norm of assertion (or on whether there is one), where every branch is a question and every leaf is a named position.

The angle, because otherwise you'll hand me the canned three-way: don't start the tree with 'knowledge, truth, or belief?' and stop there. I want the earlier and later splits to do the real work:

- first split on whether assertion has a constitutive norm at all — one whole subtree for the views that say no (it's Gricean conversational maxims all the way down; or assertion is a family of speech acts with no single rule; or the only genuine norm is the norm of belief and assertion just inherits it)
- then, for the constitutivists: does the norm govern the act or the belief expressed; is it one norm or a package (permissibility of the act, plus seperate norms for retraction, for challenge, for sincerity); is it constitutive or merely regulative — i.e. can a liar assert, or is the insincere speaker still asserting and merely doing it badly; is it context-sensitive (stakes, what this particular audience needs)
- make sure these leaves show up somewhere: the knowledge norm; the 'you must know that you know' strengthening of it; the truth norm; the belief norm; the adequate-evidence / justification norm; a standing-or-authority norm (you must be the one entitled to speak to this); the pragmatist 'no norm, it's all conversational scoreboard' view; and the 'assertion is a family' view. Add any occupied leaf I've left out.

Phrase every branch as something a student could answer without knowing the labels, and wherever you can make the branch turn on a concrete case rather than an abstraction — the lottery ticket, the meeting time you misread in the email, 'it's raining but I don't believe it's raining', the true belief you got by sheer luck, the low-stakes and high-stakes versions of the same claim, the actor on stage saying 'I arrest you', the sat-nav telling you to turn left. Say which case each branch is really testing.

Two more asks. For each leaf, one sentence on its most awkward commitment, the case it handles worst.
