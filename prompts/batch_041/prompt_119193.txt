I'm a PhD student working on dispositions, and I want a joke I could actually tell at a department party — one that lands for people who know the finks-and-masks literature rather than reading as a generic philosophers-have-weird-vocabulary bit. The machinery the punchline should turn on: a fink strips an object of a disposition at the very moment its trigger arrives, so the trigger arrives and nothing manifests; a mask leaves the disposition intact but blocks the manifestation; a mimic produces the characteristic manifestation in something that never had the disposition at all. Write the joke so the laugh comes from the structure of the case rather than from the jargon, and don't let the joke itself explain the distinction. To keep it from being the obvious joke, build the case so that it is initially ambiguous which category applies — for instance, a mask that is itself removed by the trigger, or a disposition that has never manifested and, as stipulated, cannot — and let the punchline settle it. Avoid the stock fragile-glass-and-sorcerer example if you can find something fresh. Keep it short, and after the punchline add one sentence saying which category is doing the work and why the nearest rival doesn't fit.
