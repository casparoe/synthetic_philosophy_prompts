I am putting together the first unit of a graduate seminar on rationality over time, and before we compare rival accounts I want the standard of adequacy written out: what any theory of diachronic rationality — a theory that says when an agent's preferences and choices at different times are rational — has to deliver. Not the contenders yet; the list of requirements they are competing to satisfy.

Give me the requirements, each with the consideration behind it. Include the cases the theory must classify correctly: an agent with cyclical preferences who can be walked around a cycle of trades at a guaranteed loss; an agent who pays to be bound against her own future choice and later pays again to be released; an agent whose preferences shift so gradually that no single step looks bad while the endpoint does; an agent who foresees her own reversal and endorses it in advance; and a set of bets that can be priced against her all at once rather than sequentially. Include the distinctions the theory cannot collapse: between a change of preference and an inconsistency; between foreseeing one's future choices and failing to; between being exploitable and being irrational; between the rationality of a choice and the rationality of the preference it expresses; between requirements that hold at a time and requirements that hold across times. Include the formal results it must be consistent with: whatever can be established about which preference structures are pumpable, under what assumptions about foresight, and the fact that our best descriptive models treat foreseen reversal — including the discounting pattern in which a fixed delay matters less the further off it is — as ordinary rather than as an error.

I especially want two things. First, which requirements are non-negotiable and which are house rules of a particular camp. Second, whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable: if the demand that the theory be neutral among an agent's time slices conflicts with the demand that it vindicate commitment, or if the demand for action-guidingness conflicts with the demand for descriptive adequacy, show me the conflict rather than smoothing it over. Then, briefly, how resolute choice, the sophisticated and myopic chooser models, and the view that preference change simply falls outside rationality's jurisdiction fare against your list, and name the single requirement that eliminates the most candidates. I need the criteria, not the citations, so skip names and dates. Keep the scorecard to a few lines; the list is what I am after.
