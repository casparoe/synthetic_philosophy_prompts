I've been in a long and only half-productive argument on a game-theory Discord about whether "rationalizable" and "survives iterated elimination of dominated strategies" really name the same procedure, and I've come to think the whole dispute turns on a single unstated choice: whether mixed strategies are allowed to serve as the dominators. I'd like to settle it with one worked example rather than another appeal to a textbook case everyone has already seen, so please construct the example to order. Keep it a finite two-player normal-form game — a 3×3 matrix at most, framed as something mundane like three friends choosing among three venues or three firms choosing among three prices — with small, plausible payoffs rather than arbitrary large integers, since part of the point is that nothing exotic is needed.

Here are the specifications the example must meet. First, at the outset no pure strategy is strictly dominated by any other pure strategy, so that if dominators are restricted to pure strategies the procedure removes nothing at all, at any round. Second, at least one pure strategy is strictly dominated by a genuine, non-degenerate mixture of two others — strict inequalities, no ties — so that the moment mixtures are admitted as dominators, that strategy is eliminated in the very first round; state the weights and verify the inequalities. Third, that same strategy must not be a best response to any mixture over the opponent's strategies, so it appears in no Nash equilibrium, and the game should have a unique equilibrium that both procedures leave standing — the two procedures must therefore disagree about a strategy that actually changes the prediction, not about one that turns out to be irrelevant.

Then do the work: display the matrix, verify each specification against the actual payoffs, run both elimination procedures step by step and show exactly where they part ways, and identify the structural feature of the payoffs doing the real work. Tell me what happens if that feature is removed — say, by nudging a single payoff — and whether the two procedures then agree. Finally, give the strongest objection a defender of the pure-dominance-only view could raise against your example, and say what would change if the elimination were run with weak rather than strict dominance. This is for a short note I want to circulate to the group, so I care about the demonstration being airtight more than about the scenario being colorful. Open with the game itself — the matrix and a one-line framing — with no preamble, then give the verification.
