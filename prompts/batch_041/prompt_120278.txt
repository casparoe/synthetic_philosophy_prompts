Something thats been bugging me and I want you to help me think it through properly.

Suppose there are infinitely many people — infinite universe, or a world that just goes on forever, doesn't matter which. Now take a world W where every person's life is worth living, and a world W+ that's exactly the same except one extra happy person exists in it, or everyone's life is slightly better, whatever you prefer. Total value of each: infinity. Infinity equals infinity. So the totalist seems forced to say W+ is exactly as good as W. Mildly weird, fine. Now the ugly version: in W+, make that one extra person's life as horrific as you like, or in W make one person's life unbearable — total is still infinity, so still exactly as good as before. And if a world contains infinitely many happy lives and infinitely many miserable ones, the "total" is infinity minus infinity, which isn't even a number — so it's not that the totals come out equal, it's that there's no total there to compare at all. (Yes, I know there are different sizes of infinity — if you think countable vs uncountable matters here, say so. I suspect it doesn't rescue anything, but you tell me.)

If that's right, then in an infinite world nothing anyone does makes that world better or worse. Which either means consequentialism goes nihilist the moment the population is infinite, or it means something has gone wrong with the whole idea of ranking worlds by their total. I know there are supposed escape routes — deny that infinite populations are possible, or say comparisons between infinite worlds simply have no fact of the matter; compare something other than totals, like a kind of density, or an ordering that only declares "better" for purely local improvements; keep consequentialism for the finite cases and stay silent about the rest — but I want a verdict, not a menu of options.

So: write an essay on whether this "infinite world, so nothing matters" argument is sound. Lay the argument out as carefully as you can, go through the most promising escapes and say what's actually wrong with each, then commit. Either the argument works — in which case say precisely what we should replace totalism with, and check that the replacement still gets the ordinary finite cases right — or it doesn't, in which case tell me which premise fails and why. No hedging at the end, no "this remains a deep and open problem". And dont bother with citations or who-said-what, I just want the argument.
