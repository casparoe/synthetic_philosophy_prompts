Last spring my housing co-op voted on whether to spend most of its reserve fund on a rooftop solar array. We voted in a fixed order, and I have to admit that by the time my turn came I voted yes largely because the people ahead of me had. That is why an argument two of my neighbors had afterward has stayed with me, and why I would like help adjudicating it rather than taking a side.

R's position: R called the vote a case of irrational herd behavior. He had privately looked at the numbers and thought the array would never pay back, but he voted yes anyway because everyone before him had. His claim is that a person who sets aside his own information in order to copy the group is simply being irrational, and that no further story is needed.

S's position: S disagreed. She said R faced an unavoidable problem: he could not see the others' reasons, only their votes, and if they had reasons he lacked, deferring to them was exactly what his evidence supported. On her view the failure, if there is one, lies in the group's outcome, not in R's individual choice.

R's reply: R answered that this cannot be right, because he knew very well that everyone else might be reasoning the same way he was — copying the person ahead of them — so the votes he was following carried no independent information. If you know that a process produces copying, he said, you should discount the crowd rather than defer to it. (The second voter later admitted she had in fact only been following the first, which he treats as proof.)

S's reply: S said that is the move she cannot accept: knowing the mechanism does not give R access to anyone's private reasons, and it is precisely the fact that later voters copy that makes the early votes worth attending to. Either his point proves too much, she said, or it merely relocates the problem.

R's closer: R then noted that the array has underperformed, which he takes as confirmation that the decision was irrational. S said that is outcome bias: what matters is what it was reasonable to believe at the time.

Please adjudicate this move by move — who is right about which specific point — and tell me whether either of them is missing something that would dissolve the disagreement.
