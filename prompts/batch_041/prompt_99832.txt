So I've been chewing on this ethical view for a few months now, ever since a stretch where I was dealing with some pretty rough chronic pain and it made me notice how lopsided my intuitions actually are about suffering versus enjoyment. Here's where I've landed, roughly. Call it lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. The idea is that there's a threshold of intensity for suffering -- not some fixed number in a hedonic currency, just the point where an experience becomes genuinely bad in a way that isn't merely the mirror image of a good one. Any amount of suffering at or above that threshold trumps any amount of pleasure or preference-satisfaction whatsoever, no matter how big the pile; it's lexical, like how no finite run of 0s before a 1 outranks that 1 in a lexographic ordering. Below the threshold, though, the ordinary commensurability story holds -- small aches, mild annoyances, everyday pleasures, these all trade off against each other in a pretty unremarkable way, and it's fine to eat a bit of them for a bit of good. So the view is a hybrid: one big lexical break at the threshold, garden-variety weighing everywhere else.

What I want from you is the case against it. And I don't mean "negative utilitarianism is weird" -- I already know it's weird and I'm at peace with weird. I mean: what would actually count against this specific hybrid? Which intuitions, thought experiments, or formal results should move me, and how much? Sort them by how decisive they'd be, and be honest about which ones I could shrug off versus which would genuinely force me to revise the view. In particular I'm stuck on whether the lexical break is even coherent once you let the threshold itself be vague -- if there's a fuzzy zone where it's indeterminate whether some suffering is at the threshold, doesn't the lexical dominance get smeared out into ordinary weighing, or does the vagueness just mean the dominance is itself vague, and is that fine or fatal? And does anything in decision theory about lexicographic preferences, non-Archimedean utility, or the impossibility of representing such preferences with a real-valued function make trouble here? I'd also like to know what a defender of the view would themselves have to concede as counting against it, not just what a hostile critic would throw at it. Finish by telling me whether the thing, after all the patching, risks anything at all -- because if I've qualified it into unfalsifiability I want to hear that, and whether that's actually a problem for a view like this or just the price of the game.
