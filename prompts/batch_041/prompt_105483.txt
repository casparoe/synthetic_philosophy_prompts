I don't have any formal training in philosophy — I run a small online community for people who build things in a sandbox building game, and I've spent the better part of a year moderating disputes that keep collapsing into the same argument. Not long ago a member's save file was corrupted and she lost a town she'd been building for four years; another member, meaning to be kind, told her that since nothing physical had been destroyed she hadn't really lost anything, and that what she was feeling was grief about a fiction. She was, understandably, furious, and the thread ran for weeks. Out of that mess I've tried to state the thing I believe as a single principle, which I've been calling substrate indifference: if two losses are alike in every respect except that one concerns a virtual object and the other a physical one — the same desires frustrated, the same memories and habits disrupted, the same hours of work destroyed, the same felt sense of having lost something — then they are equally real harms, and the fact that one object exists only virtually is no reason to discount the loss. My own credence in that principle is about 0.65. The friend I argue with most, who is otherwise quite sympathetic to the gamer's side of these fights, puts hers at roughly 0.15, on the grounds that a harm has to leave a mark on the shared physical world before it counts as real. I'd like your number, but mainly I want the reasoning broken out.

Please give me a credence for substrate indifference and decompose it into the sub-questions it actually rests on, with a rough probability for each and one line of justification apiece. The ones I've come up with are: (1) what a harm is — whether it's constituted by a change in a person's experience and desires, or whether it requires a change in the physical states of the world; (2) whether the value of an object is response-dependent, valuable because someone values it, or whether physical instantiation contributes value on its own; (3) whether a loss in a virtual world really does produce the same psychological states as its physical analogue, or whether there's an ineliminable as-if quality to our attitudes toward virtual things that quietly makes the states different after all; (4) whether the whole dispute is verbal — whether "real" is simply being used as a synonym for "physical," in which case there may be no fact of the matter to estimate; and (5) whether accepting the principle commits me to consequences I'd reject, such as putting grief over a deleted save file on a par with grief over a lost photograph, or generating obligations toward characters in a game. Add, drop, or split these as you think the question actually requires. Then tell me which term my number is most sensitive to, and which single term, if you moved it, would be enough to flip my friend from 0.15 to my side — or whether she and I most likely disagree not about any of these but about something upstream of all of them. Finally, say what argument or piece of evidence would move your own number the most.

One more thing: if the way I've set this up is confused — if substrate indifference isn't really one proposition but several glued together, or if it turns out to be trivially true once you spell out "alike in every respect," or if my confidence and my friend's are quietly answering different questions — please say so plainly rather than handing me a number. I would much rather learn that the question is malformed than receive a tidy decomposition of a malformed question.
