I sit on the assurance panel for a family of autonomous clinical-triage systems, and one sentence has become a conversation-stopper in our reviews: 'the buck stops with the human in the loop'. It gets produced whenever someone asks who answers for a bad recommendation — the suggestion being that because a clinician must authorise any action before it is taken, the question of accountability is settled and everyone upstream of the clinician, from the model's developers to the deploying trust, is off the hook. I am fairly sure the sentence is doing more work than it can bear, but I cannot say exactly what it is claiming.

Could you lay out, as a menu I can take into the next review, the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making — ordered from the trivial to the radical, with a verdict on each? I want to know which readings are near-tautologies that nobody would deny, which are substantive and defensible, which are straightforwardly false, and which reading my colleagues most probably have in mind when they use the phrase to close a discussion. Please separate the readings along the distinctions that actually matter here: causal responsibility versus moral responsibility; blameworthiness versus answerability, in the sense of being obliged to give an account, versus liability, in the sense of being obliged to make good; backward-looking fault versus forward-looking role duty; and responsibility borne directly versus responsibility traced back through earlier decisions about design, procurement, deployment or monitoring. Two further questions should shape the menu. First, is any reading of the phrase consistent with the human's having no realistic alternative but to approve whatever the system proposes — with the sign-off being, in practice, a rubber stamp produced under automation bias rather than an exercise of control? Second, is there a reading on which the phrase accurately names a genuine residual gap in accountability rather than closing one? I would like you to state explicitly which reading would have to be true for the sentence to do the work it is doing in those meetings, and whether that reading survives scrutiny. Please write in British English.
