my friend Dana has been telling anyone who'll listen for about twenty years that the valley's aquifer is going to run dry and everyone here will have to move. She has an actual model of it — withdrawal grows faster than recharge, she can do the arithmetic on the back of an envelope — and she's been giving dates. 2003, then 2009, then 2014, then 2019. None of them happened. Each time she has a reason: the meter upgrade bought us five years, the wet winter bought us three, the almond operation going under bought us a bit more. And some of those she called in advance — she said the north field wells would drop first, they did, she said the county would start metering, they did. She's not a crank about anything else. But the recharge rate, which is the number the whole thing hinges on, is also the one number nobody around here has ever measured properly, so any miss can always turn out to be that number being off.

Now there's a dry spell, the reservoir is low, and she says see, it's starting, sell the house, get out while you can. I asked what would have to happen for her to say she'd been wrong and she said if the recharge turned out to be double what she assumed she'd drop the whole thing — but we won't know that for years. Which is a strange thing to hear from someone telling me to sell.

I have to decide by the end of next month whether to sell. Is she being reasonable? Is "the mechanism is right, I was just early" a real defense or is that just moving the goalposts? I keep feeling like the honest answer is that she's been wrong five times, and then I remember the north field wells. And even if I decided her model was probably right about the long run, is that the same question as whether I should act on it now, or is that a seperate thing? I cant tell if I'm the stubborn one here.
