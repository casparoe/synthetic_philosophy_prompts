Bit of background first: I retired a couple years back after ~30 yrs as a field ecologist (long-term population studies, lots of mark-recapture work), and I've spent the retirement reading philosophy — epistemology mostly, a little phil of science. So I'm a tourist, be gentle with me, but I want the real thing.

Here's the itch. Everyone trots out the distinction between the "inside view" and the "outside view" — inside, you reason about the particulars of the case in front of you; outside, you treat it as one draw from a reference class and go with the base rate. Planning fallacy, that whole litany. And the takeaway is always "use the outside view." But I've definately seen this used badly in my own field for decades, and I've slowly come round to thinking nobody has actually written down what an adequate account of this distinction would even have to deliver. So write me that — the desiderata. The full spec for what a theory of the inside/outside distinction has to do to be worth anything as a discipline against overconfidence.

What I want in the list, each requirement with the case or consideration behind it:

- it has to work on single, unrepeatable cases, not just ensembles. My grant, my one restoration project, my one shot — those are the decisions where overconfidence actually bites, and you only get n=1.
- it has to say how you pick the reference class, because every case belongs to infinitely many of them (my study is one of "all ecology grants," "all grants," "all projects by sixty-something men with tenure," ...) and the base rate swings wildly with the choice.
- it has to tell me when the outside view loses. Sometimes the mechanism really is knowable and the base rate is just pooling me with a crowd I'm not in.
- it can't collapse into "always defer to the base rate" (that's fatalism with a spreadsheet) or into "trust your model" (that's the disease itself).
- and this is the one that nags me: picking the reference class looks like it's itself an inside-view judgment. So the outside view may never be the independent check people advertise it as. I want that squared with the whole project, or I want it admitted as fatal.

Then the part I care about most: which of these can't be had at once. I suspect a couple are in tension — name the pair, say why they pull apart, and tell me which one you'd throw overboard and why. Commit. Don't hand me a menu of options and leave me to pick, give me your call. And a last line or two on which single requirement does the most work — the one that, if you drop it, makes the whole thing worthless — and which of the usual framings fails that requirement worst.

No reading list, no citations please, I'm not after the literature, I want the spec.
