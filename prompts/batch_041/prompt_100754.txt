I'm giving a paper at our department's formal epistemology workshop in a few weeks and I'm trying to put together my Q&A prep, so I'd like your help. The paper defends Jeffrey conditionalization for updating on uncertain evidence, but with a twist I haven't seen anyone else push: I argue that the famous order-dependence worry (that feeding your uncertain observations in as soft evidence can land you in different posteriors depending on what order you feed them in) isn't a bug to be patched with extra conditions but is actually telling us something right — namely that the sequence of experiences is itself part of what you've learned. So my proposal is to build the history of observations explicitly into the state space as a variable you conditionalize on, and then show that on the enlarged space the update does commute, and that the rigidity assumption (that your conditional probabilities on the evidence partition don't shift when the evidence arrives) falls out as a theorem about the enlarged space rather than being a brute stipulation. The bit I'm least sure about is whether I'm just relabeling the problem instead of solving it, but lets set that aside for now.

The audience is what worries me. What I want from you is a prep sheet, not a defense: the objections this specific room will actually raise, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would phrase it (so the Bayesians in their vocabulary, the accuracy people in theirs), with a short two-minute reply for each and an honest note on where the reply is soft.
