Next month I'm doing a long-form interview for my ideas newsletter with a philosopher whose whole career has been spent arguing that desert is always relative to a practice — that theres no such thing as someone deserving something prior to the rules of some institution or game being in place. I half buy it and half dont, and I want the conversation to actually go somewhere instead of her just restating the thesis. Can you draft me eight to ten interview questions?

I want at least one question on whether her view can criticize the rules themselves rather than just apply them, at least one on the effort regress (if my effort comes from a character I didnt choose, whats left of desert), at least one on the empirical side of meritocratic belief, and one closer on what could actually change her mind. For each of the main questions, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.

Some things I'd like worked in somewhere:

- the distinction between what someone deserves and what they're merely worth to others — I think that gap is where her view either gets interesting or falls apart
- a concrete case I plan to put to her: a firm that now measures "merit" with a peer-review score, where everyone has learned to game the score. What exactly is the desert base when the metric is the moving target? Does anyone deserve the bonus, or only the rating?
- the worry that "desert is institution-relative" is either trivially true or is just desert-abolitionism wearing a lab coat — and if it's the latter, whether that's a problem for her
- whether the word meritocracy was ever meant as a compliment or always as a warning, and what that does to people who now use it as a straight-faced ideal
- the standard objection that if desert is only ever internal to a practice, she loses the resources to say a whole practice is unjust — and what she says back

Keep them concrete and a bit adversarial without being gotcha-y. No "what drew you to philosophy" filler, and nothing that depends on her having read a specific book or on me citing one — she should be able to answer everything straight from the view itself.
