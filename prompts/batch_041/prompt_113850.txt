For thirty-five years I classified things for a living — insects, mostly — and I have lately retired into philosophy, where I have been working through the exchange between Hart and Fuller from 1958 and the books that grew out of it. What I want is the thing my old trade would have handed me on the first day: a dichotomous key. Please write me a decision tree that carries a reader from a handful of commitments about law and morality to a named position in this dispute, in the manner of a field guide's key, with the diagnostic characters spelled out at each node. Aim for roughly ten nodes and no more than eight or nine leaves.

Two points of method. First, build the key out of the logical space rather than out of a list of names: begin by identifying the small number of independent questions on which these positions genuinely disagree, let the combinations of answers generate the cells, and only then place the actual positions in them. If the questions are not all dichotomous, say so and give me the matrix rather than pretending it is a key. Second, every node must be a question I can answer from the description given in the node itself — a concrete case, a claim, a verdict — and never a question that presupposes a label. Nothing of the form: do you think law and morality are conceptually connected? I should be able to work the key through without knowing any of the names in advance.

The positions I need to be able to land on: Hart's own positivism; the later, softer positivism on which a rule of recognition may itself incorporate moral criteria; the exclusive positivism associated with Raz, on which every law is source-based; Fuller's inner morality of law; the classical natural law view that treats unjust law as law only in a watered-down or focal sense; the Radbruch-style non-positivism on which gross injustice can defeat validity, along with its contemporary successors; Dworkin's interpretivism; and, as a leaf, the deflationary view that the dispute is at bottom terminological, the moral question being the real one and the question about the word being idle. Give each leaf one sentence naming its most awkward commitment and one naming the case that would embarrass it most.

Please hold apart, as separate diagnostic characters, the places where the alleged connection between law and morality can be located: in the validity of individual norms, in the existence of a legal system, in the analysis of the concept, in the reasoning of adjudicators, or in the moral obligation to obey. I care about the difference between the claim that moral merit is a condition of validity and the weaker claim that any legal system that lasts must contain a certain minimum content, and about the difference between formal or procedural constraints on law-making and constraints on what law may say. Whether the concept of law is descriptive or interpretive, and whether law necessarily claims moral authority, should appear as characters on the tree rather than be assumed.

These cases must appear as nodes, at least: the post-war West German prosecution of the woman who denounced her husband to the authorities in 1944 and was later tried for unlawful deprivation of liberty; the American fugitive slave statutes of 1850 and the courts that enforced them; a statute enacted with perfect procedural regularity whose content is monstrous; a system whose rules are unpublished, retroactive, contradictory, or impossible to comply with; a decent legal system containing a single monstrous rule, set against a monstrous system containing mostly ordinary rules; and a judge in a thoroughly wicked regime deciding a routine commercial dispute. Describe each in a sentence or two, so that I can answer the node from the page in front of me.

Finally, tell me where the key fails. Some of these positions may be cryptic with respect to one another — indistinguishable on every character in the tree — and I would far rather see that stated than papered over. For the cells of the matrix that no one occupies, tell me whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

No citations or reading lists, please. I want the instrument, not a bibliography.
