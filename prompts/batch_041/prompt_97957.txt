I chair the deployment-review committee at a regional health insurer. Our claims-triage system assigns each incoming claim to approve, deny, or escalate to a human, and we must now decide how it will be supervised once it goes live. One faction on the committee wants step-level supervision: the system would emit an itemized rationale for every claim and reviewers would grade those intermediate steps, on the theory that catching defective reasoning early prevents defective decisions. The other faction wants outcome-only supervision: let the system decide, then audit a sample of decisions against verified results, on the theory that only results matter and that grading steps mainly teaches the system to produce rationales that sound persuasive. Two findings complicate the choice. An internal probe established that the system's printed rationales are generated after the decision and frequently do not reflect the features that actually drove it. And the verified outcomes we would audit against arrive, on average, eleven weeks late and are themselves disputed in roughly a fifth of cases.

Please draft the committee's decision memo: recommend one supervision regime, or a defined hybrid with the boundary between them made explicit, and specify the rollout and review timeline, the measure by which we will judge the regime a success or failure, and the single assumption that would have to hold for the option I am rejecting to be the better choice. Give the strongest version of the case against the recommendation, since two members will dissent and I want their objection stated fairly enough that they can sign it.
