So I've been reading about norms of assertion and I get the standard fight -- truth vs. belief vs. knowledge vs. justification -- but there's a different axis I cant get my head around. When someone says the norm of assertion is knowledge, is that meant to be a rule built into the act itself, so that an unwarranted "assertion" isn't a defective assertion but isn't an assertion at all (it's a guess, a bluff, a conjecture, something else)? Or is it a rule the act can violate while staying the same act? And what exactly is being evaluated -- the speaker, the act token, or the move as a contribution to what the conversation is now treating as common ground?

Here's the thing that's actually bugging me. Take assertions with no uptake: I say something true and known into a dead microphone, or to my sleeping roommate, or I write a claim in a private diary, or I silently assert it to myself while brushing my teeth. If the norm is grounded in the practice of giving and asking for reasons -- in someone being able to take it up, challenge it, rely on it, hold me to it -- then it looks like the norm is either suspended or trivially satisfied in all those cases, which feels wrong. If instead it's grounded in belief or in knowledge, the norm seems to apply identically with or without an audience, which makes the whole communicative machinery irrelevant to something that is, on its face, a communicative act. My friend has a distinction she swears by: a license norm (what you're allowed to say) versus a move norm (whether what you said even counts as a move in the game), and she thinks the knowledge norm is doing move-work rather than license-work. I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just a verbal one.

So please explain the conceptual issue that's really at stake in that choice -- what a norm of assertion is a norm of, and what it's a norm for -- and say what actually hangs on getting it right. I don't want a survey of who says what. I want the shape of the problem, the moves available, which move costs you the least, and whether the silent/diary/no-uptake cases are a genuine test or a distraction. And if the answer is that there are two norms and I'm conflating them, tell me what the two are and why the split isn't just a way of dodging the question.
