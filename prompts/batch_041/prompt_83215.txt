I am a doctoral student working mainly on early modern metaphysics, and I have, somewhat impulsively, taken up keeping ants: a small formicarium of Lasius niger sits on my desk, and over the past several months I have watched it rather more than my dissertation deserves. What has unsettled me is how naturally I have slipped into treating the colony as a single agent. It finds the sugar-water feeder by the shorter of two routes, and when I wipe the pheromone trails clean it recovers an efficient path within the hour; when I move the heat lamp to the far end of the formicarium, the whole colony relocates its brood over the next day or so; on cold mornings it is sluggish, and I catch myself saying that it is not awake yet. A colleague who works on social insects tells me this is old news and that such colonies have long been called superorganisms, but the label does not answer the question so much as rename it. I also keep returning to a thought experiment I read years ago, in which an ant colony is not merely colony-like but is a genuine conversational partner — something you can interview, which remembers, holds opinions, and has a temperament, even though no individual ant understands anything at all. Ever since, I have called my colony Aunt Hillary.

This has become more than a curiosity because I am currently in a seminar on Leibniz, and I have been trying to work out what he would have made of Aunt Hillary. What I want from you is a careful reconstruction of his likely verdict, not a modern one, and I suspect the answer would be neither a flat yes nor a flat no but would turn on machinery that people usually forget when they invoke him: the difference between a genuine unity and a mere aggregate, the thought that only simple substances are strictly real, the suggestion that a composite is, strictly speaking, only a being by aggregation, with no substantial unity of its own, and the idea that a body may have a dominant monad around which the rest are arranged. Please predict, as precisely as you can, whether Leibniz would say the colony has a mind, and make explicit which parts of his system are doing the work and where they begin to strain. In particular, I would like you to address the following: whether Aunt Hillary is a substance, an aggregate, or something awkwardly in between; whether his picture of walking around inside a thinking machine and finding only parts pushing against parts settles the matter, since I can literally look inside my colony and see only ants and pheromones; whether the ants' coordination would strike him as genuine communication or as something closer to a pre-established harmony; whether conversational fluency would change his verdict, given the weight he places on perception as against apperception; and whether a case like this would have forced him to revise anything, or whether he would have felt entirely at ease. Where you think he would have been genuinely uncertain, or where the case exceeds his framework altogether, please say so rather than papering over it, and please reason in his own vocabulary rather than giving me a generic panpsychism-versus-emergence answer that could have been given about any composite system whatever.

Finally, since I want to compare your reconstruction against what my seminar-mates come up with, please close with a single line of exactly the form \answer{VERDICT: one-sentence summary}, where VERDICT is one of MIND, NO MIND, or QUALIFIED.
