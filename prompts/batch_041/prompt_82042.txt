I am a reliability engineer by training, and I think habitually in terms of failure modes, stressors and load, injected faults, redundant paths, instrumentation, and the well-known nuisance that a test rig can perturb the very thing it is measuring. I have lately been reading about a puzzle in metaphysics that appears to have the same shape, and I would like you to restate it in the vocabulary I actually use, and then tell me precisely what does not survive the translation.

The puzzle is about what it is for a thing to have a disposition — to be fragile, soluble, or flammable — and about the initially tempting idea that this comes to nothing more than a conditional: the thing would manifest the characteristic behaviour if the triggering conditions were met. Two families of cases are said to defeat that conditional. In the first (sometimes called finkish), the triggering conditions themselves remove or destroy the disposition, so that the conditional is satisfied in some hollow way, or its consequent happens to come out true, even though we are unwilling to say the thing was ever disposed to behave that way. In the second (sometimes called masked), the disposition is genuinely present and the trigger occurs, but something external prevents the manifestation, so the conditional comes out false while the disposition is plainly still there.

Please translate the conditional analysis and these two families of counterexample, as faithfully as you can, into the reliability vocabulary of failure modes, stressors, injected faults, redundant or protective elements, and instrumentation. Set the mapping out explicitly, item by item. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the puzzle map cleanly onto my notions, which survive only under distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Finally, tell me whether the residue indicates that my framework is missing something real or that the original puzzle was less well-defined than it appeared — and in particular, whether the whole difficulty is merely the familiar fact that an intervention can alter the system it acts upon, or whether there is something here that my framework cannot even state.
