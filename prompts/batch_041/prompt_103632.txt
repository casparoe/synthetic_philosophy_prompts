Yet the passage ends by saying there must be some distinction between Zhou and the butterfly, and calls this the transformation of things. I need help understanding what that final move is doing. Does it cancel the skeptical worry — so the anecdote is not really a dream-skepticism argument — or does it deepen it? What does the transformation of things mean here, and is it a technical term? I'd like the competing readings, with the considerations for and against each, and a note on whether the anecdote's placement at the end of a chapter full of puzzles about perspective and the relativity of judgments should change how I read it.
