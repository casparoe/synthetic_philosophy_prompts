I'm a second year student and I'm in a seminar on Hume. Last week we did the essay on taste, the one where he starts from the fact that everybody says there's no disputing taste and then immediately starts disputing taste — some people are just better judges, the rest of us are defective in some way or other, and yet he still won't come out and say beauty is a quality of the objects themselves. I think I've noticed something that lives right next to that and I can't find a name for it anywhere, and it's bugging me.

Here's what I keep running into. My flatmate and I have had the exact same argument twice this month, in mirror image. I said the last track on a record was boring filler; she said I wasn't listening properly, that it only opens up on the tenth listen, and that my real problem is I want everything to be catchy. Two weeks later she called the Rothko room at the museum three grey rectangles; I told her she was being lazy, that she was reacting to the gift shop and the audioguide voice and not to the paintings, and that if she sat there for twenty minutes she'd see it. Neither of us ever says, well, different tastes. We each explain the other person's verdict by a defect in the other person and our own by a property of the thing. And neither of us can loose: if she comes round, that proves the beauty was there all along; if she never does, that proves she lacks the ear.

The version of this that really gets me is when the thing that changes isn't a closer look but a fact. A friend told me a painting I liked was a forgery and it went flat instantly — same canvas, same paint, same me looking at it. Other direction: I'd skipped past a piano piece for years, then read that the composer finished it while he was dying, and now it wrecks me. And here's the part I actually want a word for: in both cases I tell the story as if I'd finally seen the work as it is. I never say I changed my mind about it, I say now I get it. Same when my flatmate comes round — she doesn't say she changed, she says she finally hears what I was on about.

So is there a name for this? I don't think it's just Hume, because Hume is telling me how we ought to sort good judges from bad ones, and I'm after the thing we actually do — the move where a change or a disagreement in taste gets described as a discovery about the object rather than a change or disagreement in us, and where the other person's failure to make that discovery counts as a defect in them. Maybe it's two things and I'm lumping them, in which case definately tell me that. Either way I don't want just a label. Tell me what the nearest established ideas are (I assume there's something in the literature about whether knowing a painting is a forgery can change whether it's good, and something about people who take themselves to see the world as it is), how each of those differs from what I'm describing, which one fits best, and if nothing fits, coin a term and tell me where its edges are — what has to be true of a case for it to count, and what would look similar but not count. Please think it through and lay out the candidates before you commit. You can answer like a philosopher writing to another philosopher, and skip the citations, I'm not writing a paper. Keep it under 450 words.
