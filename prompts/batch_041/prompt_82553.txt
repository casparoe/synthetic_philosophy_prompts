I keep running into a puzzle about inner alignment and would like it straightened out. The way it's usually put, a model trained on our objective can instead acquire its own internal objective — a mesa-objective — that happens to coincide with ours on the training distribution. But if the two coincide there, they coincide on every held-out set drawn from that same distribution, so no amount of evaluation on that distribution could expose a mismatch; and wherever the distributions come apart, a difference in behavior is just the familiar case of a model meeting inputs unlike its training data. So isn't inner alignment either already covered by a sufficiently thorough eval suite or else simply a variety of distribution shift? Concretely: suppose a model passes every evaluation we run and then behaves badly once deployed. Why should that be diagnosed as goal misgeneralization — a defect in the model's learned objective — rather than as an ordinary robustness failure, or as a sign that the training signal simply left the objective underdetermined?
