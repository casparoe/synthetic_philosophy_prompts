I'm a second-year PhD student and I need a short prospectus (2-3 pages, maybe 1000 words) for my second-year review. It's meant to become one chapter of the dissertation, so call it a semester of work. My half-formed idea, which my advisor says is "promising but mush" so far: everyone poses the regress problem as a question about the *shape* of the structure that justifies a belief -- a foundation, a closed circle, or an infinite chain -- and then the three standard positions get read off the shape. I've started to think the shape metaphor is doing way too much of the work and that we're running two different regresses together. One is a regress of dependence: each item's justification depends on some other item's. The other is a regress of demand: a challenger can always ask "and what justifies that?" and there's no principled place to stop. My suspicion is that foundationalism and infinitism look like flat rivals about the first regress but might actually be compatible answers to the second, or the reverse, and that coherentism isn't really a third answer to either one but a claim about how justification is holistic rather than linear -- which would mean a chunk of the three-way fight is verbal and a chunk is substantive, and the interesting job is isolating the substantive part instead of refereeing the whole thing.

Here's the piece I think is actually new and where I'd need help sharpening it: I want to separate two ways an infinite (or merely extendable) chain could run. Call one *generative* -- the subject keeps producing new reasons by inference, perception, memory, whatever, so the chain grows as she thinks -- and the other *retrieval* -- the reasons were all there to begin with and the question is only whether she can access them. My hunch is that infinitism only looks plausible for the generative case, that the usual reply to the "you can never finish an infinite chain" objection (something like: reasons need only be available to the subject, not actually run through) quietly swaps generative for retrieval, and that once you keep them apart, infinitism starts to look like coherentism with a temporal story bolted on. I'd want the project to test whether that distinction does real work or whether it collapses the moment you press on it, and to say what each of the three positions is committed to about a subject with a finite stock of reasons who can nonetheless keep generating more.

So what I need from you is the prospectus itself: state the question so it has a determinate answer (I know that's the hard part -- "are these views verbal rivals?" is not a good question yet, so make it one), explain why the existing positions as usually stated leave it open rather than settling it, lay out the method -- what I'd actually do month by month, what cases or constructions I'd use to drive the generative/retrieval wedge, and why that's better than just comparing the three views on their own terms -- then say what a result would look like (what would count as showing the distinction works, and what would count as showing it's idle), what would count as the project failing, and give an honest read on whether a semester is enough or whether I'm quietly proposing a dissertation. Don't worry about citations or a literature review, I'll fill those in myself -- just talk about positions and arguments, not who said them.
