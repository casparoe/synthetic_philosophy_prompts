Retired physicist here — forty years on semiconductor materials — and since I packed it in I've been reading philosophy of mind, which I've definately gotten more out of than I expected, and I've ended up about 60% a panpsychist. I'm going to a seminar dinner next month and I want a joke to tell there. Not 'tee hee, my kettle might be conscious' — I want the punchline to turn on the actual content, so that a panpsychist laughs and a philosopher doesn't wince.

The thing I want it to land on is what nearly talked me out of the view. Panpsychism says experience is fundamental, so each electron has some sliver of it, and the ethics is then basically a counting problem — how many subjects, how much suffering per run of the collider. But electrons are indistinguishable. Swap two of them and you haven't changed the state at all — not 'can't tell them apart', but the formalism genuinely doesn't keep score about which is which. So 'each electron has its own sliver' has no fact of the matter underneath it, and the head count the ethics needs can't be issued. (I know electrons aren't really the bottom floor — it's fields, and 'particle' is loose talk. If the joke works better down at that level, go for it.)

Can you write that one? Under a minute to tell, and don't make it a cheap shot at panpsychists, since I'm one.
