I am the staff economist for the Office of Shared Assets in a mid-sized city. Our office reviews proposals for managing resources that multiple city departments, neighborhoods, or private partners share: community gardens, municipal broadband, a shared vehicle fleet, public datasets, cooling centers, and the like. We see roughly thirty such proposals a year, and the stakes run from a few thousand dollars in annual maintenance to multi-million-dollar capital commitments. The decisions are made in a two-hour meeting by program officers who have no formal training in game theory, public finance, or institutional economics. I need a reusable procedure they can run on a proposal, with a one-page worksheet, grounded in the theory of common-pool resources and public goods rather than in whatever analogy happens to be in the room.

The procedure should use four diagnostic axes that I have found more useful than the standard two-by-two of excludability and rivalry. First, subtractability of units versus congestion of the flow: does one person's use remove a unit from others, or does it mainly degrade the quality of the experience for others even if no unit is removed? Second, provision externalities versus appropriation externalities: who bears the costs or benefits of creating and maintaining the resource, and who bears the costs or benefits that one user's taking imposes on others? Third, aggregation technology: whether the good is produced by summation, weakest link, best shot, or a weighted sum. Fourth, nestedness: whether the relevant governance unit is the neighborhood, the department, the city, or a regional body. Treat these as the axes the procedure must assign values on, and explain any axis that does not apply to a given proposal. Translate each axis into a plain-language question a program officer can answer.

Write the procedure. It should give the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, where it routes to a different governance form such as a fee, a club-good arrangement, a contract, a regulation, or a polycentric body, meaning a nested set of overlapping authorities, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Keep it to no more than nine steps. It must be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure. Do not give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I need the procedure itself.

Then run the procedure on three proposals. First, a neighborhood group proposes converting a vacant city lot into a community garden with a locked gate and a waitlist. The group will maintain it, and the city will pay for water and insurance. The lot is currently open and used informally by dog walkers and by people who sleep there. Second, the chief information officer proposes a city open data portal. Each department would voluntarily publish datasets. The portal's usefulness depends heavily on the least-contributing department: if one large department withholds its data, the portal is much less valuable. The city wants no mandate and no new funding. Third, the parks department proposes a two-dollar entry fee for a popular downtown plaza to fund maintenance and security. The plaza is currently free, crowded at lunchtime, and used by office workers, tourists, and unhoused residents. The mayor has publicly supported the fee.

For each case, give a definite recommendation and a confidence level, and say what would change your mind. At least one recommendation should be one I will not like; do not soften it to spare the office. If the procedure cannot settle a case, say so and identify the value judgment that must be made by the city council or the city attorney. I want a committed response: make definite choices, give your reasons, and do not present every option as equally defensible. Where you are uncertain, give a number and the evidence that would move you.

After the procedure and the cases, give a structured summary as a JSON object with these keys. procedure_steps is an array of objects, each with step, an integer, plain_language_question, a string, sufficient_answer, a string, if_no, a string, if_yes, a string, judgment_call, a boolean, and escalation, a string or null. worked_cases is an array of objects, each with case, a string, resource_diagnosis, an object, recommendation, a string, confidence_0_to_1, a number between 0 and 1, would_change_if, a string, and asker_may_dislike, a boolean. resource_diagnosis has subtractability, congestion, provision_externality, appropriation_externality, aggregation_technology, and nestedness, each a string. value_judgment_points and procedure_limits are arrays of strings. bottom_line is a string. In the bottom_line, state the one thing the procedure cannot do and the one case where it is most likely to misfire.
