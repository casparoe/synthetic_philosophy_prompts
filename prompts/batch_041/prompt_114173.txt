ok so I'm drafting a chapter on how conventions get replaced, and I want to use Sweden's H-day as the case, but I keep going back and forth on whether Lewis is the right frame or whether I'm just using him as a foil. Can you do the analysis properly and then tell me where it actually breaks?

Facts so you don't have to guess at them: Sweden drove on the left until 3 September 1967, even though Norway, Denmark and Finland all drove on the right and something like 5 million vehicles a year crossed the borders. In a 1955 referendum about 83% voted to keep driving on the left. The Riksdag approved the switch anyway in 1963, and after a multi-year publicity campaign the country changed over at 5am on a Sunday: roads cleared, cars stopped at 4:50, moved to the other side, stopped again, then off at 5:00. Fatalities dropped sharply for a while, then the accident rates were back to pre-switch levels by 1969. Trains and the Stockholm metro never switched and still run on the left.

Set it up as a Lewis convention first: who the agents are, what the coordination problem is, whether the old left-hand rule met his conditions including the one about there being another regularity that would have served just as well, and whether the new rule is a convention or something else given that a parliament installed it and a large chunk of the population had voted against it.

Then the strains. Does the arbitrariness condition hold for the old rule at all, once the border traffic is in play — is the relevant game two drivers meeting on a road, or an n-player game with a network externality across the border, and does the answer change whether the left-hand rule was ever a convention? What do we do with a state that can manufacture common knowledge on a national scale, which looks like it's doing the entire explanatory work and isn't really in Lewis? If everyone follows a rule and expects everyone else to follow it, but it's also backed by law, is it still a convention? And the trains: same country, comparable coordination problem, no switch — is that the theory correctly declaring itself out of scope because signals replace mutual expectation, or is it a genuine miss? Also say something about the temporary safety gain and what it implies about whether the content of the new rule mattered or only its salience.
