For a metaphysics course I'm taking, I need to produce a joint statement from an adversarial collaboration between an endurantist and a perdurantist about persistence. The two of them have spent the term arguing past each other, and the assignment is to write the document their collaboration should yield, one that both could actually put their names to.

Please include: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can sign -- about what ordinary objects do over time, about what work a theory of persistence is supposed to do, and about the temporary-intrinsics problem and which responses to it remain live; (2) the residual disagreements stated precisely, each in wording its own side would accept, rather than a caricature of either; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side -- and, for each, the case, argument, or formal result both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and (4) an explicit verdict on how much of the dispute, if any, is merely verbal, with your reasoning.

Given that the deliverable is meant to be signed by both parties, I'd like you to flag where you think the joint formulation is weakest or most likely to be rejected by one side, and to say what would change your mind about which parts of the dispute are substantive.
