I'm a science journalist working on a piece about AI and the philosophy of deliberation, and there is an idealised picture — usually called HCH — that I cannot get past. As I understand it, a question is answered by an agent who splits it into subquestions and hands each to a fresh copy of itself, recursively, until the remaining pieces are small enough to be trivial; the promise being that a mind of fairly limited understanding might, in the limit, settle questions too large for any single mind to hold in view. But every copy is a copy of the same agent, so nothing enters the system that the original did not already contain; and if nothing new enters, the limit can be no better than that one agent thinking harder, with the copies doing the work of a roomful of identical clerks passing the same papers round. On that reading, is HCH anything more than a picture of bureaucracy — and if it is, where precisely does my reasoning go wrong, because I cannot see how an identical mind, multiplied however many times, reaches anything the single mind did not already have within reach?
