I keep running into a pattern at work and I can't tell if it's a known thing or if I'm just describing normal commitee chaos. Here's the setup. Our housing co-op board had to decide whether to fund a roof repair, and the bylaw says the money can only be released if three things are true: the roof is actually failing, the quote fits inside the reserve fund, and no member has filed a formal objection. So we voted on each of the three separately, and each one passed with a clear majority, like 70-30 on all three. Then someone said wait, that means the repair is approved. But when we took a straight up or down vote on the resolution itself, it lost, 60-40 against. Nobody had changed their mind and nobody was being sneaky. The same people who voted yes on each part voted no on the whole, because for each of them there was one part they thought was the weak link, just a different part for different people. Fine, that alone might just be a framing thing. But then we tried to fix it by agreeing in advance to a rule: from now on we vote on the parts and then just derive the outcome. Then a different weird thing happened on the next motion, something about the fence, where the parts passed but the derived outcome was one that most members said in discussion they didn't actually want, and a couple of people said this shows the rule itself is broken. I've also seen something that might be the same shape in a bigger setting. A friend was complaining that her city elected a mayor whose platform had like five planks, and when you poll the city on each plank separately, a majority opposes every single one, yet the mayor won. So my question is, does this have a name? I feel like there's probably a name, maybe two names for the small committee version and the election version, and I want to know which one is the real one and how they're different. I'm not satisfied with just the label, I want the exact shape of it. Like, what are the boundaries: is it the same thing as the Condorcet cycle where you get A beats B, B beats C, C beats A? Is it the same as a group just having incoherent preferences? Does it require the questions to be logically linked, or would any bundle of separate yes/no questions do it? And what's the actual bad thing here, is it that majority rule is inconsistent, or that we asked the question in the wrong order, or that we assumed the group has a single mind that can be consistent in the first place? If there's no exact name for my roof-repair version then tell me that and give me a name and a definition, plus a case that sits right on the edge where you'd be unsure whether it counts. Just write it as plain prose please, no bullet points, no headings, no bold, like you're writing me an email.
