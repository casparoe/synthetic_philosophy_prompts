I am a working software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, currently preparing an application to a career-transition fellowship that supports people moving from industry into AI safety research. The application requires a research proposal of no more than 800 words, and I would like you to draft it. Please write in British English, and please do not include citations, a bibliography or a literature review: refer to positions and arguments descriptively and I will supply the references myself.

My half-formed idea is a proposal on the problem of logical omniscience. In the standard Bayesian picture an agent's credences are defined over a language and closed under its logical consequences: the agent is certain of every logical truth and never revises its beliefs merely because a consequence of them has been pointed out. The systems I build for a living are nothing like that, and the usual responses treat the gap as a defect to be tolerated — add noise, shrink the language, or declare the ideal an unattainable limit. I want to try something else: to treat the agent's own future reasoning as an uncertain event. On this view the agent's credence function ranges over the world and over the outputs of its own later inference, so that it can hold a genuine credence about what it will conclude once it has worked something through, and its present attitude to a proposition is a mixture over the verdicts it expects from its later self. The question I want the proposal to make precise is what coherence demands of an agent like that. My guess is that the interesting version of the constraint indexes the demand to the cost of finding the inconsistency: an agent should not be exploitable by a bookie who is also working under a budget, though it need not be immune to an idealised one.

Please turn this into a proposal that opens with a single sentence stating the question, and then gives: a formulation of the question sharp enough to have a determinate answer; an account of why the existing positions leave it open, described rather than cited; the approach, including what I would actually do month by month across six months of part-time work, given that I can write code but am not trained in philosophical argument; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; an honest assessment of what could make it too ambitious for the time available; and a note on where my framing is most likely to be confused or to be reinventing something that already has a name, since I would rather be told now than in review.
