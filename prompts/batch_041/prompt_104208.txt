I'm a science journalist working on a piece about how the Galileo affair keeps getting recycled in arguments about science and religion, and I keep bumping into a chunk of the Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina (1615) that I think I understand but probably don't. My rough gloss of the core move, in my own words since I don't have the translation in front of me: Galileo grants that scripture cannot err, but says its authors wrote for a general audience and therefore describe natural things in the language of common experience rather than in the language of nature as it actually is -- the way we still say the sun rises. He then borrows from Augustine the thought that when a physical conclusion is demonstrated with certainty, any scriptural text that seems to contradict it must be reinterpreted, since it would be impious to suppose the same God contradicts himself in the book of nature and the book of scripture. And he cites Cardinal Baronio to the effect that the purpose of the Holy Spirit in scripture is to tell us how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go.

What I can't get straight is what this position actually commits him to. Is he claiming scripture has nothing to say about astronomy at all, or only that when it does touch on it, it speaks in accommodated idiom? Those sound close but they pull in opposite directions, and the second one seems to undercut the first: if scripture isn't an authority on the heavens, why does he spend so much of the letter trying to show that a properly read scripture doesn't contradict Copernicus? Also, when he says a text must be reinterpreted, who is supposed to do the reinterpreting, and on what grounds -- is he making a claim about God's intention in composing the text, or just about what an ordinary reader can handle? I have a suspicion that the heaven-and-not-the-heavens line is doing more work than people usually admit and actually cuts against the tidy "science and religion are seperate domains" reading, but I can't articulate why.

Don't give me a both-sides survey; I want your actual verdict, and I want you to flag anywhere I've mangled the argument in my paraphrase.
