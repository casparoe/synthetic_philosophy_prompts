I am planning a one-semester research leave with a single co-author, and I need to decide what we should work on. Our target is the chance-credence relation: the family of principles governing how a rational agent's degrees of belief should answer to objective chance. My co-author is a mathematically fluent epistemologist; I am a philosopher with solid formal training but no research-level probability theory. We can finish one substantial paper, or two short ones, and we can afford to spend the first month reading.

I want a defended agenda, not a survey: a ranking of the open questions, with the criteria you are using stated explicitly and the trade-offs among them made visible. Please work within the following map of the terrain, which is my own way of carving it up, and tell me where the carving is wrong.

Four loci at which a deference-to-chance claim can be located:

1. Credence-deference. A constraint on credences: when your evidence includes the proposition that the chance of A is x, your credence in A should be x, except for whatever contribution is made by evidence that is inadmissible for this purpose. The open work here is (a) characterising inadmissibility without circularity, and (b) determining whether the constraint is a full-blown rational requirement or only a defeasible default that other epistemic considerations can override.

2. Constitutive analysis. The stronger claim that this constraint is not an extra norm laid on top of chance but is partly constitutive of what makes a quantity a chance at all, chance being whatever plays this role in rational credence. The open work is whether anything substantive follows from this, or whether it merely relocates the question into what else must be true of the chance-making facts.

3. Decision-deference. The claim that chance enters rational choice directly rather than through credence: that a chance of x licenses treating A as a fair bet at odds x:(1-x), even where the credence that should guide the choice is something else. The open work is whether this is a distinct principle or a corollary of (1), and how it interacts with the split between evidential and causal reasoning.

4. Social deference. The same schema applied to deference to another agent's opinion rather than to a chance function, with the analogue of admissibility doing the heavy lifting. The open work is whether the chance case is a special case of a general deference principle or a different kind of thing that merely resembles one.

Now the prioritisation. Assume the question is which of these to spend the semester on, with the aim of producing work that others in this area will have to engage with in five years, not merely a competent contribution. I want:

- A ranked ordering of the open questions, including sub-questions I have not listed, with each item's rank defended on the criteria you name. I expect those criteria to include how many other questions in the neighbourhood depend on the answer, how tractable each question is for a team with our particular division of labour, and how thoroughly the existing literature has already covered it.

- Explicit treatment of the undermining problem: on some accounts of chance, the chance hypothesis itself can assign positive probability to a future in which the chance-making facts are different, so that strict deference to the chance hypothesis can require a credence that undermines it. Say whether this forces a self-referential reformulation of the principle, whether anything of the original principle survives that reformulation, and whether this issue is upstream or downstream of the four loci above.

- A judgement about which of the apparent open questions are genuine and which are verbal or terminological, and hence where the semester would be wasted.

- The downstream reach: for each high-ranked question, name the neighbouring debates whose resolution would be affected — self-locating credence, the interpretation of probability in physics, the epistemology of testimony, rational choice under uncertainty — and say which of those connections are load-bearing and which are decorative.

- An explicit statement of what would move an item up or down your ranking: a result, an argument, a counterexample, or a change in what the literature has settled. End with the two developments over the coming year that would most change your ordering.

Do not give me citations or a reading list; I want the reasoning behind the ordering, not the sources. If the honest answer is that the top-ranked question is not on my list at all, say so. Where you think my four-locus map distorts the terrain, re-carve it, but then rank within your own carving.
