Ok so I'm in this philosophy of mind reading group and last week we burned like an hour on sphexishness, which is the name for the thing where a creature looks like it's doing something smart but is really just running a fixed script. The stock example is the digger wasp: she paralyzes a cricket, drags it over to her burrow, and then does this fixed little ritual — she leaves the cricket right at the mouth of the burrow, goes down inside to check that everything's in order, comes back out, and only then hauls the cricket in. Here's the kicker: if you slide the cricket a few inches away while she's down in the burrow, she doesn't just pick it up and carry it in. She drags it all the way back to the mouth of the burrow and starts the whole routine over from the top — back down the hole, check again, come back out. You can do this to her as many times as you like and she re-runs the loop every single time, apparently never registering that she literally just checked the burrow two seconds ago and it was still fine. What bugs everyone is the obvious follow-up: we figure we're not like that because we can step back, notice we're in a loop, and break out of it — but maybe the stepping-back is itself just a higher-level routine, so we're only ever as free as the outermost loop we happen to be stuck in.
