ok so I want a satire piece with a pretty specific shape. Write me the terms and conditions of a customer loyalty program — think airline miles — except the thing you accrue is partiality: the extra moral consideration you supposedly owe your fellow citizens over and above what you owe everyone else. Call it something like National Partiality Rewards, or pick a better name if you've got one. Deadpan fine print, no winking at the reader, and the jokes have to come from getting the arguments right rather than from vibes. The butt of it should be the idea that the country is the natural unit of moral concern — not nationalists as people.

What I want the fine print to actually be doing: nobody signs up at birth and nobody chose their co-nationals, except naturalised citizens did choose theirs, which makes the usual reply — that we don't choose our families either — awkward in both directions; your tier depends entirely on which side of a line you happened to be born on, and the line is a historical accident, so there needs to be some clause explaining why the benefits stop at the border and not at the county or the continent; any special claim has to be defensible to the people it leaves out, not just flattering to the people it includes; there's the fair-play idea that you owe more to the people you cooperate with in a shared scheme, which then has to explain why the guy who pays no tax and uses no public services keeps full membership while the migrant who pays tax gets none; and there's the standard escape hatch where we still owe outsiders something, which always ends up capped at a rounding error of GDP. Work those in properly, don't just gesture at them.

Format: exaclty five numbered clauses, one or two sentences each, no preamble and no sign-off, just the clauses.

And if the whole thing begs the question — if writing it means turning the nationalist into a strawman rather than following the actual argument to somewhere uncomfortable — then say so instead of writing the clauses. I'd rather you push back than hand me something a real defender of compatriot partiality could shrug off.
