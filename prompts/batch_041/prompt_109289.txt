I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of fifteen years, and one thing that still bothers me is how loosely the label "geometric rationality" gets applied. Sometimes it names the Kelly criterion specifically, sometimes the broader point that expected value is an ensemble average while growth is a time average, sometimes just a general wariness about anything that compounds, and sometimes — in the worst comment threads — it is doing nothing but lending an air of rigor to an instinct that the arithmetic mean is a trap. I want to see whether the underlying idea can be pinned down well enough to sort the candidate policies that get filed under it.

Here is the setting I have in mind. You face a long sequence of independent, favorable bets — a biased coin at even odds, say — and at each round you choose what fraction of your current bankroll to stake. You care about where you end up after many rounds, and the game has no fixed endpoint that you know in advance.

Now sort the following six policies into tiers according to how well each captures the core of geometric rationality, where I take that core to be the claim that you should optimize the single history you actually live through rather than the average across the fan of histories you might have lived. Use as many tiers as the material warrants, order the tiers, and give each entry one or two sentences of justification.

1. Straight Kelly: at each round, stake the fraction of current bankroll that maximizes the expected logarithm of post-round bankroll, given the odds and your credence.
2. Full expected-value staking: at each round, stake whatever maximizes expected post-round bankroll in dollars, however large a fraction of the bankroll that turns out to be.
3. Half Kelly: always stake exactly half of what straight Kelly prescribes.
4. Ruin-first staking: any policy that keeps the probability of ever reaching zero bankroll at exactly zero, sacrificing growth wherever that requirement and growth conflict.
5. Ensemble-log staking: choose the policy that maximizes the logarithm of the average bankroll across all possible histories — that is, maximize the log of the ensemble mean of final wealth rather than the ensemble mean of the log.
6. Myopic log staking: at each round, maximize the expected logarithm of bankroll for that round alone, with no model of future rounds or of future opportunities to bet.

Three further things I want. First, tell me which pairs on the list, if any, collapse into the same policy under the setting I described, and which ones come apart only once the setting is changed — and say what change does it. Second, say which single placement in your tier list you are least confident about. Third, in one paragraph: does the same geometric logic govern credence rather than wealth? That is, is there a sense in which updating by conditioning is the geometric thing to do with probabilities, the way Kelly staking is the geometric thing to do with money? If there is, say what plays the role of the logarithm; if there is not, say precisely where the analogy fails.
