ok so for my AP history class we had to pick a thinker and apply them to something happening in our own town, and I picked Nietzsche's essay about history — the one where he says history can be good for life or bad for life depending on how you use it, and he splits it into monumental, antiquarian, and critical. I picked it bc theres a big fight in my town right now about the statue of the old mill owner in the park. He basically built the town, like everyones grandpa worked at that mill, but he also broke a strike in 1911 and the company basically owned everything, the store, the houses, all of it.

So my essay says the whole debate is really three different kinds of history fighting each other. The old guys who want to keep the statue are being antiquarian, they just want the town to stay the way it was. The chamber of commerce people who want to add a bigger plaque about how he built the town from nothing are monumental, they need a hero. And the kids who want to tear it down and put up a sign about the strike are critical, they want to judge the past and be done with it. My thesis is the fight isnt actually about the statue, its about which kind of history the town wants to live inside.

But then I had an idea I couldnt find anywhere so I just made it up. I think Nietzsche missed a fourth one and I call it repair history. Its different from critical bc your not judging the past or trying to kill it off, your digging it up to find the exact thing that broke and putting it back. Like some people in town want to use the old company payroll records to get the strike families their pensions that never got paid. Thats not monumental (no hero), not antiquarian (your not preserving it, your using it), and not critical (your not condemning it, your fixing it). It only works if the injury is still going on in the present.
