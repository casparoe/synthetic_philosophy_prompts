I am drafting a chapter prospectus for a dissertation in political theory and want help turning a half-formed idea into a proposal. The idea is this. Defenses of constitutional monarchy typically say the monarch is neutral or above politics, but I suspect that phrase is doing two jobs that should be pulled apart: impartiality, meaning the office-holder has no stake in the partisan contest, and unaccountability, meaning the office-holder answers to no electorate and can be removed by no vote. An elected ceremonial president can secure the first; only inheritance delivers the second. So the question I want to pose is whether the distinctive contribution of a hereditary monarch to a democracy is precisely their unaccountability, and whether unaccountability can ever be a legitimate basis for authority — or whether the office is legitimate only on the condition that it never acts, in which case its legitimacy is self-undermining the moment it is exercised. Write the proposal: state the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the standard positions (the division of the constitution into dignified and efficient parts, the republican objection to hereditary rule, and the debate over reserve powers and conventions) leave it open; say what the method would be and why that approach rather than the obvious alternative of comparative case studies of, say, the UK, Sweden, or Japan; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a realistic scope for a single chapter of roughly 10,000 words drafted over one semester. Don't bother with citations — I'll add those.
