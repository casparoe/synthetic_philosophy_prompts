Six weeks ago my wife was placed on the transplant list, and in the same month the county fined me under its price-gouging ordinance for selling generators at three times list after the flood — a price I still believe was fair, and which, so far as I can tell, left every buyer better off than the alternative. I have been asked to write an op-ed about both episodes, and before I do I would like to know whether the position I actually hold is coherent. I suspect I have been assembling it case by case, and that at least one of its parts is doing no work while another is doing work I have not admitted to.

Here is the position, as plainly as I can state it.

1. Compensated kidney donation should be legal, and I am prepared to participate as a buyer; I do not think I would be doing something wrong by going abroad to arrange what I cannot arrange here.
2. Disaster pricing should be legal. The seller who raises prices rations a scarce good and draws more of it toward the people who want it most, and no buyer is compelled to transact.
3. Selling votes should remain illegal, and not merely illegal — it is wrong in itself, independently of any statute. My brother-in-law says he would take two hundred dollars for his ballot, and I have not been able to give him an answer that satisfies me.
4. Most commodification objections are distaste dressed up as ethics. The claim that a good is corrupted by having a price does no independent work; where a transaction is genuinely objectionable, it is because someone is coerced or because third parties are harmed.
5. The one exception I can articulate: goods whose value consists in everyone's holding them on equal, non-purchasable terms — a vote, a jury seat, a passport — are destroyed by tiering, and so may not be sold. I will call these standing goods. Ordinary goods and services — an organ, a generator, an hour of labor — are not standing goods and may be priced.
6. Where a policy leaves the worst off better off than they would otherwise be, that counts strongly in its favor, and in the cases above I am inclined to treat it as decisive.

I do not know which of these is load-bearing and which is decoration, and I would rather find out now than in print. Please audit the set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, ordered by severity? Which look like tensions but dissolve on inspection, and why? For each real tension, tell me what would have to give, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the list. Open with the single tension I should worry about most, then the rest in descending order of severity.

Under 700 words.
