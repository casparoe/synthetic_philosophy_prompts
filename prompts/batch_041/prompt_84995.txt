ok so I'm in a reading group on karma and we keep talking past each other, because half the room thinks karma is a mechanism and the other half thinks it's a verdict, and nobody can actually say which one they hold. I want to make a one-page decision tree that takes someone from a starting commitment to a named position on karma as a theory of moral desert. Build it from the questions first and slot the names in after, not the other way round.

Branch nodes I want, in whatever order makes the logic cleanest: (1) is karma a causal law like gravity, or a judgment made by someone or something? (2) if it's a causal law, can desert survive the demotion -- can there be deserved suffering with no one who intended it? (3) does the sufferer have to be the same person who acted, and if yes, what does that do to the no-self picture, where there's a causal stream but no owner of it? (4) is karma retributive (you suffer because you did wrong), educative or instrumental (you suffer in order to be changed), or just a description of consequences with no because in it at all? (5) how long can the ripening be deferred before the claim stops being testable, and does anyone in the tree actually need it to be testable? (6) does karma attach to acts, to intentions, or to whole lives and collective groups?

For the mechanism question, I've been using three pictures that I don't think are standard and I'd like them in there: karma as a ledger (someone keeps score, debts and credits), karma as weather (an impersonal standing condition you live under, no transactions), and karma as an ecosystem (consequences propagate through a causal network and nobody is owed anything by anybody). Have the tree make the reader commit to which of those they actually picture, because I bet alot of the disagreement is just ledger-people and weather-people both saying karma.

Leaves I need: (a) theistic governance, (b) impersonal cosmic fittingness, (c) the no-self causal stream, (d) the position that the desert reading is a later accretion or a social-control story, (e) useful fiction, (f) hard determinism where karma is just causality and desert is a category error. Add whatever else the logic demands, mark the combinations nobody occupies, and say for each empty spot whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. If two leaves come out identical on every node, flag them instead of pretending the tree seperates them.

Keep it to one page, no citations, and don't tell me which leaf is correct -- it's a sorting tool, not an argument.
