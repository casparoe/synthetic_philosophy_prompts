So I've got a sabatical coming up and I want to spend a big chunk of it on the approval-directed vs goal-directed agents distinction — you know, the idea that you'd rather have an agent that tries to do what a human would approve of than one that tries to hit some outcome, plus the suspicion that once the agent has a good enough model of the approver it just turns back into a goal-directed agent whose goal is "make the approver approve." I know the shape of the debate but not where the live questions actually are, and I'd rather not burn the year rederiving things people already worked out.

Two things I want to throw into the pile, because I suspect they're where the juice is. First: "approval" seems to mean at least three different things and I keep catching people sliding between them — approval of the particular act, approval of the policy or rule the agent is following, and approval of what the agent would have done in nearby counterfactual cases it didn't actually face. Those come apart, and I have a hunch the safety story only really works for one of them. Second, there's an obvious regress worry: if the agent defers to the approver's approval, and the approver's approval is itself something that could be better or worse, what stops you from landing on an idealized approver — which is the old ideal-observer theory of value with extra steps. I want to know whether that regress is a genuine problem or just a pun.

What I'm asking for: rank the open questions in this area for someone with roughly a year, a philosophy background, and no lab. Which ones are upstream of the others — in the sense that settling them would make the rest easier or moot — which are actually tractable from an armchair with a bit of math, which are neglected, and which look important but are really the old alignment problem in a new hat. For each, tell me what would move it up or down my list, and where the crux actually is. And be honest about the state of play: tell me which parts are basically settled among people who know the area and which are genuinely contested, and don't sell me a contested view as if it were the concensus — I've been burned by that before.
