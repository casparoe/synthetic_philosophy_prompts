Question. An agent's intertemporal preferences are given by

U = u(c₀) + Σ_{t≥1} βδᵗ u(c_t), with 0 < β < 1 and 0 < δ < 1,

so that each date is discounted by the factor δ relative to the date before it, and in addition the whole of the future is discounted once, by β, relative to today. A second agent's weights are 1/(1 + kt) on date t, with k > 0. A third agent's weights are δᵗ.

(a) [8 marks] We ask each agent, on date 0 and again on date 1, to rank a pair of rewards falling on dates 3 and 4. For which of the three agents can the ranking differ between the two occasions, for some choice of the two reward sizes, and for which is it the same however the sizes are chosen? Justify your answer from the weights.

(b) [8 marks] A recent convert to the economic way of thinking says that weakness of will just is hyperbolic discounting. Assess that claim: what does the discounting apparatus describe, what does it presuppose, and is the agent who takes the smaller-sooner reward acting against her own better judgement at the time she acts?

(c) [4 marks] Would a commitment device — say, a sum the agent forfeits if she takes the sooner reward — work by changing the agent's weights? Explain.

Answer. The first agent has a present bias. Since β is applied to every date after today and not to today, a reward available now escapes the extra discount, so the sooner option is always relatively more attractive when it is the current one. The second agent's weights fall by more over short delays than over long ones — the discount rate is not constant across the interval — and this is the source of preference reversal. Both of these agents will therefore reverse their ranking of two options that both lie in the future: if the agent prefers the date-4 reward while both are distant, she will prefer the date-3 reward once it is nearer, and this is what it is for preferences to be time-inconsistent. The third agent is the time-consistent case: her weights decline geometrically, so her discount rate is the same at every horizon and she never has occasion to revise a plan.

So the claim that weakness of will just is hyperbolic discounting seems to me correct, and it is an attractive reduction: the smoker who resolves at midnight to stop and lights up at nine has the first agent's weights, and the later self, who puts less weight on anything beyond the present, simply acts on the preferences she then has. There is no separate phenomenon of the will left over to explain. If anything the model shows that the akratic agent is straightforwardly irrational: she ranks the later reward above the sooner one and then reverses that ranking, so her preferences are intransitive. A commitment device, by contrast, does not touch her weights at all: a forfeit payable on defection changes what defection costs, which is why it must be entered into before the temptation is close. That also tells us who will buy one: the sophisticated agent, who expects her future self to be as present-biased as she is now, will; the naïf, who assumes her future self will share her present ranking, will not.
