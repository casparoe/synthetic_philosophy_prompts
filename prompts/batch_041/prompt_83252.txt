My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about how a particular war should be remembered, and he finally shut the discussion down by announcing that history is written by the victors, as though that settled what actually happened. I want to be able to take that claim apart. Lay out the distinct precise things that one sentence could be asserting — from the near-truism that the winners control the archives, the schoolbooks, and the monuments, through the epistemic thesis that the losers' account is unrecoverable, to the full relativist claim that there is no historical fact of the matter and that power simply constitutes the past — and for each reading say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or straightforwardly false. For each, also say what sense of 'victors' it requires, since a military winner, a survivor, and whoever happens to control the archive later are not the same party. Bring in the cases that seem to cut against the slogan, given that a defeated cause can end up writing the history that later prevails, and say what those cases show about the stronger readings. Then tell me which of these my friend most likely meant, and whether any of them actually licenses treating a contested question of memory as closed.
