You are an analytic philosopher of religion with a strong background in classical Islamic theology (kalām). I want you to help me stress-test a position rather than defend it. Take the following view seriously and play it straight: there is no created efficient causation — fire does not burn cotton, no natural thing brings about any effect; God is the sole agent, and every event is produced directly by divine power. What we call causal necessity is only the constancy of God's habitual ordering: the connection between the alleged cause and the effect is not intrinsically necessary, and no contradiction would be involved if it failed. I hold roughly this occasionalist position, in the Ashʿarī spirit, and I want to know what would count against it.

Begin by brainstorming, in a few lines, what a view like this is actually committed to, so that the defeaters you give are aimed at the real thing and not a strawman. Then give me the observations, arguments, intuitions, and formal results that should move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them a committed occasionalist would concede as counting against the view and which he would simply dismiss. I am especially interested in the following pressure points, though don't limit yourself to them: whether the move of conceding, for the sake of argument, that the connection might be necessary while still securing miracles leaves the position insulated from any evidence; whether the appeal to divine habit can underwrite induction and the ordinary practice of prediction, and how it differs from a Humean regularity view and from the occasionalism of Malebranche; whether making God the immediate cause of every event implicates God in natural evil and in human wrongdoing, and whether the Ashʿarī notion of kasb (the creature's acquisition of its own act) does any real work here; and whether the position is empirically indistinguishable from its necessitarian rivals, and whether that indistinguishability is a defect. Close with an honest verdict on whether the position risks anything at all, or whether it has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
