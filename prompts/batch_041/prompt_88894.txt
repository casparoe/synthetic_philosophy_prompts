the idea. the usual way of sorting out when a performative goes wrong is the two bins: it can misfire (wrong procedure, wrong person, wrong circumstances, so the act doesn't happen at all) or it can be an abuse (the act happens, but you were insincere, or didn't mean to stick to it, or later act against it). i want to argue there's a third bin, and that it isn't exotic. take a committee chair who never calls a vote. at the end of the discussion she says, casually, so we're agreed then, and moves to the next item. nothing was voted on, nothing was declared, and if you asked her afterwards she'd say she never put anything to a vote. but everyone in the room now takes the thing as decided, and acts on it, and if someone later says but we never actually agreed, they're the one being difficult. i want to call these stealth performatives: acts that only come off if nobody treats them as acts. the words have to be taken up, so uptake is still doing the work, but the uptake has to be non-conscious — if anyone says out loud what's happening, the whole thing collapses and you're back to an ordinary failed attempt at a vote.

why i think this isn't just abuse: abuse is about the speaker's inner state, and my chair can be perfectly sincere — she really does think there's agreement, she isn't trying to smuggle anything past anyone. and it isn't a misfire either, because the act does happen; the agreement is real, people are bound by it. what's odd is that the procedure is missing and the act is still performed, which is supposed to be impossible.

two things i can't decide. one, is the right diagnosis that the procedure isn't really missing — the convention here is just an unofficial one, and unofficial conventions are still conventions, so this is a boring ordinary performative after all and my third bin is empty? two, does the third bin survive if i make the case less friendly — say the chair knows perfectly well there's disagreement and says it anyway to steamroll the room? that looks like abuse again, but it also looks like the same act, so i can't tell if i've found a stable category or just an insincere version of the ordinary one.

i also have a test case i want to use to seperate two versions of the view. version A: the stealth performative works because of a standing convention that we don't make these things explicit (so it's social, like any performative). version B: it works because of a local one-off mutual pretence in that room (so it isn't convention at all, and if a new member joined who didn't know the pretence, the act would fail). my case: a new member joins halfway through and hears the chair say so we're agreed then, and doesn't know it wasn't a real vote. on A the act still goes through. on B it shouldn't, but it seems to anyway, because she'll be bound by it like everyone else. i don't know if that settles anything or if i'm just confusing being bound by something with the thing having actually been done.
