I am a philosopher of biology, and next month I am chairing a two-day adversarial collaboration between a quantitative geneticist, who holds that heritability estimates for complex traits such as educational attainment are causally informative, and a critic, who holds that such estimates are artefacts of one particular distribution of environments and license no inferences about individuals, about interventions, or about differences between groups. Both parties have agreed to work from a single stipulated result: in a cohort where families were randomly assigned to a generous or a modest income supplement from a child's birth to age five, the estimate of heritability for the trait in question came out markedly higher in the generous arm. Please draft the joint statement the two of them could both sign. It should set out the claims they can affirm together — about what a heritability estimate is an estimate of, about what the randomized-income result does and does not show, and about the inferences each of them independently refuses — without collapsing the dispute into a platitude about nature and nurture. It should then state their remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept as fair, and identify the cruxes, separating those that are empirical from those that turn on what "heritability" is being used to mean. For each crux, record the argument, case, or result both sides agree would bear on it, together with each side's prediction about how it would come out; where one party would regard a crux as already settled by the other's own commitments, say so and explain why. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
