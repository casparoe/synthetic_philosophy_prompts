I'm a novelist — three books in, deep in the swamp of a fourth — and there's a thing that keeps happening in my own process that I don't have a word for, and its starting to genuinely bother me. So here's the thing. I write a scene where a character, call her Marta, does something small and slightly off: she cuts her friend off mid-sentence, say, not because I've decided she's rude, just because the scene needed to move. Eighty pages later I need her to do something quietly generous, and it doesn't work, and I sit there for an hour before I see why it doesn't work — she's now a woman who cuts people off, and I have to either lose the interruption or go back and build her a reason for it. But there was no Marta before I wrote that sentence. Nothing in my notes said rude. The rudeness wasn't sitting there waiting to be discovered; it got made, by a sentence I wrote for unrelated reasons, and from that point on it operates as a fact about her — it constrains what I can write, and if I ignore it my readers will say she's out of character, and they'll be right. The thing that's supposed to be the subject of the story turns out to be an output of the story, and then it turns around and acts like an input.

The same shape shows up from the other side. A reader once wrote to me that she knew exactly what Marta would do in a situation I'd never written — and my honest reaction was that she was right, even though there's nothing on the page about it. So there's some fact-of-the-matter about a person who doesn't exist, generated by a text that never states it, and treated by everyone including me as binding. I've got a hunch theres philosophy on this — I have a half-memory of some popular book where the self gets described as a kind of useful fiction, a 'centre' we posit the way physicists posit a centre of gravity, and I know the phrase 'narrative identity' floats around — but neither of those seems to be about what I'm describing, or maybe one of them is and I'm holding it wrong. My partner, who teaches literature, says the word I want is just 'characterisation', which is obviously not it — that's the activity, not the thing I'm pointing at.

So: is there an established name for this, and if the honest answer is that there are two or three neighbouring ideas and none of them is exactly it, I'd much rather have the comparison than a label — what the exact phenomenon is, what it isn't, where its edges are, and which of the neighbours fits best. I'm not a philosopher; I'll be reading this on my phone between drafts. So please be blunt about which parts of this are settled and which are still fought over by people who actually know the literature, and don't smooth a live disagreement into a tidy consensus just because I asked a clean question. If the thing I've described is a muddle, say so.
