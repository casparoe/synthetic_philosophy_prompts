I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for, and I'd like to know whether it already has a name. Here is the shape of it. Two or more parties are competing for something — readers, customers, grants, attention — and the way to win is to make your output harder to check. Not to make it worse, exactly: the output can stay the same quality or even improve. But every increment of opacity pays, because the party that is harder to audit is harder to catch being wrong and harder to hold to a standard, and so it wins the comparison against a competitor whose defects are visible. Each party therefore has an individual reason to reduce its own checkability, and none of them is sacrificing anything or betting on anyone else's restraint. The result, once everyone has done it, is that nobody can tell who is good. The audience that was supposed to reward quality can no longer separate quality from noise, so it stops rewarding it, and the whole comparison becomes a contest of impressions. Two instances. First, academic subfields where the papers that get cited are the ones whose claims are stated so loosely that no result could refute them, while the papers that stake out a crisp, checkable claim get shot down and forgotten. Second, firms in a market where the ones that thrive are those that keep their internals opaque, so a rival with a clean, auditable product looks naive and gets undercut.

I know this is in the neighborhood of a few things I already have names for. It isn't Moloch as I usually see it stated, because there the parties trade away something they would rather keep — safety, leisure, honesty — for competitive position, whereas here they trade away the audience's ability to evaluate them, which is a different kind of loss, and the apparent winner ends up worse off in a way that is hard to write down as a simple cost. It isn't Goodhart either, because Goodhart is about a measure being gamed by the people it measures, and here the measure is not gamed so much as dissolved. It also feels related to the market-for-lemons story, except that there the asymmetry is a fact of nature the sellers suffer from, while here the sellers are actively manufacturing it and racing each other to manufacture more of it.

Does this have an established name? If it does, I want to know exactly how it differs from the nearest concepts: what the necessary conditions are, whether the audience's inability to evaluate is essential or incidental, and whether it takes competition or can happen with a single party. If it doesn't have a name, coin one, define it, and tell me where its edges are. Is a firm that simply keeps trade secrets doing this, or is that a different thing? What about a field where the opacity comes from the subject matter rather than from anyone's choice?
