I want to settle what I actually think about the limits of free speech by working outward from my verdicts on a set of cases, and I'd like you to find the principle, if there is one, that captures them. I think a newspaper may publish a leaked internal memo exposing official misconduct, even though it embarrasses named officials and may cost them their jobs. I think a blog post that supplies a workable synthesis route for a nerve agent, and serves no other purpose, may be suppressed. I think a message to a named person saying "I am going to kill you" may be suppressed, and so may a false viral claim that a named private individual is a child abuser; but a false viral claim that the government is concealing an alien landing may not, and a cruel message telling a named person they are contemptible may not. A speaker who tells an angry crowd that a particular minority is poisoning the wells and that "something must be done" may be silenced when the crowd then attacks; a comedian whose joke a mob later adopts as a slogan may not. And I think a coordinated campaign in which ten thousand people each send one mildly menacing message to the same person may be suppressed as a campaign, even though no single message would be.

The pair I cannot square is this: I think a newspaper may truthfully report that a private citizen was convicted of a serious crime years ago and has since lived blamelessly, yet I lean toward thinking that a viral campaign whose sole aim is to surface that same true fact and ruin them may be suppressed — and I cannot tell whether that is a real distinction or merely distaste for the crowd.

I have a half-formed principle I would like tested and repaired: that speech may be restricted only when it wrongfully attributes a damaging property to an identifiable person, issues a credible threat, or hands over operational means to a serious wrong, and never merely because its content is false or offensive.

Is there a principle that gets all of my verdicts, or close? Please state it precisely, say what it implies for two cases I have not mentioned — a true but humiliating fact about a private person published on a slow news day, and a hyper-realistic fake audio clip of a candidate for office released the night before an election — and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and which I should therefore give up.
