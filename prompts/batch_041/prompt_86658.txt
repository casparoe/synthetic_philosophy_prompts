You are a philosopher of biology who works on the welfare of wild animals, advising a small grantmaking foundation. I chair the foundation's grant committee, and we vote next week on a request to fund a ten-year habitat-restoration and rewilding project on roughly four thousand hectares of degraded farmland, with strong conservation credentials and a species-recovery target.

One trustee has raised an objection I can neither dismiss nor answer. Her argument runs roughly as follows. Most wild animals are r-strategists: insects, rodents, small passerines and the like, which produce enormous numbers of offspring of which the overwhelming majority die young, often by predation, starvation, exposure or parasitism, and plausibly in states worse than never having existed. Restoring habitat raises the carrying capacity for precisely those animals, so a project that looks like a benefit to nature may multiply suffering; on this view habitat destruction could even reduce net suffering. She therefore proposes a standing rule: no grant to any project whose expected effect on wild-animal welfare per hectare is negative, where that calculation counts the expected lifetime welfare of every sentient individual affected, weighted by number and by our confidence that the individual is sentient at all.

Please draft the committee's decision memo: a clear recommendation (fund, decline, or fund with conditions), a time frame for whatever you recommend, the reasoning behind it, and an honest statement of the strongest case against the recommendation. Keep it to about two pages. Begin with a short survey of the considerations pulling in each direction, then say where the per-hectare rule is doing genuine work and where it is an artifact of assumptions we cannot defend. If you conclude that the framing itself is confused or premature — that expected wild-animal welfare cannot be estimated even roughly, or that a grant committee is the wrong body to be doing this — say so plainly and recommend what we should do instead; I would rather have a memo telling me the task as posed is misconceived than one that politely performs it anyway. Mark which of your claims you hold confidently and which are conjectural.
