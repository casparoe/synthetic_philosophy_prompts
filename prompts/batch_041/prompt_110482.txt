my reading group is closing out a semester on tranquilism -- the view that the only thing that matters is the absence of craving and suffering, and that pleasure never really lands in the plus column. i want to start the last session with a short poem, and i'm thinking a villanelle. the voice should be someone who used to be a pretty committed hedonist and has converted, but isnt fully at peace about it. i want the two refrains to be the hedonist's line and the tranquilist's line -- one should keep promising that the pleasure is worth the wanting, the other should keep insisting that only the wanting stopping counts -- and i want the form's compulsive return to feel like the craving the speaker cant shake, so that by the closing quatrain the two refrains meet and you can feel the tranquilist hasnt actually won. it should also touch the uncomfortable edge of the view: if nothing positive ever counts, the tranquilist has a hard time saying why a world with anyone in it beats an empty one. dont resolve that, just let it sit there. accurate enough that someone who knows the literature wouldnt wince, readable for someone who doesnt. american english please.
