Alright, I want a thought experiment about the common prior assumption. Gloss so we're aligned: the CPA says there's one joint measure over the world including the agents' own starting points, and each agent's prior is just that measure conditioned on who they are — their type. Note the thing people mangle: this is not the claim that two agents have the same prior. Two agents can have wildly different priors and still be CPA-consistent, so long as a single joint measure over (world, type) generates both of them by conditioning.

There are two ways to read what that assumption is doing, and I want cases that pull them apart. Reading A: it's a claim about the world — the uncertainty has one source, and both agents are drawing from it. Reading B: it's a claim about rational agents — anyone coherent can be reconstructed as conditioning one shared measure, so a genuine difference in priors is itself a defect. Dont stipulate the verdicts; I want the verdicts to fall out of the cases.

Case one: two people who sincerely believe they share a common prior — same field, same training, each would bet their career that they're working from the same starting point — in an ordinary setting (a diagnostic committee, a betting market, a hiring panel, whatever). They've each told the other everything they know and how they got there, the announcements are public, each believes the other is sincere and competent, and they still disagree, indefinately, without either being stubborn. One paragraph. Then tell me exactly which premise of the usual must-agree argument has to give for this to be possible, and why the disagreement can survive the two of them being completely honest with each other.

Case two: two people whose priors differ alot — enough that Reading B says one of them is broken — who see the same evidence and end up with the same posterior on every event, forever, and would take the same side of every bet. Constraint: the agreement can't come from evidence that settles the question, so make it come out of the numbers. Then tell me whether their situation is even compatible with the CPA.

Finally: for each case, name the one feature that's doing the work, say what happens if I remove it, and say how someone who holds Reading B would attack case one. If case one turns out to be unbuildable without quietly making the two of them differ in some way they don't realize, say so and tell me what that shows. Keep it tight — no belief hierarchies written out in symbols, no omniscient bookie.
