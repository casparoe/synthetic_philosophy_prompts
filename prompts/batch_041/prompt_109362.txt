I run a monthly reading group for grad students (8 people, 90 minutes) and I need an activity for the session on easy knowledge and bootstrapping — the thing where you glance at your fuel gauge, see it reads E, and on that basis come to know both that the tank is empty and that the gauge is reliable, which feels like getting something for nothing. This got annoyingly personal last month when I bought one of those smart scales that estimates body fat; my flatmate kept asking how I know its accurate and I kept saying "well, it gives me the same number every morning," which is the whole problem in one sentence.

I dont want to lecture first. Build the session so the group writes the inference rules themselves and then walks into it: by the end they should be holding a certificate, issued by their own agreed procedure, that some instrument is reliable — where the instrument is in fact a coin. Rules, cheap materials, roles, a timeline, and something real to win. Then a debrief that maps each move onto the pieces of the problem (the reading-belief step, the induction over readings, the later use of the now-certified instrument), says which step people will defend hardest and why that's the interesting one, what predictably goes wrong the first time I run it, and which feature of the bootstrapping problem the activity can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself.

Last line, one sentence, starting "They'll feel it when:" — the exact moment in the run where it bites.
