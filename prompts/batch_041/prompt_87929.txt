I'm putting together a dataset of adversarial-collaboration write-ups and I need you to actually draft this one, not describe it. The two camps who've been argueing past each other for years: a defender of a modest luminosity thesis — call her the Lum — who thinks there's a genuine range of facts about your own present mind such that, whenever they hold, you're in a position to know they hold, and that a restricted KK survives for exactly those facts; and a margin-of-error skeptic — call him the Mar — who thinks that the moment you let mental facts come in degrees and let the agent's powers of discrimination bottom out at some threshold, every luminosity claim and every KK claim dies, and that the "restricted" versions just push the bump under the rug.

To keep both of them off their stock examples, build the whole thing around this apparatus, which I'm stipulating (so don't go hunting for it, it's made up for this exercise):

1. Coarse vs fine mental facts. Coarse = whether you're in a state at all, or which side of a boundary you're on — e.g. "stronger than the one before," yes or no. Fine = the magnitude, on a 0–1000 scale.
2. A self-track condition. Your way of getting the coarse fact right has to still be right in nearby cases where the fine magnitude is off by a notch (let's say 1/1000 of the range). I want a section where they fight about whether that tolerance is principled or just gerrymandered so the Mar wins by construction, and whether swapping it for some other tolerance changes any conclusion.
3. The graded wager. An agent sits through a run of stimuli that get stronger by tiny increments and, after each one, either banks a fixed payoff or bets that her present state is strictly stronger than the previous one. The bet pays only if her confidence cleared a threshold the two of them get to set. Both sides have to say what happens as the increments shrink toward nothing, and whether the Lum's extra self-knowledge ever buys her a policy that beats a merely well-calibrated agent with no special access to her own states.
