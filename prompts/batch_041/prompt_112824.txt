Ok this is a weird one, and I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy training, so bear with me. A while back I read a summary of Lakatos's thing about research programmes and it kind of broke my brain, because it's basically what my last platform team did, except we didn't have the words for it.

Here's what I took from it, so you know what I mean: a research programme has a hard core of commitments you're not allowed to touch, and around it a protective belt of auxiliary assumptions you *are* allowed to keep patching. There's a negative heuristic (don't touch the core, ever) and a positive heuristic (the plan for building the belt out). You call a programme progressive if it keeps predicting new stuff that pans out, and degenerating if it's just patching the same old anomalies after the fact. And the part that really got me: Lakatos says you can't judge any of this in real time. No instant rationality. You can only look back later and say, rationally reconstructed, that it was progressive or degenerating all along.

So here's what my old team did. The hard core was "everything is an event-sourced microservice." You could not question it. The protective belt was retries, sidecars, a cache-invalidation layer, three generations of sagas, a whole config DSL. Every outage added a belt item. We never shipped anything that predicted a new capability; we just kept patching regressions. And every time someone asked "is this architecture actually working," the answer was always "you can't judge it yet, these things take years, we'll know in hindsight" — which is, I now realize, Lakatos's own escape hatch used as a way to never ever call a thing degenerating. Also, whenever a bug showed up that broke the core's assumptions, we'd quietly redefine the spec until the bug wasn't a bug anymore. Which I later learned is basically monster-barring.

What I want: a mock internal postmortem / "programme health review" doc for that team, in deadpan corporate incident-review register — Summary, Impact, Root Cause, Action Items, that whole genre — maybe 700 words, numbered sections. The joke should be that the Lakatosian framework is the *official* process, so every section is a straight-faced application of it, and the whole doc quietly demonstrates that the framework, the way this team runs it, can never conclude anything bad about the programme, because the verdict is always deferred to a retrospective rational reconstruction that never gets scheduled. Target is the practice — teams using "no instant rationality" to immunize a dying programme from criticism — not any real person. Please actually get the concepts right (hard core, protective belt, negative vs positive heuristic, progressive vs degenerating problem shift, novel predictions, monster-barring, retrospective reconstruction) so someone who's read Lakatos laughs instead of wincing. Keep it deadpan — don't wink at the reader or explain the joke. And if you can land one beat where the main "action item" is to schedule the rational reconstruction for some quarter that keeps receding, that'd be perfect.
