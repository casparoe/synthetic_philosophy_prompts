Right, I need help building a decision tree for my evolution reading group, because we've had the same arguemnt about levels of selection four weeks running and it always ends with everyone naming their favourite position and then talking past each other.

The deliverable: a tree where every branching node is a question about a claim — yes/no or three-way — phrased so that someone could answer it without already knowing which position it drops them into, and every leaf is a named position plus one line on its most awkward commitment. Start from the bit we all accept, that some traits spread through a lineage because their bearers leave more descendants than others, and split on the things that actually divide us. Six or seven nodes and a dozen or so leaves is about right.

Things I want to be visible on the tree:

- whether the level you count fitness at has to be the level where the trait is heritable, or whether you're allowed to aggregate over the individuals in a patch and treat the patch as the thing that's selected;
- whether groups need only to differ in their composition, or whether they have to be reproducing entities in their own right, with the variation among the individuals inside them counting as noise rather than signal (one of us keeps writing MLS1 and MLS2 in the margin of whatever we're reading and I want the tree to make me see whether that's a real distinction or just a rebranding);
- the difference between the thing that gets copied and the thing whose fitness we're counting. Someone in the group insists these are two separate questions and that half the literature is people answering one while believing they're answering the other, and I think they're right;
- whether selection is a cause at all, or only a statistical summary of who happened to leave more offspring, with drift as the sole real process. I want a node for this, because it changes what "the unit of selection" could even mean;
- and the bookkeeping worry. One of us says group selection and kin selection are the same covariance partitioned two different ways, and I've read somewhere that the partition isn't even unique — in which case some of these "positions" are notation rather than disagreement, and I'd rather the tree showed that than hid it.

Leaves I definitely want in: the gene's-eye view; the inclusive-fitness version of it; the weak group-selection view; the strong one where the group itself becomes the individual; species selection. Anything else your tree turns up, include it.

Two things to be careful about. Where a combination of answers lands nowhere, and nobody defends it, say so and say why — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored? And where two leaves are really one position wearing different clothes, flag it rather than inventing a question that separates them. That's the bit I'd actually learn something from.

Use concrete cases at the nodes where you can: a gene for helping behaviour in a patchy population; a trait that only pays off if everyone in the patch has it; a merger in which two lineages now reproduce as one; a species-level trait like geographic range size that tracks speciation rate; a behaviour passed on by imitation rather than by genes.

No reading lists please — I want the shape of the space, not a bibliography.
