I'm a novelist, and my writers group keeps having the same fight alot about sexist language and default assumptions, so I want to make a quiz we can all take before we argue again. It should sort people by what they actually think about cases, not by whether they call themselves a feminist, a classicist, a free-speech absolutist, whatever. I've been rereading Hofstadter's Person Paper — teh one where he swaps race in for gender to make the default pronoun look as bizarre as racist language — and I want the quiz to get at the different things people might be taking from a trick like that, without anyone needing to have read it. So: ten questions, each one a concrete sentence, style-guide rule, or tiny fictional scenario, with three answer options. No question can just ask do you think generic he is sexist. The cases should include: a grammar book saying man embraces woman; a novel where a doctor is he and a nurse is she; a conlang I'm building where the unmarked pronoun is the one for landowners; a translation from a language with grammatical gender; a style guide that bans singular they; a scene where a character's gender is never stated; a satire that swaps in another axis of privilege to expose a default; a reader who says they always pictured the generic person as male; a writer who says changing pronouns is cosmetic because power lives in institutions; and a case where a female character is described by her looks and a male by his deeds. For each, three options that pull apart the positions. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least these positions: semantic neutralist, pragmatic defaultist, reformist, mirror-satirist, language-skeptic, craft-first, and the no-gendered-pronouns-at-all view. Each leaf should get a name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Also tell me which positions the quiz can't really separate and why, and which answer combinations are empty — nobody there because incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep it usable by people who aren't philosophers. And please don't give me a reading list or citations, just the quiz and key.
