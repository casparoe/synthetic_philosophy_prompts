I'm submitting a proposal for a one-day interdisciplinary workshop on consent and need a short document (one page, about 600 words) built out of a half-formed idea. The idea is this: we standardize consent into three conditions — competence, adequate information, and absence of coercion — each with its own threshold, and we treat them as independent. But they plainly trade off. A decision taken under mild pressure by someone fully informed and fully competent can still be valid; the same pressure on someone who barely clears the competence bar invalidates it. My suspicion is that the three conditions are three probes for a single underlying property: whether the agent's endorsement would survive removal of the influence in question — whether she would still consent if the deception were corrected, the pressure lifted, the decision deferred. On this view consent is valid to the degree that the endorsement is robust across those counterfactuals, and the thresholds are not independent because each influence erodes the same quantity.

Write the proposal. State the question so that it has a determinate answer. Explain why the standard three-conditions picture leaves it open, and why a robustness account is worth developing rather than the obvious alternatives — treating the three conditions as separate necessary conditions, or collapsing consent into actual endorsement. Set out how I would actually test the account against cases, especially cases where the counterfactual probes disagree: a deception about a feature the agent would never have weighed, a pressure the agent endorses on reflection, a competent decision that is unstable across trivial reframings. Then say what would count as the account failing — in particular whether it can avoid collapsing into the claim that any influence whatsoever invalidates consent. Be honest about how much of this can be defended in a short paper and what would have to wait. Assume no bibliography; I will add references myself.
