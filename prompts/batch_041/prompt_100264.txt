My mom is retiring after 25 years running a scrappy community health clinic, and I'm giving the toast at her dinner. Here's the wrinkle: two years ago the clinic started a program for prolonged grief — grief that just won't lift — treating it as a condition in its own right, not as depression and not as something you're meant to simply endure. Half the room will be her staff and former patients, who want to hear it defended; the other half are two philosophers of medicine who've publicly called this medicalizing a normal human experience. Write me a five-minute toast that actually walks into the naturalism-vs-normativism mess: is calling something a disease a matter of locating a biological malfunction, or is it always us drawing a value-laden line? It can't pretend the question is settled, and it can't dodge it either — she has to commit to something about what the clinic was actually doing when it decided grief that won't lift is treatable. Make the 'why not just call it depression' point do real work, since that's the part her critics jump on. And it still has to land as a toast — warm, personal, a little funny, not a seminar. Please don't just trot out the standard Boorse-vs-Wakefield summary; I've read that. She lost my dad twelve years ago and was basically told to give it time, so that's sitting in the background if it helps.
