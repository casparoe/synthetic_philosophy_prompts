I am an undergraduate in a second-year epistemology course, and I want to ask about a pattern I keep encountering that I suspect has a name but cannot find in any of the readings so far.

The pattern is this. In my study group there are two people who, on any given question, produce the correct answer. The first gives the right answer and can do nothing further with it: if I ask why, they restate the answer more emphatically; if I raise the obvious objection, they abandon it; if I ask a slightly different question that turns on the same point, they do not see the connection. The second gives the same answer and can do all of those things — defend it, hold it under pressure, carry it to new cases, use it as a step in a longer argument. Both beliefs are true, both people seem equally confident, and yet only the second belief is of any use to its holder. The difference does not appear to be that the second belief is more likely to be correct, since it is already correct; so I cannot say what precisely has been added. Is there a name for this added element?

I also have the sense that a trap lies nearby. Suppose I explain the second belief's superiority by saying that the reasons attached to it will generate further true beliefs later on. Then whatever makes the first belief good — its being true — seems to be doing all the work, and the extra element appears to contribute nothing of its own; on that account two beliefs that are both true would be equally valuable no matter how well or badly they are held, which is the opposite of what I observe. I take this to be a genuine difficulty rather than my own confusion. Does it have a name, and is it the same difficulty as the one I described above, or a different one that merely resembles it?

What I am after is not a bare label. I would like to know which established concepts come closest to what I have described, and for each, what it captures and what it leaves out. If the pattern I have in mind matches none of them exactly, I would like a proposed name and definition for it, along with the cases that would mark its boundaries: does a belief held on reliable authority but not understood fall inside it or outside? Does a belief that is true and well-supported but that no one will ever have occasion to use count, or not? I am less interested in being told that this is a well-known question than in being told how the specific thing I am pointing at should be carved.
