I have been working through Sidgwick's ethical writing alongside the early literature of the Society for Psychical Research, and I have arrived at a set of commitments that I would like audited before I try to defend them in print. I hold: (1) that inquiry must be conducted from an impersonal standpoint on which my own hopes and interests carry no evidential weight at all; (2) that practical reason is dualistic — egoism and universal benevolence are each fully rational, and no argument closes the gap between them; (3) that only a moral order of the universe, one in which duty and interest coincide, could close that gap, so that the question of survival is the pivot on which the rationality of morality turns; (4) that the trance communications and veridical reports in the early literature are the best evidence we shall ever get on survival, and that they deserve sustained and sympathetic investigation; (5) that the conditions of the séance make fraud and self-deception nearly ineliminable and the published record is filtered by people who want it to be true, so that no accumulation of such reports should move a careful inquirer's credence; and (6) that no factual discovery can alter what reasons for action there are, so that survival, even if established, would be a discovery in natural history and would leave ethics where it found it.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to, and do not treat the set as uniformly rickety — I want the discriminations. Order the genuine conflicts by severity, and for each give the cheapest repair and what that repair costs.

Return a single JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "genuine_tensions": array, most severe first, each element an object with "commitments" (array of the numbers involved, e.g. [3, 6]), "severity" (one of "high", "moderate", "low"), "explanation" (the conflict, in prose), "what_gives" (which commitment must be revised, and how), and "cost_of_repair" (what is lost by revising it).
- "apparent_tensions": array, each element an object with "commitments" (array of numbers) and "why_it_dissolves".
- "least_costly_overall_revision": string, naming the single change that cleans up the most, and stating what it leaves unresolved.
