I keep getting into arguments with a friend who works in development about what counts as corruption, and I want to work out what I actually think by going case by case. Surgeon gets a bottle of wine from a grateful patient a fortnight after a clean operation: not corruption. Same surgeon gets an envelope the night before the operation: corruption, obviously. Waiter gets 20%: fine. Clerk in a licensing office takes a couple hundred rupees to move my file to the top of a queue that's otherwise strictly first-come-first-served: corruption, even though it's small and my file would've come up eventually. Same clerk, same office, takes the same money to issue me a permit I'm legally entitled to and he's legally obliged to issue, where the only alternative is a six-month wait: I don't think that's corruption, though I can see it's the same shape as the queue case and that bothers me. Minister's nephew gets a ministry job he's qualified for and no ones else applied: corruption. Shop owner hires her competent friend over a better-qualified stranger: not corruption. Company pays a retired regulator a fat "consulting fee" a year after she ruled its way: corruption — but then why isn't the wine corruption, since both are payments after the fact for something already done?

Also, my grandmother's town has an actual customary scale, written down, where officials get a fixed "harvest share" every autumn, everyone knows the amounts, and nobody expects anything in return — the ones who take it call it a salary top-up, and the ones who refuse it are thought stuck-up. I feel like that's corruption and I can't say why the wine isn't.

What principle, or small set of principles, gets all of these? Where it can't, which verdict should I give up, and what does the surviving principle say about the harvest share and about a case I haven't listed — say, a Western engineering firm paying "facilitation" money to customs officers abroad? And be honest with me about whether I've just dressed up a Western standard as a universal one.
