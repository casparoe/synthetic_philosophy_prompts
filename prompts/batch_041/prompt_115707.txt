I'm in a reading group that has spent most of this year on moral realism, and two of us have basically stopped listening to each other. One guy (call him R) thinks there are moral facts that don't depend on what anyone thinks or feels, and that we know some of them by intuition or reason or whatever you want to call it — he's not a naturalist about it, he says the facts aren't the same kind of thing as facts about water or pain, and he gets annoyed when people treat that as a dodge. The other (call him E) thinks that when we say something is wrong we're mostly expressing and coordinating attitudes, and he's totally happy to say moral sentences are true and false in the thin deflationary sense — 'murder is wrong' is true just in case murder is wrong, end of story — he just thinks R keeps adding a layer that does no explanatory work. E says he isn't changing the subject; R says of course he is, and that if you never meant anything by 'wrong' beyond your attitude you can't turn around and use it to tell me what to do. Every Tuesday since February, same fight, and honestly I can't tell anymore whether they're disagreeing about anything real or just about which words to use for the same picture.

So write the document they should have produced: the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between these two would end in. Start with a numbered list of agreed claims — but don't pad it, and don't hand me a list of platitudes that a nihilist or a fictionalist would sign too; if a claim is that weak, flag it as weak and set it aside instead of putting it in the agreed section. Then the disagreements, each one written in a wording its own side would actually accept, not a caricature of one side and not a question that already leans toward an answer. For each disagreement, the crux underneath it — the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one of them. Then, for each crux, a case, argument, or bit of evidence that both sides agree would bear on it, with R's prediction and E's prediction recorded in advance, before either gets to spin the result.

The thing I most want in there: suppose it turned out that human moral beliefs are produced by a mechanism that was selected for group cohesion, and that it would have cranked out pretty much the same beliefs whether or not anything was mind-independently wrong. Both of them agree that's a genuine test case, so get their advance predictions on it and make them say what each would count as evidence against his own position, not just against the other guy's. Also make them deal with the compounding issue: does a belief need to be explained by something that tracks the truth in order to be justified, or is that demand just something R has already given up on for his own non-natural facts? And at the end, tell me honestly whether any part of this is verbal — if some chunk of R's view is something E can also sign, spell out exactly which sentences they're both entitled to, and which one has to be dropped before there's a real fight left. I'd rather find out they agree than get a fake compromise.

Format: start straight in with the document itself, no preface and no restating my request — agreed claims first, then the disagreements as a table with a column for the crux and a column for each side's prediction on it, then a short closing section with the verbal-dispute verdict. Keep the whole thing under two pages, and if they genuinely can't agree on a wording for something, say so rather than papering over it.
