For most of my career I was a field biologist; I came to philosophy late and have been reading my way into it with more enthusiasm than system. I have just finished Tolstoy's What Is Art? — the book he completed in 1897 and that first appeared in English the following year — and I find myself largely persuaded by the core of his account, though I have assembled my own version of it. Before I commit it to writing, I want a consistency audit.

Here is where I have landed:

(1) Art is, at bottom, a communication of feeling. A work is art to the degree that it transmits to an audience a feeling the maker has actually undergone. Tolstoy's formula — "Art begins when a man, with the purpose of communicating to other people a feeling he once experienced, calls it up again within himself and expresses it by certain external signs" — captures this well enough for my purposes.

(2) The definition of art should be descriptive and morally neutral. If a work effectively transmits a feeling, it is art, whether the feeling is compassionate or cruel, elevating or degrading.

(3) Sincerity is a necessary condition of successful transmission. An artist who does not actually undergo the feeling cannot infect others with it; a cynically manufactured emotion is a broken signal.

(4) Nevertheless, the value of art is partly a moral matter. Art that transmits fellow-feeling, compassion, and a sense of shared humanity is better than art that transmits contempt, cruelty, or degradation.

(5) The greatest art is universal — accessible across classes, cultures, and epochs. Obscurity is a defect, not a sign of depth.

(6) Individuality matters: the feeling transmitted must be the artist's own, not borrowed or imitated, or the transmission is hollow.

I would like you to audit this set. Which commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the cheapest way to restore consistency? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity and, for each, say what would have to give and what the cost of giving it would be. Do not tell me that everything is fine; I suspect at least two of these pull against each other, and I would rather find out now.

Please deliver the audit as a JSON object with the following fields:

- "genuine_tensions": an array, ordered by severity (most severe first), where each item is an object with fields "pair" (the two commitment numbers in tension), "diagnosis" (a paragraph explaining why they conflict, not merely that they do), "severity" (one of "low", "moderate", "high"), "cheapest_fix" (which commitment to revise and how), and "cost_of_fix" (what is lost by revising it).
- "apparent_tensions": an array where each item is an object with fields "pair" (the two commitment numbers), "why_it_dissolves" (a paragraph explaining why the tension is only apparent), and "what_makes_it_look_like_a_tension" (the surface feature that misleads).
- "single_most_cleaning_revision": an object with fields "commitment" (the number to revise), "revision" (the proposed change), and "why_it_cleans_up_the_most" (a paragraph explaining which tensions it resolves or softens and at what cost).
- "overall_verdict": a string, one of "jointly tenable as stated", "tenable with minor revision", "tenable only with substantial revision", or "not jointly tenable", with a brief justification.
