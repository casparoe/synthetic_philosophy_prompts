I sit on the standards panel at the small charity I work for (I'm a retired nurse, not an academic, so excuse the rambling) — four of us get shown anything that looks dodgy and decide whether a partnership survives. I keep getting the feeling we're making it up as we go along, and I want to know what we've actually been doing. Here's the record, roughly.

1. A partner's director took cash from a supplier under the table for a contract. We ended the partnership. Nobody on the panel lost any sleep over that one.

2. At the close of a project the community threw a big do: we paid for a goat and some crates of beer out of the project budget, and the district officer was handed a carved stool in front of about two hundred people, with our own staff taking the photos. We said that was fine — that's how you close a project there, and the donor's auditors accepted it.

3. A staff member paid a customs clerk a small amount in cash to get a container of medical supplies out of the port in two days instead of three weeks. It went through the books as handling charges. We said fine, that's expediting, and the supplies reached the clinic.

4. A partner's director hired his two nephews as field officers. No advert, the posts hadn't existed before, but the two of them did the job okay, and where he's from a man in his position is expected to look after his relatives and would be shamed if he didn't. We said no — nepotism — re-advertise the posts, and we came close to pulling the partnership.

Now the new case, same country as 4. Our country director steered a contract to a cooperative from his own village, where his uncle is the chair. The thing is, their bid was genuinely the best and the cheapest, the work's been excellent, the clinic opened on time, nothing was skimmed. He didn't declare the connection — but he didn't hide it either, the whole office knew.

So what do we decide, and what does that commit us to saying we've been doing all along? I definately go back and forth on whether we have a rule at all or just four gut feelings. My own test has been to ask whether the schoolkids in the donor country who did the sponsored runs would be angry if they saw it on the front page, but I've noticed that test would have got 2 and 4 both wrong, so maybe I'm the inconsistent one.

I'd also like an honest word about what we give up either way. Alot of the panel thinks we're just imposing rich-country morality on people who organise their obligations differently and that we should get off our high horse; the other half thinks a bribe is a bribe. Don't fudge it. And tell me which of the four is the closest analog to the new case, and if you think two of our old decisions were really driven by different principles that happen to agree so far, tell me what kind of case would finally separate them.
