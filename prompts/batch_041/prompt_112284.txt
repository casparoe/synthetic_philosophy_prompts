I keep having the same argument with a friend about the colour phi phenomenon and we never get anywhere, so I want you to diagnose the disagreement instead of settling it. Setup first, so we're on the same page. You flash two dots one after another, in different spots, and the second is a different colour from the first. If the timing and spacing are right, people don't report two dots — they report a single dot moving across the gap, and weirdly they report the first dot changing into the second dot's colour partway along its path, i.e. as if the colour change happened before the second dot was even shown. But it can't have, since the information about the second colour only shows up when the second dot does. So something in the system is doing time-travel-ish work.

There are two ways ive seen this explained, and I'll give them made-up names so I don't just get the famous version back. Call them rewrite and construction. On rewrite: you genuinely get the first dot in its own colour, and then afterwards the memory of it is quietly doctored, so you misremember having seen the change earlier (the past gets edited after the fact). On construction: the first dot never reaches you in its own colour at all — what you end up experiencing is a later, already-smoothed-together construction, so nothing is rewritten; the edit happens before you're aware of anything.

My friend says these are two rival empirical hypotheses and neuroscience will eventually tell us which one is real. I think the argument is stuck for a different reason, and I want the diagnosis. Is it a genuine empirical disagreement that's just been under-tested? A verbal one, where the two sides are describing the same machinery and 'when the experience happened' quietly means two different things on each side? Or is the real trouble that the question 'when did you experience the first dot' has no determinate answer, so both sides are chasing a fact that isn't there? And if it's that last one — is it even coherent? Can you say experiences don't really happen at times without giving up on there being experiences at all?

I have a hunch, and I dont trust it: I think the whole thing turns on a distinction nobody states out loud, between the time at which the brain represents something and the time the brain represents the thing as having happened — when the representation occurs versus what time it says the event occurred. If that's the crux, say so and explain why it breaks the tie (or why it doesn't). If it's a red herring, tell me what the actual crux is. And tell me what would count as progress here: a cleverer experiment, a conceptual distinction, dropping the question, or honestly nothing. If I'm wrong that it's even stalled — if there's a version of the dispute that isn't stuck, or if it dissolves once you ask a slightly different question — I'd rather hear that than a tidy survey.
