I've got a talk next month to our social cognition group — mostly people who run situation experiments, a couple of them have spent the last few years trying to replicate the classics with nothing to show for it, plus two philosophers who only got invited because I'm the third. They care a lot about effect sizes and they're allergic to constructs they can't operationalize. My thesis: the situationist critique only bites if you assume traits have to be global, i.e. context-free and all-purpose; once you individuate a trait by the class of situations it's defined over (the indexed reading), the variance-accounting picture the whole debate runs on — how much of behavior is person, how much is situation — is a category mistake rather than an emprical finding, and a trait can be real and causally efficacious while showing up only inside a narrow band of situations.
