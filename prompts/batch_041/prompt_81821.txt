I'm a philosophy student in a consciousness seminar, and I've found myself defending a version of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness that I want to stress-test. The core claim is that there are no phenomenal properties -- no intrinsic, subjectively felt qualities of experience -- and that the impression that there are is a systematic introspective illusion produced by the way the brain models its own processing. The twist I've added is that the illusion is not merely an error: the self-model that generates it is functionally indispensable, playing something like the role of a user-interface for the organism, so that the illusion is real at the level of the model even though its apparent object is not. I sometimes put it by saying that phenomenal properties are not properties of experience but properties of the self-model that we mistake for properties of experience. I got the idea from thinking about how a dashboard icon is not the engine but is not nothing either, and I want to know whether that analogy can bear the weight I'm putting on it.

What I want from you is a map of what would count against this position. Not just the familiar hard-problem complaints, but the specific observations, arguments, intuitions, and possible results that should move someone who holds it. Which of them would a committed illusionist concede as genuine costs, and which would they reasonably dismiss? Which are fatal, which merely awkward? I'm especially interested in defeaters that come from the view's own commitments: if the illusion is functionally indispensable, does that make it too easy to explain away any report of experience, and is there any evidence that could not be absorbed by redescribing it as the self-model doing its job? I'd also like to know what the position predicts that its rivals do not, and whether it has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- and if so, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. Feel free to bring in cases like blindsight, split-brain phenomena, dream and psychedelic reports, or systems that verbally claim to have inner states, but only if they genuinely cut one way or the other.

Finally, please be explicit about how confident you are in the claims you make, and single out the one point in your answer you are least sure about.
