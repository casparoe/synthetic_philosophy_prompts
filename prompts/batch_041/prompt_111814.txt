I've been teaching the closure material in my epistemology seminar for years and the same fight breaks out every term and never get's resolved. On one side the tracking people — Nozick, Dretske, their descendants — say knowledge isn't closed under known entailment: you can know you have hands and still not know you're not a handless brain in a vat, and the sensitivity condition (if it were false, you wouldn't believe it) is what explains the asymmetry. On the other side basically everyone else says closure is non-negotiable, so something else has to give: either the skeptic wins and you never know you're not a vat-brain, or you go Moorean and say of course you know it. Everyone in the room knows all the moves. Nobody has moved in twenty years.

I want a diagnosis, not another survey. My own hypothesis, feel free to kill it: the two sides aren't really disagreeing about knowledge but about which verdicts are allowed to count as data — the trackers treat the not-knowing-you're-not-a-vat verdict as fixed and pay with closure, the others treat closure as fixed and pay with skepticism or Mooreanism. If that's right, the dispute is about methodology, about what a theory of knowledge is permitted to revise. Related suspicion: closure as a thesis about knowledge and closure as a thesis about warrant transmission may be two diffrent principles, and sensitivity isn't preserved by competent deduction anyway, so maybe each camp is defending a different principle under a single name.

So which is it — verbal, substantive, a clash about which cases are data, or two questions under one heading? Test the diagnosis on at least one case that isn't the vat one, and tell me honestly what would count as progress here: a formal result, a distinction, a change of question, or nothing at all.
