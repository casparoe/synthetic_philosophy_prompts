I am a philosophy PhD student trying to reverse-engineer the semantic theory my advisor is implicitly running, because her explicit commitments are vague and her case-by-case verdicts are far more informative than her slogans. Three verdicts so far. In the original Twin Earth scenario she says my doppelgänger's "water" and mine differ in meaning, so meaning is not in the head. In the familiar case of a patient who sincerely reports arthritis in his thigh she says he has a false belief about arthritis rather than a concept of his own, so a speaker's terms can mean what his community's terms mean even when he misunderstands them. And she says a community that has always held false beliefs about the biology of whales nevertheless means whales by "whale," not some fish-kind. I can capture all three verdicts with either a causal-historical externalism, on which a term's reference is fixed by the chain of transmission back to an original act of naming, or a communal-usage externalism, on which a term's reference is fixed by the linguistic community's current use. Now she has to rule on a case I have constructed for her: colonists settle a landmass whose only plentiful liquid is a substance chemically unlike water; their ancestors carried the word "water" from a home world where it named H2O, and no one on the landmass has ever encountered H2O, yet they have always applied "water" to the local liquid. Predict her verdict on whether their "water" means H2O or the local substance, say which of the two rules she is really running and which other rules fit her past verdicts just as well, give your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and name a further case that would separate those rules.
