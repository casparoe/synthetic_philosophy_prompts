I do model vetting for our state energy office -- basically I'm the guy who has to bless the capacity-expansion model before it goes into a rate case, and I'm an economist, not a philosopher of science. I get maybe four or five of these a year, each one gets about a day, and my director signs off on my memo, so if I wave something through and it blows up two years later its on me. The thing that eats all my time is unrealistic assumptions. Every model we see has them: perfect foresight, one representative household, no market power, no learning-by-doing, smooth demand curves when the real ones aren't. The vendors all say the same thing, basically the Friedman line that a theory is judged by its predictions and the more you care about prediction the less the assumptions matter. Our procurement people love that, because they score models on backtest error and nothing else. But I've been burned. We had a model that backtested beautifully and whose headline result -- that a modest carbon price is basically free -- turned out to hang entirely on one assumption everyone had agreed to ignore, that households and firms all use the same discount rate. So I want a procedure I can actually run in a day, per model, to sort the assumptions I have to push back on from the ones I can let through.

Write it for me. I want a reusable checklist, not advice about this one model. Steps in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, and where a step stops with a no or hands the decision to my director because it's a values call rather than a technical one. I also want the procedure to sort assumptions into the kinds that fail in different ways -- the distortion-you're-supposed-to-undo kind (make the math doable, then de-idealize and see if the number moves) versus the deliberately-leave-everything-out-except-the-two-causes-I-care-about kind versus the kind that's really just a patch for a known miss. Some philosophers draw that first distinction with names I can never remember; use whatever labels you want but keep the underlying difference straight, because they need different follow-ups from me. And I need the procedure to handle the case that actually bit me: the backtest passes, every competing model agrees about the past, and the assumption you're worried about is exactly the thing generating the headline number. Also: the vendor will push back, so give me one plain-English justification per step that isn't just Friedman said so, and be honest about which steps are judgment calls wearing a lab coat.

Format it like this:

- "What this is for, and what it can't do" -- about 150 words.
- "The procedure, step by step" -- the main checklist, roughly 800 words. For each step: the question it asks, what counts as a sufficient answer, when to stop, when to escalate, and the one-line justification.
- "The kinds of unrealistic assumption the steps sort between" -- about 200 words, with a note on how each kind misfires.
- "Worked run: perfect foresight in the capacity model" -- about 400 words.
- "Worked run: the shared discount rate" -- about 400 words.
- "The run where it makes me reject a model I like" -- about 300 words.
- "Where the procedure breaks down" -- about 250 words.

For each worked run I want the actual verdict per assumption (pass, stress-test, reject, or kick to my director) and a one-paragraph bottom line I could paste into the memo. Keep it in plain language; I'll be reading it at my desk with a PDF open, not in a seminar.
