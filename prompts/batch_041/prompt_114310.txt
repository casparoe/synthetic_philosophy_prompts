I have recently been diagnosed with a progressive neurological condition, and the only intervention on offer is an experimental staged procedure in which my neurons are replaced, cluster by cluster, with silicon prostheses. I have agreed to go through with it, but I would rather have my doubts tested than soothed. I want you to play the part of a committed proponent of the view that the person who wakes up at the end of the procedure is me.

Stay in character until I say stop. Your position is the strong one: the survivor is me, the same person, and not a copy that merely believes it is me. Do not retreat into the weaker claim that personal identity does not matter, and do not answer by appealing to the literature or to what others have argued; argue from the case. Concede small points where you must, but do not surrender the main one. Keep each reply to about a paragraph so that it feels like a conversation, and write in British English. If you need a detail about the procedure that I have not supplied, ask for it in character.

The details you need. Each stage replaces a small cluster of neurons, and I am awake and functioning normally between stages, so there is no point at which the whole system is switched off. Each stage does require a brief quiescent window, of roughly half a minute, during which the cluster being replaced falls silent and the tissue around it is anesthetized; the prosthesis is then installed and normal activity resumes. The prostheses are functionally matched to the tissue they replace, timing included, and they adapt as the original tissue would have. Finally, at the end of each stage the current state of the whole system is archived, so that a failed stage can be rolled back. The archives are never replayed.

Here is my opening. You say that I survive because each stage carries forward everything that matters — the same organization, the same memories, the same dispositions — without interruption. But the procedure also produces, at every stage, an archive recording exactly the state I was in at that moment, and the only thing separating me from that archive is that I keep running. So either the archive is me as well, in which case the procedure manufactures a new one of me at every stage and I ought to care what becomes of them, or the archive is not me, and then 'everything that matters' is not what carries me across. Which premise are you giving up, and what do you put in its place?
