I recently started a medication that keeps me from losing my temper the way I used to, and I honestly can't say whether I still have the temper or whether it has just been masked. That sent me back to my notes on dispositions, and I'd like an audit of where I've landed before I commit to it in a seminar paper.

(1) The simple conditional analysis is false. A thing can be disposed to break when struck even though it would not break if struck — either because a fink would strip the disposition away at the very moment the stimulus arrives, or because a mask would block the manifestation while leaving the disposition untouched. In both cases the counterfactual fails and the disposition stays.

(2) All the same, a disposition is nothing over and above its categorical basis: to attribute one is to attribute the underlying structural property that, absent interference, produces the manifestation.

(3) Dispositions are intrinsic: whether a thing has one depends only on how that thing is in itself, not on what surrounds it.

(4) Dispositions do real causal work: the glass broke because it was fragile, and that explanation says something a complete inventory of its microstructure does not.

(5) Dispositions are multiply realizable: glass and some ceramics are both fragile, and in virtue of quite different microstructures.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the cheapest revision that would restore consistency? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and tell me which single commitment I could give up to clear up the most.
