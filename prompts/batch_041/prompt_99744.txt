I am a high school student trying to understand why the debate between illusionists and phenomenal realists about consciousness has not been resolved, even though both sides seem to accept the same neuroscience and the same first-person reports. The illusionist says that what we call phenomenal consciousness is a kind of introspective illusion: there is no additional intrinsic, ineffable, private feel over and above the functional and representational states that produce our judgments that we have such a feel. The realist says that this cannot be right, because the existence of a felt quality is the one thing we know directly, and any theory that denies it is changing the subject. In our club we keep getting stuck on whether the illusionist is denying the datum or reinterpreting it, and on whether first-person seemings count as evidence or as the very thing to be explained. I suspect the disagreement may be partly verbal, partly a dispute about what an explanation of consciousness is supposed to explain, and partly a hidden disagreement about whether introspection gives us acquaintance with intrinsic properties or only a representation that it does. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using the positions as I have described them, and say what would count as progress: a distinction, a way of counting first-person evidence, an empirical result about metacognition, or a reason to think the question is ill-posed. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
