I'm on a small panel advising a country that's deciding whether to keep, create or scrap a ceremonial head of state, and we've got basically one researcher for a year. Our comparitive cases are the UK, Denmark/Norway/Sweden and Japan. I keep finding that two very different things get run together: (a) the monarch as a real actor who might use reserve powers -- refusing assent, dissolving parliament, picking a PM after a hung parliament -- to block something, and (b) the monarch as background furniture whose mere existence changes what elected politicians do even though the monarch never lifts a finger. Which open questions in this area would you actually put at the top of our researcher's list, and why? I want a defended ordering rather than a survey: for each, why it's load-bearing for the practical recommendation, how tractable it is with a political theorist's toolkit, and what would move it up or down. And tell me which of the questions everyone fixates on we should quietly deprioritise because the answer wouldn't change our recommendation either way. No reading list, just the argument. British English please.
