For the past several years I have been writing novels in which the interior lives of characters are rendered almost entirely in the vocabulary of belief and desire — she believed the letter had been sent, he wanted only to be forgiven, and so on — and I have lately been reading about the eliminativist proposal that this vocabulary names nothing at all, that it is a folk theory destined for the same discard pile as phlogiston. I am not yet persuaded, but I have come to see that I cannot adjudicate the dispute by trading intuitions, and I would rather have the specification first: before I decide whether my narrator may keep talking this way, I want the full list of desiderata that any adequate theory of belief and desire must satisfy. I mean the spec sheet in full — the phenomena the theory has to explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it may not collapse, and the commitments it cannot contradict — each accompanied by the particular case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference. I am especially interested in three kinds of case that I suspect are load-bearing and that the usual discussions pass over: the beliefs we attribute to fictional characters, who by hypothesis never existed and never had any functional organization to speak of; the beliefs we now attribute, half-seriously and half-not, to large language models; and the wholesale-error scenario the eliminativist needs, in which our entire practice turns out to have been systematically mistaken. A requirement that survives only because the awkward case is never raised is not a requirement, so tell me plainly which items on your list are genuine constraints on any theory and which are merely the house rules of one camp.

Then, because I would rather see the fault lines than a scorecard, mark for each desideratum whether it is non-negotiable, optional, or the proprietary demand of a particular position, and flag any pair of desiderata you believe cannot be jointly satisfied — the eliminativist's fate may well turn on one such pair, and that is the part of your answer I will read most closely. Close with a short assessment of how the eliminativist program and its principal rivals fare against the list, but keep it brief; the criteria are what I need, not the verdicts.

Give the whole thing as a single JSON object with these fields. desiderata: an array, one object per requirement, each with id (a short slug), name (a few words), statement (what the theory must deliver, in one or two sentences), motivating_case (the case or consideration that makes it a requirement), rules_out (the kinds of theory or claim it would exclude), and status (one of "non-negotiable", "camp-specific", or "optional"). irreconcilable_pairs: an array of objects, each with pair (the two ids) and why (the reason they cannot both be met). most_exclusionary: an object with id and why, naming the single requirement that eliminates the most otherwise plausible candidates. scorecard: an array of objects, each with contender (the position), standing (one sentence), and weakest_link (the id of the desideratum it struggles with most).
