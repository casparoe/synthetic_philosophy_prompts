Luck egalitarianism is the view that the only unjust inequalities are the ones that come from brute luck — stuff you had no say in, like being born with a disability or getting hit by a random car — whereas inequalities that trace back to your own choices are fine and justice has no complaint about them. The whole theory hangs on the brute luck / option luck distinction. He defined it like this: brute luck is when the outcome is outside your control, option luck is when you're in control of the outcome. So he said if you blow your paycheck on lottery tickets and lose, that's option luck, you eat it; but if your house gets flattened by lightning, that's brute luck and you get made whole. He also said it doesn't really matter what you're equalizing — resources, welfare, whatever — because the luck/choice line does all the work, so all luck egalitarians basically agree on everything except the details. Then he said luck egalitarians are just strict egalitarians who tacked on a responsibility clause to look modern, and he wrapped up by citing the objection from relational egalitarians that luck egalitarianism leaves the imprudent to starve, and said that's a decisive refutation — no theory that would let a reckless person die in the gutter can be right.

Is this fair? I'm pretty sure the guy who takes responsibility seriously isn't supposed to be that heartless, but I honestly don't know where the summary goes wrong.
