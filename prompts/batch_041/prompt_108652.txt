I am a high-school student in my school's philosophy club, and I would like help with a ranking exercise. In ceramics I cast a small bronze figure by the lost-wax method: a wax model is buried in plaster, the wax is melted out so that the figure's shape survives only as a cavity, molten bronze is poured into that cavity, and the plaster is broken away. Two further bronze figures were then cast from the same mould, and the wax model no longer exists. Our club cannot agree on how to describe what has happened, so please rank the following responses to the statue-and-clay problem in order of how well each handles this case, giving one sentence of justification for each: (a) the figure and the bronze are a single object described in two ways; (b) they are two distinct objects that share all of their material; (c) only the bronze is real, and talk of the figure is just a way of speaking; (d) the figure is constituted by the bronze without being identical with it; (e) there are no composite objects at all, only simples arranged figure-wise; (f) the figure and the bronze are distinct but coinciding series of temporal parts. Finally, please tell me which single consideration is doing the most work in your ranking, because our club tends to settle such disputes by appealing to whichever intuition is nearest to hand.
