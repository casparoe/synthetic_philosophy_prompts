I've taken to reasoning about uncertain things with ranges instead of single numbers: rather than pretend I know the exact chance that my flight will be delayed, I write down that it lies somewhere between 20% and 50%. A friend tells me this is called imprecise probability, and my reason for liking it is that fixing on a single number always felt arbitrary, whereas a range seems to spare me the arbitrary choice. The trouble is that when a decision actually arrives — whether to buy the insurance, say — the range by itself tells me nothing, and I appear to be back to picking a number. Lately I've been resolving this by using whichever value in the range supports the option I was already inclined towards, on the grounds that since the range contains that value I'm entitled to it. Is that a legitimate way to get from a range to a decision, and if not, what is the correct procedure — how do I extract a decision from an imprecise credence without the arbitrariness I adopted it to escape? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
