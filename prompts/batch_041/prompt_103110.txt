In my seminar on theories of well-being we have just finished the unit on hedonism, and the heterogeneity problem is the part I keep circling back to. The standard framing—sensory pleasures feel like one thing, the pleasure of solving a proof or of a friend's success feels like nothing in particular—seems to me to get resolved too quickly in both directions, and I would like your actual credence rather than a survey of positions.

Take the proposition to be: there exists a single property P such that (a) every state we would pre-theoretically call a pleasure instantiates P, (b) no state we would pre-theoretically call a displeasure instantiates P, and (c) P is the property in virtue of which pleasures contribute positively to a person's welfare, so that amounts of P are comparable across otherwise dissimilar cases.

Give me a number for that, then decompose it. I expect the terms to include at least: whether "pleasure" names a natural kind or a disjunctive folk category; whether the sensory, attitudinal, and functional candidates can each cover the full range of cases; whether a disjunctive account of P counts as a genuine unifier or only a verbal one; and whether hedonism's comparative and aggregative machinery actually requires univocality, or whether it could survive a plural notion of pleasure with separate, non-fungible scales. Assign a rough probability to each term, one line of justification apiece, then show me how they combine.

I have also been toying with a fourth candidate and would like you to price it in rather than ignore it: that a pleasure is any experience whose presence raises the hedonic tone of the encompassing stream of consciousness, even when its local felt quality is neutral or negative. That is meant to handle cases like savoring grief or enjoying melancholy music, where the local state is unpleasant but the whole episode is not. Treat that proposal as a live hypothesis and tell me how much it moves your number relative to the more familiar candidates.

Finally, a classmate puts the same proposition at about 0.8 and I am nearer 0.4. On the basis of your decomposition, tell me which single term most plausibly accounts for that gap, and which sub-question your own number is most sensitive to—that is, which answer, if it went the other way, would move you furthest, and what would have to be shown to make you move.
