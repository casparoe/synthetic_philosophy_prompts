I'm a reporter at a mid-sized magazine and I've somehow ended up on the committee that decides how we pay freelancers. Two years ago we moved off flat rates onto a rate that scales with how many readers get to the end of a piece — the theory being that if people finish it, it was worth writing. The completion numbers went up. The magazine got worse. Everyone can see it, nobody can prove it, and the people who like the new system like it precisely because they can point at the graph.

Meeting's next month and there are three options on the table: keep it, scrap it and go back to flat rates, or "fix" it. The fix is our data lead's idea — bolt on five or six sub-metrics (shares, return visits, comment civility, whatever) and weight them so the score can't be gamed. That one feels wrong to me but I can't articulate why, and "it can't be gamed" is a hard thing to argue against in a room full of people who trust numbers.

Here's my half-baked theory, tell me if it holds up. I think there are three different ways a number goes bad, and people lump them together. One: the number stops tracking the thing, but the thing itself is fine — we're just measuring badly now. Two: people start doing the number instead of the job, so the job quietly becomes a different job. Three: the number was never a measure of the thing at all, it was a symptom of it, and it moves for reasons of its own. Are those actually distinct, or am I just drawing lines through the same mush? And does the second one even require anyone to be cynical? Our freelancers aren't villains, they're responding to what we put in front of them, so I'm not sure "gaming" is the right word for what they're doing.

Related thing someone said to me years ago that I've never shaken: you can't turn a virtue into a checklist, because knowing what kindness actually asks for in a particular situation *is* the kindness — it's not a seperate skill you could hand off to a rulebook. If that's right, then my data lead's plan is doomed in principle and not just in practice, and no number of sub-metrics saves me. Is that the same problem in a different dress, or am I conflating two things that only rhyme?

So: which option do I argue for, and what do I actually say in the room? I dont need the name of the phenomenon, I need to know what I'm looking at and what to do about it.
