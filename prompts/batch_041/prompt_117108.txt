Write a joke about the knowability paradox. I intend to tell it at the end of a graduate seminar in epistemology, so it may be dry and it may presuppose the argument, but it must be an actual joke, and the humour must come from the argument rather than from the foibles of epistemologists.

So that nothing here turns on your recall of the literature, the argument is this. Take a truth that is not known: p, where p is true and no one knows that p. If every truth is knowable, then it is possible to know the conjunction p and no one knows that p. Knowledge is factive, and it distributes over conjunction: to know a conjunction is to know each conjunct. So knowing that conjunction would require knowing that p and knowing that no one knows that p, and the second of these entails that no one knows that p, which contradicts the first. The conjunction is therefore not knowable at all; by the knowability principle it cannot be true either; so p is known after all. A principle that every truth is knowable thus yields the conclusion that there are no unknown truths.

What I want is a joke that exploits this, not one that merely alludes to it.

1. Two jokes, turning on different steps of the argument. Each should be intelligible to someone who has only the paragraph above, so no names, dates, or titles, and no dependence on the audience having read anything. Neither may be a pun on know or knowable. Each should be short enough to deliver aloud without a blackboard. It may help to imagine that half the room knows the proof cold and half has only ever heard the headline.

2. For each joke, identify the step it exploits — factivity, distribution over conjunction, the move from the unknowability of the conjunction to its falsity, or the range of truths the principle is allowed to cover, including the manoeuvre of exempting propositions whose knowability can be shown to be impossible — and say in two or three sentences what becomes of the joke if that step is dropped or repaired. A joke that survives the loss of the assumption it was built on is not doing the work I want.

If the only way to make it funny is to misstate the argument, I would rather have a duller joke.
