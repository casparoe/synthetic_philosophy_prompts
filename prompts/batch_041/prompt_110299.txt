Write a satirical excerpt, around 700 words, from a self-help book by a fictional productivity guru who has become obsessed with the absent-minded driver problem and now recommends its solution for every domain of life. The excerpt should be deadpan and earnest, in the voice of someone who believes he has found a universal key to decision-making; never wink at the reader, and never have the guru state the obvious objection to his own position. The satire should target the overextension of decision theory — specifically, the impulse to treat a formal puzzle about imperfect recall and mixed strategies as a general model of human choice. To make the humor land for someone who knows the literature, the guru must get the technical details exactly right: the two intersections, the payoffs (0 for exiting at the first, 4 for exiting at the second, 1 for continuing through both), the driver's inability to distinguish the intersections, the ex ante optimal strategy of exiting with probability 1/3 at each intersection (expected payoff 4/3), and the tension between that optimal plan and the act-by-act reasoning that seems to favor continuing once one is at an intersection.

The guru should then apply the 1/3 rule to careers, marriages, and small daily choices, always with the same serene confidence, and the piece should quietly expose the absurdities that follow from ignoring the differences between the formal problem and real life — without ever breaking character. Include a book title, a chapter heading, and a few subheadings; the prose should be polished enough that a specialist recognizes the author knows the problem, and a non-specialist can still follow the joke.
