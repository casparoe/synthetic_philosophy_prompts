I am preparing a graduate seminar in normative ethics on prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection. For a written exercise, I want a critical comparison of two versions of prioritarianism, not a survey of the literature. The first is absolute prioritarianism: the moral weight of benefiting a person depends only on that person's absolute level of well-being. The second is relative, or comparative, prioritarianism: that weight depends at least partly on how that person's level compares with the levels of others. I also want you to use two injected notions. A view is strongly leveling-down-proof if it never ranks an outcome as better than another when the only change is that some better-off people are made worse off and no one is made better off. A view is weakly leveling-down-proof if it never ranks such an outcome as better merely because the inequality between the better off and the worse off has been reduced. Your task is to compare the two versions of prioritarianism as answers to the leveling-down objection. Address each of the following. First, is absolute prioritarianism strongly or only weakly leveling-down-proof, and can relative prioritarianism fail even weak leveling-down-proofness? Second, consider a two-person case in which A is at 100 and B is at 50. Policy L lowers A to 60 and leaves B at 50. Policy U raises B to 90 and leaves A at 100. Policy L* lowers A to 60, leaves B at 50, and thereby changes B's relative position. How does each version rank L, U, and L*? Third, can relative prioritarianism avoid the leveling-down objection without collapsing into egalitarianism, and can absolute prioritarianism accommodate the intuition that inequality itself matters without turning into relative prioritarianism? Fourth, which version better explains why leveling down strikes many people as objectionable in the first place? Do not supply a bibliography, reading list, or citations; argue from the definitions given. Conclude by choosing which version of prioritarianism is more defensible for someone who takes the leveling-down objection seriously. End with a single final line beginning Verdict: followed by a one-sentence verdict.
