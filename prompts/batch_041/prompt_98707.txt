I'm on the board of our neighborhood food co-op. Nine of us, all volunteers, and our bylaws say every motion gets decided by a plain majority, one motion at a time, no bundling, and once a motion is settled it cant come back up at the same meeting. Last Tuesday we voted on three things that I'm only now realizing were all tangled up together, and I'm the one who has to read the results out to the membership on Thursday, and I honestly dont know whether what we decided even makes sense.

The three motions were:

1. The co-op should start carrying fresh produce.
2. If the co-op starts carrying fresh produce, it has to raise member dues.
3. The co-op should raise member dues.

The votes came out like this:

- Motion 1: 7 yes, 2 no
- Motion 2: 6 yes, 3 no
- Motion 3: 4 yes, 5 no

So we said yes to produce, yes to the idea that produce means higher dues, and no to higher dues. And the weird part is that every single one of us voted in a way that fits with the other two things we each voted for -- nobody's being a hypocrite on their own. It's only the board as a whole that's talking nonsense.

The two members who voted no on everything are saying the whole vote was a sham and we should scrap it. A newer member says the fix is obvious: motion 3 is really just what motions 1 and 2 add up to, so we should treat it as passed too, since a majority approved the things it follows from. But the people who voted no on 3 say that's exactly the trick -- you cant let a majority on two side questions overrule the actual majority on the question that matters, which is whether we raise dues.

One more thing. A friend who deals with this kind of stuff for a living suggested we amend the bylaws so that whenever this happens, the decision just defaults to the status quo (nothing changes) until the next meeting. That sounds tidy, but I cant tell if it's a real fix or if it just hands a veto to whoever's against the change.

Our bylaws wont let us redo a motion in the same meeting, so I cant just call a do-over. What I want to know is: is one of these procedures actually more defensible than the others, or is it just pick-your-poison? And whatever you tell me, what am I supposed to say to the membership on Thursday?
