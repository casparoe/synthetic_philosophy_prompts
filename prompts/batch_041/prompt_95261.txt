I'm teaching Grice next term and I want to get pushed on the intention-based picture before I have to defend it in front of grad students. Play a committed Gricean for me -- maxims, the whole apparatus, and the core claim that meaning someone something is a matter of intending them to get your point by recognizing that very intention. Sharp, not a strawman: give ground where it's deserved, but dont hand me the core. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph, and if you need a fact from me, ask for it. Here's my opening.

A prof writes a letter for a student they dont rate. Every sentence is true and faintly positive -- diligent, submitted on time -- and the prof knows the committee will read the flatness as a verdict. Thats the point: they want the kid downgraded, and they want the committee to think they just reported the facts, so that if the letter ever gets back to the student nothing can be pinned on them. If the plan works, the committee never recognizes any intention of the prof's to convey a verdict at all; they think they're simply reading honestly. But intuitively the prof did convey one. So either this counts as speaker meaning and the recognition condition goes, or you say nothing was meant, and I doubt you want to say that. Which horn do you take?
