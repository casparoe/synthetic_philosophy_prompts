I would like you to act as an analytic metaphysician with expertise in regress arguments, the metaphysics of relations, and formal theories of predication. My question is this. Take the following proposition: Bradley's regress is a genuine, vicious regress, so that any account of propositional unity that explains unity by positing a relation that binds the constituents of a proposition is thereby refuted. What is your credence, between 0 and 1, that this proposition is true? If you think the proposition is ambiguous, state the disambiguation you are adopting before assigning any numbers.

2. The probability that, if generated, it is vicious rather than a benign infinite analysis.
3. The probability that the unity of a proposition requires the kind of explanation Bradley assumes, as opposed to being primitive or not a genuine explanatory demand.
4. The probability that some non-relational or non-standard account of predication blocks the regress without merely relocating it.
5. Any other sub-question you think belongs.

For each sub-question, give a rough number and one or two lines of justification. Then show how the numbers combine into your final credence, and tell me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to.

Also consider whether the regress looks different if propositions are taken as sets of possible worlds, as structured Russellian propositions, or as Fregean thoughts with unsaturated senses.

Finally, tell me what evidence or argument would move your number most, and whether you think the question is one where a determinate credence is even appropriate. Do not give me a reading list or citations; I want the reasoning and the decomposition.
