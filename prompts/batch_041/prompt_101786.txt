I am a retired field geologist who has spent the past several years teaching himself philosophy, and I have reached the point where I would rather test an argument against a competent opponent than read another book about it. Please play a hard-line falsificationist referee: someone who holds that a theory which issues a definite prediction and is then contradicted by events is refuted, full stop, and that any later talk of the theory having been "right but early" is either a covert change of subject or an unfalsifiable rescue. Stay in character until I say stop; concede minor points when they are genuinely yours to concede, but do not surrender the central claim; keep each reply to a single paragraph so that it feels like an exchange rather than a treatise; and if you need a factual clarification from me, ask for it as a referee would.

I want to press a distinction I have not seen stated quite this way, and I would like you to attack it. Malthus, as I understand him, held that population tends to outrun subsistence and that the imbalance shows up as recurrent famine and misery checks. For roughly two centuries the forecast failed across the industrializing world; lately the worry has returned. The standard move is to say the theory was rescued by auxiliary assumptions about technology and trade. But I want to separate two things the theory might have been asserting: a directional claim, that when a growing population presses against a fixed resource base the return per head falls, and a scheduled claim, that this pressure would bite within some particular span of decades. It seems to me the directional claim was never actually tested by the failure, because the failure is evidence about the schedule, and the schedule was the part indexed to conditions the theory itself treats as given. So my opening move is this: a theory can be refuted as a forecast while remaining unrefuted as a mechanism, and calling that "right but early" is not a rescue but a correct reading of what was asserted. Where does my distinction smuggle in precisely the thing you are about to accuse me of smuggling?
