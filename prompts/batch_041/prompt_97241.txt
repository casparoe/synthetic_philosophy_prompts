I keep running into the same explanation for why time has a direction: it's entropy, and entropy is like a room getting messier. Nobody's room tidies itself, the story goes, so mess only ever piles up, and thats the arrow of time — we remember the tidy past because it was tidier, nothing more mysterious than that. A book I'm reading uses this to say the arrow of time isn't a deep fact about the world at all, just a fact about probability and our ignorance.

Can you audit that analogy for me? I want the correspondence laid out (what's the room, what's the mess, what plays "tidy," what plays the cleaning), then the places the mapping breaks. I have a nagging feeling "messy" is doing way more work than it's allowed to, and that a room only looks irreversible because we've already smuggled in that rooms start out tidy. I even filmed my kitchen getting dirty and played it backwards, and of course the backwards version looks wrong — but my roommate says that's only because I know kitchens don't self-clean, not because the physics forbids it, and I can't tell if hes right.

Then the real question: does the conclusion actually follow — that the arrow is just probability and ignorance, not something deep — or was the analogy quietly carrying a conclusion it can't support? And if it can't, is there a better picture to reach for?
