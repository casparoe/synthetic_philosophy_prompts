I'm in my final year and taking a course on decision theory this term, which may be why the following has been bothering me. I would like an outside view on an argument a friend has been pressing, and on what I should actually do.

I have applied for a funded place on a programme. The results are released on a fixed date three weeks from now; everything is already submitted, there is no appeal, and the alternative offer I would be weighing against it does not fall due until after the release date. Nothing I do in the meantime can change the outcome. A friend who works in the office that handles these applications has offered to look up my result now and tell me in confidence. What he can see is a preliminary ranking rather than the final decision, but he says it is usually accurate.

My roommate thinks I should take him up on it. Information is free, she argues, and free information can only help: whatever it says, I can still do everything I would have done without it, and if the news is bad I get three weeks to absorb it instead of three minutes. When I objected that I would simply be miserable for three weeks, she said I could settle in advance what I would do in either case, write it down, and only then look, so that the news could not change anything.

That still strikes me as wrong, but I cannot say why. What I keep returning to is that I cannot un-know it: if I read that I was turned down, I am not the person who did not know, and there is no version of the next three weeks in which I get to behave as though I had never read it. She replies that this is merely a taste for being ignorant — a taste for having false beliefs — which no reasonable person should have.

Should I look? And is there a flaw in her argument, or in mine?
