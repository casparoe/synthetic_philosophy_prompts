I'd like a short story about a woman who volunteers at a farm-animal sanctuary and slowly gets talked into the "crop deaths" argument — the idea that harvesting grain and soy kills more wild animals (mice, birds, the insects that go through the combine) than raising one big cow does, so that eating beef might actually kill fewer animals than eating tofu. Shes an actuary by training, so she tries to actually do the arithmetic instead of just trading slogans. I want the story to turn on the fact that the whole comparison hangs on choices she can't make non-arbitrarily: whether insects count, whether you count deaths or suffering-years, whether an animal killed by a harvester counts the same as one killed on purpose, and whether the pasture the cow grazes on would have been cropland otherwise. Work in the beef-vs-chicken angle too — one cow covers hundreds of meals, one chicken covers a few — and let her notice that this cuts against her gut in a second direction. Don't resolve it neatly. End somewhere honest, like her at a dinner table unable to say what she wants or why. Maybe 1500 words, and no lectures — the ideas should come out of the scene and her notebook, not out of anyone speechifying.
