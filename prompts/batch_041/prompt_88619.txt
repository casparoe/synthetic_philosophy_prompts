Assume for the sake of the question that we have the following thesis in hand. Call it No-Desert: no one ever non-instrumentally deserves the distributive share they get, because desert requires that the agent be the ultimate author of the traits and choices for which they are credited, and no agent is. I want your credence in No-Desert, decomposed.

To keep this from collapsing into the standard free-will-and-desert slog, run it against a case I haven't seen discussed much. Two workers produce identical output through what each experiences as effortful, self-directed industry. In the first, that industry is the upshot of a long, costly regimen the worker deliberately chose and stuck with. In the second, the same felt resolve and the same output are produced by a device implanted in infancy, of which the worker is unaware and which, from the inside, is indistinguishable from the first worker's discipline. If you find that the second worker deserves nothing, or less, that suggests our desert judgments track causal origin rather than the quality of the present action or the felt effort -- which is the pivot No-Desert turns on.

So: what's your credence in No-Desert? Decompose it -- the probability that desert requires ultimate self-authorship, the probability that no one has it, the probability that some compatibilist notion of responsibility grounds desert anyway, the probability that the origin-indifference reading of the case is wrong and present facts can ground desert, and whatever other terms belong. Give a number and one line for each, then the combination. Then tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to and what argument or evidence would move it most. A friend puts No-Desert at 0.85 and I'm nearer 0.4; tell me where our disagreement most likely lives.
