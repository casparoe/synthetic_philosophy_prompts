Take the role of a philosopher of physics and walk me through this carefully. The part I most want nailed down: the move from 'simultaneity is relative' to 'the future already exists' feels like a trick, but I can't see the seam — where does the argument have genuine teeth, and where is it sleight of hand?

Keep it readable for a novelist, but don't flatten the philosophy. And be honest about your own footing: tell me where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong, and what would change your mind about any of the verdicts.
