A colleague of mine defends a package of claims about time with an analogy: entropy is like shuffling a deck of cards. A fresh deck in order is one arrangement among 52!, and so is any shuffle you deal out; "disorder" therefore names nothing in the world, only our coarse-grained description of it, and the second law reduces to the near-truism that a large system is far likelier to be found in a common-looking arrangement than a rare one. From this she draws two conclusions: that the direction of time is a matter of our ignorance rather than a fact about the world, and that the usual puzzle about why the early universe was in such a low-entropy state dissolves, since "low entropy" at the beginning just means "an arrangement we would not have predicted." Please audit the analogy. Set out the correspondence between the cards and the thermodynamics explicitly, mark where the mapping breaks, separate the disanalogies that matter for her two conclusions from those that do not, and say whether either conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table — and if it turns out the analogy is carrying the argument rather than merely illustrating it, propose a better one.
