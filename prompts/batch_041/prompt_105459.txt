Please argue that the Liar paradox is best resolved by treating truth as a norm governing assertion rather than as a property of propositions. Defend a distinction between a proposition's truth and the warrant for asserting it, and use that distinction to address a case I will call the normative Liar: a sentence that asserts of itself that it is not warranted for assertion. Explain whether this is a genuine paradox for the normative view, what logical principles the view must give up, and why the resulting position is preferable to rival treatments that give up bivalence or relativize truth to context. I am looking for a constructive argument, not a survey.
