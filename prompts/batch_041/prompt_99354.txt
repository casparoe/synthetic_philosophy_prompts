I'm a backend engineer with zero formal philosophy training, but a friend of mine (self-taught, reads a lot of rationalist blogs) dropped a numbered argument on me in a group chat and now I cant stop thinking about it. He claims it proves morality is baseless and I can't find the hole. Here's his argument, verbatim from the chat:

(1) A valid argument can't hand you a concept that wasn't already in the premises.
(2) A moral conclusion contains the concept ought.
(3) Purely factual premises contain only is-concepts.
(4) So no moral conclusion can follow from purely factual premises. [he calls this the is-ought gap]
(5) So any argument for a moral conclusion needs at least one moral premise.
(6) If that premise is more than a bare assumption, it needs an argument of its own.
(7) By (4), that argument needs a moral premise too.
(8) So every moral argument either bottoms out in an unsupported assumption or goes on forever.
(9) So no moral belief is really justified -- morality is just arbitrary commitment.
(10) If it's arbitrary, then "you shouldn't torture a kid for fun" is just a preference, same status as liking vanilla ice cream.
(11) But nobody who thinks torturing kids for fun is wrong thinks that's all it is.
(12) So some step is broken. He says it can't be (1), because (1) is just logic.

I want to keep (11) -- I really don't think torture-is-wrong is just a taste. So: which step do I give up, why did it look fine when I read it, and what do I actually pay for giving it up? And be honest with me about whether his chain is valid and the real cost is hiding somewhere else, versus there being a genuine flaw in one of the steps.
