I work on reinforcement learning and AI safety and have a decent working command of the standard apparatus — MDPs, policies, action masking, reward shaping, discounting, the β–δ model of time-inconsistent preferences, safe interruptibility, corrigibility — but only a layperson's grasp of the political and legal theory of precommitment. I would like you to translate the debate over Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report.

Start with the core structure as the philosophers and constitutional theorists state it: an agent — an individual like Ulysses before the Sirens, or a people constituting a state — deliberately restricts its own future options, or raises the cost of certain future actions, in order to make its later, predictably wayward self do what its present self judges best. Render that as precisely as the RL framing allows. Say which parts are best modeled as a change to the action space (masking, hard constraints), which as a change to the reward or transition structure (making defection costly), which as a change to the effective discount factor, and which as an interruption or interruptibility problem of the sort discussed under safe interruptibility and corrigibility. Distinguish, in my terms, the case where the bound self could in principle undo the binding if it wanted to — the key is in the box — from the case where it genuinely cannot, and tell me whether the classical debate turns on that distinction or quietly ignores it.

Then the residue report, which is the part I care about most. Tell me what does not survive the translation. I suspect at least three things have no counterpart in my vocabulary, and I want you to say whether I am right and, if so, whether the loss is a defect of my framework or a sign that the political concepts were doing work that is unclear. First, a constitution binds not one agent across time but a plurality of agents — constituent power binding ordinary politics, one generation binding another — so that the precommitment is as much between persons as within one. Second, there is the question of who enforces the binding, when the enforcer is itself part of the thing being bound. Third, there is the whole normative dimension of whether self-binding is an exercise of autonomy or a violation of it, which seems to have no grip on an agent whose preferences are just a reward function to be maximized.

Be as committed as the material allows: where the translation is clean, assert it; where you have to fudge, mark the fudge and say why. And if my framing is confused — for instance, if the RL picture quietly presupposes that the precommitting and the precommitted agent are one agent with a single utility function, which is exactly what is in dispute — say so plainly rather than translating politely around it. I would rather be told that the task as posed cannot be done well than receive a smooth translation that hides the seams.
