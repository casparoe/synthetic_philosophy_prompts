I'm in a metaphysics reading group and there is a move that keeps recurring, and I have started to suspect that it is a specific thing with a specific name that I simply do not know. Here is the pattern. Someone proposes an answer to the question of when several things add up to a single thing — for instance, "when they are in contact", or "when together they do something that none of them does alone", or "always, however scattered". Someone else objects that the proposed line is arbitrary: it draws a boundary where nothing in the world marks one. The proposer concedes that no criterion could avoid drawing a boundary somewhere, and concludes that the charge of arbitrariness therefore has no force, since every answer must stop somewhere and so none can be faulted merely for stopping. So far, so deflationary. But then the very same people go on arguing as though their criterion were the correct one and the others simply mistaken: they press objections, they write up replies, they speak of getting it right. And when you push them, they say that they are not claiming their answer is privileged, only that it is the most useful, or the one that best fits our ordinary practices.

What I cannot decide is whether this is a coherent position, an unstable halfway house, or an instance of something well known under a name I lack. Is there an established term for the stance — a view that the question has no answer privileged over its rivals, held by people who nevertheless continue to treat one answer as privileged? And how does it relate to, and differ from, the nearest neighbours I can think of: the claim that the whole dispute is merely verbal; straight relativism about the question; and quietism, which I take to be the recommendation that we stop asking? I do not want the label for its own sake. I want to know what to call the thing, where its edges are — whether it is a stable position, a self-undermining one, or a disguise for ordinary partisanship — and which of the neighbours, if any, it actually turns out to be. If nothing established fits, then coin a term, define it, and tell me which cases fall inside it and which outside.
