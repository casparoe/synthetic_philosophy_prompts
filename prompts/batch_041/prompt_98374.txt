In my statistical mechanics course, the instructor repeatedly summarizes the resolution of Maxwell's demon with the phrase 'the demon is defeated by the cost of forgetting.' Last week he used it to dismiss a question about whether a demon equipped with a reversible computer and finite memory could still sort molecules. I can distinguish several possible readings of the slogan: that measurement itself has a thermodynamic cost; that logically irreversible erasure has a cost; that any demon must at some point dump correlations into the environment; or that the second law is saved only by a particular accounting of memory. Could you lay out the precise claims the slogan could be making -- what kind of cost, paid by what, at which stage of the demon's cycle -- and say for each whether it is a theorem, a contingent feature of current technology, or a contested interpretation? I would especially like to know which reading, if any, actually supports my instructor's dismissal of the reversible-computer demon. Please end with a short note separating what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on the physics of information, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
