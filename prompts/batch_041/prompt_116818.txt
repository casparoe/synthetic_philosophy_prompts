I am the instructor for a graduate seminar in philosophical theology and modal metaphysics — mixed MA and PhD enrolment, all of them fluent in first-order modal logic, none of them trained in higher-order logic. The final examination is a single long essay question worth 30 marks, to be written in sixty minutes. Students may bring the printed axiom sheet into the room, so the question paper reproduces the entire formal system in its stem; nothing else is permitted. I need three deliverables from you: the examination question itself, a model answer of roughly 900 words, and a marking scheme.

The subject is Gödel's modal ontological proof. I want to avoid the two essays I receive every year — one reciting the Kantian dictum that existence is not a real predicate, the other summarising the familiar contrast between Anselm, Descartes and Plantinga's maximal-greatness argument. Both can be produced without engaging the formalism at all. The question must therefore be fought out inside the system, and it must require work that no summary of the literature will supply. Assume the candidate has read nothing beyond the stem.

Print the following in the stem, keeping my numbering and my labels, prefaced by one sentence of orientation: the language is second-order modal logic over a constant non-empty domain, the underlying modal system is S5, and the higher-order quantifiers range over properties in the broadest sense, with no restriction to natural, intrinsic, or otherwise privileged properties.

Primitive: P(φ), read as φ is positive.
A1. P(φ) ∧ □∀x(φ(x) → ψ(x)) → P(ψ).
A2. P(¬φ) ↔ ¬P(φ).
A3. P(G), where G(x) ↔ ∀φ(P(φ) → φ(x)).
A4. P(φ) → □P(φ).
D1. φ ess x ↔ [φ(x) ∧ ∀ψ(ψ(x) → □∀y(φ(y) → ψ(y)))].
D2. NE(x) ↔ ∀φ(φ ess x → □∃y φ(y)).
A5. P(NE).
T1. P(φ) → ◇∃x φ(x).
T2. □∃x G(x).
Lemma L. If x is Godlike, then G is an essence of x. (Proof sketch to print: any property a Godlike being has must be positive, since otherwise its negation would be positive by A2 and would also be had by that being; so by A4 the entailment required by D1 is necessary.)

Print the following objection as a numbered derivation, attributing it to no one and presenting it as a piece of reasoning the class must assess rather than as established doctrine:

O1. Suppose x is Godlike.
O2. By L, G is an essence of x.
O3. Let p be any true proposition, and let ψp be the property λy.p — the property that everything has if p is true and nothing has if p is false.
O4. Since p is true, ψp(x).
O5. By O2, O4 and D1, □∀y(G(y) → ψp(y)).
O6. By T2, □∃y G(y); hence □∃y ψp(y), that is, □p.
O7. Since p was arbitrary, p → □p for every proposition p.

The question must require four things of the candidate. First, locate the burden of proof: show which of the results above are already available from A1–A3 and which require A4, D1, D2 and A5, and explain why the theist is not obliged to supply a separate possibility premise. Second, assess the derivation O1–O7 step by step, saying whether it is valid as it stands and, if it is, exactly which licence it exploits. Third, evaluate at least two repairs, one of which must be the restriction of the higher-order quantifiers so that properties such as ψp fall outside their range, and say what each repair costs elsewhere in the proof — which result it endangers and why. Fourth, say whether the collapse shows that the axioms are inconsistent, and what a theist who accepts the axioms owes us about contingency.

Write the question in the register of a real examination paper, with the four parts marked out and their marks summing to 30. Neither the question nor the model answer may demand citations, reading lists, or attributions, and neither may depend on when the proof was written or where it was published; if the model answer needs a name at all, restrict it to Gödel, Anselm, Descartes, Kant and Plantinga. Do not put anything in quotation marks unless you are certain of it, and do not quote any text.

The model answer should be a candidate's answer, not a lecture: it should carry out the derivations it appeals to, use the numbering above, and take a position on the third and fourth parts rather than surveying options. The marking scheme should consist of five to seven must-hit points, each with its marks, each with one line on how candidates typically lose those marks, and a closing note on what separates a first-class answer from a solid second-class one given that both will contain most of the same material.
