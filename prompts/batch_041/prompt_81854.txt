Build me a case rather than a description of one. The pattern to instantiate is this: several agents can each independently undertake some action, it suffices for the action to be undertaken that one of them decides to, and it makes no difference how many act; because each decision rests on an unbiased but noisy estimate of the action's value, the action is undertaken exactly when at least one estimate is unusually high. The result is that the group undertakes actions that are worse than they look, with no bias, misinformation, or disagreement among the agents. (This is sometimes called the unilateralist's curse.)

Specifications: three or four agents; ordinary stakes, nothing catastrophic; all of them rational, with the same evidence and the same values, and common knowledge of that; the action is in fact worth not doing, and the error traces to the decision structure rather than to any individual's mistake; and each agent's decision to act is nonetheless defensible on her own information, so that no one is plainly to blame.

Then, in order: (1) show that the case meets each specification, supplying whatever numbers or estimates you need, and state the ex ante correct decision; (2) give the verdict of three procedures — act whenever your own estimate clears your threshold; act only when the pooled evidence supports it; act only if you would be content for every agent in your position to act on the same rule — and say which gets it right, and why; (3) identify the feature doing the work (dispersion of estimates, number of agents, the threshold, or the unilateral structure), then neutralize it and say whether the pattern survives with everything else held fixed; (4) say how a defender of the first procedure would attack the case, and whether the attack lands.

If the specifications cannot be jointly satisfied — for instance, if an agent who knows the structure would already discount for the selection effect, so that no defensible individual decision remains — say so plainly and explain the obstruction, rather than manufacturing a case that only appears to meet them.
