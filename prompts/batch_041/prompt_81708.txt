I've wangled a slot at my uni's cognitive science reading group next month and I need to prep for the Q&A rather than for the talk itself. What I'm defending: the whole "is it an agent or just a simulator" fight is a false dichotomy. Agency, I want to argue, isn't a property of the weights at all — it's a property of the loop (weights + scaffolding + user + deployment context), so the same checkpoint can be a genuine agent in one set-up and a glorified autocomplete in another, and asking which one it "really" is is like asking whether a river is a person. There are three claims the two camps keep sliding between and I want to prise them apart: behavioural parity, functional role, and being the right kind of thing to have a mind. The bit I'm most likely to get nailed on is my proposed test, which I call prompt-invariant commitments: perturb the persona, the framing and the stakes as hard as you like and see whether anything the system does survives. If nothing survives, I say there's no agent worth the name in there. That's my load-bearing beam.
