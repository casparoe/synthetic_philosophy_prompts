ok so context: i'm a backend engineer, zero formal philosophy training, but i joined a reading group thing and we've been chewing on egalitarianism for like four months and i'm goign a little nuts.

the shape of it, as best i can tell. there's the luck egalitarian crowd: the thing equality really cares about is that nobody ends up worse off because of brute bad luck, only because of their own choices. dworkin's insurance-market picture, cohen's version, arneson's, the whole family. and then there's anderson's crowd, the relational egalitarians, who say no, equality is about people standing in relations of equality with each other -- no oppression, no being looked down on, able to look others in the eye -- and that luck egalitarianism is actually a pretty cruel doctrine that basically tells the imprudent and the severely disabled that they're on their own, and that it misses the point of equality entirely.

every meeting, both sides talk past each other. relational people say "you've made the imprudent guy's own inferiority the center of the theory," luck people say "fine, we can bolt on a social-relations clause, but the core is still the distribution of brute-luck-caused disadvantage," someone brings up the disability case, someone else says "thats about domination, not distribution," and we circle. nobody has moved an inch.

my working theory (as a hypothesis, i'm not sure) is that these aren't two theories of the same thing at all. one is answering "what makes an outcome unjust?" and the other is answering "what does it mean for people to relate as equals?", and the word "equality" is doing all the work of making them look like rivals. so i want a diagnosis, not a survey of who said what: is this verbal, or is it a real substantive disagreement underneath -- about whether the imprudent are owed anything, or about whether relations between people can even be described without talking about who has what? if it is the two-different-questions thing, is there any principled way to say which question the word "equality" should belong to, or is that just a fight over a label? and does the leveling-down objection belong to this same confusion or is it a genuinely separate problem -- because every time someone raises it the conversation definately dies. basically: what kind of disagreement is this, and what would count as actual progress, if anything.
