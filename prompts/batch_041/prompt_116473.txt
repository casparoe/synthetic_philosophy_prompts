I am preparing a seminar on whether moral status admits of degrees, and I want a case built to order rather than borrowed from the literature. Construct a thought experiment with three candidate entities, at least one biological and at least one artificial, engineered so that three positions give genuinely different verdicts: a one-dimensional gradualism that ranks entities by the quantity of valenced experience alone; a two-dimensional gradualism that treats the quantity of valenced experience and its temporal integration — whether there is a persisting subject with interests extending into its own future — as independent axes; and a threshold view on which nothing below a cut-off matters morally at all and everything above it matters equally. The case must yield at least one rank reversal between the two gradualisms, and must place at least one entity on the other side of the threshold from where both gradualisms would put it.

Constraints: the entities' relevant features are stipulated in the scenario rather than inferred from what we in fact know about insects, cephalopods or language models; no exotic physics; the divergence turns on a single variable rather than a bundle of differences; and the biological/artificial distinction does no work.

Then state the verdict each position gives and why, identify the feature doing the work, say what becomes of the case when that feature is held fixed across the entities, and explain how a defender of the worst-served position would argue that the scenario is rigged. The exercise tests whether gradation is one-dimensional, so a case that stipulates the ordering rather than generating it from the theories fails.
