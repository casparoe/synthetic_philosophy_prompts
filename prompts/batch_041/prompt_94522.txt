Although I have no academic background, I have been trying to think through the dispute over the color phi phenomenon. In the standard demonstration, two dots of different colors are flashed in different positions in quick succession, and at the right interval observers report a single dot moving and changing color in mid-flight, before the second dot has appeared. One camp, the Orwellians, holds that the experience itself occurs in the normal order and is then edited in memory or report; the other, the Stalinesques, holds that the experience itself is delayed and rewritten before it ever becomes conscious. Draft the joint statement that a committed Orwellian and a committed Stalinesque could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims they agree on about the phenomenon and about what any theory must explain; the precise points where they still disagree, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and, for each crux, the experiment, argument, or case both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Include a section on whether any part of their dispute is merely verbal, and if so which part. Finally, consider a variant I have not seen discussed: the observer is probed for the midpoint color at delays that fall before the second flash would have appeared; state what each side predicts and whether the variant would separate their views. Format the result as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
