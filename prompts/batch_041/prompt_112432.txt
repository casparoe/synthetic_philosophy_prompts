I'm drafting a position statement on the precautionary principle for a policy workshop and want a consistency check before I circulate it. Here is what I hold.

(1) Where there is a threat of serious or irreversible harm, lack of full scientific certainty is not a reason to postpone cost-effective measures against it.
(2) The only defensible rule for deciding under uncertainty is to maximize expected value using the best subjective probabilities available; every alternative is either expected value in disguise or a rule that whoever sets the framing can exploit.
(3) Uncertainty about harms is asymmetric: I would rather wrongly restrict a technology than wrongly wave it through, so the burden of proof belongs on whoever wants to introduce it.
(4) I reject status quo bias. A risk we already tolerate gets no discount relative to a novel one, and inaction is a choice that counts exactly as much as action.
(5) Where the probability distribution over damages has a sufficiently heavy tail, expected value alone licenses paying almost any price to reduce the risk, so precaution and (2) converge more than people admit.
(6) Given the same weak evidence, a regulator should restrict a chemical but should not withhold approval of a drug: the people who die because a treatment is withheld are identifiable and here now, whereas the chemical's victims are diffuse and statistical.

Audit this. Which commitments are in genuine tension, and how severe is each tension; which pairs only appear to conflict; and what is the cheapest way to make the set consistent, together with what that repair would cost me. Please rank the tensions rather than treating them all as equally bad.
