I want a position of mine put under real pressure, and I would rather have the strongest case against it than a sympathetic hearing. The position has three parts. First: in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma — no repetition, no reputation, no observers, no anticipated future dealings — defection is rationally required, and there is no sense in which a solitary agent who defects is making a mistake. Second: it follows that everything resembling one-shot cooperation among human beings is either a repeated game in disguise (kinship, reputation, anticipated dealings, third-party observation) or a psychological adaptation built for repeated play misfiring in a setting it was never calibrated for. Third: for the iterated and evolutionary cases the received story is essentially complete — error-tolerant reciprocity, together with assortment, meaning disproportionate interaction with those who resemble you or who have treated you well, accounts for the evolution of cooperation without invoking group selection or genuinely altruistic preferences.

The part I would most like tested is a distinction I now state more sharply than I have seen it stated. Call a shadow any channel by which my action can influence yours, causal or merely evidential, present or future. I claim that cooperation tracks the total shadow, and that the evidential component is not a channel at all but an artifact of mistaking correlation for control. If that is right, my second claim stands: apparent one-shot cooperation is never rationally required, and the only real question is where the causal shadow is coming from.

So what would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked by how much they should actually move me, with decisive separated from merely awkward, and with an eye to what the position predicts that its rivals do not. Specifically: what would have to be true for the evidential shadow to be a genuine channel, and what would the decision theory and the world have to look like under each answer? Which formal results about what can and cannot evolve in the absence of repetition or assortment genuinely bite, and which go quiet once I am allowed error-tolerant strategies and assortative matching? Which laboratory findings on one-shot anonymous giving, and which results on noise in iterated play, are awkward for me rather than absorbable by the misfiring clause? Tell me, too, which of these a defender of my view would concede as counting against it rather than dismiss as question-begging. And does that misfiring clause risk anything at all — is there a finding that would force me to give it up rather than absorb it, or have I insulated the position past the point where evidence can reach it? If the latter, say whether that is a defect in this kind of position or simply its shape.

For what it is worth, what would move me most is a single case in which the causal shadow is provably absent, the evidential shadow is absent too by construction, and cooperation is nonetheless both rational and not explicable as an adaptation misfire.
