I'm TAing the first meeting of a metaphysics seminar on material constitution next week and I want to run it off a decision tree instead of the usual list of isms — last year I handed out the list of positions and everyone just matched lables to the vibes they walked in with. Can you build me the tree?

Start it from a judgment I don't think anyone in the room will give up: the sculptor squashes the statue back into a lump, and the clay is still there while the statue isn't. From there, every branch node should be a question a student can answer without already knowing where it leads — about whether 'still there' and 'the same thing as before' can come apart, whether counting is relative to the sortal you're counting under, whether the number of things at a place can be settled without vocabulary that already presupposes an answer, whether two things can share every part and every location, whether surviving a change of shape is essential to one candidate and accidental to the other.

The leaves should be the positions, named where there's a standard name and described in a sentence where there isn't: coincident objects with one constituting the other, the 'only the clay is real' line (with 'statue' as a phase sortal or a façon de parler), the nihilist view that there are no composite objects at all, four-dimensionalism where the two are different sums of temporal parts, counting-relative-to-sortals, and the annihilation view where the lump stops existing the moment the statue begins. Mark which combinations of answers nobody occupies, and flag any pair of leaves my tree can't actually seperate — I suspect the relative-counting view and the one-thing-two-sortals view collapse into each other on these nodes, and I'd rather be told than pretend.

Two cases to bake in. First, the tree has to handle something that isn't the usual clay: a hedge trained for years into a topiary shape, then let grow out (or clipped into a different animal) — the plant clearly survives both, the topiary probably doesn't survive either, and neither is a mere mass of matter. Second, an ice sculpture that melts overnight and refreezes into a shape nobody made — same sculpture or not, and does it matter that the shape came back by accident? I want the nodes to earn their keep on both.

No reading list please, I just want the tree and the diagnoses.
