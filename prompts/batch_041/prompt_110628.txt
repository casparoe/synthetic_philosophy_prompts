I've been messing around with typogenetics, Hofstadter's toy system from GEB, and I want to watch two people argue about the claim he makes for it. Quick recap of the toy, in case it's fuzzy: bases A/C/G/T, strand is a string of them, you read it off in adjacent duplets and each duplet is an instruction — the A-somethings give you cut/del/swi, the C-somethings mvr/mvl/cop/off, the G-somethings the four inserts (ina/inc/ing/int), the T-somethings the four searches (nearest purine or pyrimidine, left or right), and AA is just punctuation meaning end of enzyme. A strand translates into a sequence of amino acids; each amino acid kinks the enzyme left, right, or not at all; and where the first and last kinks leave it pointing decides which base the finished enzyme binds to. Enzymes walk along strands and edit them. Some strands, when you translate them and run the resulting enzymes, produce a copy of themselves — self-reps. If any detail is hazy in your head, fill it in however you like, just keep it consistent with itself and don't fuss about matching the book.

Now the argument. My friend thinks there's a genuine isomorphism between this and Gödel numbering — not a vibe, a structural identity: a code that maps a system's own objects into operators on those objects, a fixed point, and (he'd add) an unavoidable blind spot, since no strand can encode the ribosome and folding rules that give its enzymes their meaning. I think it's a suggestive analogy that falls apart the moment you press it. Gödel's construction needs negation and a provability predicate that's definable inside the object language; typogenetics has no sentences, no truth values, and the translation table is an external given — in the toy the player does the ribosome's job. What a self-rep really is is a fixed point of an iterative map. That's a quine, recursion-theorem territory, and quines aren't Gödel sentences.

Write that as a dialogue between the two of us at a whiteboard, a page or so, no citations or reading lists, just the mechanics and the argument. Neither of us is a strawman and we actually answer each other rather than talking past each other. Have him press me on whether the diagonal argument really needs negation at all, and on whether "no strand can model the machinery that reads it" is a theorem or just an artifact of how the toy was set up. Have me press him on the externality of the ribosome, and on whether self-reps are forced by the rules or are just accidents of which strands happen to exist in the soup.

Then let one of us float a modification and see whether the isomorphism survives it: suppose the folding rule itself — the thing that turns kink-shape into binding preference — were encodable in a strand, so an enzyme could rewrite the very rule that decides what enzymes mean. Does that bring the picture closer to the real thing, machinery now internal and the system able to tinker with its own rules of inference, or does it just show the whole comparison was a metaphor, since the modified system can do something Gödel's can't, namely change the rules mid-proof? End with the disagreement pinned down: what exactly are we disagreeing about, and how much of it is verbal.
