Make them sharp and a little impatient, the kind of person who thinks "so where exactly does it die?" is a confused question. Here's the case that's bugging me. Say the clinic does it exactly as advertised, one neuron at a time -- except the chips don't get their state by reading the neuron they're about to replace. There was one scan at the very start, and the machine just runs a model of you forward from that scan; every new chip is initialized from the model, not from the living tissue it's displacing. The wrinkle is that once the last neuron is gone the model keeps running on a server in the basement, and the chips in the skull are slaved to it -- they're basically output devices. So which one am I? If they say the body, I want to know why the thing actually generating the states doesn't count as me; if they say the server, then what carries me forward is a representation of me rather than me, and I don't see why a representation gets to inherit the person -- it starts to look like I died in the chair and something has been impersonating me ever since. My guess is they'll say this is a cheap trick and not real gradual replacement, and maybe they're right, but I want them to actually work for it instead of just ruling it out.

And when I say stop, drop the character and tell me straight: what would have to be shown, or what argument would I have to make, for the replacement-preserves-you view to actually be wrong -- and where is its own reasoning most likely to break down?
