I'm a phd student and I've been stuck in this seminar on epistemic logic where we spent what felt like six weeks on logical omniscience and nobody in the room thought any of the standard fixes actually works, and honestly the literature reads the same way to me — people have been proposing solutions for decades (impossible worlds, awareness parameters, just dropping closure, neighborhood semantics, procedural or syntactic models, treating beliefs as representations instead of propositions) and each one seems to get run through the same two horns: either you keep closure somewhere in the machinery and the agent is still omniscient in some form, or you give it up and now the agent can believe P and believe P implies Q and still not believe Q, which looks like you're no longer modelling belief at all, just bookkeeping about sentences. I don't want another survey of the approaches, I want a diagnosis of why this thing keeps stalling and what kind of disagreement it really is.

My working hypothesis, tell me if it's right: it isn't really one problem, and the camps are answering different questions under a single heading. One side is asking what an ideally rational agent's belief state has to look like as a normative matter, where omniscience is arguably just a consequence of what rationality means rather than a bug to fix; the other is asking how to describe an actual finite reasoner that has to compute, and the word 'belief' is quietly doing duty for both. And underneath that there might be a methodological fork — whether the formalism should model propositions (semantic) or sentences/representations (syntactic) — such that omniscience appears or vanishes depending on which you pick, so people end up talking past each other without noticing. If that's the shape of it, is the impasse basically benign (two different projects, so stop expecting one theory to satisfy both) or is there a real substantive disagreement still sitting there once you pry them apart? And if my diagnosis is wrong and it's something else — a genuinely unsolved formal problem, say, or a live disagreement about whether closure is even a constraint on beleif rather than on something else — then say so, and tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, or just a decision to change the question.
