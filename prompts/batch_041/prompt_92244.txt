ok so this is a bit of a weird one. i got into an argument with my flatmate (he's a maths person, thinks everything bottoms out in axioms, we were a bottle of wine in) about whether logic needs foundations, and i ended up dragging in the tortoise and achilles thing, and now i can't stop thinking about it. i've scribbled notes for a short essay and i'd like you to look at them and tell me where they're wrong.

the notes, roughly:

1. the standard diagnosis of the tortoise is that he makes a category mistake: he keeps asking for the conditional to be written down as a *premise*, when what's actually needed is for him to *use* modus ponens as a rule. rules aren't premises, so demanding that a rule be justified in the way a premise is justified is demanding something that doesn't exist. fine. i buy this.

2. but i think it's too quick. the standard fix kills one regress and leaves another standing. call them the premise regress and the uptake regress. the premise regress is the famous one: he accepts P and if P then Q, won't accept Q, so you add the conditional as a premise, and now he wants *that* inference licensed too, forever. the uptake regress is different: grant him the rule, don't make him state it as a premise, let him have modus ponens in his pocket — he can still ask why the rule should *govern his transitions*. he isn't asking what to believe; he's asking why he should be *moved*. and no pile of further propositions answers that, because propositions by themselves don't move anyone. so a version of the tortoise survives the standard diagnosis.

3. my move: fine, so what. accepting a rule is a commitment or a disposition, not one more belief, and asking for it to be grounded in a belief is asking for something incoherent — like asking for a proof that you should find a joke funny. so the tortoise's demand, in its uptake form, isn't a request for justification, it's an expression of refusal. and refusal isn't refutable.

4. the bit i'm worried about: doesn't that make me an irrationalist? if the only answer is 'this is how we go on', i've just dressed up convention in a suit. maybe i can distinguish a rule being *grounded* from a rule being *non-optional for anyone who reasons at all*, but i can't tell if that distinction does any work or is just a relabel. i keep loosing the thread here.

(2) is the joke analogy doing anything or is it just cute? (3) is the 'non-optional' escape hatch in 4 any good, or am i quietly conceding the tortoise's point in a nicer voice?
