Alright, so I'm putting together a weird little philosophy zine and I need a satire piece for it. The setup I want you to use is the old torture-vs-dust-specks thought experiment — the one where you have to choose between torturing one person for fifty years or giving some astronomically huge number of people a single dust speck in the eye, and the hard-line aggregator says fine, bring on the specks. I want you to write the thing as if that verdict actually got implemented.

Specifically: write it as the public FAQ / customer service page for the government agency that has to run the policy. Call it whatever you want — something like the Bureau of Aggregate Minor Discomforts — and give it maybe 8 to 10 numbered questions with deadpan answers. Voice is flat bureaucratic optimism, like a DMV pamphlet written by someone who genuinely believes they're doing good. No narrator winks, no winking asides, the reader should never be told it's a joke.

The funny has to come from the actual mechanics of the aggregation, not from inventing silly details. So lean on the real problems: how do you even set an exchange rate between one dust speck and one second of torture, and what happens to the policy when someone points out the rate was just asserted; what the agency does when a citizen files an appeal claiming their particular speck was worse than the standard speck; whether the policy is justified by each individual speck being unnoticeable or by the sum being enormous, and why the FAQ keeps sliding between those two without noticing; total vs. per-capita accounting; the budget line item; the ombudsman. Somewhere in there I want one question whose honest answer quietly implies the agency's own logic would license something considerably worse — but don't flag it, just answer the question straight.

The target here is the aggregationist reasoning itself, the practice of summing tiny harms into a verdict — not any real person. A card-carrying utilitarian should read this and laugh because you clearly understand the machinery, and maybe wince a little too. Around 700 words. American English. And try to keep it in character the whole way through — dont let it drift into essay mode.
