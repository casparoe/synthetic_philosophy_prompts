I've been reading rationalist blogs since the old OB days, and I've never quite got my head around coherent extrapolated volition. Everyone talks about it like its the gold standard for aligning AI with human values. But here's my confusion: if CEV is just what we'd want if we knew more, thought faster, were more the people we wished we were, and had grown up together, then isn't it by definition the correct thing to want? And if idealized preferences are what we'd have under ideal conditions, doesnt that make them objectively true values, so the whole is-ought gap just disappears? Like, if we could compute CEV, we'd have a procedure that spits out the morally right answer, and any remaining disagreement would just be because someone hasn't been extrapolated enough. But then people say CEV isn't a moral theory and doesn't tell us what to do, and I dont get how both can be true. Also, if CEV is supposed to be coherent, what happens when our extrapolated selves disagree with each other? Does it average them, or pick the one with the most reflective endorsement, or what? I guess I'm asking: since CEV is defined as what we would want under idealized conditions, why isn't it just the same as ideal observer theory, and why doesn't that make it objectively authoritative?
