I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy of biology, and for the past year I've been sitting in on a mechanistic-interpretability reading group in the CS department. I want to turn something that keeps coming up there into a proposal for a nine-month internal fellowship, and I'd like you to draft it. My half-formed idea is this. The long-running dispute between etiological (selected-effects) accounts of function, causal-role or systemic accounts, and intention- or design-based accounts is usually fought over biological traits and artifacts, and each side has a well-worn repertoire of cases. What the interpretability work has quietly handed us is a third kind of thing: internal components of a trained neural network -- a feature, a circuit, a particular attention head -- that plainly seem to *do* something for the network's overall capacity, that were shaped by gradient descent on a loss, but that have no evolutionary lineage, no designer who specified their role, and (often) no stable identity across training runs. My advisor, who is an etiological theorist, thinks extending the function debate to such cases is a category error, because without reproduction, heritability, and differential reproduction there is no selection and therefore no function; a friend in the reading group thinks the opposite, that gradient descent *is* a selection process and the etiological account simply generalizes. I suspect both are moving too fast, and that the case is interesting precisely because we can manipulate the training history in ways we can't manipulate phylogeny.

The question I want the proposal to make precise is something like: under what conditions, if any, does gradient-descent training confer a function on an internal component of a trained network, and does answering that vindicate a generalized etiological account, a causal-role account, or a hybrid that takes selection history as necessary for some function-claims and current causal contribution as sufficient for others? I want the proposal to say why the existing positions genuinely leave this open rather than settling it by fiat -- in particular why the standard etiological criteria (that the item be a reproduction-inheritance-selection product) either do or don't transfer to training, and why causal-role accounts, which handle artifacts easily, still struggle to distinguish a component's function from its incidental effects and from its malfunction. Then I want the method spelled out concretely: how I would precisify the competing criteria, how I would construct a small battery of controlled cases that pull them apart -- a component that training seems to have shaped *for* a role, a byproduct or spandrel of training that nonetheless contributes, a component repurposed after training, and a component whose current contribution has drifted from what training optimized it toward -- and how standard interpretability interventions (ablation, activation patching, causal tracing, comparing runs that differ only in seed) would let me test where the accounts' verdicts actually diverge. The proposal should say what a result would look like, what would count as the project failing (for instance, if the accounts turn out to agree on every case, or if the divergences reduce to a terminological dispute that no theorist would call a real disagreement), and whether nine months is realistic or whether I should restrict the study to a single phenomenon such as induction heads and ask whether they are *for* in-context learning or merely a byproduct of it. Please write it as a proposal I can submit -- question, why it's open, approach and why this approach rather than the obvious alternatives, deliverables, failure conditions, and an honest scope assessment -- and don't bother with citations or a literature review, since I'll add those myself.
