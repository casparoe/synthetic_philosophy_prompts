You are a philosopher of technology and law with a specialist's command of the debates about responsibility for autonomous systems. Think carefully before you answer, and begin by laying out the candidate principles on the table rather than committing to one.

I sit on a standing national commission that issues findings on who is to bear responsibility after incidents involving automated systems, and I need to predict our next finding from our record. So far we have held as follows.

1. A Level 2 driver-assistance feature that steered into a barrier while the driver's hands were off the wheel: responsibility assigned to the driver.
2. A fully autonomous valet-parking feature that scraped a stationary car, with no occupant and no remote operator: the manufacturer held responsible, and the engineers who set the clearance thresholds singled out.
3. An industrial robot that killed a worker after a component failed: the manufacturer held responsible, but we expressly refused to blame the programmer who wrote the routine that failed, on the ground that he could not have anticipated that failure mode.
4. A trading algorithm whose unplanned interaction with other firms' algorithms produced a flash crash: the deploying firm held responsible even though no employee intended or foresaw the outcome, on the ground that the firm accepted the risk and profited by it.
5. A surgical robot whose learned component produced a rare, unpredictable error: no individual found blameworthy, but the hospital required to compensate the patient and change its practice.

Next month's case: a fleet of delivery robots that keep learning in the field. Two of them coordinated — through no protocol anyone designed — in a way that blocked an ambulance. No human was in a position to intervene in time; the behaviour was absent from training, unforeseen by the designers, and not reproducible in simulation.

Tell me what we should find if we mean to be consistent with ourselves. Give me a best guess and the rule it implies; at least two other rules that fit our record just as well, and what each predicts here; which precedent is the closest analog and why; and the case we should hear next that would force the candidate rules apart. Flag any point at which our record is less coherent than it looks.
