I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have spent the last several years building automated reasoning and agent tooling. A recurring mismatch in that work has pushed me toward the puzzle usually called logical omniscience: the formal systems I use treat the consequences of what an agent knows as a well-defined mathematical object, closed under entailment, while the programs I actually run discover consequences one step at a time, sometimes never, and often incorrectly. I understand that the standard move is to call the closure an idealization and move on, but I want to know which parts of the problem are genuinely worth attacking and which are artifacts of how the question is posed.

Two thoughts of my own, which I would like you to treat as hypotheses rather than premises. First, that the puzzle is less about knowledge than about the individuation of propositions: if a proposition simply is a set of possible worlds, then logically equivalent propositions are the very same object, and an agent who knows one and not the other has been described incoherently rather than falsely. On that reading, logical omniscience is a theorem about what propositions are, not a claim about what agents do. Second, an analogy from my own field: a term has a type whether or not any algorithm ever infers it, so the semantic fact and the syntactic process come apart cleanly. I suspect the useful question is not how to make agents omniscient but where precisely the semantic fact ends and the syntactic process begins.

Suppose I can give this one focused day a week for two years, and can recruit a colleague or two. Which open questions in this area most deserve that effort, in what order, and why? I care considerably more about the reasoning than the list: which questions are upstream of which; which are tractable for someone with my background, since I can read a paper a week and write code but will not be proving new theorems in modal logic; which would actually change how I build things; and what would move a question up or down the ranking over the next year or two. Where do my two hypotheses mislead me, and would correcting them reorder the agenda? I do not want a reading list or a survey of the standard positions; I want the argument for the ordering.
