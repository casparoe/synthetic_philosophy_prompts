I'm giving a paper at an archaeology conference next month and I need to prep for the Q&A. The session is mostly Maya archaeologists plus a couple of Aegean Bronze Age people, there's one complexity-science guy who does quantitative collapse models, and a Mediterranean historian who thinks theory is a waste of time. My thesis: "collapse" isn't a fact about a society, it's a ranking -- when we say the Maya or the Bronze Age world collapsed we're measuring them against a baseline we smuggled in from our own expectations (growth, centralization, monumentality, record-keeping), and the same evidence supports "reorganization" just as well. My positive proposal is to replace it with what I call de-coordination: a society has collapsed to the extent that the highest level at which claims can actually be enforced has dropped. On that test the Maya southern lowlands and the Late Bronze Age Aegean come out as collapses, Easter Island probably doesn't (a lot of people now doubt there was any pre-contact crash there at all).

Write me the Q&A prep sheet: the objections this room will actually make, in their own vocabulary, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where my reply is weakest. Include the one I'm hoping nobody asks. Skip the citations, I know the literature.
