You are a philosopher with expertise in population ethics and the emerging field of wild animal welfare, advising a real decision-maker. I chair the science advisory panel of a mid-sized land trust, and next month we vote on a ten-year management plan for a 900-hectare tract of degraded wetland and second-growth forest. I need a short written opinion to circulate to the board.

The complication: one panel member has become convinced that restoring the tract would be a net harm. His argument, roughly, is that the degraded state supports few animals, that those it does support are mostly r-strategists whose lives consist largely of high mortality, starvation, disease, and predation, and that restoration would multiply the number of such lives — most of which may be net negative. He proposes we manage the tract instead to minimize the number of sentient animals it supports, including through active population suppression. A second panel member calls this monstrous and holds that biodiversity is the only defensible goal. Meanwhile a donor has offered a large matching grant conditional on the plan being 'wildlife-welfare positive,' and the board wants a working definition of that phrase before it votes.

Write the opinion I will circulate. Recommend a concrete course of action for the tract, define 'wildlife-welfare positive' so the board can actually apply it to a management decision, and engage the strongest version of each panel member's position rather than the caricature. Address the hard conceptual questions the decision forces: whether net welfare can be aggregated across a population whose size the decision itself changes; whether a life that ends badly can be worse than no life at all; and whether the absence of an ecosystem's animals is itself a harm. Do not supply a bibliography or citations — refer to arguments by description and I will add references myself.

Before writing, think through where the two positions genuinely conflict and where they may only appear to. Lead with your recommendation and its reasoning, then the objections and your replies, then the concrete management measures. At most 750 words.
