I'm part of a small reading group on the logical foundations of self-modifying agents, and before we next meet I want to write down my own position, because I keep discovering that I agree with everyone in the room and still can't get the pieces to sit together. Here is what I actually believe, stated as flatly as I can manage. (1) An agent should be able to take its own conclusions as reasons: if my best current deliberation endorses a plan, or if I can foresee that my future, better-informed self will endorse it, that should be able to settle the matter for me, without my first having to step outside my own reasoning and certify it. (2) The same agent should be able to reason about its own machinery — not merely to watch its outputs, but to inspect the algorithm, and in particular to inspect the algorithm of a successor agent that it builds, so that its approval of that successor is derived from reasoning rather than stipulated by fiat. (3) The agent's deductive apparatus is something it endorses as sound: not merely a tool it happens to use, but a commitment it holds — whatever the proof search certifies is true. (4) No agent should be a machine for proving anything: a principle of self-trust under which every sentence becomes derivable is not a generous epistemology but a broken one, and it would also mean that anyone able to get the agent to accept an implication could steer it anywhere. (5) Whatever licenses the trust should be the same at every level: I don't want a system that trusts itself by a rule it refuses to extend to the agent it builds, or that trusts its successor only by an appeal to being the root of the tree. I have been told that a theorem of Löb's is supposed to make trouble for a package like this, and I believe it, but I genuinely cannot see which of the five it is aimed at; my instinct is that (3) and (4) are the same demand stated twice and that any trouble must lie between (1) and (2), or between (2) and (5).

My own guess, which I would like you to evaluate rather than adopt, is that the difficulty is a matter of size: I cannot have blanket trust in everything my proof search returns, but perhaps I can have trust in what it returns quickly, with the trust bounded by the length of the derivation, which would let me keep (3) in a weakened form and still keep (1). I also wonder whether the repair is to stop treating soundness as a theorem of the agent's own theory and start treating it as a rule the agent reasons with — though I can't tell whether that is a real distinction or whether I am just relabelling one thing as another.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. Tell me which pairs among (1) through (5) are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; for the genuine ones, rank them by how much would have to be given up, say what the cheapest repair is in each case, and say what that repair costs elsewhere in the package; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what it would leave broken. If one of the five is simply false as stated — not in tension with the rest, just wrong — say so plainly and describe what the defensible version of it would be. And if my bounded-proofs guess or my rule-versus-theorem guess is confused, tell me where the confusion lies; I would much rather be told that one of my own proposals is a dead end than be handed a tidy reconciliation that leaves it standing.
