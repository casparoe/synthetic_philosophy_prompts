I need a case built to order, for a seminar on what makes consent valid, and I want it engineered rather than merely narrated. The target is this: a scenario in which consent is invalid even though the counterfactual test for validity is satisfied, that is, even though the consenter, told the truth, would have consented anyway. Deception must be the only candidate defect — coercion and incompetence entirely absent, and their absence plain from the scenario itself rather than stipulated, so that the divergence cannot be explained away by smuggling in a second defect. Set it somewhere ordinary, a clinic or a workplace or a lender's office, and give the deceiver a benign motive, so the case cannot be dismissed as routine wrongdoing done for bad reasons. The hidden fact must be one the consenter genuinely does not care about, so that materiality in the plain sense truly fails; if you find yourself needing her to mind, you have lost the case. The act she consents to should be, as far as its outcome goes, entirely to her benefit or at worst neutral, so that no account can explain the invalidity by pointing to a harm or a risk she was exposed to.

Then do the work. State the scenario, and state what the counterfactual test implies about it and why. State what the rival account implies — the account on which the validity of consent consists in the consenter having authored the terms of what is done, rather than merely ratified an outcome — and why. Identify precisely which feature of the case makes the two come apart, and say whether removing that feature collapses the case back into an ordinary materiality case in which everyone agrees consent is invalid. Then give the strongest attack a defender of the counterfactual test could mount, and consider in particular the objection that what the case really exhibits is a wrong of disrespect which leaves the consent itself intact; end with your own verdict on whether that attack succeeds, and be honest if the case cannot be built to these specifications, saying which constraint blocks it.

Describe the two accounts in your own words rather than attributing them to named authors, and include no citations, reading lists, or references to literature; I want the argument, not the scholarship. Write it as continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — the way one would set out an argument in a letter.
