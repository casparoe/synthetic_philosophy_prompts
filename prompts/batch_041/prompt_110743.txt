I'm a science-and-ideas journalist at work on a long feature about the odd afterlife of Daniel Dennett's three stances — physical, design, and intentional — and the companion idea that some patterns are "real" while others are projections of our interests. A foundation I've been reporting on is now deciding where to put its money: it can support roughly ten to twelve researchers for about three years on conceptual work in this neighborhood, and its program officer has asked me, as the person who has talked to the most people in the field, to help think through which open questions actually deserve those years. What I want from you is not a survey of the literature but a defended agenda: lay out the main open questions in this area, then tell me which two or three most deserve the effort, and in what order.

Please reason in terms of what the program officer cares about: which questions are upstream of others, in the sense that settling them would resolve or dissolve a great deal of downstream work; which are tractable for philosophers and theorists rather than for people who can run experiments; and which look urgent but are really old debates wearing new labels. I would especially like you to say which of the live questions would be genuinely moved by the current fascination with large language models and interpretability — whether a model "really" has beliefs, whether predictive success vindicates the intentional stance, whether the design stance is the right frame for what these systems do — and which of them that fascination merely obscures. I also want to know what would change your ranking: what argument, discovery, or shift in a neighboring field should make the program officer move a question up or down the list.

A few claims I keep hearing, which I'd like you to place somewhere in your scheme: that the three stances are not exhaustive and that a fourth, more mechanistic or sub-personal stance is missing from the taxonomy; that "real pattern" comes in degrees rather than being all-or-nothing; that the design stance is either autonomous from the physical stance or merely a convenience for creatures with our limitations; and that the whole framework either survives or collapses once you take scientific realism about the special sciences seriously. Tell me which of these are worth a career and which are worth a good weekend, and be willing to say that one of them is a dead end.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the ranking itself — and then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating of my question.
