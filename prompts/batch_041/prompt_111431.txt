We did bargaining problems a few weeks back and I think I followed Nash okay — the one where you take the feasible set of utility pairs, subtract the disagreement point, and pick whatever maximises the product of the two gains. But the next lecture was on the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution and it's the one I keep chewing on. The way our lecturer put it: forget the product, just draw a straight line from the disagreement point up to the utopia point — the pair where each player gets the best payoff they could possibly get from any feasible agreement — and see where that line crosses the outer boundary of the feasible set. So you end up with the agreement where both people get the same proportion of the way from their fallback to their dream. And the reason it's meant to be interesting is that KS drops Nash's independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives axiom and replaces it with some monotonicity axiom, on the grounds that the whole set of options should matter, because otherwise the person whose best option has just come onto the table has no claim on anything.
