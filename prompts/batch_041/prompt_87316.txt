My roommate and I got in a three-hour fight last night because he told me he's stopped getting angry at anyone on principle — resentment is just leftover punitive instinct, he says, and once you really see people as products of their circumstances it drains out. I think hes wrong but I couldn't say why without reaching for "they could have done otherwise," which I don't believe anyway. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it hangs together:

(a) Strawson was right that resentment, indignation and guilt don't need a metaphysical permission slip — asking whether someone is "really" responsible, over and above whether the anger fits the facts, is confused.
(b) But the attitudes are still answerable to facts. When I learn something was caused by a tumor, or by a childhood that left the guy no other option, the anger definately drains out, and that's not a mistake on my part.
(c) So I do think there's a right and a wrong to blaming. And I think people who can't be moved by reasons are outside the practice altogether — a real psychopath isn't excused, hes just not in the moral community.
(d) At the same time, responsibility comes in degrees, and most of us sit somewhere in the middle.
(e) And my roommate's loss isn't factual. He's given up what I've been calling the standing to be hurt. Being angry at someone isn't just a verdict on their agency; it's what makes you someone who can be wronged at all. If nobody can wrong you, nothing was taken from you.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for each real one? Rank them by how bad they are, and tell me which single change cleans up the most.
