I'm supervising a grad student whose chapter draft keeps leaning on the same move: culture evolves by natural selection, memes are the cultural analogue of genes, so when a belief or a tune or a fashion spreads and sticks it's because it got selected for, and it can spread even when it's bad for the people carrying it — Dawkins's old point that the meme doesn't care about the host. He's got the standard cites (the 1976 book where Dawkins coins the thing, Blackmore's The Meme Machine) and he treats the analogy as if it's already been earned. I don't think it's that simple, but I also don't want to just say "culture is more complicated." Can you audit the analogy properly?

What I want: a correspondence table first — what's supposed to map onto the gene, the genotype/phenotype distinction, the mutation rate, the selection pressure, drift, recombination, the germ-line/soma wall, species boundaries. Then the places the mapping actually breaks, and be honest about which breaks are fatal versus merely untidy. Some of the obvious candidates: copying fidelity, whether cultural "units" are even discrete, whether there's any clean vehicle/replicator split, whether inheritance is blending or particulate, whether it's Lamarckian (acquired stuff gets passed on directly), and the fact that a lot of transmission is reconstructive rather than replicative — the Sperber / cultural-attraction crowd make a lot of that last one, and I'd like you to say whether it kills the meme-as-replicator picture outright or just narrows it to a few special cases.

Then the part I actually care about: the student's conclusion is that cultural change is Darwinian all the way down, and that this explains why harmful things spread. Does the analogy carry that, or was the conclusion riding on the analogy the whole time? Which disanalogies are load-bearing for that specific claim, and which ones don't matter for it? And if the meme frame is the wrong one, what's the better frame — the epidemiology-of-representations thing, dual-inheritance models, something else — and what does the better frame predict differently about, say, why a costly religious practice persists?

Skip the bibliography, I know the literature — give me the audit and tell me which single sub-question the whole thing hinges on.
