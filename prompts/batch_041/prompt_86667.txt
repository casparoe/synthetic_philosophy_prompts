I want you to help me stress-test a position I have been defending, which is a deliberately weakened descendant of the Gödelian argument against mechanism, and I want to know what would count against it. I do not claim that human mathematicians can prove theorems that no machine can prove; I claim only that there is an asymmetry of entitlement in self-trust. Any consistent formal system rich enough for the incompleteness phenomenon cannot earn, by its own lights, the conviction that it is sound: it may carry that conviction as an added axiom, or not at all. A reflective reasoner who surveys her own inferential practice, by contrast, can accept her own soundness, and this acceptance is not merely one more axiom she has elected to adopt; it is what her practice already commits her to. Mechanism, on this telling, is not refuted by any theorem we can prove and a machine cannot. It is refuted by the fact that the entitlement structure of a machine's self-trust and that of a reasoner's self-trust come apart, and no amount of added strength on the machine's side repairs the gap, because the gap is not one of strength but of standing.

Now tell me what would tell against this. I expect the serious objections to be these, and I want them assessed rather than merely listed. First, that the acceptance can be modelled as a system generating its own successors by an effective rule, so that a single machine running such a progression reproduces everything the idealized reasoner does, and the asymmetry is only between stages of one hierarchy rather than between humans and machines. Second, that if the acceptance is genuinely knowledge rather than a practical stance of trust, the same reasoning should entitle us to settle questions we demonstrably cannot settle, so the position proves too much. Third, that the idealization is doing the work: an errorless reasoner with unlimited time is not me, and if I drop the idealization the asymmetry evaporates, while if I keep it the position may be immune to any empirical finding. Fourth, that the mechanist can simply grant the asymmetry and remain a mechanist, since a system's inability to certify its own soundness is a fact about its indexical position, not about its nature. I want each of these sorted by how decisive it would be if it went through, from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which of them defenders of the position already concede, as opposed to which they simply deny. Tell me also what the position predicts that its mechanist rivals do not, so that I can see whether it is doing any work; and say plainly whether it has been qualified into something no evidence could touch, and whether that is a defect in a position of this kind or an acceptable price. The objection that would most move me is a demonstration that the reflective acceptance is mechanizable without residue, so give me your honest estimate of whether such a demonstration is available, and of what the position would look like if it were.

Please write this in continuous prose, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold, and no tables, the way you would write a long letter.
