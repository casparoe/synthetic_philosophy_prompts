My friend Priya is in my epistemology reading group and she's definately got a verdict on basically every "do you know that you know?" case we throw at her. Her record so far, as best I can reconstruct it:

- Tomato on the counter, good light, right in front of her: knows there's a tomato, and knows that she knows.
- A long division she did on paper and then checked over: knows the answer, knows that she knows.
- The toothache she's had since breakfast: knows she's in pain, knows that she knows.
- A bird on the fence she's just barely willing to call a sparrow: knows it's a sparrow, but does not know that she knows.
- The clock across the room: knows what time it is, does not know that she knows, since she's misread that clock before.
- The first few minutes of a headache, where she honestly can't tell whether it's a headache or just tired eyes: says she doesn't know she's in pain, and obviously doesn't know that she knows.
- The number of people in the seminar room, which she counted twice: knows, and knows that she knows.

(She also keeps insisting that pain is the one thing you can't be in without knowing it, which is maybe why the headache-threshold case got under her skin.)

New case from last night: she read two numbers off her bank statement and then, step by step, deduced that she's overdrawn by $14. She spelled the whole derivation out to me and every step is one she can state cleanly. But one misread digit, or a transaction that hasn't posted yet, flips the answer, and she has no way to rule that out.

So: does she know that she knows she's overdrawn? I care less about whether she's actually right than about the rule she's running. Give it to me as:

"The rules that fit" -- two or three rules that fit every verdict above equally well, each stated in a sentence or two, plus a note on which verdicts each one is doing the most work on. About 350 words.

"What each rule predicts for the overdraft" -- go rule by rule, say what each says about the new case and where exactly the disagreement is coming from. About 250 words.

"My bet" -- your prediction for what she'll actually say, and the best reason to think that's the rule she's really running. About 150 words.

"The case that would settle it" -- the single further case that would best separate the rules you've got, what each rule says about it, and why it's a cleaner test than the overdraft case. About 200 words.

"Tensions in the record" -- any of her verdicts that sit badly together, and whether you think she's running one rule or quietly switching between two. About 150 words.

Don't bother with names or citations, I know the literature well enough.
