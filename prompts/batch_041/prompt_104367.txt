OK so I need a favour. I'm opening my rational-choice seminar with a joke about the common prior assumption, and every version I write is just "two Bayesians walk into a bar" — technically on topic, no laughs, and half the room has seen the same joke on the internet already. What I want is a joke whose punchline actually turns on the doctrine, so that someone who's done the reading laughs and someone who hasn't just sits there quietly feeling dumb.

The doctrine, so we're on the same page (and no, I'm not asking you to cite anything): if two agents start from the same prior over the world, both update by Bayes' rule, and each one's posterior ends up being common knowledge between them, then their posteriors have to be equal — they literally can't "agree to disagree." On this picture, any persistent disagreement has to be explained either by one of them having better information or by one of them updating badly. A difference in the prior itself isn't a respectable option; it's supposed to be ignorance or a mistake. (Aumann's theorem, if you want a label for it.)

And here's the bit I'd love the joke to touch, because it's where the real comedy lives: nobody can tell you where the common prior came from. You can't learn it, since learning is the thing you do with it. So the position is that every rational disagreement traces back to evidence, while the one item the whole picture rests on is itself unevidenced — and if two people do turn out to have different priors, there's no further court of appeal, just "well, I came by mine honestly."

So: give me a joke. Keep it short. Setup can be anything — a marriage, a tenure committee, a split-the-bill arguement — but the punchline has to cash out in that structure. E.g. one party weaponising the theorem ("we can't really be disagreeing, so you must be the one who's been misinformed"), or a couple who can't even get to "let's agree to disagree" because they're supposed to have identical priors and neither will own up to the regress. No bibliography, obviously, and it should still land if you strip the jargon out at the end. If all you've got is a pun on the word "prior", keep thinking.
