so i moved from Ohio to Amsterdam last year for a masters in political theory and i keep stepping on the same rake: i told a dutch friend i'm a liberal and she looked at me like i'd just announced i vote VVD (she does, i don't), a german guy in my seminar uses liberal to mean roughly the free-market individual-freedom parties, and my british flatmate says it and means the Lib Dems, which is yet another thing again. it's turned into a running joke in our little reading group, and now the term-end evening has the theme words that lie, and i've volunteered to read a poem. i want to do the false-friend thing with liberal itself, but as a real poem, not a bit.

here's what i'm after. no fixed form, roughly 20-30 lines, but built around a single refrain line that uses the word liberal and repeats word-for-word — say four times — and each time the surrounding stanza drags it into a different national register, so the same sentence quietly means a different thing each time it comes back. i want one pass where it reads as the american thing (welfare state, civil liberties, the opposite of conservative), one where it reads as the continental free-market / individual-freedom thing, one where it reads as the british social-liberal thing, and i'd love a fourth that leans on the older, almost dead sense of liberal as generous, lavish, free-handed, the one that survives in liberal arts and liberality — treat that ghost sense as the ancestor all these kids are fighting over. the reader should feel the word rotate under them without the poem ever pausing to explain itself; the explanation is the structure, not a line. let the final repetition be genuinely ambiguous, readable at least two ways, so it ends unresolved rather than tidy. someone who's thought about comparative party politics should find it right; someone who hasn't should just find it a good poem.

also, under the poem, give me a short prose note — for a creative piece that's the honest version of what's settled vs contested — on where the poem leans on a contested reading of the word. i know the clean america-vs-europe split is a simplification (the uk sort of breaks it, and i've heard liberal / libéral can land as an insult in parts of continental europe, which i'm not sure how to handle), so tell me which of the usage claims i'm leaning on are actually agreed by people who study this and which are just a convenient story i've picked up. no citations, no name-dropping, just be straight with me about the shaky bits.
