Exam question:

"Quine's field linguist hears the native utterance gavagai each time a rabbit scurries past. Explain how this example is supposed to support the thesis of the indeterminacy of translation. Then explain the relation between the indeterminacy of translation and the inscrutability of reference. Finally: suppose the linguist could point at objects, ask the native to count, and ask whether one thing is the same as another. Would these techniques eliminate the indeterminacy? Explain why or why not."

Student's answer:

Quine asks us to imagine a linguist translating a wholly unknown language with no interpreter and no prior information. Each time a rabbit runs past, the native says gavagai, and the linguist records the stimulus meaning — the range of stimulations that prompt the utterance. But the native's behavior is equally consistent with gavagai meaning rabbit, or undetached rabbit parts, or a momentary rabbit-stage, or rabbithood in general. Since every possible observation is compatible with all of these, there is no fact of the matter about which one the native means, and this is what the indeterminacy of translation amounts to. The inscrutability of reference is the term-level version of the same point: just as whole utterances can be translated in incompatible ways, individual terms can be assigned incompatible referents, and the behavioral evidence never decides between them. Quine therefore denies that there is any absolute standard of right and wrong in translation and treats translation as a pragmatic matter.

Suppose, though, that the linguist can point, count, and ask whether one thing is the same as another. Once the native's word for identity is available, the linguist can ask whether this gavagai is the same as that earlier gavagai, and the answer settles whether gavagai names an object, a stage, or a part. The indeterminacy is therefore confined to radical translation between unrelated languages, and it disappears when we translate within our own language, where we already know what our words mean.
