I hold a chair in philosophy of mind, and I also work closely enough with the reinforcement-learning literature to be at home in its vocabulary — policies and value functions, model-free versus model-based control, rollouts and internal simulation, priors and inductive bias, meta-learning, and the several senses in which an agent can offload computation onto its environment. I keep finding that when I try to render a certain taxonomy of minds in those terms, the translation goes smoothly for three of its four grades and then quietly falls apart at the fourth, and I want to know whether the failure is mine or the framework's.

The taxonomy is Dennett's ladder of four grades of creature. Darwinian creatures are the products of natural selection alone: their behavioural repertoire is effectively fixed at birth, and they are tested only by whether they survive. Skinnerian creatures can be shaped by reinforcement, keeping what is rewarded and dropping what is not. Popperian creatures can represent possible futures internally and try candidate actions against that representation, discarding the ones that fail before risking them in the world. Gregorian creatures, finally, are set apart by their use of culturally transmitted thinking tools — language, notation, and other external scaffolds that they install and then think with.

What I want from you is a systematic translation of each grade into the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can manage, followed by a residue report. Tell me which grades map cleanly onto distinctions the reinforcement-learning framework already draws, which translate only with distortion and precisely what gets distorted in the process, and which have no counterpart at all. Where something fails to carry over, I want your view on which way the failure cuts: does it mark a genuine expressive limitation of the target vocabulary, or does it show that the original distinction was doing less work than its rhetorical force suggests? I am especially interested in a verdict on the last grade. Is the Gregorian creature a fourth kind of mind in its own right, or is it really a claim about the sort of environment the first three kinds happen to inhabit — a claim about external scaffolding rather than about a distinct internal capacity?
