I'm writing an essay for my philosophy of physics course and would rather argue for a definite position than survey the field. Take Newton's bucket and strip the universe down: imagine a cosmos whose entire material content is a single bucket of water rotating about its axis — no other matter, no fixed stars, nothing to rotate relative to. Argue that the water's surface would nevertheless be concave, that there is a fact of the matter about the bucket's rotation even in such a universe, and that this shows relationism to be false and something like absolute rotation to be real, even if we need not reinstate absolute space as a substance. Then meet the strongest replies head-on: that rotation is undefined or vacuous when there is nothing to rotate relative to; that a verificationist ought to say there is no fact to be had; and that the relationist can simply deny the intuition. Be explicit about what the bucket experiment can and cannot establish once all other matter has been removed, and about whether your conclusion is a claim about what exists or about what is kinematically possible.
