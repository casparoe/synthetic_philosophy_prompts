I write about physics and philosophy for a general-interest magazine, and for the better part of a year I've been reporting on the foundations of quantum mechanics — sitting in on seminars, arguing with decoherence people over lunch, that sort of thing. My editor has now insisted, not unreasonably, that a piece which keeps treating some readings of the formalism as serious and others as beneath discussion had better come with my own view stated openly, so that readers can discount it. So here is my position, as I would write it down for a colleague, and what I want is an audit rather than a tutorial. (1) I am a scientific realist: our mature theories are approximately true of a mind-independent world, and when a theory's central posit does ineliminable causal-explanatory work I take it to be real — with "real" meaning something like "has causal powers," not "occupies a region of space." (2) I am psi-ontic: the wavefunction belongs to the world's basic furniture, not to an observer's bookkeeping. (3) I hold that the quantum state is a complete description of a system — there is no further fact about the system, lying beneath the state, that the state leaves out. (4) I take the unitary dynamics to be exact and exceptionless: no collapse, no additional stochastic law, and no branching either, so that a measurement has one outcome and there is one world. (5) I hold that quantum probabilities are epistemic, i.e. rational degrees of belief grounded in ignorance rather than irreducible chanciness in nature. (6) I hold that "measurement" names a role and not a special kind of physical process; measuring a system is just interacting with it in a way that leaves a record. (7) Methodologically, I give genuine evidential weight to mathematical unification and to the absence of ad hoc patches: when the choice is between revising a metaphysical prejudice and bolting an extra law onto an otherwise clean formalism, I would sooner revise the prejudice.

Two of the physicists I have interviewed say this package cannot hold together, but they disagree sharply about which joint is the weak one, and I would rather not surrender the parts that do the most work simply because they are unfashionable. Tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other, ranked by how severe the tension is, and for each one name the cheapest revision that would relieve it and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the list. Say explicitly which conflicts are only apparent — I would be surprised if none of them were — and explain why the appearance is misleading. Then commit to a single diagnosis: which one revision buys the most consistency for the least loss, and what I would be giving up by making it. Please take a position rather than laying out a menu of options, and end with a final line beginning "Verdict:" that names the commitment you would revise and the revision itself, in a short phrase.
