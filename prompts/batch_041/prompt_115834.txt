Over the summer I read one of those big narrative pop-history books about economics — not a textbook — and the chapter on Malthus has been bugging me, but I don't know enough to tell whether the author is being unfair to the guy or the opposite.

The chapter goes roughly like this. Malthus said population always outruns the food supply — people multiply geometrically, food only arithmetically — so any gain in living standards just gets swallowed up by more children, and the only real checks on population are famine, disease, and war. He predicted a big catastrophe that never showed up; the Industrial Revolution and then the Green Revolution blew past every limit he had in mind, so he was simply wrong, and the only reason we still say his name is that he gave us a scary word for overpopulation. The author also says Malthus was kind of a heartless guy who wanted to cut off aid to the poor, and that this cruelty follows straight from his population math. Then, sort of out of nowhere, the last paragraph says that alot of economists nowadays think Malthus was basically right and we're just living on borrowed time before the limits bite again.

I'm not a student, I just read this stuff for fun. Something feels off — "he was simply wrong" and "he was basically right" can't both be the whole story. Could you go through the chapter's claims one at a time and tell me, for each, whether a serious historian of economic ideas would accept it, whether it's a fair simplification, or whether it's just wrong? A few things I'd especally like sorted out: is it actually true he thought the only checks were famine, disease, and war, or did he think people could hold themselves back on purpose? Is a failed prediction really a refutation of his theory, or was his theory doing something sneakier than just making a forecast? Did his hard line on helping the poor really follow from the math, or is the author reading his politics back into his theory? And is there a way to be "right but early" that isn't just a cop-out — like, is there a real difference between a theory being false and a theory being about a world that stopped existing? Basically I want to know which parts to trust.
