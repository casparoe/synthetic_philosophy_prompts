I run a small strength-and-conditioning gym. I have no academic training in philosophy; what I know comes from coaching and from reading on my own. I recently worked through Plato's Laws, especially the long stretch in Book II where the Athenian argues that drinking parties are not a concession to weakness but a deliberate institution — a controlled, law-governed way of inducing the condition that reveals and trains a person's self-command. My rough grasp of the argument is this: wine produces a kind of shamelessness and childishness in a way that is voluntary and reversible; since courage is tested by fear, self-command ought to be tested by pleasure; and wine is the cheapest, safest way to manufacture that test, safer than waiting for war or catastrophe. He adds age rules, roughly nothing before eighteen, moderation until thirty, full participation after forty, and suggests wine was given to humanity as a remedy for the grimness of old age, so that the old can loosen and become young again. He also seems to hold that whether education holds together at all depends on getting this practice right. Correct me if I have garbled any of that.

Restate the position as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: progressive overload, training stimulus, dose and intensity, form breakdown under fatigue, supervised versus unsupervised sets, training to failure versus training for skill, specificity, recovery and deload, screening and injury risk, testing versus practicing, spotting, and the difference between a gym people come to in order to be tested and a gym people come to in order to be trained.

Then give me the residue report. Which of his claims come across intact, which only come across if you quietly smuggle in assumptions that are not in my vocabulary, and which have no counterpart there at all? What I care about most is the material that drops out — the divine gift, the shame, the lawgiver's mandate, the claim that the whole educational system stands or falls with this one practice. Tell me whether those are things my framework simply cannot say, or things he never made clear enough to say in the first place.
