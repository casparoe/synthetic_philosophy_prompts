I've been reading about efforts to catch AI systems doing something other than what they were trained to do by examining their internals, and I keep running into a wall I don't have a word for. The wall is that whether two runs count as the same mechanism seems to depend entirely on how finely you slice. Slice at the level of individual neurons or features and almost nothing ever matches; slice at the level of what goes in and what comes out and almost everything matches; and in practice the slice seems to get chosen to fit the conclusion, so a run that looks suspicious is called a different mechanism under one comparison and the same mechanism under another, with no rule for which comparison is the correct one. What bothers me is that the phrase 'the same mechanism' then stops looking like a finding and starts looking like a decision in disguise. I suspect this is near the point that the same job can be done by different stuff, or near the idea that evidence never fully pins down a theory, but neither feels like quite the thing, because my problem isn't about different stuff doing the same job or about ambiguous evidence — it's that the thing being compared is itself up for grabs, and whoever is making the claim gets to set it. Does this have an established name? If it does, tell me which, and how it differs from the closest candidates rather than merely listing them; if it doesn't, coin one and give me its definition and its boundary cases — what it covers and what it does not. Don't hedge: I want a definite verdict on whether a genuine concept exists here, and if the fit is imperfect, a definite judgment about which existing idea comes closest and exactly where it breaks down.
