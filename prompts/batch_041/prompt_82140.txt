My colleague teaches determinism with the story of the sphex wasp: she hauls a paralysed cricket to her burrow, leaves it at the door, goes inside to check the burrow, and — as the story goes — if you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's in there, she comes back out, drags it to the door again, and runs the whole inspection from the top, and she'll do it as many times as you care to move the cricket. The word sphexish comes from that wasp. His punchline is that she looks purposeful, even careful, but she's a wind-up toy, and if determinism is true that's us: our deliberating is the wasp's burrow-checking at a fancier level of description, and the felt difference between choosing and re-running a routine is an illusion we're just too close to see.

I want to use this in the seminar but I need it audited properly, not just admired or jeered at. So: what maps onto what here — the cricket, the burrow check, the nudge, the restart from the top — and which of those items has any honest counterpart in a deliberating human, rather than a counterpart we've stipulated? Then where the mapping breaks. Two things nag me. First, the wasp's rigidity is a known fact about her only because we've mapped her triggers from outside, which is exactly what nobody has done for us. Second, sphexish seems level-relative: a system can be rigid at one level of description and flexible at another, so the wasp is sphexish and we are sphexish might not be the same claim at all, just the same word. Does that level point rescue the analogy or is it the disanalogy that kills it?

And then the honest question: was the punchline ever carried by the analogy, or was it assumed first and the wasp brought in as decoration? If I've got the wasp herself wrong (I've heard the story is overstated) or the setup is confused, say so plainly instead of humouring me — I'd rather find out from you than from a sharp student.
