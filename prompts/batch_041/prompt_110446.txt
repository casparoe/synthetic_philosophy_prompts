Consider a lottery with N = 1,000 players, each of whom independently decides whether to send in a single card. If exactly one card is sent, its sender wins $1,000,000; if zero cards are sent, or two or more, nothing is paid to anyone. The players cannot communicate, and each knows that the others face the identical decision and reason in the same way.

Please argue that the superrational case for universal abstention is unsound, and that the correct policy is for every player to send with probability 1/N. In doing so, meet the strongest objection to that conclusion: that a player who is certain the others reason exactly as she does cannot coherently treat her own card as an independent random draw, and that once the others are expected to send with probability 1/N, sending for certain is plainly better for her, so the symmetric mixed policy cannot be rational.

Give the argument as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.
