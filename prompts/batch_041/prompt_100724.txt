Suppose an interpretability study finds that a single direction in a language model's residual stream decodes a target property with near-perfect probe accuracy, and that adding this direction to the model's activations shifts its outputs in just the way the property predicts. The same direction, however, also degrades several unrelated capabilities, and it fails to reappear when the model is retrained on identical data with a different random seed. Rank the following verdicts on what this shows, from most to least defensible: (a) the model represents the property; (b) the direction is a useful steering handle but not evidence of representation; (c) the model represents a coarser property of which the target is one facet; (d) there is only a behavioral disposition here, not a representation; (e) the evidence underdetermines the representation question entirely. Give a one-sentence rationale for each placement, and name the single additional observation or experiment that would most change your ordering.
