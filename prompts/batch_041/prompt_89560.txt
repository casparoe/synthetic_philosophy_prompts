Im giving a talk at our games-and-logic reading group in a couple weeks and I want to stress-test my thesis before I stand up in front of everyone, so I need a Q&A prep sheet.

Here's the position I'm defending. Everyone trots out the strategy-stealing argument as a proof that the first player wins Hex (and Chomp, and the m,n,k games, etc.). I want to argue that's a misdescription of what the argument actually does. Taken strictly, it's a reductio on the hypothesis that the *second* player has a winning strategy — it shows P2 can't win, full stop. Getting from there to "P1 wins" requires a determinacy premise (no draws, and one of the two players has a forced win). For finite games that premise is trivial, and if it's trivial then the argument is doing no work, because you could reach the same conclusion by backward induction. But where determinacy is actually up for grabs — infinite games, loopy games, anything where a draw is on the table — that's exactly where the argument is carrying real weight, and there the premise is precisely the thing in dispute, so the argument is circular. Either way it isn't earning its keep. And I've got a second, smaller point: the argument silently needs a monotonicity assumption (an extra stone of your own can never hurt you), which everyone waves through, and it fails in exactly the zugzwang-flavored games where people are fondest of appealing to symmetry.

The room is going to be maybe a dozen people. Mostly combinatorial game theorists who use this argument every other week and know Hex and Chomp cold, plus two constructive mathematicians / proof theorists who are going to fixate on the law of excluded middle, plus one complexity person. Not a friendly crowd exactly, but not hostile either.

Give me the prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from *this* crowd — not the generic ones — roughly in the order I'll get them, sorted by how much damage they do, each phrased the way that particular person would phrase it, with a short reply I can actually deliver and an honest note on where the reply is soft. If one of them is fatal, say so, don't soft-pedal it. And tell me the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
