A friend who follows popular physics often says that in quantum mechanics the observer creates reality, and last week he used the line to settle an argument: because observation brings the outcome into being, he said, Everett's many-worlds reading and Bohm's pilot-wave theory must be unscientific, since both treat the observer as just another physical system. I suspect the slogan is doing far less work than he thinks it is. Please lay out the distinct claims that sentence could be making, ordered from the nearly trivial to the strongly metaphysical — about measurement disturbance, about whether observables have definite values before measurement, about the role of consciousness, about what "reality" in the claim is even supposed to refer to — and for each reading say how defensible it is, which interpretation of quantum mechanics it fits, and whether it actually supports his verdict that only something like Copenhagen leaves physics respectable. Give me the readings as a menu with verdicts rather than picking one and defending it. Please write your answer in American English.
