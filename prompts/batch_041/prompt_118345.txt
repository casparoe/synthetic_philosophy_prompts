My friend Priya and I spent about two hours last night arguing about whether the future already exists, and she gave me an argument that I can't get out of. I think it proves way too much — she doesn't think so, she thinks it just proves what physicists already believe — but I can't find the bad step. Every time I try to say where it goes wrong I end up just gesturing at the word "now." So I want you to find the step for me. Here it is the way she laid it out, tidied up a bit.

(1) For two events far enough apart in space, there's no absolute fact about which one happened first. Some inertial frames order them one way, some the other way, and exactly one frame makes them simultaneous. We both take this as just true, it's the relativity of simultaneity.

(2) No frame is privileged. If a frame says two events are simultaneous, that's not a worse answer than another frame's answer. It's the answer.

(3) So when a frame says two events are happening at the same time, they are happening at the same time. Not "happening at the same time from the perspective of that frame" — that qualifier is already folded into (2). They're just simultaneous. And if one of them is happening now, so is the other one.

(4) Setup. I'm here on Earth, call this t = 0. There's a star ten light years away, sitting still relative to Earth. Take the event of some flash going off at that star at t = 5 years. A ship passing Earth at half the speed of light heading toward the star has a frame in which that flash and me sitting here right now are simultaneous. She did it with the Lorentz formula on the back of an envelope and I checked it, it comes out right.

(5) So by (2) and (3), that flash five years from now is happening now.

(6) Second hop. That flash at the star, and me ten years from now, still sitting on Earth, not going anywhere: those two are ten light years apart and five years apart, so they're spacelike seperated too, and there's another frame — this one moving at half lightspeed the opposite way — in which they're simultaneous. By (2) and (3) again, me ten years from now is happening now.

(7) Keep hopping like that and you can reach any event in my future, however far ahead, whether or not it's spacelike seperated from me. My whole future is real right now. She calls this the block universe and says it's old news.

(8) If my whole future is real right now, then the coin I'm going to flip tomorrow has already landed, the decision I'm agonizing over is already made, and nothing I do in between can change any of it. Deliberating is theater.

I don't trust (8), but that might just be because I hate where it lands. I pointed out that you can run the same hopping backwards and get that the Battle of Hastings is happening now, and she said fine, that's what the block is, which to me sounds like "now" has stopped meaning anything at all. She says the only exit is to insist that only the present is real, and that everyone who knows any physics gave that up a century ago.

I'd rather keep (1) and (2) — obviously — and find something else to give up, but she says that's not on the menu. So which step do I reject, why did it look airtight at the time, and what am I paying if I reject it? Please don't just tell me this is a famous puzzle and hand me a reading list, I don't have library access here and I don't care what it's called, I want the diagnosis. And write it as plain continuous prose, no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables — just explain it the way you'd explain it in an email, my eyes slide right off anything formatted.
