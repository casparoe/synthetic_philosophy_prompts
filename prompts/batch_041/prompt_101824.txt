Ok so I'm building a set of writing exercises and I need one short-story prompt built around the old "a number stops measuring the thing once you start paying people on it" idea — Goodhart, target-chasing, gaming the metric. Problem is the standard version (numbers go up, everything quietly rots underneath, sad ending) has been done to death and any model will just trot out the same beats, so I want the premise loaded with a mechanism it has to actually think through.

Use this setup: the workplace in the story tracks two numbers. There's the public number that everyone's pay, ranking and promotions hang on. And there's a private number that only the audit office keeps, and the whole point of the private number is that it is officially forbidden to touch anyone's incentives — the auditors cannot reward or punish, cannot even tell the staff their own readings, they just measure and file it. On paper that's supposed to keep the private number honest. The story should live inside what happens when that firewall starts leaking anyway, because people can smell what's being watched even when it's officially not being watched — or because the private number starts quietly tracking the public one for reasons nobody can pin down.

A couple of details you can use or drop: the private number is only computed once a year, from a random sample of shifts, so nobody knows if they were in it; the auditors are temps who rotate out.

Setting: keep it small and physical — a regional blood bank, a fishing-quota office, a call center floor, a school district's attendance office, whatever you like, but one workplace, one or two characters, one scene. Third person, present tense, 700–900 words. No narrator speeches about incentives and no naming any economist or theory inside the story — let the machinery carry it. And please don't land a neat moral at the end; close on a decision or on a line somebody says.

After the story, give me a section titled Why I did it this way, about 150 words, where you say which beats you used to make the two-number setup do the work and where you were tempted to reach for the obvious ending instead.
