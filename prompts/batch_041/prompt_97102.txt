Ok so I'm putting together a little zine of short fiction where each story smuggles in exactly one idea and never says what it is, and I need one from you. The idea I want this time is the tangled-hierarchy thing: two levels where each one seems to ground the other, so you genuinely can't say which is doing the grounding — the hand drawing the hand that's drawing it. The only way a loop like that doesnt just eat itself is if there's some level outside the loop that never gets pulled in, the thing that actually settles the question, the level you're not allowed to ask about from inside. You've definately seen the Escher print and the usual examples, so don't give me those. In fact don't use a computer, a machine, a god, a dream, or a painting at all. Give me a short story set somewhere dead ordinary — a family, a small business, a local government office, a choir, a landlord and tenant, whatever.

Somewhere in it, two things have to be visibly holding each other up: each one seeming to be the reason the other is the way it is, and the characters have to start trying to work out which one is really in charge, and be unable to, from where they're standing. Somewhere else, quietly, there has to be a third thing that resolves the loop just by not being part of it, and I don't want the story to point at it, name it, or have anyone notice it. If a reader misses it, good, that's the point. One more requirement: at some moment the loop has to come close to actually breaking — a real paradox, not a spooky vibe — and the story has to show what the third thing is doing at that exact moment, without a single character registering that it's doing anything. No naming the concept, no character explaining it, no lecture paragraphs.

After the story, give me a JSON summary, fields exactly these:

- title: string
- the_two_levels: the two things that each seem to ground the other, one line each
- the_loop_almost_breaks: the moment of near-paradox, one sentence
- inviolate_level: what the third thing is, and why it can't be inside the loop
- where_it_hides: the sentence or image where you buried it, copied out of your own story
- wrong_candidate: the thing a careless reader would probably pick as the resolving level instead, and why they'd be wrong

Keep it under 900 words or so. No ending where it turns out to be a simulation or a story someone in the story is writing — that's a cop-out and it also gives the game away.
