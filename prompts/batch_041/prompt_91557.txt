ok so I'm on this little working group that's drafting an internal memo about when an AI system should count as "agentic" for purposes of review and liability, and the longer we meet the more I think we're all just using "tool" and "agent" as vibes words. my friend Dana and I got into it over lunch about whether a recommender that updates from clicks is "an agent," and neither of us could say what would settle it. before we write another sentence of the memo I want the spec. write me the desiderata for a theory of the tool/agent distinction -- what any adequate account of "this system is a tool" vs "this system is an agent" actually has to deliver: which cases it has to classify the way a reasonable person would, which distinctions it absolutely cannot collapse, which facts about how these systems are built it has to stay consistent with. give me the case or consideration behind each requirement, not just the requirement itself. I know people will want to trot out their favorite defintions, so hold off on that; I care about the criteria first.

the cases I keep getting stuck on, roughly in order of how much they bother me: a thermostat; a plain chess engine; a recommender that updates from clicks; a chat model that just answers questions; the same model wired into a tool-calling loop; a model trained with RL against a reward model; a model with no objective at deploy time that will still rewrite its own scaffolding to get better feedback; a human-in-the-loop pipeline where the composite clearly behaves goal-directed even though no single component does; and a swarm of narrow bots whose interaction looks like one agent. the distinctions I don't want flattened: having a goal vs merely behaving as if you had one; capability vs autonomy; whether agency is a property of the system or of the deployment and context; and agency as a matter of degree rather than a binary. also be honest about which of these are real requirements and which are just things one camp wants -- the safety people seem to need a sharp line, the capabilities people say it's all degrees, and I suspect at least two of the desiderata can't be satisfied at once, which I'd actually find useful to know.

so: flag which requirements you'd call non-negotiable and which are camp-specific, and tell me the single requirement that does the most to kill the popular definitions. and for that last case -- the one that rewrites its own scaffold -- say whether your theory calls it a tool or an agent, and whether it lands on that answer for a good reason or by accident.
