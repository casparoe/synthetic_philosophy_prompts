Ok so I'm not a philosopher, I never went to college, but I've gotten kind of obsessed with this on my own — I listen to debates in the car. The reason it's not just a hobby is that I sit on a volunteer citizens panel that advises our county board on parole and early release, so we actually write recomendations and this stuff keeps coming up.

Here's what I want. There's Daniel Dennett, who says free will survives even if everything is caused, and that someone who does something terrible really does deserve something for it. And there's Gregg Caruso, who says free will is an illusion, so nobody deserves anything in that deep sense, but we can still lock up dangerous people for the same reason we'd quarantine someone with a contagious disease — safety, not payback. I've now listened to them go at each other twice and I honestly can't tell how much they actually disagree about, versus one guy saying "desert" and the other saying "no cosmic desert" and them just talking past each other.

So write the document they should have written together. Not a summary of each side, I can get that anywhere — a joint statement, the kind of thing they'd both put their names on, aimed at people like me on a county panel:

1) A numbered list of claims about punishment, desert, and what we can honestly say to victims that both of them would sign. Don't water it down until neither would actually sign it — that's the trap.

2) Then a table of where they still disagree. For each row: the question in neutral wording, then the answer each of them would insist on, in terms each of them would accept. Dennett's column shouldn't read like Caruso's caricature of him and the other way round.

3) Then the cruxes — the sub questions where if you actually settled it, one of them would have to move. For each crux, give me the one case or piece of evidence both would agree is relevant, and write down what each predicts about it in advance. I especially want the crux about whether the victim's mother needing the sentence to mean something counts as a reason for keeping someone locked up, or is just a fact about her we have to work around.

And anchor the whole thing in one case so it doesn't float off into space: a guy who was 19, drunk, killed another kid with a bottle outside a bar, 18 years in now, spotless record inside, got his GED, runs the kitchen. The victim's mother comes to every single hearing and says if he walks out it means her son's life was worth nothing. Tell me what each of them says we should do with him, and which parts of their answers are actually different.

Last thing: if some chunk of their fight is just words — two labels for the same policy — say which chunk, and tell me what my panel should stop arguing about and what we have to decide ourselves because philosophy can't help us. And don't send me a reading list, I'm not going to get through it.
