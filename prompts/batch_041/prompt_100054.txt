I belong to a small reading group that has been chewing over the old question of whether a deterministic universe must also be a predictable one, and we keep running together two claims that I am convinced need to be held apart: first, that the future is already fixed, and second, that an observer situated inside the system could in principle work out what that future is before it happens. The usual illustrations only separate them in the weak, practical sense — chaotic systems whose sensitivity to initial conditions makes long-run forecasting hopeless in practice while leaving in-principle predictability untouched — and that is not what I want. I want a thought experiment that separates the two claims in the strong sense: a system that is fully deterministic, with exactly one actual future, and yet in which no observer who is part of the system can in principle foreknow some specified later state of it, where the obstacle is logical or informational rather than a matter of measurement precision. Constraints: the setup should fit in a single paragraph; it must be a machine or process that a person could in principle build and run rather than a science-fictional device; and you may not reach for quantum indeterminacy, an external oracle, or backward causation. If it helps to have raw material, you may let the system's later state be defined partly in terms of what the in-system predictor announces it will be — but do not simply retell the liar paradox, and make whatever you build a concrete physical process rather than a bare semantic trick.

After the scenario, do the analysis I actually need. State the verdict the determinist thesis gives and the verdict the predictability thesis gives, and show that they come apart for real rather than by equivocating between what holds in principle and what holds in practice. Identify precisely which structural feature of the case is doing the work of breaking the entailment, and tell me what happens when I remove it — does the case collapse back into the ordinary chaotic-but-in-principle-predictable situation, or into something else entirely? Then argue the other side: put on the hat of someone who holds that determinism does entail in-principle predictability, give the strongest objection they could mount against your case, and tell me honestly whether it succeeds or whether the case survives it.
