I run a very small interview podcast — like 300 listeners, mostly grad students and a few people who found me through a link somewhere — and next month's guest is a philosopher whose work I've been following for years. She defends what she calls the stepladder view of moral status, and I want to prep properly rather than wing it. Here's the view so you know what we're dealing with, because I don't want generic questions about animal welfare: she fully accepts that sentience comes in degrees, but she denies that moral status slides up smoothly with it. Instead there are four thresholds — bare experience, valenced experience (pleasure/pain), a self that persists across time, and preferences about one's own future — and crossing any one of them bumps a creature up an entire tier, while all the variation in richness of experience within a tier is morally inert. So on her view the gap between a bee and a fish is supposed to be much bigger than the gap between a fish and a dog, and below the first threshold (thermostats, plants, maybe some very simple nervous systems) nothing counts at all. She also holds that insect numbers don't swamp the calculus, because weight below a threshold is literally zero rather than small-but-positive, which is the part I suspect is most exposed.

Give me eight to ten questions that actually make her work. I've heard her do a bunch of interviews where she just restates the four tiers and everyone nods, and I want to avoid that entirely. At least a few should go after the discreteness itself — if the underlying capacities (integration, memory, whatever it is) vary continuously, what makes the moral jump discontinuous, and isn't that just parking the cutoffs where our intuitions already sit? Then the obvious trap: where exactly is the tier 1 / tier 2 line, what does she say when it turns out to be vague, and how does she stop that vagueness from metastasizing up through every tier. Also press her on the epistemic side — how would we ever know a shrimp is tier 2 rather than tier 1, and what would change her mind — and on the modal case, a creature that has the machinery for valenced experience but in practice never has any. I'd also love one about a creature that flickers: valenced experience for a few seconds a day, or only under stress, or only in juveniles. I'm definately going to run long, so give me a follow-up for each question keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, and flag which ones I should be ready to cut.

Format the whole thing as a JSON array, one object per question, in the order I should ask them, with these fields: "q" (the question in the words I'd actually say into a mic, not a topic label), "target" (one line on which part of her view it's aimed at), "expected_reply" (the answer she's most likely to give), "follow_up" (my next question if she gives that answer), "risk" (high, medium, or low, for how much it could rattle her), and "cut_first" (true or false).
