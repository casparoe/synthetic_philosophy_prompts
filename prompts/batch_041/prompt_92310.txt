I'm in an upper-level seminar on the philosophy of logic, and we've just read Carroll's dialogue in which Achilles and the tortoise argue over the inference from a pair of premises to a conclusion, and I came away convinced that I have seen the real point more clearly than the rest of the class did. As I reconstruct it, the tortoise grants the premises and even grants the conditional that if those premises are true the conclusion is true, yet still refuses to accept the conclusion until Achilles supplies that conditional as a further premise; when he does, the tortoise accepts the new premise but demands yet another conditional licensing the use of the first, and so on without end. The lesson, it seems to me, is that no deductive rule — not even modus ponens — can be justified without circularity, because any attempt to justify a rule of inference must itself employ the very rule it is meant to justify. So the tortoise's demand is perfectly reasonable, and the honest upshot is that deduction bottoms out in an arational commitment, a kind of faith in logic that cannot be argued for. A classmate tells me the escape is to say that modus ponens is not a premise but a 'rule of inference', which strikes me as a mere verbal dodge: a rule I cannot justify is still only a rule I have decided to follow, and I could equally well decide not to follow it.

What I want from you is guidance on where to go from here. If deduction is unjustifiable in the way the tortoise shows, should I embrace some form of pluralism and hold that deduction is answerable to a non-deductive standard, perhaps inference to the best explanation, or is there some way of granting the tortoise his demand and still meeting it — for instance by letting the hierarchy of rules run on forever and calling that a foundation rather than a failure? And what does this leave of mathematics: if the tortoise is right, is proof itself just a very disciplined exercise of the will, or is there something in the very idea of following a rule that the tortoise has misunderstood from the outset? I would rather be told that my reading is confused than be handed a comforting reconciliation, so please be direct about which of my commitments has to go.
