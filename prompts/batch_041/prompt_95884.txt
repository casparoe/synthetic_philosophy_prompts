Here's my reconstruction of the post, roughly in its order. Kim's argument starts from the causal closure of physics — every physical event that has a cause at all has a sufficient physical cause. So when I decide to raise my arm and it goes up, the neural and muscular story is already enough to produce the rising. The post adds that on any physicalist picture the mental supervenes on the physical anyway, which it glosses as: same brain, same thought, the mental is nothing over and above the physical. Now if my decision is also a cause of the arm going up, the rising is overdetermined, like a man killed by two bullets fired at the same instant. Real overdetermination is a coincidence, and we shouldn't think the mental-physical case is a coincidence, so the mental cause gets excluded and the physical one wins by default. This shows, the post says, that non-reductive physicalism collapses: you can have irreducible mental properties, or you can have mental causation, but not both. Its verdict is that Kim himself thinks honesty forces the second horn — we're stuck with epiphenomenalism, your decision doesn't really cause your arm to go up, and belief-desire psychology survives only as a convenient shorthand, the way we still say the sun rises. The post also says the whole thing is really Descartes' interaction problem in modern dress: the same mystery as how an immaterial mind could shove a physical body, just with the ghost subtracted. And it closes with the image of the mind as software running on the brain's hardware — it describes whats happening, but the transistors do all the work.
