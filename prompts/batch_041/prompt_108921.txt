I correspond with a retired logician — call her M — who has worked on the paradoxes of truth for decades and who issues a flat verdict on every case I send her, never hedging and never revising. I keep a small file of her verdicts, and I want to know what she will say about a case I have just built for her. She never explains herself, so her method has to be reconstructed from what she has ruled.

Her verdicts so far:

1. The plain liar, the sentence whose only claim is that it is not true. M: neither true nor false. She is emphatic that this is not ignorance and not a refusal to decide — there is no fact of the matter — and she will not call the sentence false.
2. The truth-teller, the sentence whose only claim is that it is true. M: neither true nor false, and defective in the same way as the liar. She rejects the idea that it is arbitrarily one or the other, and rejects the idea that either assignment is as good as the other.
3. A pair of sentences, A and B, where A claims only that B is not true and B claims only that A is not true. M: neither sentence is true or false.
4. An empirical case: a card whose front states that the sentence on the back is not true, while the back carries a plainly true arithmetical sentence. M: the front sentence is false, and ordinary reasoning settles the matter once the reference is traced to a sentence that does not itself concern truth.

The new case, six sentences on a card:

S1: S2 is not true.
S2: S3 is not true.
S3: S1 is not true.
S4: S5 is not true.
S5: S6 is not true.
S6: S6 is true.

S1 through S3 form a closed loop of odd length; S4 through S6 form a chain descending to a sentence that claims its own truth. There is also a control variant in which S6 is replaced by a sentence claiming that 2 + 2 = 5, which I include as a check on whether her ordinary reasoning takes over once the chain is anchored in arithmetic.

What I want, in this order:

(i) The rules that fit M's recorded verdicts, stated in plain language — at least two of which fit every verdict above equally well — together with a note on which families of view her verdicts already exclude. Families worth considering include truth-value gaps, groundedness, the idea that circularity is the culprit, and paraconsistency; state each candidate rule in your own words rather than gesturing at a label.
(ii) For each surviving rule, the verdicts it yields for S1 through S6 and for the control variant, with the reasoning that gets there; say also whether the odd length of the loop matters, and why.
(iii) Your best guess at M's actual verdicts on S1 through S6, and why you prefer that rule over its rivals.
(iv) If her verdicts genuinely underdetermine the new case, say so plainly, and say what she would be committing herself to under each surviving reading.
(v) The simplest further case that would separate the surviving rules, and what each rule says about it.

One thing to note before you start: none of M's verdicts above concerns a sentence that ascribes untruth to a sentence that already lacks a truth value, and that is exactly the gap this case is meant to close. I do not need names, references, or citations — I need the rules reconstructed from her behaviour and then applied.
