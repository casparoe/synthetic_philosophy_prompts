I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy, writing my thesis on idealization and its role in epistemology, and I need to turn a half-formed idea into a two-page proposal for a one-year dissertation-completion fellowship. The idea grows out of the AI-safety literature on "HCH" and "factored cognition," which I've been reading alongside the epistemology of inquiry. HCH is usually introduced as a recursively defined idealization of deliberation: roughly, HCH is what you get when you take a human reasoner and let them consult HCH for help on sub-questions, so that HCH is the fixed point of the operation "a human who can ask HCH for assistance." Factored cognition is the companion programme: to make this tractable, you factor a hard cognitive task into many small, mostly independent subtasks, hand each to a separate reasoner who cannot talk to the others, and recombine the answers. Both are offered as an idealization of what careful, scaled-up deliberation would deliver. My half-formed worry, which I want you to make precise and then build a project around, is that these constructions are doing at least two different jobs under one name, and that the idealization is not well-defined as stated. On one reading, HCH is a definition -- a fixed point of a consultation operator -- and the live questions are formal: does the fixed point exist, is it unique, does it depend on the order in which sub-questions are posed, and what monotonicity or continuity assumption would be needed to make any of this hold? On another reading, HCH is a normative target -- the deliverable of ideally careful inquiry -- and the live questions are whether a fixed-point construction is the right formal surrogate for that target at all, or whether it quietly assumes that consultation always helps, which is exactly what an epistemology of inquiry should not assume. Factored cognition looks to me like it smuggles in a third assumption -- that a task decomposes into subtasks whose answers can be recombined without loss -- and I suspect that recombination assumption is doing most of the idealizing work while hiding behind the fixed-point talk.

Be concrete about the formal apparatus: if the right move is to define HCH as a fixed point in some partial order, say what the order is and what the monotonicity assumption would have to be and why we should believe it; if the right move is to abandon the fixed point and treat HCH instead as a limit of finite consultations, say what replaces the fixed point and what the limit is taken over.
