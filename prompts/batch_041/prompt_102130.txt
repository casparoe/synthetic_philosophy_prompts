ok, so this is something I've been meaning to actually sit down and build for a while. I mod a small forum/discord for people who like arguing about this kind of thing (we're the crowd that read the Sequences, then got really into the replication-crisis discourse, then started reading history to recover), and every few months we have the same fight about the Holocaust perpetrators and whether "ordinary men" or "willing executioners" is right, and it always goes in circles because nobody actually disagrees about the facts, they disagree about which facts are load-bearing. So I want to turn it into an actual instrument: a decision tree / flowchart that takes someone from the shared starting point to a named position, where every branch is a question they can answer about a concrete case without already knowing which label it's going to land them on. Sort people by what they believe, not by which book they've read.

Start node, shared by everyone: some large number of fairly ordinary people — not obviously sadistic, not obviously fanatical, with normal jobs and families — took part in killing civilians and Jews, and most of them were not forced at gunpoint. Assume we grant that. Now the tree has to figure out what they actually think explains it.

Things I want the tree to force a verdict on, roughly in this order (but rearrange if the logic wants it):

1. Whether the perpetrators' *beliefs* about what they were doing (did they think it was necessary, justified, or at least not wrong?) are doing the explanatory work, or whether their beliefs are basically epiphenomenal and what matters is the situation/orders/peer pressure. This is the big split for me, because it's where the culture/ideology explanation and the situational explanation come apart, and I suspect a lot of people hold a muddle here.
2. Whether character/disposition matters at all — i.e., is "ordinary" true, or is it a myth and these were mostly a self-selected or self-revealed minority of monsters? Put the dispositional view on the tree even if nobody in my forum defends it.
3. Whether the relevant situation is best characterized as authority/obedience, as gradual escalation and desensitization, as role/culture within the unit, or as sheer careerism and cowardice. These aren't the same thing and I want to know if they come apart on any case.
4. Whether the question of individual guilt/culpability should even track the causal explanation — i.e., does "the situation did it" exculpate or not?

Leaves I want to see named: the eliminativist-culture/antisemitism view (Goldhagen, Hitlers Willing Executioners), the situational/ordinary-men view (Browning), the obedience-to-authority view, the role/internalized-norm view, the thoughtlessness/banality view, a dispositional bad-apples view, a two-level view where culture sets the ceiling and situation decides who fills it, and a this-question-is-malformed/no-single-explanation leaf. If you think there's a leaf I'm missing that belongs, add it.

Cases you're allowed to use as branch prompts: the reservist police battalion in occupied Poland where the commander offered his middle-aged men the chance to sit out the first massacre and almost none took it; the Milgram obedience setup; the prison-guard experiments; the camp-staff-vs-frontline-killer distinction; the well-documented fact that some men asked to be excused and were not punished. You can phrase these in your own words, I don't need you to cite anything and honestly please don't — no reading lists, no page numbers, no "see X 1996". I want this answerable by someone who just knows the general shape of the debate.

Two more things I actually care about: (a) flag any pair of leaves your tree structurally cannot separate — I have a suspicion that the obedience leaf and the situational leaf are the same leaf wearing different hats, and that the thoughtlessness leaf might be unfalsifiable in a way that makes it not really a position, and I want you to tell me honestly if that's right. (b) For any combination of branch answers that no real position occupies, note it and say why — incoherent, just unattractive, or merely never defended. And phrase every branch node as a question about a case, not as "do you think character matters?", because otherwise people just answer with the label they already like.

Size: aim for 6 to 9 branch nodes and up to about 10 leaves. Audience is smart non-specialists who know the pop versions of all this, so don't explain what the Milgram experiment is.
