Bit of a weird one, hope this is the right place for it. I run a small catering thing with a mate of mine and we're always having to split stuff between us — money, shifts, who gets the van on the weekend — and I've realised I keep judging whether a split is fair by one particular rule that I've never seen written down anywhere. I want to know if it's a real idea with a name, or just something I invented in the van.

Here's the rule. Work out the best each of us could possibly get — the deal that's best for me if we completely ignore what's good for him, and the deal that's best for him if we ignore me. Work out the worst each of us would put up with. Then pick the deal where we've each travelled the same *fraction* of the way from our own worst to our own best. So if the very best I could walk away with is £200 and the very best he could walk away with is £5,000, and every deal trades one against the other, my rule says find the deal where I've got half of my 200 and he's got half of his 5,000 — so £100 for me, £2,500 for him. Massively unequal in money, and my gut still says that's the fair one and that splitting things down the middle is the wrong instinct. I couldn't defend that to you if you pushed me though.

Four things I get stuck on:

The "best I could possibly get" is a deal where he rolls over and hands me everything. It's not on the table, nobody would ever offer it, so I'm measuring myself against something that can't happen and then taking wherever the line to that impossible point hits the edge of what's actually possible. Is that how it's meant to work? Feels like cheating.

My mate did a bit of economics and reckons the actual textbook answer is to pick the deal that makes the *product* of our two gains as big as possible, and that when you're just splitting a pot of cash it comes out as half each anyway. Fine, but then why do the two rules agree in the easy case and disagree as soon as we're dividing different sorts of things — he wants the Saturday off, I want the cash?

Who says it's half, anyway? If we agreed on 60/40 of our own maximums, is that still the same idea or a different one with a different name?

And there's three of us sometimes, and then my rule just dies, because there's no single deal where all three of us get our maximum at once so I can't even draw the line. Does the whole idea only work for two people, or is there a version that survives three?

One more: if he's got another job offer and can just walk, I've been treating that as his "worst he'd put up with". Is that right, or is the walk-away a separate thing?

What I'm after is not a label I can drop in conversation — I want to know exactly what my rule is, which of the things that do have names it's closest to, how it's different from each of those, and precisely where it stops working. If it turns out nobody's named it, tell me that and name it yourself, but I'd rather have the carving than the name.
