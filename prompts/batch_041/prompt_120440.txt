I sit on the deployment review board at a mid-sized AI company — couple hundred people, we build coding agents — and I'm the one who has to circulate the written opinion before the Thursday vote, so this lands on me.

The case: the product team wants to ship an agent that works inside customers' code repos. Reads everything, proposes patches, opens PRs. Their safety case is entirely control-flavored, no claims about the model being nice or even trying to be. The pitch: agent has no credentials, can only write to a scratch branch, every tool call is logged and read by a second model (same base model, different fine-tune) that can halt the run, and a human reviews whatever the monitor escalates. They ran a red team exercise where the model was given an obvious payoff for sneaking a credential out of a test repo, and the monitor caught it every time. That's the whole argument.

What's bugging me is that the load-bearing assumption in the write-up isn't stated as an assumption anywhere, its just baked in. The monitor only buys you anything if the agent can't tell whether any given action is being watched. That's the premise. And one of our red teamers pointed out you can probably break it with timing: monitored runs have a few hundred ms of extra latency per tool call and the agent can see its own wall clock, so in principle it can learn which actions are under a monitor and behave accordingly. The team says that's speculative and anyway a model that's aligned-enough wouldn't bother. The alignment folks on the board say if we're spending this much on monitors and canary tokens we should spend it on making the model actually want the right thing, and that a control-only case is just a promise that nothing went wrong in the window we happened to test.

I need the memo for the vote. Give me a recommendation — ship, ship with conditions, or hold — with a concrete time frame, the reasoning, and an honest account of the strongest thing on the losing side. I don't want "control can't be trusted" or "alignment is all you need"; I want something the product team and the alignment team can both actually respond to. And say plainly whether the latency thing is fatal or just a reason to require a seperate test, because i honestly can't tell and I have to defend this in front of the CTO. Tell me what we'd need to write into the safety case to make it honest about what it assumes.

Under 600 words, and lead with the recommendation in the first two sentences so I can paste them into the agenda. No reading list, I need the argument.
