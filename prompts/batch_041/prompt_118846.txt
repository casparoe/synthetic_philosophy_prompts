The idea, still half-formed. The standard defence of Malthus is that he was right but early; the standard objection is that "early" has no expiry date, so the defence looks indistinguishable from an unfalsifiable theory with a promissory note attached. What interests me is that the usual ways of spotting an illegitimate rescue — that it was introduced only after the anomaly, that it wasn't independently motivated, that it left the theory less falsifiable than before — all seem to misfire on theories of this kind, because Malthusianism isn't a dated prediction at all. It's a claim about a tendency: population presses on subsistence unless something checks it, where what counts as a check is left remarkably open. That structure makes almost any outcome compatible with the theory unless we say in advance what would have counted against it.

My hunch is that we've been asking the wrong question. Instead of asking when a modification was made (post hoc) or whether it eventually paid off (which only hindsight settles), we should ask what a modification does to the theory's confirmation horizon — the interval over which the theory commits you to a determinate, checkable claim about an observable ratio. I want to sort rescue moves into three kinds and ask whether they differ in what they do to that horizon: (a) horizon extension, "not yet, but later," where the predicted outcome is kept and its date pushed back; (b) domain contraction, where the antecedent is narrowed, e.g. the tendency is said to hold only where some factor is genuinely fixed, or only while fertility remains non-discretionary; (c) indicator substitution, where the same underlying tendency is said to surface in a different observable, mortality crises giving way to delayed marriage and lower fertility.

Tentative thesis: (b) preserves empirical content when the narrowing condition can be supported by evidence available independently of the anomaly it was introduced to absorb; (a) and (c) do not, unless they carry a new, dated, risky prediction about an indicator the unmodified theory left open. I'd want a criterion that isn't merely the ad hocness intuition restated — roughly, a modification preserves content only if it leaves the theory's central quantity in principle measurable and adds at least one new dated commitment. And I want the proposal to be honest that this may collapse into a standard criterion: if the three-way taxonomy turns out to be extensionally equivalent to an ordinary falsifiability or probabilistic account, the project has failed as an original contribution, and I'd rather learn that in month two than month eight.
