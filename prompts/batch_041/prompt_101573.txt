Two research camps in evolutionary biology have been arguing past each other for decades about how much of organic form is adaptation, and I want the document they could actually both sign. On one side stands the adaptationist: someone who holds that natural selection is the default and usually sufficient explanation for traits that look designed, that optimality modeling is a legitimate and productive method rather than a confession of Panglossian faith, and that appeals to byproduct, constraint, and drift are too often escape hatches dressed up as findings. On the other side stands the spandrel critic: someone who holds that the adaptationist inference is systematically over-applied, that developmental and phylogenetic constraint, correlated response, and non-adaptive history account for a large and under-appreciated share of form, and that in the cases that matter the standard adaptationist practice is close to unfalsifiable. Take both figures at full strength: the adaptationist must concede nothing a working biologist would reject, and the critic must be allowed the whole force of the critique.

Draft the joint statement these two could put their names to, doing the following work.

First, a numbered list of agreed claims, substantive rather than platitudinous. I especially want the two camps to prise apart three things that are habitually run together under the single word adaptationism: (i) the empirical claim about how prevalent adaptation is; (ii) the methodological claim that adaptation is the right default hypothesis, or the right research heuristic, in the absence of evidence; and (iii) the explanatory claim that once an adaptation has been identified there is nothing further left to explain. State where each camp stands on each of the three, and which of the three the real dispute is about. Get them to agree, too, on the logical geography of a trait that is currently maintained by selection without having been selected for its present effect, and on whether adaptation and exaptation name exclusive categories or successive stages of a single history. Where a claim cannot be made non-vacuous, cut it rather than pad the list.

Second, the precise statement of each surviving disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. Do not smuggle a refutation in under a neutral label.

Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. I expect at least these candidates, and I want a verdict on whether each is a genuine crux or a merely verbal dispute — whether the spandrel critique is an empirical thesis or a methodological one; whether the charge of unfalsifiability lands against optimality modeling itself or only against a degenerate form of it; and whether the prevalence question is even well posed, given that prevalence must be relativized to some set of traits, some level of description, and some timescale.

Fourth, for each genuine crux, a test, case, or argument that both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Where the two sides would accept the same observation but read it differently, say so explicitly rather than manufacturing a difference in prediction.

Fifth, a stipulated case treated as a worked example: a lineage in which a costly and apparently well-engineered trait is shown by a well-powered comparative and experimental study to have a selection gradient indistinguishable from zero, while a developmentally correlated trait in the same structure is under strong directional selection. State what each side predicts, what each would say the study shows, and whether the two readings can be separated by any further observation either side would accept as decisive.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a table of disagreements, with a column for the crux each row turns on and a column for the discriminating evidence each side proposes. Where you judge a disagreement to be verbal, name the part and say why, instead of forcing a row. Begin directly with the joint statement itself, with no preface and no restating of this request.
