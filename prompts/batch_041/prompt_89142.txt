I keep noticing a pattern in how political and philosophical slogans wear out, and I dont know whether it has a name. A phrase starts out making one specific, arguable claim — a protest chant that demands a particular policy, or a word like 'harm' that used to pick out one kind of injury. Then it spreads. And the versions that spread furthest seem to be the ones where the arguable part has quietly dropped out, so after a while the phrase just means 'good thing' or 'bad thing' and nearly anyone can endorse it. The people still using it in the old narrow sense get treated as pedants. It doesn't feel like people misusing a word so much as the phrase itself being selected for how many people can sign on, with the disagreement bred out.

Is there a name for that? I know the euphemism treadmill and concept creep are nearby, but the first is about replacing words for unpleasant things and the second is about categories quietly widening. What I'm after is a term losing its edge in order to maximise its reach. Is it just semantic bleaching, or is there a specific idea in the memetics / cultural-evolution literature about selection favouring the most endorsable version? I dont want just a label — I want to know what the mechanism is supposed to be, and where the line is between this and ordinary meaning drift.
