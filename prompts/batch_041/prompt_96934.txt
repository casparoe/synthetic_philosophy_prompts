I'm giving a talk to my department's metaphysics-of-science reading group in a few weeks and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a summary of my own paper.

The talk: I argue the usual fink/mask distinction is doing less work than everyone assumes. Quick gloss so we're on the same page about what I'm attacking: a fink is a case where the trigger itself kills the disposition (the wire that stops conducting the instant you touch it), a mask is a case where the trigger fires and the disposition survives but something else in the way blocks the manifestation (the fragile glass packed in foam), and a mimic is where the manifestation shows up with no disposition behind it. My claim is that finks and masks are the same phenomenon wearing different hats — in both cases what's really going on is that the disposition's own internal structure has a slot where the trigger and the background conditions interact — and that once you admit this you need a seperate third category, gates, where the trigger neither kills the disposition nor blocks it but reroutes it, so the thing manifests as something other than what its name advertises. From there I argue dispositions are individuated by their whole manifestation profile — the mapping from trigger-kinds to manifestation-kinds across normal-ish background conditions — and that "what this disposition is for" is just a claim about the shape of that mapping, nothing more.

The room: three Humeans about laws (one best-systems guy, two who think dispositions are placeholders until you hand them a categorical ground), one neo-Aristotelian who treats powers as irreducible and will hate that I'm taking the profile as primitive, a philosopher of physics who will ask whether any of this survives contact with statistical mechanics, two cognitive scientists who build causal Bayes nets and will translate everything into graphs wether I want them to or not, and one emeritus who worked on the conditional analysis in the 70s and thinks this was all wrapped up decades ago.

Give me the objections I'll actually get, ordered by how much damage they do, each phrased the way that person would phrase it — the Humean's version will be about truthmakers, the Bayes-net people's will be about whether my gates are just unobserved confounders, the emeritus will be about whether I've read the paper that already said this. For each: a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Then tell me which objection I should just concede, which one I have to win for the talk to land, and the one question I'm hoping nobody asks. Q&A is 25 minutes, and no, I don't need a reading list.
