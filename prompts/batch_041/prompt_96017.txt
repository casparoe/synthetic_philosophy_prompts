What I want: read that scene through the centipede game and the paradox of backward induction. Quick version in case it helps -- in a centipede game two people take turns, and at each turn you either grab a slightly bigger share of a pot that keeps growing or you pass it along, and if you reason backward from the last round you get a unique subgame perfect equilibrium where the first player grabs on turn one, so nobody ever gets the big pot. The paradox is that the logic looks airtight but it makes both players worse off, and in lab experiments people pass for a few rounds and end up better off than the theory predicts. I also know the usual worry is that backward induction needs common knowledge of rationality in a really strong sense, and that it forces you to plan out what youd do at nodes you never actually reach.

So: is Vizzini's ladder of reasoning backward induction, or is it a different kind of failure?
