My closest friend has been offered a peculiar deal, and I promised to help her think it through before she gives an answer. A wealthy eccentric she knows — the sort who funds odd experiments — has offered to pay off her student loans in a single payment. The only condition is that she sit for a session with an experimental device its makers call an "intent scanner," which they claim can tell, from a person's brain activity, whether that person genuinely intends, at that moment, to perform a particular act later. The act is to swallow a small dose of a substance that will make her feel genuinely unwell for an evening — nothing dangerous, just miserable — and she is free, once the scanner has been read and the money transferred, to walk away and never touch the stuff. The payment is for the intention alone; whether she actually goes through with it makes no difference to the money.

She is tempted, and she has two worries she can't sort out. First, she says she cannot simply decide to intend something she has no reason to do — and if she has no reason to drink the stuff once the money is banked, how can she sincerely intend to drink it now? She suspects any intention she managed to manufacture for the payout would be a counterfeit, and that trying to produce one on purpose is somehow self-defeating. Second, she wonders whether it even matters: if the scanner works, why not just take the money and see what happens; if it doesn't, she's being paid for nothing. And she keeps circling back to whether a machine could ever read off "her intention" in the first place, or whether what it reads would be some state she never really occupied.

What should I tell her? I want a serious answer to the practical question — should she take the deal, and on what understanding of it — but I'm really after the conceptual diagnosis underneath: what intentions must be, such that a reward can be offered for merely having one with nothing further required; whether an intention can be formed for a reward and still be genuine; what the case reveals about the difference between intending, wanting, and merely predicting one's own behavior; and whether the answer shifts if the scanner is unreliable, if the act were pleasant rather than unpleasant, or if she were permitted to change her mind the instant the money cleared. Please keep separate the parts of this that are settled among people who study it from the parts that are genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as though it were the consensus. If the practical advice turns on which contested view one holds, say so plainly and tell me what each view would advise.
