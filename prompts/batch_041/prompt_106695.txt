I teach a grad seminar on the metaphysics of biology, and every year the species unit melts everyone's brain — the students walk in thinking a species is basically a chemical element and walk out realizing it's more like... a vibe? Anyway, I want some comic relief for the reading packet, and I figured you'd be good at this.

Here's the ask. Write me a satirical FAQ / customer-support help page for a fictional startup, Panbiota Genomics, that sells a mail-in DNA kit promising to tell you with total confidence "what species your sample is." Think 23andMe, but for biodiversity. Format it as the company's official help page, roughly 700–800 words, as maybe 8–10 question-and-answer entries.

The comedy has to come out of the actual science and philosophy, not out of wacky branding or made-up jargon. Every answer should be something the company could plausibly say while quietly committing itself to a real view about what a species is. So for instance: what happens when the sample is a ring species, or a hybrid, or an asexual clone, or a bacterium whose "species" is just a convention set by a sequence-similarity cutoff, or a fossil that's really one slice of a continuous lineage? How does the company deal with the fact that its answer depends on which species concept it happened to pick, so a different lab running a different concept would hand the customer a different answer? What's the refund policy when two experts disagree? Is there a clause about the number of species it reports shifting every time it updates its reference database? The "we give you the single true answer" guarantee should be doing alot of ironic work here.

The target is the idea that there's a mind-independent fact of the matter about where one species ends and the next begins — and the way that idea gets marketed and bureaucratized. I want someone who's read the species-problem literature to laugh and also wince in recognition. Don't explain the joke, and don't wink in the copy — the fine print stays deadpan the whole way through. It should still be readable by a smart person who's never heard of the species problem; they should finish it understanding why the question is genuinely hard.

One last thing: no fake studies and no fake citations, definately don't invent papers or experts — you don't need any references, just the science as it's actually understood.
