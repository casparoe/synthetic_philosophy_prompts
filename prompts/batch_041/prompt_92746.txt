I'm an intern on a state senate committee, and last week we sat through a hearing on a bill setting a statewide minimum standard for short-term rentals, and the chair must have said six times that decisions should be made as close to the people as possible — mostly to argue the bill should be gutted, but once, weirdly, to argue the state ought to overrule a city that had gone its own way on the same issue. I want to understand the thing before I draft the memo, because it definately can't mean what it sounds like it means.

So lay out the different precise claims that slogan could be making, as a menu rather than a verdict, running from the trivial to the radical. What is "close" supposed to be: physical or administrative distance, the smallest unit containing everyone affected, the unit with the best local knowledge, the unit where preferences are most uniform, the unit that bears the costs of what it decides, the lowest unit that can actually solve the problem, the unit with the cleanest democratic accountability, the unit people can exit? And does "the people" mean the people of the locality, or the people as a whole — because that one seems to flip the whole thing. For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude nobody could deny, a substantive and defensible claim, or false or self-undermining, and which readings would actually support each of the two opposite uses the chair made of it. My suspicion is that the literal version bottoms out at the individual, which is not what he's after, but tell me if that's wrong. I'd also like to know, per reading, where the burden of proof lands — on the state or on the locality — and whether any of these readings could settle a case on its own or only tell you who gets to decide.

One more thing: a colleague says the EU treaties contain the same idea plus an extra clause to the effect that the higher level acts only if the goal can't be achieved well enough lower down. If that's roughly right, say what that clause does to the slogan, since it sounds like it could cut the other way.

Keep it as a list I can hand to my boss. No citations needed — she wants the arguments, not the litrature.
