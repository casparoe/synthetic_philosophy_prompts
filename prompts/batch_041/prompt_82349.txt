I am an undergraduate in a philosophy of science course, and the session I am leading next week is on Lakatos and the methodology of scientific research programmes. Rather than summarise the framework for the group, I would like to open with a poem that makes the machinery visible — one in which the structure of a programme, rather than its vocabulary, does the work. Could you write it?

What I have in mind:

- Roughly six quatrains. It should rhyme; the scheme is yours, but the recurrences below need to be audible.
- One line is the hard core. It returns verbatim in every stanza, is never altered, and is never argued for anywhere in the poem. It should read as something held rather than something established — the reader should feel that no stanza could have produced it and that it would survive any of them.
- A second line returns in every stanza after the first, but with exactly one word changed each time. That line is the protective belt: one and the same claim, continually re-worded so that whatever the preceding stanza put in its way no longer touches it. By the final stanza the drift should be plain, and a reader should be able to locate the point at which the re-wordings stop anticipating anything and begin merely accommodating what has already happened.
- At least two stanzas should do no patching at all. They should be the programme building out what the world must be like if the core is right — work done ahead of the evidence, from the programme's own agenda rather than in response to a threat. Someone who knows the methodology should be able to sort the building stanzas from the patching ones without being told which is which.
- The ending should refuse a verdict. The criterion of progress is retrospective and comparative, so I want the programme left suspended between its two possible fates, with the hard-core line standing unchanged in the last stanza as though nothing had occurred.

Two constraints on the material. First, keep the technical vocabulary out of the poem itself: the terms research programme, hard core, protective belt, positive and negative heuristic, progressive and degenerating should not appear in the verse; a title may do a little work. Second, do not build it around a famous episode from the history of science. Invent the programme — something small and unglamorous, in a corner of a discipline — so that we feel the machinery rather than recognise the case. I would like a real poem, intelligible to someone who has never heard of Lakatos, and accurate enough that a specialist would find each stanza in its right place.

After the poem, please add a short note: say which reading of Lakatos the poem commits to and where it takes liberties with him or leans on a contested interpretation; name the single line or structural move you are least confident about and say why; and give me your confidence that a reader who knows the methodology would identify each stanza for what it is.
