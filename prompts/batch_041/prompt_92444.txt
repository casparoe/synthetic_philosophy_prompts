I have been trying to work out what I actually think about believing things on other people's say-so, and I would like a principle that captures the pattern, if there is one. I have no training in philosophy, so what follows is just my own reaction to cases, and I would rather have an honest account of them than a flattering one.

When a stranger in the street tells me the bank on the corner is shut for refurbishment, I think it is perfectly reasonable to believe him and walk to the next one. When my sister tells me that a mutual friend said something cutting about me at a dinner I missed, I believe her, but I have a nagging sense that I ought not to — that I am taking a single unchecked word for something that matters, and that I would be a fool to repeat it. I cannot see what the difference is supposed to be. Both are one person, unverifiable by me, with nothing much obviously riding on it.

Some further verdicts, in no particular order.

When a doctor tells me my knee is not broken, I accept it completely. When the same doctor, at the same appointment, tells me the government's economic policy is sound, I do not accept it at all, though I have no more evidence against him on the second point than on the first.

When someone tells me about a pain in his own chest, I take it as settled. When the same man tells me why he did something — that he was not really angry, only tired — I think I know better than he does.

When a chemistry textbook states the boiling point of a substance, I accept it without a flicker, though I have never checked it and could not. When a newspaper reports that a bridge has been closed, I accept it with a small discount; when it reports what a minister really meant, I discount it almost to nothing.

When a man I know to be a shameless exaggerator tells me the post office closes at noon, I believe him. When the same man tells me the woman at the counter was rude to him, I do not.

When two strangers who have not spoken to each other independently tell me the same unlikely thing, I believe it far more readily than either alone. When one man tells me a thing and then tells me that a second person confirmed it, and it turns out the second person had only heard it from the first, my confidence collapses.

When I ask a passer-by the way to the station, I follow his directions without a second thought. When a passer-by volunteers, unasked, that the station is the other way, I check for myself before turning round.

When a child tells me there is a wolf in the garden, I do not believe it, though I have no reason to think the child a liar.

When my neighbour tells me his fence is on my land, I want to see the deeds. When he tells me it will rain by evening, I take an umbrella.

My tentative principle is something like this: I am entitled to believe what I am told when the person telling me has no reason to want me to believe it, and when the sort of thing being said is not the sort of thing that gets shaped by wanting it to be true. That seems to cover the stranger, the textbook, the exaggerator on the post office and the doctor on the knee. But it plainly does not cover the doctor on economics, or my sister on the dinner, or the neighbour on the fence, and I suspect it gets the child wrong as well.

What I want from you is this. Tell me what principle, or small set of principles, actually fits the verdicts above, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a new case. For each clause, say which of my cases forces it. Then tell me what the resulting account says about three cases I have not put to you: whether I should believe a mechanic who tells me my gearbox is failing but who stands to profit from the repair; whether I should believe a dying man's account of a quarrel when everyone else present tells it differently; and whether I should believe a man who tells me, sincerely, that he is not in pain when I can see that he is. Finally, be straight with me about which of my verdicts the principle cannot accommodate, and whether it is the principle or the verdict that should give way. I am not after a survey of what philosophers have written on this; I want to know what the principle is.
