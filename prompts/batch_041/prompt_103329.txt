I sit in on a seminar on classical social theory, and last week two of its participants, D and K, spent the whole session arguing over whether Max Weber's Protestant ethic thesis can be treated as a genuine causal explanation or is at best an interpretive redescription of a historical episode. Neither gave ground, and the discussion simply ran out of time. I would like a point-by-point adjudication of the exchange as it actually went, not a general essay about Weber or about explanation in the social sciences.

The exchange, as I recorded it, went roughly as follows.

(1) D opened by saying that the thesis claims a religious ethic caused the rise of a rationalized, acquisitive economic order, yet the only evidence ever offered is a correspondence: where the ethic flourished, capitalism flourished. Since both terms are described after the fact, and since the ethic is itself inferred largely from the same disciplined behaviors it is invoked to explain, the thesis is a correlation dressed up as a cause. It makes no differential prediction that could fail.

(2) K replied that this misstates the claim. The thesis is not that Protestantism correlates with capitalism but that a specific mechanism is at work. The doctrine of double predestination left believers unable to know whether they were among the elect, while the ordinary means of relieving that anxiety — confession, sacraments, the church's mediation — were unavailable. Worldly labor in a calling was then reinterpreted as evidence of election, so the anxiety was channeled into methodical, continuous, self-denying work. A mechanism with named links is not a correlation, and each link can be tested: where the doctrine was weakened, or the anxiety channeled elsewhere, the behavioral consequence should be absent.

(3) D granted the mechanism for asceticism but said it still does not deliver capitalism. Anxiety plus a duty to work predicts hard work, frugality, and a suspicion of enjoyment; it does not predict the reinvestment of the resulting surplus in an ever-expanding rationalized enterprise rather than in land, luxury, charity, or hoarding. Weber needs an extra premise, namely that the surplus could not be consumed and therefore had to be plowed back — but that premise follows from any ascetic ethic, so the specifically Protestant content is doing no work. A Confucian mandarin ethic, a Stoic ethic, or a certain reading of Jewish law could each supply work plus frugality. What is supposed to be unique here?

(4) K answered that the content does work, because the demand is not merely work plus frugality but the organization of the whole of life as a coherent methodical system, together with the requirement that success in a calling be read as a sign. That combination — anxiety about one's state, the injunction to read worldly success as its sign, and the ban on resting in that success — is unstable in a particular way: it drives endless systematic expansion and forbids the terminal enjoyment of its own results. Other asceticisms can produce discipline, but they typically terminate in contemplation, ritual purity, or otherworldly withdrawal rather than in the rationalized accumulation of capital. The specific content therefore predicts a specific direction of behavior.

(5) D then turned to Weber's own ending. Once the capitalist order is established, the ethic becomes unnecessary and is in fact eroded by the very success it produced, leaving a hardened casing that outlasts the beliefs that built it. So the thesis claims a cause that is destroyed by its own effect. That is coherent for an origin story, but it leaves the causal claim about a single, unrepeatable transition, with no independent variation to exploit and no way to run the counterfactual. If the ethic had been absent, would capitalism have failed to appear, appeared later, or appeared in different ideological clothing? The thesis gives no principled answer, because every outcome can be accommodated. And if the ethic is not needed to sustain capitalism, its causal role shrinks to a nudge at one moment in one region.

(6) K conceded that the thesis is about origins and that the ongoing role is not the claim. But origins are legitimate causal questions, and a cause that is later outgrown does not thereby cease to be a cause — scaffolding can be removed once the building stands. As for the counterfactual, the thesis does specify what should be found: not simply capitalism somewhere, but a particular sequence in which religious rationalization precedes and shapes economic rationalization, and in which the carriers of the ethic are the ones who first organize enterprise methodically. That is checkable against the record of who did what, when. K added that Weber's notion of elective affinity is not a causal claim at all; it explains why this ethic was taken up by these strata rather than others, and it can be spelled out as a selection or socialization story rather than a psychological one: communities that disciplined their members this way produced members who prospered in the new economy, and the ethic spread along with them.

(7) D replied that if K retreats to selection, new debts come due. A selection explanation needs variation, a selection pressure, and a mechanism of inheritance, and it must show that the trait is reproduced because of the advantage rather than merely accompanied by it. The claim that firms or families with the ethic outcompeted others is exactly the pattern that gets called a functionalist fallacy when the feedback loop is left unspecified: an outcome that looks beneficial is read back as its own cause. And on K's own account the ethic spread by socialization, not by differential reproduction, so the selection story and the psychological story are two different theses, and Weber cannot have both without choosing.

Please adjudicate this move by move, rating each of the seven moves on a scale from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and briefly justifying each rating. In doing so, distinguish the disagreements that are substantive from those that are merely verbal — for instance, whether causal explanation is being used in a mechanism sense or in a difference-making and contrastive sense. Say what both parties are missing, and say who is more right overall. You need not cite any literature or supply a reading list; the task is to assess the reasoning as presented.
