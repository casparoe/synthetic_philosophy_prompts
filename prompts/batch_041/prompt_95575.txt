I'm running a 50 minute session for a 20 person intro epistemology section and I want them to feel the regress problem in thier bones rather than just hear me list foundationalism, coherentism and infinitism. Here's the shape I want: pairs, one person names a belief they actually hold, the other just asks why they think that, over and over, and the defender writes each reason on an index card and lays it on the table as they go. Every new card has to be one of three moves: a fresh reason, a card thats already on the table (which they have to physically pick up and move back into the chain, so the loop is visible), or saying this is where I stop and I can't go further -- they put down a token and it costs them a point. The why-er scores every time the defender either loops or stops; the defender scores for every three fresh reasons the why-er accepts as actually supporting the previous one. Swap roles halfway through.

Then the debrief. I want them to notice the moment they wanted to say it's just obvious and how much that felt like losing, the moment the circle stoped feeling like a justification and started feeling like cheating, and the fact that nobody ever actually ran out of cards -- that last one is the hook for infinitism. Don't name any of the three views until the debrief, and don't tell them up front that there are three.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of the idea this can't reproduce. My guess is infinitism comes out looking like we got bored rather than like a serious option, and I'd like to know how to keep it from landing as a punchline.
