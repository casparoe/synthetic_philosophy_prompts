I am a high school student who helps run our school’s philosophy club, and I need to design a 50-minute session for about 18 students, most of whom have never read any political philosophy. I want them to feel the force of the question equality of what before they hear the labels welfarism, resourcism, or the capabilities approach. Please design a runnable game with rules, materials, a payoff scheme that does not need real money, and a debrief. The game should put players in a fictional town council that must distribute a fixed budget of civic tokens among four residents I have invented: one who converts tokens into well-being at a normal rate; one who needs an expensive assistive device merely to do what the others do easily; one who has been raised to care intensely about opera and fine dining and reports feeling worse without them; and one who is content with very little and reports high well-being almost regardless. The council should have to allocate under three different rules in sequence—by equal tokens, by equal reported well-being, and by equal ability to do a list of concrete things—and the outcomes should visibly come apart. The rules and incentives should make the participants argue their way into the problem themselves, without a lecture up front and without hearing the names of the three positions until the debrief; the debrief should map each round onto the distinction between welfare, resources, and capabilities, explain what each measure ignores, and note which feature of the problem the game cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply in discussion. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that.
