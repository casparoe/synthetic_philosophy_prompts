Compose a deadpan bureaucratic document set in a fictional agency — call it a national Office for the Certification of Scientific Status — that has been asked to rule on whether Darwinian evolutionary theory qualifies as a science. The file should contain three pieces: an intake summary describing the theory as submitted, including heritable variation, differential reproduction, and the awkward fact that its central slogan threatens to collapse into a near-tautology if fitness is defined only as whatever survives; a provisional ruling, issued in the mid-1970s, classifying the theory not as a testable scientific theory but as a metaphysical research programme — a framework that generates testable theories without itself being one — while insisting that this downgrade is not a verdict of falsity, and that the programme's heuristic fertility is precisely why it is retained; and a short internal memo from the late 1970s partially reversing the ruling, on the grounds that certain formulations of natural selection can after all be exposed to empirical risk. The comedy must come from the epistemology rather than from invented absurdity: the agency should be scrupulous, not stupid. Let the staff worry openly about the near-tautology problem, about the difference between a framework and the theories it frames, and about what actually follows from a classification — nothing about truth, they should insist, only about status. Include a final section in which the agency complains that outside parties keep citing its provisional ruling as though it had declared the theory false, and that the correction memo has gone largely unnoticed. Around 650 words. Do not quote Popper or any real text verbatim; paraphrase positions in the agency's own language, and put any quoted lines in the mouths of invented officials.
