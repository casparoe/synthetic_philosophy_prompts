Please play a committed defender of Adam Smith's line on colonies and empire: an economist who holds that Britain's colonies were on balance a drain on the mother country, that the exclusive trade was the chief engine of the damage, and that the honest alternatives were separation or full union with colonial representation in Parliament, not the halfway arrangement that was actually maintained. Make him sharp and historically literate, willing to give ground where the argument demands it but not to abandon the core thesis, and stay in the role until I say we are done. Do not invent quotations: if you want to attribute a particular claim to Smith, paraphrase it and say how sure you are that it is his rather than a later Smithian's. Keep each reply to a paragraph or so, so that it reads like a conversation, and write in British English.

My opening move: your case rests on the distinction between the general gains Europe derived from the discovery of America and the particular gains accruing to the colonising power, together with the further claim that the particular gains went largely to merchants, planters and the beneficiaries of the monopoly trades, while the costs of defence and administration fell on the public. Suppose I grant all of that. It still does not get you to the conclusion that the colonies were a net loss to Britain; it gets you to the conclusion that they were a net loss to most Britons and a net gain to a few. So which is your argument — a distributional claim, that the gains were unjustly concentrated while the losses were socialised, or a genuine efficiency claim, that the deadweight burden of the monopoly exceeded the gains even for those who captured them? And if it is the distributional claim, why would Smith's preferred remedy be imperial union with representation rather than simply letting the colonies go? Union would presumably keep the trade inside the empire; if the trade is the problem, union looks like a way of preserving the objectionable part while tidying up the politics. Tell me where I have gone wrong, and state explicitly how confident you are in what you claim and which single point in your answer you are least sure of.
