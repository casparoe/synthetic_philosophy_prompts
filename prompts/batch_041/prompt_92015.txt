My friend has been ill for most of this year — an unremarkable run of bad luck that has since hardened into something chronic — and over the past few months I have lost count of the people who have said some version of 'what goes around comes around' in her hearing, most of them meaning it kindly, one of them not: that one was her brother-in-law, over a dinner table, and what he meant was that she had earned it. I have spent about a decade reading rationalist blogs and forums, so my reflex in these situations is to suspect that a sentence like that is doing several different jobs at once and that the argument is really about which job it is doing — which is why I want a menu rather than a verdict. Take 'what goes around comes around', together with its cousin 'you reap what you sow', as a slogan whose content is badly underdetermined, and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making: a testable claim about how outcomes are actually distributed in the world; a claim about how habits, reputations and expectations feed back on the people who have them; a claim about the fittingness of suffering to conduct rather than its cause; a claim about a ledger and whoever or whatever is keeping it; a claim, in the Buddhist register, that actions ripen in ways that leave nobody deserving anything in particular; and whatever else you can prise apart that I have not thought of.

For each reading, tell me what it commits you to, whether it is trivial, defensible or false, and — this is the part I actually want — whether it would license the use I saw it put to. I suspect a sleight of hand in which a causal claim about consequences gets quietly promoted into a claim about desert, and I want to know exactly where the promotion happens, whether any of the readings avoid it, and whether a wholly secular reconstruction can reach desert without a ledger; if it cannot, say so plainly rather than softening it. One of the regulars in these conversations has been reading about Buddhism and insists that karma is a law of causation and has nothing to do with desert at all — a position I cannot distinguish from having your cake and eating it — so please say whether that is a substantive doctrine, a refusal to answer the question, or the correct reading that the rest of us are simply getting wrong. Order the list from the most anodyne to the most metaphysically loaded, and note which reading each of my interlocutors was probably running on.
