I serve on the performance and accountability committee of a state health authority. We are being asked to adopt a new funding-linked metric for community mental-health clinics: the proportion of patients discharged from an inpatient psychiatric unit who attend a follow-up appointment within seven days. The clinical rationale is strong, but our pilot data are troubling. Some clinics appear to have improved the number by scheduling five-minute telephone check-ins that do not involve a prescriber, by discharging patients later so that the follow-up window is easier to meet, and by steering patients with complex needs to other providers. The committee must decide whether to adopt the metric as proposed, adopt it with modifications, or reject it. I need to circulate a written recommendation before the next meeting.

Draft the memo. It should recommend a concrete course of action, with a time frame and specific conditions if you recommend adoption. Use Goodhart's law and the concept of specification gaming as the analytical frame, not as slogans. Distinguish three ways a proxy can fail: regressional failure, where the proxy is only imperfectly correlated with the underlying goal; extremal failure, where optimizing hard on the proxy pushes the system into a region where the correlation breaks down; and causal failure, where pursuing the proxy changes the underlying goal itself. Say which of these the pilot data illustrate, and what each implies for the design of the metric. Distinguish gaming from genuine improvement, and say how the committee can tell the difference in practice. Address the strongest objection to your recommendation: that any quantitative target will eventually be gamed, so the committee should abandon funding-linked metrics and rely instead on professional judgment and qualitative review. Take that objection seriously, say what is right and wrong in it, and explain why your recommendation is better. The memo should be usable by the committee: a clear bottom line, reasons the clinics and the public could understand, and safeguards that could actually be implemented, such as paired outcome measures, risk adjustment, audit of a random sample, patient-reported measures, a shadow metric not tied to funding, and a sunset or review clause. Do not provide a bibliography or citations; I need the argument, not the literature. Aim for roughly 1,000 to 1,500 words.
