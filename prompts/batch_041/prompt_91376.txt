I am a novelist, and I am writing a historical novel set in a small invented polity — call it the Vale — that I want to be intellectually serious rather than merely picturesque. I would like you to play a committed historical materialist so that I can hear my antagonist's position argued at full strength and test my plot against it. The character is an erudite Marxist historian of the older school: he holds that the economic base — the mode of production and its relations — ultimately determines everything else in a society, including law, religion, politics, and art; that these superstructural elements nonetheless possess a real but strictly bounded autonomy of their own; and that they can react back upon the base without ever becoming its master. He has read the standard objections and is not rattled by them. He is not a strawman, will not merely chant formulas, and will concede particular points when they are genuinely owed. He is a composite of no living person. He speaks in the first person, in measured academic prose, and addresses me directly. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a single substantial paragraph, so the exchange feels like a seminar rather than a treatise.

Here is my opening move, and I want you to meet it as he would. In the Vale, the decisive event of my plot is doctrinal before it is economic. A reforming monastic order wins a bitter dispute over the sacraments and then persuades the great landholders that lending at interest is damnable; the new piety spreads through the guilds, and within two generations the Vale's agriculture and its credit are reorganized along lines that no one could have predicted from its prior economy. I have deliberately written the Vale so that the idea arrives first: as far as I can construct it, there is no prior shift in the mode of production that the doctrine merely reflects, and no emergent class whose interests the doctrine transparently serves. So tell me, in his voice: how does the base determine the superstructure here, when the superstructure appears to have moved first? And if his answer is that I must have smuggled in an economic cause I cannot see, I want him to say plainly what that cause is and how he knows it is there. Do not lay out the range of available positions and leave the choice to me — commit to one and defend it, and tell me exactly what it would cost his theory if I refuse to grant it.
