I am a high school student preparing an independent research project on collective decision-making, and I have a question about information cascades and rational herding that I cannot resolve on my own. I understand the basic setup: when people observe others choosing an option, they may rationally decide to follow the crowd and set aside their own private information, because the crowd’s choices seem to reveal something they do not know. My confusion is this: if following the crowd is rational in that way, does it follow that the crowd’s decision is itself rational, or at least that we have no grounds to call a herd, a fad, or a market bubble irrational? I keep reading that bubbles are irrational, but the cascade explanation seems to show that each individual in the bubble is doing the sensible thing given what they observe. I also read that cascades can be fragile, so that a small piece of new public information can make the whole crowd reverse course. If every step was rational, how can the outcome be something we rightly call irrational, and what does the later collapse tell us about whether the earlier crowd was rational but mistaken or irrational all along? Please explain the conceptual issue here and take a definite position: can an information cascade make an outcome irrational even when every individual choice within it is rational? I would like a committed answer with its reasons, not a neutral survey of possible views.
