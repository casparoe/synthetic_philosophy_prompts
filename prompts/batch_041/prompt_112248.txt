Ok so I'm putting together a handout for a first-year seminar I'm TAing on "is the world getting better?", and what I need is a decision tree, not an essay. The thing it sorts people on is "is violence declining?" — but I don't want it sorting them by which author they'd cite, because half of them have read nothing and the other half will just say "the Pinker thing" and "no but the Pinker thing is wrong" without being able to tell me what they mean by any of it. I want the branches to be things a student can answer about their own view, so that by the time they land on a leaf they've found out something about what they were already commited to.

Build it out of the distinctions, not out of the names. These are the ones I think have to be on the tree somewhere — the order is yours to work out, they just all have to be doing real work as forks:

- whether "violence" is a moralized term for you (nothing can be violent and justified) or a descriptive one (there's violence that's permissible — a police shooting, a just war, a parent physically restraining a kid)
- whether the harm has to be intended, or at least foreseeably avoidable by someone who had a duty to avoid it, or whether slow impersonal harm counts — a famine engineered by price policy, a city laid out so that crossing the street kills people, deaths of despair
- whether what you're comparing is an event or a condition — a massacre is an event, a homicide rate of 5 per 100k is a standing state of affairs, and I want the tree to force people to say which of those the word is picking out
- whether you're counting rates or totals, and if rates, per what: per person, per person-year, per interaction, per unit of available lethal capacity
- whether you count the capacity to do harm or only the harm actually done (a world with fewer war deaths and more warheads is less violent in one sense and much more in another)
- whether the unit is the world or the region, and whether a global average is a real quantity or a fiction that hides the distribution
- whether the meaning of the word has drifted enough across centuries that the comparison is between two different quantities rather than the same one measured twice
- and then the meta fork: once you've fixed all of the above, is there a fact of the matter about whether violence declined, or several legitimate answers, or none?

Every leaf gets a short name — make one up if nobody's ever defended the spot, and say that you did — plus one sentence on the commitment that's hardest to defend from there.

Two things I specifically want. First, wherever the tree throws up a combination of answers that nobody seems to occupy, say so and diagnose it: is that spot empty because the view is incoherent, or just unattractive, or merely unexplored? I have a hunch that "broad definition + per-capita rate + a genuine decline" comes out nearly empty and I want to know if that's right. Second, flag any two leaves my questions can't actually seperate, instead of pretending the tree distinguishes them.

Phrase every branch as a question with two or three answers rather than as a thesis, so a student can just walk it. Under 800 words, and no intro or conclusion, just the tree and the notes underneath it.
