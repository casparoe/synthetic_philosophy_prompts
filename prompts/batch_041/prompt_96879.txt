next wednesday is my turn to run the reading group and I've landed the superrationality session — the Platonia dilemma, the luring lottery he actually ran on his readers, and the follow-up where he decides irrationality is the square root of all evil. I'd rather open with a poem than a summary, since the setup is fiddly and I want them to feel it before they start arguing about it.

The Platonia one, as I remember it: some eccentric trillionaire gets twenty people together and tells them that if exactly one of them sends him a telegram by noon tomorrow, that person gets a billion dollars; if two or more send one, or none do, nobody gets anything, and they're not allowed to talk to each other. The superrational answer is supposed to be: randomize — send with probability 1/20, since thats the p that maximizes p(1-p)^19 times a billion. Then the real one, the lottery he ran in the magazine: you enter by mailing a postcard with a 1 on it, but you're allowed to mail a single postcard with any number on it and get that many entries; the pot is a million dollars divided by the total number of entries received, and it goes to whoever holds a randomly picked entry. If everyone else has mailed in T entries between them, your best move is to mail exactly T yourself, because n/(T+n)^2 is maximized at n = T, so it runs away — and it did: people sent in numbers they had to write as expressions, at least one sent a googolplex, the prize came out to a fraction of a cent, and the magazine couldn't even figure out who had won.

What I want from the poem: six four-line stanzas, and the last line of every stanza is a refrain naming the payoff, the pot. But the refrain has to shrink by real division as the poem goes — the speaker's entry count keeps multiplying, so the number in that last line keeps getting divided down (a million, then thousands, then a dollar, then a cent, then something you'd need a microscope for). By the final stanza I want that refrain as close to nothing as you can get while still being a line of verse. Voice: one reader who starts out meaning to be a good superrationalist and roll the die, and then talks themselves, stanza by stanza, into doubling, and doubling again, until they're writing a googolplex in very careful handwriting. I want to watch the lure work on them, so that we can feel why the clean superrational answer gets eaten alive.

The Platonia bit should be in there, but I don't want two poems — maybe the first stanza is the telegram and the exactly-one-ness, and the fact that twenty people who reason identically either all send or none do, and either way nobody wins, and then the rest is postcards. And no moral at the end, no tidy closing line about irrationality being the root of all evil. Just leave the prize too small to pay and the winner unidentifiable, and stop. Rhyme or not is up to you, but the arithmetic has to be right — I'm definately reading this out loud to people who will check the division.
