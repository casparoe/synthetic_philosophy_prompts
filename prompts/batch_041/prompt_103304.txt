Write me a parody consumer-product review, in the deadpan style of a Consumer Reports or Wirecutter roundup, where the products being compared are the rival solutions to the problem of universals. Treat each theory as a thing you'd actually buy. At minimum cover: full-blooded Platonism (the universals live in their own separate realm, no shipping address, no delivery window), Aristotelian immanent realism (the universal is just right there in the particular, nothing extra to assemble), Armstrong-style immanent universals with a strict no-uninstantiated-properties return policy, trope theory (you buy the individual property-instances one at a time, bundle optional), resemblance nominalism (nothing but particulars that happen to look alike, sold as-is), and class nominalism (a property is just the set of its instances, cheapest option on the shelf). Run every model through the same tests a real shopper would: the one-over-many problem, the third-man regress, the companionship problem (why do being-white and being-swan always turn up in the same bird?), coextensive-but-distinct properties like being-renate and being-cordate, and what happens when nothing in the world actually has the property — do you still get billed for it? Rate each on price/parsimony, explanatory coverage, and after-sales support, and give a 'Verdict' plus a 'Who this is for' line at the end. The jokes have to come from getting the metaphysics right, so a metaphysician laughes instead of wincing, and a reader who's never heard of the problem should still follow the gist. Around 700 words. Dont name any real living philosophers — use the positions themselves, or invent brand names for them, as the products.
