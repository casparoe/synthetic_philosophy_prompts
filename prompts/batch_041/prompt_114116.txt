I'm part of a small philosophy-of-physics reading group that has bogged down on the block universe, and I want to clear the ground before we go any further. The sticking point is the argument — usually called the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and dramatized by the Andromeda paradox, in which two pedestrians passing on the street, one walking toward Andromeda and one away, disagree about whether the Andromedans have already launched a fleet — that because simultaneity is relative to a frame, different observers carry different planes of simultaneity, and so, if we take each observer's present seriously, every event in spacetime turns out to be equally real. Half the group treats this as a proof that special relativity forces four-dimensionalism; the other half suspects a trick, and generally mutters that the present is only a local notion that cannot be inflated into a single global now, but nobody can state precisely what a theory of time would have to do in order to settle the question, as opposed to trading intuitions about it. So, before we read anything else, please write me the desiderata: what any adequate account of the metaphysics of time — of what the now is, and whether there is becoming — must deliver. I want the cases it has to get right (the pedestrian disagreement, the felt flow of experience, the asymmetry between past and future, the fact that presentism and the static block view each carry costs their defenders rarely advertise), the distinctions it must not collapse (between what exists and what our tensed talk commits us to; between the relativity of simultaneity as a physical result and as a mere convention of synchronisation; between an objective present and a now that is only an indexical, on a par with here), and the constraints it cannot violate (special relativity as ordinarily understood, together with the fact that a Lorentzian reading of relativity — one positing a genuine but empirically hidden privileged foliation, a real now that Lorentz invariance conspires to conceal — is not obviously incoherent). For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp.

The part I most want, and the reason I am asking at all, is the tension analysis. Flag any pair or triple of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say exactly where the conflict bites: I expect the requirement of objective becoming and the requirement of full consistency with relativity to be the obvious candidates, but I want to know whether the hidden-foliation manoeuvre genuinely rescues the pair or merely relabels the loss and pays for it elsewhere, and whether the same device that saves becoming also delivers a robust past–future asymmetry or leaves it stranded. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to eliminate the popular positions — presentism, the growing block, the moving spotlight, the static B-theory — and explain why that one, rather than any of the others, is doing the real work.
