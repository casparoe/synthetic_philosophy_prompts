I've been trying to get straight on the idea of moral trade — where two people with different moral views both supposedly come out ahead by each doing something the other values more: a vegan who thinks animal suffering is the main thing agrees to donate to an anti-malaria charity, and a global-poverty person agrees to go vegan for a year, so each ends up with more of what they care about in the world. My question is where the gain actually comes from and what the "exchange rate" is even supposed to be. If each of them already ranks whole world-states, including the other's actions, in what sense is the post-trade world better for either of them, and what's the precise condition for a trade being a genuine improvement rather than the two of them just doing their second-best thing? Does the exchange rate need some common currency between their values, or is barter across incommensurable values enough? Is there a difference in kind between trading with someone who disagrees with you about what matters and trading with someone who agrees with you completely — does the second case collapse into nothing but comparative advantage in producing the same good? And does the whole idea quietly presuppose that my obligations are about outcomes rather than about what I do, since if my duty is to do the thing myself no trade can discharge it, which would make moral trade unavailable to anyone with agent-relative duties? Please keep straight what's actually settled here versus what's contested among the people who work on this, and dont present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
