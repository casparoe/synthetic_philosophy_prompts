For the last few months a friend and I have been circling the same argument about the inside view and the outside view, and neither of us can tell whether we actually disagree or are just talking past each other, so I'd like you to write it out as a dialogue. I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, so I've absorbed the standard spiel about base rates, the planning fallacy, and calibration — and I basically believe it — but every time I try to apply it to my own forecasts the thing slides through my fingers, and I'd rather watch two competent people fight about it than read another summary of it. Put them at a café after a local meetup, once the crowd has thinned out. Neither should be a strawman, and they should engage each other's actual points rather than restating their own.

One of them, call her the base-rate person, holds that whenever we forecast anything about ourselves we should step outside our own heads and anchor on the reference class of similar past attempts, because the inside view is just the planning fallacy in a nicer suit; on her account, the discipline of consulting base rates is what epistemic modesty actually amounts to once you cash it out. The other, call him the skeptic, agrees that people overrate their own plans but thinks the outside view as usually stated is nearly vacuous: every event belongs to indefinitely many reference classes, and the choice among them is itself an inside-view judgment, since you pick the class that already encodes what you take to be relevant, so the outside view can never genuinely correct the inside view — it can only launder it. Let them fight over a concrete case: a mutual friend who is about to quit a stable job to try to make a living as an illustrator, and who has asked them both whether she's being reckless. What is the right reference class here — all freelancers, all illustrators, all people who quit stable jobs, all people who quit stable jobs in a downturn, all people with her portfolio and her savings? And is a base rate for that class a fact about the world or an artifact of where you draw the line?

I'd also like them to press on whether the outside view is really a claim about base rates or a claim about causal structure — that is, about which features of a situation are the kind of thing that tends to matter — and whether that distinction rescues the outside view or merely relocates the difficulty. And have them argue over whether epistemic modesty is a genuine intellectual virtue or just a low-variance strategy that starts looking like a virtue once you're scoring in Brier points; if the skeptic is right, does her own position escape the regress, given that "be modest about how you chose your reference class" sounds like an outside-view instruction? Don't force a tidy resolution, but let each of them land a real hit, and let the exchange run long enough for the argument to actually develop.
