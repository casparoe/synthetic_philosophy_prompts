Trying to find a name for something, if it's got one. Here's the thing I keep noticing and it's been bugging me for a while.

Take any word with a fuzzy edge — 'heap', 'bald', 'rich', 'city'. Clear yes cases, clear no cases, and a long foggy stretch in the middle. Fine so far. But people talk about that foggy stretch in two ways that can't both be right, and the part that gets me is they switch between them depending on which way they want the arguement to go. When someone's trying to pin a borderline case on you, they'll say the answer is in there somwhere, there's a real line, we're just not looking hard enough. When someone's trying to wriggle out of a borderline case being pinned on them, it's 'there's no fact of the matter, it's just vague, stop trying to make it precise.' Same person, same word, different mood.

Concrete version: a friend of mine insists a town we both know is 'basically a city now' and that I'm just wrong to call it a town. I push back, and within two minutes it's 'well, city is vague, there's no fact of the matter, it's just a label.' He'd spent the first two minutes telling me there WAS a fact of the matter and I had it wrong.

Is there a name for that flip? I know there are whole theories about what vagueness actually is — I half remember one that says the line is real but it's impossible in principle to know where it falls, and another that says there's no line at all but some statements are still determinately true and others determinately false. But those are answers to what vagueness IS. What I'm describing feels like something people DO with it, in the middle of an argument. So: does the flip have its own name, or is it just a case of something more general — motivated reasoning, or the pragmatics of disagreement? And if it is its own thing, where are the edges? Is it still the same phenomenon when the person never claims a hidden line and always says 'no fact of the matter', but then treats you as objectively wrong anyway? Is there a principled version of this, or is it always just convenient? Happy to take a coinage plus a careful definition — I just don't want the bare label, I want to know what the thing is and what it isn't.
