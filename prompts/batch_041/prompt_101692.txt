I've come round to thinking that evidential cooperation in large worlds is a real thing and not just a cute bit of decision theory: my choices are evidence about how other agents decide, including agents I'll never causally touch, so if the relevant others are running something like the same deliberation I am, I have reason to do the cooperative thing even though nothing I do ever reaches them. What I want is the case against, from someone who takes it seriously rather than swatting it away. Which of the obvious objections actually bite -- that my choice is only evidence via a shared cause, so the inference dies the moment the shared cause is correlated with things that make cooperation bad; that if it were this cheap the universe would already be full of agents who exploited it; that the whole thing rests on a story about how one computation can be evidence about another that nobody has made precise; or the nastier possibility that the logic doesn't care whether the counterparties are friendly? Rank them by how decisive they'd be, say which of them a defender would concede rather than dismiss, and tell me whether the view has quietly been qualified into something nothing could count against. And what does it predict that a plain causal-minded agent wouldn't? I dont need a reading list, I need the map of where it's exposed.
