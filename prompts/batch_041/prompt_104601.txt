"Aumann proved that two perfectly rational agents who start out sharing the same information can never end up disagreeing about anything, even in principle. If you and I look at the same evidence and reach different conclusions about, say, whether a coin will come up heads, then by Bayes' theorem at least one of us must have reasoned badly. The theorem's punchline is that persistent disagreement -- between economists, between doctors, between you and your uncle at Thanksgiving -- is proof that somebody is being irrational, and the only cure is to keep announcing your credences until the math forces you together. That's basically why prediction markets are so efficient and why real experts always converge."
