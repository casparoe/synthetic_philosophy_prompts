Last week a seminar on aesthetics spent its whole session on whether an image produced by a generative model trained on scraped paintings, then prompted and curated by a person, counts as a work of art, and at the end a visiting student closed the discussion with one sentence: art is whatever the artworld says it is. Everyone nodded and the question was treated as settled. I don't believe it is settled, and I can't yet say why. What I notice is that I can hear several quite different claims inside that one sentence — some about how the word 'art' is used, some about what makes a thing art, at least one about who has the standing to say — and they cannot all be doing the same work.

I want a menu, not a verdict on the AI image. For the sentence as it was actually used there — to end a dispute about whether something is art — set out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing, ranging from the nearly trivial to the genuinely radical. For each, say exactly what it is a claim about (the word, the concept, the property of being art, the sociological practice, or the authority of that practice); what else accepting it would commit you to; whether it is a trivial platitude, a substantive claim that is defensible, merely contentious, or straightforwardly false; and whether, on that reading, it genuinely supports the move it was used to make — namely, closing the question of whether the image is art.

Return this as a table with the following columns, one row per reading:

- Reading: a short name for the thesis.
- Precisification: one or two sentences stating exactly what the reading asserts and what it is a claim about.
- Commitments: what accepting the reading would also commit you to — in particular what it implies about who may settle a dispute over whether something is art, and about whether the artworld itself can be mistaken.
- Status: one of trivial / substantive and defensible / contentious / false, with a brief reason.
- Supports the dismissal?: yes, no, or partly — and the precise move it licenses in the seminar exchange.

After the table, add two or three sentences naming the reading the visiting student most plausibly intended, and identifying which reading would actually be needed for the dismissal to work.
