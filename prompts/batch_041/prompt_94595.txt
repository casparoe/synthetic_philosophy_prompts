For a paper in my seminar on Augustine's ethics of truth-telling, I'm trying to work out the practical payoff of the taxonomy of lies he sets out in De Mendacio. Since he ranks the kinds of lies from the gravest down to the least serious — the officious lie, which I take to be the harmless white lie told to help someone — I assume the ranking is meant to tell us which lies are permitted, with the officious lie as the category that is normally acceptable. What I want to know is how to apply that criterion to a case he never discusses: if a hospital administrator will only admit a patient to an experimental treatment when the intake paperwork is filled in in a way that is not strictly accurate, does the officious-lie exemption cover it, and if not, at what point on Augustine's scale does a lie become obligatory rather than merely excused?
