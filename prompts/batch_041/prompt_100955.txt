After a career in the natural sciences—I was a palaeontologist—I have spent my retirement reading philosophy, and I have become preoccupied with the thought that archives are not neutral deposits of fact but instruments that decide what can later be known. My own field taught me that a record can be systematically silent, and I should like to see that thought pursued in the human archive. I should be grateful if you would write a dialogue between two equally competent archivists, with invented names: one who defends the traditional ideal of custodial neutrality, and one who argues that archives are inevitably sites of power. Let them debate a specific case: a municipal archive for a mining village dominated by a single company, whose holdings consist almost entirely of the company’s ledgers, correspondence and photographs, together with the appraisal decisions that preserved those records and discarded others, and a later oral-history project with former residents. Please introduce and use a concept I have not encountered in the literature but which seems to me important: the archival shadow. By that I mean the penumbra of unrecorded lives and events created not merely by accidental loss but by the very categories, provenance rules and acquisition decisions that determine what is worth recording in the first place. Let them also argue over whether the principle of respect des fonds—keeping records in the order and grouping in which they were created—helps to expose the archival shadow or merely naturalises it. The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth: each speaker should answer the other’s strongest point, and neither should be a strawman. The custodial archivist may concede that selection is unavoidable but argue that professional standards can minimise distortion; the critical archivist may concede the value of preservation but argue that neutrality is a mask for power. Let them disagree about whether the oral-history project repairs the silence or simply creates a new, differently shaped record. After the dialogue, please provide a structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields: case, a one-sentence description of the archival case; speakers, an array of objects each containing name, position, strongest_argument, key_concession and unresolved_crux; points_of_agreement, an array of strings; remaining_disagreement, a string; and archival_shadow_assessment, an object with definition_used, explanatory_power and limitations. There is no need for references, reading lists or citations; I am interested in the argument itself. Please write in British English.
