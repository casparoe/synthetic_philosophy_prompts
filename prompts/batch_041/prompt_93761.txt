I run a small reading group — six of us, a mix of philosophy postgrads and one lapsed physicist who keeps us honest — and we've spent most of this term on al-Ghazālī's case against the philosophers' account of causation. Predictibly, two of us have now staked out camps. The first is a committed occasionalist: fire doesn't really burn, he says, there's no necessity at all in the link between what we call a cause and what we call its effect, and whatever happens is produced directly by God, with nothing created having any genuine causal power of its own. The second is a Humean who agrees with the first that we never observe any necessary connection between cause and effect — it's just constant conjunction plus the mind's habit of expecting the one after the other — but thinks that's the whole story, with no agent needed to do any producing. The annoying thing is that half the group reckons the two of them agree far more than either will admit, while the other half reckons they're talking past each other entirely; either way, our evenings keep collapsing into shouting about what 'necessity' is even supposed to mean.

What I'd love is the document the two of them should have written instead of arguing: the joint statement they could both actually sign. Part (a) should be a numbered list of the claims they agree on — about what we observe in the world, about what (if anything) we can know a priori, about whether anything in nature has causal power, about what grounds our confidence that the future will resemble the past, and about whether miracles are possible. Part (b) should be a table of the points where they genuinely still part company, with these columns: the point at issue; the occasionalist's position, worded so he'd accept it; the Humean's position, worded so she'd accept it; whether the crux is a real substantive dissagreement or merely a verbal one over what 'cause' or 'necessity' means; the single case, argument or thought-experiment that both agree would bear on it; and each side's prediction about how it comes out.

Then finish with a short JSON summary — fields 'agreed_claims' (list of strings), 'genuine_disagreements' (list of strings), 'verbal_disputes' (list of strings), 'cruxes' (list of strings) and 'what_would_move_the_debate' (string) — so I can paste it into our notes. Please don't just adjudicate who's right: the point is a text both of them could put their names to, and if you think one of the rows is really a dispute about words rather than about the world, say so and say why. Also, no need to give me reading lists or citations — I can't chase them down anyway.
