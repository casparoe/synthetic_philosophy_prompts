ok so I run a small interpretability lab and there's a fight that's been going on for like a year between two groups and I want to force them to write it down. Camp one, the realists: they think a trained network really does have internal states that stand for things -- features, variables, whatever -- and that the whole point of interpretability is to recover the ones the model is actually using, so that when we find a direction or an SAE feature we're discovering something that was there, and there's a fact of the matter about whether we got it right. Camp two, the deflationists: they think all the feature talk is bookkeeping we project onto the weights, that the only non-negotiable facts are about behavior and causal structure, that "this neuron means X" is a tool for prediction and control and not the kind of thing that's true or false, and that if two decompositions predict held-out activations equally well there's no further question which one is correct.

I want the joint statement they could both actually put their names to. Ground it in stuff we can all see in the lab, not vibes. Things we have on hand: a tiny transformer trained to add numbers mod a prime (113 is what the usual demo uses) where the embeddings come out arranged on a circle, i.e. Fourier features; induction heads in small models doing in-context copying; linear probes that decode a property from layer L at like 95% accuracy but where ablating that direction doesn't move the output at all; activation patching where swapping one head's activations across prompts flips the answer; SAE features on the residual stream that look like "starts with a vowel" or "this token is inside a string literal," some of which do steer the model when you clamp them; and two SAEs with different sparsity penalties that give us totally different dictionaries that both reconstruct held-out activations about equally well. Also steering vectors, adding a direction at inference changes the sentiment of the continuation, but it also messes up three other things, which is part of why this got heated.

What I want out of it: (1) numbered agreed claims -- the stuff that holds under both readings, nothing vacuous, nothing that only one side would nod at; (2) the disagreements, each one stated twice, once in words the realist would sign and once in words the deflationist would sign, no strawmen; (3) the cruxes, the sub-questions where settling one would actually move somebody; (4) for each crux, a case, argument, or experiment both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Suspect one crux is whether causal efficacy is required for representation or recoverability is enough, and another is whether the mod-113 circle represents numbers or is a lookup table in fancy dress, but you tell me. And if a chunk of this is just a word fight -- "representation" being asked to mean two different things at once -- say which chunk and why, don't paper over it. Table for the disagreement section is fine. Skip the references, we all know the papers, I want the actual content. Don't write a compromise that secretly hands it to one side either; they both have to be able to sign it.
