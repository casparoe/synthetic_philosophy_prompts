In my philosophy of social science seminar we keep circling a dispute that has run for decades without either side giving ground. The memetics camp—Dawkins's original proposal, Dennett, Blackmore—treats culture as a population of replicators subject to blind variation and differential retention. The cognitive-anthropological camp around Sperber, working under the heading of the epidemiology of representations or cultural attraction theory, and sometimes described (following Sterelny) as a Paris school set against a California school of Boyd and Richerson, insists that cultural transmission is mostly reconstruction rather than copying, so that what spreads is pulled toward cognitive attractors rather than filtered by fidelity of replication; Sperber is willing to treat representations as replicators only in special cases, chain letters being his stock example. Meanwhile Boyd, Richerson, Henrich and others build models and field cases in which cultural variants are replicated with bias and then selected. Each side knows the other's arguments; neither concedes. Underneath sits the general question of what a process must have to count as Darwinian—variation, differential retention, and heredity—and whether heredity requires anything as strong as replication.

My hypothesis is that this is two questions under one name, one about how variants spread in a population and one about why representations are transformed in the passing, and that the equivocation sits in the notion of a replicator. Test that hypothesis against what each side actually says. Is the disagreement verbal, substantive, or a hidden empirical question about how faithful human imitation really is? And what would count as progress here—a sharper distinction between copying and reconstruction, a measurable fidelity threshold above which selection takes over, a formal result showing one framework subsumes the other, or a demonstration that the two camps are answering different questions and should stop presenting themselves as rivals? Tell me what would change your mind and where your diagnosis is likeliest to fail. Please write in American English.
