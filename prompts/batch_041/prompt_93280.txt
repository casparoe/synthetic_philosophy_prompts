Karl Popper argued that the historicist dream of laws of historical development — laws from which the future of society could be forecast — is incoherent, and he offered several distinct arguments to that effect. Consider a research programme that has arisen since his death and that looks designed to escape them. It applies machine learning to a large corpus of historical, economic, and demographic data and reports stable quantitative regularities: a composite index of elite competition and fiscal stress, for example, is said to predict the onset of civil war within a decade at a stated error rate, and the regularities are said to hold across two centuries and dozens of polities. Its authors are careful in three ways: they issue only conditional, probabilistic forecasts with confidence intervals; they claim to predict only political violence, never the future growth of scientific or technological knowledge; and they keep their forecasts private, so that none can become self-fulfilling or self-defeating.

Reconstruct Popper's verdict. Which of his arguments does this programme actually engage, and which does it fail to touch at all? Does it supply "sociological laws" in the sense he allowed, or only the extrapolated trends he insisted on distinguishing from laws — and does that distinction survive the addition of error bars? Would he read the privacy of the forecasts as an evasion of the Oedipus effect or as a tacit admission that the effect is real? Say which of his theses the programme would refute if its claims held up, what he would have predicted about the programme's long-run track record, and what he would have advised its authors to do instead. Answer in at most 350 words, in no more than four short numbered sections.
