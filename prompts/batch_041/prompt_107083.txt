I've been chewing on Kripke's reading of Wittgenstein on rule-following — the plus/quus thing, where supposedly there's no fact about my past behavior, my dispositions, or anything going on in my head that determines that I meant plus rather than some gerrymandered function that agrees with plus on every sum I've ever actually performed but goes haywire on a sum I've never done. I get the setup and I get that Kripke's skeptical solution is to stop asking what fact makes my meaning determinate and instead ask under what conditions my utterances are assertable, with the answer coming out in terms of communal agreement and the rejection of both the dispositionalist picture and the picture of grasping some abstract rule-entity. But I keep feeling like this is basically the difference between a spec and an implementation, or between source and runtime behavior, and that Kripke is making very heavy weather of something I deal with every working day.

That's the thing I want you to actually do. I'm a working programmer — compilers and interpreters, a bit of ML on the side — so I want the rule-following considerations and the skeptical solution translated, as faithfully as you can manage, into the vocabulary I think in: specifications vs implementations, interpreters vs the programs they run, evaluation semantics and reduction rules, overfitting vs generalization, test suites and what a passing test does and doesn't pin down, and what it even means for two deployments to be running "the same function." Please do a systematic translation, claim by claim, and don't just hand me a cute analogy and call it done.

Then the part I actually care about, which is the residue report. What survives the translation cleanly, what only goes through if I distort it, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. My suspicion is that the normativity of meaning — the idea that a rule tells me what I ought to do, not merely what I'm disposed or trained to do — is exactly the thing my framework can't express, and I want you to tell me straight whether that's because my framework is missing something real or because the normativity talk is doing less work than the philosophers think it is. Related: are the gerrymandered quus-style rules a genuine phenomenon in my world or an artifact of stating the whole problem in set-theoretic terms where you can slice up a function any way you like? If it's an artifact, say what that implies about whether the paradox is deep or is downstream of one particular way of individuating functions. I'm fluent on the programming side and shaky on the philosphy, so flag anywhere you're quietly leaning on a distinction I probably don't have.
