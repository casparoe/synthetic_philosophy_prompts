Ok, I need help settling a long-running arguement with my brother, who's a protein chemist, about whether DNA is really a code or whether the word code is just a metaphor that biologists got stuck with in the 60s and never gave back. I don't want a "well, it depends what you mean by code" dodge. So let me nail down what I mean, and then I want an actual number.

Call something a genuine code, and not just a metaphor, if it satisfies all four of these:

1. Arbitrariness -- the mapping from sign to what it stands for could have been otherwise and the system would still work. There's no physical necessity forcing this sign to mean that thing.
2. Vehicle/content separation -- the sign carries its content only in virtue of how some downstream system treats it, not in virtue of the sign's own physical properties.
3. A distinct decoder -- there's some component whose job is turning signs into effects, and it isn't the same thing as the message.
4. Compositionality -- the system builds complex messages out of reusable parts, and the meaning of the whole is a function of the parts and their arrangement.

What's your credence that DNA satisfies all four, i.e. that the genetic code is a literal code in that sense? I'm at about 0.7, my brother is at like 0.25, and I genuinely can't tell if we're disagreeing about biology or just about how to use a word.

Break it into the four pieces and give me a rough probability for each, with a line of justification:

Does codon reassignment in odd mitochondria or in ciliates show the mapping is arbitrary, or only that it's re-tunable inside tight constraints?
- Vehicle/content: is there any sense in which a stretch of DNA means a protein the way a word means a thing, given that nothing about the DNA does any work until the tRNA, the synthetases and the ribosome get ahold of it? And does it cut for or against the code reading that the decoding machinery is itself encoded in the same DNA? I keep going back and forth on whether that circularity is a problem or the whole point.
- Decoder: is the ribosome a decoder, or just a catalyst that happens to be shape-selective? Does it matter that the codon-anticodon match is a physical pairing rather than a lookup in a table?
- Compositionality: genes get spliced, edited, read in overlapping frames, sometimes nested inside each other. Does that help the code reading or hurt it?
