So I'm not a philosopher, I just read alot of econ and some history of science, and theres a move I keep running into that I don't have a word for and it's been bugging me for months.

Here's the shape of it. Somebody builds a model. The assumptions are obviously false — everyone's perfectly rational, information is free, there's no friction, the population is infinite, take your pick. You push on the assumption and they say: sure, it's not literally true, it's a simplification, what matters is whether it predicts or whether it illuminates something. Fine, no argument from me. But then the same person, or their textbook, or the guy on the podcast who read the abstract, states the model's conclusion as a flat fact about the world. No hedge, no "under these assumptions." "The model shows rent control creates shortages." "The model shows X causes Y." Often it's the same paper doing both: the abstract states the result, the assumptions section says none of it is meant literally, and nothing in between connects the two. Same with a result that needs infinitely many traders getting quoted about a market with five, or a representative-agent theorem getting applied to actual people who are nothing like the agent.

What I want a name for isn't "models simplify," and it isn't the position that assumptions don't need to be realistic as long as the predictions hold up. Those are at least consistent stances. The thing I keep seeing is the switching: the false assumption is a fiction the moment somebody attacks it and a description the moment it's being used to make a claim, and the switch is never announced. Same object, two readings, picked by whatever's convenient right then.

Does this have an established name? I don't just want a label, I want the carving. Where does it sit next to instrumentalism, or as-if theorizing, or idealization, or the charge of treating an abstraction as if it were the concrete thing? Is the physics move — derive the law in the frictionless case, then tell you the law holds for the real case — the same thing or a different thing? I have a feeling they're different and I can't say why. If there's no name for it, coin one and tell me what it includes and what it rules out.

And please be straight with me about which parts of this are actually settled among people who study it and which are genuinely contested — I don't want one camp's answer dressed up as the consensus.
