I retired a couple of years ago after fourty years in a wet lab — I was a chemist, ran a spectroscopy group — and since then I've been working my way through philosphy, mostly metaethics lately, which is a world away from what I did for a living.

Here's my problem. When I read the moral realism versus anti-realism fight, I keep feeling like I already had this argument, for decades, just with different words. The argument about whether the electron is real or just a useful bookkeeping device. Whether the ideal gas law is true or just a model that works in a regime. Whether to be an instrumentalist about the wavefunction and just compute and stop asking what it means. I sat through that fight in seminars and at coffee breaks for forty years and I know it from the inside, not from the literature.

So I want you to take the metaethics debate — realism, anti-realism, and whatever the in-between positions are — and restate it in the vocabulary I actually used in the lab. Not a cute analogy; a real translation, position by position, using the distinctions I'd actually draw. Here are the ones I'd draw, so you can use them:

1. Things I'd bet the lab's budget on. I can isolate it, manipulate it, and something else in the apparatus visibly breaks when I do. Call those handleable.
2. Things I only ever know through a fit. The only evidence for them is that a model with them in it predicts the data better than a model without them, over a range I've actually measured. I never touch them directly, and if the fit is bad I don't know whether to blame the entity or the functional form.
3. Things I could swap out for a completely different formalism and lose nothing measurable. They earn their keep by convenience — they make the algebra short — not by contact with the instrument.

For the record, that's where I landed: a realist about (1), a shrug about (2), and I thought (3) was the only place where the argument was worth having, because two people who disagree about (3) can agree on every measurement anyone will ever make.

So: restate moral realism in that vocabulary. Then restate anti-realism. Then restate the middle positions — I gather there are several, the ones where moral claims are true but only relative to something, or true but not mind-independent, or where they express attitudes rather than describe anything. I don't know the names, so translate whatever the main ones are.

Then the part I actually want: the residue report. What carried over cleanly? What had to be bent to fit, and what does the bend hide? And what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all?

I'm suspicious of one specific thing and want you to address it head on. In my world, is it real is a question about what happens when you interfere with it. The realist/instrumentalist fight was always resolvable in principle, eventually, by some experiment — even if it took two hundred years and a better laser. In the moral case I don't see what the experiment is, and I don't see how the disagreement could ever be settled by anything like interference. So tell me: does my vocabulary lack a resource for talking about the kind of authority or necessity the realists want, or is the metaethics debate really thinner than it looks — mostly a fight about what to call a projection? Don't be diplomatic. If the honest answer is that my vocabulary can't even state the question, say so and tell me what the missing piece is.

Format, please, and don't skip any of these:

- Ground rules (~150 words): what vocabulary you're translating into, and the two or three places you expect the translation to strain before you start.
- The translation (~800 words): each position restated in lab terms, one at a time, with a flag wherever you're having to stretch a term.
- Clean carries (~200 words): what went across without loss.
- Bent (~250 words): what only went across with distortion, and what the distortion hides.
- Didn't survive (~250 words): the residue, as a numbered list, most important first.
- Verdict on the residue (~200 words): for each item in the residue, is it my vocabulary that's deficient, or is the original claim less clear than it looked?

One more thing: don't cite papers at me. I can't get them and I don't care who said it first. If you need a name for a position, just describe it.
