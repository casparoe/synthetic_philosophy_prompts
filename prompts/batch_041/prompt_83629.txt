Alright, I've been binging old rationalist fourms latley and I keep circling back to evidential cooperation in large worlds. My rough gloss of it: my action isn't just a cause of downstream stuff, it's also evidence about what other agents do, and any agent whose decision procedure is correlated with mine — same source code, same reasoning, same training, whatever — is someone whose choices my choices are evidence about. In a big enough world there are presumably a lot of those, including ones I could never causally touch (civilizations too far away to ever reach, other branches of the wavefunction, maybe copies of me). So the move is to act like I'm making one joint decision with all of them, which makes one-shot cooperation with strangers look less like a sucker's bet and more like a deal worth keeping.

Suppose that's roughly right. What are the most important implications of it for the small stuff — the choices I make that nobody will ever check on? Like whether to leave a note on a car I dinged in a parking lot, whether to go back and pay for something the cashier forgot to ring up, whether to keep a wallet I find on the sidewalk, or whether to spend an extra hour on something that only helps people I'll never meet. I don't want the generic "be good even when nobody's watching" sermon. I want the implications that actually bite, including the ones that make the view look unlivable, and the ones that hinge on which agents even count as correlated in the first place. Answer in American English.
