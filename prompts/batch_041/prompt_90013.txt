Hi — I sit on the board of our municipal utility (mid-sized, one reactor, ~1.1 GW, currently scheduled to shut in 2029) and we vote in three weeks on whether to approve a life extension to 2041. Refit is quoted at 2.4bn, and our own consultants say the same money would buy roughly the same firm low-carbon output as wind + solar + 8-hour storage, just with more variance and a slower start. I have to circulate a written opinion before the vote and I want it to be the document people actually argue with rather than a summary of the consultant deck.

Here's the thing I can't get straight, and I want the memo to actually engage it. Two of my colleagues frame this as a straight expected-value call: annual accident risk is tiny, the climate damage from the gas we'd burn in a slow buildout is large and near-certain, so extend. Fine. But the failure mode of the reactor isn't the same kind of thing as the failure mode of the gas. It's low-probability, irreversible, and potentially jurisdiction-ending, and the waste stays hazardous past any point where we can honestly claim an institution will still exist to guard it. Someone at the last meeting called this the institutional horizon problem and everyone moved on. I dont think you can move on. Our charter says any decision has to be justifiable on grounds the people affected could in principle accept, and the people who'll be living with the spent fuel can't be asked. Is that a real objection or am I importing a philosophy seminar into an engineering decision?

And here's the bit I care about most: if it IS a real objection, does it also kill the wind/solar option? Because that buildout has its own intergenerational bill — a system that needs land, mining and continuous maintenance for a century, with the harm just spread thinner and later. My suspicion is that we're treating concentrated catastrophic risk and diffuse deferred harm as commensurable when they aren't, and that this asymmetry is doing real work in the decision — but I genuinely can't tell whether it argues for or against extending. Half the board points at France and says a fleet that's run for decades hasn't killed anyone; the other half points at Germany and says a country is allowed to just be done with it. Neither of those seems like the actual argument to me.

So: recommend extend, don't extend, or extend-with-conditions, with a date certain, and give me the reasoning in a form the two expected-value colleagues can respond to instead of dismissing. Be honest about the strongest thing they've got — if their position survives, say so, I'd rather find out now. Also dont just tell me to commission more study, and please don't cite literature at me; half the board will tune out and this needs to be about our reactor. Casual is fine, it's an internal doc.
