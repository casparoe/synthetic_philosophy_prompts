Ok so I've been reporting on mind-and-cognition stuff for years now and I've ended up with a pile of views about the "could an ant colony think?" thought experiment that I'm honestly not sure fit together. I write for a general audience, so I have to be able to defend this stuff live, and I'd rather find the cracks now then in the middle of a Q&A.

Here's where I've landed:

1) When a big enough ant colony is organized the right way, it really does have beliefs and desires — literally, not as a cute metaphor. "The colony thinks the food is east" is a true sentence in roughly the same way "I think the food is east" is.

2) Having a mind is a matter of being wired up the right way: the right kind of information processing, the right causal roles holding between inputs, inner states, and outputs. It's not about what you're made of. Neurons, ants, silicon, doesn't matter.

3) But I also think that for something to have a mind in the full-blooded sense, there has to be something it is like to be it — one subject having the experience, not just a bunch of mini-minds sitting side by side.

4) And I think a real mind's states have to be about things in their own right. If something only "represents" the food because an observer like me decides to read it that way, then it isn't really representing anything.

5) Finally, I think the colony's whole mental life is nothing over and above the ants and the way they bump into each other. You list every ant and every interaction and you have listed everything there is.

A friend who does philosophy of mind listened to all this and just said "you can't have all of those" and then changed the subject, which was maddening. So go through the list properly: which pairs are actually pulling against each other and how badly, which ones only look like they conflict but don't once you seperate them out, and what's the cheapest thing I'd have to give up to make the whole set consistent. Rank the real problems by how bad they are, and tell me if there's one single change that fixes the most at once.
