I moderate a couple of online game and VR communities, and there's an argument I keep having that I don't have a name for, and it's bugging me. Here's the shape of it. Somebody's avatar gets grabbed in a social world, or somebody spends real money on a plot of virtual land and the platform deletes it, and the community splits in half. One side says it's just a game, it's just pixels, nothing actually happened to anybody. The other side says she really was harassed, he really did lose something. And what I've noticed is that the deciding factor isn't how bad the person feels, and it isn't any fact about what the virtual stuff is made of. It's whether the thing that happened went against the understood point of that particular world. In a shooter, getting shot is the whole idea, so nobody's been wronged. Take that exact same code event -- an avatar reaching out and touching another avatar -- and drop it into a world where you're not supposed to be able to touch anyone, and now it's a real violation that gets people banned. Same pixels, opposite verdicts. I've even watched a community flip on something without a single line of the software changing: the norm shifted, and all of a sudden a thing that used to be fine is a harm. So whether a virtual harm counts as real seems to ride on what the space is for and what its members take the rules to mean, not on the metaphysics of virtual objects at all. Which is odd, because those same people will tell you flat out that virtual objects aren't real when it's about whether the platform owes you anything for your deleted land.

People keep telling me this is definately already a known idea and I should stop worrying about it. One friend says it's just Searle's institutional facts, another says it's the magic circle from game studies, another says it's the same as saying fictional objects aren't real. None of those quite lands for me. The institutional-facts one seems right that the harm depends on collective acceptance, but it doesn't explain why the same accepted rule structure can make one event a harm in one world and totally fine in another, and the magic circle seems to be about the boundary between play and ordinary life, which isn't what I'm circling.

So: is there an established name for this specific thing -- that the reality or unreality of a virtual harm is fixed by the norms and purposes of the virtual world rather than by facts about virtual objects? If there is, tell me which concept it is and how it differs from the near misses above. If there isn't, coin one and define it, and tell me what it excludes -- is it just a restatement of "harms are socially constructed," or is it doing something seperate? Where does it break down, like cases where the harm seems real no matter what the norms say? I don't want just a label, I want the seams.

Please answer in American English.
