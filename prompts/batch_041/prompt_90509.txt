I'm profiling a philosopher who offers what she calls a "two-ledger" reading of Kripkenstein. On her account, the meaning-skeptic's paradox belongs in one ledger: there is no fact about me that fixes whether I mean plus or quaddition, and no disposition, image, or cited rule closes the gap. The community's corrective practices belong in the other: they make our meaning-attributions permissible, not true. Her charge is that nearly every defender of Kripke's skeptical solution quietly moves funds from the first ledger to the second, treating "we would correct him" as though it settled a question about facts. I'd like the conceptual issue laid out: what exactly is supposed to sit in each ledger, where the illicit transfer is alleged to occur, and whether the two-ledger reading is a genuine alternative to the skeptical solution or just that solution relabeled. Please commit to a verdict on whether the reading is coherent, and say what would have to be true for the skeptical solution to answer her charge rather than merely restate the paradox.
