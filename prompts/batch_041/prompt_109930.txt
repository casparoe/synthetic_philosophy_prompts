Write an essay adjudicating between two accounts of singular causation: the event-based counterfactual account, on which an event c causes an event e when e counterfactually depends on c — had c not occurred, e would not have occurred — subject to the standard patches for preemption and overdetermination; and the variable-based interventionist account, on which c causes e when some possible intervention that fixes the value of the variable standing for c, holding the rest of the system fixed in the appropriate way, would change the value of the variable standing for e, so that causal claims are always relative to a choice of variables and to a contrast.

Test both against the following case. A canal gate can be raised from 0 to 100 percent, and a farm downstream floods whenever the gate is open more than 40 percent. On the day in question the gate operator raised the gate to 60 percent, and the farm flooded. At 30 percent the farm would have stayed dry; at 59 percent it would still have flooded.

Each account seems to answer the question of whether her raising the gate caused the flooding only once we fix something the account itself does not supply. The counterfactual account needs to know which alternative to consider — 30 percent, 59 percent, or some default — and its verdict flips with that choice. The interventionist account needs to know whether the variable is the coarse open-or-closed one or the fine 0-to-100 one, and its verdict flips with that choice.

Weigh the best replies on each side — that causal claims are always contrastive and relative to the purposes of an inquiry; that one grain is privileged by the structure of the system or by the question being asked; that the case is gerrymandered and shows nothing about ordinary causal talk — and defend a verdict about which account handles the case better and what the other must concede. If you conclude that the deeper dispute here concerns how the grain of events and variables gets fixed rather than counterfactuals versus interventions, argue for that instead. Do not end on a noncommittal note.
