After nearly forty years as an experimental natural scientist, I have retired into philosophy of mind, and I cannot shake an analogy from my old field. There, a theoretical posit justified itself by doing explanatory work that no redescription of the observed phenomena could do; when the redescription finally caught up, the posit was exposed as an idle wheel and retired without ceremony — phlogiston, caloric, and the luminiferous ether all went that way. The concept of qualia, as it is used to name the intrinsic, private, ineffable felt character of experience, looks to me like a posit of exactly this kind, and I want a sustained argument about whether it should be eliminated or kept.

Write an essay that reaches and defends a verdict on this question: is qualia, in that robust sense, an incoherent posit that does no explanatory work beyond what a functional or representational account of mind already supplies, or does it name something those accounts genuinely leave out?

Let me propose a working criterion to organize the discussion. Call a posit an idle wheel if everything it is invoked to explain can be explained, without remainder, by a redescription that never mentions it. Your essay should do all of the following. First, define qualia precisely enough to be argued about, separating the robust sense from any thin or merely verbal sense. Second, give the strongest available version of the eliminativist case, using the idle-wheel criterion and the history-of-science analogy, and confront the best objections to that case. Third, give the strongest available case for retention, including the most serious objection to eliminativism, and actually engage with that objection rather than merely listing it. Fourth, describe at least one concrete case that is supposed to distinguish two systems that are functionally identical yet differ in their qualia, and judge whether such a case can be stated without begging the question. Fifth, end on a clear verdict, even a qualified one.

Write in polished, formal American English, in continuous prose rather than bullet points, and do not retreat into a non-committal survey of the literature.
