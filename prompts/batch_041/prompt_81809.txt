I've spent the term in a reading group on the socialist calculation debate, and I want to put a view to you that I've arrived at and have you test it. As I understand the dispute, the disagreement between Mises and Hayek, on the one hand, and Lange, on the other, was ultimately a matter of information processing: Mises's challenge was that a planned economy lacks any procedure for solving the enormous system of simultaneous equations that rational allocation requires, and Lange met the challenge by showing that a central planning board could set prices and then revise them by trial and error, in much the way a competitive market does, until supply and demand came into balance — so that, given sufficient computational power, the board would arrive at the same prices a market would generate. If that is right, then the debate has simply been settled in Lange's favour by technology: the obstacles he faced in the 1930s have dissolved, and a planning board equipped with modern data processing and machine learning could run his iterative adjustment continuously and in real time. A colleague of mine who works in forecasting puts the point even more bluntly: the calculation problem was an engineering problem, and engineering has moved on.

What I'd like from you is a careful appraisal of whether that framing is sound, and, if it is, what remains of the Austrian case. If it is not sound, I'd like you to say exactly where it goes wrong — which of the disputants really held the position I have attributed to them, what the true point of contention was, and why cheaper computation does or does not speak to it. Please don't give me a reading list or a set of citations; I want the arguments reconstructed on their own terms, with the distinctions that actually matter drawn explicitly, and I would also value a short statement of what a contemporary defender of the Austrian side would still want to press. Write your answer in British English, and don't worry about keeping it short — I would far rather have the nuances than a tidy summary.
