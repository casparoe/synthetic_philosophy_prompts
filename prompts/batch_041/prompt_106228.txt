OK so I've been going back and forth with a couple of friends about the Church-Turing thesis and I've landed on a position I want to stress-test. My claim is that the thesis -- roughly, that anything we'd count as an "effective procedure," something a human mathematician could grind through step by step with no insight required, can be carried out by a Turing machine -- is not just a definition or a convention about what we choose to call "computable." I think it's a substantive, even empirical, claim about the actual world: it says something about the limits of what any physical process and any idealized human calculator can do. And I think it's true, not merely stipulative. The usual quantum-computing stuff doesn't bother me, since a quantum computer is still a Turing machine in the relevant sense (maybe faster, not more powerful), and neither does the fact that chaotic systems are unpredictable -- unpredictable isn't the same as non-computable.

What I want from you is the other side of it. What would actually count against this position? I mean: what kind of discovery, result, or argument should make me give it up, and which of those are merely logically possible versus genuinely live? I'm especially interested in the ways the different versions come apart -- the version about what humans can compute by rote, the version about what physical systems can do, and the version about some formal notion of "algorithm." Do they all stand or fall together, or could one turn out false while the others hold? What would a committed defender concede really does count against them -- for instance, does the existence of oracle machines, or "super-Turing" gadgets like a machine that completes infinitely many steps in finite time (Zeno machines), or the fact that analog/continuous systems seem to have unbounded precision, threaten anything at all? The thing that would honestly move me most is a concrete physical process we have good reason to think is genuinely non-Turing-computable, not just hard or unpredictable. And be straight with me: has this thesis been qualified so heavily that nothing could falsify it, and if so is that a bug or just what you'd expect for a claim at this level? Rank the defeaters from "would genuinely sink it" down to "a defender just shrugs."
