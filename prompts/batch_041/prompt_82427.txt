Quick one. I sit on the volunteer committee that writes the wall labels for our small peace museum, and we're stuck on the next room.

Our form so far, roughly: 1914, chief responsibility on the German government, even though Austria-Hungary declared war first and the whole thing kicked off in the Balkans. 1956, Britain, France and Israel, no hedging, they colluded and Israel went in first, and we don't dock Egypt anything for nationalising the canal because that was legal. 1939 Finland, the USSR, but with a line noting Finland's refusal of the territorial swap as a contributory choice it was perfectly entitled to make. One Balkan war where the aggressor's neighbour had spent years arming rebels inside it: aggressor gets the blame, neighbour gets one sentence saying it shares responsibility. And we never use the word guilt about a nation, only about governments and officers, because we all know what happened the last time a peace treaty pinned war guilt on a whole people.

The next room is on a war where A's army crossed the border first, but only after B spent two years funding and arming a separatist movement in A's border province and publicly swore that province would never be allowed to stay with A; A's stated aims never went past the province; both governments were authoritarian and neither public had any say; and a great power had guarenteed B's borders and then sat on its hands.

What should the label say if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which of our old labels is the closest analog, and where the record genuinely underdetermines it, what rule are we adopting with each choice? If some other war would tell those rules apart, name it. Last line: Verdict: [who we blame, five words max]
