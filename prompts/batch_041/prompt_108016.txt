I'm in this decision theory / rational choice seminar right now, and my friend Devon, who's also in it, has these really strong and weirdly consistent takes on threats, and I'm trying to figure out what he'd say about a case we havent gotten to yet.

His track record so far. One-off mugger wants my wallet -- I know he wont come back and nobody will ever hear about it -- Devon says just hand it over, not worth being a hero. A blackmailer who'll keep coming back for more as long as I keep paying -- he says refuse every time, even when the first payment is cheap, because you're buying an endless series of demands. Deterrence type stuff, where you build a weapon and commit to using it and the whole point is that you'd actually have to do a thing you'd hate -- he says that's fine, you want to be the kind of agent that follows through. Then the weird one: an acausal threat. Some agent far away that can't send me any signal and can't reach me at all has run a simulation of a mind like mine and will torture it unless my choice comes out a certain way. Devon says ignore it completely -- nothing I do causally reaches them, and paying just advertises to the universe that threatening minds like mine works. But when the prof floated a "surrogate goal" -- you add some goal you don't actually care about, like making paperclips, so a threatener aiming at your real values hits the decoy instead -- Devon said that was clever and not a betrayal of anything.

Now here's the case I want called. Same setup as the surrogate goal thing, except it's subtractive instead of additive: the only way to make myself un-extortable is to genuinely stop caring about something I actually do care about. Say a threatener is holding my little sister hostage acausally, and the one move that kills his leverage is a pill that erases my love for her -- afterwards he's got nothing to threaten me with and I go on with my life indifferent to her. Devon thinks adding a fake goal is fine. Does he think deleting a real one is fine too?

What I want: your best guess at his verdict, and way more importantly the rule he's secretly running, cause I'm pretty sure he has one and doesnt know it. Then the other rules that fit all five of his answers just as well -- I bet there's at least one that fits everything so far but flips on the pill -- which one I should think he's really using and why, and the single new case that would actually force him to choose. No reading list or anything, just the reasoning.
