Ok, I'm supposed to lay out my take on Bell for a reading group next week and I want a consistency check first, because I suspect I'm holding things that don't fit and I can't see where. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Bell's theorem is airtight. Any theory that gives every measurement a definite outcome and is local — nothing travelling faster than light — is dead. The loopholes are lab problems, not conceptual ones.

(2) I take Minkowski spacetime literally. No absolute simultaneity, no privileged slicing of the world into moments, and I don't think that's an approximation some deeper theory will repair. Lorentz invariance is exact.

(3) The correlations in entanglement are a real influence of one measurement on the distant outcome, not just an inequality that correlations happen to break. If you press me I'd put it the screening-off way: the outcome here should be screened off from what gets chosen over there by everything in the common past of the two events, and it isn't — and I think that failure is a fact about the world, not an artifact of the formalism. It's a genuine directed causal relation, not a fact about our descriptions.

(4) But I'm firm on no conspiracy. Any story where the setting choice is correlated with the particles' hidden state from the start, so that free choice is a sham, is a non-starter — you couldn't do science.

(5) Outcomes are just ordinary facts about detectors clicking. No observers, no minds, nothing created by anyone looking.

(6) I've drifted into thinking the quantum state isn't a physical field sitting out there but a way of encoding what an agent knows and can predict. I can't bring myself to take a wavefunction in configuration space literally.

(7) And I always say the no-signalling result — you can never use entanglement to send a message — is what keeps all of this compatable with relativity.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and how bad is it? Rank the genuine ones by what they'd cost me, say which pairs only look like tensions and why they're fine after all, and then tell me the single change that would clean up the most.
