I have become convinced that the regress problem admits only one non-arbitrary solution, and that it is infinitism — though a deflated version of it. On my view, to be justified is to be able to meet any challenge with a further reason, where the "chain" is not a series of occurrent beliefs or a stock of propositions subsisting somewhere, but a standing disposition to keep answering indefinitely if pressed; the demand that a finite thinker actually hold infinitely many beliefs is, I think, simply a misreading of the view. Justification is a potential infinite, like the ability to keep counting, rather than an actual one.

What I want is an exposure map. Which objections to this would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which would its own defenders concede as counting against them? Is that collapse real, and if it is, is it a refutation of the view or just a redescription of it?
