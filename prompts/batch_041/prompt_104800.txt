I am taking a seminar on truth and paradox this term, and the final assignment allows one creative piece in place of a short essay. I would like to submit a pantoum about the Liar, and I want the verse form itself to carry the argument rather than the terminology. Here is what I am after.

Write a pantoum of five quatrains, observing the strict rule: the second and fourth lines of each stanza return as the first and third lines of the next, and the final stanza closes the loop by returning to the opening stanza's first and third lines, so that the poem's last line is also its first. I want that closure to be unavoidable rather than ornamental; the poem should contain no line that can serve as its own ground.

Two of the recurring lines should be the poem's two ungrounded sentences: one that denies its own truth, and one that affirms its own truth and nothing else. Because the pantoum recycles them, each will appear in different company on each pass, and a reader who knows the literature should be able to tell from the verse alone that only one of them is genuinely paradoxical while the other is merely idle, since the self-affirming line has the same shape as the self-denying one but never forces a contradiction. Somewhere in the middle, the poem should also contain a short cycle of at least two lines, each whose truth depends on the other's, so that a reader can set a two-line loop beside the one-line loop and see why only some loops bite.

I would also like the revenge problem to be visible in the structure. If any stanza tries to exempt itself, either by denying that it is the sort of sentence that can be true or false at all, or by adding a clause that pushes the question up another level, that manoeuvre should read as one more turn of the same loop rather than as an escape. The poem should not name a philosopher, a theory, or any technical term; if the structure does not show the distinction, naming it will not rescue the poem. Give the piece a title that does not announce the paradox in advance.

Finally, supply the closing stanza twice. In one version the loop should come out merely ungrounded, so that nothing in it receives a truth value but nothing explodes either; in the other it should come out genuinely contradictory, with a recurring line both true and false at once. The two versions should differ only in the final stanza, and it should be evident from the verse alone which reading each ending commits to.

The piece will be read aloud at our last meeting, so the repetitions need to sound like they could not have fallen otherwise, rather than like an exercise in recall.
