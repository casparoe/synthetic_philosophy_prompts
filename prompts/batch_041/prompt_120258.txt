Getting my views on credence and accuracy in order before a reading group next week, and I'd like a consistency check on the package first. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Accuracy is the only thing that's epistemically good in itself; a credence is good just insofar as its accurate.
2. The score has to be strictly proper — you should never be able to raise your expected score by misreporting your own credence.
3. The credence function to have is the one that maximizes expected accuracy, expected by your own current lights.
4. Incoherence is irrational because incoherent credences are accuracy-dominated by some coherent rival. I don't need betting or money to make that point.
5. Conditionalization is required, and I think that's an accuracy result too, not a separate coherence requirement.
6. Logical omniscience is too much to ask — you can rationally be less than certain of a tautology.
7. A norm that a creature like us can't follow isn't a norm for us.

Which of these actually pull against each other? Rank the tensions by how bad they are, and mark the ones that are only apparently in tension — don't just tell me everything conflicts. For each real one, say what the cheapest thing to give up is and what that costs me, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
