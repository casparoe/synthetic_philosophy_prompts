I am a novelist, and I have written myself into a metaphysical corner that I would rather have quantified than narrated.

The book concerns Mara, a night-shift emergency dispatcher. Under her employer's protocol — and under a promise she made to her late father, a firefighter — she is obliged to relay any credible report of smoke to the fire service. One night she takes a call reporting smoke in an apartment building, judges it a hoax, and does not relay it. A tenant dies of smoke inhalation. Two wrinkles complicate the causal story. First, earlier that evening a man named Ivo had disabled the building's alarm system; had he not done so, the alarm would have woken the building and the tenant would very likely have escaped. Second, the warning, had Mara given it, would have saved the tenant only with some probability — he was a heavy sleeper, and I put the chance at roughly two in five — so her omission raises the probability of death without being strictly necessary for it.

The proposition I want a credence on is this: Mara's failure to relay the call is a cause of the tenant's death — that is, it belongs in the correct causal explanation of the death alongside ordinary positive causes, and not merely as a background condition or a moral shortcoming. Read 'cause' in the metaphysical or explanatory sense, not the legal sense of liability.

Give me your credence, and decompose it. I want the sub-questions the number rests on, a rough probability for each, and one line of justification apiece, including at least: whether absences and omissions can be causes at all; whether the correct account of omission causation demands counterfactual dependence, or probability-raising, or the violation of a norm in addition to dependence; whether Ivo's positive act crowds out or preempts Mara's omission; and whether the fact that Mara's duty was self-imposed rather than institutional changes anything. Combine them into a number, tell me which sub-question the final figure is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move it most.

For what it is worth, my own credence is about 0.3 and my editor's is about 0.75; tell me which of the two of us is more likely mistaken, and about which sub-question we most likely differ.

Begin with your bottom-line number in the first sentence, then give the decomposition; no preamble, and do not restate the case back to me.
