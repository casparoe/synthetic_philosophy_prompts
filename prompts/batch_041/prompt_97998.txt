I sit on the content appeals board for a mid-size social platform — we're the last internal stop before a takedown or a reinstatement becomes final, our rulings get published in a quarterly transparency report, and honestly the board has been so split lately that I want to nail down what rule we're actually running before the next batch lands on my desk. Background so you know the flavor of the disagreement: I'm the token academic on the panel, most of the others are trust-and-safety people trained on the US First Amendment, and two of the European members keep invoking the hate-speech statutes back home, so our published reasoning reads like three different courts wrote it. Here's what we actually did over the past year, roughly in order. We left up a long post calling a religion's core doctrines incoherent and its founder a fraud — no slurs, no threats, just contempt for the beliefs. We left up a satirical cartoon of a sitting politician, since politicians aren't a protected class under our policy. We took down a post that used an ethnic slur and said the group were "vermin who should be driven out of the neighbourhood," posted the evening before a rally at a named hall. We left up a thread disputing a religious group's historical claims and calling its scripture a fabrication. And we took down a post that called a sexual minority "a disease on the body politic" and "rats in the walls," again with a time and a place attached. Nobody's vote has changed on any of these, and when I ask people *why* they voted the way they did, the answers are all over the map.

Now the new case, and I need a recommendation I can actually defend in the report. An account with about 400k followers posted a meme: a photo of a member of an ethnic minority group (the group is protected under our policy) with a slur in the caption, plus a line saying people from that group are "stupid and prone to violence." No call to action, no venue, no date, nothing about anyone doing anything — just the slur and the claim. It's getting tens of thousands of shares and the replies are grim. Half the board wants it down yesterday, the First Amendment people say we'd be punishing an opinion, and I genuinely can't tell whether our past rulings commit us one way or the other.

Tell me what we should do if we want to be consistent with our own record. Lead with the decision, then lay out the rule — or rules — our five past rulings are compatible with, say what each of those rules predicts about this post, give me your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and name the one future case that would cleanly separate the rules we can't decide between. I care less about the answer than about whether the rule you extract would have predicted the five rulings above without fudging. And no preamble, please — first sentence is the bottom line, reasoning after.
