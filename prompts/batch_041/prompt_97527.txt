I have no background in philosophy, but I have been trying to think through the puzzle of whether self-deception is even possible. A friend of mine, who is quite sure it is not, keeps using the analogy of tickling yourself: you cannot do it because the part of you that would do the tickling knows exactly what the other part is about to feel, and the surprise that makes tickling work is missing. He takes this to show that the whole idea of deceiving yourself is incoherent—you would have to be both the liar and the one lied to, and you would have to not know what you already know. I want an audit of that analogy rather than a general essay on self-deception. Please map out what corresponds to what: who or what is the tickler, the ticklee, the knowledge of the action, the surprise, and the reflex? Then tell me where the mapping breaks down, and which breaks matter for his conclusion.

One thing that makes me suspicious of the analogy is that I have read that people can tickle themselves if a machine introduces a delay between their own movement and the touch. If that is right, then the impossibility in the tickling case is not absolute; it depends on the timing and indirection of the knowledge. That seems relevant to self-deception, because a person might know the truth in one sense but not have it present in the right way at the moment they form the comforting belief. So please consider whether self-deception could work like delayed self-tickling: not a divided mind, but a single mind whose knowledge is somehow out of sync with its belief-forming processes. Is that a genuine possibility, or does it just rename the problem?

Finally, I would like you to separate what is actually settled from what is genuinely contested among people who study this topic, and not to present a contested view as the consensus. I am not asking for citations or a reading list. What I want is: the correspondences, the disanalogies that matter, a verdict on whether the tickling analogy can support the conclusion that self-deception is impossible, and, if it cannot, a better analogy if one exists. I am especially interested in whether the tickling picture quietly assumes a unified, fully transparent self, and whether there are accounts of belief and attention on which self-deception is possible without a divided mind.
