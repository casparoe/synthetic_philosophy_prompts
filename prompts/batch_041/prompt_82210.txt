ok so this is a slightly weird ask. over the holidays i got into it with my brother about a situation where i couldve broken a promise to stop someone else from doing something worse, and ive been chewing on it for weeks. a friend told me this is basically the paradox of deontology — the view says a wrong is bad and shouldnt happen, but also that you mustnt do the wrong thing even to stop more of the same wrong from happening, so it looks like the theory is at war with itself, or like it secretly treats the same act as worse just because its your hands. i dont want to just read about it, i want to feel the shape of it.

can you write me a villanelle about it? nineteen lines, and the two refrains should be the two horns: one refrain is the voice that says you still may not do it, whatever it forestalls; the other is the voice that says if the wrong is really bad, why not have fewer of them. the form should do the actual work here — the refrains keep coming back and never quite join up until the closing quatrain, which is pretty much why the two sides keep talking past each other. someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which refrain is which and why the last two lines put them side by side instead of resolving them.
