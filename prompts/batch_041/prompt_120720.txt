I am a graduate student in a seminar on anthropic reasoning and self-locating belief, and next week I am presenting a paper defending the self-indication assumption against the presumptuous philosopher. My thesis is that the presumptuous philosopher refutes only a version of the self-indication assumption that applies to the reference class of all observers; if the assumption is restricted to the reference class of observers who share your total evidence, the argument loses its force, and what remains is a disagreement with the self-sampling assumption about whether the reference class should be evidential or ontological. The audience will be the seminar: two cosmologists who use anthropic reasoning in their work, a decision theorist who works on anthropic decision theory and thinks the self-indication assumption is a mistake, and a philosopher of probability who doubts that self-locating credences are coherent. I need the objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way each would phrase them, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
