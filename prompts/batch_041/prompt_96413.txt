I've been half-drafting this post for like two years and I want a second pair of eyes before I inflict it on anyone. It's about the Dennett/Searle thing on original vs. derived intentionality — Dennett's line that all the aboutness we attribute to thermostats and two-bitsers and brains is just the intentional stance paying off, Searle's line that no, your thermostat's intentionality is borrowed from us while yours is the real thing, and that distinction is real and matters. I've never been satisfied with either, so here's the draft.

The move: both sides are asking a yes/no question — does it have the real kind or the borrowed kind? — when the interesting quantity is continuous. Proposal: original intentionality is how well a system's internal states keep playing the same representational role across interventions on the world, not just on the inputs. The two-bitser that maps coin signatures to dispense loses its belief that this is a quarter the instant you swap the coin's alloy or jam a slug in — its internal state is pinned to the sensor reading, and any world-intervention that leaves the reading fixed while changing the fact destroys the representation. That's what derived means, spelled out: the state-to-world mapping is stipulated by us, so it holds only where our stipulation happens to be right. A thermostat is the same story one level up — put a lamp next to it and it believes it's 40 degrees. Meanwhile a system trained only on sunny-day photos that still says dog under a strobe light, in a mirror, in a costume, has states whose representational role is held in place by something other than our stipulation. Searle wants a line, Dennett wants no line; I say it's a gradient and the gradient is set by intervention-robustness. Bonus: it tells you where to look — at the mushy middle of the gradient, not the endpoints where everyone already agrees.

Things I want you to hit hard: (1) is this just "a model is a thing that generalizes" wearing a lab coat? (2) can Dennett just say, yes, and the robust one is the one worth interpreting, that's the whole point — i.e. have I actually disagreed with him or only restated him? (3) does Searle get anything out of it, given that survives-interventions is still something we check from the outside, so it might be observer-relative in exactly the way he's objecting to? (4) counterexamples in either direction: a dumb system that's definately super intervention-robust but obviously has no original intentionality, or a system we all want to call genuinely intentional whose states are fragile. (5) is any of this just the causal/informational theory of content with extra steps? Comments on accuracy first, interestingness second, and if the whole framing is a category error rather than merely wrong, say that plainly rather than being polite about it. If I've mis-stated either guy's position, steelman him for me before you criticize.
