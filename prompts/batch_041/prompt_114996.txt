I have spent the last few years doing clinical research and reading philosophy of science in the evenings, and there is a pattern I keep running into that I have never been able to find under any name. I would rather have the concept than the citation, so let me describe it as carefully as I can and let you tell me what it is.

The first instance runs like this. A category is introduced to describe a group of people who are already turning up in clinics. Once the category is public, some of those people come to understand themselves in its terms: they notice features of themselves they had not noticed before, describe their difficulties with its vocabulary, and ask for the treatments it names. Nobody has redefined anything, and the manual's wording is exactly what it was. But the population the category now picks out is not the population it picked out at the beginning — its typical member has a different age, a different severity, a different course.

The second instance runs the other way. The label acquires a cost, because insurers, employers and schools treat it as a mark, and people who might have received it arrange their lives so as not to. The category's extension contracts toward the cases that cannot avoid it, so that it becomes a category of the severe. Again, no one has redefined anything.

What makes both cases hard for me is the last step. In each of them the numbers are reported as stable: prevalence is steady, the instruments are recalibrated, the thresholds adjusted, so that the change in who is being counted is not visible in the data at all. The consequence is that a body of research accumulates across two different populations and is cited as though it were about one thing, and nobody can say when the referent shifted, because nothing in the record marks the shift.

Is there a name for this? I know the self-fulfilling prophecy, but that is about an expectation producing the outcome it predicts, and it says nothing about the part where the measuring apparatus is adjusted so that the change does not register. I know the Hawthorne effect, but that is local and temporary, and it is about being watched rather than about being classified. I know Goodhart's law, but here nobody is gaming a measure — the classified people are not trying to satisfy anything, and the researchers are not cynical. I have a suspicion that philosophers of the social sciences have discussed something adjacent, to the effect that a classification can act on the people it classifies, but I do not know what they call it or how far the discussion goes, and in any case I want the phenomenon carved rather than labelled.

So: is it one thing or two? Which features are essential — must the classified be aware of the category, or can the same shift happen to a category nobody knows they are in? Is the silent recalibration of the instruments part of the phenomenon, or a separate one that tends to accompany it? How do I distinguish it from a category that was simply ill-defined from the start, and from the case where the criteria are openly revised and everyone can see it happening? And if nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and tell me which of my two instances is the better example and whether the second is the same phenomenon or its mirror image.

Please answer in continuous prose, with no headings, lists, bold, or tables — the way you would write a letter. A bare name would leave me exactly where I am.
