I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am drafting an essay on the rhetoric of privacy. At a recent data-governance panel, one participant kept repeating a one-liner: privacy is not a wall; it is a set of locks. He used it to argue that because locks are selected one by one, privacy should be managed through individual contracts and technical settings rather than broad regulation. I want to take the line seriously enough to see what it could actually mean before deciding whether it supports him. Assume you cannot look anything up; I do not want a literature survey, citations, or a reading list. I need a conceptual disambiguation. The line is not a canonical quotation and should not be attributed to anyone; treat it as a piece of live argument.

Please set out the distinct precise claims the one-liner could be making, from the nearly trivial to the genuinely radical. For each, state what argumentative work it does, whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something dubious or false, and whether it would actually support the panelist's conclusion. Separate readings that sound similar but differ over what privacy is: for example, secrecy versus control, access versus use, alienability versus inalienability, a boundary versus a bundle of permissions, individual consent versus contextual norms, ex ante permission versus ex post accountability. Also identify which reading the panelist most likely needs, which reading would be most useful to a regulator, and what crucial distinction the one-liner obscures.

Return a single JSON object with the following fields:

- one_liner: the line under discussion.
- context: one sentence restating the panelist's use of it.
- readings: an array of six to ten objects, each with:
  - label: a short name for the reading.
  - precise_claim: the reading stated as a precise thesis, in one or two sentences, without relying on the metaphor.
  - kind: one of "platitude", "substantive", "dubious", "false".
  - argumentative_work: what the reading would let someone conclude, especially about contract versus regulation.
  - supports_panelist: one of "yes", "no", "partly", "only with an extra premise".
  - extra_premise: the needed premise if the previous field is "only with an extra premise"; otherwise null.
  - counterexample_or_limit: a brief case or limit that tests the reading; otherwise null.
- most_likely_reading: the label of the reading the panelist probably means.
- best_reading_for_regulator: the label of the reading that would most help a regulator.
- obscured_distinction: the most important distinction the one-liner hides, in one or two sentences.
- overall_assessment: a short paragraph on whether the one-liner, on its best reading, supports the panelist's conclusion.

Keep the readings distinct and non-overlapping. Do not use citations, names of scholars, or historical references. I want the conceptual map, not a bibliography.
