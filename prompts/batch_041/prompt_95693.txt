Suppose Cicero got dropped into 2025 and spent a week reading the news. He's the guy who wrote that little dialogue about old age, where the old man insists it can be a decent life as long as you keep your wits and your friends. So I'm curios how he'd react to a few things that weren't really on the table back then.

First, the anti-aging scene: people taking rapamycin and metformin off-label, rich tech guys spending millions to shave years off their "biological age," and the idea that if medicine could add a year of healthy life for every year of research, we'd basically stop dying of old age. Is that the fulfilment of his position or a betrayal of it?

Second, the boring policy stuff: governments pushing the retirement age up, old people staying in the workforce, grandparents parked in care homes and minded by robots.

Third, a niche idea some economists float where parents get extra votes on behalf of their young kids — which would quietly shift political weight away from the old.

Where would he land on each, and would the three answers hang together? Don't just list conclusions, say why, and flag wherever you're extrapolating past what we actually know about him.
