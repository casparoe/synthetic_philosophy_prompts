I'm setting the final for a third-year course on logic and its philosophy, and the one essay question has to be on Carroll's tortoise — "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles". The lazy version ("explain the regress, then explain how to stop it") is a free pass for anyone who skimmed it, so I want something sharper. Here's the seed I want to build from: in a seminar a student claimed the tortoise only gets traction because Achilles keeps writing "if A and B, then Z" into the book as one more premise, and that once you insist modus ponens is a rule we follow rather than a proposition we believe, the whole regress just evaporates — the tortoise isn't owed a further reason, he's simply refusing to play the game. I think that's half a solution and half a dodge, and I want the exam to force students to work out which half is which. So: write the essay question (something answerable in ~50 minutes of writing), a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme — five must-hit points adding to 20 marks, the marks each is worth, and a one-line note on how students typically fluff each one. Don't make the question hinge on naming Russell or Winch or anyone else.
