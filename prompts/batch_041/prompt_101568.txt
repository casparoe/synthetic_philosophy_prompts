I've been going in circles on something and I can't find the right label for it, so I'm hoping you can help. Here's the pattern I keep running into.

Everyone accepts a certain kind of trade. You make a person worse off now for that same person's sake later -- take the bitter medicine, sit through the boring physio, study tonight instead of going out -- and nobody thinks anything's wrong with it. It's just prudence. But if you make A worse off for B's sake, even when the total comes out bigger, everyone's alarm bells go off. Fine, so far so ordinary.

What I keep tripping on is that the line between those two cases isn't where we act like it is. Take someone who's had a stroke and come out with a different personality and no memory of who she was. I can make "her" miserable now for the sake of the person she'll become, and it feels like the cross-person case, not the within-one-life case, even though legally and biologically it's one person. Or the other way round: a couple who've been together so long they finish each other's sentences -- trading one off against the other starts to feel less like A-vs-B and more like the medicine case. And with dementia, or a thirty-year coma, or someone who's simply changed a lot, I honestly can't tell which side of the line I'm standing on, and neither can anyone I ask.

What bugs me is that people argue as if the line were sharp -- either it's a legitimate within-a-life trade or it's a forbidden between-lives one -- when the cases that actually matter morally are the fuzzy ones, and the fuzziness is doing all the work. Is there a name for this? The thing where a trade-off's status flips depending on how continuous a person is over time, and where we pretend the flip happens at some definite point but it dosen't.

I know there's the idea that persons are separate, so you can't just add up their pains as if they were one person's; and someone once told me the important thing isn't whether it's the same person at the end but whether the earlier and later selves are connected in the right way. But neither of those is quite the thing I'm pointing at -- they both treat it as all-or-nothing, same person or not, and it's precisely the in-between that I can't get a handle on.

If there's an established name, great, but a bare name won't satisfy me. I want to know exactly what it covers: where the line actually is, whether it's one line or several different ones (memory? character? bodily continuity? caring about your future self?), and what the concept is *not* -- in particular how it's supposed to relate to the blunt "persons are separate" principle, since that one seems to assume the very sharpness I can't find. And if there's no name for it, coin one and define it, with the edge cases spelled out.
