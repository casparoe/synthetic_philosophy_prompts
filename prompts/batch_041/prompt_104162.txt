I'm in this small reading group that's been working through the theory side of AI safety — the stuff that tries to make "an agent that reasons about itself and rewrites its own code" precise. We spent the last month on the Löbian obstacle and honestly we're all a bit stuck on what to do with it. My (probly shaky) understanding: if you build an agent whose reasoning is a formal theory strong enough to talk about itself, and you let it trust its own proofs in general — it accepts "if I can prove P, then P" for every P — then by Löb's theorem it can prove P for any P at all, which makes it inconsistent, which makes its "trust" worthless. So a self-modifying agent can't just take its future self's proofs at face value; it needs some restricted or hedged form of self-trust. The usual moves seem to be: restrict the trust to a fragment (Π₁ sentences, say), or find some kind of tiling scheme where each generation trusts the next, or drop proof-based trust altogether for something more decision-theoretic, or the Vingean-reflection version of the problem (how can you be confident in a successor that's smarter than you are?). One person in the group keeps insisting you can get infinite tiling as long as each generation is strictly weaker in consistency strength than the last, which sounds like it dodges Löb but might just relocate the problem into the base case and throw away the self-improvement we wanted in the first place.

We've got maybe three months and four people who'll actually do the work — two philosophers who are comfortable with logic but don't love arithmetic-flavored proofs, two with real math backgrounds — and I've been asked to write a short memo on where the group should put its effort. So: inside this cluster (the Löbian obstacle, self-trust, tiling, Vingean reflection, bounded/computational versions of the same), which open questions actually matter most, and which are overrated or not worth our months? I want the reasoning far more than the list. For each thing you'd prioritize: what is it upstream of — would settling it make some other problem easy or moot, or does nothing else really depend on it? How far could this particular group realistically get in a quarter? And is the whole obstacle maybe an artifact of insisting that the agent's reasoning be a formal theory it can inspect — because if it is, that seems like it should reorder the entire list. Also tell me what would have to happen over the next year or so for something to move up or down your ranking, and where you'd expect the most disagreement amoung people who know the area.
