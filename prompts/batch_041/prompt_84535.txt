I keep reading about firms that are allowed to check their own work — they build the thing, they test the thing, they sign it off and send the file to the regulator, and as far as I can tell nobody outside ever re-runs the tests. There's a twist that bothers me more: the software doing the checking is itself checked, either by itself or by an older copy of itself, so the whole loop closes back on itself. And yet the same firms will tell you that a rival's self-testing proves nothing and insist on an independent lab. You cant have it both ways, can you.

So what does that practice actually assume about a thing vouching for itself? Does it need the strong claim that a system can reliably judge its own reliability, or would something much weaker do — that the checks are usually right, or that the people running them are honest, or just that it's cheaper than the alternative? Which rival views does it rule out, and are its two halves consistent with each other or are they quietly running two different theories? And if we gave up on the idea altogether — trust has to come from outside, no exceptions — what would really have to change about how we sign things off? British English please, and go gently, I haven't read any of the philosophy.
