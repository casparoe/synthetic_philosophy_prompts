I write about science and ideas for a living (mostly for a general smart-reader audience) and I'm trying to do a Fermi-paradox piece that isn't the same Drake-equation shrug everyone else files. I've got a framing I like, and I want your actual numbers on it, not vibes.

Heres the framing. Call a civ 'loud' if it expands outward at a decent fraction of lightspeed and visibly reworks the stars it reaches — waste heat, megastructures, the kind of thing you could eventually spot from across a galaxy. Two stipulations: loudness is a choice as much as a capability (a civ can have the tech and still decide to sit still or stay quiet), and going loud is plausibly a one-way ticket (you can't un-light up a galaxy once you've lit it up).

The proposition I want a credence on: E = no loud civ has ever existed anywhere in our observable universe before now — i.e. if one ever exists, we're it, or near the front of the line. Give me P(E).

But the number alone is useless to me. I want the decomposition: the sub-questions P(E) rides on, a rough probability for each, how they combine, and which term the final number is most sensitive to. At minimum I'd like you to price these, and add any I've missed:
- life actually starting on a habitable planet at all (and whether that's even the right place to stick a probability, or whether it's more like a coinflip that's already come up heads once and that's all we can say)
- given life, it getting to a big-brained, tool-using species
- given such a species, it surviving long enough to have the option of going loud
- given the option, it taking it — as opposed to staying quiet forever, or going loud and then flaming out fast
- whether galaxy-spanning expansion is even physically and temporally feasible on the relevant clock, or whether I'm just smuggling in a sci-fi premise
- the timing term: the galaxy is old and our star isn't, so if loud civs were even mildly common, someone should've arrived long ago — how much does that actually constrain E, and does it depend alot on stuff I'm assuming without saying so?

Also tell me where the terms are secretly correlated (I suspect several of them are and I keep pretending they aren't), which single observation or argument would definately move the number the most, and whether E is even the right thing to be putting a number on — if the honest answer is that the silence tells us nothing about our earliness, say so and give me your credence in that instead.
