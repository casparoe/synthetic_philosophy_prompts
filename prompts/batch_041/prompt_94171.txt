Anyway it hit me that Christopher Nolan's The Prestige is basically that thought experiment turned into a magic trick, and I want a proper reading of the film through it.

Quick recap of the bits that matter, since I'm sure you know it: Angier gets Tesla to build him a machine for his teleporting act, and the machine makes a duplicate of him every time, and every night one of the two of them ends up drowning in the tank under the stage while the other takes the applause. So my big question is this. If Parfit is right that what matters in survival isnt the persisting "you" but just the psychological continuity — memory, character, that kind of thing, what he calls Relation R — then is Angier actually dying every night, or is he murdering some stranger who happens to be him? The film really wants us to feel the horror of the tank, and at the end there's a room full of his drowned copies, so it's staging the loss as completely real. Does the movie land on Parfit's side or against him? My hunch is it's against him emotionally even though the plot is basically a giant Parfit illustration, but I'd like someone to actually argue it out rather than just agree with me.

The other thing I keep chewing on is the Borden twist — turns out Borden is identical twins sharing one whole life, one name, one wife. That feels like the "branching" case Parfit talks about, where the relation forks and you supposedly can't say which branch is the real you. Is the film saying branching survival is a nightmare (the wife kills herself over which twin actually loves her) or just that two people can share what matters without sharing a single identity? And does that undercut Parfit or kind of support him?

One more angle: Parfit thinks if identity isn't what matters then we should be less terrified of our own future death and more willing to care about the continuity instead of the particular person. Angier drowning himself every night just to keep the trick running is that idea pushed to a sociopathic extreme. Is he a Parfitian hero or a walking reductio of the whole view?
