Right, so my flatmate and I have just spent the best part of three hours arguing about whether the agricultural revolution was a mistake, and I need a second opinion before I lose my mind. He's read all the Diamond and the Sahlins and he's absolutley convinced we should have stayed foragers — taller, healthier, more equal, barely any work, and then somebody planted a seed and it was all downhill from there. I don't buy it, but I never manage to say why in the moment.

So: make the case that the agricultural revolution was *not* a mistake. And please don't give me the usual "but it gave us art and science and dentistry" line, because he'll just say those turned up millenia later and were no consolation to the people who actually lived through the transition. I want something he won't have heard. Here's the angle I want you to take seriously and then defend: the thing to compare isn't how well the average person did, but how many people there were to do it. If farming made each individual life somewhat worse while vastly more people got to live lives that were still, on balance, worth living, then it's not at all obvious the trade was a bad one — and I want you to argue that it wasn't, as tightly as you can.

Then, because he'll be straight back at me with it: deal with the reply that you can't just tot up worthwhile lives like that, and that any view which says more people at lower welfare is better ends somewhere we'd all find monstrous. Meet that head-on instead of dancing round it. Take a side, don't hedge everything into mush, and tell me where your argument is weakest, because I'd rather know that going in.
