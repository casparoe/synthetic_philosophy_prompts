what im defending: the standard justification for advance directives — that they carry the competent persons autonomy forward over the later, incompetent one — is quietly doing something it cant admit, namely treating the earlier self as having authority over a self it is no longer. my alternative is that a directive is a status power, closer to a will than to a promise i make to my future self: what binds is that the institution (law, hospital, committee) treats the written declaration as decisive, not that the earlier me owned the later me. that shifts the real question from "what did she want" to "what does the institution owe the person who is actually here now." i lean on the will analogy hard, i concede up front that a body isnt property, and i also want to say the current self can revise in a way a will cant — my advisor says thats the crack in the whole thing and i think shes definately right.
