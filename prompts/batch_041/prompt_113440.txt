So I moved to this small town about a year ago and I've started to suspect that most of what I know about the place I know because of the order in which people happened to tell me things, not because of anything I ever saw for myself. The water here is supposedly bad. The old mill is supposedly a done deal. The school is supposedly great. My sister lives in a town with basically the same water situation and over there everyone is convinced the opposite thing. Neither town is full of idiots. Its been bugging me for months and I want to make something out of it.

Give me a short story about a group that talks itself into a confident belief, where the belief comes out of the sequence of who spoke first rather than out of anyone finding anything out. A village, a department, a neighborhood, a big family, your call. Things I'd really like it to do:

Nobodys dumb. Every move should be sensible given what that person has actually seen. Not a conformity story, not a cowardice story — the frightening version is where everyone reasons well and the group still lands on something no individual's private evidence supports. Please don't have anyone explain the mechanism or give it a name.

There should be a point where the public record goes dead: after the first handful of people go on record, the next person's choice stops telling you anything about what they actually know. I want a character who does have real information, goes along anyway, and the reader can see the group is now running on nothing.

Before that, a long stall. Everyone would rather let the next person try it first, because whoever goes first eats the cost and the information spills out to everybody for free. Show me the years where nothing happens, for exactly that reason.

Then something small — a rumor, one bad week, a newcomer saying something offhand — and the whole thing falls over faster than it went up. And at the end I don't want to be able to tell whether the new belief is any better founded than the old one. No narrator tipping the scales, no reveal that one side had it right.

It should turn on timing. Shuffle who spoke first and the town believes the opposite. I want that to feel inevitable rather than like a gimmick.

Bonus if the thing they all believe in also carries a plain coordination payoff — everyone uses the same well, or the same babysitter, or the same bank — so that inferring-from-everyone-else and just-wanting-to-be-on-the-same-page get tangled together and neither the characters nor I can sort out which one is doing the work.

Setting, period, tone, length are yours. Tighter is better. If it reads like a lecture I've messed up.
