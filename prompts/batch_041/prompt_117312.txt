I'm in Year 12 and doing an EPQ on nuclear strategy (I know, cheerful stuff), and there's a pattern I keep noticing that I don't have a name for. It's definately doing my head in a bit. The pattern is this: two sides both really don't want a war, but each side thinks the only way to stop the other side attacking is to make its threat believable. And the way you make it believable is by making it not fully under your control. Like if I say to someone that if they take my seat I'll punch them, they might not believe me, because I might calm down and not do it. But if I set up some stupid tripwire so that anyone who takes the seat gets a bucket of paint on them, that's credible, but now I can't stop it when the wrong person takes the seat, or when someone trips over it by accident. In the nuclear version it's like putting launch authority lower down, or making the missiles look like they'll fire on their own if the early warning says so, or saying the decision is already made. It makes the other side more scared, which is supposed to make them back off, but it also means a false alarm or a random officer could start the thing no one wants. Another version is stationing your own soldiers in a place you might not actually want to defend, so that if it's attacked you get dragged in whether you like it or not. So the thing I'm trying to name is this trade-off: the more credible you make the threat, the less control you have over it, and the more you succeed in making it credible, the more likely it is to go off by accident. And then there's a second bit: once you've made it automatic, is it even a threat anymore, or is it just a machine that might kill everyone? I can't seperate the two bits in my head. I've heard the word brinkmanship, but that seems to be about deliberately going close to the edge, not quite about the giving-up-control bit. Is there an established name for this exact bind? And if there isn't, can you name it and define it so it's actually clear what's included and what isn't? I'm not just after a label, I want to know how it's different from just making a threat, from the security dilemma, from mutually assured destruction, and from ordinary risk-taking. Also, does the name still apply if only one side does it, or if it's accidental rather than deliberate? And what about when the whole point is that you can't tell whether it's deliberate or accidental? Cheers.
