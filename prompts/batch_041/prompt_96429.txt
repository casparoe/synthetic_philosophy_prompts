Write me a short story built around a perceptual puzzle I can't stop chewing on. Flash two dots in quick succession — one at the top of a screen, then a different-colored one lower down — and with the right timing people see a single dot gliding down the screen that flips color exactly halfway along its path. Here's the part that gets me: they see the flip happening midway, i.e. at a moment before the second dot was even on the screen. So either the brain sat on everything and only assembled the experience after both flashes had arrived (meaning the experience you actually had came later than you'd ever guess), or it built the experience live and then went back and quietly rewrote the early bit, so that what you remember seeing isnt what you originally saw. From the inside, and from the outside, those two are indistinguishable, and one of them means your own past is partly a cover story. Don't explain any of this in the story. Just put it in a world where somone is making a living off of measuring when experience happens — a tiny lab that sells a timestamp for the moment of experience, to courts, insurers, whoever — and let them find out the machine can't do the thing they've been selling. I want the reader to close the story unsure which of the two kinds of creature they are. 1200 words or so is fine.
