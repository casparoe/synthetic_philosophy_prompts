ok so there's this view that circulates in the suffering-focused ethics corner of the internet that people call tranquilism: the only thing that's actually good is the absence of craving and suffering, and pleasure is never a positive good sitting on top of that. when you enjoy something, it's just a lack being briefly filled, and the filling isn't the good -- the lack not being there is. it's not that pleasure is bad, it's that the ledger only has one side. i'm half-convinced on bad days and think it's definately unhinged on good ones.

i want a villanelle in the voice of someone who fully holds it, and i want the form to do the arguing. two refrains: one a first-person statement of craving, one a statement of the quiet that's left when the craving stops. the craving refrain comes back unchanged, obviously, thats what a villanelle does -- but by its fourth or fifth outing it should read as a completely different kind of sentence than it was in line one, and the reader should be able to see exactly why. then the ending: the form is built to make its last two lines land as a resolution, and the doctrine says there's no such thing as a resolution that adds anything, only the noise stopping. so the closing couplet, where the two refrains finally sit side by side, has to hand the reader the satisfaction the form promised while the view quietly declines to cash it. no cheating by letting the peace turn out to be a bonus.

accurate enough that someone whos read the hedonism-vs-tranquilism stuff nods, good enough as a poem that someone who hasn't still finishes it. the objection i want it to feel breathing down its neck, without ever stating it, is the one that says if only the absence of suffering counts then a contented life, a happy lobotomy and a life never lived start to look suspiciously similar. 19 lines, strict form, and don't name-drop any philosophers.
