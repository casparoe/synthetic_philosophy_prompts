Write me a mock FAQ page for a company that's obviously a parody — call it The Sphex Method, an executive-coaching program that claims to cure "sphexishness," the thing where the wasp drags its cricket up to the burrow, goes in to check it, and if you nudge the cricket while she's inside she just hauls it back and checks again, over and over, because the interruption resets the whole routine. About 600 words, numbered Q&As, deadpan corporate wellness voice. The funny should come from getting the philosophy right so someone who knows the sphexishness/free-will discussion laughs instead of wincing: the program promises "flexibility" and "genuine choice," but every answer the founder gives is itself the same fixed loop, and somewhere in the fine print it quietly admits the method works by making your loops *bigger* rather than getting you out of any of them. Let the FAQ chew on the real objections — is anything short of infinite non-sphexish, does noticing you're in a loop need something outside the loop, is the founder's own sales pitch free or just a longer routine — but never announce that that's what it's doing. Target is the self-help/optimization discourse that treats a philosophical problem as a productivity bug; invent the founder, don't name real coaches, no citations.
