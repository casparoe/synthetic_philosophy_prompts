The talk: I argue that the Frege-Geach problem is not a special problem for expressivism about "wrong" — it's the exact same shape of problem you get with epistemic modals ("might") and predicates of personal taste ("tasty"), and everyone agrees those embed fine. So expressivists should stop hand-building bespoke machinery (attitudes towards attitudes, hierarchies, whatever) and just help themselves to whatever the taste/modal people use to handle embedding. My version of the borrowing: "wrong" gets one stable semantic value, a function from a practical setting (a plan, a set of commitments) to a set of licences on action, and embedding in a conditional antecedent just means the licence is suspended rather than issued. Assertion issues it. So "if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong" composes without any attitude-hierarchy trickery.
