I'm in a perception lab but I also sit in on a philosophy of mind reading group, and for about a year now two of us have been going at it about cognitive penetration. One of them (call her the penetrator) thinks what you believe, want, and know can get into perceptual experience itself -- the banana looks yellower to you because you know bananas are yellow, a face looks more menacing because you've been primed to expect menace, and so on. The other (call him the encapsulator) thinks perception is a sealed module and that every one of these alleged effects is really a shift in judgment, response bias, memory, or attention, none of which is perception. They keep talking past each other and I've been volunteered to write up the doc they should have produced together, so I need you to draft it.

I want it as a numbered list of claims both of them can actually sign -- about what the dispute is even about, about what "perceptual" is being contrasted with, about which findings are agreed to be real as findings and merely disputed as interpretations -- and then a disagreements table. For each row I want the disagreement stated twice, once in wording the penetrator would accept for her own side and once in wording the encapsulator would accept for his, and then the crux: the sub-question such that settling it would actually move at least one of them. Two things I specifically want them to have to fight over, because I think they're the real joints: (a) whether there's a principled line between "attention changes perception" and "belief changes perception," or whether that's just a distinction the encapsulator needs and can't get; and (b) the case where the relevant priors are learned slowly over days rather than cued in the moment -- does slow perceptual learning count as penetration, or is it a different beast both camps can happily accept? This is meant to be an adverserial collaboration, so nothing vacuous and nothing slanted.

Then, for each crux, a concrete case or experiment both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. One case I keep proposing to them and they keep squabling about: you spend an hour in a room where faint gray blobs are followed 80% of the time by a red flash, and by the end the blobs strike you as slightly reddish even on trials where no flash has come yet and you're quite sure the blob is gray. The penetrator says that's perception being dragged around by expectation; the encapsulator says you're just reporting a hunch in perceptual language. Use it if it helps, or swap in something better if you can think of a case they'd both have to take seriously. And if you decide part of their fight is really just verbal -- that they mean different things by "looks" or by "experience" -- say which part and why.
