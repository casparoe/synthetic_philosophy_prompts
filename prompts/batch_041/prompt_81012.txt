I'm in a seminar on the American Enlightenment and we spent two weeks on Franklin's autobiography, specifically the moral perfection project: thirteen virtues, one a week, the little book where he marked his faults in black, the rotation he ran four times a year, Order being the one that beat him, and Humility only getting added after a Quaker friend told him everyone thought he was proud. For the final I'd rather write something funny than another paper. Could you do a parody FAQ page for a virtue-tracking app built on his scheme, like the support page you'd get if the little book were a subscription service? The jokes need to come out of the actual mechanics, so someone who's read the autobiography laughs and someone who hasn't still follows: why the weekly rotation means you're always failing at twelve things at once, why your Humility score drops every time you check the scoreboard, why Order sits at zero forever and the FAQ blames your filing habits, whether a black dot breaks your streak, and whether "not perfect, but better than I was" counts as completing the course. Deadpan the whole way, no winking, and the FAQ should never once admit the product might be broken. Every joke has to trace back to something real in his scheme, dont invent features. 600 words max, short answers of a sentence or two each, American English.
