So my older cousin is a philosophy major and she showed me Gödel's proof that God exists and I actually followed most of it, which was cool, but then she said something that ruined my whole week: she said if you take the axioms seriously you can also get out of them that nothing could have been otherwise. Not just that God exists, but that every true thing is necessarily true. No contingent truths at all. So it's not just that I couldn't have skipped breakfast this morning, it's that the fact that I ate toast is a necessary fact, true in every possible world. And the annoying part is I can't find the bad step.

Here's the argument as I wrote it up. Gödel starts with a notion of a property being positive (the intuition is something like: positive = good, or perfect, or not a privation; she said the intuition is thin on purpose and that this is part of the problem).

1. For every property, exactly one of it and its negation is positive.
2. If a property is positive and it necessarily implies another property, then that other property is positive too.
3. A being is Godlike if it has every positive property.
4. Godlikeness is itself a positive property.
5. Necessary existence is a positive property.
6. Positivity is necessary: if a property is positive, then it is positive in every possible world. (This is the axiom usually called 4 in his system.)
7. So there's a possible world where a Godlike being exists (from 1-4, since a positive property can't be impossible to instantiate, otherwise its negation would be the positive one).
8. So in that world there's a being with all positive properties, which by 5 has necessary existence.
9. An essence of a thing is a property of it that necessarily entails every other property it has. Godlikeness is an essence of any Godlike being (this uses 6).
10. Necessary existence means: for any essence of a thing, there is something with that essence in every possible world.
11. So the Godlike being from step 8 exists in every possible world. Using S5, where if something is possibly necessary it's just necessary, God exists necessarily.

Fine so far, I can live with that even if I don't buy it. But then she says:

12. Take any true proposition at all, like that I ate toast. The Godlike being exists in every world, and Godlikeness is its essence, and Godlikeness entails everything that being has.
13. So whatever is true of God is true in every world.
14. And since God is the ground of everything that happens, and what God does flows from God's nature, which is the same in every world...
15. So everything that happens happens in every world. Nothing is contingent.

She says 12-15 is how you get the modal collapse, that some people noticed this problem with the proof and it's a known thing, but she also says other people claim you can tweak the proof so the collapse doesn't happen, and that either way the positivity axioms are doing all the work.

My question: which step do I reject, and what does it cost me? I'd like to keep step 11, honestly, since it's a proof and I don't have to like the conclusion. But I'd really like to keep it being possible that I had cereal instead. If I give up 6, then 9 and 11 both break and the whole proof never gets off the ground, which seems like a lot to pay. Is the flaw actually in 12-15, like does the collapse really not follow from 1-11 without some extra premise smuggled in at 14? Or is the honest answer that 1-11 really do give you the collapse and I just have to choose? Also she keeps saying the whole thing is really a proof about the word positive and not about God, and I don't totally get what she means or whether thats a cheap shot.
