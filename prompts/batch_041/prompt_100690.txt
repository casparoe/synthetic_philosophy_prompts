Write a short story that lives inside a distinction the rationalist blogs I read tend to blur: utility as whatever a set of choices can be represented as maximizing, versus utility as whatever actually matters to the person doing the choosing. The seed — keep the mechanism, re-skin the details freely — is a near-future service that infers a client's "true" utility function from her choices and then quietly reorders her life around it. The narrator signs up willingly. The trouble is that some of her choices are not merely noisy or incomplete; they flow from a factual error she would disavow the moment she saw it, so the service has to choose between honoring the choices it observed and "repairing" them toward what she'd pick if she knew better. I don't want the story to settle which reading is correct. I want it to make a reader feel the pull of both, and to notice how much of the service's authority rests on the fact that afterward she tends to endorse whatever it did. Give me at least one concrete scene of the machinery at work, and keep the decision theory in the joints of the plot rather than in anyone's mouth. Begin with the story itself, with no preface or commentary.
