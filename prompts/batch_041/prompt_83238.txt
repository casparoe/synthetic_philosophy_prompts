A newsletter to which I subscribe recently summarized the case for prioritizing suffering risks (s-risks) as follows:

'S-risks are risks that the future contains astronomical amounts of suffering. They are strictly worse than existential risks, because extinction is merely the absence of value, whereas s-risks are the presence of disvalue. Since even a tiny probability of an infinite amount of suffering dominates any finite amount of happiness, we should be willing to sacrifice any finite number of happy lives to reduce s-risk by any nonzero amount. The paradigm case is an unaligned AI that tortures digital minds for eternity. The whole agenda is a utilitarian project, so if you are not a utilitarian you have no reason to prioritize s-risk reduction.'

I have a general sense of the s-risk literature but lack the background to determine where this characterization is fair. Please audit it for accuracy: which claims would a researcher working on s-risks accept, which are common simplifications, and which would such a researcher explicitly reject? I am especially interested in whether the claim that non-utilitarians have no reason to prioritize s-risk reduction follows from anything accurate in the summary, and whether the summary's treatment of the infinite-suffering argument fairly represents how s-risk prioritization is usually defended. Separate what is accurate, what is an oversimplification or conflation, and what is simply wrong.
