I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade, and there is a pattern I have noticed and cannot name. It concerns who bears the consequences of a wrong, and it surfaces whenever I put cases side by side that ought to be alike. Some burdens move between people without friction: a third party may pay my debt, my insurer may cover the damages from my breach of contract, my employer may answer for my negligence, and in none of these cases does anyone think a category mistake has been made, even when we argue about who was really at fault. Other burdens seem immovable: nobody can serve my prison sentence for me, and if a friend volunteers to, we do not merely think it unwise, we think the offer does not even succeed, as though the thing in question were not a burden that could be handed over but a relation between me and what I did. The line, though, is not where I would have drawn it. A fine is a punishment and anyone may pay mine. A corporation can be convicted of an offence that only its employees could have committed. A surety forfeits money when the defendant flees. So the material weight of a penalty plainly travels; something else apparently does not, and the only description I have for it is being the one who did it and the one who answers for it.

I first noticed the shape of the problem while reading about the old theological claim that someone else was punished in our place and that this settles what we owed. Depending on which of the cases above I hold it up against, it looks either incoherent or obviously true, which makes me suspect I am missing the distinction rather than that the doctrine is confused. My question is whether there is an established concept here, and how exactly its edges are drawn. I know the neighbourhood — vicarious responsibility, scapegoating, the thought that a punishment is essentially a kind of message rather than a cost — and each of these seems to catch part of the pattern and miss part of it, so I cannot tell which, if any, is the real one.

I do not want a bare label; I want the carving. What precisely is the thing that fails to transfer when punishment fails to transfer: the hardship, the blame, the liability, the standing to be blamed, the record, the relationship between act and agent? Is there a general name for the distinction between burdens that survive a change of bearer and burdens that do not, and what test would let me classify a new case? A concrete instance I keep turning over is a jurisdiction considering a scheme under which a volunteer serves part of a prisoner's sentence in exchange for a payment to the volunteer's family. Is that closer to paying someone's fine, to paying someone's debt, or to something with no name yet? And where does the fine itself fall — on the side of the prison sentence, with which it shares a purpose, or on the side of the debt, with which it shares a form?
