Next month I present a work-in-progress paper to a mixed departmental seminar, and I want a Q&A prep sheet rather than a defense. My thesis is that the lottery paradox and the preface paradox are one paradox, not two: the lottery case is the preface case with the preface deleted, and both dissolve once we stop treating rational acceptance as a threshold on credence and treat it instead as a context-relative practical stance — acceptance behaves more like an indexical than like a probability, so there is no single global set of a person's accepted propositions for a closure principle to range over. One consequence I am willing to defend: the same author may rationally accept each of two claims in one chapter and reject their conjunction in the next without having changed her mind about anything. The audience is four people: a formal epistemologist who works on belief revision and holds a Lockean threshold view; someone who works on nonmonotonic and defeasible reasoning, who will want to know why I don't simply adopt a defeasible consequence relation and be finished; a Wittgensteinian who is suspicious of belief-set talk altogether; and a statistician who thinks both paradoxes are bookkeeping errors. Give me the objections I will actually get, ordered by how much damage they do, each phrased in the vocabulary of the person who will raise it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Tell me what to concede in the first five minutes, and give me the one question I am hoping nobody asks. Take a definite position on which of these objections have real force rather than laying out considerations on both sides.
