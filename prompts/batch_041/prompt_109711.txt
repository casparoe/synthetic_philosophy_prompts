I'm a backend/infra engineer — fifteen years of git, I've force-pushed over a bad commit and regretted it, I've kept a deprecated endpoint alive for two years behind a warning header because one team's integration would break, I've archived a repo and left a banner on the README. My sister-in-law is a historian and we keep argueing about statues and street names. She says taking a statue down is erasing history; I think that's confused, and I've been trying to say why in a way she'll accept. My rough position: a monument isn't a record of what people believed back then, it's an ongoing act of honoring — it keeps doing something as long as it stands in a place where people have to walk past it. Removing it stops the honoring; it doesn't stop the history, which is in the books and the archives and, honestly, in the coverage of the removal. But I don't want to pretend the thing never existed either. I like the move of putting a toppled statue in a museum, flat on its back, spray paint left on it, with a wall of text explaining what happened. Same with streets — my city renamed one and left the old name on the corner sign for a year, and that felt right to me, like a redirect. What I'm less sure about is the plaque people: bolt a contextualizing sign on the base and leave the thing standing. Half of me thinks that's a cop-out and half of me thinks it's the honest option.

What I want: restate my position as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually think in. Repos, commits and commit history, branches, tags, HEAD, revert vs reset vs force-push, deleting a file now versus deleting it in a later commit, deprecation notices, archived-but-not-deleted APIs, redirects and aliases, README banners, semantic versioning, breaking changes, "the history is still there, it's just not what's checked out anymore." Use whatever of that genuinely fits and flag it when you're reaching. Then the part I actually care about — the residue report. Which parts of my position translate cleanly, which only translate with distortion, and which have no expression at all. For that last category, say whether the problem is that version control is missing something or that my position is vaguer than it sounds. In particular: what happens to "an ongoing act of honoring" under the translation — does any of it survive, or does it collapse into a claim about what's deployed at HEAD? And does the translation vindicate my sister-in-law's erasure worry in some form, or dissolve it?

Be honest about two things rather than smoothing them over. First, whether "we should remember what happened here" has any counterpart at all in a system where the history is private to whoever holds the repo and the public only ever sees the working tree — because it seems to me that a monument is addressed to people who never opted into the repo, and I don't know if that's a real distinction or me being sentimental. Second, whether the plaque is a comment in the code, a deprecation warning, or nothing, since my instinct is that the plaque does something to the audience rather than to the object, and I have no word for that. Don't be polite about it if the mapping fails. I'd rather find out where my vocabulary runs out than get a version where everything lines up.
