Hey, so I'm in a third year ethics course and the final assignment lets us either write a normal essay or write a short story that dramatises one of the problems from the unit, plus a page of reflection on what the story was doing. I want to do the story, but I've been staring at a blank doc for a week, so I'm hoping you can write a draft I can then work over.

The unit I want to use is the hedonism one, and specifically whats been bugging me is the thing the prof called the heterogeneity problem. Quick version: hedonism says the only thing thats good is pleasure and the only thing thats bad is pain, and when you're choosing between options you're supposed to compare how much pleasure each one brings. Fine. But what is pleasure, actually? Hot bath. Getting a proof to finally click. Laughing at something dumb. The second a headache stops. Being kissed by someone you like. None of those feel like each other. There's no one sensation running through all of them, so if 'pleasure' names a feeling, I can't find it, and if it names something else -- a way of liking a thing, an attitude you take toward it -- then I don't see how you're meant to add it up and compare it across people. And hedonism really needs it to add up.

So here's what I want. A short story, maybe 1500-2000 words, that makes that problem feel like a thing that happens to people rather than a thing a character stands around explaining.

Stuff I care about:

- Not the experience machine / wireheading story. Everyone in my class is doing that and the prof has read it a hundred times.
- Ordinary-ish people, recognisable setting, one clean speculative premise doing all the work.
- Premise I'd like you to use: there's a bureau (or a clinic, or a private firm, whatever fits the world you build) that can measure pleasure and convert it between people, like an exchange rate. You hand over your pleasure in one thing and get the equivalent amount of pleasure in a different thing instead. On paper the arithmetic is clean. Then someone has to actually process a real case.
- Cases I want it to walk into: (a) a pleasure that's inseparable from something bad, so you can't tell whether trading it away is a loss or a relief; (b) a pleasure that only exists because it's yours, some taste or hobby that couldn't be handed to anyone else; (c) a pleasure so tangled up with pain that the ledger has no honest entry for it.
- Let at least two rival answers to 'what do pleasures have in common' get a fair run -- one character treating it as a feeling tone you could in principle locate, another treating it as a kind of wanting or liking directed at something -- and let both of them come up short in a way the reader can see. No lectures about it though.
- Don't resolve it. I'd rather the last scene is just the exchange rate being applied to something it obviously shouldn't be, and the reader feeling the wrongness.
- It needs to actually be a story. Dialogue, a tense or sad scene, at least one character I care about.
- One thing: don't make the bureau sinister. I think it's better if the people running it are reasonable, well-meaning and mostly right, and it still isn't enough.

Thanks -- if it helps, assume I can handle it being weird.
