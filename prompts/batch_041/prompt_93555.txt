I work on structural causal models — interventions, mechanisms, and counterfactual queries under well-defined interventions — and I have only a second-hand grasp of the theology I want to ask about. The Molinist scheme divides divine knowledge into natural knowledge of what is necessary, middle knowledge of what any free creature would freely do in whatever circumstances it were placed in, and free knowledge of what will actually happen once God decrees. Restate that scheme as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, then give me the residue report: what carries over cleanly, what carries over only under distortion, and what finds no expression at all. Pay particular attention to three points. First, can a counterfactual of freedom be represented as a counterfactual under an admissible intervention on a mechanism, given that no one, God included, is supposed to be setting the creature's choice? Second, is the middle position in the ordering a claim about explanatory priority rather than about time, and can my apparatus express that distinction at all? Third, does anything survive of the claim that these counterfactuals are true yet rest on nothing prior to God's decree? Conclude by telling me whether the residue is a defect of causal modelling — something it simply cannot say — or a sign that the Molinist claim was never fully determinate. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble, and do not restate my question.
