I'm writing the 90-minute final exam for an upper-level metaphysics seminar on modality, and I need one essay question on counterpart theory versus transworld identity that students cannot answer by reciting the standard menu of positions along with their usual objection and reply. So I plan to stipulate a position inside the question and make them evaluate it. Here is the stipulation I will hand them.

Rigid Counterpart Theory (RCT). Nothing exists at more than one world: "x is F at w" is analysed as "x's counterpart at w is F". But, unlike the version usually discussed, RCT requires the counterpart relation to be unique, one-one, and fixed independently of context, so that each individual has exactly one counterpart at each other world and essential properties come out absolute rather than relative to a relation.

Then the question itself: Is RCT a stable position, or does it collapse — either into transworld identity or into the very relativism it was meant to avoid? Students must address (i) whether the familiar "could have been" objection to counterpart theory is any less pressing for RCT, (ii) whether uniqueness and context-independence actually deliver absolute de re necessities, and (iii) what, if anything, still separates RCT from the view that individuals themselves exist at many worlds.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words that takes a definite line rather than surveying the options, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks that names the specific moves a top answer must make and the specific errors that should cost marks, with a one-line note on each common error. The model answer must be able to earn full marks without citing anything, so no references, reading lists, or bibliographies.
