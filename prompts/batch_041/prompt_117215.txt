Before I read any more accounts of indirect normativity — the view that the correct standards for what to do or believe are fixed by what some suitably idealized agent or deliberation procedure would endorse — I want the specification first. Write the desiderata any such account must satisfy: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the constraints it cannot violate, with the consideration behind each. I specifically want requirements covering (i) determinacy, that the account return verdicts on contested first-order questions instead of deferring them to the idealization; (ii) self-application, that the account's own defining conditions survive the idealization rather than being quietly exempted from it; (iii) guidance, that it say something usable to agents who are neither idealized nor able to run the procedure; (iv) non-circularity, that the choice of idealization parameters (full information, unlimited reflection, stipulated moral motivation, and the like) not presuppose the very values the account is meant to derive; and (v) authority, that it explain what binds actual agents to the verdicts of a merely hypothetical idealized counterpart. Flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are local preferences of one camp, and tell me whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — if non-circularity and determinacy pull against each other, that tension is the part I most want to see. As a stress test, take two independently plausible idealization procedures that issue incompatible verdicts on the same question, and say what an adequate account owes us in that case. I don't need the existing contenders scored against the list; the criteria themselves are the deliverable.
