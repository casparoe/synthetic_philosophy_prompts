A foundation has asked me to design a three-year program on the uses of history, aimed at institutions -- museums, universities, firms, city governments -- that are trying to decide what to do with their own pasts. The working frame is the trio of modes Nietzsche distinguishes in his essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life, and I want to fund conceptual work rather than case studies. Suppose the budget supports six researchers for three years: which open questions about how those modes operate, and about where each of them breaks down, should the program prioritize, and in what order? One trustee keeps pushing a fourth mode she calls prospective history, on which an institution reads its past to specify what it must become; I cannot tell whether that is a genuine addition, a relabelling of the monumental mode, or something the essay already covers, so treat it as one candidate for the top of the list and say whether it survives. I care less about the list than about the reasoning: which questions are upstream of others, which are tractable for people who work from texts and arguments rather than fieldwork, and what argument over the next couple of years would move an item up or down.
