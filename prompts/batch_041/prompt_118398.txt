ok so my marxism reading group wants a one-page "where i stand" from everyone next week and i want a consitency check before i hand mine in, because i have a nagging feeling i'm running two different theories at once and papering over the join.

here's the package:

1. the relations of production of a society explain its legal and political arrangements and its dominant ideas. explain, not merely shape or constrain.
2. the shape of that explaination is functional: an institution is there because of what it does for the reproduction of the existing relations of production. i'm sold on explaining things by there effects, the way you explain a trait by what it does for the organism that has it.
3. the productive forces have a tendency to develop over the long run, and when the relations of production start to hold them back, they get broken and replaced. that's the engine.
4. big transitions — the end of feudalism, whatever comes after capitalism — happen because people organise and fight. no organised agency, no transition. i really don't believe in a story where the change happens behind everyone's back.
5. it isn't all economics. religious doctrine, scientific ideas, legal doctrine have their own causal pull. the superstructure is not just a mirror.

and here's the thing that started me worrying: roughly the same level of technique in early modern agriculture in different places, and one ends up with serfdom again while another ends up with wage labour and agrarian capitalism. if the level of the forces is supposed to be doing the explaining, i don't see how it explains that.

so: which of 1–5 are actually in conflict, how badly, and what's the cheapest revision that makes them consistent — and what does that revision cost me? rank the tensions by severity instead of telling me it's all a bit loose at the edges. if something has to go, name it and defend the choice, don't hand me a menu. i also have a hunch that at least one pair here only looks contradictory, so say so if you think that's right.
