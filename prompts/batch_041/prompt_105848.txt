I am trying to get my own views on rational choice and the St. Petersburg paradox into defensible shape, and before I commit them to writing I would like an audit of the whole set. Here is where I have landed.

1. I am an expected utility maximizer. I hold that the correct theory of rational choice under risk is to maximize expected utility, using subjective probabilities and a utility function over outcomes, and I accept the standard axioms that are supposed to deliver such a representation: completeness, transitivity, and a continuity (Archimedean) condition.

2. The utility of money is diminishing for me. Each additional dollar is worth less than the one before it, so I am risk averse over monetary gambles of any appreciable size.

3. There is no principled finite ceiling on utility. Nothing is the best possible outcome, and I reject as arbitrary any proposal that we simply cap utility — for instance, by declaring that no outcome may be more than some fixed number of times better than another.

4. I regard the St. Petersburg paradox as fully dissolved rather than genuinely paradoxical. The game has infinite expected monetary value, but once monetary value is replaced by utility and marginal utility is allowed to diminish, the expected utility of playing is finite and the paradox evaporates. On my view there is nothing left over for a theory of rationality to explain.

5. Accordingly, I would pay only a modest sum — a few dollars — for a single play of the game, and I take that refusal to pay more to be exactly what expected utility maximization recommends rather than a lapse from it.

6. I have been told that for any unbounded utility function one can construct a St. Petersburg-style game whose expected utility is itself infinite, so that diminishing marginal utility does not by itself rescue the general case. I am inclined to think such constructions are further pathologies of the same harmless kind, and that a correct application of expected utility disposes of them just as it disposes of the original game.

I suspect that some of these pull against one another, but I cannot see where, and I do not know which one I would give up if pressed. Please audit the set. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to be abandoned and what that abandonment costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most for the least.
