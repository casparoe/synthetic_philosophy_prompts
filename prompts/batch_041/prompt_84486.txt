I want a single essay question worth 20 marks and answerable in fifty minutes, one that cannot be passed by reciting the standard literature on why audiences take pleasure in tragedy.

The question should turn on a distinction I drew in the last lecture, which the students will not find in their reading. I distinguished grief that has a job to do — sadness fused with not knowing and with hope, the price of caring how things turn out — from grief that is the destination, where the ending is already known, nothing is to be resolved, and the sadness is sought for its own sake, as when one rereads a final chapter only to feel it again. I claimed in the lecture that these are not two intensities of one thing but two different things, and that each of the familiar families of answer (that pain is converted into pleasure by beauty or eloquence; that the pleasure is a compensation, such as the pleasure of having felt rightly; that fictional grief is not grief at all) accounts for one of my two cases and falters on the other. The question must force candidates to test that claim against the spoiler case: knowing the ending ought to spoil the first kind of reading while leaving the second untouched, or even improving it.

Then supply a model answer of about 700 words, in continuous prose suitable for showing to students, and a marking scheme that names the moves separating a first from a 2:1, with marks attached to each.

Give it all to me as a JSON object with these fields: 'question' (string); 'model_answer' (string); 'marking_scheme' (array of objects, each with 'move', 'marks' and 'typical_failure', the last being one line on how candidates lose those marks); 'total_marks' (number); and 'a_versus_b' (one sentence stating what a first-class answer does that a 2:1 does not).
