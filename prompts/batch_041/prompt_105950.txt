Write me a parody employee handbook entry, like the "Our Decision-Making Philosophy" page of a new-hire onboarding packet, for a small company that has officially adopted superrationality as its operating policy. The founder read Hofstadter's thing about how two identical agents stuck in a Prisoner's Dilemma should both cooperate, since they're running the same reasoning and will land on the same answer, and decided this should now govern everything from expense reports to who gets the last slice of pizza. Around 600-700 words, formated as numbered policy clauses, deadpan the whole way through, no winking at the reader.

The jokes need to come from actually getting superrationality right. The whole idea leans on the agents being identical, or at least reasoning identically, so the handbook should keep bumping into the awkward cases: what happens when two employees aren't identical, or when someone could quietly make themselves the exception, and the policy either has to forbid that or pretend it can't happen. Give me at least one clause that tries to convert the "act as if everyone did the same thing" logic into a concrete rule about the communal coffee fund, and a footnote from an in-house lawyer or an actuary who is nervous about the defector case. Please don't let it slide into a generic "we're a team" memo -- the specific flavor I want is that eerie metaphysical assumption that everyone here is basically a copy of everyone else. It should be funny to someone who knows the PD / superrationality literature and still readable to someone who's never heard of it.
