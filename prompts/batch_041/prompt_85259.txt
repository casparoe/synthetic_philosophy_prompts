I serve on the research review board of a large public school district. A university genomics center has proposed a pilot program: every entering kindergartener would be offered a polygenic score for reading difficulty; children in the top decile would receive an intensive phonics intervention, while the rest would receive the standard literacy curriculum. The proposal argues that because reading ability is substantially heritable, genetic screening is a more efficient way to target scarce intervention slots than screening by socioeconomic disadvantage. The university's discovery sample is overwhelmingly of European ancestry, while our district is not. The board votes next week, and I must circulate a short written opinion beforehand. Please draft that opinion: recommend whether to approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a clear bottom line and concrete conditions if applicable. Engage the strongest point on the other side—that the program is voluntary and may reach children whom environmental screening would miss—and explain what heritability does and does not mean, including why a high heritability estimate does not show that environmental interventions are ineffective, why a low estimate would not show that genes are irrelevant, and how gene-environment correlation, the population- and environment-relative nature of heritability, and the distinction between group-level prediction and individual-level decisions bear on the proposal. Write for a lay board, not a technical review.
