Write an essay on the following controversy concerning the moral status of suffering.

A cluster of views in the ethics of welfare, often gathered under the heading of negative utilitarianism, holds that the reduction of suffering has a moral priority that is not merely a function of how the totals happen to come out. A sharper version of the view posits a lexical threshold. Below the threshold, pains and pleasures are weighed against one another in an ordinary, roughly additive way; but once a harm crosses the threshold — whether in intensity, in duration, or in the number of sufferers — it takes priority over any finite quantity of sub-threshold benefit, however large.

The proposal is usually motivated by the conviction that extreme suffering is in some sense incommensurable with ordinary pleasure, and is usually attacked by the observation that any threshold must be located on a common scale that also ranks the goods it is said to dominate. Your essay should adjudicate this tension.

To make the dispute tractable, separate two things that might be meant by the commensurability of pleasure and pain:

(i) *Scale commensurability.* There exists a single measure of welfare on which every experience, pleasant or unpleasant, has a position, so that any two experiences can be ranked as more or less good.

(ii) *Exchange commensurability.* For any two experiences, some finite quantity of the one is exactly as good, or as bad, as some finite quantity of the other; equivalently, no experience is lexically prior to any other.

Now assess the following thesis: lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism requires scale commensurability but is committed to denying exchange commensurability, and this combination is coherent — the threshold can be located on the shared scale even though, above it, no exchange rate with sub-threshold goods exists.

Your essay should do at least the following. First, explain why someone might think the thesis is true, and why someone might think it is impossible; in particular, confront the objection that an ordering with genuinely lexical priority cannot be represented on a single cardinal scale at all, so that the threshold view is either not lexical or not scalar. Second, bring at least one concrete case to bear — for example, a choice between relieving a very large number of mild headaches and preventing a single episode of agony — and say what the threshold view should say about it and whether that verdict is defensible. Third, consider whether the threshold is best understood as a feature of the experiences themselves, of the moral weights assigned to them, or of the decision procedure that uses them, and whether that choice changes the verdict on the thesis.

Do not end on a non-committal note. State whether the thesis is correct, correct only with amendment, or false, and defend the verdict against the strongest objection you have raised.
