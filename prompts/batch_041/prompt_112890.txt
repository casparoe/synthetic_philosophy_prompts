I recently had a long argument with my friend Maya about discounting the future. She has a stable set of verdicts on a series of earlier cases, and I want to predict what she will say about a new one. Her earlier verdicts are as follows.

1. Between $100 today and $120 in one year, she chooses $100 today. She says this is due to an urgent liquidity need, not because she discounts future money as such.
2. Between $100 in 10 years and $120 in 11 years, she chooses $120 in 11 years. She says the extra year makes little difference at that distance.
3. Between saving one person today and saving two people in 100 years, she chooses saving two in 100 years. She says she applies no pure time discount to lives.
4. Between a policy that saves 100 people today and a policy that saves 100 people in 100 years, where the future people's existence is uncertain, she chooses the present policy. She says this is only because the future people might never exist; if their existence were certain, she would choose the future policy.
5. Between a policy that benefits 1,000 people today and a policy that benefits 1,000 people in 100 years whose existence depends on the policy, she chooses the present policy. She says she will not trade off benefits to people who owe their existence to the choice against benefits to people who exist independently.
6. Between spending $1M to reduce a 1-in-10,000 risk of a catastrophic event in 200 years and spending $1M to save 10 lives today, she chooses the catastrophe risk reduction. She says catastrophic risks get lexical priority.

The new case is this: a choice between (A) a policy that gives a modest improvement in the quality of life to 1,000 people now, and (B) a policy that gives a somewhat larger improvement in the quality of life to 1,000 people in 100 years, where those future people will exist regardless of the choice. She has no urgent need for the present benefit.

What would Maya say about the new case? Please identify the candidate rules that fit her earlier verdicts, say what each predicts about the new case, give your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and name a further case that would tell the rules apart. Please answer in American English.
