I'm a senior at a pretty big public high school and somehow I got volun-told to give the speech at our Decision Day assembly next week. It's the thing where everyone wears their college hoodie or their gap-year flannel and the principal reads off stats about how many of us are going where. I'm not trying to be deep or anything but it's been bugging me alot, because in my stats class we did a unit on the reference class problem and now I can't unsee it. The basic idea is that if you want to say what's likely to happen to one person, you have to pick a group to compare them to, but there's no one right group. Am I "a student from our school"? "A student with a 3.7 GPA"? "A first-gen kid"? "A kid who did marching band and worked at Dairy Queen"? Each one gives a different answer, and the data doesn't tell you which one is the real one. My teacher said it's a genuine problem in philosophy of probability, not just a stats worksheet thing.

But the part I can't stop thinking about is that the assembly itself changes the reference class. If the principal gets up and says "students like you get into places like this," then "students like you" stops being a neutral group and becomes something we all start measuring ourselves against. The prediction kind of folds back into the class. I want a speech that actually uses this instead of just saying "you are more than a number." It has to land for the whole gym: the kids who are hyped, the parents who are quietly freaking out, the counselors who basically live in the Naviance data, and the teachers who just want it to be June. Can you write me the speech? Like 4 or 5 minutes. Warm, not a lecture, but not fake either. I'm definately not trying to give a TED talk or a commencement cliche. Maybe it ends on some way of picking a reference class that's honest about being a choice, and about who gets to make it.
