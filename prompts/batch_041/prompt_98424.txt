You are a scholar of comparative politics who specializes in the conceptualization and measurement of democratic erosion, and you are advising a small research center on a hard case. Think carefully before you commit: first work out, for yourself, the several different rules that could underlie the record below, and only then decide which rule the record is really tracking. Write in American English, open with a single sentence stating your bottom line, and keep the entire answer to at most 700 words. I do not need sources; I need your reasoning.

I prepare the annual designations for my center's democracy watchlist. Each year the center decides whether to designate a country as a case of democratic backsliding. Here is our record.

Hungary, from 2010 onward: designated. A governing party has repeatedly won two-thirds majorities and used them to rewrite the constitution, restructure the courts, and bring most private media under allied ownership. Elections remain competitive enough that the opposition can still win races, but the playing field is tilted. We have designated Hungary every year.

Poland, 2015 through 2023: designated. A similar legalistic capture of the constitutional tribunal, the ordinary courts, and public broadcasting. In 2023 the opposition won and took office, and in 2024 we removed Poland from the list, even though the captured tribunal and its allied judges remain in place and the incoming government has been unable to dislodge them.

Turkey: never designated. Through the 2000s and 2010s, competitive politics gave way to one-party dominance, media consolidation, and, after a failed coup attempt in 2016, mass purges. We have always coded Turkey as an authoritarian regime rather than as a case of backsliding, on the reasoning that backsliding names a decline that occurs while a country is still a democracy.

United States, roughly 2016 through 2021: never designated. We recorded a decline on several indicators and an attempt to overturn a presidential election that failed when courts and officials refused and power transferred. We coded the United States as elevated risk, not as backsliding.

The case now before us. A country of about forty million, led for the past three years by a nationalist president who has defied two constitutional-court rulings that struck down his judicial restructuring and proceeded with the restructuring anyway; replaced the entire election commission with governing-party appointees through ordinary legislation; called the judges enemies of the people at large counter-rallies; revoked, through a regulator he now controls, the license of the country's largest independent television network; and brought politically motivated charges against two opposition legislators. Elections are still held. The opposition holds about a third of the legislature and governs several large cities, and the governing party is favored to win the next election, though by a narrower margin than last time. There has been no transfer of power since the president took office, and none of these measures has been reversed. Domestic civil-society groups disagree: the larger ones call it backsliding, while a smaller group argues the term should be reserved for the slower, more legalistic erosion it was coined to describe. Our board is divided, and the divisions track the members' politics more closely than their methods.

I want four things. First, set out the competing rules our record is consistent with, precisely enough that they actually disagree, and say what each predicts about the new case. Second, give your best guess at which rule we have in fact been running, with your reasons, and say which of our past designations that rule forces us to admit was mistaken. Third, tell us what we should decide on the new case if we want to be consistent with ourselves, and name the single further case that would best distinguish the rules we are torn between. Fourth, and most important to me: since our own record can be described by more than one rule, is the choice among them an empirical matter or a value choice dressed up as measurement? Is backsliding a determinate property we are trying to track, or a label whose application is a political act? Be direct about which parts of this are judgment calls.
