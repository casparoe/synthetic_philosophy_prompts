Me and a friend have been arguing about astrology for weeks now and we keep circling the same analogy from opposite sides. He says astrology is to astronomy what alchemy is to chemistry: the embarrassing ancestor, all magic and wishful thinking, that the real science grew out of and then quietly left behind — which is why nobody has to take astrology seriously as a knowledge tradition, it had its shot and blew it. I say fine, then explain Kepler, because Kepler breaks that story. He spent basically his whole career casting horoscopes (he drew one up for Wallenstein), churned out yearly almanacs, did hundreds of natal charts, and wrote a short piece whose title is roughly about giving astrology sounder foundations — and his pitch there was that most of the traditonal apparatus (the houses, the twelve equal signs, all of it) is junk, but the aspects are real, because aspects are just geometry, and the earth has a soul with a sense of geometry that responds to those angles the way a shepherd enjoys a tune without knowing any music theory. (He also compared most of the traditional rules to the dung a hen scratches through hoping to find a grain of corn.) And the harmony he was chasing when he wrote that is the same harmony he was chasing when he found his third law. So the alchemy analogy seems to me to prove the opposite of what my friend wants: the magical part wasn't a husk he shed later, it was load-bearing. He says I'm romantisizing, that the astrology was the day job that paid for the astronomy, and that Newton's alchemy is exactly the same situation.

Can you actually adjudicate this, instead of the usual both-have-a-point? Start with the correspondence table for the alchemy/chemistry analogy — what's supposed to map onto what — and say where the mapping genuinely holds once you run Kepler through it and where it doesn't. Then the places it breaks, and which of those breaks actually matter for what each of us is using the analogy to prove versus which are just decoration. Then the verdict: does his line (superseded stage, so ignore it) survive, does mine (integral, so take it seriously) survive, and say so plainly if the answer is neither. If the analogy is doing the work rather than the evidence, what would be a better analogy for what actually happened between astrology and astronomy? And tell me what the alchemy story quietly smuggles in about how a pseudoscience is supposed to die.

Last thing, since I'm going to repeat this at parties: have I got the Kepler material wrong anywhere? Especially the bit about houses and signs being the junk and aspects being the real thing — that's from one podcast and I've never checked it.
