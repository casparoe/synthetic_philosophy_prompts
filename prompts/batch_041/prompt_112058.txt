I'm a TA for a 300-level seminar called Reasons and Motives — 14 students, 90 minutes, ordinary classroom, chairs we can move, whiteboard, no laptops. Next week is the Bernard Williams internal vs external reasons distinction and I'm sick of how it goes every time: everyone nods along when I say a reason has to be able to move the agent, and then the second I hand them a case where the agent is genuinely, coldly unmoved, the whole room quietly turns externalist and nobody can say what changed. So I want an activity where they have to live inside internalism with their hands for 45 minutes and then walk into the wall themselves, before anyone in the room hears the words internal or external.

Here's my sketch — wreck it if you have something better, but keep the core mechanic, which is that the rules *are* internalism. Everyone gets a hidden motivational set: a hand of four or five cards, each one a desire, project or commitment (you want to be someone your sister can rely on; you'd rather be disliked than pitied; you want to finish the boat). Then a citation market. Any player can cite a reason to any other player — you have a reason to give up your seat — but the citation only lands if the citing player can trace a route from the target's actual cards to the action, and the route has to survive the target's challenges: no false beliefs, no wishful thinking, no skipping steps, no just imagining harder. No new cards, and no "come on, anyone would want that". If you can't trace it, the target says I remain unmoved, the citation is void and you burn your turn. Then mid-game I want to inject the cases where the room's moral confidence is highest and the cards refuse to cooperate — someone indifferent to their own future agony, someone with no desire to be liked and no desire to be decent, someone whose every remaining desire is conditional on their still being alive, so nothing in the set gives them a reason to take the pill — and the group has to either break the mechanic or eat the conclusion.

What I need from you is the whole runnable thing: materials, how the cards get dealt, the citation and challenge rules, turn order, a timeline for the 90 minutes, a way to keep it tense without real stakes, and a debrief that walks back over specific moments — this citation was the internalism claim, this is the bit where you stretched route until it swallowed everything, this is where the externalist temptation showed up, this is the blame and irrationality question — plus what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and an honest note on the one feature of the distinction the activity can't stage and that I'll have to supply myself. Keep it tight enough that I can run it off a single page. Last line of your answer, on its own: \unstaged{the feature the activity can't stage}
