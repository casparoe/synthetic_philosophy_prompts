I'm applying for a small departmental grant — it covers a term of part-time work and ends in one long paper, maybe 10k words — and I need a two-page proposal out of a half-formed idea.

The topic is the three ways of doing history in Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation, but the bit I actually want to pin down is critical history. The standard story is that monumental, antiquarian and critical history are three seperate modes, each necessary and each with its own danger, and that critical history is the one that puts the past on trial and finds it guilty. What bothers me is where the standard of judgement comes from. The self doing the condemning is itself an outcome of the very past it's condemning — he says as much in that essay, we're the product of earlier generations and of their errors and crimes and we can't just step out of that chain. So the standard looks like it can only be one more inherited second nature, and then critical history is either circular or it's a power-play with a philosophical alibi.

My hunch is that there are two readings here and they come apart.

(a) Critical history as severing — a cut, not a verdict. It needs no standard because it isn't claiming correctness; its warrant is only that life, or this life, here and now, requires the break. On this reading the circularity worry dissolves, but critical history ends up continuous with forgetting, which feels off, since in that essay forgetting is a condition of life rather than a way of handling the past at all, and the three modes are meant to be ways of handling it.

(b) Critical history as trial — a judgement that claims to be right about its target. That does need a standard, and then the whole question is whether any standard is available that isn't itself a piece of the past under indictment.

Turn this into the proposal. I need the question stated so that it has a determinate answer; why the usual readings of the three modes leave it open; what I'd actually do, month by month (close reading plus conceptual arguement, no archival work — and please don't give me a reading list or citations, I'll add those myself); what a result would look like, i.e. what it would take to establish (a) rather than (b) or the reverse, and whether there's a reading that keeps the essay's own contrast between forgetting and critique intact; what would count as the project failing, since one honest failure mode I can see is that the text simply underdetermines it and my choice ends up resting on my prior about what critique can be; and an honest verdict on whether a term is realistic for this or whether I'm kidding myself.
