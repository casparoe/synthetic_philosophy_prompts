I'm a PhD student in political theory writing a chapter on what the classical Marxist apparatus can and cannot say about today's commercial reproductive market. I'd like you to reconstruct what Marx would have made of commercial surrogacy and paid egg donation, practices that postdate him entirely. The landscape he would be confronting: France, Germany and Italy prohibit surrogacy outright, and Italy now prosecutes its own citizens for arranging it abroad; the UK permits only unpaid, expenses-only arrangements; Spain, Czechia and Greece allow anonymous egg donation but with flat, statutorily capped payments rather than negotiated fees; the EU Charter and the Oviedo Convention both declare that the human body must not be a source of financial gain, while enforcement is left to member states. In the United States there is no federal statute at all, and several states run openly commercial markets in which agencies broker gestational carriers and donors, fees track the traits buyers want, and intended parents cross borders to buy what their own jurisdiction forbids.

What I need is not a verdict on whether he would have approved or disapproved. I want to know which parts of his analysis actually do the work here — labor versus labor power, productive versus unproductive labor, the reproduction of labor power, alienation, commodity fetishism, the family as an economic institution — and where his categories run out. Would he read the European bans as a genuine restraint on commodification, or as moralism that leaves class relations untouched? Could he treat gestation as labor in his sense at all, or was that something his framework was built to exclude? Defend the reconstruction, mark clearly where you are extrapolating past anything he wrote, and tell me which of your claims would be most likely to collapse if the textual evidence ran the other way. Add a brief note on where Engels would have answered differently.
