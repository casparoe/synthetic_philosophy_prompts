I took early retirement from the lab a couple of years back — I was a chemist, did spectroscopy for thirty-odd years — and I've been doing a part-time philosophy MA to keep the brain ticking over. This term I have to pick a topic for a long-ish essay, and I've drifted into the literature on idealised preferences: the family of views where what we ought to do is fixed by what we would want if we were smarter, better informed, more coherent, and generally more the people we wish we were. The "coherent extrapolated volition" thing is the version everyone cites, but there seem to be half a dozen cousins of it. The trouble is I can't tell which of the open questions in this area are actually worth a year of my life and which are just people admiring the machinery.

So: what are the main unresolved questions here, and which two or three would you put at the top of the pile for someone like me? I'm after the reasoning more than the list. I keep circling a few candidate puzzles and I'd like you to tell me whether they're the real ones or red herrings. First, the regress: the idealisation operator is meant to take an agent and improve it, but if the improved agent would endorse a different operator, is there any guarantee the thing has a stable resting point at all — and does the answer change when you idealise over a group rather than a single person? Second, the suspicion that demanding completeness and transitivity from the idealised agent isn't a neutral bit of book-keeping, because it may quietly rule out exactly the unresolved, incommensurable value conflict that many of us think is the real texture of valuing. Third, whose initial preferences get fed in — present people, future people, animals, minds we might build — and whether that's a seperate problem or just the same one wearing a hat. Fourth, whether extrapolation is anything other than a very elaborate way of laundering the extrapolator's own values.

For each, I want to know how upstream it is of the others (does settling it dissolve or sharpen the rest?), how tractable it is for a philosopher working alone with no lab, no funding and a creaky grasp of the formal stuff, and what would move it up or down my list over the next year or two. A defended ordering please, not a survey — and if you reckon the whole framing is a dead end and something else in this neighbourhood matters more, say so and argue it. Write it in British English; I've got used to the spelling and it keeps my supervisor happy.
