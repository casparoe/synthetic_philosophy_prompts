I'm in the second year of a PhD and have to turn a vague hunch into a one-year fellowship proposal, so what I need from you is a plan for a paper-length project (say 12 months, with 3 of those for writing). The topic is verisimilitude -- the idea that one false theory can be closer to the truth than another, and that this is part of what makes inquiry progressive even though most of our theories get rejected. Heres the hunch. Every version of the idea I've met seems to need a target: you have to know what the true theory is (or the actual world, as a point in a space of possibilities) and then measure how far a candidate falls from it. Which means the whole thing only works from the vantage point of a theory we don't have yet, and it can't tell us anything about comparing two live rivals neither of which is true. That seems backwards. What I want to try is an account of truthlikeness that only uses resources available from inside the theories being compared: define a notion of a local correction -- a step from one representation to another that fixes something the first got wrong, where you can certify that it's a fix without knowing the whole truth -- and then say A is closer to the truth than B iff B can be reached from A (or A from B, I always mix this up) by some chain of certified local corrections. Truthlikeness then comes out as a partial order generated by local repairs rather than as a global distance to a target. I half remember some old objection that you can re-describe the same theories in a different vocabulary and the rankings flip; I don't know if that kills this version too or whether the local-correction idea has resources against it, so it has to be part of the project rather than a footnote.

Turn this into the proposal: a precise statement of the question that actually has a determinate answer, why the target-based accounts leave it open (in your own words, no citations, I'll add the references myself), the method month by month, what the deliverable looks like, what would count as the project failing, and an honest section on what could make it too big for a year. Give it a title and a one-paragraph abstract. I'd rather you tell me the idea is dead on arrival than write around it. Use cases I can describe without looking anything up -- phlogiston and oxygen, caloric and kinetic heat, humoral medicine vs germ theory, that kind of thing -- and make at least one of them a case where the two theories don't even agree about what the possibilities are, since that's where I think the target-based accounts cheat. Also dont make me prove that the order is total; if it turns out comparisons are usually incomparable, that might be the result, and I'd want the proposal to say so up front.
