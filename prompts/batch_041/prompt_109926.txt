Design a decision problem that isolates what the growth-optimal (Kelly) criterion adds, if anything, to ordinary expected-wealth maximization; I want it for a graduate seminar on decision theory, so the point is to separate the criteria rather than to exhibit a famous gamble. Construct a single betting scenario, in one paragraph, with a named finite number of rounds and a small fixed menu of positive fractions of current wealth that the bettor may stake on each round, arranged so that three criteria prescribe three pairwise distinct menu items: maximizing expected final wealth, maximizing expected logarithmic final wealth, and maximizing the probability of finishing with at least as much as one began with. Constraints: state every probability and payoff numerically; use a transparent gamble (even money or equivalent); no borrowing or leverage; no unbounded, St. Petersburg-style payoffs; no appeals to infinite repetition or to what happens in the long run; and a bankroll an ordinary person could plausibly have. Then, for each criterion, name the fraction it selects and derive it in a sentence or two; identify the structural feature of the setup that produces the three-way split, and say whether that feature can be removed without collapsing the split; say what becomes of the three verdicts if each round's stake is a fixed dollar amount rather than a fraction of current wealth; and state the strongest objection an expected-wealth maximizer can raise against the growth-optimal verdict, and whether it succeeds. Each verdict must be generated by its criterion, not stipulated.
