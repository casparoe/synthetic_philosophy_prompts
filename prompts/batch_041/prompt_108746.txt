Here is a possible prompt for that dataset:

A colleague of mine who works on Quine has been giving verdicts on translation scenarios, and I want to predict what she will say about a new one. Her track record so far: (1) two field manuals for an isolated community, one translating a recurring utterance as "rabbit," the other as "undetached rabbit-part," constructed so that they predict identical dispositions to assent and dissent under every stimulation -- she says both are correct, and there is no further fact that could choose between them; (2) two manuals that are behaviorally equivalent but differ over whether a certain native expression is a name or a predicate, with compensating differences elsewhere -- same verdict, both correct, no fact of the matter; (3) two manuals that are behaviorally equivalent yet disagree about the truth value of a whole theoretical sentence of the native language -- still, she says, no fact of the matter, both are admissible; (4) two manuals that are not behaviorally equivalent, since they predict different assent at some stimulation -- she says this is not indeterminacy at all, one manual is simply wrong; (5) the homophonic translation of our own language into itself -- she insists there is indeterminacy here too, we just fail to notice it because we share the language. The new case: two manuals that are behaviorally equivalent, agree on the truth value of every whole sentence, and are related to each other by a permutation of the domain that preserves all the native's structure, so that each is a notational redescription of the other rather than a rival hypothesis about what the native means. Predict her verdict on the new case, identify the two or three rules that fit her earlier verdicts equally well, say what each rule predicts here, give your best guess with reasons, and name the further case that would most cleanly separate the rules.
