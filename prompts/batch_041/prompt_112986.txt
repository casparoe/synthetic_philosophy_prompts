Last night I lost an argument, or at least ran out of arguments, with a friend about Hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture of creativity — the thought that making variations on a theme, turning the knobs of a received conceptual space until it deforms into something new, is the crux of creativity rather than a mere preliminary to it. What we actually fought about was a distinction I keep wanting to draw: between turning the knobs of a space and rebuilding the space, that is, adding or deleting dimensions rather than sliding along them. She thinks that distinction is a difference of degree that dissolves on inspection — any apparently new dimension is a recombination of old ones, or else the space was larger than we knew — and that it therefore cannot carry any interesting claim about what machines cannot do. I think it is a difference of kind, and that it is the real content of the intuition that creativity is not interpolation.

Give me your credence in the sharp version of my claim: that no system whose variations are generated by moving within a fixed representational space, however many nested levels of knob-twiddling it has, can be strongly creative, where strong creativity requires changing the space's dimensions rather than its coordinates. I am at roughly 0.55 on this; she is at roughly 0.1. Do not give me a bare number. Decompose it into the sub-questions the number rests on, each with a rough probability and one line of justification: whether the inside/outside-the-space distinction can be drawn non-arbitrarily at all; whether human creative episodes in fact involve dimension changes or only very deep knob-twiddling, and how confident anyone could reasonably be either way, since I suspect this is an empirical question in disguise; whether creativity names a natural kind rather than an honorific for novelty we happen to value; and anything else you think belongs, including any information-theoretic way of grading novelty relative to a prior space that would turn my dichotomy into a continuum rather than a line. Then give me the combination, tell me which term you are least sure of, say which term her credence and mine most likely divide over, and name the single piece of evidence or argument that would move your number most.
