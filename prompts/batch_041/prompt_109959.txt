I am putting together a short position statement for my school's philosophy club, and before I present it I want an audit of whether my own commitments about sunk costs actually hang together. Here is the set.

First, the bygones principle: once a cost has been paid and cannot be recovered, it should carry no weight at all in what I do next. I am tempted to treat this as almost definitional. If the money or the time is genuinely gone, then raising it in a present decision can only be a way of punishing myself.

Second, I believe it can nevertheless be rational to honor a sunk cost. The clearest case is deliberate self-binding. I pay for a nonrefundable gym membership, or I promise a friend money if I quit the team, precisely because I know that future-me will want to bail, and the unrecoverable payment is the only thing that will hold me to a plan that I now, calmly, endorse.

Third, I believe the sunk cost effect is still a real and widespread error — that the feeling of having come too far to quit is usually the voice of a mistake rather than of wisdom, and that people would decide better if they could ignore the past entirely.

Fourth, I believe waste is a genuine loss and that finishing what one has started is a real virtue, not merely a strategy. Abandoning a project I have poured years into seems to damage something about my character even when the future gains are identical either way.

Fifth, I believe the past can be evidence about the future. If I keep investing in something and keep being glad that I did, that history tells me something about how much I actually value it, and I should let it inform what I do next.

I can feel that some of these pull against each other while others only appear to. What I want is an audit rather than a verdict. Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only seem to be in tension and why the appearance is misleading, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine one, say what would have to give — which commitment has to be narrowed, weakened, or abandoned — and what that costs me elsewhere. Then tell me which single revision would settle the most at once, and whether I can keep everything I actually care about if I make it.

Two notes so that you do not spend effort in the wrong place. I am not asking for citations or further reading. And I am aware that "rational" and "waste" are doing a great deal of work here; if the audit turns on how I am using those words, say so, and tell me how I ought to be using them instead.
