Background: my reading group has been chewing on the idea that talk of what people "prefer" or "want" is just shorthand for how they choose. We built an argument out of it that we all think is wrong but none of us can find the leak. I care more about keeping the first couple of premises than the last one, if that matters.

(1) To say a person prefers A to B is just to say that, offered a straight choice between A and B, they'd pick A. Nothing else is meant by "prefers."
(2) So whatever someone picks out of a set of options is, by definition, the option they prefer most in that set.
(3) So for every choice a person makes, they're choosing what they most prefer.
(4) So the claim "people choose what they most prefer" is true of every choice anyone could ever make — even a guy who picks A over B, then B over C, then C over A in the same afternoon, even someone who picks the thing that ruins his life.
(5) A claim that's consistent with every possible choice can't be tested or refuted by any choice.
(6) A "theory" with no testable content doesn't tell us anything about the world.
(7) So the whole utility-maximization story — preferences, utility, all of it — is empty. It's not an empirical theory of behavior, it's a fancy redescription of whatever people happen to do.
(8) And so whenever an economist "explains" a choice by saying the person wanted it more, they've explained nothing.

I dont think (8) can be right — it feels like it proves too much — but I can't say why, and neither can anyone else in the group.
