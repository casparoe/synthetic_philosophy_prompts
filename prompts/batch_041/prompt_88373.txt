My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the two-envelope paradox — two envelopes, one containing twice what the other does, you take one, and the familiar argument says the other envelope is worth five-quarters of what yours holds, so you should swap, and the same argument then says you should swap back. Our argument turned into an instance of the puzzle: each of us kept insisting that by the other's own reasoning, the other was the one who should have switched. I want to send him a joke as a peace offering, and I want it to be a real joke rather than a nerd in-joke — funny to someone who knows the puzzle because the punchline lands on the actual hinge of it, and instructive rather than merely bemusing to someone who has never heard of it. Write me that joke. Please make the five-quarters calculation visible in the setup, and make the punchline turn on the diagnosis I take to be correct: that the calculation quietly treats the sum in your own envelope as fixed while treating the other envelope as a fair coin between twice and half that sum, when no genuine distribution over the possible amounts can make that coin fair for every value of the sum; and that the decision changes character once you open your envelope, since what you then see — and what you know about how the amounts were chosen — is what determines whether swapping is worth it, whereas before opening there is no gain to be had either way. A joke whose punchline is that the victim swaps forever, or that expected value is a broken tool, is the joke I already know and do not want. Keep it short enough to send in a message.
