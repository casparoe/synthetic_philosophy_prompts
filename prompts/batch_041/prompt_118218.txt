I'm putting together a session for a democratic theory reading group on who should get the vote and whether voting should be compulsory, and I want a decision tree rather than a list of positions. Build it so the branch nodes come first and are just questions anyone can answer about concrete cases -- a 16-year-old, a long-term resident who isn't a citizen, a citizen who's lived abroad for a decade, someone who's plainly disengaged and doesn't want to vote, a sharp 14-year-old -- and only the leaves carry a name for the position (name the position, not the person; say "unnamed" if nobody defends it). The distinctions I care about are: whether the franchise is grounded in affected interests or in being subjected to the law, whether voting is a right, a duty, or both, whether abstention counts as a protected form of expression, and whether the demos boundary is something that could itself be decided democratically or is fixed in advance. Make sure these come out as leaves: the view that compulsion is impermissible, the civic-duty view, the affected-interests cosmopolitan who'd enfranchise resident non-citizens but not children, the all-subjected view, and the combo that drops the age to 16 but keeps voting voluntary. Note any combinations nobody occupies and say whether that's because they're incoherent or just unattractive, and flag any leaves your tree can't actually seperate.
