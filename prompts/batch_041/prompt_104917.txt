I teach a grad seminar on cosmology and philosophy, and every year the Boltzmann-brain discussion goes the same way. The cosmologists in the room say, look, in an eternally inflating or de Sitter-like universe with thermal fluctuations, the ratio of brain-like fluctuations to ordinary evolved observers is astronomically large, so if we take our cosmological models at face value we should expect to be a Boltzmann brain, and that is a reductio on the models. The philosophers say, wait, this is all downstream of a measure problem: you are comparing infinities, or comparing densities in a cutoff you chose, and nobody has a principled measure, so the conclusion is an artifact. Then someone says self-locating credence, someone says typicality is not a probability, and we end where we started. Nobody's position has moved in a decade, and I am tired of assigning the same five moves to students.

I want a diagnosis, not another survey. Is this a real disagreement about physics, a disguised disagreement about the axioms of probability and self-location, or two groups answering different questions under one name, one about ontology (how many Boltzmann brains are there) and one about epistemology (what should a situated observer expect)? My own hunch, which I would like you to test rather than assume, is that the stall comes from conflating what I have been calling the counting question with the location question. The counting question asks for a measure-weighted abundance of Boltzmann brains relative to ordinary observers in a given cosmological model; the location question asks what credence a particular observer, with her evidence, should assign to being a Boltzmann brain. Maybe there is no single quantity that answers both, and the debate keeps treating one as evidence for the other. If that is right, say what would count as progress: a formal separation of the two questions, a demonstration that one reduces to the other under some assumption, or an argument that the whole thing is ill-posed. If it is wrong, tell me what the real crux is, using what each side actually claims, and be honest about whether any new physics, theorem, or conceptual distinction could end it. I am definately not looking for a reading list or citations, just the diagnosis and what would move it.
