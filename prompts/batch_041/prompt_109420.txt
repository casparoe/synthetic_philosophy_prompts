I teach a seminar on the ethics of drug policy and there's a pattern I keep running into that I've never seen named, and it's driving me a little nuts. Wondering if anyone has the term for it.

Here's the shape. A jurisdiction softens the law on some drug — decriminalizes possession, or legalizes supply. Two or three years later everyone is waving numbers around, and Portugal gets cited at me from both directions, and the strange part is that both camps are waving the same numbers, because the numbers are all from the system that was just dismantled. Possession arrests are down by half, say. The reformers say: there, we stopped criminalizing users. The other side says: the users didn't go anywhere, they were moved — there's now a fine, a ticket, a referral to an assessment board, and because the threshold for those is lower than the threshold for arrest was, more people are inside the new machine than were ever inside the old one. And hardly anyone compares the new system's totals to the old system's totals, because they don't live in the same table. The old ledger is the scoreboard. The reform's own ledger — the one it built — never gets audited. So "it worked" means the old numbers went down, and "it failed" also means the old numbers went down, but the drug is still around.

Second case I use in class. Retail supply gets legalized. Street-level seizures and possession arrests collapse, and that gets reported as crime falling. But the thing the old numbers were a rough proxy for — how much of the stuff is actually moving, who's getting the money, what's in it — was never measured directly, and is now measured by the licensed market's own reporting, which has every incentive to look healthy. So the reform appears to have solved a problem that was only ever visible through the lens the reform took away. I've watched the same movie with school discipline (suspensions down, police referrals up) and with immigration enforcement, so I dont think it's really about drugs.

I know the neighbourhood here. Balloon effect: enforcement pushes production somewhere else. Net-widening: decriminalization pulls more people in through side doors. The iron-law thing where prohibition makes the substance stronger rather than rarer. And the old point that a measure stops measuring anything once people start managing to it. I'm not asking about those, I can recite those. I'm asking whether there's a name for the narrower thing: a reform that changes the unit of account and then gets graded in the old unit, so that the machinery the reform itself created is never the object of assessment, and one statistic can be read as vindication or as a shell game depending on which side of the aisle you're on. The part I care about is that nobody is cheating — the metric isn't being gamed, it's just no longer the thing, and everyone keeps scoring on it anyway.

What I want out of this: if there's an established term, give it, and then tell me which of the concepts above it's closest to and exactly what it adds to that one — I won't be satisfied with a name and nothing else. If there isn't one, coin one, but I want the definition and the boundary and the edge cases, not a slogan. Three edges I'd want sorted: (i) does it still count if the new ledger is genuinely better and the activity really did shrink? (ii) what if the reform creates no new machinery at all and just deletes the old — is there still a phenomenon there, or does it need the migration into something? (iii) is the essential ingredient the relocation of the activity, or the inertia of the scoreboard? Those feel separable to me and I suspect they only travel together sometimes.

I'm a philosopher by trade, so what I'm really after underneath is the general shape: what happens to a normative vocabulary when the practice that gave it its point has been replaced and the vocabulary just stays put. But start with the concrete thing.
