I am preparing a specification before committing to any particular account of time's arrow, and I want the specification itself to be the deliverable. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of the thermodynamic arrow must meet — where the theory is an account of why the world displays a systematic asymmetry in entropy, records, and agency even though the fundamental dynamics look reversible. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is a genuine demand on a theory or merely a commitment of one camp.

1. The phenomena to be explained, and I want the full spread rather than entropy alone: the gradient from low to high entropy; the asymmetry of memory, traces, and records; the fact that deliberation and intervention reach forward but not backward; the fact that we explain past states while merely predicting future ones; the radiative asymmetry between retarded and advanced solutions; and the alignment of these arrows with one another. For each, say whether it is a separate item to be explained or a downstream consequence of the others.

2. The distinctions the account must not collapse: between an asymmetry in the laws and an asymmetry in the boundary conditions; between the existence of an arrow and its direction; between fine-grained and coarse-grained entropy; between the Past Hypothesis treated as a law and as a posit; between the thermodynamic arrow and the causal or epistemic arrow; and between an arrow that is a feature of the world and one that reflects only our ignorance.

3. The results it must not contradict: the time-reversal invariance of the classical and, modulo weak-interaction effects, quantum microdynamics; the measure-preservation results that generate the reversibility and recurrence objections; and the observed low-entropy past.

4. Test cases it must adjudicate. Two in particular. First, a cosmology whose entropy is low at both temporal ends and maximal in the middle: must the theory yield one arrow, two arrows pointing away from the middle, or none, and at the maximum what becomes of records and of agents? Second, a procedure that drives a system toward lower entropy — spin echo, or a gas manipulated by a demon — where the theory must say what distinguishes this from a reversal of the arrow, and must not manage it by definitional fiat.

Then, and this is what I most want: mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a camp; identify any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied and state exactly what would have to be surrendered; and tell me which single requirement, if enforced, excludes the largest number of otherwise popular accounts. Close with two or three sentences on how the fluctuation account, the low-entropy-boundary-condition account, and the added-time-asymmetric-law account fare against the list.
