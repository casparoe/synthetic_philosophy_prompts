My friend's a clinical psychologist and we got into this long arguement the other night that I cant stop thinking about. She was telling me how once she started using a particular diagnostic label she suddenly saw it everywhere, and how her clients would, over the months, start describing themselves in almost exactly the terms of the diagnosis -- not because they'd memorized the manual, but because that's how the condition gets talked about in the support groups and the online forums they join -- and then they'd come back in and report symptoms that lined up neatly. Her take was "so it's real, but we're also kind of making it real." And I said, isn't there something wierd in there: the main evidence you have for the diagnosis is the person's own account of their experience, and that account has been shaped by the diagnosis, so the diagnosis is partly propping up its own evidence. Is there a name for that? I know Hacking wrote about looping effects and making up people and that's obviously close, but his thing reads to me as being about the people changing and the classification getting revised, whereas the bit that's bugging me is the narrower move where the *evidence* for a category gets manufactured by the category being applied in the first place. Does that have an established name, or is it just looping effects with extra steps? And of the things it's sitting next to -- self-fulfilling prophecy, reactivity, iatrogenic effects, concept creep -- which is actually nearest, and how does the one I'm describing differ from each? I don't just want a label slapped on it, I want to know what the thing really is and where its edges are, including an honest call on whether the "evidence generated by the category" part is a genuinely distinct phenomenon or me over-thinking a standard idea. And please be straight with me about what's settled here versus what people who actually study this fight about -- dont hand me one contested view dressed up as the consensus.
