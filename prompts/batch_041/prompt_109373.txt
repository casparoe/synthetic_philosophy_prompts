I have a set of verdicts about cases where I can save either a larger group or a smaller one, and I want to know what principle, if any, captures them. When the choice is between five strangers and one stranger, I save the five. When the choice is between five strangers and my own child, I save my child. When the choice is between five strangers and one stranger who is a close friend, I save the friend. When the choice is between five elderly strangers and one young stranger, I save the young stranger, even though the numbers favor the five. When the choice is between five strangers and one stranger who is a convicted murderer, I save the five. When the choice is between five dying patients and one healthy stranger whose organs could save them, I refuse to kill the healthy stranger. When the choice is between five strangers and one stranger who is a doctor who will otherwise go on to save many lives, I save the doctor. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? If not, which verdict should I give up, and what does the resulting principle say about a case I haven't listed: a choice between saving my own child and saving two strangers?
