im putting together a little reader for a seminar on Augustine on lying and i want a poem to open it with. the part i care about is the bit everyone skips: for him a lie isnt a false sentence, its a divided heart, one thing in the mind and another on the tongue. which means you can say something perfectly true and still be lying, if you believe it false and say it to deceive — and the other way round, you can say something false and not be lying at all, so long as you believe it. i want a pantoum (five quatrains is fine) in the voice of a man who did the first thing: he pointed the pursuers down the road the fugitive had really taken, all the while thinking he'd gone the other way, meaning to send them wrong. every word he said was true. he knows it and it doesnt help him. let the pantoums repeats do the work — the same line should keep coming back and read differently each time, sometimes as the true report it is, sometimes as the lie he meant it as. end on Augustines refusal to let even the kindest lie off the hook, the one you tell to save someones life, but dont let it turn into a sermon; i want it to land as a wound. someone whos read De Mendacio should recognize the distinction and someone who hasnt should still find it a good poem.
