so my reading group just wrapped a unit on the Ellsberg paradox and I want to close the semester with something people can actually feel in their chest, not another handout. What keeps tripping me up is the three-colour version — the urn with 90 balls, 30 of them red, and the other 60 split between black and yellow in some proportion nobody will tell you — because I can watch myself prefer betting on red over black (red is a counted 30 out of 90), and then a minute later prefer betting on black-or-yellow over red-or-yellow (now the 60 feels like the safe side), and I know perfectly well those two preferences can't both be right, but knowing it does nothing to make either one go away. It feels less like a mistake and more like a reflex I have about the difference between a number I've been handed and a number I have to imagine.

Could you write me a villanelle about this? I want the two refrains to be the two voices that keep arguing in my head — one that wants the urn it can count, one that keeps noticing the odds refuse to sit still no matter which colour you name — and I want the form itself to do the work: let the refrains take turns through the tercets the way the two bets take turns, then bring them down onto the same two lines at the end so it becomes obvious, without anyone having to say it, that they can't both stand. Somebody in the group who knows the paradox should be able to point at that final couplet and see the contradiction sitting right there, and somebody who's never heard of Ellsberg should still close the poem with the pull of the counted urn in their hands. Take your time with it — maybe start by jotting down, for yourself, what each refrain is actually claiming and why the two claims collide, before you commit to a single line. You're a poet who also happens to know their way around decision theory, so please don't let the verse get sloppy about the structure of the bets, and don't let the theory flatten the poem into a lecture. No meter beyond what the villanelle already demands, but keep it tight.
