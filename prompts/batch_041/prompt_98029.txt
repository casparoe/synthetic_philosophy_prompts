I have recently begun giving a substantial share of my income on longtermist grounds, and in doing so I have had to decide how much weight to give the simulation argument — which, I have come to think, damps fanaticism about the far future rather than intensifying it. The rough idea is not that the argument lowers the stakes of the far future as such, but that it relocates them: if simulated minds vastly outnumber non-simulated ones, then most of the value at stake in the far future is plausibly located in a level whose course is set by simulators rather than by us, so our branch's leverage over astronomical stakes should be discounted rather than treated as the whole of the matter. I would like a map of what counts against this. Which premises, if they fail, most reduce the damping — and what would the world have to look like for each failure? Is there a step here that is simply a fallacy, for instance treating the simulation argument's trilemma as though it were a posterior over its disjuncts? And has the position quietly insulated itself, by construing "simulation" so loosely that nothing could tell against it? Rank the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, and say which ones a sympathetic defender would concede rather than dismiss.
