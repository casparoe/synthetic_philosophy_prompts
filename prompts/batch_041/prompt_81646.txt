Ok so I'm trying to write up where I stand on Skolem's paradox for a reading group and I want a consistency check before I embarrass myself in front of people who actually know this stuff. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(i) There's a determinate universe of sets — the cumulative hierarchy — and its not just one model among many. CH has a definite truth value, whether or not we ever learn it.

(ii) I'm a first-orderist. First-order ZFC with the ordinary Tarskian semantics is the right foundation for set theory. I'm wary of second-order logic here: its standard semantics just presupposes a background notion of 'all subsets', which is circular when the whole point is to ground set theory. Same worry about plural quantification if it's meant to be doing the same work.

(iii) I think Löwenheim–Skolem has real bite: no first-order theory can pin down the intended interpretation of its own predicates. In the set-theoretic case that means ZFC has countable models and there's no first-order way to exclude them.

(iv) But I dont think 'uncountable' is a relative notion. The reals are uncountable, full stop — a fact about the sets, not about a model. I'm happy to use 'absolutely uncountable' as a substantive predicate. I think of it as the set-theoretic cousin of 'genuinely finite': a nonstandard model of arithmetic thinks some of its numbers are finite when they aren't, and in the same way a countable model thinks some of its sets are uncountable when they aren't.

(v) The ω vs P(ω) asymmetry is the crux for me. Take transitive models: ω is absolute, ω^M is just ω, no funny business. But P(ω) never is — P(ω)^M is only ever a fragment of the real P(ω). Absoluteness fails exactly where the interesting math lives.

(vi) A verdict I feel pretty good about: hand me a countable transitive model M of ZFC and ask whether M's 'reals' are uncountable. I'll say no — I can count them — and I'll also say M is right when it says its reals are uncountable. Both answers are correct; they're answers to different questions.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and whats the cheapest repair in each real case? Order them by severity, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
