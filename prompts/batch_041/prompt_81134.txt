I am working through the literature on scientific continuity — the family of views holding that something survives when one successful theory is replaced by another, and that this something explains why the successor inherits the predecessor's predictive successes. Before I commit to any of them (epistemic structural realism, ontic structural realism, the Ramsey-sentence strategy, and the entity-realist and selective-realist alternatives), I want the specification on the table: what any adequate account in this area must deliver, with each requirement tied to the case or consideration that forces it.

Work through the following, and add whatever I have missed.

Cases it must get right. The retention of the wave equation and of Fresnel's laws of reflection and refraction through the replacement of the elastic-solid aether by an electromagnetic theory of light; the fate of phlogiston, and of caloric, where Carnot's cycle survived a theory of heat that did not; the status of the classical gene once molecular genetics arrives, where it is contested whether the successor preserved the predecessor's units or replaced them; and the electron, which was both referred to as an entity and embedded in equations, so that an account licensing only structural continuity must either explain that persistence or admit that a blanket ban on entity talk is untenable.

Distinctions it must respect. Structure versus ontology; preservation of a predecessor's structure versus mere reproduction of its predictions; continuity as a historical fact versus continuity as a licence for present-day belief; and the difference between what a theory says exists and what its formalism commits it to.

Constraints it must satisfy. It must not simply dismiss the pessimistic induction as a historical generalization, nor the no-miracles intuition that motivates realism in the first place. It must pick out the preserved structure by something stronger than a cardinality condition, or the thesis risks being trivially satisfiable by any successor whatsoever. It must say whether the preserved structure is a feature of our theories or of the world, and if the latter, what it is about the world that allows successive false theories to track it. And it must apply to a live theory, not only to settled history: I want to know what to believe about a current research programme, not merely how to narrate the nineteenth century.

One constraint I have not seen stated, and want treated as a requirement in its own right: the verdict about what is being carried over must be available to a working scientist at the time, not only to a historian who already knows which successor won. An account whose continuity claims can be issued only retrospectively is, for my purposes, not an account of what is preserved but a redescription of what happened.

For each requirement, give the consideration that forces it, and mark it non-negotiable or camp-specific. Flag any pair you believe cannot be jointly satisfied — I have a suspicion about where the conflict lies, but I want yours. Then name the single requirement that does the most to narrow the field, and which of the positions above it eliminates. I will do the scoring myself; the list is the deliverable.
