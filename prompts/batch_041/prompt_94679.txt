I'm a philosopher — I work on Rawls — and my old college, a small Catholic liberal arts school, has asked me to give this year's commencement address. The tricky part: most of the graduates are devout, so are their families, but a decent chunk of the faculty are secular, and I don't want to be the guy who either tells the kids "let your faith lead you in politics" (easy, dishonest) or "leave it at the door" (also easy, and honestly worse). What I actually want to do is put Rawls's public reason to work in the speech: religious reasons are welcome in, but with the proviso that you also owe everyone else public reasons, in due course; and that proviso bites hardest in the formal public forum — judges, officials, candidates — while the background culture of civil society is looser; and under the so-called wide view a citizen can even cast a vote on religious grounds if she sincerely believes her view is also supported by public reason. Can you write the address? Around 800 words. Take the role of someone who's written a lot of these and knows the material cold — think it through first, maybe sketch the arc before drafting. The whole difficulty is that it has to land for both halves of the room at once: no lecturing the devout, no winking at the skeptics, and it can't just split the difference into mush. After the speech, give me a short JSON object with these fields: "speech" (the full text), "proviso_move" (where and how the speech states the proviso), "what_it_refuses_to_say", and "riskiest_line" (the single sentence most likely to lose one half of the room, plus which half).
