I chair the academic policy committee at a small liberal arts college, and we vote Friday on whether the new quantitative-reasoning requirement should apply to transfer students as well as first-years. Bylaws are simple: majority passes, any member can move to divide the question. Heres my problem. Basically everyone on the committee agrees the requirement should cover transfers if it's pedagogically sound and if we can staff the extra sections; two members have said flatly they'll vote no if either condition fails. But our straw votes came out strange: a majority said it's pedagogically sound, a majority said we can staff it, and a majority said we should NOT extend it to transfers. As far as I can tell every individual is being perfectly consistent. Draft the short memo I'll circulate Thursday: what motion should I put, in what order, should we be voting on the premises or on the bottom line, and what do I say to the members who insist it's illegitimate to settle the bottom line by counting votes on the premises? One of them is a game theorist who's already hinted she'll move to divide the question if the order doesn't suit her, so say something about that too. I need a recommendation I can defend to the dean, plus a fair statement of the strongest case against it.
