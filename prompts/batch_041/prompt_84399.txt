I sit on the residency board of a small country (lets call it Norland) and we decide permits case by case, and we're supposed to be consistent with ourselves. Our recent run: we admitted every nurse a rural hospital had recruited, including the two whose spouses would go straight onto housing benefit. We refused a software contractor who was going to keep billing foreign clients and pay no Norland tax. We refused a roofer, even though roofers are on our shortage list, because his wife's disability benefit made the household number negative. And we admitted a care worker, hired by a care home, who'd be below the tax threshold for a decade with two kids in the local school, on the strength of the finance ministry's model showing her net-positive over 15 years. Now the new file: a nursing assistant, on the shortage list, same profile as the care worker, except the ministry has re-run the model over a longer horizon and now gets net-negative — and she'd be moving to a district where the school is half empty and her two kids would keep it open, which the ministry's national model doesn't count. What do we do with her? Give me the rule we've actually been running, the other rules that fit our record just as well, what each predicts here, which earlier case is the closest analog, which way I should vote to stay consistent with the board, and what we'd be committing ourselves to either way. Then the one case that would settle which rule we're really running.
