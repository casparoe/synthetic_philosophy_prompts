I'm drafting a long-form feature on the units-of-selection debate, and I want to sidestep the familiar framing in which the gene's-eye view is declared the winner and the alternatives are waved away. Here is the exercise I would like you to run. Take the following candidate units — the individual organism; the trait group; the species or clade; the holobiont, meaning a host together with its microbiome; the heritable cultural variant transmitted between hosts; and the selfish genetic element, such as a meiotic-drive locus — and rank them by how much independent explanatory work each does once selection on genes, understood in terms of inclusive fitness, is already on the table. A tier list is fine if the ordering is genuinely coarse. For each item, name the one phenomenon that earns it its place, if it has one; say plainly which items you would drop as redundant with the gene's-eye view; and flag any item that is more often cited as evidence for that view than against it. I am less interested in a verdict on the debate than in knowing, for each candidate, exactly what it buys.
