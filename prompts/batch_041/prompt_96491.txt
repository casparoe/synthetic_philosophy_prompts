I've been going down a rabbit hole on indicative conditionals and there's something that's been bugging me for months now. Everyone in this area accepts the basic triviality results — the ones showing that if you take the thesis "a conditional's probability just is the conditional probability of its consequent given its antecedent" seriously, and you also assume the conditional is a genuine proposition that's true or false and gets its probability the ordinary way, then the equation can only hold in a language where conditionals are probabilistically independent of their antecedents, or where every proposition has probability 0 or 1, i.e. a language nobody could speak. The math isn't in dispute. What's in dispute is what it shows, and that dispute hasn't moved in decades. One camp says: fine, the thesis is refuted, conditionals just aren't the kind of thing whose probability is a conditional probability, so we need a different story about them. Another says: no, it only refutes a bad assumption — conditionals don't have probabilities at all, they have degrees of acceptability, and a conditional's acceptability is its conditional probability, so nothing got refuted. A third group says the triviality proofs quietly assume that conditional probability is the probability of some proposition, which is exactly the thing in question, and if you treat conditional probability as primitive there's no triviality to begin with. Same equations, same derivations, three camps, zero movement.

My suspicion is that this is at least half a verbal dispute — that "the probability of a conditional" is doing different work in different mouths and each camp is answering a question the others aren't asking, so nobody's actually disagreeing about a fact. But I don't trust that suspicion, because it might be papering over a genuinely substantive disagreement about whether conditionals have truth values at all, which seems like a real question about semantics rather than about words. So what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this a verbal dispute, a substantive one, or several different questions wearing one label — and how would I actually tell the difference here instead of just gesturing at "it's verbal"? If it's partly verbal, does that make it pointless, or is there still something real underneath? And what concretely would count as progress: a formal result, a distinction, someone agreeing to stop asking one of the questions? If the honest answer is that no progress should be expected, tell me that too, but tell me why.
