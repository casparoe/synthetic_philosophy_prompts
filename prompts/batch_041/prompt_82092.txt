I'm a PhD student in comparative politics, midway through a chapter on whether social trust is something institutions produce or something they inherit, and what that means for places trying to import the Nordic model. For a seminar I want to use Vinterberg's Jagten (The Hunt, 2012) -- Lucas, the kindergarten teacher in a small Danish town, accused by Klara, his best friend Theo's little girl, and the whole community closing in on him even after Klara takes it back and the police case collapses. I want a real reading of the film through the trust literature, not a plot summary.

Some threads I'd like you to pull on. First, the film seems to stage the inverse of the usual Nordic-model story: the national institutions actually work -- there's an investigation, a hearing, a case that falls apart -- and the local community doesn't care. Does that mean that when we say high-trust society we're talking about something different from institutional trust, something more like a local, dense-network, personal good that universalist welfare institutions can't manufacture? Second, I'm struck by how the accusation goes viral before its even a fact: everyone believes it beacuse everyone believes everyone else believes it, and the expert interview plus the parents' meeting just launder that into something official. How much of the film's cruelty is an epistemic failure -- a common-knowledge cascade -- rather than a moral one? Third, is there a reading on which high trust is really high exposure, i.e. the same dense networks that make cooperation cheap make ostracism cheap and unescapable, with the small Nordic town as the limiting case? The cultural-psych people who write about tight vs loose societies usually tie tightness to perceived threat; if that's right, was that town tight all along and just waiting for a threat to tighten around?

And then the ending -- the shot out of the sun the next fall, after everything is supposedly forgiven and the men are all back to hunting together. What is the film saying about whether a trust equilibrium can reset, and what does it imply for the export question: if trust isn't produced by institutions, what is the actual lesson for a place trying to buy the model off the shelf? Keep the film and the theory honest in both directions, and tell me what the film shows that the debate doesn't.
