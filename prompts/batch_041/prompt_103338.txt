So I'm on the committee of my university's philosophy society, and we're putting together the end-of-term party, and I've ended up in a proper argument with a mate about a game he wants us to run on the night. Here's the game. We tell everyone in the society (there's about two hundred of us) that anyone who fancies it can privately write their name on a slip of paper and drop it in a box, or else write nothing at all and drop nothing in. Then we open the box in front of everyone. If exactly one person put a slip in, that person walks away with the whole pot, which this year is a thousand pounds. If nobody puts one in, or if two or more people do, then nobody wins and the money just goes back to the society to be spent on next year's events. Nobody sees who's played before the box is opened, and you don't have to announce beforehand whether you're in or out.

My mate reckons the clever move is obviously to put a slip in. His reasoning is that if you happen to be the only one who bothers, you get a grand, and if you're not the only one then you've lost absolutely nothing — you were going to get nothing anyway. So playing is never worse than not playing, and sometimes it's much better. I said fine, but that's precisely what all two hundred of us will think, and if we all "obviously" play then nobody wins and the whole thing's a waste of an evening. He says that's just sour grapes and that "everyone else will think it too" isn't a real argument, it's just a coincidence of how this particular game happens to be set up. I half see his point, but I've also got this nagging feeling that there's something genuinely interesting going on that I can't quite put my finger on — something about the fact that we're all sitting in the same boat and all reasoning in exactly the same way.

Can you help me settle it? I want to know what I should actually do on the night, and more to the point how I should be thinking about it — is my mate right that the individual logic is the whole story, or is there a real sense in which the "we'd all reason the same way" point changes the answer? And if it does change it, does that mean I should refuse to play altogether, or play in some clever partial way, or something else entirely?
