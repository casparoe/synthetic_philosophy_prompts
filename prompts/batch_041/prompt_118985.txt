I am a third-year student in a formal epistemology seminar, and next week our class is interviewing a visiting philosopher who defends the Dutch book argument for probabilism in its strong form: the claim that incoherent credences are irrational, not merely imprudent, because they expose the agent to a sure-loss set of bets. I would like eight to ten interview questions that press the view rather than let him restate it. I want at least one question each on how the argument crosses from the prudential fact of exploitability to the verdict of irrationality, and what the reply that this is really a matter of internal consistency among one's preferences actually commits him to; on the premise that the agent will bet on either side at odds matching her credences, including the awkward case of someone whose credences are plainly incoherent but who declines every bet and therefore cannot be pumped; on whether the diachronic extension to conditionalization inherits the synchronic argument's force or merely its appearance; on whether the logical-omniscience and partition assumptions leave the argument inapplicable to any actual agent; and on whether the betting apparatus does any work that a purely accuracy-based argument could not do. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give. Close with a question asking what he would count as telling against the argument.
