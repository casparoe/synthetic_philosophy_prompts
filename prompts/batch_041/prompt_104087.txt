I dont have any philosophy training, I just read the libertarian guy Nozick a while back — minimal state, rights as side constraints, the experience machine, Wilt Chamberlain. My memory is that somewhere in that same book he also says we should be utilitarians about animals while keeping Kantian rights for people, i.e. animals count only through how much they suffer, they don't get rights of their own. I might be mangling it, but suppose that's roughly his line.

He died in 2002, so he missed a lot: actual lab-grown meat being sold, the argument gaining steam that insects, shrimp and octopuses feel pain while we farm insects by the billions, and the wild animal suffering crowd who say nature is a giant horror show and we should be out there vaccinating wild animals, tweaking predators so they don't need to kill, even gene-editing animals so they suffer less.

So what would he have made of all that? Does his own setup push him toward the wild animal thing — if animals only get counted by their suffering and not by rights, aren't we obliged to intervene massively in nature, and doesn't that clash with the whole minimal-state, leave-people-alone guy? Would lab-grown meat count as a fix, or does it dodge the point? Where's his cutoff on which animals count — bugs? And would he be into editing animals' genes, given he was fine letting parents pick genes for their kids?

Just predict, and flag which bits of his known views are doing the real work versus where you're guessing because he never said.
