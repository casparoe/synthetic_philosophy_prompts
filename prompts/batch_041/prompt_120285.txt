Hey — I'm a science-and-ideas reporter, and I'm about 6,000 words into a feature about generational labels (the whole Boomer/Millenial/Gen Z thing), and I've hit a wall that I'm starting to think is the actual story. I interviewed a demographer who basically thinks "generation" is a statistical artifact: you bucket people by birth year, draw the lines wherever the vibes tell you, and then when differences show up between buckets you can't tell whether you're looking at a generational effect, an age effect, or a period effect — he said those three are mathematically tangled together in a way that no amount of data untangles unless you just assume the answer up front. Then I talked to a sociologist who thinks that's naive in the opposite direction. Her line was that generations are real social kinds in the classic Mannheim-ish sense: a cohort becomes a generation when it lives through something formative together and starts acting like a "we," and the fact that you can't isolate it in a regression doesn't make it any less real — it just means it's the wrong kind of thing to measure that way. Both of them were completely convincing in the room and I have no idea how to adjudicate, which is why I'm asking you.

Could you compare these two ways of thinking about what a generation actually is — generation as measurement artifact vs. generation as a real kind with a shared consciousness — and tell me which one you think is doing better as a description of the world, and whether there's a way to hold both without just waffling? Two things I keep turning over that I'd love you to fold in. First, my roommate studies historical demography and she pointed out that the word does double duty: there's the genealogical sense (parents, kids, grandkids — generations as rungs on a family tree) and the cohort sense (everyone born in a given window), and those two used to line up much better than they do now that people have children at wildly different ages, so "the next generation" can mean three or four different sets of people depending on which sense you grab. Second, and maybe related — the labels seem to do something to the people wearing them. I've got a stack of quotes from 22-year-olds who talk about "my generation" like it's an organization they joined, and I don't think that's just a figure of speech; the name seems to give them something to be, and then they go be it. Is that a reason to say generations are real, since they make themselves real, or is it a reason to say they're a self-fulfilling fiction? American English please, and don't worry about handing me reading lists or citations — I just want the argument.
