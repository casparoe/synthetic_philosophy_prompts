Ive been reporting on the philosophy of pleasure for a feature, and I keep hitting the same wall. One camp says pleasure is a distinctive felt quality — there's a what-it's-like that all pleasures share. The other says no, what unites them is that the subject likes or wants them, which is why the relief when a headache finally stops, the sour satisfaction of news you'd been dreading, and the pleasure of at last getting a proof all count, even though they feel nothing alike. Then there's a third group saying there's simply no single kind here and hedonism is in trouble. Three years of workshops, nobody budges, and both sides keep re-citing the same cases (relief, sadism, sad songs) as if they'd settled it. My hunch is that this isnt really a disagreement about pleasure so much as two groups doing different jobs — one descriptive psychology, one welfare theory — with 'pleasure' being a word like 'jade', which turned out to name two minerals that don't have much in common. Is that right, or is there a hidden empirical question underneath (one reward system in the brain, or several?) that would actually decide it? And is the question maybe just ill-posed rather than hard? Give me a diagnosis with reasons and tell me what would count as progress, if anything. Also say how confident you are in the diagnosis and single out the part you're least sure about.
