I'm not academic at all — I fix bikes for a living — but I've been turning something over for months now and can't get it out of my head, so I'm going to try to describe it and hope you can tell me it's a known thing. Here's the shape of it. I share a house with five other people and we have a kitty for coffee, toilet roll, dish soap and so on; nobody checks who pays in. For about a year I paid in every month, and the reason I gave myself wasn't "I'm generous" or "I'd feel bad" — it was that the others in that kitchen are in the same spot I'm in, same cupboard, same information, so there's no decent story on which the honest answer for them comes out different from the honest answer for me. If I decide the right thing here is to chip in, then that decision has to come out the same for anyone standing where I'm standing. Fine. Then I started noticing the same move giving me answers I don't trust. I'm one voter out of millions, and if I ask what happens when everyone in my situation stays home on a wet Thursday the answer is awful, but that doesn't seem to make it wrong for me to stay home. When I tried it on the last seat on the evening train, the situation only exists because the other people didn't get there first, so there's no crowd of people "in my position" at all, there's just me, and the move seemed to evaporate. And when a housemate told me he never reasons like that and just pays in when he feels like it, I couldn't work out whether that kills the whole thing — if the people around me aren't running that kind of thinking, what's left of "everyone in my situation would do the same"?

So: is there a name for this? Not the golden rule and not do-as-you'd-be-done-by — I mean specifically the move of treating your own choice as also being, in some sense, the choice of everyone who's in your shoes, and treating that as a reason about what's right rather than a reason about what would be nice if it happened. I've got a feeling game theory or philosophy has a word and I'd like to know it, but honestly the word is the small part. What I actually want is the edges drawn: where is this move legitimate and where is it a trick I'm playing on myself, and what exactly seperates the kitty, the vote, the train seat and my housemate who doesn't think this way? Is it the same animal as the Kant business about only acting on rules you'd be happy for everyone to follow, or does that just look similar from where I'm sitting? And whatever you tell me, say how sure you are of it, and point at the one part you're least sure about — I'd rather have the wobbly bit flagged than get a smooth answer that turns out to be wrong.
