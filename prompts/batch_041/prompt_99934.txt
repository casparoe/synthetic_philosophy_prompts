Ok so I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background at all, and last week me and two coworkers burned like 90 minutes of a lunch break arguing about whether the labels in our ML classifier are "real" or just stuff we made up for the sales deck. It started as a joke and got weirdly heated. One guy kept saying the model is just discovering the categories that were always there, the other guy said every taxonomy is a business decision wearing a lab coat, and I was mostly eating a burrito.

Then I fell down a wikipedia rabbit hole and found out actual philosophers argue about this exact thing, like whether the sciences carve nature at its joints or whether the joints are something we draw for our own reasons. Apparently there's a thing where biologists can't even agree on what counts as a species, and another thing where some kinds are held together by a bunch of clustered properties instead of one shared essence, and jade turned out to be two totally different minerals that everyone had been calling one thing. Anyway now I can't stop seeing it everywhere.

What I want: write me a fake landing page for a startup, like a real SaaS product page, for a company that sells an API that claims to "carve nature at its joints" for you. The whole page should be deadpan corporate, no winking, and the funny has to come from the philosophy actually being right -- somebody who knows this stuff should laugh and not cringe, and somebody who's never thought about it should still get why it's absurd. Roughly 650-800 words.

Stuff I want on the page: a hero headline and subhead, a few feature bullets, pricing tiers, a couple of testimonials from made-up customers (bonus points if two of them quietly disagree with each other about whether the kinds are real or just useful), and an FAQ. Then a block of fine print at the bottom that takes positions without ever announcing that it's taking positions -- like a refund policy for when a joint turns out to be a projection, or a disclaimer that the essentialism module is still in beta, or legacy support for kinds that got retired by science. Don't have the page state any of the objections out loud, just let them leak through the product decisions. If you want to invent a fake competitor or a fake internal research team, go for it.

Don't cite anyone or quote anybody real, and don't assume I've read the books -- if you use a technical term, the page itself should make it obvious what it means. American English please, and keep the voice casual and a little smug like every startup page is.
