for my reading group on creativity we've been chewing on hofstadter's idea that creativity is basically twiddling knobs on an existing theme -- you take some given and vary it along a few dimensions, and the concept just is whatever stays invariant while you turn them. i want a poem that does this instead of just describing it. pick a one-line theme and write maybe five or six stanzas, each a variation on that line, and make it so a reader can tell which single knob got turned in each stanza (register, tense, scale, who's the one acting, whether it's negated, that sort of thing). the first few variations should be "slippable" -- obviously still the same theme -- and then in the last stanza turn a knob that breaks it, so we feel the concept snap and realise the edge was there the whole time. should be a real poem, not a lecture in verse, and i'd rather it leave the is-there-an-invariant-essence question open than answer it for us.
