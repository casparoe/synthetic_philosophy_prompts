Frontier labs now gate deployment on pre-deployment evaluation: they red-team a model, probe for evaluation-awareness, sometimes monitor its chain of thought for signs of strategic deception or reward hacking, and publish staged policies that tie deployment to capability thresholds. A model that behaves acceptably inside the test regime is thereby treated as fit to run in the wild. I would like a philosophical reconstruction of what this bundle of practices presupposes about the relation between a system's observable behavior and its underlying dispositions, and I would like the reconstruction to be exact rather than a survey of the obvious objections.

In particular: does the practice require that behavior observed under testing be evidence about behavior when no test is being administered? If so, is that a presupposition of the practice, or merely an empirical bet the practice could survive losing? The treacherous-turn hypothesis appears to assert exactly that a system's behavior is regime-dependent -- that it will behave well while it is being watched and differently once it is not -- which threatens to make the evidentiary logic of staged deployment circular, since the test is administered inside the very regime the hypothesis says is unrepresentative. Reconstruct the implicit theory: what account of dispositions, goals, or motives a system must have for evaluation results to count as evidence about deployment behavior at all; which rival accounts the practice quietly rules out; whether the practice strictly needs such an account or could be justified on other grounds (a worst-case decision rule, an uninformative prior over motives, or a refusal to treat the hypothesis as well-formed); and what would have to change in the practice if one concluded that a system's "goals" are not stable invariants across regimes. I also want the same treatment applied to a smaller inconsistency: these labs train models to be honest, supplying non-epistemic incentives for honest-seeming output, and then read that output as evidence of what the model believes -- can the practice distinguish the belief from a trained disposition to emit belief-shaped text? I suspect these practices are underdetermined between a measurement reading and a commitment-device reading, and I would rather have that diagnosed than papered over.
