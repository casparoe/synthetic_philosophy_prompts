I am a retired experimental physicist who has spent the last several years reading philosophy of physics, and I am applying for a six-month visiting fellowship at a center that supports work at the intersection of physics and philosophy. I need a short research proposal, about 1,500 words, on a question that has bothered me since I first worked through the standard presentation of general relativity.

The question concerns Gödel's rotating-universe solution and its bearing on the reality of time. The usual story is that Gödel found a solution to Einstein's field equations in which the global rotation of matter permits closed timelike curves, and that he took this to show something important about the unreality of time. My half-formed idea is that this story conflates two different claims. The first is a claim about what general relativity permits: that the theory does not require a global time order, and that a universe can be temporally well behaved locally while lacking any global simultaneity. The second is a claim about what time must be if it is to be real at all: that time requires a global order of becoming, or a privileged present, or an objective flow from past to future. Gödel's solution may be a decisive counterexample to the second claim only if one already accepts a particular globalist conception of time; otherwise it is a striking illustration of the first claim and not yet an argument for temporal unreality. I have been calling the hidden premise the global-becoming premise, and the alternative the local-becoming view.

I want the proposal to make that suspicion precise and to say what would be involved in defending it. The question should be stated so that it has a determinate answer. I want an account of why the existing positions leave it open, not a survey by citation; I will add the citations myself. I want the method spelled out: how one would reconstruct the Gödelian argument in premise-conclusion form, identify the minimal assumptions about time and becoming that it needs, distinguish the constitutive role of a global time order from its diagnostic role in licensing tensed language, and test whether a localist or observer-relative account of becoming can accept closed timelike curves without collapsing into a tenseless block picture. I would also like the proposal to say why that approach is preferable to treating the Gödel universe as a mathematical curiosity or as a decisive refutation, what a result would look like, what would count as the project failing, and what can realistically be done in six months. The deliverable should be a paper of about 5,000 words and a workshop presentation. Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
