Ok so I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so go easy on the jargon — but I keep hitting the same wall while I'm drafting and I want to know if there's one rule behind the way I actually judge these things, or if I'm just making it up as I go.

1. My sculptor character makes a bronze figure, then melts it back down. I'm sure the figure is gone and the bronze is still there. So they were never the same thing. But while it stood on the pedestal I'd have said there was one thing on it, not two, and I also don't believe two things can be in the exact same place at the exact same time. Something's off with me here.

2. The ship whose planks get replaced one at a time until none of the original wood is left: same ship, obviously. Now the museum takes all the old planks and reassembles them into a ship. I want to say that one is the original too, and I know I can't have both. When I'm in a museum mood I give it to the planks.

3. My grandparents' house, walls moved, kitchen gutted, extension added: still their house. Burned to the ground, then someone builds an identical house on the same lot: not their house, not even close, even though it's more similar to the original than the renovated one is.

4. Most of my cells have been replaced since I was a kid and I'm still me. My grandfather at the end, who didn't know my name anymore: still him. A perfect duplicate of me that pops into existence tomorrow with all my memories: not me, no matter how good the copy is.

5. I wrote my last book in a notebook, typed it up, revised it twice, and it came out as a book. One book. But the notebook and the printout are two seperate objects, and if the notebook had burned the book would still be there. So the book isn't any of the objects, which makes it feel like a different kind of thing than the statue, and I don't know if that's real or me being sentimental.

6. The one I keep going back and forth on: I have a character who, over four drafts, has changed name, job, hometown, and dies in draft one and doesn't die in draft four. I still think of her as the same character, which by every test above she shouldn't be — no plank of the original left, nothing continuous except my habit of calling her the same name in my head. Is she the same character, or is that just a fact about me?

My own guess at a rule: a thing stays the same thing as long as you can trace a continuous path of change back from it to the original, and it doesn't matter what it's made of. That gets the ship, the house, me, the duplicate, and maybe the character. It definately loses the statue, because the lump is continuous with the figure and I still say the figure is gone.

What I want: the principle, or two or three candidates, stated precisely enough that I can test them. For each one, tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, and don't tell me I can keep them all by adding a clause unless the clause is actually doing work. Then give me 3 or 4 new cases I haven't thought of where the candidates come apart, with your verdicts on those. And if some of my cases above aren't even the same kind of question as the others — the book and the character smell different to me — say so instead of forcing them together.
