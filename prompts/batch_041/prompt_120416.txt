Not a philosopher, just a backend dev at a health-tech company, which is probaly why I keep tripping over this. I spend all day staring at diagnostic codes and my partner's a therapist, so we end up arguing about how these categories actually get defined. There's a pattern I keep noticing and I can't find a name for it — except I half suspect it's two patterns that people lump into one.

The first one I'll state quick, because I bet it already has a name. A new diagnostic category comes out. It spreads. People who sort of match it start reading up on it, finding forums, comparing notes, and after a few years their symptoms and the way they describe themselves drift toward the textbook version. Then somebody runs a study and finds that people with the diagnosis really do present in a tight, consistent cluster, and that gets treated as proof the category is tracking something real. But it seems like the tight cluster could just be the category rubbing off on the people, in which case the "proof" is circular. I'm guessing there's a term for that. Fine.

Here's the one that actually bugs me, and it's the mirror image. The category falls out of fashion — a study fails to replicate, insurers stop paying, it becomes embarrassing to have. Diagnosis rates fall off a cliff, and people who'd have described themselves that way ten years earlier now describe themselves some other way. And everyone reads it as: well, there you go, it was never real, just a fad. But that's the same circularity pointing the other direction. If the thing faded because people stopped believing in it, then the belief was doing work — and that's just as consistent with "it was real and the belief was suppressing it" as with "it was never real." You see versions of it with diagnoses that basically evaporated over the last century, and with war trauma, where the name and the symptom picture get redrawn every generation or so. So: is there a name for the first thing and a name for the second, and are they one phenomenon or two? Is the asymmetry — same loop, opposite verdict depending which way it runs — a real distinction, or am I just describing one thing twice? And please dont just hand me labels. Tell me where the boundaries are: how does this differ from a self-fulfilling prophecy, from a placebo effect, from a moral panic? If the second one has no established name, coin one and tell me what it leaves out. Under 300 words, please — I'd rather have the carving than a tour of the literature.
