I run a five-person evaluation unit at a mid-sized state agency that funds workforce-development and reentry programs. Roughly fifty proposals come through each year, and every one of them now arrives with a claimed effect size and a timeline — a thirty percent reduction in recidivism over three years, a fifteen percent increase in job retention at twelve months — because our board asked applicants to stop being vague. The board votes on our scores, we have about twenty minutes per proposal, one page of space in the packet, no journal access, and nobody on staff with statistical training. My problem is that I no longer know what to do with the numbers once they are in front of me. I keep oscillating between two rules of thumb that I am fairly sure are each wrong on their own: trust the mechanism and the implementation plan, because every case is particular and the people running these programs know their clients better than I do; or discount the claim toward the historical average for programs in the same broad category, because every case is also a case of something. The evidence that I am getting this wrong is that our unit's forecasts have been overconfident in a very specific way — of the proposals we rated as likely to cut recidivism by more than twenty percent, nearly all underdelivered, while the programs that quietly did fine were the ones whose claimed effects were small and boring. I am not looking for an essay on the planning fallacy; I am looking for a procedure I can hand to my analysts and defend to a board.

I want the procedure grounded in the actual distinction between the inside and the outside view rather than the folk version in which the outside view just means being pessimistic, and in what epistemic modesty really requires of someone in my position. Two things about that distinction strike me as under-appreciated and I would like the procedure to survive them. First, the outside view is not a single number waiting to be looked up: it requires choosing a reference class, the choice is underdetermined by the case, and a sufficiently motivated analyst can gerrymander the class until it returns the answer they wanted — so I need the procedure to say something about how a reference class gets selected and how a selection gets challenged, rather than pretending the class is given. Second, I am much better at seeing an applicant's confidence as self-interest than at seeing my own scoring habits as anything other than judgment; whatever modesty amounts to here, it has to bite on my unit and not only on the people we are grading, and I want to know whether it ever licenses deferring to an applicant's inside view or is always a constraint pulling the other way.

Please give me the procedure itself: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure terminates in a no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question has become one of values rather than of fact. It has to fit on one page and be runnable in twenty minutes by someone who will never read the underlying literature. Then run it in full on three cases: a reentry program modeled on a strong trial conducted in another state, claiming a thirty percent reduction, with a charismatic director and a mechanism I find genuinely plausible but a rural service area and a different corrections agency; a novel peer-mentoring model with no external evidence at all, claiming an eight percent improvement, where every reference class I can construct looks either empty or gerrymandered; and one case where I expect the procedure to give an answer I will not enjoy, so choose a proposal that a reasonable evaluator would be tempted to wave through on the strength of local relationships and a sympathetic mission, and show the procedure forcing a number I would have to say out loud to a board. Finish by telling me what this procedure cannot do, including which of my twenty minutes are genuinely judgment rather than procedure, and what would have to be true of our forty past forecasts for me to have any evidence at all that the procedure is working.
