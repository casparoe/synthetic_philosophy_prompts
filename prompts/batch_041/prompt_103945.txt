Question: Explain Neurath's conception of a picture language and its relation to his physicalism, his treatment of protocol sentences, and the unity of science. Then assess whether a picture language could replace ordinary scientific prose without loss.

Student answer:

Neurath developed his picture language through the ISOTYPE system. It was designed as an international visual language for presenting social and economic statistics, using standardized icons and repeated symbols so that quantitative relations could be grasped at a glance. He thought this mattered for adult education and democratic citizenship: pictures could cross barriers of national language and class, and they made statistical claims less easy to manipulate than prose. The project belongs to his broader physicalism. Neurath wanted scientific statements to be formulated in an intersubjective, observable language rather than in introspective or metaphysical terms, and pictorial statistics were a way of making social facts public and checkable.

He also connected the picture language to protocol sentences. On his view, protocol sentences are the basic observation reports that serve as the foundation of empirical science; because they record what is directly given, they are certain and cannot be revised in light of later theory. A picture language would display these protocols in a perspicuous form, so the unity of science could be built on a secure observational basis. Since pictures are immediately understood, Neurath expected that the picture language might eventually replace verbal language in science, or at least in the parts of science that deal with observable statistical data. In this respect his project is close to Wittgenstein's picture theory of meaning, because both treat representation as a kind of depicting of possible facts. Neurath's version is different, however, because it is public and statistical rather than logical, and so it avoids the solipsism that troubles the Tractatus.
