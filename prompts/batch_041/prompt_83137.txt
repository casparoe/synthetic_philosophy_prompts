I'm a novelist and I need a joke for a scene, and I keep botching it myself, so I'm outsourcing.

Setup: my guy has just been publicly dragged — dropped by his agent, quietly uninvited from a festival, group chat went dead, the usual. At a dinner he ends up sat next to a philosopher who works on punishment, and he asks her the only question he actually cares about: was I punished? So she walks him through the checklist people use for what counts as punishment — it has to be hard treatment, imposed for an offence, aimed at the offender, and imposed by some authority that has the standing to do it — and he flunks that last one. Online, nobody has standing. So her answer is: no, you weren't punished.

Now I want the joke he tells about that dinner two weeks later. The punchline has to land on the thing I find genuinely interesting here: being told you werent punished is the bad news, not the good news, because if no authority did it then no authority can undo it either — theres no sentence to serve out, no remission, no appeal, nothing that ends. Punishment is at least bounded by whoever hands it down. This isnt. The relief and the horror are the same sentence, and he only notices halfway through saying it out loud.

Practical stuff: short, a setup and a punchline, the kind of thing a real person tells at a table where half of them know what getting deplatformed looks like and one of them did a course on punishment theory. Dont explain the theory inside the joke, he wouldnt. Dont do the so-much-for-the-tolerant-left bit or the cancelling-is-just-consequences bit, those are dead. And dont make it about politics at all really — make it turn on the structure of the concept, not on who deserves what.

If you have to choose between the laugh and getting the idea right, get the idea right. Someone who works on punishment should recognise their own field in it and wince, then laugh.
