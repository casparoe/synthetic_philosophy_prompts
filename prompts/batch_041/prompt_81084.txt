I am a high school student writing an extended essay on moral testimony, and I would rather have a defended ranking than a survey of the topic.

The thing I am trying to explain is a small asymmetry I keep running into. If a friend whose moral judgement I trust tells me that something I said was cruel, I can come to believe it, hold it as confidently as a conclusion I worked out myself, and act on it without hesitation. All the same, if someone pressed me on it I would feel that I was passing on a verdict rather than defending a judgement of my own, and I suspect most people would say the same about themselves. I want to know which account of that gap is doing the most work.

Here are five candidate explanations, in my own words:

1. The gap is epistemic: a moral belief taken on trust can be true and firmly held but lacks the warrant that comes from having weighed the considerations yourself, so it falls short of knowledge, or is knowledge in a weaker sense.
2. The gap is about agency: the belief itself may be perfectly fine, but a moral verdict I merely inherit is not attributable to me in the way that praise, blame, and personal integrity require.
3. The gap is about understanding: moral judgement consists partly in seeing why the considerations count as they do, and testimony can pass on the conclusion but not the seeing, so what I end up with is not quite the same judgement my friend has.
4. The gap is relational: moral judgement is a practice of holding one another accountable, and a verdict I cannot defend drops me out of that practice, so the deficit is social rather than epistemic or personal.
5. The gap is deflationary: nothing is missing in kind; testimony about morality is simply less reliable than testimony about chemistry, given disagreement and bias, and my unease is a statistical or pragmatic caution rather than a deep one.

Please rank these five from the explanation you find most adequate to the least adequate. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, ordered by your ranking, each item naming which of my explanations you are placing there and taking one or two sentences to justify the placement and to make clear what standard you are ranking by; where two explanations seem to be making the same point, use the justification to say which one survives. Do not include an introduction or a conclusion, and do not offer reading suggestions.
