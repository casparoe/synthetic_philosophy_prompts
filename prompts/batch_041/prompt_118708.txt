ok so im in a statutory interpretation seminar and our prof does this thing where she invents a fake judge — Judge Renata Okonjo of the (fake) Calder Court of Appeals — and hands out a packet of her opinions on a fake statute, the Calder Wetlands Protection Act of 1998. our final is a new case and we have to predict how Okonjo would vote and defend it. the problem is i think the packet is definately consistent with like three different methods and i cant tell which one she's actually running, so i want your read before i commit.

the fake statute, as far as the packet tells us: §2 is a purposes clause — "to protect the hydrological and ecological functions of the state's regulated wetlands and to prevent the cumulative loss of those functions." §4 bans discharging fill material into a regulated wetland without a permit. §5 says: "Where the discharge described in a permit application will result in the loss of more than one acre of regulated wetland, the Department shall require compensatory mitigation." §6 exempts discharges "incidental to the construction or maintenance of a public road." §9 sets a civil penalty of not less than $5,000 "for each day on which the violation continues." §10 says the Department must publish notice of a permit application "in a newspaper of general circulation in the county in which the affected wetland is located."

her track record in the packet:
- shredded tires and crushed glass used as structural fill count as "fill material" (the term is undefined, and she leaned on §2)
- a privately built logging road that the public is allowed to use is not a "public road"; the exemption reaches only roads maintained at public expense
- no good-faith defense to the §9 penalty, even for a two-person outfit; she said the text leaves the Department no discretion
- a 1.02-acre fill on an already-degraded, isolated wetland patch still triggers §5 mitigation; she refused to read in a de minimis exception because the one-acre line is the legislature's, not the court's
- Calder County's last newspaper folded in 2019, so a literal §10 would mean no permit could ever lawfully issue there; she held that notice posted on the Department's website satisfies §10
- twice she has cited a 1997 committee report as "confirming" a reading she says she got from the text anyway

the newspaper case is the only one where she didnt just follow the operative text, which is why i keep going back and forth.

the three rules i think fit all of that: (a) hard textualism — operative text controls, §2 is a preamble with no operative force, and she'll depart only where a literal reading makes the scheme impossible to run; (b) purposivism — read the Act to advance §2's protective purposes and depart from a literal reading whenever it defeats them; (c) intentionalism — reconstruct what the 1998 legislature was actually trying to fix, from the reports and the problem in front of them, and read the Act to fix that, falling back on the text when the specific intent is silent.

the new case: Halvorsen Development files six permit applications over 14 months, one per phase of a single 240-home subdivision on one 40-acre tract. each application describes a discharge of between 0.7 and 0.95 acres of regulated wetland. total, 5.1 acres. the Department treats §5 as keyed to each application, so it requires no mitigation for any of them. the Calder Wetlands Alliance sues, arguing the phases are really one project and that §2 makes cumulative loss the test.

so how does Okonjo vote, and why? i want the candidate rules laid out with what each predicts here, a best guess with reasons, and — since i suspect no single case in the packet separates all three — which further case would actually discriminate, and whether i need more than one. if one of my three rules isnt really live, say so and say what else the packet could be running. and if the packet genuinely underdetermines it, tell me what principle i'd be signing up for either way.
