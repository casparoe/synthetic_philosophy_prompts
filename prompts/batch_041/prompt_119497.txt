Fix a class of states by enumeration rather than by theory: the taste of a ripe peach, the relief when a headache lifts, the satisfaction of finally seeing why a proof works, the enjoyment of a rival's deserved embarrassment, and the pleasure of scratching an itch. Treat these as pleasures, and where you think the enumeration stacks the deck — if you suspect that one of them is not a pleasure at all, or is a marginal case in a way that matters — say so and say what turns on it.

The heterogeneity problem is the difficulty of saying what, if anything, these states share that makes them pleasures, rather than a merely nominal collection assembled by a word. I want your credence in the following proposition, stated as precisely as I can state it: there is a single property — phenomenal, attitudinal, evaluative, or functional — common to every member of that class, which grounds its status as a pleasure, where "single" excludes the bare disjunction that some are like this, others like that, and there is nothing further to say.

Decompose the number rather than reporting it. Give me a probability for each of the following, then show how they combine:

1. the class is unified by a uniform phenomenal quality — a distinctive feeling tone, whatever its variation in intensity or flavor;
2. it is unified attitudinally, so that every pleasure is a state the subject stands in one and the same relation to (liking, endorsement, being moved toward), with the felt character varying freely;
3. it is unified by content, so that every pleasure in some way presents something as good or as to-be-pursued, making the commonality representational rather than sensory;
4. it is unified only functionally or etiologically — a shared causal role, such as reward learning or the promotion of repetition — with no shared felt character;
5. there is no unity, and "pleasure" names a family or a pragmatic grouping.

Tell me explicitly how you are counting case 4, since whether a shared causal role satisfies the proposition is itself a substantive choice about what the proposition asks. Separately from the five, give your probability that the dispute is verbal — that the rival answers are notational variants disagreeing about no fact.

Then report the aggregate credence, name the single sub-question your number is most sensitive to, and say what argument or empirical finding would move it most. I would also like your view on whether this question is decidable a priori or is hostage to affective science, and whether your answer would shift if the enumeration were extended to include masochistic pleasure and the pleasure taken in cruelty. I put the proposition near 0.3 myself, mostly because I cannot see how any felt-quality answer survives the marginal cases; if you disagree, tell me which of my terms you would move.
