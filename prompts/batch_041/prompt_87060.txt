Okay so I'm not a philosopher, I just read alot about animal minds — fish, bees, octopuses, the whole thing — and there's a move I keep running into that bugs me, and I want to know if it has a name.

Here's the move. To argue that an animal *does* feel pain, people point at something it has or does: the right kind of nerve cells, receptors that painkillers act on, limping on the bad leg, giving up a nice dark hiding spot for a chance at food. To argue it *doesn't*, they point at something it lacks: no cortex, not enough neurons. Fine so far. But the same feature gets used both ways, and it's treated as decisive both ways. Pain-looking behavior counts as proof when the animal is a fish; when a plant pumps out a chemical alarm while something is eating it, or a machine backs off when its sensor says too hot, the same kind of behavior gets waved away as "just a mechanism." And a cortex is treated as *needed* for pain right up until someone mentions birds, at which point the cortex stops being needed and we quietly reach for a different feature that gives the same verdict.

But that feature can't be doing both jobs. If it's a sign that pain is there, then lacking it shouldn't settle anything. If it's required, then having it shouldn't be enough. And when the contradiction gets pointed out, it never gets dropped — it gets swapped for a slightly different feature that keeps the original verdict.

We already know this in humans, right? A hand pulls back from a hot pan under anesthesia, and nobody says the patient felt it. So we know the reflex isn't the thing. Yet it's the thing again the second we're talking about a trout.

Is there a name for this? I definately don't want a bare label, I want to know what the thing actually is. Is it just moving the goalposts, or something more specific, since here a single feature is being used as a test for presence *and* for absence at once? Is it the old problem of other minds, or is that a different problem, since that one seems to be about *any* other mind while this one is about *which* minds we're willing to grant? And is there a principled way to pick a test that isn't just smuggling in the verdict you started with? If nothing established fits, coin something and tell me where the edges are — what's in, what's out, and how it differs from plain circular reasoning.
