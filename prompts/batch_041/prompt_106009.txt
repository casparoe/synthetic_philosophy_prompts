My brother Ray is retiring after 25 years running a pest-control company, and I'm giving the toast at his party this weekend. Problem is, a few months ago I fell down a rabbit hole about insect sentience and now I cant stop doing the math. The gist, as I understand it: nobody actually knows whether insects feel pain, but there's a real chance some of them do, and even a tiny per-insect probability gets multiplied by numbers so huge they stop meaning anything — insects alive at any given moment, insects killed by humans every year — so the expected amount of suffering could come out enormous even if the odds for any single bug are minuscule. I can't shake it. And now I have to stand up and be warm and funny about my brother, a genuinely good man who has spent his whole working life killing bugs, in front of his crew (lovely, no-nonsense guys who will absolutely not want a lecture), our mom, and my aunt. Write the toast. It has to land as affection, not as an accusation or a seminar, and it can't say anything I'd now think was false — but it also can't turn his dinner into a philosophy lecture. Keep it under 350 words.
