I am a PhD student in media and communication studies, with secondary training in network science; I am fluent enough in the latter to reason directly in terms of nodes and edges, degree distributions, information cascades, homophily, broadcast versus peer-to-peer topologies, and the distinction between an aggregated audience and a constituted public. I would like you to restate Kierkegaard's diagnosis of the press, the public, and leveling in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can and concept by concept: the public as a phantom or abstraction that is nobody and yet everyone; leveling as the reduction of everything to an abstract equality; chatter as talk without substance or commitment; and his insistence that the crowd, as mere numerical aggregation, is where truth and responsibility are lost. Then give me the residue report. Which of his claims translate cleanly into network and platform terms; which survive only with distortion, and what exactly has to be fudged to make them fit; and which have no expression in that vocabulary at all. For each piece of residue, say whether it marks a genuine limitation of the network vocabulary — something about normativity, agency, or the single individual that descriptive terms cannot carry — or whether it is instead a sign that the original claim was less determinate than it sounds. Keep the translation honest: do not quietly substitute the target framework's assumptions for his, and treat the residue as the interesting part.
