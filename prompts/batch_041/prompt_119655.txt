I'm a teaching assistant grading short-answer exam responses for an undergraduate ethics course. The question was: "Explain the distinction between killing and letting die, and evaluate whether that distinction is morally significant. Support your answer with at least one concrete case or argument." One student's answer reads:

"The distinction between killing and letting die turns on whether an agent does something that brings about a death or merely allows a death to occur by not intervening. Rachels's famous pair of cases makes the contrast vivid: Smith drowns his young cousin in the bath in order to secure an inheritance, while Jones, with the same motive, stands by and watches as the child slips under and drowns. Most of us judge the two men equally blameworthy. Since the only difference between them is the bare difference between an act and an omission, and since that difference does not change our moral verdict, the distinction between killing and letting die cannot itself be doing any moral work. Rachels therefore concludes that the distinction should be abandoned.

The distinction is usually thought to matter because it tracks the difference between negative and positive duties: we have a strict duty not to harm others, but our duty to help is weaker and more limited, so killing is worse than letting die. The distinction also lines up with the difference between intending a death and merely foreseeing it. When a doctor gives a lethal injection, the death is intended; when a doctor merely withholds a treatment, the death is foreseen but not intended. So the real moral line here is between intending harm and foreseeing it, not between acting and omitting.

Cases like withdrawing a ventilator make the classification harder, since switching the machine off looks like an act. I take it the standard view is nonetheless that withdrawing life support is letting die, because it is the underlying disease, not the doctor, that does the killing. All things considered, the distinction matters less than people suppose, and Rachels is right that killing and letting die are morally equivalent."
