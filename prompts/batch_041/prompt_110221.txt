I'd like you to argue for a position in the philosophy of causation — one I hold and would like to see defended properly, since I've never managed it myself.

My position: the long-running contrast between counterfactual theories of causation and interventionist theories (Pearl-style structural models and do-operators, Woodward-style invariance under intervention) is not a standoff between two rival analyses of one thing. It is a disagreement between a metaphysics and a methodology, and most of the apparent conflict dissolves once you ask which question each tradition is answering. The counterfactual tradition asks what makes a causal claim true. The interventionist tradition asks what makes causal claims usable — what lets them support explanation, prediction, and control. On that reading, the standard circularity objection (an intervention is itself specified in causal terms, so the account can't be a reductive analysis of causation) is not an embarrassment to be patched with ever more careful definitions; it is the tell that no reduction was ever on offer, and that the interventionist's "intervention" is a piece of idealization rather than a primitive in the world's furniture.

Defend that, but do it the hard way, by running it against a case I find awkward for both sides. Consider a gas's temperature and the mean translational kinetic energy of its molecules. These stand in a constitutive, not a causal, relation: the counterfactual analysis will report mutual dependence and so, on a naive reading, mutual causation, while the interventionist will say no intervention on either is possible because there aren't two separate variables here to intervene between. I want you to distinguish cases where intervention is impossible in principle because the relation is constitutive from cases where it is merely nomically locked, say whether interventionism can be stated so that the distinction does the work it needs to, and say what the counterfactual theorist ought to concede.

I'd also like one prediction of my view tested rather than restated: the metaphysics/method split implies that interventionism earns its keep on type-level causal claims and struggles with token and actual causation, while the counterfactual tradition has the opposite profile. If that's wrong, I'd rather find out from you than from a referee.

Keep the metaphysics and the method distinct throughout; don't let "counterfactual" and "interventionist" blur into one generic "manipulationist" view. Assume I know the standard literature well enough not to need it cited — no bibliographies, no references, and please don't put any passage in quotation marks, since I have no way to check them. What I do want is a clean separation between the parts of your verdict that are settled among people who actually work on this and the parts that are genuinely contested, with the contested parts flagged as such rather than smoothed into a consensus. Roughly 1,200 words.
