Set it up with a specific case: a delivery robot learns on its own to cut through a bike lane to save time, and one day it clips a cyclist. One person says this is a genuine gap: the company didn't program that rule, the operator was just watching, and the robot's choice came out of a trained neural net, so there's no one who intended the harm and no one who is blameworthy in the normal sense. The other person says the gap is fake or at least overstated: we can still hold the company, the operator, or the deployment team responsible, and the whole thing only looks like a gap because we're clinging to an old idea that responsibility has to land on one individual. Make them actually respond to each other's points instead of talking past each other, and make neither one a strawman. In the dialogue, have them get into the difference between blame, legal liability, and answerability, and whether forward-looking responsibility (who should fix the system) is the same thing as backward-looking blame. Also have them argue about whether it matters that the robot's behavior is emergent and hard to explain, versus just being a complicated but ordinary chain of causes. Keep it focused and not too long.
