I've been in a consciousness reading group for about a year and a half and we're completely stuck on basically one question: whether the global workspace framework is even in the business of explaining phenomenal consciousness, or only access to it. The setup is roughly this. One camp says the workspace theory is a theory of access -- global broadcast, reportability, whatever feeds working memory and report -- and that the "phenomenal" part is either the same thing under a different description or something no functional/cognitive theory was ever meant to touch, so the framework was never claiming to solve that. The other camp says that's a dodge: if it only explains access, it leaves the interesting question exactly where it found it, and quietly calling access "consciousness" changes the subject without saying so. Then someone always brings up the cases that are suposedly meant to drive a wedge between the two -- partial-report / iconic memory results that supposedly show more is experienced than can be reported, blindsight, inattentional blindness, no-report paradigms -- and we burn the rest of the session arguing about whether any of those actually dissociate access from experience or whether they're just weak or degraded access, or reportability failing for uninteresting reasons.

Nobody moves, ever. My suspicion, which I'd like you to test rather than just nod at, is that this isn't one disagreement but several stacked on top of each other: a verbal dispute about what "consciousness" is being used to mean, a real empirical dispute about whether access and phenomenality ever come apart, and something underneath that neither side can argue for -- a starting intuition about whether a functional account could ever be an account of experience at all. Is that right? Or is one of those layers doing all the work? Or is it a genuinely substantive scientific disagreement that we're all just bad at resolving?

I want a diagnosis, not a survey and not a verdict on who's right about consciousness. Tell me what kind of disagreement each layer is, which parts are actually empirical and which are conceptual or verbal, whether the overflow-style cases could in principle settle anything or are question-begging, and whether the pre-registered adversarial-collaboration attempts -- getting rival theories to bet on different neural signatures -- could ever break the tie or just relocate the fight. And given your diagnosis, say what would actually count as progress here: a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, or admitting the question is ill-posed. Be concrete about what each side would have to concede for this to move at all.
