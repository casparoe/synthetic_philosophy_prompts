For my school's ethics bowl team I'm putting together a one-page decision tree on the trolley problem and the doctrine of double effect, and I'd like you to write it. Everyone starts from the same commitment — that the bystander may divert the trolley onto the side track even though the person working there dies — and every branch node should be a question a teammate can answer without already knowing which position it leads to. I'd like the nodes to turn on the distinctions that actually do the work here: whether the death is intended or only foreseen, whether it is a means to saving the five or a side effect of doing so, whether what makes the case wrong, when it is wrong, is that the victim is used, whether numbers can ever license harming someone, and whether the real line is between doing and allowing rather than between intending and foreseeing. The leaves should be named positions — strict double effect, a threshold version of it, a view that keeps only the doing/allowing distinction, a view on which diverting is permissible only because there is no other route to the five, and at least one leaf that the tree's own logic forces but that nobody defends under a name. Besides the ordinary bystander, the tree has to handle pushing a large person off a footbridge to stop the trolley, and the variant where the side track loops back to the five, so that the one person's body stops the trolley and the five survive only because he was standing there. Keep it to roughly eight to ten leaves so it fits on a single page; flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate, and for each combination of answers that nobody occupies, add one sentence saying whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
