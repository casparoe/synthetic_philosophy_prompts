So I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I'm in this seminar on consciousness where we spent two whole weeks on Dennett's Consciousness Explained and the Orwellian-vs-Stalinesque thing. You know the drill: flash a blue dot up top, then a red dot down low, and at the right timing people swear they saw one dot travel across and switch color halfway, even though the switch "happened" before the second stimulus was even on screen. Dennett's move is that the Orwellian reading (the experience happens, then memory gets rewritten afterward) and the Stalinesque reading (the brain edits the experience before you ever really see it) aren't two rival hypotheses about some hidden inner event; they're just two ways of describing the same finished product, so there's no fact of the matter about when you experienced the color change. I find this deflating and I half suspect it's too quick.

Here's the thing I keep chewing on, and I want you to take a side for me. I've been trying to cook up a third option that isn't just a relabeling of those two. Call it the placeholder view. On this picture, what gets fixed early is only the schema of the experience, a slot marked something like motion, color-to-be-determined, and the slot's actual content gets bound in later, after the second dot. The idea is that the schema/content split gives you something to point at that's neither editing the show before broadcast nor doctoring the recording afterward, because at the early time there's no complete show to edit and no complete recording to doctor. I think this reopens a question Dennett declared closed, but I honestly can't tell if I'm just dressing up Stalinesque in new clothes.

So please argue for the position that the placeholder view really is a genuine third option and that it lets us say there is a fact of the matter about the timing of the color-change experience, i.e. push back on Dennett. Be specific about what a fact of the matter would even mean here, and pin down where the placeholder view has to make commitments that could in principle come apart from both the Orwellian and the Stalinesque stories. If at some point it collapses into one of them, say so and say exactly where. Also flag any spot where I'm seperating two things that aren't really seperate. American English please, and don't worry about citations or reading lists, just make the argument.
