My working vocabulary is the vocabulary of reinforcement learning and representation learning, and I would like a faithful rendering of teleosemantics in it: objectives and loss functions, policies and value heads, observations versus latent state, shared trunks and the heads that read them, probe decodability, distributional shift, reward hacking, and the working assumption that any goal worth having can be written as the expectation of a cumulative reward. I know the philosophical position well enough not to need it explained; what I want is to see it stated in the machinery I actually use, and then to be told what the machinery cannot hold.

Treat the following as the commitments to be translated, one at a time. Content is fixed by selection history rather than by current dispositions, actual use, or what the system's designer intended. A state can be about something only if it has a function, so misrepresentation is a species of malfunction rather than a species of error. Where a state correlates with many things at once — the small dark moving speck, the fly, the frog's own retinal activity — something in the organization of the system must break the tie, and it is the downstream consumer, not the correlation, that does the breaking. A state that nothing downstream reads represents nothing. There is a difference in kind between a state that says how things are and a state that says what to do. And a physical duplicate assembled without any selection history behind it represents nothing at all, however well it behaves.

Render each of these in my terms: gradient descent on a stipulated objective as a selection process, and evolutionary or meta-level search as the outer loop; features in a shared trunk and the heads that consume them; world models versus value functions; what a probe can decode from a representation versus what the representation is for; the difference between a model that is wrong and a model that is merely unread. Be honest about the direction of translation. Where my vocabulary merely relabels a claim without adding anything, say so, and do not let mutual information stand in for content or a loss curve stand in for a norm.

Then the part I actually want. For each commitment, tell me whether it carries over intact, carries over only as a stipulation, or does not carry over at all. I expect at least three candidates for the last category, and I want your verdict on each: the source of the norm, since my objectives are authored by someone while the theory's functions are supposed to be authored by nothing; the fact of the matter about what a state is about, since probing seems to make that relative to a task someone chose; and whatever work selection was doing that training cannot do, given that training is always training for something. Where the loss seems to indict my framework — that it has no room for a norm no one wrote down, and so no room for content — say so plainly. Where the loss instead indicts the theory — that it was never doing more than redescribing use, and the biological packaging made it look deeper than it was — say that too, and say which of the two you think it is. Finally, if the translation suggests that the theory's real difficulty was never selection but the origin of the objective, describe what an RL-shaped version of the theory would have to look like in order to be true.
