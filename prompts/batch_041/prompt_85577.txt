I'm an undergraduate in a decision theory seminar, and our term assignment is a little unusual: instead of a finished paper, we each submit a short research proposal of the kind you'd send to a fellowship committee, on any open question in the theory of rational choice under risk. I've been going back and forth with a classmate about risk-weighted expected utility — the view that keeps the expected-utility framework but replaces the linear weighting of outcomes with a risk function, so that an agent's attitude toward risk is located in how she weights the cumulative chance of landing at or above each rank, with the possible outcomes ordered from worst to best. My classmate thinks the ordering step is a harmless technical convenience. I think it's doing real normative work, and that once you notice it you have to ask what the theory is even supposed to say about an agent whose values don't line up on a single scale — someone weighing a small certain loss of income against a moderate chance of chronic pain and a small chance of losing a friendship, where there is no fact of the matter about which outcome is worse than which. Risk-weighted expected utility appears to have nothing to say to such an agent, and I suspect that this is not a gap that can be patched without surrendering something the theory needs.

Turn this into a one-page proposal (hard cap: 650 words) with a question stated precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, an account of why the existing defenses of the theory leave that question open, a method I could actually execute in a single semester — I'm imagining something like constructing small finite cases with partially ordered outcomes, formulating candidate extensions of the risk function to those cases, and testing each against the axioms the theory is supposed to preserve — and a clear statement of what would count as the project failing, including the real possibility that the whole issue collapses into the familiar problem of incomplete preferences in expected utility and I end up with nothing new. Say honestly how much of this is doable in ten weeks, where I would have to cut, and why the question is worth anyone's time even if the answer comes back negative. I'll add the citations myself, so please don't include any references or suggested reading.
