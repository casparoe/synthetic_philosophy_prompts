ok so me and my friend Maya were doing a unit on consciousness in psych and she brought up this condition, pain asymbolia, where people (i think its damage to the insula, or anyway something got disconnected from the part that makes you care) can still feel pain — they can point at exactly where it hurts and tell you how intense it is — but it doesn't bother them at all. They'll say yeah that hurts and then just not care, and apparently some of them won't even pull their hand off a hot plate, which is why they keep getting hurt. Maya thinks this is a huge deal and honestly it kind of broke my brain, so we built this argument and now I'm pretty sure it proves way too much and I can't find the loose thread.

(1) Something is bad for you only if there's some reason for you to dislike it or avoid it.
(2) Asymbolics feel the pain (they localize it, they rate how intense it is) but they don't mind it and they don't flinch away.
(3) So the sensation part and the minding part can come apart, at least in principle.
(4) So what makes pain bad for you can't be the sensation. It has to be the minding — the unpleasantness.
(5) So the badness of a pain is exactly however much you mind it, and nothing about the sensation itself (how sharp it is, how long it lasts, where it is) is bad in its own right.
(6) So if you didn't mind it at all, being burned alive wouldn't be bad for you, not even a little. And if you minded enormously with no pain at all, that would be just as bad as being tortured.
(7) So pain is never bad in itself. The bad thing is minding, which just happens to usually show up attached to pain.
(8) So a hospital could ditch the morphine and just give you something that makes you not care, and that would fully fix the problem — the pain is still there but there's nothing bad left over.

I really want to keep (2) and (3), the asymbolia thing seems like solid evidence and I don't think I can wriggle out of it. I obviously can't keep (8). But (8) feels like it just falls out of the rest. Maya says the problem is (1), that badness doesn't have to be something you mind or avoid, but (1) is basically the only reason I believed (4) in the first place, so I feel like I'm going in circles.
