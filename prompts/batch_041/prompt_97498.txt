Write an essay on whether qualia are an incoherent posit. Begin from a familiar thought experiment: two coffee tasters both used to enjoy a particular coffee and now both find it repulsive; one insists that the coffee's taste has changed, while the other insists that the coffee is unchanged and that his own palate has changed. Dennett and others use cases of this shape to argue that the notion of qualia — intrinsic, private, ineffable properties of experience — does no explanatory work and should be denied existence outright. Your essay should weigh the strongest form of that case against the strongest reply available to a qualia realist.

To keep the discussion from recycling well-worn slogans, address the following specific pressure point. Suppose a neuroscientist proposes a case of "silent inversion": two subjects are stipulated to be identical in every capacity that can be observed or measured — every report, every discrimination, every association, every downstream effect on memory, inference, and action — yet the neuroscientist claims that what it is like for one of them to see red is what it is like for the other to see green. Consider three verdicts on this case: (a) the stipulation is coherent and describes a genuine possibility, so qualia are real but in principle beyond any functional or behavioral test; (b) the stipulation is incoherent — once you subtract every difference that makes a difference, there is nothing left for "qualia" to name, so qualia are an incoherent posit; (c) the case is coherent but shows only that the word "qualia" is doing two different jobs, and the dispute dissolves once those jobs are separated. Defend one of these, or a clearly stated fourth position, and say what your choice costs you.

Your essay should distinguish qualia understood as intrinsic, non-relational properties from the looser "what it's like" locution, should engage at least one serious argument on the side you reject, and should reach a definite verdict rather than suspending judgment.
