I am a novelist under contract, revising a draft whose unity depends on a handful of recurring motifs, and I need a working procedure rather than encouragement. Hofstadter's account of creativity as variation on a theme, with its image of knob-twiddling, has been more useful to me than any craft manual, but I cannot operationalize it when I am tired and behind. Please turn it into a procedure I can run on a scene, a chapter, or the whole book: the steps in order; how to identify a motif's invariant core, its surface knobs, and its relational knobs, where relational knobs govern how one variation constrains another; how to vary knobs one at a time or in licensed gangs rather than by random cleverness; the tests that tell me a variation has crossed from the same theme into a different one; and the points where the procedure must stop and hand the decision back to me because the question is about meaning and not structure. Treat each motif as a point in a variation lattice, with single-knob turns as edges and ganged turns as moves across several edges at once, and say when a ganged turn is warranted. Run the procedure on three cases from a hypothetical novel: a repeated image, a character's signature gesture, and the book's ending. Include one case where the procedure tells me to cut or radically alter something I am attached to, and close by saying what the procedure cannot do. I revise daily, have two weeks before the manuscript is due, and cannot add new research. Lead with the procedure in one sentence, then give the steps. Keep the whole answer to one page, no more than 900 words.
