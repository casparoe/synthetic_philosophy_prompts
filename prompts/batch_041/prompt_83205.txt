I'm trying to put together a term-paper plan for a grad seminar (one semester, maybe 6k words) and I want it to be about Dutch book arguments, but not the "here's why you have to be a probabilist" version that everyone writes.

My half-formed idea, and I've been carrying it around for years: the whole argument is stated in dollars. The bookie sells you a set of bets at your own odds, you take them because they look fair, and you lose money whatever happens. But the sure loss is a loss of money, and money doesn't have constant marginal utility for me or for anyone, so if the only book that works against my credences needs stakes that actually bite, the "sure loss" isn't obviously a loss in utility. That makes it look like the argument quietly needs risk neutrality, which is a huge substantive assumption to smuggle in. The other half: I've been reading LW and adjacent blogs for going on fifteen years and the standard line is still that you're either coherent or you're not. I don't buy it. Some credence states can be punished for pocket change with two or three small bets; others only come apart if the bookie gets to pick absurd stakes and offer bets that are individually terrible. I want to call that gap the 'price of the book' and ask whether the normative verdict should track it -- whether "your credences are incoherent" is one accusation or a bunch of different ones that come in degrees.

So: write me the proposal. I need the question stated sharply enough that I could actually answer it or fail to answer it, why the usual positions don't settle it, what the actual work looks like week by week (my guess is it's mostly defining the measure, then proving a few small results about it, plus some worked cases), what a good outcome would look like, what would count as the project failing, and an honest take on whether it's too big for a semester -- if it is, tell me what to cut. I'd also like the proposal to say whether any of this changes for diachronic books, since those seem to need you to pre-commit to a betting strategy and that feels like a seperate can of worms. And if my framing is confused, please just say so and give me the better version of the question. No need for a lit review or citations, I know the territory and I'll fill those in myself. American English, please.
