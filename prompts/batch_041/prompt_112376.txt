Here's the case they were fighting about. A woman we know, Nadia, got fired. Two things were happening at once. Her boss had decided to get rid of her and pushed the paperwork through on a Friday. Separately, the company was going to lay off her whole department the following Monday. She was fired Friday. Which means: if her boss had never made that decision, she'd have been out on Monday anyway.

Dev's rule: to tell whether A caused B, ask whether B would still have happened without A. If yes, then A didn't cause it. On his rule, the boss's decision didn't cause the firing, because she was gone either way.

Anne's rule: that's the wrong question. You ask whether changing A would change B, and you have to picture changing just that one thing, the way a surgeon would, one cut and nothing else moved. Change the boss's decision, leave the layoff plan exactly where it was, and the Friday firing doesn't happen. So the boss's decision caused it. Her point was that Dev's rule asks whether it would have happened anyway without ever saying anyway how, or when, and that's where all the work is.

Dev's comeback: then your test is rigged by how I describe the outcome. Call it Nadia lost her job and the boss's decision changes nothing. Call it Nadia lost her job on Friday and it changes everything. Same event, two descriptions, two answers. He said Anne only gets her answer by picking the description that suits her, and if she says some descriptions are more real than others then she owes an account of why, which is the very thing she's supposed to be explaining.

Anne: at least her test says which descriptions are allowed to count, namely the ones that pick out something you could actually have tinkered with. And she said his rule breaks in the other direction too — if two people shoot a man at the same moment and either bullet alone would have killed him, his rule says neither of them killed him.

Dev: that just shows neither shot was necessary, and maybe that's the honest answer, since neither one made the difference. She said a rule that says a man wasn't killed by either of the two bullets in him isn't honest, it's broken.

Then he said, fine, take the barometer. The reading drops before the storm, and the reading doesn't cause the storm. His rule gets that right straight away. Hers only gets it right if she already knows which changes count as surgical, which is the same thing she was accusing him of.

Anne: the difference is that the barometer and the storm are both downstream of the same thing, and her rule shows it — wiggle the barometer all you want and the storm doesn't care. His rule only tells you about dependence, and to get from dependence to cause he has to say which other situations count as close to ours, which is just as much of a choice as hers, and a less honest one because he pretends it isn't a choice.

Dev tried one more thing. What about something that never happened — the safety inspector who never showed up? Her change-one-thing picture has nothing to grab onto, since there's nothing there to change. Anne said sure there is, you change whether he came, that's a perfectly good thing to change.
