A colleague in our department, summarizing the Ramsey equation and the Stern–Nordhaus dispute for a seminar, put it this way: "The Ramsey equation says the social discount rate equals pure time preference plus the product of the elasticity of marginal utility and the growth rate of consumption. Stern sets pure time preference near zero because he thinks future generations matter equally; Nordhaus sets it at a market-observed rate because he thinks we should discount the future the way people actually do. So the disagreement is fundamentally ethical rather than empirical: Stern is the prescriptive utilitarian, Nordhaus the descriptive positivist. The only technical question is whether the elasticity of marginal utility is greater or less than one, which determines whether the rich future should be taxed more or less for the sake of the present."

I suspect this is a caricature, but I cannot say exactly where the distortions lie.
