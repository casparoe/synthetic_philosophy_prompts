I am a PhD candidate in political theory, and next week I am running a ninety-minute session of our political economy reading group on the socialist calculation debate — the Mises–Hayek challenge to central planning and Lange's reply. The room is eight political theorists and one historian of economic thought; none has economics beyond an introductory course, and most arrive assuming that planning fails, if it fails, for reasons of will and incentive. Do not explain the debate to me; give me a plan for staging it.

I want the students to arrive at the knowledge problem themselves, before any of the names get attached to it. So: the opening question, what has to be true of the answer for the next move to be available, and the order in which the pieces must be introduced, with the dependencies stated explicitly. The whole session should hang on one carrying example — say what it must contain and what it must lack, and give me your reasoning about why it has to do that work (I am inclined toward a single scarce input with two competing uses, but I will take your judgment).

Then the misconceptions, in the order they will surface, with the exact point in the sequence where I should head each off. I expect "this is about incentives," "this is about computational difficulty, so computers settle it," and "Lange won because he accepted prices," but tell me what else this particular room will produce. The session needs one deliberate pivot: the moment at which a student who has followed everything so far is forced to see that Lange's scheme presupposes the very thing in dispute. Tell me where to place that pivot, what makes it land, and what I must not say, because they have to say it. The pivot should turn on the difference between the computational reading of the challenge, which a defender of Lange can answer, and the discovery reading, which he cannot; I want that difference to become visible rather than to be announced.

Finish with one closing question that separates a student who has grasped the problem from one who has merely learned that prices carry information, and say what I should leave out at this level and openly announce that I am leaving it out. No readings.
