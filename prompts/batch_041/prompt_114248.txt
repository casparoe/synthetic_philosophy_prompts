I have been keeping a private list of situations in which someone would rather not learn something, and I cannot find the right concept for what they have in common. Four entries, all of which I have either witnessed or been told about first-hand.

A man whose father died of a hereditary, untreatable neurological condition declines a free genetic test. He is explicit that the result would not change a single decision he makes — no treatment exists, he will not have children — and that he simply does not want to spend thirty years knowing. A friend checks his brokerage account only on days when the market has risen, and since a bad year at work has stopped reading the news altogether. A woman says she does not want to read her late father's papers in case they reveal a half-sibling she never knew about, since by now nothing could be done. And a reader avoids reviews for fear of spoilers, which seems like the same shape in miniature.

Here is the part I cannot place. In each case the information is free, and in each case the person insists it would not alter any action — which, by a result I half-remember from decision theory, to the effect that information that costs nothing can never lower your expected welfare, ought to mean it cannot hurt them. Their answer is always some version of: at worst you ignore it. But you cannot ignore it. Once you know, the knowing does something to you that you did not choose, and they seem to be protecting against precisely that, not against any bad decision.

I can already name the strategic cases, where you shun information because of how it would change what you do or what others believe you know — not reading a contract you have already signed, a negotiator not wanting to learn the other side's reservation price, a doctor not ordering a test that would oblige her to act. What I am after is the residue: the case with no action and no audience, where the only thing at stake is what it is like to be you, and the information is still worth avoiding.

Does this have an established name? I have run across the phrase information avoidance, but it seems to cover the strategic cases as well, and I am not convinced it is one phenomenon rather than several. What I actually want is the carving, not the label: what exactly is being avoided, whether the strategic and non-strategic cases belong to a single kind or two, how the non-strategic case differs from ordinary risk aversion and from mere incuriosity, and whether the result I half-remember is narrower than I think or whether these cases are a genuine exception to it. If no established term fits, please coin one and mark its edges, including the cases that fall outside it.
