I'm reading Polybius, Book 6, for a seminar on cyclical versus linear views of history, and I'm stuck on the bit where he lays out the six kinds of constitution. Here's the passage:

"Most of those whose object it has been to instruct us methodically concerning such matters, distinguish three kinds of constitutions, which they call kingship, aristocracy, and democracy. Now we should, I think, be quite justified in asking them to enlighten us as to whether they represent these three to be the sole varieties or rather to be the best; for in either case my opinion is that they are wrong. ... We should therefore assert that there are six kinds of governments, the three above mentioned which are in everyone's mouth and the three which are naturally allied to them, I mean monarchy, oligarchy, and mob-rule. Now the first of these to come into being is monarchy, its growth being natural and unaided; and next arises kingship derived from monarchy by the aid of art and by the correction of defects. Monarchy first changes into its vicious allied form, tyranny; and next, the abolishment of both gives birth to aristocracy. Aristocracy by its very nature degenerates into oligarchy."

My problem is that I can't see how the two halves of this fit together. He says the best constitution is a mixture of the three good kinds, which is what Lycurgus did at Sparta and what Rome supposedly has. But he also says each good kind turns into its vicious twin by something like a natural process. If that's right, a mixed constitution isn't an escape from the cycle (anacyclosis, if thats the word) — it's three cycles running at once, and the thing should rot from three directions instead of one. So is the cycle meant to be a law even the best constitution can't dodge, just slow down? Or is dodging it exactly the point of the mixture? And what work is "art" doing in the kingship bit — is that the escape hatch, a legislator who can interrupt nature?

I'd also like to know whether calling this "cyclical" is even right. Is Polybius claiming the same sequence will recur forever, or just giving a typical order in which constitutions tend to succeed one another? Please don't just hand me the standard checks-and-balances line — argue it from what's actually on the page. And tell me where your reading is most likely to fail and what would change your mind about it. British English, please.
