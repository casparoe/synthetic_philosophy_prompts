I have arrived at a position about the intentional stance that I would like to have tested, both against its rivals and against its own commitments. The position is a realism about patterns rather than about the states that figure in them. To attribute beliefs and desires to a system, on this view, is to claim that the system's behavior instantiates a pattern that is real in a specific sense: a minimal description of the relevant behavioral data, framed in an appropriate language, is shortened by positing the pattern, and shortened more than any description that omits it. The pattern is therefore not a projection of our convenience but a feature of the world that a sufficiently compressed description is obliged to include. The physical, design, and intentional stances then stand as three levels of description, with the intentional stance neither a useful fiction nor a mere manner of speaking, but a level at which genuine structure is captured.

I hold this. What would count against it? I want the defeaters assembled and ranked, from those that should be fatal to those that would be merely awkward, and I want you to be honest about which of them the position's own defenders would themselves concede rather than merely fend off. I am unsure about the following in particular, and would like each addressed:

- Whether the compression criterion does any work or is circular. What counts as the appropriate language, and who decides? If the criterion is only definable relative to a chosen description language, does it collapse into the pragmatism it was meant to escape?
- Whether realism about patterns is compatible with eliminativism about the entities that display them, or whether the two are distinguished only by rhetoric.
- Whether the design stance has any autonomy, or whether it is merely a staging post on the way from the physical to the intentional, in which case the three-level picture threatens to collapse.
- Whether the taxonomy of stances is exhaustive, and whether the line between a real pattern and a merely useful one can be drawn without covert appeal to our interests.
- What the view predicts that its instrumentalist and its eliminativist rivals do not, and whether any of those predictions could fail.

Tell me which of these are live worries and which are red herrings, and whether the position, once suitably qualified, risks anything at all. If it has been insulated into unfalsifiability, say whether that is a defect for a view of this kind or simply the price of doing metaphysics. I would find it most persuasive to be shown a concrete case in which the compression criterion and our antecedent sense of which patterns are real come apart, if such a case exists.
