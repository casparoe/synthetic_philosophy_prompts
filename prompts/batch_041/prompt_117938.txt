I am preparing a seminar on epistemic environments and want to begin from the specification rather than from the case studies, so write me the desiderata for a theory of echo chambers and epistemic bubbles: what any adequate account of these phenomena has to deliver. Fix the purpose as diagnostic. I want the finished theory to let someone outside a community — a journalist, a regulator, a friend, a department chair — look at a forum, a congregation, a research group, a family, and say whether this is an epistemic environment that is going wrong, in what way, and how badly; so the requirements should be ones an outsider with limited access could actually check.

Begin from the distinction my students will arrive with: an epistemic bubble as an information deficit, a group that has simply not encountered the relevant evidence, and an echo chamber as something stronger, a group whose members have been brought to distrust whatever comes from outside, so that contrary evidence does not get a hearing at all. The list should say what a theory must do with that distinction — preserve it, refine it, or show that it collapses under pressure.

I want the account to handle three further cases, which are the ones that make me suspicious of the received picture:

- a community that is unanimous because dissent is costly to voice rather than because anyone has been taught to distrust outsiders — nothing is discredited and nobody speaks;
- a community that is unanimous because those who disagreed have left, so that the agreement is an artifact of sorting rather than of anything the community does;
- a community that is information-poor and does discredit outsiders, but whose beliefs are true and whose discredited outsiders are the unreliable ones, as with a small clinic that has stopped taking seriously the claims of a well-funded industry group.

I have been calling the first a hollow consensus and the third a warranted exclusion; I want the list to say whether those are genuine categories or merely the bubble and chamber distinction in disguise.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that generates it, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are commitments of a particular camp (that the diagnosis concerns the believer's epistemic vice, that it concerns the structure of the environment, that it must not presuppose which side is right, that it must come in degrees, that it must be applicable without access to the community's internal reasons, and so on). I especially want the part where you say which requirements cannot be jointly satisfied. My suspicion is that a theory usable by an outsider who lacks access to the community's evidence cannot at the same time tell warranted from unwarranted dismissal of outside sources; if that is right, argue it, because it would mean the diagnostic ambition has to be weakened or abandoned, and that is the result I most want from the exercise. Skip the scorecard of existing views — the criteria themselves are the deliverable.
