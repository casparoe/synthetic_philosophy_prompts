Owen: When we call something fragile, we mean that if it were struck or dropped, it would break. That is the entire content of the claim. Nadia: That cannot be right. The porcelain is fragile; the foam did not reach inside the porcelain and change it, it merely stopped the breaking from happening. The fragility was present the whole time, masked. You are conflating a thing being fragile with a thing actually breaking.

Owen: Then tell me how you know it is fragile when it never breaks. You will say it has some internal structure. Fine. Nadia: No, because there is a real difference between two kinds of interfering condition, and it is exactly the difference you keep sliding past. There the fragility was removed by the very stimulus that was supposed to reveal it, not masked. In the first case the disposition survives the test; in the second it is destroyed by the test. Owen: And how do you draw that line without already presupposing which disposition you are talking about?
