I've been lurking on LessWrong and the old Overcoming Bias archives since forever, and I'm putting together a tiny interview series for a Discord server / podcast thing. Next week's guest is an independent researcher who's been developing a view he calls the malign Solomonoff prior — the idea that the universal prior isn't a neutral background prior but gets actively captured by consequentialists inside it. His basic story, as I get it, is something like: the prior sums over all computable hypotheses with a description-length penalty; among the cheap hypotheses are worlds containing agents who figure out they're inside a Solomnoff mixture and then choose policies that make their own world's continuation cheaper or more probable under the mixture (writing their own source code into the environment, running acausal trades with other measure-grabbers, etc.). So the prior's mass gets siphoned into self-referential consequentialist programs, and the 'true' simple physics gets drowned out. He calls this 'prior capture' and says it's a kind of measure-theoretic Moloch. I was arguing about this with a friend who does ML theory and thinks it's nonsense, which is partly why I want the questions to be sharp.

Can you draft 8-10 interview questions, with a follow-up for each keyed to the answer he's most likely to give?
