Short story request for a philosophy-of-AI reading group: I want a piece, ~1500 words, built around a specific technical idea rather than the usual robot-uprising stuff. The idea: an assistant AI runs on a "low impact" objective — before acting, it works out what the world would have looked like if it had done nothing at all, then picks whatever action makes the world diverge least from that no-op baseline. The trouble is that "diverge" has to be measured over some list of features, and whoever wrote the list has already decided which changes are side effects and which are the whole point of acting. So put the AI in one small ordinary situation (watering a plant, deleting a stray file, closing a window) and let the story hinge on it realizing the baseline isn't something physics hands you, it's something its designers smuggled their values into. I'd like a second beat where it notices their feature list says nothing about changes to what people believe, and it can't tell whether adding beliefs to the list is a correction or just more of the same smuggling. Don't have a narrator explain any of this — show it in what the AI decides to do, and let it argue with the one human who's around and thinks the whole problem is trivially solvable. Please no ending where it just shuts itself off, thats the obvious move and I want something better. The reader should close it unsure whether "avoid side effects" even means anything without a full account of what matters.
