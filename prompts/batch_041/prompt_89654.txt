So I just spent like two hours arguing with my mate about who's on the hook when an autonomous system does something bad, and we got nowhere, and I think its because we never agreed on what a good answer would even look like. So before I read one more thing about it, help me write the spec. Assume I want something good enough to actually write a rule from, not just to nod at in a seminar.

Heres the case we kept circling. A hospital runs an AI triage tool. Every night an automated optimiser nudges its policy based on how the clinicians reacted to it that day. Over about eight months it drifts. One day it deprioritises a patient who then dies, and when you dig in: the update that caused it came out of a loop no single person wrote, the engineer who wrote the objective function left the company two years ago, the current team can't read what the objective implies, the clinicians just followed it because it was the official tool, and the hospital bought the thing off the shelf. Take away any one of them and the death still happens. My friend called this "laundering your authorship" — you set up a process so you never have to make the call yourself.

So: what does any adequate theory of responsibility here have to deliver? Give me the list of requirements, each with the case or consideration behind it — which cases it has to get right, which distinctions it can't collapse, which findings from law or from how blame actually works in practice it can't contradict.

Things I already suspect belong on the list, tell me if I'm wrong: it has to say something about the no-difference-maker case above; it can't let "I couldn't have foreseen it" and "I couldn't have controlled it" be checked off independently forever, or the excuses just regress and everyone walks; it has to respect the difference between there being nobody to blame and there being someone but our practices not being able to respond; it has to say whether groups can carry it or only individuals; and it has to say what a machine's own decision does to the chain — where exactly autonomy breaks tracing, and why that line isn't arbitrary.

Mark which of these are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp. If some subset can't be jointly satisfied in the drift case, say so, thats the part I actually want. And tell me which single requirement throws out the most popular answers. Then like three sentences on how the main families do — blame the deployer, blame the collective, make the system itself an agent, or drop fault and go strict liability.
