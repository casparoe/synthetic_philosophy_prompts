I retired last year after four decades in experimental physics, and I have spent the time since working through the philosophy of space and time as a serious amateur. What I want from you is an honest credence — a number, not a survey of positions — in a fairly specific proposition, together with the decomposition that produces it.

The proposition is this: that a thoroughgoing relationalism can account for every phenomenon standardly offered as evidence for substantivalism about spacetime, including the inertial effects of rotation, without positing spacetime points or an absolute frame as entities over and above the relative configuration of the material contents of the world.

To fix the case, consider the leanest example I can construct. Take a universe containing a single rigid body — a gyroscope, say — and nothing else whatsoever: no distant stars, no fields, no further matter. Suppose that, as described from within, the orientation of the gyroscope's axis is changing relative to the body's own principal axes. Is there a fact of the matter about whether it is genuinely rotating? And can a relationalist recover whatever facts there are here from the relational configuration and its history alone — perhaps by defining rotation against the total angular momentum of the world, so that in a one-body universe the notion threatens to collapse?

Please give me your credence that relationalism, in the form stated above, is correct. Decompose it into at least the following terms, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each:

(a) that there is a determinate, mind-independent fact about inertial frames — that is, that the bucket-style cases genuinely disclose structure requiring explanation;
(b) that a relational, configuration- or shape-based dynamics can reproduce all such disclosed structure, including the single-body case;
(c) that reproducing the phenomena would amount to relationalism being true, rather than merely empirically equivalent to a substantivalist alternative — the underdetermination question;
(d) any further term you judge belongs, for instance whether the question has a determinate answer at all given how the phrase entity over and above is to be read.

Then combine the terms and give me the final figure. Finally, tell me which single term I should track if I wish to update, and what argument or result would move it most.
