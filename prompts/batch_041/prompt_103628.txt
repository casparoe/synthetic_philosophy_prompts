I've been stuck in the middle of a long fight between two friends in my archaeology-and-philosophy reading group and I want to force them to write something together instead of just sniping at each other. One of them is what I'd call a collapse realist: he thinks complex societies really do fall apart -- populations crash, cities empty out, writing and long-distance trade stop, centralized authority dissolves -- and that the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean, the Classic Maya, and Rapa Nui are all genuine cases, and that the concept of collapse is doing real explanatory work (plus, he adds, we should be worried about our own version of it). The other one thinks "collapse" is a sloppy, question-begging word that smuggles in a baseline and a value judgment: what usually happens is reorganisation, decentralisation, or a shift in what counts as a polity, and calling that a "failure" is an artifact of looking at things from the point of view of the old elites. She likes to point at how much continuity there is in the Maya lowlands after the so-called collapse, and at how contested the Easter Island "ecocide" story is -- she says the population crash people talk about may have been mostly post-contact. They've been arguing for months and I think half the time they're just using "collapse" to mean different things.

Can you draft the joint statement they should have produced, the kind of document an adversarial collaboration produces? I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both of them could actually sign, including claims about what "collapse" should be defined to mean and about what the three cases do and don't show; (2) the real points of disagreement, each written in wording that the person who holds it would accept as fair; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one of them -- and for each crux, a test, case, or piece of evidence that BOTH of them would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

A few things I'd like you to build in. Make them agree in advance on some kind of indicator bundle or "resilience ledger" (population, urbanism, literacy, trade reach, central authority, monumental building) so that "collapse" stops being a vibe and becomes a threshold claim -- and make them say what threshold, if any, they'll both accept. Make them say explicitly whether collapse is observer-relative (a collapse for the palace might be a liberation for the village) and whether that changes the definition or just the moral coloring. And get them to commit on whether Easter Island is a real test case or a contaminated one, since they disagree about that. If it turns out part of their fight is definately just verbal, say which part and why. Numbered agreed-claims first, then a disagreements table.
