Ok so I keep going back and forth on this and I want you to help me actually sort it out rather than just nodding along. There seem to be two completely different pictures of what corruption even is.

One is the picture you see in every NGO report and transparency index: corruption is when someone entrusted with public power uses it for private gain. The official is a fiduciary of an abstract "public," and taking the envelope or hiring your nephew is a betrayal of that trust. Call it the trustee picture.

The other picture is the one you get if you actually sit around in a lot of West African or South Asian or southern Italian towns for a while: the official is not first of all a trustee of some abstract public, she's a node in a web of reciprocal obligations -- to her kin, her village, the patron who got her the job in the first place -- and routing a contract or a post to them isnt stealing from anyone, it's paying a debt that everyone around agrees is real. On that picture the "corrupt" official who cuts off her own people is the one who's actually behaving badly.

So: compare these two as rival theories of what corruption is. I dont want a literature survey or a list of who said what -- I want the comparison done on the merits. Which one gets more of the cases right? Take seriously the ones where they pull apart: the nephew who gets the job, the gift that isn't quite a bribe, the road that runs to the chief's village instead of where it's most needed. Does the trustee picture just beg the question by assuming that "public" and "private" carve the world up the way a modern Weberian state does, so that it can't even see the alternative as a moral position rather than a failure of moral development? And flip it: does the reciprocity picture have any principled way to condemn the thing we all want to condemn -- the minister skimming the vaccine budget into an offshore account -- or does it quietly collapse into "whatever the local norms are, goes"?

Then the part I really care about. Is the disagreement here substantive, or is it just two vocabularies for the same set of facts with different spin? And if it's substantive, does that mean the trustee picture is provincial -- one local moral framework pretending to be the neutral one -- or can you make the case that it's actually the right one and the reciprocity picture is a rationalization that insiders and outsiders both have their own reasons to keep alive? Give me your honest read on that, not a both-sides hedge.
