I keep going back and forth on which inner states are actually about things, so I'm going to write down my gut verdicts and see if you can find a principle underneath them.

The frog: a cell in its eye fires at small dark moving specks and the frog snaps. That state is about flies, or prey anyway. Dangle a lead pellet and the frog's getting it wrong, not just doing something useless.

The bee: she waggles at an angle and the others fly off to the nectar. The dance is about where the nectar is, and the bees watching it end up in a state thats about that spot.

The bacterium with the little chain of magnetite that swims along the field lines: about nothing. It's a compass needle, it just goes, nothing is represented.

A thermostat, a smoke alarm: same, nothing represented.

Then the awkward ones. A blind bloke with a camera wired into a pad on his tongue. After a few weeks he says the tingling feels like things in front of him and he ducks without thinking. Those tingles are about the obstacles, I want to say. But it's a tongue being tickled, so why isn't that the bacterium over again?

My mate's laptop, which he trained on photos until one of the layers lights up whenever there's a face in the picture. That layer is about nothing, it's just a mechanism we built. But then the frog's cell is also just a mechanism...

The one I'm sure about: me, thinking there's milk in the fridge. About the milk, obviously, and it can be wrong.

So: is there one account of what makes a state represent something that gets all of these, or do I have to give one of them up, and if so which? And whatever you land on, what does it say about a case I haven't listed: a bloke with a magnet in his fingertip who says he can feel when a transformer's on -- is that state about the field, or about his finger tingling? Tell me honestly which of my verdicts your account definately can't keep. My mate who did a philosophy degree reckons this is all teleosemantics, but I can't see how that copes with the tongue.
