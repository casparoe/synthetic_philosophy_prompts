Ok, long-ish setup, sorry. I'm a third-year PhD student working on early modern Europe, mostly court and church records, and there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I don't have a word for. It's adjacent to the standard line that history gets written by the winners, but that line has never felt like it's describing what I'm actually seeing, and when I float this to people they either say 'oh that's just X' or they nod and start talking about silences in the archive, which I don't think is it either. So here's the thing. Take any group that spent a long time on the recieving end of power: peasants dragged into seigneurial courts, enslaved people whose words survive only as the clerk's rendering, women whose entire recorded existence is four depositions, colonial subjects filling in questionnaires they didn't write. There is no shortage of text about them. There are thousands of pages. The catch is that every page is an answer. Somebody with authority asked a question, and what survives is the reply -- in the questioner's language, cut to the questioner's categories, written onto the questioner's form. So when I try to say what these people thought -- their cosmology, their politics, the stuff they said to each other when nobody was taking notes -- everything I have is reactive by construction. I can recover what someone said while being interrogated, and I keep catching myself treating that as what they thought. There's a second-order version of the same worry: because the reactive material is all there is, my reconstruction comes out looking like a set of positions on the issues their rulers cared about, which might be an artifact of the archive rather than a fact about them. I know there's a famous argument that the subaltern can't speak, and I know people write about archival silence, but silence is the wrong word here -- there's plenty of text, it's the *shape* of the text that's the problem.

So: does this predicament have a name? If it does, tell me which one and be exact about how it differs from the nearest neighbours, because the near ones all seem to be either about missing evidence or about the ethics of speaking for others, and this seems to be about evidence that's abundant but structurally responsive. If there isn't a name, coin one, define it, and tell me where the edges are -- what falls inside it and what doesn't, and what the boundary cases look like. I'm also genuinely unsure whether I'm describing one phenomenon or two (the sources are all reactive vs. the content that comes out is all reactive); if those come apart, say so rather than papering over it, because I suspect the two halves have different fixes.

Please lead with the bottom line -- the name, or the verdict that there isn't one -- in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my question.
