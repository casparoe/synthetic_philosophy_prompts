I work as a software engineer and have no formal training in philosophy, but I took an online course on probability and rational choice a couple of years ago and have been grading problem sets for it ever since, which mostly means checking submissions against the instructor's model answer and the rubric she sends along. She insists that graders return a score out of ten plus written comments tied to the individual parts of the question, that "this is nearly right" is not a comment, and that we should never dock points for things the question didn't ask about. I've now hit a submission I don't trust my own judgment on. It is fluent and well organized and uses the course's vocabulary correctly, which is precisely the situation in which I can't tell whether I am looking at a good answer or a good imitation of one, and I would rather be slightly harsh than let something through that the instructor catches later.

Here is the assignment. "Recall the principle we have been calling the Principal Principle: (PP) for any proposition A, any time t, and any number x between 0 and 1, an agent's credence in A, conditional on the proposition that the objective chance of A at t is x, together with any evidence E that is admissible at t, is exactly x. Evidence is admissible at t if and only if it concerns only the way the world is up to and including t; evidence that concerns how things turn out after t is not admissible. Now consider the following case. A coin will be flipped at 3:00 pm. The chance of heads is 0.5 at every time up to 1:00 pm and 0.7 at every time from 1:00 pm until the flip; the change is caused by a technician who, at 1:00 pm, swaps the coin in the box for a different one. Take both chances as stipulated, and do not worry about how the physical setup could produce them. A log beside the box records the current coin's chance of heads and any replacement that has occurred; the log is accurate, and Chloe trusts it completely. Answer the following. (i) At noon, Chloe reads the log's first entry, which says that the coin now in the box has a chance of heads of 0.5. What credence should she have in heads at noon? (ii) At 1:00 pm, Chloe reads the second entry, which records the replacement and states that the coin now in the box has a chance of heads of 0.7. What credence should she have in heads at 1:00 pm, and does she violate (PP) if she changes it? (iii) Suppose instead that the log is blank and that Chloe knows nothing whatever about the coin's chance. Does (PP) tell her what credence to have in heads? (iv) Is (PP) a substantive constraint on credence, or is it true by definition? Explain."

And here is what the student wrote. "(i) Chloe knows that the chance of heads at noon is 0.5, and she has no other evidence that is admissible at noon, so (PP) requires her credence in heads to be 0.5. (ii) At 1:00 pm her credence should be 0.7. The log entry is admissible, because it records how the world is at 1:00 pm — the coin has been replaced, and it is the coin now in the box that will be flipped — so her credence, conditional on that evidence together with the chance of 0.7, is 0.7. She does not violate (PP) by changing her credence, since the change is driven by admissible evidence and by the chance itself having changed at 1:00 pm; (PP) is indexed to a time. (If Chloe had instead learned the new coin's exact weight, diameter, and surface profile, (PP) would tell her to set that information aside, since it bears on the outcome directly rather than through the chance.) (iii) Yes. (PP) says that an agent's credence in a proposition should be equal to the objective chance of that proposition, so Chloe's credence in heads should be 0.5, the chance of heads at noon. (iv) (PP) is substantive rather than definitional. It links two things that can come apart — an agent's credence in A and the objective chance of A — and an agent can be perfectly coherent while violating it: someone who is certain that the chance of heads is 0.5 but has credence 0.9 in heads has no inconsistency in her credences as such, and (PP) is exactly what rules her out." Please grade this out of ten, with comments organized by part (i) through (iv) and an overall note, saying for each step whether it is licensed by (PP) as the question states it and, where it is not, exactly what has gone wrong.
