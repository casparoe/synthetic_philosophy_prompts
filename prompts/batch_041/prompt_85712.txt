I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy, and I am assembling a single session for a graduate seminar on truth, paradox, and self-reference. Twelve students, ninety minutes, one room, a whiteboard, and index cards; no laptops or phones. I want an in-class activity that makes the students feel, in their own hands, the pressures that motivate non-classical and iterative accounts of truth, rather than hearing those accounts summarized. By the end they should have collided with three distinct phenomena: (i) a small network of sentences for which no assignment of truth values satisfies both the ordinary disquotational reading of 'true' and classical bivalence; (ii) a network that is perfectly consistent but floats free, admitting two different assignments with nothing internal to choose between them; and (iii) a network whose values, under a natural rule of revising each sentence in light of the others, never settle but repeat in a cycle. They should meet all three before anyone in the room says 'grounded,' 'fixed point,' 'revision,' or 'paraconsistent.'

Design that activity. It should be built so that the participants themselves embody or assign the truth values, and so that the problems arrive as consequences of following the rules rather than as announcements. Give me: the rules and the materials, including a concrete, ready-to-copy set of cards forming at least the three networks above plus one grounded control network; a procedure with timed rounds, including the revision step and how ties or disagreements are resolved; roles or rules if you use them; and a payoff or scoring scheme only if it sharpens the pressure rather than distracting from it. Then give me a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the theoretical options they correspond to — the classical impasse, the ungrounded-but-consistent sentences, the oscillating case, and what each option has to give up to handle one of these without losing another. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to recover, and be candid about which feature of the underlying idea the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself. Write the answer in American English.
