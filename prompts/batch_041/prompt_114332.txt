I'm a retired biochemist — 35 years on enzyme kinetics — and since I stopped I've been reading philosophy of science, mostly the stats-wars / replication stuff. There's an old colleague, Dana, who was the person our whole department emailed before they submitted anything, and I've been trying to work out where she actually stands so I can guess what she'd say about a paper I just read.

Here's what I've watched her say over the years. Big observational study, p = .01, enormous sample, effect pointing flat against a mechanism everyone's sure of: dont trust it, something's off. Tiny underpowered study, p = .03, no mechanism, first of its kind: interesting, chase it, but don't build on it yet. Very well powered study, p = .001, but the effect is a 0.3% difference: it's real, it's just too small to care about. Study with p = .08, big effect, mechanism everyone already believed: that's real, they just didn't have the power.

So the paper in front of me: preregistered, large sample, p = .04, moderate effect, mechanism that's plausible but nothing anyone was already committed to, first of its kind, no replication yet, and it nudges against an older smaller study. What's Dana going to say? I want the rule (or rules) she seems to be running, what each one predicts here, your best guess with the reasoning, and the single further case that would actually separate the rules. I care less about the verdict than about seeing where the seams are.
