I'm a third-year phil student and I sit in on a teleosemantics reading group — the whole project where you try to get mental content out of biological function instead of out of whatever's going on intrinsically inside the head. My friend in the group is all-in on the bee-dance / vervet-alarm-call analogy as the master model, and he uses it as an argument, not just an illustration. The pitch is: the dance is about "nectar that way" and the alarm call is about "leopard" because dances and calls were selected for getting hive-mates to nectar and troop-mates up trees — so a belief is just that same thing one level up, a state whose job it is to indicate something. He says this fully naturalizes content (no spooky "original intentionality," it's all selected-for indication), and that even misrepresentation comes free, because a call can go off at a false alarm the way a damaged heart can fail to pump. So the analogy isn't decoration for him, it's the load-bearing part of the argument.

Here's what's nagging me. My roommate is doing a PhD in behavioral ecology and she says the "selected to inform" framing is basically a folk story in her field — that signals are selected to change the receiver's behavior for the sender's benefit, and any information they carry is at best a byproduct, sometimes not even that. If that's right, then the whole "the call means leopard because it was selected to convey leopard" line is built on something that isn't there, and my friend has smuggled the receiver's interests into the selection story. I can't tell if that sinks the analogy or just trims it.

So: can you audit this for me? Give me the correspondence table first — what maps onto what (sign, referent, producer, consumer, selection history, malfunction) — then where the map actually breaks. I care about the breaks that matter for his conclusion specifically: compositional beliefs, beliefs about absent or abstract stuff, the fact that a belief has no receiver whose behavior it's tuning, truth vs. mere success, that kind of thing. Then tell me straight whether the naturalization conclusion survives once the disanalogies and my roommate's objection are on the table, or whether the analogy was quietly doing all the work the argument needed. And if it breaks, what would a better analogy be for what he actually wants.
