I chair my state's sentencing-reform task force and we vote in two weeks on a proposed overhaul of the guidelines. The drafters want to delete "desert" from the scheme entirely: sentence length would come from a recidivism-risk score plus a schedule of reparations owed to the victims' community, with no separate weight for how serious the offense itself was — and the same presumptive sentence whether the harm actually landed or was only attempted. Their memo says retributive desert is unscientific and rests on a folk notion of free will that philosophers have already gutted; two members push back that a system with no desert just uses people as levers and will end up punishing the risky and the unlucky instead of the culpable. I've followed the Dennett–Caruso exchange enough to think both sides have real arguments and neither is just confused, so I don't want a memo that strawmans either camp. Write the short opinion I'll circulate before the vote: a concrete recommendation (keep desert, cut it, or rewrite it — and if rewrite, how), the reasoning, and the strongest consideration on the losing side treated honestly. And tell me what would change your mind — which argument or bit of evidence would flip your recommendation, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
