I'm running a session on personal identity next week and want to open with a poem instead of a reading. Could you write me a sestina about the Ship of Theseus? The form needs to actually do the work here, not just decorate it: make the six end-words the six parts (or six terms) of the puzzle, so that the way each end-word migrates to a new slot in every stanza is the plank-by-plank replacement -- by the last stanza every word has sat in every position, but they're still the same six words, which is kind of the whole trick. Then use the envoi, where all six get crammed into three lines, for the confrontation: the ship that got repaired plank by plank over the years and the ship somebody rebuilt out of the original discarded planks (thats the version Hobbes tacked on), both sitting in the harbor, both claiming the name. Give each ship a voice, even just a line or two -- the repaired one arguing from unbroken history, the rebuilt one insisting it's the actual old wood. And let a third thing hover: the harbor registry, which has been logging "the Theseus" the whole time no matter what the wood was up to -- the thought that the name is just a convention we keep renewing. Don't resolve it. Hard limit: 39 lines (a sestina is exactly 39, so don't pad it out).
