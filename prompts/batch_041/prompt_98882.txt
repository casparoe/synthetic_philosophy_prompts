I look after the paperwork at a small plumbing firm, and right now I'm stuck on the insurance renewal. The form has about 180 yes/no boxes — do we store solvents in a locked cupboard, do we test the boiler every year, have all the lads done the manual-handling course, and so on. I go through them one at a time, and for each single box I'd say I'm 95% sure we're doing what it asks. But I also know we've never once got one of these forms 100% right. There's always some box that turns out to have been wrong. If I actually multiply my confidence out, the odds that the whole thing is accurate are basically nil.

Then there's the declaration at the bottom: "I confirm that the information given above is true and complete." I can't make myself sign it, because I don't think the whole form is true. I think at least one line is wrong — I just don't know which one. My boss says everyone signs it, its a formality, the insurer only spot-checks a few. My mate says I'm contradicting myself: if I believe every line, I've got to believe all of them together, so either I think the form's fine or I should go back and find the line I don't believe. And another lad reckons I should just hunt for the box I'm least sure about, flag that one as uncertain, and sign the rest.

Am I making a fuss about nothing, or is there something real going on here? What should I actually do — sign it, refuse, hedge, flag my weakest box? And if there is a genuine puzzle, I'd like to know what it is, because it feels like my beliefs are being pulled two ways at once and I can't see which way is right. If I've set this up in a confused way — say the 95% is a red herring, or I'm muddling two seperate questions — please just tell me straight rather than humouring me.
