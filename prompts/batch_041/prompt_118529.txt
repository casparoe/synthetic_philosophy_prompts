I run a meetup, about fifteen people, 90 minutes, mixed crowd — a couple of grad students and the rest people who read too many blogs. Next month I want to do the norms of assertion, but as a game, not a talk. What I'm after: I want them to spend most of the session actually asserting things under rules, and to leave having felt the pull of four different answers to "when are you allowed to just say it?" — it's true / I believe it / I've got good grounds / I know it — because those only bite when they come apart in play, so the mechanics have to be built to pull them apart. Ideally someone wins a round by being right for a bad reason (lucky inference, sketchy source, guess that happens to land) and the room has to decide whether to dock them, and the room splits, and that split is the lesson. Fold in if it works: a challenge phase where anyone can demand "how do you know?" and answering costs the speaker something; a retraction rule; and enough reward for plain accuracy that the truth rule gets a fair hearing rather than losing on round one. Give me the rules, materials, a payoff scheme that doesnt need real money, a timeline, and a debrief that maps each moment onto one of the four candidate rules and flags the moments that don't cleanly fit any of them. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time — my money's on someone gaming the challenge phase into a filibuster — and be honest about the part of the real position (that assertion answers to knowledge and not merely to truth) that a game like this definately can't reproduce, so I know what I have to just stand up and tell them.
