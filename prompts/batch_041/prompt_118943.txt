For the better part of my career I was an experimental physicist, and since retiring I have been reading my way into epistemology; I have now reached the regress problem and find that the standard slogans leave me roughly where I began. I would like an explanation that works through the mechanics rather than restating the labels of foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism.

To keep us on common ground, please work with a distinction I will draw as follows. On the thin reading, the relata of the justifying relation are propositions, and the issue is whether any proposition can confer justification without itself standing in need of it. On the thick reading, the relata are a subject's acts or attitudes of basing one belief on another, and the issue is whether a chain of basing can terminate in something that is not itself a case of basing — a perceptual seeming, a disposition to assent, an observation report. Then say what becomes of each of the three positions under each reading, and in particular whether the thick reading causes any of them to collapse into one of the others.

Please also address whether the infinitist requires an infinite chain that is ever actually traversed or only one that is available on demand, and whether anything substantive turns on that difference. And tell me whether my scientist's instinct — that observation reports are where the regress stops — amounts to foundationalism in a lab coat, or to something the three familiar positions do not quite capture.

Answer as exactly five numbered items of one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion.
