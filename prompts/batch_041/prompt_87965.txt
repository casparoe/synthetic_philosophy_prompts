Free will unit starts Monday and I want to open with the sphex wasp -- she inspects the burrow, you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she comes back out, finds it gone, drags it over, and then the whole routine resets so she inspects the burrow again, and you can apparently keep this up all afternoon. Nice clean picture of a deterministic system: same input, same output, no exceptions. Dennett loves her for this (Hofstadter gave us "sphexish" for it), and his line is that we're sphexish too, just with a much longer menu of routines -- which is supposed to be the good news, elbow room without any spooky gap in the causal order. But isn't that just handing the incompatibilist his premise? If the wasp is what being determined looks like from the outside, and Dennett says we're determined, only fancier, then calling us free because our loop is bigger is the kind of thing a sharp first-year will spot in ten seconds, and I'll spend the lecture defending a redefinition. So two things: is my read of the example even right, and how would you set it up so the wasp actually does the work I want her to do?
