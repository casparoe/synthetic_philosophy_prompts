I'm in a grad seminar on philosophy of language and we've been doing content externalism, and a friend in the class — a bit of a troll, honestly — built the following argument after we read the Twin Earth stuff, and now half the room thinks it's a reductio of externalism and the other half thinks it's just a bad argument with a cheap trick buried in it. I can't tell which. I'd like you to act like someone who knows this literature cold and take it apart. Please think it through carefully rather than pattern-matching to "Twin Earth, therefore externalism is true"; maybe start by seperating out the moving parts — reference, content, environment, linguistic community — so you're not treating externalism as one big blob.

Here's the argument, numbered so we can point at pieces:

(1) Twin Earth shows that what a word refers to is fixed by the speaker's environment, not by anything inside the speaker's head.
(2) But no two speakers have the same environment. Everyone sits at a different spatiotemporal spot with a different causal history, so the total set of facts surrounding each of us differs in at least some respect.
(3) If reference is fixed by the environment, then any difference in environment is a potential difference in reference.
(4) So for any two speakers there's a possible difference in what their word "water" picks out.
(5) Words that pick out different things don't express the same content.
(6) So no two speakers can be gauranteed to mean the same thing by "water"; in fact there may be no determinate fact of the matter that they do.
(7) So no two people ever share a thought, and communication is basically a fiction.
(8) But the entire selling point of externalism was that meaning is public and shared — that's what made it more than a curiosity. So externalism refutes itself.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'm pretty attached to (1) — I really do think Twin Earth shows what it's supposed to — and obviously I want to keep (8), so if the fix requires giving one of those up, tell me what I'm paying and whether there's a cheaper exit. I'd also like to hear why whichever step turns out to be the problem looked fine in the first place, and whether that's a one-off patch or something that keeps coming back for views like this one.
