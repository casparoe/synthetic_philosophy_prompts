Ok so I've been chewing on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument for a while now — the one where you do what you do because of the way you are, so being responsible for the doing would mean being responsible for the way you are, which would mean having made yourself the way you are, which would mean being responsible for the way you already were when you did the making, and so on back forever; so nothing is ever truly up to you in the sense real blame or credit would need. I want a short story built on it. Not the usual "guy realizes determinism is true and shrugs" thing.

Setting: a small inland town, present day, no sci-fi, no lab. Over about twenty years the town has rebuilt its courts, schools and family life around the idea that nobody is ultimately responsible for anything. No punishment — only "containment and repair." Here's the wrinkle I want in it: they kept the praise. Once a year there's an evening where people stand up and get thanked and honoured and credited for what they've built and become. The person at the center of the story is the town's one paid responsibility auditor, whose job is to catch resentment creeping back into official proceedings, and who has started to notice that she can't do that job without using it herself — and that the town's whole anti-blame reform is the thing everyone is proudest of. Also give her the containment side to sign off on: she has to authorise holding somebody indefinitely, not because they're at fault, just because of what they'll probably do.

Constraints: nobody ever lectures anyone about the argument — the regress has to be load-bearing in the plot, not explained in dialogue. At least one scene where she feels something (gratitude, or a flash of real contempt for someone) that her own commitments say shouldn't be able to land on anyone. Don't end with everyone deciding compatibilism was right, and don't end on a speech.

Cap it at 900 words for the story, then a note under 120 words: where the story takes liberties with the argument or leans on a contested reading of it (the credit/praise side especially — whether the regress really does symmetric work there, or whether it would even reach a being that had somehow made itself), and what would change your mind about whether the staging is fair to the argument — like if you think its real bite is somewhere other than where the story puts it.
