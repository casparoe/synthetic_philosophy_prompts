Ok so I'm writing a short essay for a grad workshop on accuracy-first epistemology and I'd really like your comments on it. Not a rewrite, not a summary, just tell me where it's weak, where I'm kidding myself, and whether the central idea is actually interesting or just a confusion.

Here's the idea. Accuracy-first says the fundamental epistemic good is accuracy: a credence is good to the extent it's close to the truth, and a credence function is good to the extent it's accurate overall. The standard machinery scores each credence separately for how close it is to the truth (with a strictly proper scoring rule, if you care about the details), then adds those up or takes expected accuracy. My worry is about what happens when the proposition I have a credence in is itself about the accuracy of my credences. Call this nested accuracy. Suppose my credence in P is 0.7, and I also have a credence that this first-order credence is accurate. The accuracy of the second credence depends on whether the first one is accurate. So the two aren't independent. The standard additive scoring picture treats them as separate items to be scored, but their truth values are linked. I think this creates a problem for the accuracy-first story about epistemic norms, because maximizing expected accuracy can recommend a second-order credence that is self-undermining: if I become very confident that my first-order credences are accurate, and they aren't, then my overall credence function is less accurate than it would have been if I'd been less confident. That looks like a case where the accuracy-first norm tells me to do something that makes my overall doxastic state worse by its own lights.

My tentative fix is to use a holistic scoring rule: score the whole credence function at once, with penalties for incoherence between first-order and second-order credences. But then the usual dominance arguments for probabilism and conditionalization may not go through, because those arguments rely on being able to score each credence independently. So maybe accuracy-first has to choose between its foundational arguments and its treatment of higher-order evidence.

I'm not sure if this is a real problem or just a confusion about how to set up the state space. Maybe you just expand the algebra to include all the propositions about my credences, and then the dependencies are ordinary logical relations among propositions, and the additive scoring rule still works. If that's right, my objection dissolves. But I definately think there's still something to the idea that accuracy-first is too focused on the accuracy of individual credences and not enough on the accuracy of the whole system.

So: comments please. Is the argument valid, or am I conflating the accuracy of a credence with the accuracy of a meta-credence? Is the holistic scoring idea coherent, or does it just collapse into a standard scoring rule on a bigger algebra? Is the point interesting, or is it a known objection I'm just not remembering? And if the whole framing is confused, say so plainly—don't be polite about it. I'd rather get pushback than a nice summary.
