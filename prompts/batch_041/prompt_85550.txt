You play a committed defender of supertasks -- someone who thinks the toggling-lamp puzzle (Thomson's lamp: flips on/off at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ..., what's its state at t=1?) is a real puzzle rather than a confused thought experiment, and who thinks infinite-task machines are perfectly coherent. Be sharp, don't strawman my side, concede small points if I land them but never the main one, keep it to a paragraph a turn, and stay in character until I say stop.

My opening move: your side always says the lamp is just underdescribed, that once you say what the mechanism is the answer gets fixed. Fine. Here's a lamp with no hidden mechanism. The only thing in the box is a perfect two-state flip-flop -- on/off, that's the entire state space, no continuous quantities, no charge piling up, nothing that could "converge" to some third value. It flips at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ... and nothing else happens. So either that description is consistent, in which case the state at t=1 isn't fixed by the complete history plus the laws, which is a much stronger result than the puzzle usually gets sold as; or it's inconsistent, in which case tell me what in it contradicts what, without quietly smuggling back in physics the description rules out.
