For the last several months a historian friend and I have been circling the same argument — whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler — and it has become clear that we are not really disagreeing about the facts but about what kind of thing a cause is. I would like you to take both sides of the Great Man versus structural-explanation debate and restate them as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use. I am a biostatistician who works on causal inference, so my working tools are potential outcomes, directed acyclic graphs, and the do-calculus; I think in terms of unit-level versus population-level effects, necessary and sufficient causes, the sufficient-component cause picture in which each named cause is only one slice of a pie that yields the outcome when the whole pie is assembled, overdetermination and competing risks, and probabilistic attribution of the kind where you compare the actual world against a world in which a given exposure has been removed and ask how much of the outcome depended on it. Take the structuralist claim that Hitler was a replaceable product of deeper economic and political conditions and the Great Man claim that the individual was a necessary, non-substitutable cause, and render each as a claim about causes in that sense.

Then give me the residue report, and make that the honest part. Is the question of whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler even well-posed in potential-outcomes terms, or is the target of the question not a cause in my framework's sense at all — and if so, is the gap just missing counterfactual data or a genuine mismatch of concepts? Tell me where my vocabulary is quietly smuggling in assumptions (that causes are manipulable, that units are well-defined, that there is a single well-specified intervention) and where the historians' disagreement is really less substantive than it sounds once it is forced into this mold. I do not want the anodyne answer that both factors mattered; I want to know which parts of each position my framework can represent, which it distorts to fit, and which it simply cannot say.
