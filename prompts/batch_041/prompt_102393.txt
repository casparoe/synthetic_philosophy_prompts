I am assembling a framework for evaluating theories that treat the self as a centre of narrative gravity, and I want the specification before I look at any particular theory. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory in this area must satisfy: the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the constraints it must respect. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. I am especially interested in requirements drawn from less familiar corners: the difference between narrative coherence and narrative ownership, where a story can hang together and yet be experienced as alien; the possibility of narrative revision that is felt as discovery rather than invention; and the way institutional narratives—clinical, legal, carceral—can be imposed on a person and later endorsed from the inside. The theory should also say what happens at the boundaries: depersonalisation, dissociation, confabulation, dementia, pre-linguistic infancy, and non-human animals. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement rules out the most otherwise attractive theories. Keep the focus on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring named contenders.
