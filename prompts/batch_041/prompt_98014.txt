My neighbour Ed is a hobby woodworker, and last weekend he showed me a little cube he'd turned on his lathe. He says the machine sets the side length to some value between 1 and 2 inches, he won't tell me where, and — his claim — no value in that range is more likely than any other. Then he offers me a bet: is the side longer or shorter than 1.5 inches? Even odds, I say, sure. Then he grins and offers a second bet: is the volume bigger or smaller than 3.375 cubic inches? That's the same question, I say. But his says no — if I'm really ignorant of the setting, then by the same "no reason to prefer any value over any other" logic I should also be at even odds on the volume being above or below the middle of its possible range, and 3.375 isn't the middle of the range of possible volumes, so the odds come out different. We went back and forth until we were both confused, and now I can't tell who's right or even what the right way to think about it is. (I spent thirty years measuring things in a lab and thought I knew what "equally likely" meant, but I've only been reading philosophy for a year now that I'm retired, so go easy on me.) Should I take his bets, and if so, which one?
