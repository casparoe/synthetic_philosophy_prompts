So I've been betting on horses and football games for about 15 years now, never went to college, but I keep a serious spreadsheet and I think in a pretty specific language: EV, edge, the vig, the takeout, variance, bankroll, tilt, and closing line value. I trust that language more than I trust most things. Its how I decide whats a good bet and whats me being a sucker.

Lately I've been stuck on voting, which I do every time, and I cant make it fit the spreadsheet. My vote has never decided anything and realistically never will. I looked the number up once and for a presidential race it was something like one in ten million, maybe worse, dont hold me to it. And yet I vote, and I get genuinely annoyed at people who dont, and I cant tell if I'm the mark at the table.

Heres what I want. Take the whole why-bother-voting argument and every standard comeback to it — the one where the stakes are so enormous that even a one-in-ten-million shot is worth thousands in EV, the one where its not about the outcome at all but about expression or duty or solidarity or being counted, the one where if everyone reasoned like me the whole thing collapses, plus whatever else is in that debate that I dont know about — and restate each one in my language. Odds, stake, payout, takeout, the other side of the bet, the long run, results-oriented thinking versus judging the decision by the closing line. Do it faithfully, one at a time, not as a cute analogy. If an argument cant be said in my terms, say that instead of fudging it.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Which claims go through clean. Which only go through if you bend them, and exactly where the bend is. What has no home in my vocabulary at all. Some things I already suspect are the real meat:

- In betting the payout is mine and the other guy pays it. Here, if I'm the deciding vote, the payoff goes to everybody, including people who stayed home. My spreadsheet has no column for that, and calling it my payoff feels like cheating in my own terms.
- My vocabulary has no way to say what if everyone did that. Every bet gets priced on its own, one at a time. Voting might not be that kind of thing.
- When someone says they vote out of duty, my language can only hear I get a warm feeling, which turns a duty into a preference. Is that a translation, or is it a demolition?
- In betting I grade myself on whether I beat the closing line, not on whether the bet won. Is the its-about-expression answer just that, said worse? Or is it something my version of it genuinely misses?
- The size thing: my local school board race got decided by about 40 votes out of a few thousand people. Thats a completely different bet from the presidential one. Does the theory just say local races are better bets, or is there something in it about why the big one still feels like the one that counts?

And give me your call on the residue. Is what doesnt survive a hole in my framework — something about collective action or meaning that a betting mindset literally cannot represent — or is it a sign the philosophers are being vague and my framework is exposing that? Dont flatter my vocabulary and dont flatter theirs. If its my side thats broken, tell me what it would take to fix it. And skip the reading lists, I'm not gonna read papers.
