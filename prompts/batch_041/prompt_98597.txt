I'm putting together a reading-group handout. Two friends of mine have been argueing in circles for months about whether we owe our compatriots more than we owe strangers: one's a patriotic partialist who thinks co-nationality grounds genuine special obligations, the other's a cosmopolitan who thinks any partiality toward compatriots has to be justified from the ground up by what it does for everyone. Write the joint statement they could both actually put their names to after a proper good-faith collaboration.

I want: a numbered section of claims they both accept -- about what a "special claim" even is (a permission to favour, a duty to favour, or extra weight?), about what could make partiality permissible in the first place, and about whether the ground is the co-national relationship itself, reciprocity in a shared scheme of cooperation, or something else entirely. Then a disagreements table, each row phrased so that the side it belongs to would accept the wording of its own view. Then the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling them would move at least one side.

Make them work through at least these cases and say, for each, which crux it bears on and what each side predicts: (a) two co-nationals in a collapsed state where the shared institutions have basically gone; (b) a long-term resident who shares every institution but not the nationality; (c) a closed colony or generation ship, where everyone shares a scheme and a common fate but there's no nation and no shared history; and (d) a pair who share a nationality and nothing else. And if you think part of their fight is really just verbal, say which bit and why.

Don't worry about citations or who said what, I'll fill that in. Finally, tell me how confident you are that these formulations are ones each side would actually sign, and single out the one you're least sure about.
