Write a joke about the St. Petersburg paradox. I want you to use this setup: a casino runs the game — a fair coin flipped until it lands heads, paying 2^n if the game ends on flip n — but it pays winners in its own scrip, and the scrip's only backing is the casino's promise to let holders buy into further rounds of the same game, so the casino's bankroll is exactly the scrip outstanding, and the value of the payout depends on the value of the currency, which depends on the value of the payout. The joke should turn on the actual structure of the paradox rather than on the general weirdness of infinity: on the gap between the infinite expected value and the fact that the payout is 2 half the time, on the two standard responses (a finite bankroll, diminishing marginal utility), and on what the scrip arrangement does to both of them. Someone who knows the paradox should be able to tell from the joke that its author does too, so a punchline that would work just as well for any divergent series counts as a failure. Keep it short, and don't explain it afterwards.
