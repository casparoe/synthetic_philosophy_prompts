I am preparing a recorded interview for a graduate student paper series with a philosopher who defends a hybrid theory of punishment: retributivist desert fixes a range of proportionate sentences, and consequentialist considerations such as deterrence, incapacitation, and rehabilitation select the sentence actually imposed within that range. I need eight to ten questions that push past a restatement of the position, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give. At least one question should press him on why desert, if it only bounds a sentence rather than setting it, amounts to more than a side constraint that a careful consequentialist could accept, with a follow-up for his likely reply that desert marks the outer limit of what a person may be made to suffer for a wrong. Another should ask what justifies the institution of punishment in the first place on his account, given that retributivists and consequentialists typically disagree about whether a justifying aim is even available. Include a question about cases in which the consequentialist optimum falls below the floor of the desert range, so that the theory would require punishing more severely than any forward-looking aim recommends, and ask whether he accepts that implication. Add one about offenses for which no proportionate desert range can be specified, and one that distinguishes his view from a purely consequentialist system that imposes upper limits out of respect for rights. Please write in American English.
