I'm writing a short paper on the Judy Benjamin problem and I'd like your comments on the main argument before I commit to it. The problem, in outline, is this: Judy is dropped into a territory divided into Red and Blue, and into Second Company and Headquarters; she initially assigns equal credence to each of the four quadrants. She then receives a radio message that tells her that, conditional on being in Red, her probability of being in Second Company is 1/4. The standard worry is that no update rule — Jeffrey conditionalization, minimum relative entropy, and so on — handles this case in a way that is both well-motivated and intuitive; in particular, many rules shift her credence in Red, even though the message seems to be about a conditional probability given Red and thus should not, on the face of it, tell her anything about whether she is in Red.

My argument is that this entire framing is a mistake, because the message should be interpreted not as a constraint on the conditional probability of Second Company given Red but as a constraint on a conditional whose antecedent is a supposition rather than an event. Once we treat the message as expressing a suppositional conditional — roughly, the thought that, were she to learn that she is in Red, her credence in Second Company would be 1/4 — the update is straightforward: we simply conditionalize on the proposition that this suppositional conditional holds, and the apparent counterexamples to the standard rules dissolve.

I want you to tell me whether this move is sound, whether it really solves the problem or merely relabels it, and whether there are any hidden costs — for instance, whether it commits me to an implausible semantics for the radio message or to a non-standard account of suppositional credence. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
