I'm in a metaethics seminar and we've been circling the wrong kind of reasons problem for what feels like forever, and I think I've finally got a view of my own that I want you to try to wreck. Here's the view. The reason a demon's threat gives you to admire him -- or fear him, or believe some awful thing -- is not a reason for the attitude at all. It's a reason to want the attitude, or to bring it about that you have it. So let me call reasons that genuinely bear on an attitude "attitude-directed" and the rest "attitude-adjacent." My claim is that once you sort them this way the whole puzzle just dissapears: fitting-attitude analyses of value come out safe, because the only reasons that make an attitude correct are attitude-directed ones, and the demon's threat is attitude-adjacent more or less by definition. I know that sounds like I'm cheating. A friend in the seminar says it's just the old "reasons for attitudes vs. reasons for actions" move dressed up, but I don't think that's quite it, so here's the test I've been using: a reason is attitude-directed just in case you can cite it in answer to the question "why is it fitting or correct to have this attitude?" without mentioning the attitude itself or its effects.

What I actually want from you is the map of what would count against this. Not a survey of who said what -- I'll find the citations myself -- but: which cases would I, as a defender, have to concede are genuinely hard? What does my view predict that the obvious rivals don't (the rival that says there are just two kinds of correctness, the rival that says the right/wrong-kind distinction is primitive and undefinable, the rival that says wrong-kind reasons are really reasons for a different attitude)? And is there a case where my line gets drawn in the wrong place -- a reason that looks attitude-adjacent but that we'd all agree makes the attitude correct, or an attitude-directed reason that we'd agree doesn't? Rank the defeators from "this kills the view" to "awkward but survivable," and be honest about the big one: have I just relabeled right-kind reasons "attitude-directed" and wrong-kind reasons "attitude-adjacent" in a way that makes the whole thing circular and unfalsifiable? If so, tell me whether that's fatal or just the price of a view like this.
