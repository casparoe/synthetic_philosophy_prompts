Bramble & Wexler Cryogenics is a mid-sized manufacturer of dilution refrigerators and a supplier of liquid helium to hospitals, physics laboratories, and quantum-computing firms. Its board has adopted a policy of ontological inclusion: the firm proceeds on the assumption that experience is not confined to animals but is present, in some form, wherever there is a physical process, and that at least some of that experience is valenced. It therefore files an annual Ontological Impact Statement under an invented disclosure standard.

Write an extract from that statement, of roughly 900 words, in four labelled parts: the foreword by the firm's Chief Ontological Officer; the materiality determination, showing the threshold arithmetic; the independent assurance statement; and an excerpt from the investor question-and-answer session, including one question from an invented advocacy group and the firm's reply.

Use the following invented standard consistently, and make its rules reconstructible from the document alone:

- The standard is the Ontological Materiality Standard, fourth edition, issued by the International Board on Ontological Materiality. Both are fictional; name no real standards body, regulator, company, or rating agency.
- The reported quantity is valenced experience generated within the reporting boundary, denominated in millivalences per megajoule of process energy.
- A source is reportable only if its experience belongs to an integrated subject, defined as a process whose experiential states are bound into a single point of view lasting at least one second.
- There are three scopes: Scope 1, experience generated by the firm's own plant, including the vast numbers of low-energy events in its liquefaction trains; Scope 2, experience embodied in purchased electricity and helium; and Scope 3, upstream, downstream, and deferred experience, including experience caused by the firm's products after sale and by what those products will enable, such as decoherence events in customers' superconducting qubits.
- A materiality threshold of ten thousand millivalences per year, below which a source need be neither reported nor offset.
- Offsets are Restorative Ontological Credits, one credit being one millivalence-year of positive valence from a verified project.
- Assurance is limited for Scopes 1 and 2 and absent for Scope 3, on the ground of measurement uncertainty arising from the subject-summing problem.

The piece should be deadpan from first line to last: no character may remark that any of this is strange, and there should be no narrator's aside and no wink. The comedy must come from the philosophy rather than from the premise alone; a reader who works on these questions should be able to tell that the author does too, and should find the document funny rather than embarrassing. Nobody should dispute the metaphysics on the page — the firm has settled it by policy, and that settlement is part of the joke.

Without ever labelling them as such, the document must engage: the combination problem, and why the integrated-subject clause is where the whole ledger's credibility rests; the possibility that experience but not valence is fundamental, in which case the ledger has no units at all, a collapse the firm should fail to notice; whether suffering requires temporal unity, so that momentary micro-subjects might have something it is like to be them without having a welfare; cosmopsychism, on which there is one subject and one ledger, so that local offsets are incoherent; the asymmetry between false positives and false negatives under a precautionary approach, handled in a way that sounds principled and is quietly self-serving; and the epistemic asymmetry between one's own experience and the experience one infers elsewhere, which should force an enormous confidence interval while the reported figures stay absurdly precise.

Include at least one inconsistency that a careful reader can catch: a criterion the firm uses to exclude its own operations that, applied consistently, would also exclude the sources it pays to offset. Let at least one number's apparent precision visibly fail under the document's own pressure. No citations, no bibliography, and no real quotations; the dissenting voice, if you include one, must be invented or unnamed and should press the firm's figures rather than the metaphysics.
