ok so my reading group just wrapped a term on knowledge of our own minds and i want to close the last session with a poem instead of a summary. the two things that stuck with me: the KK principle (if you know p, do you know that you know p — whether knowing even iterates), and the anti-luminosity argument, which as i understand it isnt the boring we're-bad-at-introspection point but a margin-of-error point — any condition that comes in degrees (feeling cold, being in a position to know) tolerates nearby cases, so if you're only just on the knowing side of the line you cant be in a position to know you're on it, because the cases right next to yours are ones where it fails. so no state is luminous.

i want a pantoum about this, maybe 5 or 6 quatrains. the interlocking repeats should be the i-know-that-i-know regress — the form doing the iteration rather than just describing it — and the loop should be the thing that fails to ground anything, not a fact the poem tells us it fails to ground. two constraints: (1) the repeated lines shouldnt come back word-for-word, shift a word on each return so the reader cant quite point to the case where knowing tips into not-knowing; (2) keep KK and luminosity distinct, i dont want them smeared into one vague can-we-ever-be-sure-of-anything poem. also give the luminous claim its best, most sympathetic line early on so the poem has something to lose, and dont let it resolve into either skepticism or a reassuring pat on the head — leave the margin open at the end.
