I am a novelist, and the institution at the center of my current book is one I have invented; I need its philosophy straightened out before I can write a scene inside it, because I keep catching the place describing itself in one vocabulary and running on another.

The institution is a residential academy for adolescents, run on frankly benevolent lines. No door locks and no one is confined; a resident may walk out whenever she likes. Each wears a band that records location, pulse, sleep, and the volume of her speech, and the readings stream to a public wall display that ranks every resident by a single index the staff call composure. Every evening each resident writes a private reflection; at morning assembly a synthesized voice reads it aloud, and the resident is expected to affirm it. Residents may flag one another anonymously for anything that "lowers the room," and a council settles the flags without ever naming the flagger. Advancement through the academy's levels turns on the composure index. The prospectus insists the index does not judge the resident but discloses the self she already is, and that the academy frees its students by making them legible to themselves. Leaving forfeits every level and is entered in the record.

What I want is the theory this place presupposes. Reconstruct, as tightly as you can, the view of power and of knowledge that its practices only make sense under: whether power here is the kind that must confine and punish or the kind that produces and shapes; whether knowledge of a person is a neutral reading of a settled fact or is itself part of what makes the person; whether the subject being governed is free or determined; and what the academy must assume about the interior life it claims to reveal. Then separate the parts that strictly require that theory from the parts that merely sit comfortably beside it — I suspect the whole apparatus could be run, in outline at least, on a rival view, and I want to know where it is underdetermined. Mark the places where the prospectus and the mechanics contradict each other, and say what the institution would have to surrender if its presupposed view were replaced by the one on which power is essentially repressive and the self is finished before any institution touches it. I have been told the place is Foucault with the serial numbers filed off, but the label is not what I need and I do not want a reading list or citations; I want the implicit theory stated plainly enough that I can put it in the mouth of a character who half understands it.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the single sentence that says what the place presupposes — then give the reasoning. No preamble, and do not restate my question.
