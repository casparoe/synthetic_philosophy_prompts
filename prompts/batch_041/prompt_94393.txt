I sit on the standards committee for one of those farm-certification schemes — the little logo on the egg carton — and we're being dragged into an argument about insects. We currently cover vertebrates only. Now the black soldier fly and cricket producers are knocking on the door because the retailers want a label, and two of our members want us to go further and say something about shrimp and about wild insects killed in pest control. Our consultant handed us a formula in a slide deck and left. Roughly: expected annual welfare loss = number of individuals × probability they're sentient × how bad it is per individual, benchmarked against a broiler chicken. We voted to adopt it "in principle" and then discovered nobody on the committee can actually run it.

So: write me a decision procedure my committee can run, on a quarterly agenda slot of about 45 minutes, no philosopher in the room, most of us vets and producers. Questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it has to hand the call to the board vote because it's values and not facts.

Two things it has to handle honestly. First, the probability step. Tell us what evidence a vet can actually gather and assess — the anatomy, the behavioural trade-offs, the pharmacology, whatever you think belongs on the list — and say what yes, no, and unclear look like, rather than "consult the literature." Second, the arithmetic. Multiplying a small probability by an absurd number is where this thing gets weird, and the numbers here really are absurd, billions or trillions depending on who's counting. I want the procedure to say plainly when the multiplication has stopped being informative and started being a way of smuggling in a moral weight nobody agreed to, and to flag that as a stopping point instead of a result.

Run it on three: (1) black soldier fly larvae in a mass-rearing shed, killed by heat or maceration; (2) crickets, which is where the retailers are pushing hardest; (3) a proposal from our chair to extend the no-avoidable-suffering clause to wild insects killed by agricultural pest control, where our leverage is basically zero but the headcount is astronomical. At least one of these should come out in a way the committee won't like. Don't soften that one — say which case bites and why.

Constraints: anything that costs producers money needs a one-page justification they can't lawyer us out of, and the board signs off. Finish by telling me what this procedure can't do, so I can say that out loud at the meeting before someone else does.
