Concretely, take three rules for a credence c in a proposition that's either true or false. Quadratic (Brier): score 1-(1-c)^2 if true, 1-c^2 if false. Log: score log(c) if true, log(1-c) if false. Spherical: score c/sqrt(c^2+(1-c)^2) if true, (1-c)/sqrt(c^2+(1-c)^2) if false. All three are strictly proper. But they disagree about basically everything else that matters. Brier and spherical are bounded and treat the two directions of error symmetrically -- being dead certain of a truth and dead certain of a falsehood cost you the same. Log isn't: certainty in a falsehood is infinitely bad while certainty in a truth is merely optimal, and nothing is ever bounded. So "properness" alone doesn't fix how bad error is, or whether the two ways of being wrong are symmetric, or whether you're ever allowed to be certain at all.
