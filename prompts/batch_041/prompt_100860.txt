I grew up between Rotterdam and Osaka and I've been thinking about this alot. My Dutch side says just tell people the truth, my Japanese side says there are ways to tell the truth that don't make the other person lose face, and I can't definately tell if that's a real moral difference or just two ways of doing the same thing. Write a dialogue between two people who disagree about white lies: one thinks a polite falsehood is still a lie and lies corrode trust, the other thinks a polite falsehood both people know is false is not a lie at all but a ritual, like saying you're fine when you're not. Let them fight about concrete cases—a friend's terrible cooking, a coworker who smells, a gift you hate—and about whether directness is respect or just dumping your discomfort on someone else. One of them should have this idea that every white lie is a loan against future trust, and the other should push back that that only works if you think all talk is information transfer. Both should be smart and actually answer each other, not strawmen.
