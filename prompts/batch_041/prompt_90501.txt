My two flatmates got into a long arguement at the weekend about whether you could survive having your brain slowly swapped out for computer chips, one neuron at a time, and I ended up trying to referee and made a mess of it. Can you sort out who's right about what?

Nadia's line: if the swap is done one cell at a time, and you carry on behaving the same, remembering everything, feeling like the same person, then it's you at the end. If nobody, including you, could ever spot the difference, nothing has really been lost. Callum's line: you wouldn't notice being replaced by a perfect copy either, so 'you wouldn't notice' proves nothing. What keeps you you isn't the pattern, it's that the same living thing carried on without a break.

Nadia came back with this: fine, then do it one cell at a time but freeze the brain solid for a thousand years between each swap. Still one at a time, still gradual. If that's survival, then the gaps and the speed don't matter, so doing it all at once should be fine too. If that's death, then it was never the one-at-a-time that was doing the work, it was the uninterrupted running — and Callum owes me an explanation of why sleeping every night isn't death. Callum said sleep isn't a break because the brain is still there, still organised. Nadia said then it's the organisation that matters, and organisation can be copied, so a copy should count as you as well.

Then Callum changed tack and said maybe there's no fact of the matter here — that 'the same person' is a label we stick on things for practical reasons (who gets the house, who goes to prison), so we can just decide the chip version counts. Nadia said that can't be right, because if it's just a decision then we could decide the copy in the next room counts too, and then there'd be two of you, and you can't be in two places at once.

Can you go through this move by move and tell me who's right at each step? Please don't just lay out the options — take a side, stick your neck out, and tell me what each of them is missing. I realy can't tell. British English, please.
