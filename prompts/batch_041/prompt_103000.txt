I never went to college, I drive a truck for a living, and this is the thing I chew on during long hauls. I've ended up believeing something that makes people at the bar uncomfortable: that in a democracy, how much your vote counts should depend on how much you actually know about the specific question being voted on. Not the old literacy test thing, which was obviously just a way to keep people out. Everyone still votes, nobody gets barred from anything. But the weight of your vote on a given question scales with whether you can show you actually understand that question. Ballot measure about transmission lines and battery storage: the grid engineer's vote counts for more than mine. Measure about closing an elementary school: the parents and the teachers count for more than the engineer. Bond measure for sewer laterals that nobody understands: it's basically one person one vote again. I think this produces better decisions, and I don't think it's undemocratic, because I don't buy that better and more democratic pull apart the way people assume. A decision made on bad facts fails the people it's supposed to serve, it doesn't honor them.

What I want from you is not the standard objections read out loud at me. I want to know which ones actually land. Sort them: which would genuinely sink the view, which are just engineering problems I could build around, and which are noise that sounds serious but isn't. Give me the ones people like me dont see coming.

I also want the stuff my view is committed to that I might not have noticed, even if I won't like it. If the same logic runs through juries, through who gets a say in their own medical care, through parents versus kids, through whether a community gets to decide its own future when it's badly wrong about what its options are, then say it straight. Don't sand it down to spare my feelings.

And tell me what my view predicts that plain one person one vote doesn't. If I'm right about this, what should we actually see that we don't see now? I want something I could point to, not just a nicer feeling about how things would go.

One thing I keep going back and forth on. I split competence into two kinds: knowing what will happen, and knowing what it's like to live with the result. I only weight the first kind. But alot of decisions are really about the second. If the second kind of knowing is spread out completely differently across people, and maybe can't be tested at all, does my whole proposal fall apart, or does it just get harder in a way I could live with?

Last thing. A friend says my view is really just rule by experts with extra steps and I can't tell if that's a gotcha or just a description. If it does collapse into that, where exactly does the collapse happen, and is collapsing into rule by experts a problem for me on my own terms or only on his? And if it turns out the only way to keep my view alive is to keep bolting on exceptions, say so, and then tell me whether that's a real problem or just what a view like this has to look like.
