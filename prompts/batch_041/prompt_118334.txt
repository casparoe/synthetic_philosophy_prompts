Engels gets a look at the present for a day. My reading group keeps re-fighting surrogacy: Germany and France ban it outright, the UK allows it only unpaid (expenses, and the deal isn't enforceable), California has a functioning market with enforceable contracts and paid egg donors. Half of us say the bans are the feminst position, half say they just push the trade offshore. Which side does the Engels of the Origin of the Family land on, and more to the point, what's the rule doing the work.

Three things I actually want. (1) Does his framework even have the distinction between selling nine months of your labour power and selling the child or the parental rights at the end of it, or does it flatten the two? If it flattens them, is that a feature or a gap? (2) There's a jurisdiction drafting what I think is the real test case: payment allowed but capped and paid out by a state agency instead of the intended parents; the surrogate can withdraw any time up to birth and keep the baby, no penalty; intended parents can't demand termination or selective reduction; egg donors get a flat fee with no trait selection. Real third option, fig leaf over the same market, or the closest thing to what he'd want? (3) Is the wet-nursing trade the right analogy, or does he file this under domestic service, or under prostitution?

Then say where you had to lean hardest on his commitments to get an answer at all -- that's where the extrapolation is doing the work rather than the texts -- plus what he'd have had to think about adoption or abortion for the verdict to flip, and one concrete fact that would change your mind.
