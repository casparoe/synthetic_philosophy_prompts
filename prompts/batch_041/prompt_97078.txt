I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind with a background in cognitive science, and I need to turn a half-formed idea into a one-page research proposal for a departmental fellowship — effectively a prospectus for the first substantive chapter of my dissertation. The idea concerns pain asymbolia, the condition in which damage to the brain (classically insular lesions, sometimes described as a disconnection between the insula and limbic structures) abolishes the aversive quality of painful stimuli while leaving the localization and intensity aspects intact: patients report that a stimulus hurts but that it doesn't bother them, and they show absent or inadequate withdrawal and emotional responses without any primary sensory deficit. In the literature this is standardly recruited as evidence for a "two-component" picture of pain, on which a sensory-discriminative component can be separated from a motivational-affective one. My worry is that the asymbolia data are being asked to settle a question they don't by themselves touch, because the argument slides between at least four things that "the badness of pain" might pick out: (i) the phenomenal quality of unpleasantness; (ii) the subject's dislike of the sensation; (iii) the sensation's functioning as a reason or an imperative to act; and (iv) the prudential badness of the experience for the subject. What I want is a proposal that pins down one determinate question rather than gesturing at "the badness of pain." My candidate is whether asymbolia supports a relational account on which pain is bad because it is disliked (so that removing the dislike removes the badness) over an intrinsic account on which unpleasantness is a feature of the sensory state itself, and which of (i)–(iv) the clinical data actually bear on. Please write the proposal so that it states that question precisely; explains why the leading intrinsic and attitudinal positions each leave it open rather than already answering it; specifies the method — what I would actually do over a term, including how I would handle the reliance on asymbolic self-report and how I would bring in neighboring dissociations for comparison (for instance, noxious stimulation that is registered without being felt, versus a felt sensation that is judged painful but not disliked); identifies what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing (for example, if the four-way distinction collapses under pressure, or if the reports turn out too thin to support any of the readings); and gives an honest assessment of whether this fits a single term. Keep it to 700 words or fewer, and don't include citations or a literature review — I'll supply those myself.
