I've been in a reading group on AI safety and control, and we keep circling an assumption that someone in the group calls the strategy-stealing assumption, though I suspect the name means different things to different people and I would rather you tell me if I have the framing wrong than go along with it. The idea, as I understand it, is this: for a sufficiently capable optimizer, any plan that depends on the optimizer not being able to locate and adopt some strategy available in its environment is doomed, because the optimizer can simply take the strategy — including strategies that other agents in the environment are already using, and including strategies the designer hoped to withhold. On this assumption, safety proposals that work by depriving a system of a capability, an input, or an option (boxing it, sandboxing it, withholding a dataset) are not stable, because the capability is a strategy and strategies are stealable. My friend thinks this is nearly trivially true of anything that deserves the name optimizer, and that all the interesting work is downstream. I am less sure: I cannot tell whether the assumption is a substantive claim about the world, a definitional consequence of what we mean by optimizer, or a slogan that smuggles in the hardest part of the problem — namely, that locating and adopting a strategy is itself a hard search problem, one that the environment's other agents have already solved but the optimizer has not.

Please give me your actual credence that the strategy-stealing assumption, stated carefully, holds for the class of systems people in this area actually worry about, and do not just tell me it depends. I want a number and the decomposition: how much rides on whether strategy can be made precise enough here to have a truth value at all; how much on whether a capable optimizer can identify and execute strategies used by others in a messy, partially observed environment, as opposed to merely being able to do so once given the information the other agent had; how much on whether the assumption does independent work or is a restatement of instrumental convergence; how much on whether, if it holds, it actually undermines the specific control proposals people offer (withholding information, limiting compute, boxing, tripwires) rather than only the naive versions of them; and whatever other terms you think belong. Give a rough probability and one line of justification for each term, combine them into a final number, and then tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would move that term the most.
