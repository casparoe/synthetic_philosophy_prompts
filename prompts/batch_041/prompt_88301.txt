I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about gradual replacement, and I want a number from you rather than a survey. The case: my neurons are swapped out one at a time, over months, each for a silicon device functionally identical to the neuron it replaces, with no break in my behaviour, memory, or reports of experience, until no biological tissue remains. My friend is sure the person at the end is me, and just as sure that if the identical end state were produced in one discontinuous jump on a single afternoon, the person who woke up would be a copy; I find both verdicts unstable and cannot tell whether that is insight or cowardice. Give me your credence that in the slow case the emergent individual is me in the sense needed for first-person anticipation, so that it is rational for me now to care about its future as I care about my own, and decompose that number. How much rests on whether personal identity consists in continuity of functional organization rather than biological continuity? On whether continuity comes in degrees and so admits no determinate threshold? On whether the end system is conscious at all, and whether a subject of experience can outlive the replacement of its substrate? On whether identity is even the relation that matters, given that the same process could as easily terminate in two of me? And on whether these intuitions track a fact rather than a limit of our imagination? Give a number and one line of justification per term, say how they combine, name the term you trust least, and tell me what argument or result would move it most. Then tell me where my friend and I most likely actually disagree.
