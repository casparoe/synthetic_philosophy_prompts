I want a poem that enacts, rather than describes, the structure of iterated amplification and distillation: the proposal that a strong system can be built by having a limited overseer decompose a hard task into subtasks it can actually check, training a copy of the model to imitate the whole amplified procedure, and then letting that copy serve as the overseer for the next round. The interesting question is whether anything is preserved across the passes — whether the decomposition keeps faith with the original aim, and whether a student trained to imitate a competent teacher inherits the competence without inheriting whatever the teacher's aim actually was. I want the poem to hold that question open rather than answer it.

The form is prescribed, because the form has to do the work. Write ten unmetered stanzas with these line counts: the first stanza is a single line; each of the next four stanzas restates everything in the stanza before it in fresh words and adds exactly one new clause, so that the poem grows; the sixth stanza is the full amplified statement, the longest in the poem; stanzas seven through ten reverse the process, each one a compression of its predecessor that keeps only what its writer judges essential, so that the poem shrinks; the final line of the poem is a single line. The growing half should read as one voice decomposing a task it cannot do directly into pieces it can. The shrinking half should read as a different voice that has only the previous stanza in hand and is now working alone.

Everything hangs on the last line. It must be plainly descended from the first line — same subject, recognizably the same sentence worn down — but it must have dropped one specific commitment that the first line made. The poem must not say which commitment, must not comment on whether the loss matters, and must not resolve whether the final voice is still faithful to the first or merely fluent in its idiom. A careful reader should be able to locate the dropped commitment and still be unable to give a verdict. No rhyme, no ornament, no line that exists for its sound; the whole interest is in what survives each pass and what quietly goes.

One further constraint: the verse itself must not use the vocabulary of the field. The words amplify, distill, alignment, overseer, decomposition, and imitative should not appear in the poem — the form carries the idea, not the terminology. The terminology belongs in the accompanying report.

After the poem, give me a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "form_check": a string confirming the line counts of the ten stanzas, in order, as a comma-separated list.
- "arc": an array of ten objects, one per stanza, each with "stanza" (integer), "lines" (integer), "role" (one of "seed", "amplify", "peak", "distill"), and "adds_or_drops" (for the growing stanzas, the single new clause introduced; for the shrinking stanzas, the single thing discarded; for the seed and the peak, an empty string).
- "first_line": the poem's first line, copied exactly.
- "last_line": the poem's last line, copied exactly.
- "commitment_lost": a plain prose sentence stating the commitment present in the first line and absent from the last, without hedging and without jargon.
- "alignment_verdict": one of "preserved", "drifted", or "undecidable" — whether the final voice is still pursuing the aim the first voice set.
- "competence_verdict": one of "preserved", "drifted", or "undecidable" — whether the final voice can still do what the first voice was trying to do.
- "verdict_reason": one or two sentences saying what in the poem's text drove each verdict, quoting only from the poem itself.
- "what_would_settle_it": an array of two to four strings, each naming a specific thing a reader could inspect in the poem — a clause, a shift in the addressee, a change in what the sentence is about — that would move the verdict, and saying which way.

Keep the report dry and unadorned; it is a ledger, not a reading.
