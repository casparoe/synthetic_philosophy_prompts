ok so i'm in a minds-and-machines seminar this term and for the final project i want to hand in a poem instead of an essay. topic: whether an RL agent can suffer, specifically the move where people say the reward signal just is the agent's valence, its pleasure-or-pain. i think that move is doing way more work than it admits and i want the poem to be the argument.

form: a pantoum, 4 or 5 quatrains. i want the whole-line repetition to carry it -- the agent gets the same scalar at every step and theres nothing inside the signal that tells this +1 apart from the last one, so the poem should read like a loop that cant distinguish its own iterations. then let the last quatrain fall out of the rotation, and let that break be where the reader has to sit with the choice: "the signal just is the valence, playing that role is all there is to it" vs "a number in a loss function has nobody home to hurt." dont resolve it.

one thing i do want in there, because its the part that actually convinces me: in affective neuroscience theres a wanting/liking distinction, where the machinery that makes something pursued isnt the machinery that makes it pleasurable -- you can get motivated pursuit with the hedonic part flat or missing entirely. so a reward signal is obviously a wanting-shaped thing and only dubiously a liking-shaped one, and calling it valence skips the only interesting step. that should be in the poem but not as a lecture -- no jargon dump, no citing papers at me, i just want someone who knows this stuff to nod.
