I work in a hospital and I sit on a comittee that picks the numbers our departments get judged on, and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a name for. I'm not even sure it's one pattern or three wearing the same coat.

We adopt a measure — say, how long people wait for a scan. Year one it tracks the thing we actually care about pretty well. Year five it doesn't, and everyone has a different story about why. The nurses say people are fudging it (some are, a bit, in small ways). The analysts say of course it stopped working: we now use it to decide which departments get money, so we're staring hard at the top of the distribution, and the top of a noisy number is mostly noise — nobody has to be cheating for that to happen. And the old hands say the thing itself has moved underneath us: staff now train to the measure, so being good at the measure and being good at the job have quietly become the same thing, and nobody can point to the meeting where that was decided.

I half remember some line about a measure that becomes a target not being a good measure anymore, but that only seems to cover the first story. Is there a name for the whole syndrome, or are these three seperate things that get bundled because from the outside, in hindsight, they look identical? I want the boundaries, not just a label: where does one stop and the next start, can you ever tell them apart in a single case, and does it matter which one you've got — because the fixes I can think of for one seem to make the others worse. Also, if the thing itself has shifted to fit the measure, in what sense is the measure still wrong about it? That one keeps bothering me. And tell me what would change your mind — what would you have to see to decide this really is just the old target-and-measure thing wearing three hats?
