I have just finished a year's work as the fact-checker for a nine-hundred-page reference volume, and last month I was deposed in a libel suit against the publisher. Counsel read out the volume's 240 substantive claims one at a time and asked, of each, whether it was true; I answered yes to all 240. He then asked whether every claim in the volume was true, and I said no — that I was confident the book contained at least one error, though I could not tell him which. When he pointed out that one of my previous answers must therefore have been false, I said I was aware of it and would give the same answers again. I have not been able to put the exchange out of my mind. I do not believe I lied and I do not believe I was merely confused, but I cannot give a clean account of why, and the two colleagues I have discussed it with have taken opposite sides.

I would like to argue this out with a real opponent rather than a straw man. Please play a committed defender of the principle that knowledge carries across conjunction — that whoever knows each of two things knows their combination — and who therefore regards my testimony as flatly inconsistent rather than as a delicate report of two different grades of confidence. Give him the strongest version of the position: he may insist on the difference between knowing and being almost certain, he may freely grant that my conduct in the deposition was reasonable or even admirable, and he may allow that ordinary speech is loose, but he must not allow that both of my answers can be true. He should be courteous, precise, and difficult to move; he should argue rather than appeal to authorities; and he should stay in character until I say 'stand down'. One or two paragraphs per reply, please, so that it feels like an exchange rather than a lecture.

There is one further wrinkle, and it is the part I most want to see answered. The volume's preface carries a sentence disclaiming any pretense of perfect accuracy, and that sentence is itself item 240 among the claims I affirmed under oath. So my opening move is this: I know every claim in that volume to be true, and I know that the volume taken as a whole is not true, and I am prepared to defend both halves of that under oath. Tell me which of my answers you would retract, and what I should have said in its place, given that any retraction would have been a denial of something I had checked as carefully as I check anything I claim to know.
