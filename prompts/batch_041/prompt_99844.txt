Here's where I've ended up, and I'd like to know if it hangs together.

(a) Falsifiability is the mark of the scientific — a theory only earns the name if it rules something out.
(b) I've drifted towards thinking it's really a matter of degree rather than a line: a theory is more or less scientific depending on how far it sticks its neck out and how much it would cost to give up.
(c) I think the many-worlds reading of quantum mechanics, and the string landscape, are proper science, even though I'm told nothing we could ever do would rule them out.
(d) Astrology and the old Freudian stuff are pseudoscience, and for me the giveaway is that their defenders can always absorb any result after the fact.
(e) I'm a scientific realist — our best theories are at least roughly true.
(f) I think untestable-in-practice and untestable-in-principle are seperate beasts, and only the second should count against a theory.

I'm pretty sure this doesn't all hang together, but I honestly can't see where the crack is. Go through it: which bits are genuinely in tension and how bad each one is, which ones only look like a clash but aren't, and what the cheapest fix is for the real ones. Rank the real tensions by how much they'd force me to change my mind, and dont just tell me it's all fine. British English please.
