Okay, so I'm in a weird spot and I need an outside check before I present in a seminar. I'm Chinese-American, and after my grandfather's funeral this summer — all these obligations I'd never thought about, who bows to whom, who's responsible for what — I started reading up on Confucian role ethics, and I've basically talked myself into a position I can't tell is coherant anymore. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The self doesn't exist before or apart from its roles — daughter, sister, student, coworker — and those aren't hats I put on and take off, they constitute who I am. (2) So ethics is mainly about doing those roles well, rather than applying rules or maximizing outcomes; there's no neutral moral agent standing outside the relationships. (3) The family is the root — filial piety and sibling love are where moral competence is first learned, and everything else grows out of that soil. (4) But I also think some roles and some family traditions are genuinely rotten (I watched a cousin get eaten alive by the expectation to tank her career for her in-laws), and that a person can be obligated to refuse or push back on a role they're in. (5) And I think real self-transformation is possible — you can become a different, better person through learning and practice, not just a better-fitting cog. Also a friend in the seminar keeps insisting this whole thing is just virtue ethics with extra steps, that roles are only the social practices you need to be virtuous in, and I honestly don't know how to answer her.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad each tension is. I have a suspicion it's (1) and (4) that bite, but maybe it's (3) and (4), or something I'm not seeing at all. For every real tension, tell me what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs me — I don't want to just drop one of these, I want the least bad revision. And if two of them are secretly the same commitment wearing different clothes, say so. Please write it in American English.
