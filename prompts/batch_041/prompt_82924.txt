You are a philosopher who works on moral uncertainty, value conflict and the theory of compromise; please think carefully and reason from first principles rather than reaching for the nearest familiar position. I am a novelist, and the book I am writing turns on a marriage between two people whose moral outlooks are not merely differently weighted but genuinely incompatible — one holds that certain acts are absolutely forbidden, the other that any act may be justified by its consequences, and neither will be argued out of it. What I want is not a verdict on the marriage but a number: your credence that there are genuine gains from trade in moral compromise — that is, that two parties with incompatible value systems can reach an agreement which is better for each of them by their own lights, and better in a way that neither could have reached alone, without either having to abandon the value system that makes the agreement better for them.

Begin by setting out the sub-questions you think that credence actually rests on, then assign each a rough probability with a single line of justification. I would expect the list to include something like: whether a value system of that kind fixes enough of a ranking over outcomes for 'better by my own lights' to have a determinate answer at all; whether what survives radical incompatibility is compromise in a morally interesting sense or merely mutual forbearance; whether each party's own integrity constraints rule out precisely the trades that would otherwise be available; whether any gains are stable rather than self-undermining, or dependent on a third party to enforce them; and whether the 'gains from trade' picture is the right frame in the first place, or smuggles in a common currency that the parties do not share. Show me how the terms combine into the final number, and tell me which one the number is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most.

For what it is worth, my own credence is about 0.6. If yours differs materially, tell me which term we most probably disagree about.
