My two flatmates got into it last night about our third flatmate, Dan, who blew off his sister's surgery to go to a LAN party. He genuinely doesn't care about her, doesn't care about being the sort of person who shows up, doesn't feel guilt, nothing. Callie said Dan had no reason to go. Her line: a reason has to be able to move you, so run it through everything Dan actually cares about and nothing gets him to the hospital; saying he has a reason is just moralizing dressed up as a fact, or a threat with better manners, and the alternative is some spooky reason floating around unattached to any possible motive. Rosa said that's backwards — the fact that his sister was alone and terrified counts in favor of him being there whether or not it moves him, and that's exactly what's wrong with him: he's not a guy with no reason, he's a guy failing to respond to one. Callie said fine, but then what makes it a reason, other than that you and I happen to be moved by it? Rosa said that's like saying a proof isn't valid unless everyone follows it. Rosa also said Dan just isn't thinking straight, and if he saw it clearly he'd be moved. Callie said that move is the giveaway: your test for seeing it clearly turns out to be ending up agreeing with you, and deliberation from what someone already wants doesn't manufacture new wants — if he needs one, that's a conversion, not reasoning. Rosa's last word was that on Callie's view the only thing wrong with Dan is that he's inconvenient, since he isn't violating anything he has reason to care about.

Who's right about what here? Go move by move and rate each one from clearly mistaken to clearly right, then say what both of them are missing.
