ok so this is gonna sound random but in psych we did the Libet thing — you sit there, watch a clock with a dot sweeping around it, flex your wrist whenever you feel like it, and write down where the dot was the instant you felt the urge to move. Then they show you the EEG and this readiness potential starts building like a third of a second before the moment you wrote down. Which is the whole your-brain-decides-before-you-do thing everyone quotes at parties. But then our teacher mentioned a reanalysis where somebody argues the readiness potential might not be a decision signal at all — it might just be the motor cortex's background noise randomly drifting upward until it crosses some threshold and tips you into moving, in which case the signal is more like the last straw than the choice, and the ramp only looks like a deliberate buildup because they average a bunch of trials together. And I keep going back and forth on whether that gets us off the hook or makes it worse, because either way something in me is doing the thing before I am.

Anyway for creative writing I have to write a short story and I want it to be about this exact mess, and I want it to be a story for people who actually know the experiment, not the science-proves-free-will-is-dead, ooooh version. So: a girl our age volunteers as a paid subject in one of these studies (it's at the local uni, she does it for gas money or because her bio teacher hooked her up, whatever you want), and the meat of the story is her trying to game it — trying to move in a way the machine won't see coming, trying to veto, trying to catch herself in the act — and slowly realizing she can't tell anymore whether she's choosing to press the button or just noticing that she's about to. Two people in her life should be pulling her in different directions: someone who says the readiness potential is just threshold-crossing noise, so the experiment never showed anything about choice to begin with, and someone who says fine, but you still have the veto, you can still cancel a movement after the urge arrives — and I want the story to show that the second person's escape hatch has its own problem, like if the urge was never a decision, what exactly are you vetoing, and how would you know the veto itself wasn't just another threshold getting crossed. Also the experiment only works because subjects honestly report when they became aware, so at some point I want her to realize she's being asked to be the authority on her own awareness by an experiment that's supposed to be explaining that awareness, which is the part that actually keeps me up.

Please don't end it with her heroically proving free will or tragically losing it, and please don't make it a story about a machine that predicts crimes. I want it to end on an ordinary moment — she's in the chair, the dot's going around, she feels something and can't tell if its an urge or just noise — and let it sit there. Also the science should be right-ish, like get the clock and the reporting and the timing of the signal roughly correct, because my teacher will read this. And if you can work in that the readiness potential is averaged over tons of trials, that'd be cool, cause I think thats the bit everyone forgets. Deadline's friday lol.
