My book club is doing The Reader next month and I somehow got picked to lead the discussion. I've been chewing on Jaspers' idea that guilt comes in different layers -- criminal, political, moral, and the weird metaphysical one -- and I want to read the book through that. The thing I can't get past is that Michael feels guilty for something he didn't do, while Hanna seems guilty for something she won't say, and the trial turns it all into law. Does the story show that the second generation inherits a guilt that isn't really theirs, or does it show them hiding behind Hanna's illiteracy so they don't have to feel the political kind? I want a reading that takes the novel seriously and doesn't just conclude that collective guilt is bad. Tell me which scenes matter and where Jaspers' categories stop fitting. If the film changes the answer, say how.
