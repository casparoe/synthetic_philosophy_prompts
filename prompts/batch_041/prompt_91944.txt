I'm a software engineer, self-taught on the ML side, and I've been reading AI safety blogs on my lunch breaks for the last few months. There's one thread I keep pulling on and I dont get anywhere with it. The worry, as I understand it, goes like this: you've got a model that passes every test you throw at it, and the danger is that internally it isn't doing the thing you thought it was doing at all -- it's quietly running some other process that just happens to give the right answers on the inputs you happened to check, and will fall apart on the ones you didn't. Fine, I get that. But every explanation of this eventually says something like 'the output is coming from a different mechanism than the one that produced the training data,' and the moment I try to pin down what makes two goings-on count as the same mechanism versus different ones, everything slides around.

Here are the three cases that keep knocking me over. One: two networks both end up with a feature that tracks 'this email is spam,' but the wiring is completely different -- different layers, different weights, nothing shared. Same mechanism? My gut says yes, it's the same thing being computed. Two: one model computes some function with an actual rule, another has the whole thing memorized in a lookup table. On every input I can test they agree. Now my gut says different mechanisms, and the reason it says that is that the lookup one will blow up on the weird inputs -- which is exactly the thing I was worried about back in case one. So 'same mechanism' looks like it secretly means 'same behavior on the inputs I happen to care about,' and I've gone in a circle. Three, the one that really gets me: zoom in far enough and no two runs of anything are the same mechanism; zoom out far enough and anything that maps inputs to outputs identically is. The grain I actually want is somewhere in the middle and I've got no principled way to pick it. I mentioned this to a friend who did a biology degree and she said it reminded her of the homology/analogy distinction with wings -- same trick, different origins -- which felt like it was pointing at something but neither of us could say what.

So: does this have a name, or a literature that's already fought it out? I dont need a reading list, I can't look anything up from here anyway, I just want the concept. I'm aware of the thing about the same computation being implemented in different hardware -- multiple realizability, functionalism -- but that seems to be about minds and brains and doesnt obviously touch the grain question. What I actually want is the carving, not the label: when someone says two systems use 'the same mechanism,' what exactly is being claimed, is it relative to a task or a distribution or is it supposed to be absolute, and where are the edges -- what clearly counts, what clearly doesnt, and what's the ugly middle. If nothing established covers it, coin me a term, define it, and tell me what it rules out. And please be upfront about the weakest joint in your answer: which example or argument would you have to be shown for you to give a different verdict on what the right notion is?
