Okay, I'm writing a feature on where meaning actually lives, and I've ended up holding a position I'd like you to try to knock down. The position: a message on its own means nothing — the meaning is in the frame, i.e. the outer message that tells you how to take the inner one, plus whatever capacity the receiver has to recognize "this is meant as a message at all." It's the frame-message / inner-message thing from GEB, at least as I read it, and my extension of it — the part I'm least sure I've earned — is that the regress has to bottom out somewhere, and where it bottoms out is a convention-free frame-detector that any sufficiently general intelligence would share, which is why talking to an alien, or to a system trained on a corpus nothing like ours, is hard but not hopeless. So: what counts against this, ranked from fatal to merely awkward? I want the cases where content looks recoverable with no frame at all — a self-extracting archive, a quine, a message that ships its own decoding key, a diagram, a bit of pure math — and I want to know whether the whole thing just collapses into the ordinary line that meaning is the utterer's intention. What does my view predict that a rival doesn't? Which of these would a defender of my position actually concede, and which would they wave away? And has this been insulated into unfalsifiability — if so, is that fatal, or just the price of a view this general? Tell me how confident you are in each claim, and definately flag the single point you're least sure of.
