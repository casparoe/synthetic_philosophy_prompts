I run a small fortnightly reading group on free will and moral responsibility, and we have spent the last several sessions on Frankfurt's original case and the many descendants it has spawned. The problem we keep running into is that our intuitions about every version we look at seem to be hostage to the same piece of machinery: a counterfactual intervener who would have stepped in had the agent shown any sign of doing otherwise. I would like a case that secures the same result without that machinery, built to specification rather than adapted from anything already in print. Please construct a thought experiment in which a single agent is morally responsible -- indeed blameworthy -- for what she does, even though she could not have done otherwise. The constraints, all of which must be met: (1) it is realistic, set in contemporary ordinary life, with no neurosurgeons, chips, demons, brainwashing, or mind-reading, and in particular no counterfactual intervener or backup device of any kind, so that nothing in the story has a merely conditional causal role; (2) the impossibility is total, so that there is no alternative action and also no alternative decision, intention, or even attempt -- no residual flicker of freedom for a libertarian to seize on, and if someone objects that she could at least have tried, the case as you have built it must already have closed that door, which you must show me; (3) the impossibility is not itself an excusing condition, so there is no coercion or threat, no ignorance or false belief, no addiction, phobia, compulsion, or illness, no physical constraint, and it is not that she has been made to care less about the option she cannot take -- she understands her situation and her options perfectly; and (4) the inability is a contingent fact about her of a kind a reader could recognize in someone they know, and it is not smuggled in by fiat, since a bare stipulation that she could not have done otherwise, unsupported by anything in the story, counts as cheating. Keep the scenario itself to a tight paragraph or two; the analysis can run as long as it needs to.

Then the demonstration. State what each of the following delivers on your case, and why: the principle of alternative possibilities in its simple form; Frankfurt's own actual-sequence answer; a mechanism-based reasons-responsiveness account of the kind that asks whether the actual sequence was responsive to reasons, saying explicitly whether your case satisfies the strong version of that condition or only the weak one; and a source libertarian who insists on ultimate origination, who will want to locate a residual alternative somewhere in the case -- tell me where she will look and whether the construction genuinely blocks her. Add the matched control: the same scenario with exactly one feature reversed, in which the agent is not responsible, so that the variable doing the work is visible against an otherwise fixed background.

Finish with three things: which single feature of the case is doing the real work; what happens if that feature is removed, that is, whether the case collapses into an ordinary excuse or back into a standard Frankfurt case; and how a defender of whichever theory comes out badly should attack the construction. If, having tried, you conclude that some pair of these constraints cannot be jointly satisfied, do not quietly stipulate the verdict -- tell me which constraint is doing the damage and give me the best case available under the weakest relaxation of it, flagging exactly what you gave up. The point of the exercise is to test whether responsibility without alternatives can be motivated without the backup-intervener device, so a case that merely renames a familiar one is worthless to me.
