Though I have no academic background in philosophy or entomology, I have spent several years thinking on my own about how much moral weight insects deserve, and I have watched two people whose judgment I respect — one a committed proponent of the scale argument for insect welfare, the other a committed skeptic about it — talk past each other for so long that I no longer know whether they disagree about facts, about ethics, or only about words. I would like you to write the document their collaboration should have produced: a joint statement both of them could put their names to.

By the scale argument camp I mean the position that, because there are on the order of quintillions of insects and the probability that at least some of them have valenced experience is not negligible, insects may dominate the expected moral ledger and therefore deserve serious moral priority. By the skeptic I mean the position that the per-individual probability of morally relevant experience is low and the evidence thin, that our uncertainty about insect welfare cuts both ways, and that the scale argument therefore does not establish that insects should be a priority — not that insects certainly do not matter.

The statement should have two parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign: about the numbers involved and how uncertain they are; about what sentience, valenced experience, and a welfare range would each require; about the structure of the expected-value reasoning; about our epistemic situation and the asymmetry between wrongly attributing and wrongly denying experience; and about which interventions are even available. Second, a disagreements table with, for each remaining disagreement, a precise statement of each side's position in wording its own side would accept; the crux — the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side; and, for each crux, a case, argument, or piece of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, together with the prediction each side records in advance. Please keep the disagreement genuinely alive: do not dissolve it into agreement, and do not sharpen it into a strawman.

Two things I would like you to treat carefully. The word insect covers an enormous and heterogeneous range of animals, from bees to aphids to springtails, so a single probability of sentience is almost certainly a fiction; I want you to say whether that heterogeneity is itself a crux or merely a complication both sides can absorb, and how it interacts with the scale arithmetic. And I want you to distinguish sentience — there being something it is like — from valenced experience with a magnitude, since the scale argument seems to depend on whether the morally relevant quantity is the number of individuals or the sum of welfare ranges.

Finally, be candid about your own confidence. Separate what is actually settled among people who have studied this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. If part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
