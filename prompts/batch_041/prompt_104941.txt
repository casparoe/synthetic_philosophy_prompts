So my mate Dan and I got into a two-hour argument last night about whether anything survives death, and it ended with him saying my position was basically Ayer's with extra steps, and me realising I don't actually know what Ayer's position was well enough to know if that was a compliment, an insult, or just a category error on his part.

I want to settle it by building a proper sorting instrument. Can you make me a decision tree (or taxonomy, whichever fits better) that takes someone from commitments they already have to a named position on whether anything survives death — and that places Ayer's view somewhere in the resulting space, plus my position, which I'll describe at the end.

How I want it built:

Start from the logical space, not from a list of famous names. Each branch node should be a question a normal person could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. No node should just ask "are you a dualist?" or "do you believe in an afterlife?" — it should be a concrete case or a concrete claim, and the positions should genuinely come apart on it.

The tree needs to route through at least these five things, in whatever order makes sense:

1. A truth-aptness branch: is "I will survive my death" the kind of claim that can be true or false at all, or is it a picture that fails to get a grip on anything (the Ayer-style move)? Phrase this as a case, not as a request to self-identify as a verificationist — e.g. a scenario where two people agree on every observable fact and disagree only on whether something survives, and ask whether that disagreement is a real disagreement or just a difference in attitude.

2. A personal-identity branch: what exactly has to be preserved for it to count as me surviving rather than a replacement? Build this from cases — gradual memory loss, total amnesia with intact body, a body destroyed but memories transferred, a soul swapped into a different body.

3. A replica/copy branch: if a being with all my memories and my personality is produced after I die, does that count as survival, and if not, what does the difference consist in? This is where the tree should be able to separate "survival requires the very same subject" from "survival requires only the right kind of continuity."

4. A gap branch: if I stop existing at death and then exist again later, was there a period during which I didn't exist? Does that matter, or is a gap in existence no worse than a gap in sleep? This should separate views that need continuous existence from views that allow reconstitution.

5. An evidence branch: what would near-death experiences have to be like to count as evidence for survival rather than for brain activity under stress? Include a case where someone reports verifiable information they couldn't have known, and a case where the report is vivid and life-changing but contains nothing checkable. The tree should make clear which positions can absorb the first case and which can't.

For the leaves, I want 7-9 named positions, each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Ayer's view has to be one of them, and it should be described by what it actually claims about the meaningfulness and coherence of survival, not by a stock label. Please don't make me a bibliography or tell me which essays to read — I don't have access to any of that right now and I don't want the tree resting on titles or dates I can't check. Paraphrase everything.

Two more things. First, flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate, and say why. Second, note any combinations of answers that look coherent on paper but that nobody seems to hold — and say whether you think that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. I'd rather have the gaps in the instrument exposed than papered over.

Finally, here's me, so you can tell me where I land: I think death is the end of the person, full stop; I think near-death experiences are entirely brain events; but I also think the question "will I survive death?" is perfectly meaningful and probably false, not meaningless. Dan says that last bit makes me a closet Ayerian and that I can't have it both ways. Build the tree so I can see whether he's right.
