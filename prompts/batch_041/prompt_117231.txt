Two people in my department went at each other after seminar last week and I got dragooned into refereeing, so I'm outsourcing it to you. Rae is a postdoc on the CS side of the building; Tomas is one of ours, phil of science. Both smart, neither of them a strawman, which is the problem.

It started over whether it's kosher to assign probabilities to things that are already settled — whether some enormous specific number is prime, what the 10^12th digit of pi is, that kind of thing. Rae's line: obviously it is, and we don't do it by vibes, we do it by a principle. When nothing you know links two unknowns, you presume they're independent and multiply. That's what licenses 'each late digit of pi is roughly equally likely to be a 7, so the chance that three particular ones are all 7 is roughly one in a thousand.' He calls the move presuming independence, and says it's the default you get for free when you're ignorant; anything else is smuggling in information you don't have.

Tomas said independence isn't a description of your ignorance, it's a claim about a joint distribution, and it can be false while you know nothing at all. Then he pulled this: say you know nothing about a coin's bias. Rae's default makes each flip 50/50 and independent, so five heads in a row is 1/32. But if you really know nothing about the bias, the fifth flip after four heads should be 5/6, not 1/2 — the streak is evidence about the coin — and five heads should come to 1/6. So the 'default' answer is the one you'd give if you already knew the coin was fair. It converts ignorance into a very specific piece of knowledge.

Rae didn't fold. He said the 5/6 falls out of Tomas's flat prior over the bias, and that's just another heuristic argument wearing a costume — why flat in p rather than flat in the log-odds? — so Tomas has relocated the presumption rather than eliminated it. And he never claimed the presumption is above criticism: it's a heuristic argument, and if you can point to a common cause you stop presuming. The alternative, he says, is an epistemology that can't explain why our pi-digit guesses are any good.

Tomas came back with a case where he thinks the move isn't just defeasible but incoherent. Take an unknown positive integer N. Presume independence between 'N is a multiple of 6' and 'N is a multiple of 4' and you get 1/6 times 1/4, so 1/24. But that conjunction is just 'N is a multiple of 12', which on the same style of reasoning has probability 1/12. Same claim, two answers, no evidence in sight. Rae said that only shows the heuristic arguement for independence fails in that case — the two events share the factor 2 — and that a principle which demands an argument each time isn't the same as one that fires mechanically.

Tomas's last shot: then the independence in the pi case isn't a presumption either, it's a conclusion from the empirical fact that pi's digits pass randomness tests, and if they stopped passing you'd revise — which is not something a presumption can do. Rae said fine, call it whatever you like, the point stands that we're entitled to those probabilities and the entitlement isn't deduction.
