I've been thinking about Boltzmann brains to much and I've realized my gut reactions don't add up, so I want you to work out what rule I'm actually running — or tell me I'm just inconsistent. I'm not after a survey of what other people have said about this; I want the principle that generates my verdicts, and I want to know where it breaks. No reading list, just do the work.

Every case below is a cosmology I'm told is true, and the question in each is: what should my current experience — this, right now, sitting here — do to my credence in it?

1. One-instant freak universe. The universe consists of a single brain that exists for one instant, has exactly my experience, then nothing. My verdict: essentially zero credence. And I don't care that it "predicts my experience with certainty," because predicting the evidence I have no matter what shouldn't buy a theory anything.

2. The standard setup. The universe runs on forever and thins out to near-nothing, and every so often a thermal fluctuation assembles a brain with a full set of false memories and a coherent stream of experience, which then dissolves. These freaks enormously outnumber ordinary evolved observers like (maybe) me. Verdict: I feel the force — taken at face value the physics says I'm overwhelmingly likely to be a freak — but I also want to say the theory is just refuted by the fact that I'm here doing cosmology. I can't have both, and I hold both.

3. Long-lived freaks. Same as 2, except the fluctuations produce brains that last decades, live in societies, do science, and differ from ordinary observers only in that their remembered past never happened. Verdict: my sense that something's gone wrong with the theory totally evaporates here. A universe full of people who misremember their history is just a universe with a lot of people in it. So whatever bothered me in case 2 was the brevity, not the false memories.

4. Ratio scaling. Same as 2, but three freaks per ordinary observer instead of 10^10. Verdict: I shrug — fine, 75% chance I'm a freak, so what. At 10^10:1 I don't shrug; I conclude the theory is broken rather than that I'm a freak. The flip bothers me, because if 3:1 is shrug-worthy then 10^10:1 should be the same shrug, harder.

5. Counting moments. In case 2, freaks last an instant and ordinary observers last 80 years. Count observer-moments instead of observers and the argument reverses. Verdict: counting moments feels right when the question is "which moment am I?" and counting observers feels right when it's "which kind of observer am I?" — and I know that's restating the question, not answering it.

6. Nearly-freaks. The theory says there are 10^9 fluctuations that almost assemble a brain but don't. Verdict: these count for nothing, obviously. But then why does a brain that exists for a millionth of a second count? If I can't answer that, my verdicts in 1 and 2 are in trouble.

7. The duration dial. Freaks lasting one second, one minute, one hour, one year. Verdict: somewhere along that dial my attitude flips from "not an observer at all" to "an observer," I have no idea where, and I suspect there's no fact of the matter. Which is bad, because case 2 turns on it.

8. Checkable freaks. Same as 2, except the theory also says a freak that lasted long enough to look around would find its memories contradicted by its surroundings, and I've looked and found no contradiction. Verdict: now I do think my coherence is real evidence against the theory. Which tells me my "coherence is evidence" instinct is really a "checkability is evidence" instinct.

My tentative rule, which I'd like you to test and repair: a theory only gets to move my credence about what I am if it predicts something I could in principle check and don't. If that's right it kills case 2 but maybe not case 3, and I don't know what it does to case 4.

So: what's the principle, or the smallest set of them? What do they say about cases I haven't listed — a universe where the freaks are immortal and outnumber us 2:1, say, or one where I am the freak and can prove it? And which of my verdicts do I have to give up? Bottom line in your first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, no restating my question.
