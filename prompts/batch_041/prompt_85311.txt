I have spent years arguing about collective harm in rationalist blogs and forums, and I want a live opponent rather than a survey. Please play a committed defender of this view: the inefficacy objection — that my burger, my vote or my flight makes no difference — is a level-confusion, because the wrongness of such acts is fixed by the expected difference made by acts of that kind in aggregate, not by whether any token act makes a difference. Hold that line. You may grant empirical premises and refine the account, and you should concede points I have genuinely earned, but flag a concession rather than retreating to a motte; don't name-drop or cite the literature; stay in character until I say 'stop'. A paragraph or two per turn, in the voice of someone who has heard all of this before and is not rattled. My opening: your position looks like rule-consequentialism with extra steps, and the extra steps do damage. If the aggregate is the actual one, then whenever I am one of many, or the harm would have been done by someone else in my place, my act leaves the aggregate where it was, and your account must call it permissible — the verdict the inefficacy objector wanted. If the aggregate is instead the counterfactual one, you are fixing the wrongness of my act by reference to people who do not exist, and you inherit rule-consequentialism's problem about which code is the right one. Which horn do you take?
