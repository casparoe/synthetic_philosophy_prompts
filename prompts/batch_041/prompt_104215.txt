My friend and I have just spent an evening arguing about the careenium — the thought experiment in which a great many tiny magnetic marbles careen around a box and clump into stable, repeatable higher-level patterns, the simmballs, which are meant to stand to the marbles as symbols stand to a substrate. I think there is a real sense in which the simmballs push the marbles around; he kept repeating, as though it settled the matter, that "the simmballs are just a description — only the marbles do any pushing." I am fairly sure that one sentence is doing several different jobs at once and that at least one of them is false. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making — from the trivial (a description is not a physical force) through the supervenience and causal-exclusion readings to full epiphenomenalism — and for each say whether it is true and whether it would actually support his conclusion that the simmball level exerts no causal influence. Give me the menu with a verdict on each entry, and tell me which reading he probably meant.
