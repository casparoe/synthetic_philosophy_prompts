The fine-tuning argument is the one I have taught and argued about longest, and it is the one that most reliably produces an impasse. The theistic side holds that the life-permitting values of the physical constants are enormously improbable under any hypothesis of chance or indifference, and that this improbability is best explained by a designer who intended a life-bearing universe; the naturalistic side replies either that a multiverse of varying constants makes our values unremarkable, or that observers like us could not have found themselves anywhere else, so the improbability is unsurprising. Each side knows the other's moves in advance, and after decades neither has moved. I am not asking which side is right. I am asking what the disagreement actually is.

Here is a hypothesis I would like tested rather than assumed: the stall is not about God at all, but about the correct reference class for the probability. If we have no well-defined measure over possible universes, then each side may be assigning probabilities to events in a space it has itself constructed, and the quarrel is a formal problem in disguise -- the measure problem familiar from cosmology and anthropic reasoning -- which might be resolved or dissolved by mathematical work rather than by argument about design. But there are at least two rival diagnoses. The disagreement may instead concern what an explanation must do: one side treats an improbability as a datum demanding a cause, the other treats it as a datum demanding only that we stop mistaking it for evidence of purpose -- a difference in explanatory standards, not in facts. Or the two sides may be answering different questions under a single name, with one treating the question as a request for a mechanism and the other as a request for a purpose.

Diagnose the impasse using what each side actually argues, and tell me which of these it is, or whether a hidden empirical or formal question is doing the work. If the disagreement is ultimately bedrock -- a clash of standards of explanation that neither side can argue for -- say so plainly, and tell me whether any theorem, measure, or distinction could count as progress, or whether none should be expected.
