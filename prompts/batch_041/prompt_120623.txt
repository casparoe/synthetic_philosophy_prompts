I've been mainlining Stoicism-adjacent content for like two years now — the modern self-help revival kind, plus alot of the older stuff — and I also lurk in EA and rationality spaces, and I've ended up with a pile of commitments that I genuinely can't tell fit together. Before I go around telling people I'm a Stoic, I want someone to check the load-bearing parts. Here's the set:

1. The dichotomy of control, taken at face value: the only things up to me are my judgments, my impulses, and whether I assent to an impression. My health, my reputation, whether my projects work out, whether anyone is actually helped by me — none of that is up to me.

2. So only virtue is really good. Health, money, friends, interesting work are preferred indifferents — I can go after them when nothing forbids it, but losing them shouldn't move me, and the sage is supposed to be no less untroubled on the sickbed than on the throne. (I know that's cartoonish, but the direction of it matters to me.)

3. The passions are false judgments. Grief, anger and despair about anything external are mistakes about what's good, and the target is not having them at all, not having them in moderation.

4. My own refinement, which I'm not sure belongs in the package: I don't think control is binary, I think it's a gradient. The useful quantity is what I've been calling expected influence — how much the probability of an outcome shifts when I put in effort, weighted by how much I care about the outcome. Some things I influence a lot, some a little, some not at all. On this picture almost nothing is ever flatly not up to me; it's just a matter of degree.

5. I'm broadly EA. I think I have real obligations to do the most good I can, that outcomes are what matters, and that the numbers are bad enough that I should actually feel them — I don't think I'm allowed to be serene about the scale of preventable suffering. I'd take a job I hated for earning to give.

6. I do the exercises — premeditatio, the view from above, journaling before bed — and I honestly think they're the best emotional-regulation tech I've found. I push them on everyone.

7. I'm a materialist and I don't believe in providence or a cosmic logos. The universe has no plan for me and no interest in whether I flourish.

So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad is each one really? Rank them by severity rather than telling me they're all equally shaky, and for the ones that are real, say what has to give and what it would cost me. If there's a single revision that cleans up the most at the least cost, tell me that too. And I'd rather you not just tell me its all fine.
