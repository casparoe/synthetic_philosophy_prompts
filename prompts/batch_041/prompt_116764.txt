My friend sent me this blog post that she says finally made the cluelessness thing click for her, and now she's convinced consequentialism is dead in the water. I'm not so sure, but I can't articulate why, so I want you to audit the post for me. Here's the gist of what it says:

'Consequentialism says the right act is the one with the best consequences. The trouble is you can never know the full consequences of anything you do. Buy a coffee and you've nudged the global economy; the ripple effects never stop, so the total consequences of any act are basically infinite and unknowable. Call this cluelessness. Because of it, consequentialism can never actually tell you what to do — it might be true or false as a theory of rightness, but it's useless in practice, and even its defenders admit as much. The usual escape hatch is expected value: just weight each outcome by its probability and pick the best. But that's no help, because if you can't even say which outcomes are consequences of your act — and you can't, since every event has countless causes and you can't draw the line — then you can't assign probabilities to them either. So the consequentialist ends up admitting she's flying blind, and once you admit that, the whole framework falls apart.'

Some of this I buy and some of it feels like it's smuggling different things together. One commenter said the post blurs two separate problems — one where getting more info would help, and one where it definately wouldn't — but didn't spell it out, and I couldn't follow. Where would an actual consequentialist, someone who's thought hard about this and isn't a caricature, push back? Go through it and tell me which bits are fair, which are true but overstated, and which are just wrong or are conflating two different problems. In particular: does the conclusion (the framework falls apart) actually follow from the premises, or is the post sliding from 'can't tell you what to do' to 'isn't true'? And is the expected-value rebuttal as decisive as its made out to be, or does it only look strong because the post never says what expected value actually requires?
