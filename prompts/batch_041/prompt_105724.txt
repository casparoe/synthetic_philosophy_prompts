I've been having a long, slightly drunken argument with my flatmate about whether it's ever okay to care more about your own countrymen, and it's made me realise that I have pretty firm gut verdicts on particular cases but absolutely no idea what they add up to. Picture two countries next to each other, same language, most families have cousins over the border. Case by case, here's what I think. (a) I can give a scholarship to a kid from here or a kid from over the border, the two would benefit equally, and it's my own money: fine to pick my own. (b) Same choice, but I'm a civil servant handing out a fixed pot of public money and the kid over the border would actually benefit more: not fine, I should go where the money does more good. (c) Two equally good candidates for a job in my own business, one a citizen, one not: fine to hire the citizen. (d) Same two for a public job, and the foreigner is a bit better: not fine. (e) Two people drowning, one from here, one from over the border, identical in every way, and I can only get one: I think I'm allowed to grab my own, and I don't think I'm being especially virtuous or especially wicked either way. (f) The government can take in refugees; it costs people here a little and helps the refugees a great deal; I think it should take them in, and that a government which said our own first would be doing something wrong — even though that's basically case (a) with bigger numbers. (g) I'd rather the state spent a pound on someone poor here than two pounds on someone equally poor over the border. That feels right to me. (h) A bloke from here who lives off-grid, pays no tax, never votes and thinks the country is a joke — when he gets ill I still think he's entitled to the same care as anyone else here, no questions asked. (i) A cousin of mine whose grandparents emigrated, who holds the passport but has never lived here, speaks the language badly and pays nothing in — if there's a disaster here and the state hands out emergency payments, I feel he has some claim on it, but a weaker one than mine. (j) A woman who's lived here twenty years, works, pays tax, can be jailed, can't vote — I think she's owed everything a citizen is owed except the vote, and she should get the vote too. (k) And if my country is doing something seriously wrong to people over the border, I think I owe more to the people it's hurting than to my own lot who are complaining about the fallout.

My own half-baked theory was that the thing doing the work is being subject to the same coercive institutions — the law lands on you the same way — with shared culture adding something extra but lighter, and with the whole business only governing how I use my own life and how a country runs itself, not how shares get divvied up. But (f) and (g) seem to pull in opposite directions and I can't seperate them, and (i) is the one that keeps slipping out of whatever I say.

So: is there one principle that gets all this, or close? Give me the principle, or two or three rivals, stated precisely enough that I can tell them apart, with each clause pinned to the case that forces it. Lay it out like this. First a section called The bottom line, about a hundred words, saying straight out whether one principle survives and what it is. Then Candidate principles, four hundred words or so: each stated precisely, the case that motivates each clause, and an honest note on what each has to say about (f) and (g). Then Verdicts, case by case, a table with a row for each of my cases from (a) to (k), giving my verdict, which principle delivers it, and flagging any case that no principle on your list can reach. Then Cases I didn't give you, about two hundred and fifty words, with your verdicts on three I haven't mentioned: a dual national who holds both passports and lives over the border; whether we owe more to our own future citizens than to future foreigners when we set climate policy; and what happens to these claims if a region of the country votes to leave. Then What I have to give up, a hundred and fifty words, telling me which of my verdicts has to go and what would change your mind.
