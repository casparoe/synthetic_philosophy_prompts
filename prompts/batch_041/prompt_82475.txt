Rank these five by how much trouble they make for it, 1 = does the most damage, 5 = least, no ties:

1. The tile that only breaks on the second hit. A ceramic tile; hit it once and nothing happens, hit it again and it shatters. The first hit rearranges the microcracks so the second one does it. Nothing special about being hit twice in a row except this internal change.
2. The glass in the resin. Ordinary brittle glass, but it's bonded into a clear resin block, and the composite takes any blow as a whole — the glass never cracks. The resin can't be removed; there's no way to strike the glass alone.

4. The well-timed coating. A brittle glass with a coating that goes stiff exactly during an impact and relaxes after. At every moment when you're not hitting it, you can measure the glass's brittleness and it's there; at every moment when it's being hit, the coating stops the crack.
5. The brute breaker. Something with no relevant microstructure at all — nothing about how it's built makes it fragile — but it shatters every time it's struck, and would shatter in any world you like. It just does.
