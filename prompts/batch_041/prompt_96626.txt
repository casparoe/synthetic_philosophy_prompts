I'm a philosophy PhD student (metaphysics, but there are two phil-of-physics people on my committee so it has to survive both audiences) and I need to turn a half-formed idea into a prospectus for my second-year review. About 4,000 words, covering a year of work.

Heres the idea. There are two arguments I keep running into that look like the same argument in different clothes. One is the identity of indiscernibles, where the standard test case is a universe containing nothing but two exactly similar iron spheres, and the fight is over whether there are really two things there or whether the description has smuggled a difference in. The other is the family of worries about symmetry in physics: a symmetry takes a model of a theory to another model that agrees on every observable fact, and the question is whether that's a second physical situation or the same situation described twice. The hole-argument style worry is the sharpest version of it but I care about the general shape, gauge freedom included.

In both, all the qualitative facts are fixed and someone insists that a difference in *which thing is which* is still a real difference; in both, the opposing move says that difference is descriptive surplus. What I want to know is whether these are one issue or two, and if one, what the general form of it is — stated in a way that doesn't presuppose either side.

My hunch is that the spheres case is the degenerate case (no dynamics, no observables, nothing at stake, so intuitions do all the work) and that the real content only shows up once you have a symmetry group and some notion of what a theory's sayable facts are. The opposite hunch is that the physics cases are just the spheres case plus machinery, and the physics is a distraction. I honestly dont know which, and I don't want the proposal to pretend I do.

So please write the proposal. I need: the question stated precisely enough that it could come out either way; why the existing answers leave it open, in terms of positions and arguments rather than names — I'll add the citations myself, so no reading list or bibliography, and please don't invent references; the method, i.e. what I actually do, month by month, over a year; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a straight answer on whether this is too big for a year. Two things I want it to face squarely: (a) the strongest version of the objection that the two cases are only superficially alike — one has no observables, the other has a dynamical theory with a symmetry group — and what would show that objection is right; (b) whether I have to take a stand on whether conceivability is evidence for possibility, or whether the project can sidestep that. Also give me a paragraph on what a metaphysics committee gets out of this that the physics literature doesn't already have.
