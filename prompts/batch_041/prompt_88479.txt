For my history seminar's final paper I am examining a comparison our teacher has been using, and I would like a careful audit of it before I commit to an argument.

The comparison is this: a great power is like a living organism. It is born, it grows, it matures, it ages, and in the end it dies; and this, she suggested, is why we can speak of how long empires 'usually' last and treat the fate of a present-day power as something we can estimate by seeing how far along the usual span it has already come. The work she wanted the comparison to do was to move us away from treating our own moment as unprecedented and toward asking what tends to happen to things of this kind — what some of my classmates call 'taking the outside view,' meaning that we set aside the details of the particular case and reason instead from how a class of similar cases has gone.

I can see why the picture is persuasive, and I can also see ways it might be doing more work than it can support. What I would like from you is not a verdict on whether empires decline, but an audit of the comparison itself:

1. A table of correspondences: what plays the role of birth, growth, aging and death; what plays the role of the organism's biology, i.e. the thing that fixes how long it can live; and what plays the role of its environment.
2. Where the mapping breaks, separating the disanalogies that matter for the forecasting use from the ones that are merely decorative.
3. What the organism picture quietly imports that the target may not have. An organism's lifespan is bounded by its biology — there is a fact about how long a mayfly or a human can live that does not depend on what it does — and I am not sure a polity has anything analogous, or whether assuming that it does is the very thing being smuggled in.
4. A judgement on whether the forecasting conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion all along. If some other comparison would serve the forecasting aim better, please propose it.

Two worries of my own, since these are where I suspect the comparison is weakest, and I would like you to address them directly. First, the only empires I can study are empires that have already ended, so a list of their lifespans looks in a way like a list of completed outcomes; I would like to know whether this circularity is fatal or merely a nuisance. Second, I suspect that two different questions are being run together — 'how long do empires last on average?' and 'given that this one has already lasted a long time, how much longer should we expect?' — and I would like to know whether the organism picture is any help at all with the second question.

Please be explicit about how confident you are in each of the main things you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
