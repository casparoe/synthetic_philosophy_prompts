I am a novelist, and the book I am writing turns on a single hour: a woman blind from birth has her sight restored in middle age, and the bandages come off. I want that hour to be true to what is actually at issue and not to the sentimental version, so I have been reading around Molyneux's problem — the question, raised in Molyneux's letter to Locke, whether a person born blind who is made to see could at first sight tell the sphere from the cube by vision alone, having known them only by touch. The literature divides into two camps that seem to talk past each other, and I would like you to draft the joint statement they could both sign after an honest adversarial collaboration.

The first camp is empiricist and modality-specific: it holds that visual recognition of shape depends on representations built up within vision, that touch and sight are separate channels, and that the newly sighted subject therefore cannot, at first sight, recognize by vision a shape known only by touch. The second camp is amodal or nativist: it holds that spatial and geometric structure is coded in a form common to the senses, and that some cross-modal matching should therefore be possible from the outset. Both camps, I take it, must reckon with the messy record of restored-sight cases, in which the subjects are not simply blind-then-seeing but are also visually immature, and I want the document to make that difficulty exact rather than gestural.

Structure the statement as a numbered list of agreed claims, then a table of disagreements, then a section on cruxes. In the agreed-claims list, include what both camps accept about the original question, about what would count as an answer to it, and about the confounds that make any actual test of it ambiguous. In the disagreement table, state each side's position in wording its own adherents would accept, and mark any row where you judge the dispute to be merely verbal rather than substantive. For each crux, give the case, argument, or experiment that both sides agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

I would also like the document to adjudicate a case I have invented for the novel and cannot resolve on my own. My character was a potter before the operation, with an unusually fine tactile acquaintance with a set of seven carved solids that she kept on her workbench, and in the scene that matters she is shown all seven at once and asked to say which is which. I suspect the two camps will want to say different things about whether her years of handling those solids help her or are simply beside the point, and I want their competing predictions stated in a form that a later experiment could confirm or refute.

Finally, since the novel lives or dies on that first hour, I would like the statement to include what both camps agree she would experience in it — however little they agree about what she would thereby know.
