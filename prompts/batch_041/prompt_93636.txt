I've talked myself into a kind of compatibalism and I want you to try to break it. The version is this: free will isn't about being uncaused, it's about your actions coming out of your own reasons through a normal feedback loop -- you would have done otherwise if your reasons had been different, and you can be argued out of it. Determinism doesn't matter because the loop is the thing we actually care about. So what would have to be true for the loop view to be false, and what evidence, cases, or arguments would count against it? I don't mean the usual determinism objection. I mean which cases, arguments, or results should make even a sympathetic person say it fails. I keep coming back to a case I made up: a guy with an implant that quietly rewires his reasons whenever he's about to do something he'd later regret. He's reasons-responsive, he acts from his own reasons, he'd act differently if his reasons differed -- but it feels like he isn't free. Does that sink the view or can it be patched? And if it can always be patched, has the view gone unfalsifiable, and is that a problem? Rank what counts against it from fatal to merely awkward, say what someone who holds the loop view would themselves concede versus dismiss, and tell me what the view predicts that its rivals don't.
