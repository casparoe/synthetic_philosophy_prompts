Here's my rough criterion, which is the part I want you to attack on my behalf: you're entitled to say a system believes that p when p is doing real causal work in its behavior — take p out of the explanation and you lose predictions you can't get back — and when its mistakes are the mistakes of something that holds p, rather than the mistakes of something merely shaped to sound as if it did. That second clause is the one I care about and I havent seen it put this way.
