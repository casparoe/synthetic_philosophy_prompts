I'm drafting a short paper on performative utterances and have settled on what looks like a clean criterion: what marks off an utterance like "I promise" from a constative is that the sentence refers to the very act of uttering it, so that saying the words is what brings the state of affairs into being. Since self-reference looks like a property of a sentence's meaning rather than of a speaker's conduct, it seems to follow that performativity is a semantic matter, and therefore that any utterance with the right form — "I hereby resign," or a coinage such as "I hereby take it back" — is a successful performative on its own, whatever the speaker's standing, the audience, or the surrounding circumstances. Is that the right way to draw the line, and how should I handle the awkward cases: a promise made by an actor on stage, a promise texted to someone who never reads it, a promise in a language the hearer does not understand? Please say how confident you are in your answer and single out the point you are least sure of.
