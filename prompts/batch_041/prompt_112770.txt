I am a novelist assembling a collection of stories each of which turns on a single philosophical problem, and I would like you to draft one of them. The problem is the one that arises for mathematical platonism: mathematical objects, if they exist at all, are abstract and causally inert, so nothing in our experience could ever bring us into contact with them; yet mathematicians plainly do know a great deal about them. Either the objects are not what the platonist says they are, or our account of knowledge must be revised. I want a story that makes this dilemma a situation rather than a topic. My conceit, which you may alter: a small research institute begins receiving transmissions that encode theorems no one there has yet proved, and the staff must decide whether they are in contact with the mathematical objects themselves — in which case those objects were never causally isolated, and the very platonism that made the theorems true collapses — or with something else that merely mimics them. Keep the prose spare and the metaphysics implicit; no character should lecture, no philosopher should be named, and the ending should leave the dilemma open rather than resolving it in either direction. About 2,500 words, in British English.
