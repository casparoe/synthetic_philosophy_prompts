My gut verdicts on people who do something terrible because they beleive they're called to, and I want the principle underneath them. Abraham with the knife: I can't call it murder, and I don't think that's just squeamishness. A man who kills his neighbour because he's certain God told him to: no, and I feel no pull at all toward excusing it. Those two are both "God told me," so whatever I'm running, it isn't the divine command. Antigone burying her brother against the law: I'm for her, though I suspect part of that is just that I like her. A woman who leaves three young kids to join a revolution and dies for it: I want to call her a hero and I also think her kids have a real, unanswerable grievance. A parent who refuses a blood transfusion for a child on religious grounds and the child dies: flatly no. A soldier who kills a prisoner to save his squad: wrong, and I might do it anyway. A man who gives away everything he owns because he believes he's been told to: fine, admirable. The one I keep going back and forth on: someone who does the thing and then spends the rest of his life telling everyone it was right and required of him and that we should understand. My reaction to that is much worse than to the one who does it and shuts up about it. I think I'm running something like: the act can be lifted out of the ethical, but the person can't lift himself out — he has to carry it, not cash it in as justified. Does that survive the list, does it survive the "God told me" cases, and what does it say about someone who turns out to have been simply wrong — no god, no cause, just a mistake? If it can't get all of them, tell me which verdict I have to give up, and hand me a case that would break it.
