For the satire page of my high school philosophy club's journal, I am writing a mock customer-service FAQ for a ship-restoration company that promises customers they will still own the same vessel after every plank, mast, and sail has been replaced. The company stores the removed parts; a heritage society later reassembles them into a second, seaworthy ship, and both vessels now claim the original registration, insurance, and mooring rights. Please draft the FAQ, around 550 words, in the deadpan voice of a corporate support page. The humor should come from the metaphysical problem itself—what makes a ship the same ship, whether original is a material or a legal predicate, and what happens when two vessels satisfy different criteria—rather than from wacky customer complaints. Keep it readable for someone who has never encountered the puzzle, but include enough precision that a reader who has will see the positions are being handled fairly. In at least one exchange, the representative should quietly admit that the company's answer is a stipulation rather than a discovery, without breaking the customer-service tone. Do not include citations or references to specific historical texts.
