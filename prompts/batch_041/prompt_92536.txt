I'm taking a seminar on Madhyamaka and have to commit to a reading of Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi, which I'd like to do by elimination rather than by picking a favourite. Four readings have been put to us: (1) each corner is provisionally granted and then refuted, so the tetralemma is a ladder that is kicked away; (2) the corners are four mutually exclusive truth-values, i.e. a four-valued logic; (3) the tetralemma carries no propositional content and works only therapeutically, to loosen the grip of views; (4) all four corners presuppose svabhāva (own-being) and therefore fail together rather than one after another. To these I want to add a fifth of my own: that the corners mark a descending scale of commitment — asserting, denying, hedging both ways, and finally withdrawing the question — so that the fourth corner is a change of conversational stance rather than a metaphysical position. Please rank all five by how well each explains why the fourth corner is needed at all rather than being a decorative flourish, give each a score out of 1, and add a sentence or two per entry justifying its placement. Then tell me which single reading you would commit to, and why that one beats the runner-up. I don't need any reading list — just the reasoning — and please write in British English.
