I spent most of last weekend in an argument with a friend about whether risk-weighted expected utility is a genuine rival to expected utility or only expected utility with the outcomes described more richly, and we reached an impasse I would like my next podcast interview to break. My guest is a decision theorist whose forthcoming book defends a particular version of the view: the agent's risk function — the increasing function, fixed at zero for certainty and one for impossibility, that she applies to cumulative probabilities in order to discount or amplify the weight of the better outcomes — is to be elicited from her choices among small, transparent lotteries, the kind whose states and stakes can be laid out in full, and then carried over unchanged to decisions in which she cannot enumerate the states at all. My friend's closing move was that any pattern of choice whatsoever can be rewritten as expected-utility maximization once you let the description of the outcomes carry enough baggage, so the theory owes an account of which descriptions count; I could not answer him. Draft my interview questions.

Lead with the single question you would ask if you could ask only one — the one that puts the most pressure on that position — stated exactly as it would be asked, and then give the rest with your reasoning. I want eight to ten questions in total, sequenced so that the pressure accumulates rather than resets after each one, and with at least one question each on the following three matters, each accompanied by a note on what the question is after and a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give:

1. Whether the theory has content distinct from expected utility once outcomes are described richly enough, and whether the guest's own preferred description of outcomes in small lotteries is principled or convenient.
2. How the risk function is measured without circularity, given that the elicitation lotteries are themselves choices the theory is supposed to explain, and what licenses exporting a risk function calibrated in small worlds to decisions where the state space cannot be enumerated.
3. Whether a risk-weighted maximizer is dynamically consistent when she reconsiders a plan mid-course, what the theory tells her to do when she is not, and whether the appeal to resolve is an admission that the theory is not a theory of choice at each moment.

Add at least one question on the social use of the apparatus — behind a veil of ignorance, does weighting the probabilities of being anyone in particular deliver anything that a prioritarian weighting of people's claims could not deliver on its own, and is the analogy between probability weights and claims legitimate — and one on whether the view is offered as a theory of rationality or of actual preference, and one on what the machinery does when the agent cannot completely rank the outcomes, since the cumulative weighting presupposes a complete ordering of them from best to worst before any probability is weighted.

No questions about how she came to the subject, and none that merely invites her to restate the axioms. Open with the first question itself: no preamble, no framing, no restatement of this request.
