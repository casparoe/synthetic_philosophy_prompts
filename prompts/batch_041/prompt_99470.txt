For my philosophy club's final meeting of the term I would like to close with a poem about performative utterances, and I am asking you to write it. Make it twelve to sixteen unrhymed lines, spoken in the first person, in which nearly every line begins with 'I hereby' and then completes an explicit performative — promising, naming, betting, bequeathing, sentencing — so that the poem is not merely about doing things with words but is itself a run of acts, each one altering something in the saying of it. Let the repetition do structural work: by the middle the reader should feel the accumulating weight of what has been done, and then one line should fail the test, using a verb that cannot honestly take 'hereby' because it names a state rather than an act, so that this failure becomes the poem's turn and marks the difference between an utterance that performs and one that merely reports. Somewhere earlier, include one performative that comes off but is hollow, so that a reader who knows the distinction between a misfire, where the act does not come off at all because its conditions are not met, and an abuse, where it comes off but is not sincerely meant, can tell which of the two they are looking at without being told. Someone who has read Austin should find it accurate; someone who has not should still find it a poem.
