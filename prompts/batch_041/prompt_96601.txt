Ok so my school is a weird mix and I cant tell what it actually thinks about rules. Phones locked in your locker all day, no hats, shirts tucked, second tardy is a detention, teacher can write you up for dress code on the spot. But in class and on student council they keep telling us to think for ourselves and challenge what we're taught. And if one kid in a group goofs off, sometimes the whole group gets the same punishment, while at the same time theres a thing where if you report someone you can get your own detention waived. Anti-bullying assemblies say always tell an adult, but everyone knows you dont rat on your friends and honestly the teachers seem to expect that too.

I read some psych thing about tight vs loose cultures, where tight = lots of strong norms and low tolerance for breaking them. But I noticed theres really three different things in there: how many rules there are, how bad the punishment is, and how likely you are to actually get caught. My school is low on rules, harsh on punishment, random on catching. Are those one thing or three? And can a place be tight about phones but loose about what you say in class, or is it all-or-nothing?

So whats the theory of norms and rule-following my school is running on? Do they think we obey because we'll get caught, because we've swallowed the rules, or because everyone's watching each other? Does it treat following rules as good in itself, or just as obedience? Tell me which parts of the setup actually require one of those views and which parts would work under any of them, and where its just underdetermined. Then if you swapped in the opposite view -- few norms, slack enforcement, peers sort it out instead of punishments -- what at my school would have to go. And does any of this even say how much rule-following a place should demand, or is that a separate question it never answers?
