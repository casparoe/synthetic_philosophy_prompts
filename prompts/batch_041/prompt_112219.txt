I teach a small upper-level seminar on self-reference — a few weeks of Gödel, then a few weeks on Hofstadter's typogenetics and the DNA analogy — and by December the students can all say that DNA is a strange loop in thier sleep, which is exactly what I don't want the final to reward. So I'm writing the exam now and I need one essay question, a model answer, and a marking scheme. Here's the system I'll be asking about, so you're on the same page as my class: in typogenetics a strand is just a string over A, C, G, T; each consecutive pair of bases specifies an amino acid; a run of amino acids, chopped off at punctuation, folds into an enzyme that has a binding preference (which of the four bases it latches onto) and a list of operations it performs as it slides along a strand — move the cursor left or right, search for a purine or a pyrimidine, cut, delete, switch to the complementary strand, toggle copy mode so that moves start writing a second strand, insert a base. Hofstadter's prize puzzle is the self-reproducing strand: one whose own enzymes, turned loose on it, crank out a copy of itself, and he presents that as the typogenetics analogue of Gödel's self-referential sentence. There's no phenotype doing any work here — the enzymes act straight back on the strands, including the strand they came from, which a real cell mostly doesn't allow. Now the bit I care about: for the exam I've been playing with a variant where I add a rule letting an enzyme act on other enzymes rather than on strands, so the system grows a little tower of levels. Alot of my students think that makes it more Gödelian (more self-reference = more like the proof) and the rest think it just makes it more biological. I want the question to force them to settle it properly, and to say what would actually have to be true of typogenetics for the Gödel comparison to be tight instead of a vibe.

So please take the role of a philosopher of logic who also knows the molecular biology well, and think carefully before writing anything: start with a scratch brainstorm that I won't mark, first on what Gödel's construction genuinely requires — arithmetizing the syntax, the diagonal or fixed-point move, the expressibility of provability inside the system, negation — and then on what typogenetics has and lacks, and on where the self-rep strand actually sits relative to a quine and to von Neumann's self-reproducing automaton. Then give me three things. First, one essay question, doable in ninety minutes, that a student cannot answer by reciting the isomorphism.
