I'm in the middle of writing a chapter on how archaeologists infer past beliefs and institutions from objects -- temples, burials, hoards, figurines, that kind of thing -- and I keep running into the same fight. The processualist types say you can only ever infer behavior, the symbolic types want to read meaning straight off the stuff, and everyone waves at the same handful of examples without ever saying what a good inference is actually supposed to deliver. Before I pick a side I want the spec first. Write me the desiderata any theory of this kind of inference has to meet: what phenomena it has to explain, what cases it has to get right (the rubbish pit that looks exactly like a votive deposit, the burial where the grave goods are the only evidence of a rank that may or may not have existed, the figurine that could be a toy or a god or a loom weight), what distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse (say, between a belief actually held, a belief publicly performed, and a practice nobody holds any belief about; or between an institution and a merely recurring behavior), and what results from taphonomy, sampling and preservation bias, and ethnographic analogy it can't contradict. For each requirement tell me the case or consideration that puts it there, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just house preferences of one camp. Fix the purpose for me too if it helps -- I mean a standard good enough for cautious site reports, not for sweeping narrative.

And here's the part I actually care about: flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. I have a hunch that "has to be checkable against the material record" and "has to be able to recover something as thin as a belief" pull against each other, but I want you to tell me whether that's a real tension or I'm just confused. Then, briefly, which single requirement knocks out the most otherwise-plausible theories, and how the main existing camps -- the nomothetic covering-law one, the interpretive/symbolic one, and whatever the cognitive-archaeology people are up to -- do against your list.

Keep it under 600 words or so, a table for the list is fine, and dont pad it with throat-clearing about how hard the problem is. I already know it's hard.
