I've been reading alignment-adjacent blogs and forums for years, and I'm tired of the same three thought experiments recycled as fiction. Write me a short story that dramatises the distinction between intent alignment and value alignment: a system that does precisely what its operators intended to ask for, and thereby betrays what they actually cared about. No genie's-paw twist, no deceptive machine, no paperclip maximiser — the system should be honest, legible and unambiguously faithful to the instruction it was given, and the failure should come from the intention itself being thinner than the value it stood in for, so that no amount of candour on the machine's part could have prevented it. Set it somewhere mundane and procedural, with competent and well-meaning operators. I want the reader to feel the exact moment at which the thing being optimised quietly stops being the thing anyone wanted, and I don't want a villain to blame for it. Then add a short coda saying which part of the distinction the story manages to carry and which part it can only assert. British English throughout.
