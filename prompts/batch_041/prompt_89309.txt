I've picked Keynes on probability and uncertainty, mostly because I keep running into the phrase radical uncertainty in finance writing and realized I don't actually understand what's being claimed, and I suspect the table will walk in with the same half-right picture I had — basically "risk is when you've got the numbers, uncertainty is when you don't." As I understand it the interesting move is stranger than that: Keynes thought a probability was a logical relation between your evidence and a claim — how far the evidence really does partially imply the conclusion — rather than how strongly you happen to believe it, and then there's a second thing he called the weight of the argument that isn't the same as the probability at all, since piling on new evidence can push the probability down while pushing the weight up. I might be mangling it, so fix me where I'm off, but if that's roughly right it's the part I want the session to land on, because nobody at the table will have heard it before — the one guy who does quant stuff will definately say it's just subjective probability with extra steps, so build the plan ready for that.

So don't explain Keynes to me — plan how I should explain him.
