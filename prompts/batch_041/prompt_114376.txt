Refresher, in case Nomic isn't in your head in the shape I'm thinking of: Nomic is a game where changing the rules is itself a move. On your turn you propose a rule change, everyone votes, majority carries, and play continues. Rules come in two flavours — mutable ones you amend by ordinary vote, and immutable ones that take precedence and can't be touched until you pass a rule that turns them mutable first. The really important bit is that rules can change the voting thresholds, change which rules count as immutable, and even change the rule that says you have to obey the rules. The game is engineered so that a group of honest players eventually ends up somewhere the rule-set just doesn't determine what's legal.

Your job: start by brainstorming. Give me five or six candidate mechanisms for self-referential trouble in a system like this — just sketches, one or two lines each — and then tell me which are boring and why (already famous, or just a thin rehash of the rule that says it can't be amended, or whatever) and which are actually live. Then pick the best one and build it out properly.

What I want in the end is a small rule-set — say six to nine rules — plus a short play sequence that carries you from a clean starting position into a real paradox: a state where the rule-set licenses two incompatible readings of what the rules now say, and no move available from inside the game settles which reading is right. Constraints:
