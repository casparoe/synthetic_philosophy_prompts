Over dinner on Saturday an old friend and I argued for two hours about a single sentence she had said the week before. She manages contracts for a manufacturer, and she had told me, flatly and without irony, that their biggest customer's new order "will be signed by Friday, but I don't believe it will." I laughed; she didn't. She said she stood behind both halves and that there was nothing the matter with putting them in one sentence. I've been trying to say why it bothered me and I keep failing, so I'd like you to do it properly: part of what I want is a diagnosis of the sentence, and part is a verdict on her three replies.

Her first reply was that the sentence is only strange in the first person and the present tense. Say it about someone else — "Ravi says the order will be signed by Friday and he doesn't believe it" — or about herself last week — "I said the order would be signed by Friday and I didn't believe it" — and nobody flinches. She thinks this shows that whatever the trouble is, it's a quirk of style rather than a fault in her thinking.

Her second reply was that she can think the whole thing to herself, silently: that the order will be signed by Friday, and that she doesn't believe it will. That, she says, is more or less her state of mind all week. I told her no one can think that; she told me I was confusing what she thinks with what she would say out loud.

Her third reply was that she has told me nothing false. The first half is what will happen; the second half is what she expects; she has reported both accurately. If there is a rule against saying them together, she says, she would like to hear the rule.

So: what, if anything, is defective here — a contradiction, an untruth, a breach of some norm governing what we do with assertions, or nothing at all? Where does the defect live: in the sentence, in her mind, or in the act of saying it to me? Do her three replies shift the verdict, and if each half is true and she is mistaken about neither, what exactly is left to object to? I'm not asking how to handle my friend. I'm asking what she and I were actually disagreeing about.
