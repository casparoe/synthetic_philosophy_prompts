I'm heading into the second year of a philosophy PhD, mostly language and mind, and I've gotten weirdly stuck on the Kripkenstein thing — the plus/quus case, where everything in my head and everything I've ever done is compatible with some deviant function, call it quus, that agrees with addition on every sum I've ever actually performed and diverges on the first one big enough that I'd never have gotten to it. I get why the paradox grabs people. What I can't get is a sense of the shape of the field: the literature around it reads to me like a bunch of people defending their preferred fix — dispositions, biological teleology, the community, treating correctness as just primitive and unanalyzable, non-factualism about meaning — and I can't tell which of the questions underneath those fixes are genuinely live versus just turf wars between fans of different fixes. My supervisor says it's a solved-and-abandoned topic and I should go work on something with data, which I think is wrong but I don't have a good answer for him. So: what are the main open questions in this area right now, and if I've got about a year of serious part-time work and I want to come out the other end with something that lands with people outside the rule-following seminar — I'd really love it to have some teeth for the current fight over whether language models mean anything by what they output, or failing that for how we should think about rule-following in logic and math — which two or three would you actually put the year into? Rank them, don't list them.
