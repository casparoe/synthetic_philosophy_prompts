Okay, weird thought experiment I want you to take seriously. Picture dropping Marx into a modern AI lab. The whole operation runs on models trained on basically all of humanity's written output, and one of them now does work that used to need a few thousand engineers, translators, and copywriters. What's left of the workforce mostly prompts, checks outputs, and patches failures. The firm's money goes into compute and model weights, plus this gigantic pile of scraped text nobody was ever paid for, and the wage bill keeps shrinking every year.

I dont want the stock answer that he'd either cheer automation or denounce it. I want you to actually rebuild him from the machinery writing -- the 1850s notebooks (the Grundrisse), especially the stretch where he says fixed capital is the general social knowledge of society turned into a direct force of production, and the machinery chapter in Capital -- and then run that apparatus on this specific case. So:

- Would he read these models as the general intellect finally getting locked up inside fixed capital, i.e. capital swallowing the collective knowledge of everyone? And if that's right, is it a vindication or an embarrassment, given that he also seemed to treat this socialization of production as one of the preconditions for socialism?
- Where is the value coming from? If almost no living labor touches the output, is he stuck saying the stuff is close to valueless, since value for him is congealed labor-time? Or does he have an escape -- machines only transfer their own value, dead labor can't generate surplus -- that rescues the theory but turns the whole general-intellect story into decoration?
- The training data is unpaid writing by millions of people. Is that exploitation in his sense, or primitive accumulation, or something his categories just dont cover? Say so honestly if it's the last one.
- Does the shrinking wage bill look like the reserve army and immiseration playing out to him, or does he have to admit that a world where labor is nearly squeezed out of production breaks his own account of where surplus comes from?
- And the practical one: would he back a robot tax, or some kind of dividend paid to everyone whose text got scraped, or would he wave that off as a capitalist patch that just socializes the costs?

Be explicit about which of these answers are actually forced by his framework versus you extrapolating, and how confident you are on each. If two of his commitments pull opposite ways here, show me the tension instead of smoothing it over. And if some of this he'd just answer with something we already know, fine, tell me that too.
