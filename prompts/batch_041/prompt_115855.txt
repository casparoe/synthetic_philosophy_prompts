A colleague I respect told me over coffee that she has stopped believing her conscious experience does anything. The brain decides, she said, and the feeling of having decided shows up afterward, like a receipt printed once the transaction is already done. She calls it the shadow. I found I agreed with more of it than I expected, and it has been nagging at me for weeks.

Here is what I cannot get past. The only evidence either of us has that the shadow exists is that we can say things about it: that this red looks warm, that the thought just now was mine, that I am concentrating. But if the experience does nothing, then those statements are produced entirely by the machinery underneath, and they would come out exactly the same if the shadow were not there at all. The instrument I am using to detect the thing appears to be one whose readings do not depend on the thing. It is as though my smoke alarm went off every time I fried an egg whether or not there was any smoke, and I went on citing it as proof of smoke.

When I put this to her, she said I was confusing the experience with the information it carries: the report is about the brain state, and the experience is merely the format that state is displayed in, so of course the format causes nothing and the content does, which is no scandal. But the format still is not doing any work, and by her own account the report would read identically in a creature that had the content and no format at all.

I am a software person, not a philosopher, and I do not want either to abandon the view or to keep it by refusing to look at it. So I would like advice on what to do with this. If the position really does cut the ground out from under itself, say so plainly and tell me what that leaves standing. If some version survives, tell me precisely what has to give: the claim that experiences are causally inert, the assumption that a report can only be evidence for what caused it, or something I have not thought of. Give me the structure of the problem rather than a roll call of who has held which side.

Also tell me how confident you are in each part of your answer, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
