I am a PhD student in the philosophy of biology assembling an application for a two-year postdoctoral fellowship of the self-directed kind, the sort that funds a project of one's own design with a modest workshop and travel budget. I would like you to draft the research proposal. I will supply the citations myself, so please do not include a bibliography or a literature review organized around who said what; refer to positions and arguments by description instead.

Here is the half-formed idea I want you to build on. The long-running dispute over adaptationism, over whether biology is entitled to assume that a trait is an adaptation until shown otherwise, seems to me to have stalled for a structural reason. Both the critics and the defenders tend to talk as though adaptation and spandrelhood were properties that a trait either has or lacks. I suspect they are not properties of traits at all but relational or counterfactual claims: to call a trait an adaptation is, roughly, to say that it would not have been present, or would not have had the form it has, had it not been favored by selection for the effect in question, whereas to call it a spandrel is to say that it would have been present anyway, as a byproduct of something else that was under selection. If that is right, then the standard exchanges may be talking past one another, because the very same trait can come out as an adaptation under one specification of the counterfactual contrast and as a spandrel under another. On this reading the dispute is really about which contrast is the right one to ask about, not about the facts of the case.

Please turn this into a proposal with the following components.

First, state the central question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, and say what that answer would look like. I am after a question of roughly this form: does recasting adaptation and spandrelhood as counterfactual, contrastive claims dissolve the adaptationism dispute, sharpen it into a tractable disagreement, or merely relocate it into an unacknowledged disagreement about which contrast class to employ?

Second, explain why the existing positions — the adaptationist program, the pluralist middle ground, and the strong critique — leave this question open, and why the usual appeal to more data does not settle it.

Third, lay out the method. I expect this to be conceptual work anchored in case studies rather than new empirical research, so say how one would specify a contrast class for a given trait without gerrymandering it to yield a favored verdict, what would make one specification better than another, and how to handle the familiar illustrations without simply re-litigating them. If part of this can be formalized — for instance, as a claim about the counterfactual dependence of trait form on selection history — sketch what the formal apparatus would buy and where it would run out.

Fourth, say what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing. I want an honest verdict on the risk that the counterfactual reading renders the critique either trivially true, since any trait is a spandrel under some contrast, or else unfalsifiable, together with what the project could do to avoid that outcome.

Fifth, give a realistic scope for two years, with a plausible sequence of work, and flag what would make the project too large.

Keep it to the length and register of a genuine fellowship proposal — roughly 2,000 to 3,000 words, in the first person, argumentative rather than survey-like. What matters most to me is the framing: a question that is genuinely open, tractable by conceptual means, and not merely the standard adaptationism debate restated.
