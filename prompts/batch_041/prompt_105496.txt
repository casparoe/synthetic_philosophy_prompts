I'm a novelist — third book just came out, if that matters — and Ive been stuck on something that's really more philosophy than craft. In my current manuscript there's a scene where a character gets cornered at a party and asked point-blank whether she's been seeing someone's husband. She says something that is, word for word, true — "I was at his apartment on Thursday" — knowing the asker will hear it as a confession of something else entirely, and she gets away with it, and later, when the whole thing comes out, her defence is exactly this: I never lied to you. I've used some version of this move in all three books, I watch my friends do it in real arguments all the time, and I've noticed that everyone, including the person who got misled, tends to just accept the defence. There seems to be a genuine difference between saying something false and arranging true things so that someone draws a false conclusion, and that second thing keeps slipping through every word we have for dishonesty.

What finally pushed me to write this down was reading about Kant and the murderer at the door — the case where a would-be killer asks you where your friend is hiding and Kant apparently says you mustn't lie even then. It struck me that the entire discussion is about lying, and nobody in it seems to bother with the other move, the one my characters use, which feels just as deceptive but never touches a falsehood. Is there a name for that? I've heard "weaseling" and "lawyering," but those are insults, not categories. I want to know what the nearest real concept is, whether it's genuinely distinct from lying or just a species of it, and where the line actually runs between it and (a) simply keeping quiet, (b) the thing Frankfurt wrote about where the speaker doesn't care one way or the other whether what they say is true, and (c) the old religious idea of saying something true while silently adding a condition inside your head. If nothing established fits, I'd much rather you coin a term and tell me precisely what it covers and what it leaves out than hand me a vague synonym — a bare label won't help me, I need the carving.

And please be straight with me about the limits of your answer. If the distinction I'm drawing falls apart under pressure, say so rather than humoring me, and tell me what would change your mind: what case, or what argument, would make you conclude that the two things I'm trying to pull apart are really the same thing, or that I've put the boundary in the wrong place entirely.
