Write a joke about the reference class problem. It should be funny to someone who has thought seriously about probability — an expert should laugh at the structure of the joke, not merely recognize the topic — so the humor must turn on the problem's actual mechanics rather than on the general reputation of statistics for being slippery. The mechanics, for reference: the probability of a single event is always the probability of that event under some class of cases deemed similar to it; nothing about the event itself selects the class; and classes that are each defensible can yield answers differing by orders of magnitude or landing on opposite sides of one half. The joke should make a knowledgeable reader feel this, rather than be told it.

Observe the following:

1. Write a short scene or dialogue with two or three characters, a concrete setting, and several exchanges — not a one-liner, a riddle, or a definition.

2. Set it in an ordinary place (a clinic, an insurance office, a weather desk, a call center) and keep the characters competent; they should be caught inside the difficulty rather than discussing it or being dim about it.

3. At least two of the answers in play must be ones a competent statistician could not simply wave away, and the scene should end without the ambiguity being resolved — yet the final line must land as a punchline rather than a shrug.

4. The punchline should turn on the fact that the difficulty is not a defect of any particular method but attaches to the very idea of a probability for a single case. It must not be the observation that statistics can be made to say anything.

5. Do not build the joke on the Sleeping Beauty problem, the doomsday argument, or the classical chord-in-a-circle puzzle; invent a fresh case.

6. No character should name the problem or explain it; the scene should dramatize it. After reading, it should be possible to state in one sentence which two commitments about probability the joke has set against each other.

7. Keep the whole thing under about 250 words and give it a title.
