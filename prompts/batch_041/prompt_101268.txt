I am a high school student preparing for a debate in my philosophy class in which I have been assigned to defend Gongsun Long's claim that a white horse is not a horse. My teacher insists the claim concerns names rather than animals, and a classmate has explained it to me this way: the name horse picks out a shape, the word white adds a colour, so the compound name white horse covers more than horse does and therefore does not pick out the same thing. If that is the whole argument, it strikes me as either trivial or a piece of trickery, since every white horse is still one of the horses; and I suspect the dialogue turns only on an ambiguity in the word is, now the is of identity and now the is of predication, so that once I disambiguate it the paradox collapses into the harmless remark that two different names are two different names. Could you tell me what the dialogue is actually claiming, and how I should answer an opponent who objects that a white horse is obviously a horse?
