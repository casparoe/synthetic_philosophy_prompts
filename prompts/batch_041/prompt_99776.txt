Act as a philosopher of logic and mind. In the MIU formal system, one starts with MI and applies four rewriting rules (append U to a string ending in I; double the part after M; replace III with U; delete UU), and the goal is to derive MU. No derivation exists, but the impossibility can be proved only by stepping outside the system—an act often called jumping out of the system, or JOOTSing. In a seminar I run on formal systems and cognition, the debate over JOOTSing has stalled: one side holds that any such jump is merely a move to a larger formal system, since the meta-system is itself a system; the other holds that recognizing the impossibility is a distinct kind of understanding that no formal derivation delivers. Before you answer, brainstorm the possible sources of the impasse. Then diagnose it: is this a substantive disagreement about the nature of formal systems and understanding, a verbal dispute about where the boundary of a system lies, two sides answering different questions (what can be derived inside versus what can be known about the system from outside), or a clash of unargued intuitions about whether there is any privileged outside at all? Use what each side would actually say about the MU case. Finally, say what would count as progress—a distinction, a formal result, a change of question—or argue that no progress should be expected.
