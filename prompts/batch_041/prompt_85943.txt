I chair the ethics committee at a teaching hospital, and two of our members have been arguing for years. One is a neurologist who holds that death is the irreversible loss of the functioning of the organism as a whole, best identified by the irreversible loss of brain function; the other is a critical-care physician who holds that death is the irreversible cessation of circulation and respiration, and that the neurological criterion is a legal convenience that has never been shown to be the death of the organism. I have told them to stop debating and produce one document they can both sign.

Write that document. In order, it should contain:

1. Numbered agreed claims: what both accept about the biology, about what our bedside tests actually measure, about the limits of what we can know at the moment we have to act, and about the two cases neither of them can wave away — a patient who met the neurological criteria and was kept on somatic support long enough to gestate and deliver a child, and a donor whose circulation was re-established by machine after death had been declared, so that the organs could be recovered. A claim that neither would defend as doing any work does not belong in this section.

2. A disagreements table: one row per genuine disagreement, with a neutral statement of the issue in the first column and each side's own formulation of its position in the next two, each written so that its own side would put its name to it.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one of them. I expect one to be whether the brain is what integrates the organism, and another to be whether the irreversibility our protocols require is a fact about the patient or a decision about when to stop trying. For each crux, give the case, measurement, or argument both of them agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction about how it comes out.

4. A closing section, no more than a short paragraph, saying which parts of what remains are verbal — disagreements about what the word death is for, or about whether it names a biological kind or a status we confer — and which are substantive.

Both of them know the standard literature, so anything that reads as a rehearsal of the familiar arguments will be treated as padding. The whole document must come in under 800 words, the table included.
