I am preparing a seminar session on the taxonomy of minds that sorts creatures into Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian grades: the Darwinian creature is hardwired by natural selection, the Skinnerian creature is shaped by reinforcement, the Popperian creature simulates possible actions internally before committing to them, and the Gregorian creature absorbs external mind-tools, above all language, and thereby thinks with equipment it did not evolve. I want a dialogue that stages a dispute over whether this ladder marks real joints in nature or is a convenient fiction imposed by the theorist.

Please write the dialogue between two participants, both of whom know the taxonomy and the relevant biology and psychology well.

The first participant is a reductionist in the behaviorist tradition. She holds that the Popperian rung is an illusion: what looks like a creature simulating its options off-line is, on her account, nothing but covert operant behavior, subvocal and stripped of its muscular expression but still shaped and maintained by the same contingencies of reinforcement that shape any other operant. On her view there is no qualitative break between the Skinnerian and the Popperian creature, only a difference in the observability of the behavior; and the Gregorian rung fares no better, since the acquisition of mind-tools is itself just a long chain of conditioned discriminations.

The second participant is a philosopher of mind sympathetic to the ladder. He grants that reinforcement shapes a great deal, but insists that internal simulation introduces a genuine decoupling: the creature can let candidate actions fail inside itself, at no cost, instead of letting them fail in the world at the cost of its life, and that decoupling is precisely what a schedule of reinforcement cannot explain, because reinforcement by definition requires the behavior to have been emitted and its consequences to have been felt. He further argues that the Gregorian rung is a real threshold, because once a creature can store and transmit its hard-won adjustments in external, non-genetic vehicles, selection can operate on those vehicles rather than on bodies, and the timescale of cognitive change detaches from the timescale of death.

I want them to engage each other's actual arguments rather than trade slogans. Have them fight over these pressure points: (1) whether a model-free reinforcement-learning agent and a model-based one are simply two engineering solutions to the same problem or instead mark the Skinnerian/Popperian distinction; (2) whether the reductionist's covert-operant move is a substantive explanation or an unfalsifiable relabeling, and whether the ladder-builder's insistence on decoupling is a substantive explanation or an unfalsifiable relabeling; (3) whether the Gregorian rung could in principle be reached by any creature with enough operant conditioning and enough time, or whether language introduces something that conditioning cannot manufacture; and (4) what each participant would predict about a borderline case — an animal that plans, a machine that learns from reinforcement without any internal model, or a model-based system with no language — and which of them accommodates the case more gracefully.

End with each participant naming the single observation or experiment that would most damage his or her own position, and then say which of the two you find more defensible, and why.
