I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've assembled a position about group judgment mostly out of engineering experience and casual reading. I'd like an audit of it before I start repeating it to people. Someone told me the core of it is called the Condorcet jury theorem; I've never read the formal version, so tell me if I'm misusing it. Here is what I actually believe:

1. Majority voting is a truth-finding device. Pose a yes-or-no question to a large enough group and the majority is more likely to be right than any single member, and that advantage grows with the size of the group.
2. What drives this is that individual judges are at least a little better than a coin flip, and I think that's true of most people on most factual questions.
3. I see the same effect at work. When I ensemble a dozen models and take the majority answer, accuracy beats any single model, and adding more models keeps helping, at least until it plateaus.
4. I also think those models' errors are heavily correlated, because they share training data and blind spots, so they are not really independent judges of anything.
5. When a question has more than two possible answers, I take it that the natural extension is to let everyone pick a favorite and go with whichever option gets the most votes.
6. I believe a mixed group of moderately capable problem-solvers can outperform a group of the very best people when those experts all approach the problem the same way.
7. On politically charged questions, I think most people reason backward from what they already want to believe and are probably worse than a coin flip, and I still think the majority should win.
8. I trust a jury of twelve ordinary people more than a single expert witness.

Which of these can I keep together? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only look like they are? Please rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what it would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what it leaves unresolved. I don't have a stake in any particular one of these, so please don't just tell me to drop whichever is easiest to discard. I'm not looking for a reading list, just the audit and the reasoning behind it.
