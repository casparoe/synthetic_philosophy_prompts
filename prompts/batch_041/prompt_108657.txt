Background, so you see why I care: I want 'same mechanism' to earn its keep. If nothing ever gets ruled out, then 'anomalous mechanism' means nothing and the whole anomaly-detection story has no content.

(1) A mechanism is a causal structure: some components, plus the way their interactions produce the behavior.
(2) Two systems contain the same mechanism just in case there's a mapping from the components of one onto the components of the other that preserves the interactions that do the producing.
(3) The same behavior can be produced by endlessly many different causal structures. That's just multiple realizability, everyone grants it.
(4) So for any two systems you like, there's some decomposition under which such a mapping exists. Chop each system into whatever pieces the map needs. Both are made of the same stuff obeying the same laws, so the differences are arrangement, and arrangement is exactly what the map is for.
(5) Therefore any two systems that produce the same behavior contain the same mechanism. A transformer and a lookup table of its outputs. A model and my lab notebook. Two networks that share nothing but a training distribution.
(6) Then the relation excludes nothing, so anomaly detection is empty, and whatever we were calling the mechanism was never the causal structure — it was our choice of decomposition, i.e. our interests in a lab coat.
(7) But (6) is false. We really do distinguish a model that generalizes by the same route from one that agrees by a different route, and that distinction survives scrutiny, it isn't just a feeling.
