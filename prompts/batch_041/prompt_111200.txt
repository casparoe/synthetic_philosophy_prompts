You are a philosopher of personal identity with a dramatist's ear. I'm a novelist writing a chapter in which two characters — both serious, neither a fool — argue out the teletransporter case, and I need the argument itself to be sound before I can stage it.

The scene is a licensing hearing for a commercial transporter. Character A is a reductionist who accepts the machine: identity is not what matters in survival, what matters is psychological connectedness and continuity with the right kind of cause, and the machine preserves exactly that. Character B grants the reductionism but not the conclusion. On B's view, the phrase "the right kind of cause" is doing all the work, and an informational chain — a pattern read off a brain and written onto other matter — is not the kind of cause that normally sustains a person. B calls the arrival a very good counterfeit of survival, and says the counterfeit is good enough to fool the counterfeit himself.

Before you draft, brainstorm briefly and in private: the strongest version of each position, the three cases that will make each character sweat (a branch case, where the original is kept alive alongside the arrival; a gradual or partial transmission, where fidelity degrades across a series of transfers; and a person whose psychological connectedness has already come apart through illness, so that A's criterion seems to license a strange conclusion about the living), and where each will have to concede something real.

Then write the dialogue, roughly 1,000–1,400 words. Neither character may win by appeal to authority, neither may restate a position instead of answering the objection just made, and no one may reach for a thought experiment without saying what it is supposed to show. End with the two of them locating precisely what remains in dispute — whether this is a disagreement about causation or about what a person has reason to care about — rather than with a resolution. Keep the prose spare and novelistic: minimal stage directions, no lecturing, and let the philosophy do the work through what they say to each other.
