ok so I'm on the ethics advisory board at a fertility clinic — not a hospital, a private clinic, but our board has an effective veto over what the lab will and won't do, and we meet Thursday. I have to send round a written opinion the night before. It's about a page, maybe a page and a half, and it needs a bottom line we can actually vote on.

Background: we do PGT for serious conditions, that's uncontroversial here. What's not uncontroversial is that the new panel the lab bought last year reports a whole pile of non-medical stuff alongside the disease markers — height, a few personality-ish scores, some things nobody's sure what to do with. So the old rule, "medical indications only," is now basically a rule about what we say out loud, not about what we know. Every report has the other stuff on it. One of our members pointed this out and now nobody's sure the rule means anything.

The case: a couple, both with the same form of hereditary dwarfism, five embryos, and they want us to transfer the one most likely to be short like them. Their intake notes basically say a tall kid wouldn't be theirs. They'd say they're not asking us to hurt anyone, and it's not like the tall embryo goes into some other womb — for these embryos it's this transfer or nothing. I've read the procreative-beneficence stuff and I still can't tell whether it's meant to permit this, require it, or have nothing to say about it.

The policy question sitting underneath: our lab director wants us to go "blind." Screen for disease, and where embryos tie, pick by lottery, and never show the couple the non-medical scores even though we have them. She thinks that gets us out of the eugenics business without actually denying anyone anything. I'm not sure it does, but I definately don't want to just assert that.

So what I need from you is the memo. Recommend what we tell this couple and whether we adopt the blind policy. Give me the strongest version of both sides — I don't want "autonomy wins" or "dignity wins," I want the actual arguments, and I don't want alot of hedging in the bottom line. Two things I specifically want you to work through: (1) whether deliberately picking the shorter embryo can be a harm to that child at all, given that the alternative isn't "a taller version of him," it's no him — does the harm argument survive that, or is it doing something else, and what? and (2) whether the blind policy avoids the problem or just relocates it one step back — somebody still sets the disease threshold, somebody still decided to make five embryos and screen them, somebody still chose the other parent. If the honest answer is that blind is mostly cosmetic, say so, but then tell me what it's cosmetic *for*, because I think it buys something and I can't articulate what.

Also flag anything I'm missing that would change the recommendation, including if you think the right answer is to refuse the couple and let them go to a clinic with looser rules.
