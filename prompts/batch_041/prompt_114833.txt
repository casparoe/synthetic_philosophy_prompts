I've read about multipolar traps for years — arms races, races to the bottom, tragedy-of-the-commons dynamics, all of it filed under the Moloch metaphor — and I've come to think the idea is asked to do more work than any specification of it can support. Before I read another essay that invokes it, I want the desiderata written out: what any adequate theory of a multipolar trap has to deliver — the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the results it must be consistent with — each with the case or consideration behind it, and each labeled either a genuine requirement or a prejudice of a particular camp (the game-theoretic, the evolutionary, the moralizing).

Write the list so that it serves one purpose: telling me, from the structure of a situation and in advance, whether a competitive dynamic will lock into a race to the bottom, and where the intervention points are. That purpose should shape the list — a criterion that cannot help me predict or intervene is probably not a criterion.

The list needs to force a theory to handle at least these, which is where I think the popular accounts go soft: (a) cases where every participant would prefer that all of them stop, knows the others would prefer it too, and yet the racing continues, as against cases where the participants privately prefer the racing and merely call it a trap; (b) cases where the pressure comes from outsiders' expectations — third parties who will read any restraint as a signal — rather than from the competitors' own preferences; (c) cases where exit is available but its cost falls on someone who is not a player; (d) cases with one dominant player who could stop the race unilaterally and does not; and (e) the documented cases of communities that sustain a shared resource without privatization or an external enforcer, which any claim that the structure alone forces the bad outcome has to accommodate.

Tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the popular accounts, and flag any pair of requirements that looks jointly unsatisfiable — that part is what I most want. Keep the scoring of particular accounts to a sentence each; the criteria are the deliverable. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who have thought hard about this, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
