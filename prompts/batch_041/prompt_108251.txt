I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background at all (last essay I wrote was in high school), and I've got a shot at a small cross-department fellowship — 6 months, half my time, and I have to send them a one-page proposal. I want to pitch something on how archaeologists get from objects to beliefs.

My half-formed idea: people in that field slide between two very different inferences without noticing. One is "these standardized weights turn up across half a continent, so something was enforcing a standard" — that's like recovering a behavioural spec from a compiled binary. Lossy, sure, but real. The other is "these burials have ochre and grave goods, so they believed in an afterlife" — that's recovering the source code, and I dont think the physical record constrains it at all. My hunch is the fix is to stop chasing point hypotheses about what people believed and instead work with equivalence classes: sets of belief-systems that would leave exactly the same material trace, then claim the class rather than the member. And I want some criterion for when the thick step is licensed and when it's just a story with a trowel.
