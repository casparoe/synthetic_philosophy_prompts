Write the joint statement that two camps studying akrasia could both put their names to after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration. The divided-agent camp treats akratic action as strategic conflict among temporally indexed subagents with genuinely opposed preferences, treats commitment devices as intrapersonal contracts, and treats hyperbolic discounting as the fingerprint of a bargaining failure. The unified-agent camp treats there as being a single agent throughout, whose preference structure may well be non-exponential and dynamically inconsistent but is still one structure, and treats akrasia as a failure of the will to execute a judgment rather than a failure of two parties to agree.

Both sides know the standard material, so do not spend space explaining what akrasia is, what a discount function is, or why preference reversals are interesting. The value of the document is in the drafting.

Deliver, in this order:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims: about the behavioral phenomena any acceptable account must accommodate; about which parts of the dispute are empirical and which are conceptual; about what the standard data do not settle; and about the ordinary-language boundaries of the term akrasia.

2. A table of the residual disagreements, one row per disagreement, with columns for the disagreement in neutral wording; the formulation of its own position that each side would accept (a sentence each, worded so that neither side would object to the wording); the crux, meaning the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side; the case, experiment, or argument that both sides agree bears on the crux; and each side's prediction about it, recorded in advance.

3. A short closing section saying which part of the dispute, if any, is merely verbal.

Build the document around the following injected material, which both sides are to treat as on the table:

(a) Three readings of the phenomenon that must be kept apart: clear-eyed akrasia, where the agent's all-things-considered judgment at the moment of action is against the act and the agent acts anyway; unformed judgment, where no all-things-considered judgment was ever reached and the conduct answers to something weaker; and diachronic akrasia, where the agent acts against a resolution adopted earlier without having formed any contrary present judgment. Say which reading each camp is really theorizing about, and whether they are theorizing about the same one.

(b) The noise hypothesis: an agent with a single, stable, time-consistent preference ranking over dated outcomes could still demand commitment if it expects its future choices to be subject to stochastic lapses that leave the ranking intact. On this hypothesis, commitment demand is evidence of anticipated error, not of anticipated preference change. The document must say whether the noise hypothesis is a genuine third position, whether it favors either camp, and what would distinguish it empirically from anticipated preference change.

(c) The functional-form point: the label hyperbolic records a fitted curve, not a mechanism. Keep separate the question whether the discount function is non-exponential from the question whether the underlying preference structure is one thing or several, and do not let agreement on the first be mistaken for agreement on the second.

(d) A case both sides must accept: a subject chooses between a smaller reward available now and a larger one available after a fixed gap; the same pair is then offered with both options shifted later by an equal amount, and the subject's choice reverses. Does the reversal require that the preference ranking itself changed between the two occasions, or is it compatible with one unchanging ranking plus a decision rule that is not maximization over that ranking? Record each side's prediction about what further variation, such as varying the gap while holding the dates or holding the gap while varying the dates, would reveal.

(e) A case both sides must accept: a person pays to have a temptation removed from later reach. Is demand for commitment even possible for a unified agent whose preferences are time-consistent, and does the divided-agent account make any prediction here that the unified-agent account cannot also make? If the two accounts are empirically equivalent on every case either side can produce, say so.

At the end, state how confident you are in each claim in the agreed section, and single out the one claim in the whole document you are least sure of, with what would change your mind about it. Do not cite any literature, name any authors, or supply a bibliography; I want the argument, not the provenance.
