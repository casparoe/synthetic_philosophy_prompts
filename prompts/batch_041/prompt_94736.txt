I'm a retired geophisicist — mostly retired, anyway — and since I stopped working I've been reading a lot of philosophy. I've also ended up on the nomenclature committee of the International Society for Subsurface Stratigraphy, and we have a mess on our hands. I have to write the committee's decision memo before next month's meeting.

Back in 1972 the committee adopted what everyone calls the 'Rule of Two': a new formation name is valid only if the type section is at least two meters thick, unless the author can show a continuous outcrop of at least five meters. It was a compromise after a long fight, and the story is that the chair's flight got fogged in and he missed the vote, so the rule passed 7-6. Now a group has shown that the two-meter cutoff was lifted from a 1930s study of turbidites, which have nothing to do with most of the formations people name. If the vote had happened a day later, the alternative proposal — a one-meter cutoff with a measured-section requirement — would almost certainly have won. So half the committee says the Rule of Two is a historical accident and we should scrap it. The other half says it doesn't matter how it started; everyone has used it for fifty years, all the regional maps and databases and software are built on it, and some cutoff was inevitable anyway. One colleague keeps saying it's path dependent but not locked in, and I'm not sure I see the difference.

I need a memo that recommends a concrete decision: keep the Rule of Two, replace it, or keep it with some kind of grandfathering or dual system, and say what the committee should actually do and on what rough timeline. Don't give me a bibliography or citations — I can't check anything online anyway, and I don't want a general lecture on path dependence. I want the memo to do the work on this case. What exactly would it mean to say the rule is locked in? Is the arbitrariness of its origin relevant if it now coordinates everyone's expectations and makes the literature comparable? Would the same rule have arrived anyway, and does that counterfactual matter if we can't specify the alternative world clearly? And what's the strongest point for the side I end up disagreeing with? Keep it formal enough for a commitee but not stiff, maybe 700-900 words.
