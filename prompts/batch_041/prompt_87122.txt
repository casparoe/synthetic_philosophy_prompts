I'm a history of science grad student and I'm in this reading group that mixes geologists and early modernists, and we spend every single session talking past each other about the Anthropocene. The geologists want to know what the boundary marker is; the historians want to know who gets to declare it; each side thinks the other is being evasive. I want to hand out a one-page decision tree at the next meeting (we meet in a pub, so it has to fit on one page) that takes someone from the flat question 'when did the Anthropocene start, and who decides?' down to a specific position on it. Please don't give me a timeline of committees or the list of proposals with thier dates — I already know roughly whats been floated and I don't need the vote-counting. What I want is the logical space: the branch nodes should be the sub-questions that actually force a choice, each phrased so a person can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to, and the leaves should be the positions with a name (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment. Aim for eight to twelve leaves.

The sub-questions I'd like the tree built from, roughly: whether the Anthropocene is a claim about the rock record at all, or a claim about human history that happens to borrow geological vocabulary, and whether anyone can coherently hold both at once; whether a boundary has to be legible to future stratigraphers reading the strata, or only has to be the historically most significant event of human transformation, legibility be damned; whether the boundary is isochronous, a single instant everywhere, or diachronous, arriving in different places whenever colonization or industrialization got there; whether the start date is retrospective, something future geologists will discover in the record, or prospective, something we fix now because the fixing does work in the present; and who the authority is supposed to be — stratigraphers, the wider scientific community, the humanities and social sciences, political bodies, or nobody at all, because usage decides and formalization is just a formality. I want the mid-twentieth-century formal-epoch option in there, the industrial-revolution option, the early-agriculture and land-clearing option, the colonial-era option, the 'no boundary, the Holocene just continues and Anthropocene is an informal word people like' option, the 'the Anthropocene is a condition or an ongoing event rather than a date' option, and the family of views that says the real answer is a cause rather than a date (capital, plantations, colonial extraction) and that whatever marker you pick launders a political choice.

Two things I especially want from it. First, where a combination of answers looks like it should be occupied but isn't, say so and diagnose it — is nobody there because the view is incoherent, or just unappealing, or merely unexplored? Second, flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually seperate; I have a suspicion that the 'informal word' leaf and the 'condition not date' leaf are doing suspiciously similar work, and I'd rather be told that than have the tree quietly pretend they're different.
