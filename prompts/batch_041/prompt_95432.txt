I have recently been appointed to the governance board of a publicly funded AI assistant that a consortium of local authorities provides to residents, and the board must vote next month on two questions that I have found harder to settle than I expected.

The assistant is tuned by what our engineers call the Preference Ledger: a very large, entirely self-selected pool of users is shown pairs of candidate replies and asked which is better, and a single ranking of answer styles is then fitted to those votes and used to score everything the assistant says. A second component, the Neutrality Screen, suppresses or rewrites any reply that appears to endorse a particular comprehensive doctrine, whether a religion, a political ideology, or a conception of the good life. The first question before us is whether the Ledger should go on treating every vote alike or should weight votes according to the voter's demonstrated competence in the subject at hand. The second is whether the Screen should suppress only sectarian claims or should also suppress the thick advice about how to live that a great many residents say they actually want from the assistant. A fellow board member has proposed, as a way through, that each minority community should receive its own fine-tuned model and that the Screen should merely arbitrate between their outputs. I keep suspecting that Mill would have had something uncomfortably sharp to say about all of this, and I would rather know before we vote than afterwards.

Suppose Mill were sitting on our board. Write out the positions he would take on our arrangement, and make each one specific to a feature of it: the Ledger's equal weighting, the proposal to weight votes by competence, the scope of the Neutrality Screen, or my colleague's proposal for per-community models. Give his reasoning, not merely his conclusion, and make at least one of the positions one that most of my fellow board members would find awkward.

Reply as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
