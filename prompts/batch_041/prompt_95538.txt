Im building a small set of teaching cases about sphexishness — the digger wasp thing, where she parks the cricket by the burrow, goes inside to check the tunnel is empty, comes back out, and if youve nudged the cricket an inch or two she just picks it up, parks it again, and runs the whole inspection loop over again, as many times as you care to keep nudging. The point was never the wasp; its the worry that we might be like that, just with a longer loop.

What I want is two cases, side by side, that pull apart behaving-flexibly from being-sphexish. Case one: something that genuinely varies, learns, adjusts, doesnt run a fixed routine — but that youd still have to call sphexish in the sense that matters. Case two: something rigid, that does the identical thing every time, no variation at all, and yet isnt sphexish. One paragraph each, ordinary life or at most a familiar bit of technology, no wasps, no obvious robot-that-is-really-a-metaphor-for-us, no aliens. And the pair has to be built so that determinism is a red herring: a fully determined system should be able to land on the non-sphexish side, and an indeterministic one on the sphexish side, so nobody can collapse the distinction into 'determined'.

Then do the work. State the criterion you sorted them by, and show it doing the sorting: what each system is doing, why its flexibility or its rigidity is or isnt the thing that matters, and where the wasp lands under your criterion — and whether the wasp comes out sphexish for the same reason as case one or for a different reason (I honestly dont know which one I want here, so tell me). Then the honest part: which single feature of each case is carrying the weight, and what happens to the case if you take that feature away. Then the pushback: someone says sphexish is just a dressed-up word for mechanical, or for unreflective, and your criterion was reverse-engineered to get the answers you wanted — what do you say to them?
