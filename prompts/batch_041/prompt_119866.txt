ok so i have a weird job. I sit on a small committee in my city that hands out a hardship top-up — extra cash each month — and we have maybe 40% of what we'd need to cover everyone who applies. Right now the ranking is: income below a line, then whoever has the biggest medical bills. Everyone on the committee hates the rule, nobody can say exactly why, so before we redesign the thing I want to know what a good answer to "who is worst off" would even have to look like. Not a slogan — the actual spec.

So: write me the desiderata for a measure of who's worse off, i.e. what any adequate answer to the equality-of-what question would have to deliver if it's going to be used to rank real households for a real program. The list, with the case behind each item, then your honest read on which items are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste, and which pairs can't be had together. That last part is what I most want.

Some things I keep tripping over, but dont just take my list as the list:

- people who've adapted to a bad situation and report that they're fine. I visit a guy in his sixties, dialysis three times a week, says his life is good. I don't believe the survey and I also don't want to tell him he's wrong about his own life.
- broke because of a diagnosis vs broke because of gambling. Our current rule treats them identically and half the committee thinks that's obviously right and half thinks it's obviously insane.
- we can only use data we can actually collect, and people shouldn't be able to game it. We had a run of applications that were, lets say, creative.
- and "worst off" seems like a different question from "how equal is everything," which I think matters here but I can't articulate why.

Also: does the choice of currency actually change the ranking much, or is that mostly an academic worry? And if a measure satisfied everything on your list except one item, which item would you rather lose, and which single item do you think rules out the most of the popular answers? If you want, a short line at the end on whether the usual currency options — welfare, resources, capabilities — really come apart on this list or all fail the same items. Keep that part short, the list is the thing.

Dont need the literature, I'm not writing a paper, I just want the spec. Long is fine.
