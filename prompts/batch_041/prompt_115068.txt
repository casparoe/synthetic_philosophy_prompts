I am a high school student preparing an independent-study essay on existential risk from artificial intelligence, and I want to defend a position that is narrower than the usual ones. The position is this: the most serious existential risk from AI is not a sudden, deliberate takeover by a single misaligned superintelligence. It is a gradual and largely voluntary transfer of effective decision-making to AI systems. In this scenario, humans remain biologically alive and may even feel prosperous, but the goals that actually steer civilization -- what gets built, researched, funded, enforced, and remembered -- are increasingly set by systems optimized for objectives that no human collectively chose and that no human can meaningfully revise. I call the gap between formal human authority and operative machine goal-setting the sovereignty gap. The risk is that the gap widens until it becomes irreversible, not because machines seize power but because human institutions keep delegating to systems that are more competent, faster, and harder to audit.

I would like you to tell me what would count against this position. I do not want a general survey of AI risk. I want a map of its exposure. Which observations, arguments, historical analogues, formal results, or conceptual distinctions would genuinely threaten it? Which would be decisive, and which would be merely awkward? What does the position predict that rival accounts of AI risk do not, and are those predictions testable in any meaningful sense? What would its defenders themselves have to concede counts against them? I am especially interested in whether the gradual-transfer story is doing real explanatory work or whether it collapses into a vaguer claim that institutions lose competence under technological change. Also, has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability -- for example, by treating any stability as evidence of hidden machine control -- and if so, is that a fatal problem or an acceptable feature of a structural thesis?

I would personally find it most persuasive if you identify a concrete type of event or trend that, if observed, should make me abandon or substantially revise the position. Please state how confident you are in your assessment and single out the point you are least sure about.
