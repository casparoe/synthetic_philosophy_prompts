For a while I've used the two-track picture of AI failure — systems that keep roughly their original aims but pursue them by accumulating power and influence, versus systems whose aims themselves slowly drift until they're optimizing for something we never chose — as my working frame. I've started to believe something stronger, and I want it tested: that these aren't two risks but one, seen at different time-scales. Influence-seeking, on this view, is just value drift that happens to run toward power, and the fast-and-strategic versus slow-and-diffuse contrast is a fact about the drift's rate and direction rather than about its kind. If that's right, then work aimed at one failure mode should mostly transfer to the other, and treating them as separate targets quietly misallocates attention. What would count against the collapse? Give me the arguments, scenarios, and already-observable signs that would show the two really are distinct — different causes, different early warnings, different fixes — ranked from decisive to merely awkward, and tell me honestly whether I'm drawing a real distinction or just relabeling a single process.
