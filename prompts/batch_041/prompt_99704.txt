I'm a second-year PhD student in programming languages — my day job is operational semantics and interpreter implementation for a small research language — and I've been drawn into a long-running argument with a friend in the philosophy department about Kripkenstein's rule-following considerations. I keep suspecting the whole thing is already familiar to me in another guise, because the puzzle looks structurally like what I deal with every time I write a conformance test suite: a finite record of what a program has done, and an unbounded space of programs consistent with that record. I'd like you to do the translation properly rather than let me hand-wave it. Take my working vocabulary to be: concrete and abstract syntax; an interpreter or operational semantics that says what the machine does at each step; a denotational semantics that assigns a value to every term in advance; a reference implementation versus a written specification; a conformance or regression suite as a finite set of observed input–output pairs; extensibility mechanisms such as runtime method resolution or open-world dispatch; and the ordinary engineering distinction between a bug and a feature, i.e. between behavior that diverges from spec and behavior the spec never fixed. I'm fluent in all of that and a tourist in the philosophy.

Please restate the rule-following problem, including the deviant function that agrees with plus on every case anyone has ever actually computed but diverges on a case nobody has reached, and then restate the main replies — the dispositionalist reply, the appeal to communal agreement or to assertibility conditions, and the 'straight' versus 'skeptical' resolution — in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, one reply at a time. I am particularly suspicious of anything about normativity: an interpreter has no notion of a program being wrong, only of a program doing what it does, and a specification is precisely the thing whose content is in question here, so I want to know whether the 'ought' of rule-following has any counterpart in a world of interpreters, specs and conformance suites, or whether it silently drops out. And which parts have no expression at all in my vocabulary — I'd bet on whatever is doing the real work in the appeal to a community or to shared practice. Don't be diplomatic: if my framework makes the paradox look like a non-problem, say so and identify what it has quietly assumed in order to do that; and if it cannot even state what worries the philosophers, say that too. Keep the translation honest — do not import the philosophy's assumptions and present them as translation.
