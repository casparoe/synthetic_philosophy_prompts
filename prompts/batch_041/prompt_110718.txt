ok so I'm a third year PhD student in cog sci and I've somehow ended up as teh token science person in a little fiction workshop my department runs with the creative writing people, and next month it's my turn to bring a story. I want to write something that actually does the Hofstadter thing — the strange loop, the 'I' as a self-symbol, a thing that refers to the whole of which it's a part — rather than just name-dropping it. Here's the mechanic I want the story built around, and I definately don't want it softened into a vague metaphor: picture a proof assistant that's self-hosting, i.e. its kernel is verified inside its own logic, and somewhere in the library there's a single defined symbol that's meant to stand for the entire system — kernel plus library plus this very definition. Everything's fine while the symbol just sits there as a convenient abbreviation. The story turns on the moment somebody actually has to evaluate it, and evaluating it means running the whole system, which includes evaluating it. I want the reader to feel the mechanism, not just catch the vibe. Two characters would help: one who thinks the loop is harmless, a bookkeeping trick that compiles away, and one who thinks it's a cheat — that the symbol isn't referring to anything, it's just a name for a pile of code that happens to contain the name. Let them have it out in the technical terms, in dialogue, without either one winning by authorial fiat. I'd rather the story end with the loop still open and slightly uncomfortable than with a tidy reveal. Hard constraints: no it-was-a-simulation twist, no and-then-it-became-conscious ending, no dream framing, and don't let the narration itself turn out to be the loop in some cheap gotcha. Ground it in concrete details of the system — the bootstrap, the build, the checksums, the parts that are supposed to be inert — so the self-reference does real work in the plot instead of being wallpaper. Somewhere in there I want one quiet human beat where the loop costs the student something, but don't name the emotion, just show it. Aim for maybe 1200 to 1800 words. And write it as continuous prose please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no section breaks, just paragraphs like a normal story, since I'm printing it for the group and the formatting always gets mangled.
