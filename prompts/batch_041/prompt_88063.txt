I've been in a long-running arguement on a Discord server I'm on about whether half of philosophy is even about anything, and it's got to the point where I want to write my position down properly before I keep defending it. I never studied this stuff formally, I did one year of a humanities degree and then went to work, so I'm sure I'm reinventing things other people have said better, but I've been reading on my own for about a year and I want an audit rather than reassurance. My friend, who did do a philosophy PhD and is very smug about it, says my views can't all be true at once. I don't see where the problem is, but I'm worried he's right, so here's the whole thing roughly in the order I'd defend it.

(1) A huge number of the disputes about what exists — whether numbers exist, whether a chair exists over and above the atoms arranged chair-wise, whether there are any composite objects at all — aren't really disagreements about the world. Both sides agree about every fact you could point at; they just mean different things by exist or by object. (2) So the way to make progress on those is terminological: work out which notion of existence each side is using, and then see that each of them is answering their own question correctly. Nothing gets discovered, it just gets disambiguated. (3) But the fight between moral realists and people who think morality is just a human projection is a genuine disagreement about how things are, and it would stay genuine even if the two sides somehow agreed on every single descriptive fact about the universe. (4) Same-ish for free will: I think compatibilists and incompatibilists are mostly talking past each other, and once you nail down what free will means the whole arguement evaporates. (5) I also think there's a real fact of the matter about whether space and time are infinitely divisible or bottom out in tiny indivisible bits, and that this is true even though no experiment could ever tell us which way it goes. (6) And none of this makes philosophy pointless — even a dispute that's only about words is worth having, because whichever terminology you end up with changes how you think about everything downstream.

Can you go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other and how badly, which pairs only look like they conflict, and if something has to give, which single one would clean up the most mess with the least collateral damage? Please rank the tensions by how serious they are instead of just listing them all as equally bad, and don't give me a reading list, I'll look up the names myself — I just want to know whether my set of views hangs together and what the cheapest fix is.
