hey — I want to post an essay on my blog but I've been staring at it to long, so I need outside comments before I hit publish. Topic is the Hofstadter & Sander line that analogy is the core of cognition (Surfaces and Essences), specifically the bit where they claim every concept is a category and every category is a fluid analogy, so there's no non-analogical thought anywhere. Here's the draft, roughly:

Every time you apply a word to a thing you're making an analogy — calling something a chair is mapping it onto your chair-category, which is itself built out of earlier chair-mappings, and so on. Standard move. My problem: if understanding the new always works by mapping it onto the already-understood, what bottoms out? Hofstadter says nothing does, it's analogies all the way down. But then why isn't the regress vicious? I think the fix is that analogy-making is satisficing — it halts the moment the mapping is good enough that refining it stops earning its keep. I call that stopping point the marginal analogy. If that's right, then grounding isn't some special relation concepts have to the world; it's just wherever we happened to stop. Concepts aren't anchored, they're parked. Two consequences I want to draw from this: (1) what we call rigor is mostly freezing a decent analogy and refusing to thaw it, and a lot of intellectual progress is just thawing old frozen ones; (2) if thinking is satisficing analogy, then a next-token predictor matching a new case against training cases is doing something uncomfortably close to the same thing, which either deflates the whole real-understanding worry or makes it worse, I cant decide which.

What I want: tear it apart. Is the regress worry even real, or am I attacking a strawman version of the view? Is the satisficing move actually doing any work or is it just relabeling something obvious? And which of these points are genuinely novel vs. me reinventing a wheel people have already spun better — I'd rather cut something derivative than get credit for a point thats old news. Also flag anything here that's just word games dressed up as an argument. And tell me how confident you are in your main claims, and which single point your least sure about.
