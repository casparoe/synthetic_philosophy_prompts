My colleague works on the timing of conscious experience, and over the years I have collected her verdicts on a run of phenomena. Her framework is the familiar pair of options for any case in which subjects report content that outruns the stimulus: either the subject really did undergo the experience in real time and the memory or report of it was subsequently rewritten (the Orwellian reading, which I will call O), or the experience itself was assembled after the fact in the order the subject reports, so that nothing is misremembered because nothing was ever experienced otherwise (the Stalinesque reading, S). She has given me four verdicts, each with the consideration that decided it for her.

First, colour phi: two dots are flashed at different positions in different colours, and subjects report a single dot travelling between the positions and changing colour about halfway, that is, before the second dot appeared. Her verdict: S. Decisive consideration: no instruction, incentive, or training could get subjects to report the two static flashes instead, and the interpolation showed up in their other responses, not only in what they said.

Second, the cutaneous rabbit: bursts of taps delivered at two separated points on the arm are felt as a sequence of taps marching from one point to the other. Her verdict: S. Decisive consideration: the marching could not be defeated by instruction or practice, and it governed the subjects' localisation responses as much as their verbal reports.

Third, the flash-lag effect: a moving object and a flash that coincide in time are seen with the object ahead of the flash. Her verdict: S. Decisive consideration: the displacement was not defeatable by task or training, and it appeared in responses other than the report.

Fourth, and in contrast, a case in which subjects report a dot moving between two positions, but when they are instructed to judge the flashes as flashes and to set aside any impression of movement, they report the two static flashes accurately, deny seeing motion, and keep doing so; and their other responses track the true sequence throughout. Her verdict: O. Decisive consideration: the interpolation was defeatable by task, and it left no trace outside the report.

A new phenomenon has now been described, and I want to predict her verdict on it. Subjects report a dot travelling between two flashed positions with a colour change at the midpoint, exactly as in colour phi. Two further things are true of it. First, subjects given a task that directs them to the two flashes report them accurately, deny seeing any movement, and go on doing so; the interpolation is defeatable. Second, on implicit measures their processing follows the interpolated trajectory: eye movements over the trial trace the interpolated path, and responses to a probe are speeded when the probe matches the interpolated content.

Predict her verdict here, and do the rest of the work explicitly. What rule or rules is she running? I want at least the ones that fit her four earlier verdicts equally well, including any that would have produced the same answers for different reasons; what does each predict for the new case; which prediction would you bet on, and why; and what further case, which I could put to her next, would separate the surviving rules? No citations or reading lists, please.

Tell me how confident you are in the prediction and in the rule you attribute to her, and single out the point you are least sure of.
