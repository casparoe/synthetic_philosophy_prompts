Me and a friend got into it for about two hours last night about wether the Renaissance is a real period or just something nineteenth-century historians invented, and from there into whether the whole Middle Ages / Renaissance / modern split is a later imposition that everyone then went hunting for evidence to fill in. My line was that whatever you call it, something did change in how people saw themselves, and I read him this bit from Burckhardt's The Civilization of the Renaissance in Italy (the Middlemore translation):

'In the Middle Ages both sides of human consciousness—that which was turned within as that which was turned without—lay dreaming or half awake beneath a common veil. The veil was woven of faith, illusion, and childish prepossession, through which the world and history were seen clad in strange hues. Man was conscious of himself only as a member of a race, people, party, family, or corporation—only through some general category. In Italy this veil first melted into air; an objective treatment and consideration of the State and of all the things of this world became possible. The subjective side at the same time asserted itself with corresponding emphasis; man became a spiritual individual, recognized himself as such.'

He said, yeah, of course he says that, that's just the story he needs to be telling. And honestly, a page or so later Burckhardt himself shrugs off the demand for evidence on a point like this, which didn't help my case.

What I want is to actually understand the passage before I go back at him. What is the 'veil' supposed to be, exactly — a claim about how medieval people felt from the inside, or a claim about how the surviving sources represent them? And what's the link between an 'objective' treatment of the State becoming possible and the 'subjective' side asserting itself — those sound like they should be opposites, and he seems to run them together as two halves of one change. Also, does the passage just presuppose the periodization it's meant to support, or is there a reading of it that isn't circular?
