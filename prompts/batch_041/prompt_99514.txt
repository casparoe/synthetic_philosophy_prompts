I'm doing a module on military service and the state, and I keep hitting a wall with a passage from Adam Smith's The Wealth of Nations, in the chapter on the expence of defence. The passage is: "The first duty of the sovereign, that of protecting the society from the violence and invasion of other independent societies, can be performed only by means of a military force. But the expence both of preparing this military force in time of peace, and of employing it in time of war, is very different in the different states of society, in the different periods of improvement." I understand the words individually, but I can't get a grip on what work "different states of society" and "periods of improvement" are doing. Is Smith making a historical stage theory here, or just saying that the cost structure of defence varies with economic and political development? My mate thinks it's basically an early public-goods argument, but that feels too clean.

What I'm really trying to figure out is how to map this onto three modern arrangements: (a) a professional army, where defence is something the state buys on a market; (b) conscription, where defence is extracted from citizens as a duty; and (c) armed neutrality of the Swiss, Swedish, or Finnish sort, where the deterrent is partly the trained population and terrain, not a large standing force. I've been calling these the "purchase", "extraction", and "latency" models, though I'm not sure those labels are any good. Does Smith's passage give us a principled way to say which model fits which "state of society"? In particular, does "improvement" push you towards the purchase model, because division of labour makes professional soldiers more effective, or towards extraction, because the state can mobilise more resources? And does armed neutrality fit anywhere in his scheme, or is it a fourth thing -- a small state that substitutes commitment and geography for mass or money? I'd like a close reading of the passage, not a general history of conscription, and please write in British English. I don't need a bibliography or citations; I just want the argument unpacked and the three models tested against it. If my labels are misleading, say so and suggest better ones.
