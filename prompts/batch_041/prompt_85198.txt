"Debate works like this: you take two copies of a model, have them argue opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge, and because the game is zero-sum the honest side wins — any lie the dishonest side tells can be exposed by the other one. The whole point is that the judge doesn't need to be as smart as the models, unlike just having a human grade the answers, which only works when the human can actually check the work. There's a theorem that debate with a polynomial-time judge can handle anything in PSPACE, while a judge on its own can only do NP, so the debaters make the judge exponentially more powerful. They even showed it works in an experiment — a weak MNIST classifier judging a debate went from about 59% to about 89% accuracy. He said it's sometimes called amplification, or iterated amplification. The main worry people raise is the obfuscated arguments problem: a dishonest debater buries the judge in complexity so that the rebuttal can't be checked, and the judge ends up siding with whoever sounds more confident — which is basically reward hacking again. There's also an assumption that the two debaters are equally capable; if one is smarter it just wins, which is a bit circular when the whole point is to supervise something smarter than you. So in principle debate solves the alignment problem for superintelligence, which is why it's the default approach in the field now."
