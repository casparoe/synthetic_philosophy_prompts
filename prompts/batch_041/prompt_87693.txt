I retired from a career in [field] a couple years ago and, to my wifes mild alarm, I've spent the last year reading philosophy of mind. The thesis I keep chewing on is the one that says the brain isn't just *like* a computer but literally *is* one — that what matters is the program, not the meat it happens to run on. I'd like to write a satire of that literal claim, where the jokes come from the philosophy and not from me being smug about it.

My idea: an internal document from a fictional company that does "cortex maintenance" — treat a human brain the way a managed-IT shop treats a server. Say a monthly incident report plus five or six ticket entries, around 650 words. The engineers should be sincere, competent computationalists who genuinely believe the substrate is irrelevant, and then let them run head-first into the places where that gets strange — that nobody can say cleanly where the "software" stops and the "hardware" starts, that any old lump of matter can be read as implementing some program if you're clever enough, that two identical programs running on different wetware make you wonder whether it's one customer or two, that a machine shuffling symbols hasn't obviously been handed any meaning. Deadpan, no winking at the reader.

Please don't just do the lazy "hurr durr the brain isn't a von Neumann machine" bit — I know the analogy is loose and I know what multiple realizability is. The good version takes the claim seriously from the inside and lets the absurdity surface out of the details. Make it funny to a philosopher of mind and still readable to my brother-in-law. Give the company some of its own house jargon for these glitches so it feels like a real shop, and keep the target the thesis itself, not any particular author.
