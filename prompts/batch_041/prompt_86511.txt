A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about who deserves what, and I came away with a set of gut verdicts that I cannot get to line up under any single principle. I want you to tell me what principle, if any, captures them.

Here are the cases and my verdicts. A surgeon performs a difficult operation flawlessly and saves a life: she deserves the fee and the praise. A lottery winner collects ten million dollars: he is entitled to the money but does not deserve it in the same sense; I would not say he earned it. A man inherits his father's fortune: same verdict, entitled but not deserved, though I do not think anyone may take it from him. A runner wins a race after years of training, and her closest rival trained just as hard but has a body that simply will not go as fast: I think the winner deserves to win, because the race is about who is fastest, not who tried hardest. Now the case that trips me up: a researcher grinds away for twenty years on a dead end and discovers nothing of value, while a colleague stumbles onto a genuine cure in a weekend by accident. I feel the grinder deserves something and the lucky one maybe deserves less, even though she produced vastly more good. And the mirror case: a woman with an extraordinary gift composes music effortlessly, almost without trying, that moves millions; I think she fully deserves her acclaim, effort or no effort. Finally, a con artist who works tirelessly at a fraud: I do not think he deserves anything but the punishment, and the effort does not count in his favor at all.

I can feel these pulling in different directions. Is there a single principle, or a small set of them, that yields my verdicts — or close to them — and states exactly when effort matters, when contribution matters, and how desert differs from mere entitlement? Tell me what the principle implies for a case I have not considered — say, two people who jointly and equally produce a great good, one of whom could have done it alone — and be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot save. Please answer in American English.
