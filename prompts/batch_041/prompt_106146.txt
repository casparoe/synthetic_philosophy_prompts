I'm running a session on uncertain evidence for our reading group and we're using Kurosawa's Rashomon — the four tellings of what happened in the grove (the bandit, the wife, the samurai speaking through the medium, and finally the woodcutter's own version back at the gate), plus the baby at the end.

Give me a reading of the film through Jeffrey conditionalization. What I actually want to get at: the audience never learns which account is true, so there's nothing to conditionalize on except the disjunction — one of them is more or less right — and you know that from the first frame, so conditionalizing on it buys you nothing. What shifts is how much weight you give each telling, which is exactly the Jeffrey case: you end up with a mixture of the posteriors you'd have had if each story were true, weighted by your new credences in the stories. Does the film's audience really look like that? And does the reading survive the rigidity requirement — Jeffrey conditioning needs your conditional credence in the hypothesis given each evidence item to stay put while you reweight, but here the tellings contaminate each other (each one is self-serving, one arrives through a medium, the woodcutter's comes last and retroactively recolours everything), so the partition isnt cleanly reweightable. If rigidity fails, say so, and say what that leaves of the film-as-illustration.

Then the ending: the woodcutter taking the baby, the priest getting his faith back — is that an update or a leap? If no update rule licenses it, say that instead of dressing it up in probability.

Don't reduce the film to an illustration and don't bend the theory to fit the film. End by saying what the film shows about uncertain evidence that the formal literature doesnt, if anything. 600 words max.
