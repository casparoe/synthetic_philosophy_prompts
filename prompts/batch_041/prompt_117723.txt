I dropped out of a physics PhD and now read philosophy of physics for fun, and I've got a knot I can't untie. Here are my gut verdicts. Slide the whole universe three feet to the left, keeping every distance and relation the same: nothing has happened, that's one world described twice. Same if everything happens a year later, and same if the whole thing is mirrored left-right, even though nobody in it could ever tell. Swap two electrons that are identical in every respect: still nothing. But spin the whole universe around some axis and I say something has changed, because a bucket of water out there would have a curved surface -- rotation looks like a real fact about the world and not a fact about our labels. (Though maybe the bucket is spinning relative to the distant stars rather than to space itself.) Now the case that started all this. Take a solution to Einstein's equations, pick some region of spacetime, and shuffle the spacetime points inside that region however you like while leaving everything outside it alone and dragging the field along so that nothing outside changes. Outside matches perfectly; inside it doesn't. My gut says that's one world described twice, same as the slide. But if the slide is nothing, the points of spacetime can't be little individuals with identities of their own -- and then what was the bucket spinning relative to? Is there a single principle that gives me the slide, the mirror, the electron swap, the bucket and the shuffle all at once, or do I have to give one of them up? And whichever way it comes out, what does it say about a case I didnt list: an otherwise empty universe containing exactly one sphere -- does it have a size, and a position?
