Please write the retirement toast I am to give for a colleague who has spent her career on the paradoxes of constitutional self-amendment — whether a document can authorize the revision of the very clause that governs its own revision, whether an amending power can be bound by the thing it amends, and what it means for a rule to be applied to itself — and who for years has also run a game night at which the players amend the rules as play proceeds, including the rules about how rules may be amended. I want the toast itself run as that game: it should state up front two or three explicit rules governing the toast (say, that every claim in it must be true, that any rule may be changed with the consent of the room, and that at least one rule is entrenched and can be changed only by a higher bar than the rest), and then it should actually amend itself as it goes, never exempting itself from its own rules, so that the paradoxes she worked on become the structure of the speech rather than its topic. Half the room will be legal and political philosophers who will catch every move and know at once if I cheat; the other half are her family, who need to follow it without the machinery being explained at them and need to hear something genuinely affectionate. Five minutes, please, in British English, and it should be clear by the end whether the rules we finish under are the rules we began with, and why that matters.
