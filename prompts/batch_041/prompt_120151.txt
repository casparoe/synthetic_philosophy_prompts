I'm not a philosopher, just somone who reads a lot of blogs and gets stuck on things. I came across a thought experiment about a careenium, which is basically a big box full of tiny magnetic marbles called simms that bounce around and sometimes stick together into bigger clusters called simmballs. The simms are the substrate and the simmballs are the symbols, and the simmballs have their own behavior, and you can predict a lot by talking about them. But when a simmball seems to make something happen, I can't tell whether the simmball is actually pushing the simms around or whether the simms are doing all the pushing and the simmball is just a pattern we're tracking. It doesn't feel right to say the simmball is nothing, because the higher-level description is sometimes the only useful one, and you'd miss real regularities if you dropped it. But it also doesn't feel right to say it adds a new push. Is there a name for that exact in-between relationship? I know epiphenomenalism is nearby, but that seems to say the higher-level thing does nothing at all, and downward causation seems to say it really does push. I'm after the position where the higher-level pattern is real, indispensable for explanation, but all the causal work is still at the lower level.

I keep noticing the same shape in other places. A traffic jam moves backward down the highway even though no car is driving backward. The average price in a market goes up even though no single buyer or seller makes it go up. A person decides to raise their arm, and we can describe it as the person causing the arm to move, but the arm is also just muscles and neurons doing their thing. In each case there's a higher-level thing that seems to cause something, a lower-level story that seems to do the actual causing, and a feeling that the higher-level thing is not just a convenient fiction. So: does this have an established name? If there is one, what is it, and how does it differ from the nearest ideas like epiphenomenalism, downward causation, supervenience, and weak emergence? If there isn't one, can you coin one and define it so I can tell what falls inside and what falls outside? I care more about the carving than the label. For instance, is the careenium case the same as the traffic jam case, or is there a difference in whether the higher-level pattern can be read off the lower level? And is there a name for the specific mistake of thinking that because the higher-level description is indispensable, it must be doing extra causal work? I'm not looking for a reading list or citations, just the concept and its edges.
