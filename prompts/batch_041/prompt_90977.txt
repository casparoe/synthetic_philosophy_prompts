Consider a widespread institutional practice: access to a valued status is conditioned on an ordeal that is long, costly, and largely disconnected from the skills the status is said to require. The surgical residency of low-paid apprenticeship, the dissertation few will read, the months of boot camp, the initiation rites of fraternities and military units, the conspicuous expenditure on a wedding or a luxury good. Such ordeals are usually defended on the ground that they prove something -- commitment, seriousness, a certain hardness -- and their defenders often insist that the cost is the point, that a cheaper version would not do.

What does the practice presuppose about how trust and sincerity get communicated? Reconstruct the implicit theory, and separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with. One reading is the handicap principle, familiar from animal displays: a signal is trustworthy only when it is so costly that only a sender of the relevant kind could afford it, which requires that the cost fall more heavily on the wrong kind of candidate than on the right kind -- that paying it, and not merely surviving it, carries the information. Another reading is cheaper: the ordeal is just a filter for a trait that was already there, and the cost is incidental. The two are not equivalent, and the practice may be underdetermined between them.

Then press on coherence. A costly signal is supposed to be hard to fake, yet the same defenders treat the ordeal as a character test that a cynic can pass by merely enduring it; can one practice presuppose both that the cost guarantees sincerity and that sincerity is a separate trait the ordeal measures? Does the ordeal communicate because it is meant to be understood as a signal, or merely because it causes observers to form a belief -- and does the practice need the first, meaning-laden notion, or only the second? Finally, if institutions came to treat such ordeals as pure waste and replaced them with cheap, direct verification of the traits in question, what would have to change, and what, if anything, would be lost?
