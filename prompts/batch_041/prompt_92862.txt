I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, and I want to put a decision tree on our internal engineering wiki that takes a developer from "a colleague whose judgment I generally trust disagrees with me about X" to a named position on how to respond, so we can stop arguing about labels and see where we actually split. Build the tree from the logical space first and locate the positions in it second: each branch node should be a plain-language question about the situation that someone can answer without already knowing which view it leads to — whether the colleague has evidence I lack or merely weighs the same evidence differently; whether the disputed claim is a step-by-step checkable one or a holistic judgment (say, "this function is too clever"); whether I have any independent reason to distrust my own performance on this occasion, such as fatigue, deadline pressure, or a poor track record on this kind of question; and whether the other person is a peer on this topic or merely a competent generalist. The leaves should include equal-weight conciliationism, steadfastness, the total-evidence or "right reasons" view on which disagreement counts only as evidence about who got it right, and the view that peerhood itself dissolves under scrutiny; add any further positions you think belong, and name each leaf in a way a non-philosopher can act on. Where the tree reaches a combination nobody defends, say so and diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and explicitly flag any two positions my questions cannot separate rather than pretending the tree distinguishes them.
