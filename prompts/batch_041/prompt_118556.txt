A small AI safety research group has spent two years trying to train a class of failures out of their models -- confident fabrication, say -- by scaling data, compute, and reward models, on the assumption that the problem is hard but reachable from where they stand. A colleague proposes the MU-puzzle as the right lens: starting from the string MI and applying a fixed set of rewrite rules, the string MU can never be derived, and the proof does not search the space of derivations; it exhibits an invariant (the number of I's is never a multiple of three) that every reachable string preserves and the target violates. The companion idea, jootsing -- jumping out of the system to reason about it rather than within it -- is supposed to name the move such a proof exemplifies.

What are the most important implications of this pair of ideas for how the group should decide whether to keep pushing or to step outside? I care most about implications that would actually change what they do: how to tell a provable in-principle barrier from a merely unsolved problem; what distinguishes genuine jootsing from a new move inside the same system; and where the analogy breaks down, since string rewriting is a clean formal case and research programs are not.
