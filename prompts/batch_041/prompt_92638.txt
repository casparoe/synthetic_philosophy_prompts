Our school's history teacher has spent this term giving verdicts on our projects against the three ways Nietzsche says history can be used -- monumental, antiquarian, and critical -- and I want to work out her actual rule before I commit to a project for next term. Her verdicts so far, in the order she gave them: a catalogue of every gravestone in the old cemetery and every name in the parish ledgers, with no argument and no lesson drawn, approved; an essay showing that the town's most celebrated benefactor made his fortune in a trade we would now prosecute, and concluding that his plaque should come down, approved; an anniversary speech that held the founders up as proof that greatness was possible here and used them to shame the present for its smallness, while explicitly refusing to ask anyone to copy them, approved; a bicentennial display presenting the town's history as one long climb to the present, "the best moment yet to be from here", rejected; a study proving that every reform this town has ever attempted was absorbed by the same three families, and that reform here is therefore naive, rejected; and a project imagining how the town would look today had the 1910 railway vote gone the other way, with no moral drawn about the present, approved.

The project I am considering is a permanent Hall of the Great Dead in the school: three figures from the town's past chosen each year, their portraits kept up forever, and an annual assembly at which students pledge to emulate them. Its proposer says it will teach us that greatness is possible here; the pilot did in fact leave students reading more and starting more projects of their own, though several of them said the figures felt like costumes rather than people. Predict her verdict on the Hall, set out the two or three rules her six verdicts are jointly consistent with and what each of them predicts here, tell me which rule you think she is really running and why, and name the further case that would tell those rules apart. I have already ruled out the reading that she simply ranks the three modes, since the speech was monumental and she approved it, but say so if I am wrong about that.
