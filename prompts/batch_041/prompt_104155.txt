ok so I'm the chair of the community-standards board at a small residential college and I need help writing the opinion we circulate before Thursdays meeting. Here's the mess. Our honor code has a "toleration clause": if you know someone cheated, stole, or lied in an academic matter, you're required to report it, and if you don't, you're in violation yourself. It's been on the books forever and it's basically a dead letter — orientation doesn't mention it, nobody enforces it, and if you ask students (and honestly a couple of faculty) they'll tell you ratting on a classmate is worse than whatever the classmate did. Well, last month a first-year came forward to say that two weeks earlier she'd learned her roommate plagiarized a final paper, she sat on it, and now it's before us as a toleration-clause case, because somebody else found out and filed. Her whole defense is basically "everyone knows that clause isn't real." Half my board wants to dismiss it — punishing someone for failing to do a thing the whole community has quietly agreed not to do is just cruel, it was her roommate, and she's a first-year who'd have been socially torched. The other half says the clause is in the code, the code is what we voted on, and if we let it slide again we might as well not have a code at all — and besides, we cite the honor code in every admissions brochure, so the stakes aren't just internal.

I need a short written opinion I can read out: a concrete disposition of this specific case (dismiss, sanction, or something in between, and if we sanction, then what, and on what timeline), plus a recommendation for what we do with the toleration clause going forward — keep it, rewrite it, or scrap it — and I want it to genuinely engage the argument on the side I end up ruling against instead of just asserting the outcome. Also please don't just tell me "enforce the rules" or "be merciful"; I can get that from either half of the board. And I guess underneath all of it there's the thing I actually can't decide, which is whether a rule that everyone has stopped following is still a rule or just words, so say something about that too.
