My friend just got appointed to our state court of appeals and I've been reading her opinions mostly so I can give her a hard time about them. Shes been ruling on a new state law about automated hiring tools, which is actually my area since I'm a backend engineer. The law says that any employer who uses an "automated decision system" to screen applicants has to give the applicant, on request, "the score and the principal factors" behind the decision, and it defines that system as "software that makes or assists a hiring decision without meaningful human involvement." Her three calls so far: (1) a dumb keyword filter counts as an automated decision system even though there's no learning involved; (2) an employer had to hand over the factors even though the vendor swore they were a trade secret; (3) an employer that farmed the screening out to a staffing agency still had to disclose, because the employer is the one "using" it.

Now a new case is coming up: the tool is a big ensemble model and nobody can actually state "the principal factors" in plain language — the best the vendor can do is dump a few thousand feature weights, and even they can't explain which ones matter.

What do you think she'll rule? And more to the point, what rule is she actually running — it looks to me like either "read the words literally and live with the result" or "read them to serve what the law was obviously for, which is letting applicants understand why they got filtered out." Say what each rule predicts here, which one fits her three earlier calls better, and what a fourth case would have to look like to actually force the two apart. Skip the citations and the reading list, I just want the reasoning.
