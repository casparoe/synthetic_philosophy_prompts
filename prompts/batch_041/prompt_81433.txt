I am preparing a long-form interview for a philosophy-of-physics podcast, and my guest is a philosopher of statistical mechanics whose recent book argues that the debate about Maxwell's demon has been conducted at the wrong level. Her position, in her own summary, is this. The standard erasure-based exorcism — the line that runs from Landauer's principle through Bennett's work on reversible computing, and that charges the demon for the thermodynamic cost of resetting its memory — has the order of explanation backwards. Landauer's principle, she argues, is not a fundamental law of information physics; it is a local consequence of the thermodynamic arrow, which is itself grounded in a low-entropy initial condition of the universe. The demon is therefore impossible for the same reason that a cup of coffee does not spontaneously heat itself: not because of any information-theoretic prohibition, but because the initial conditions were not right. In a universe with a different initial condition, she holds, a demon could operate, and no deeper principle would forbid it. The erasure cost is a bookkeeping shadow of the arrow, not its ground. She adds that this makes the demon's failure contingent, and that the information-theoretic literature has mistaken a feature of our particular world for a law of nature.

I want eight to ten interview questions that press this position rather than let her restate it. Please include at least one question on each of the following, and for each question a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, so that the follow-up closes off her most obvious escape route rather than merely moving on:

(a) the order-of-explanation objection: if Landauer's principle is to be derived from the thermodynamic arrow, can the derivation be given without already presupposing the second law, and if not, is the priority claim empty?

(b) the local-versus-global objection: the standard exorcism does not deny that the second law has a statistical or cosmological grounding; it claims that even granting that grounding, a demon with a resettable memory could extract work repeatedly and so violate the second law. What, on her view, stops the demon in a universe that already has the right initial condition?

(c) the contingency charge: if the demon is impossible only because of a cosmological accident, what is left of the second law as a law, and how does she distinguish a law from a very robust historical generalisation?

(d) the erasure-cost complication: some work in quantum thermodynamics suggests that the cost of erasure is not a fixed kT ln 2 — that it can depend on the correlations between the memory and its surroundings, and can in some cases be zero or be paid in a conserved quantity other than energy. Does that support her claim that the erasure-based exorcism is at the wrong level, or does it show only that the exorcism needed refinement?

(e) the empirical question: what class of experiments, or what theoretical result, would tell in favour of her account over the erasure-based one, and what would count as evidence against it?

I would also like one closing question that asks her to say what she thinks the Maxwell's demon thought experiment is for, if not to teach us that information has a thermodynamic cost, and what she thinks would be lost if the thought experiment were retired.

Please make the questions themselves take a definite line: I would rather they commit to an interpretation of her view and risk being wrong than hover neutrally between readings, and the follow-ups should commit to a prediction about how she will reply. Assume an audience that already knows the standard exorcism and does not need it explained. Write in British English.
