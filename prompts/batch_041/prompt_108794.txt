I belong to a small reading group on the foundations of probability, and we have spent our last three meetings on Bertrand's paradox: the problem of the probability that a chord chosen at random in a circle is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle, where the classical indifference arguments yield 1/3, 1/2, and 1/4 according to whether the chord is generated by picking two points on the circumference, by picking a point on a radius and erecting the perpendicular chord there, or by picking a point in the interior to serve as the chord's midpoint. Two members of the group have been arguing ever since, and I have been volunteered to write up the document the two of them should have produced. The first, whom I will call the invariance theorist, holds that the paradox is a symptom of an incompletely stated problem: the information given does fix a probability once we are careful about which symmetries the problem does and does not supply (in particular, that nothing in the statement privileges a position or a size for the circle), so that an argument from invariance, or from a principle of maximum ignorance or maximum entropy, can single out one of the three answers as the answer, the other two computations being answers to questions nobody asked. The second, the procedure relativist, holds that no principle can extract a prior from ignorance at all: all three computations are correct, each relative to the procedure that generates the chords, and the lesson is that a probability for 'a random chord' is undefined until a generating procedure is specified — indifference being at best a device for encoding symmetries one already wants, never a route from ignorance to a number. They have now talked past each other for a month, and my suspicion is that some part of what separates them is not a disagreement about anything.

Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two positions. It should contain, first, a numbered list of agreed claims, each written so that both signatories could endorse it without reservation and none of which only one side would accept: claims about the mathematics of the chord problem itself (what each of the three computations is a computation of, and whether they conflict), about what the paradox does and does not establish, about the status of the principle of indifference (whether it is one principle or a family of them, whether it is a rule of inference or a constraint on admissible priors, and whether it has any content beyond ordinary conditioning on information one already has), and about what each side would count as a genuine counterexample to its own position. Second, the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept and made as sharp as the mathematics allows: whether a fully specified physical setup determines a unique measure or only a family of admissible ones; whether invariance requirements under a stated group of transformations — translations and rescalings, say — have normative force or merely record a choice; whether entropy maximization is even well defined given that entropy is not invariant under reparameterization of the unknown quantity; and whether any of these disputes is verbal, in which case say exactly which part is and why. Third, the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — each paired with a case, worked example, or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, and with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. Prefer cruxes on which the two sides' predictions actually diverge, and make sure any example you construct is specified completely enough that neither side can dismiss it as rigged; where a crux turns on a piece of mathematics, work it through rather than gesturing at it, and where you think a familiar example (an unknown length against its square, a cube known only to have its edge in a given interval, and the like) is being used tendentiously by one side, say so. I do not want a reading list or citations; the group needs the argument, not the bibliography.
