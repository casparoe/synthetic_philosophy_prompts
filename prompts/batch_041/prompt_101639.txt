I've been going down a rabbit hole lately on free will, and I keep coming back to a question that I can't find a straight answer to: which kinds of freedom are actually *worth* wanting? Like, if I had to pick, what's the freedom I'd refuse to give up, and what's the freedom that sounds nice in a speech but wouldn't change anything about my life?

I made a list of candidate "flavors" of freedom. Some of these I've seen versions of, some I just made up because they seem like things people actually care about:

1. Rewind freedom — if you rewound the universe to just before I chose, I could've picked the other thing.
2. Reasons freedom — I would've done differently if there'd been a good reason to; I notice when the facts change and adjust.
3. Self-authorship — I built my own values over the years instead of just absorbing whatever I was handed.
4. Second-order freedom — I want the wants I have; my desires don't feel like they were installed by someone else.
5. Answerability freedom — I'm the kind of thing it makes sense to ask "why'd you do that?" and to expect an answer from, and to hold to that answer.
6. Wobble freedom — my choice isn't fully fixed by my past, even if the extra wobble is just noise with nothing to do with my reasons.
7. Surprise-yourself freedom — I can be genuinely unsure what I'll do next, and be right to be unsure.
8. Veto freedom — I can't stop myself from wanting what I want, but I can flat-out refuse to act on it.

Can you rank these from most to least worth wanting for someone living an ordinary life, give each a 0-100 score for how much it actually matters, and say which ones are load-bearing for moral responsibility (i.e. if it vanished, blaming and praising would stop making sense) versus which are just nice to have? And if I could only keep one, which?

  ],

}
