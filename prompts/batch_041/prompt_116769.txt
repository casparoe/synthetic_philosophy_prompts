I'm writing a piece on the strange afterlife of Kepler's astrology and I want a joke I can actually use in it. It needs to be funny to someone who knows the material, not merely to someone who knows that the great astronomer also cast horoscopes: the punchline should turn on what Kepler actually held — that the planets act on the soul through rays and geometrical harmonies, that aspects and configurations do real work while much of the traditional apparatus of signs, houses and elaborate interpretation does not, and that he spent decades producing calendars and nativities while insisting that most of what passed for astrology was rubbish and only a reformed, physical core deserved credit. Something that captures the awkwardness of a man who wanted to be remembered as a philosopher of nature and yet earned his bread as the Imperial Mathematician whose forecasts made people treat him as a prophet, and who could not quite bring himself to abandon the art he was busy demolishing. Keep it short enough to tell at dinner, and please skip any setup about Newton and apples.
