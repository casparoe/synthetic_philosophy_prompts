I am preparing a session for newly elected city council members on the boundary problem: any democratic decision presupposes a demos, yet the demos cannot itself be democratically chosen without circularity. Plan how to teach it. What must be introduced first, and what depends on what, for the circularity to feel like a genuine problem rather than a paradox trick; which single concrete case should carry the session and what it must have and lack (you might consider a municipal tax whose burden falls on commuters and students who cannot vote in the city, but choose and justify); the misconceptions they will bring—that boundaries are natural, that inclusion is always more democratic, that the all-affected principle is either obvious or unusable—and exactly where in the sequence to head each off; what to leave out at this level and say so; and one closing question that would show whether they can state the problem without dissolving it by fiat.
