A mid-sized democracy is considering a reform of conscientious exemption from compulsory national service. Under current law, a tribunal must be satisfied that an applicant's objection to military service rests on a sincere and settled moral or religious conviction; successful applicants are excused from military service but assigned an equivalent term of civilian service. The arrangement satisfies almost no one. Applicants find the tribunal's inquiry into their beliefs humiliating and arbitrary, and the civilian alternative is unpopular enough that some who are eligible for it would rather serve in uniform.

The proposed reform replaces case-by-case exemption with a fixed annual quantity of transferable exemption permits, equal in number to the exemptions the tribunal currently grants. A permit confers complete release from the service obligation and may be sold to any willing buyer at any mutually agreed price. Two variants are on the table.

Variant A: the state auctions the permits to the highest bidders. No official asks about anyone's beliefs; the auction price alone determines who is released, and how many.

Variant B: the state continues to issue permits only to applicants it certifies as sincere, exactly as at present. Certified holders, however, are free to resell their permits, and buyers need not offer any reason for wanting one.

Proponents of Variant A argue that it is the only arrangement that keeps the state out of the business of grading consciences, and that price rations releases at least as reliably as a tribunal, and more transparently. Supporters of Variant B present it as the compromise that answers the objection that a pure auction simply sells evasion to the wealthy.

Write an essay on whether either variant should be adopted, reaching a verdict and defending it. Be precise about where you locate the objection, if there is one: in the allocation of a release by price, in the transferability of a permission whose original point was conscience, in the state's use of conscience as the trigger for a tradable entitlement, or somewhere else. Address how far the familiar objections to markets in organs, in votes, or in scarce goods during emergencies carry over to this case, and identify which features of the exchange your verdict actually turns on. If you conclude that one variant is defensible and the other is not, say what the difference between them is supposed to accomplish, and whether that difference survives scrutiny.
