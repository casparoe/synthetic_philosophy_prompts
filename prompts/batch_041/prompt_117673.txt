I'm finishing a philosophy degree and writing a paper on the ethics of promising — basically on whether promises are their own kind of reason at all, or whether "I promised" is just a dressed-up way of saying "I've given you grounds to expect something." My supervisor keeps telling me to stop reading and start from cases, so here's where I've got to. I'd like you to take the role of someone who's spent a career on normative ethics and the philosophy of promising, think carefully, and help me find the principle (or principles) behind my gut verdicts:

(i) I promised my friend Dana I'd help her move on Saturday. Friday night my kid spikes a fever. I think I'm allowed to bail — but not just to bail; I have to tell her the second I know, and I'd still owe her something (I'd offer to pay for half the movers). (ii) I promised my brother I'd co-sign a loan, then found out he'd lied to me about his debts. I don't think I'm merely *permitted* to back out — the promise is gone, void rather than outweighed. (iii) I promised a coworker I'd keep his job hunt quiet, then found out he's interviewing at a firm mid-merger with ours. I think I can tell our manager, and it's not a close call. (iv) I promised a friend I'd go to her one-woman show, then got a much better invitation for the same night. I think I have to go to the show. (v) Here's the one that bugs me: I promised a colleague I'd put his name forward for a fellowship. Nobody knows I made the promise, and he'd never find out if I didn't do it. I still think I have to. (vi) At a charity gala the host asks me to promise I'll come next year and I say "yes, absolutely." I don't think that binds me in the slightest. (vii) My father, dying, asked me to promise to visit my mother every Sunday. I think I'm bound by that, and bound *more* tightly than if my mother had asked me herself in a good mood — though I also think it was a bit unfair of him to ask. (viii) I promised myself I'd quit drinking for a month; breaking it feels like a failure of willpower, not like wronging anybody. (ix) I promised my grandmother I'd keep going to church. She has since died. I still feel bound, but I honestly can't say to whom. (x) I got drunk and promised a guy at the bar I'd lend him my car. I don't think that one counts.

The pair I can't reconcile is (v) and (vi): in both, nobody is relying on anything and nobody would ever know, and I get opposite verdicts. My tentative principle, which I'd like you to test and repair rather than just admire: breaking a promise is wrong because you've invited someone to plan around you, made yourself a fixed point in their deliberation, and breaking it betrays that. Does any version of this keep (v) and (ix) while giving up (vi)? And is my void/outweighed talk in (i)–(iii) a real distinction or am I just dressing up different degrees of weight?
