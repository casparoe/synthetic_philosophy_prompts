A friend of mine has very firm views about bringing people into existence and I can't work out the rule she's actually running, so I'm hoping you can. Her verdicts so far, as best I can reconstruct them: she says it's flatly wrong to create a child who will live a life of constant, severe, unrelievable pain; she says it's wrong to create a child whose life will contain more suffering than happiness overall; she says it's fine, though not required, to create a child who will be healthy and happy; and she says the same about a child whose life will be pleasant in a completely unremarkable way. Whenever I press her, she says the point is that the asymmetry is about the act of bringing someone into being, not about how good the resulting life turns out, and that making someone worse off and failing to give someone a benefit are seperate things that people keep running together. She also gets quite cross at the suggestion that we're obliged to create happy people just because we could.

Now her sister is deciding whether to go ahead with a pregnancy and has asked her for her view. The child would be wanted and loved, and would have a genuinely good life on the whole — plenty of happiness, close relationships, work she'd find interesting, that sort of thing — but would also have a condition causing real, recuring pain: not constant agony, but several stretches of significant suffering, plus some genuine hardship along the way. Taken as a whole the life is clearly worth living and clearly contains more happiness than suffering.

What will my friend say about this case? I'm much less interested in whether she's right than in the rule she's running. Start with your prediction, then in continuous prose — no headings, bullets, bold or tables, just write it the way you'd write an email — give me the candidate rules that fit her earlier verdicts, say which of them the new case actually pulls apart and how, and name the further case I should put to her to find out whether she really means the strong version of her asymmetry or a weaker one. For what it's worth I'm inclined to think she's just holding the sensible view that it's wrong to create a life not worth living and nothing more, but she clearly thinks she means something stronger, so tell me if the earlier verdicts are actually underdetermined between those readings and she could go either way without contradicting herself.
