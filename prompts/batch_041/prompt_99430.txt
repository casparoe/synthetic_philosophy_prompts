I keep running into a conceptual muddle when I travel and I want you to sort it out. When does a local practice -- how much to tip, where to stand on an escalator, whether to wait for the walk signal when no cars are coming -- become something a visitor is genuinely obligated to follow, rather than just something that would be rude or awkward to skip? My suspicion is that people are collapsing two different things: a convention in the coordination sense (arbitrary, could have been otherwise, held up by mutual expectation and common knowledge) and a norm that carries real evaluative weight. Explain that distinction and then actually use it on the hard cases -- the practice that's mostly dead so there's barely a convention left to follow (everyone jaywalks, tipping is going optional), the practice the visitor comes from a conflicting version of, and the case where locals themselves don't conform but still expect visitors to. I want the explanation to earn its keep on those cases rather than just define the terms and stop.
