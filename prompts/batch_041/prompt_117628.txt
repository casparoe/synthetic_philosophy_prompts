There is a familiar kind of mechanical rigidity, illustrated by the wasp that drags its prey to the burrow, goes inside to check, and — when the prey is moved a few inches — drags it back and checks again, looping indefinitely. Explain the conceptual issue that this case raises and that the label sphexishness is meant to mark: why the rigidity looks like a matter of degree rather than kind, and why the notion threatens to go vacuous once one notices that any finite creature's behavior can be described as the unfolding of some fixed routine. Then consider a wasp that, when interrupted, does not merely start over but works through a fixed lookup table of recovery routines. Is that a genuine gain in flexibility or only rigidity one level up? Say what would count as a real step away from it, and whether the distinction you end up drawing gives any purchase on the free-will question.
