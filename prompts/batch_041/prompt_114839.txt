My amateur astronomy club just got a small grant to build a beacon — a directional dish we'd point at one specific nearby star system, sending a short encoded pulse train over a few months, basically a hello. I'm the one who'd write the scheduling and encoding software, so I have a real stake in it, and the whole thing has turned into an argument at our meetings that I can't settle. One member, Dan, is going around saying this is the single most irresponsible thing a group of humans could do, and his reasoning isn't the usual aliens-will-be-nice vs aliens-will-be-mean stuff. He says the real problem is that you can never verify anyone's intentions across that kind of distance and timescale, so even a perfectly peaceful civilization has to allow that the other side might not be, and that the other side is running the same calculation about you, and knows you're running it about them — a loop you can't climb out of, no matter how nice everyone actually is. Then he adds a second part: technology doesn't advance at a steady rate, so whoever is behind right now could be far ahead in a couple centuries, which means any window you have is closing, and hitting first is cheap next to being wrong. His conclusion is that the only stable move is to stay quiet and hit anything you hear. I think it's paranoid sci-fi, but I honestly can't find the hole in it. When I point out we've been leaking radio and TV into space for decades and nothing has happened, he says that's just evidence the ones who understand this are already quiet — which feels unfalsifiable to me but I can't articulate why.

The part that actually keeps me up: he says that even if the odds of us getting everyone killed are one in a trillion, we'd be trading a tiny probability for an unbounded cost, so the expected value is infinite and no possible benefit competes with it. I write trading systems for a living, so I'm comfortable with expected value reasoning, but this smells like the kind of move that would let you justify never doing anything, ever — and also, our dish is four meters and the power is borrowed, so is anyone even listening? We vote at the next meeting and I need to know what to actually say. I don't want be careful, I want to know which assumptions Dan is smuggling in, which of them are doing the real work, and what would have to be true for him to be right. And separately: does a club of nine people even have standing to make a call like this on behalf of everyone who never agreed to it, or is that a dodge?
