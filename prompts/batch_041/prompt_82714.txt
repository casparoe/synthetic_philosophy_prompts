I am planning a ninety-minute session for a mixed reading group — philosophy graduate students, two neuroscientists, and a hospice social worker — on the thesis that a self can be distributed across brains. I want the session built around the thesis itself rather than around any particular author. Stated in my own terms: a person is a pattern rather than a portion of matter, and the pattern can be realized in more than one head. The version of you that runs in your closest friend's mind is not a dossier of facts about you but a working model that can generate judgments in your voice, including judgments you never made; on this view, your persistence after death comes in degrees and amounts to how much of you is still running somewhere. Two features of the thesis matter most to me. The relation is asymmetric — I am realized in you without your being realized in me. And the thesis owes an answer to whether a pattern realized twice is one subject of experience or two.

What I need is not the exposition but a plan for teaching it. If the statement above is really three theses rather than one, say which of them the session should teach. Tell me what has to be in place before the distribution claim can be evaluated rather than merely pictured, and in what order those components should be introduced, with the dependency stated at each step; I suspect the order is not the order in which the surrounding literature presents it. Tell me which single example should carry the session and what it must have and must not have; my tentative thought is that it needs a surrogate who produces a judgment its source never produced, but I would rather be corrected than accommodated. Tell me the misconceptions this group will bring and the further ones the session is likely to create, with the point in the sequence at which each should be headed off, including whatever my framing above invites. Give me one closing question that would show whether they have taken the thesis as a claim with truth conditions rather than as an elaborate way of saying that the dead are remembered. And tell me what I should openly refuse to settle in ninety minutes.
