For the better part of forty years I did experimental physics; since retiring I have been reading philosophy of science with more enthusiasm than system. I keep meeting the claim that when one theory displaces another, what is carried over is its structure while its ontology is thrown away — I have seen the position called epistemic structural realism — and I find that I can assent to the slogan without seeing what work it does. What I want to test is whether the structuralist's claim of continuity is a substantive thesis or an artifact of how the example is drawn up, so I would rather have a case built to order than another retelling of a famous episode.

Invent a pair of theories, an earlier one and its successor, meeting all of the following. First, they are structurally isomorphic, and you should exhibit the correspondence rather than assert it: every relation by which the earlier theory states its claims has a counterpart in the successor. Second, their ontologies are incompatible — the central entity of the earlier theory cannot exist in the world the successor describes. Third, the successor is empirically superior in at least one respect, and the improvement is owed precisely to whatever replaces that central entity; the discarded thing must have been doing real work in the earlier theory, not sitting decoratively in the background. Fourth, both theories are fictional, of a kind a competent physicist of an earlier century could have proposed, and not the ether, the caloric fluid, or any other familiar episode with the labels changed; the genuine historical cases are tangled up with details I cannot check.

Give me the case in a single paragraph, then do four things: state what the epistemic structural realist, the entity realist (for whom belief in an entity is licensed by our ability to manipulate it, not by the truth of the theory surrounding it), and the anti-realist who reasons from the graveyard of discarded theories each conclude about whether any of the earlier theory's content was retained; identify the one feature of the case that does the work and say what happens when it is removed; and give me the strongest attack available to the entity realist, other than the one I already know, that a purely structural claim is close to vacuous because almost any structure can be imposed on a collection of objects of the right size.
