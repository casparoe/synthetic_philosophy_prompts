My friend clerks for a judge on our state court of appeals and keeps complaining that she cant tell what the judge's interpretive method actually is. The statute in question is our state's mobile vending act. It defines "school" as "a public or private institution providing instruction to students in grades K through 12." It defines "cart" as "a wheeled conveyance with not more than 100 square feet of floor area." And its purpose section says the act exists "to protect children walking to and from school" and "to keep sidewalks passable." Three rulings so far: (1) a taco guy with a 110-square-foot trailer applied for a cart permit; she held he doesn't qualify, a 110-foot trailer is simply not a cart under the definition, no matter how much easier that would of made his life. (2) The act requires vendors to leave "a clear path" on the sidewalk and never says how wide; she held it means five feet, on the ground that the whole point is that people can get past. (3) The act requires a "commissary," defined as a state-licensed food prep facility; a vendor prepped in a church kitchen the state has licensed, and she said that satisfies it, licensed is licensed.

New case: a standalone early-learning center, ages three to five, licensed by the state as a child care center and not as a school, sits on the corner where a vendor wants to park, within 100 feet of the door the little kids use. The health department's FAQ says the buffer covers child care centers; the vendor's lawyer says the definition says K through 12 and a preschool is not a school.

What does she do? Which rule is she actually running, which other rules fit her three rulings just as well, what's your best guess and why, and what case would tell those rules apart? And if her three rulings genuinely don't decide it, tell me what principle she'd be signing onto either way.
