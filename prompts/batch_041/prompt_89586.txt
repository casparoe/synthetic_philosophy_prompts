I have just come away from a long and rather bruising argument with an old friend who is finishing a dissertation on Wittgenstein, and before I revise the position paper I have to hand in next month, I would like a second opinion. You are a philosopher of language and mind with a firm working knowledge of the rule-following considerations — the quus-style argument, on which any finite stretch of past usage is compatible with indefinitely many rules, and the standard responses to it (dispositionalism about meaning, the communitarian or practice-based line, and the quietist or skeptical solution that simply accepts the paradox) — and I would ask you to bring that background to bear rather than reassuring me. Please begin by restating each of the commitments below in your own words and saying what work it is doing in the package, so that I can see whether you have read me right, and only then start looking for the joints where things pull apart. Do not assume that I know which of these is the weak link; I do not, and I would rather be told than have you guess on my behalf.

Here is where I have landed. (1) Meaning is normative: if I mean plus by "plus", that must determine what I ought to answer, so any account on which my meaning something is just a matter of what I am disposed or trained to say fails, since a disposition can never be incorrect. (2) The quus-style argument seems to me to succeed: my entire finite history of usage, inner and outer, is compatible with my having meant quus all along (where quus is just like addition for pairs below 57 and yields 5 otherwise, so that 68 and 57 quus to 5), and nothing in that history selects plus over quus, so no fact about me as an individual constitutes my meaning plus rather than quus. (3) All the same, I am not willing to go quietist: there is a fact of the matter about what I mean, and my meaning plus is straightforwardly true rather than merely a move inside a practice I happen to accept. (4) What does the constituting, then, is the community: my answer is correct just in case it agrees with what the speakers around me do, and it is that shared practice, not anything inside my head, that makes my word mean plus. (5) I also hold that someone who has never belonged to any linguistic community — a child raised alone, or a mathematician who introduces a term for a structure nobody has ever conceived — can mean something perfectly determinate by their words; I would find it absurd to say their words mean nothing until a crowd turns up. (6) I am a realist about arithmetic: 68 plus 57 is 125, and that is not made true by anyone's agreement, communal or otherwise. (7) And I am nagged by the thought that (2) drags in a further commitment I do not want, namely that my use of "green" is no better grounded than some grue-like alternative, so that the distinction between them is an illusion.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension with which, and which pairs only look as though they are; where a tension is genuine, say how severe it is and rank the tensions against each other rather than treating them as equally bad. For each one, spell out what would have to give on either side and what the cheapest repair would cost me in plausibility, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most at the least expense, and whether there is a commitment I would be wisest simply to drop. I am not attached to any particular item surviving; I want the package defensible at the end, even if it looks less tidy than it does now.
