Hey, so I'm a junior and I somehow got put in charge of our philosophy club's topic for the whole spring semester (we meet like 50 minutes a week and at the end everyone has to hand in one 3000-word essay for a competition). The club voted for the sad-art thing — why we love stuff that makes us miserable. Everyone had the same story: you finish a devastating movie, you're wrecked, and then you immediately tell everyone they have to watch it. Same with sad songs, same with that one episode of a show you can never rewatch.

But now the club can't agree on what we're even asking, and I think thats the actual problem. Our advisor says the naive version (why do people like sad movies) is boring and the real question is something narrower. One kid brought up true crime podcasts and said its the exact same thing as tragedy and everyone is being a hypocrite about it. Another kid said no way, its totally different when the person actually suffered and nobody made it up — enjoying that is just being a ghoul. Then someone said ok but what about sad music, theres no character and no story at all in it, so now we've got three things and I can't tell if they're one phenomenon or three.

So: what are the actual open questions in this area, and which ones should we spend the semester on? I don't want a reading list or a bunch of names to look up, I want an ordering I can actually defend to the club. Which questions are upstream of the others — like, if we settled one of them, would half the rest answer themselves or turn out to be fake questions? Which ones can a room of high schoolers make real progress on just by thinking and arguing, with no lab, no experiments, and nothing paywalled? And which ones sound juicy but are dead ends for us, either because they need actual data or because people have been going in circles on them for centuries (I assume the catharsis answer is one of those but I honestly don't know). Last thing: what would move a question up or down your ranking — what would have to turn out to be true, or what would somebody have to show, for you to say ok, forget the rest, that ones the semester.
