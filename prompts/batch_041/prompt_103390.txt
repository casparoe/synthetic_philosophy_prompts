I've been sitting in on a reading group that's been grinding away at the Humean/non-Humean thing about laws of nature for two terms now and it has honestly gone nowhere. The Humeans say a law is just a regularity that earns its keep in the best systematization of what happens — no extra glue, no necessity, and the 'governing' talk is a picture we should drop. The anti-Humeans say that can't be the whole story, because a law is supposed to be what makes the regularities hold and what makes the counterfactuals true, and a mere description of what happens can't do that. Both sides know every move. Nobody has budged an inch. I want a diagnosis, not a survey and not a verdict on who's right.

So: is this a verbal dispute that would dissolve once we stopped letting 'necessity' do two jobs at once, a real disagreement about what there is in the world, or are the two sides answering different questions under one heading — one about which regularities we should treat as the axioms of our theory, the other about what grounds the counterfactuals? 

Here's my hypothesis, tell me if it holds up: both sides quietly agree that laws have to 'govern', in the sense of being the very thing that makes the regularities obtain, and the Humean's actual point is that this is an incoherent demand rather than a thing that could be true or false. If that's right, the fight isn't about laws at all, it's about whether a certain explanatory demand is legitimate — and it's stalled precisely because you can't argue someone out of wanting an explanation they feel they're owed. Concretely, take the best-system account of objective chance and the undermining worry: if one side thinks chance just is the credence the best system licenses and the other thinks that isn't chance at all, is that a substantive disagreement or a dissagreement about a word? Maybe though I'm wrong and there's a real, answerable question buried in there that a bit of formal work would settle. Either way I want an honest answer about what would count as progress — a distinction, a result, a change of question — or an argument that there's none to be had. Keep it concrete and use what the two sides actually say, not the labels.
