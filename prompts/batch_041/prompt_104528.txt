A friend of mine who did a philosophy minor is always going on about Dennett's Darwin's Dangerous Idea, and I finally read a long review-essay of it in a science magazine. Here's how the essay sums the book up, more or less:

"Dennett's central claim is that evolution by natural selection is an algorithm -- a mindless, mechanical process -- and that everything in biology and psychology has to be explained in terms of it. He sorts explanations into two kinds: 'cranes,' which are legitimate higher-level structures that get assembled from below, and 'skyhooks,' which are fake, mind-first explanations that pretend to do work from the top down. Anything that can't ultimately be cashed out in terms of physics is a skyhook. So when Gould and Lewontin argued in their spandrels paper that some biological features are byproducts rather than adaptations, and that higher-level constraints do real explanatory work, Dennett's verdict is that this is just a skyhook with a fancy name. His own position he calls 'greedy reductionism': the view that everything is nothing but its smallest parts, and that any explanation that stops short of that is unscientific. On his view consciousness isn't really a puzzle at all -- it's just an illusion left over from a pre-Darwinian picture of the mind -- and the only reason people resist is a sentimental attachment to human specialness."

Something feels off here but I can't quite put my finger on it. I've heard Dennett is supposed to be careful about reductionism, and 'greedy reductionism' sounds like an insult rather than a self-description, so I'm inclined to think the essay is being unfair -- but I don't know enough to say where. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me what Dennett would accept, whats a distortion, and what's just wrong? In particular: is greedy reductionism really his own label for his view, does he actually think consciousness is an illusion, and is he really calling the spandrels stuff a skyhook? Also tell me where your own verdict is most likely to be off, and what would change it -- like a specific argument or consideration that would flip your read on any of these points.
