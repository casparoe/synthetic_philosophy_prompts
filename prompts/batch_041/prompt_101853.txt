I'm a philosophy of science PhD student, and I keep encountering a configuration I can't quite place. It arises whenever someone wants to claim that a higher-level pattern is, in some sense, its own thing. The shape of it is this: the higher-level regularity holds across systems whose lower-level makeup differs sharply — the same critical exponents in a magnet, a fluid, and a binary alloy; the same qualitative dynamics in networks wired from quite different components — so the higher level is said not to "care" about the details. Yet the lower-level description is also taken to be complete, so nothing about the higher level is supposed to be left unexplained by it. What I can't resolve is that the very feature used to argue for the autonomy — indifference to lower-level detail — seems to be exactly what a fully reductive account would predict, since a regularity that survives perturbation of the micro-details is just one that is stably fixed by them. I know "weak emergence," "multiple realizability," "universality," and "robustness" are all in the vicinity, but none of them seems to name this precise combination, and I can't tell whether the tension I feel is genuine or whether I'm running two separate questions together. Does this configuration have an established name? If the nearest labels only approximate it, tell me how each falls short and which fits best; if nothing fits, coin a term and give me its definition and a few boundary cases. I care about the carving, not the label.
