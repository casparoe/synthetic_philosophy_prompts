Last week I lost an evening to an argument with a friend — a condensed-matter physicist who has never read a page of metaphysics — and it has lodged in my head, so I want to build the final essay question for my upper-level seminar around it. Her claim was this. Suppose, as the standard Humean account has it, that a generalization counts as a law of nature just in case it appears in the deductive system that best balances simplicity, strength, and fit against the totality of non-nomic fact. Then whether any particular generalization is a law depends on facts about the entire mosaic, including events arbitrarily far away and causally sealed off from everything we use the law to explain. She pressed it with a thought experiment: take a world physically identical to ours except for one extra pair of isolated particles in a distant region, arranged so that a slightly different axiom set now yields a marginally better overall balance. On the Humean account, it seems, the laws of that world are not ours — even though nothing in our neighbourhood, or anything we could observe or interact with, has changed. Her conclusion was that a law that can be redrawn from the far side of the universe is not doing the work we ask laws to do, and that this shows laws must instead be local, intrinsic, world-making features — the kind of thing that necessitates rather than merely summarizes.

I do not think her argument is a knockdown, but I do not think it is silly either, and I want the exam to make students confront exactly the pressure she put on the Humean without letting them retreat to the anti-Humean side for free. So please write, for a three-hour upper-level metaphysics of science final, one essay question that (a) forces students to state the best-system account precisely enough to see why lawhood comes out global rather than local, (b) requires them to say what the Humean must concede and what the Humean can still claim about the counterfactuals and explanations laws are supposed to support, and (c) requires them to press the anti-Humean with the mirror-image question — how a local governing or necessitating feature is supposed to secure the global scope and the uninstantiated cases that laws seem to have, and whether the added necessitation does any explanatory work the regularity alone did not.

Along with the question, give me a model answer of roughly 800 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks built from five must-hit points, each worth 4 marks, with a one-line note on how students typically fail each point. The scheme should make explicit what separates an A from a B: an A must derive the distant-event consequence from the account rather than assert it, must treat both sides symmetrically by locating the corresponding cost on the governing view, and must close with a defensible diagnosis of what the dispute is really about and what would count as progress; a B will name the positions correctly but treat one side as obviously correct, miss the symmetry, or conclude that the disagreement is merely verbal without arguing for it.
