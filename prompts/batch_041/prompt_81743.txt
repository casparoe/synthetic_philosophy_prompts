Last night I had a long argument with a friend about lab animal rules and I'm still not sure I won it. Where I used to work there was this whole oversight apparatus for mice, rats and octopuses, but crickets, fruit flies, crabs — nothing, do basically whatever you want. My friend says that's just administrative convenience dressed up as ethics. His point was that everyone agrees sentience comes in degrees (worm, dog, us), so a rule that puts an octopus inside the system and a crab outside is a line somebody drew for paperwork reasons, and once you admit it's a gradient you can't stop at crabs — why not a thermostat, why not a plant. I said fine, but you still have to draw lines somewhere, and then I couldn't say what the line is supposed to be tracking. Can you untangle what that kind of practice actually presupposes about moral status? Does it need sentience to be a switch rather than a dial, or can a graded view license the very same rules? Which parts of the practice are strictly presupposed and which are just things it happens to fit with — and is it underdetermined, so that a threshold view and a graded-utilitarian view both come out looking identical on the ground? And if we really took the degrees seriously, what would have to change — the categories themselves, the way harms get counted, where the money goes? Dosen't have to be a whole essay, I just want to know where the real commitments are hiding.
