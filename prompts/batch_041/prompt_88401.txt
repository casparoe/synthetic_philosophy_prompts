My colleague D and I run the bioethics reading group together, and she's a card-carrying procreative-beneficence person — roughly: if you're already doing IVF and you've got several embryos sitting there, you have reasons to pick the one that will have the best life, and sometimes you're obliged to. We keep arguing about what that actually amounts to, because her verdicts on our toy cases dont obviously add up to one single principle.

Here's what she's said so far. (1) Four embryos, one of which will have a condition meaning repeated fractures and chronic pain from early childhood: she says you shouldn't pick that one, and that picking it would actually be wrong when there are healthy ones available. (2) An embryo with a variant for a condition that a daily pill controls completely — no side effects, no shortened life, no stigma: she says there's no reason to prefer either embryo, flip a coin. (3) An embryo with a variant giving it somewhat better than average resistance to ordinary infections, so better than normal rather than patched up to normal: she says there's a weak reason to prefer it, but you wouldn't be doing anything wrong picking the other one. (4) Eye colour: no reason either way, though she doesn't think it's forbidden to care.

Now the case I actually want to ask her about. Two embryos, and as far as anyone can tell neither has anything that will make it ill, in pain, or functionally impaired. But one carries a variant predicting a shorter-than-average adult body and a heavier build, and in the society these kids will actually grow up in, that means lower wages, worse medical treatment, and a steady drip of low-grade cruelty. In a society that didn't do those things, the trait would be neutral.

So: will she say there's a reason to pick the other embryo? A strong one? Or will she say it isn't a reason at all, or even that selecting against it would be wrong? Tell me which rules fit everything she's said so far, what each of them predicts here, which one I should bet on and why, and what case I should put to her next to find out which rule she's really running. And if her earlier verdicts have already ruled out a rule I might reasonably have expected her to hold, tell me that too.
