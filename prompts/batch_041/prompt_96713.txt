I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, trying to turn a conference comment into a short paper on the Dennett–Searle dispute over original versus derived intentionality. Below is the argument in compressed form. I'd like your comments on its accuracy, on whether the central distinction earns its keep or merely relabels Searle's, and above all on where the weakest inference lies. Please don't send me a reading list; I want the argument stress-tested, ideally with a counterexample or a repair where you think one is needed.

The argument. Dennett's coin-machine example is usually read as follows. A vending machine accepts what he calls a two-bitser. In a nearby possible world the coinage changes and discs of the same dimensions are used as currency, so the same physical machine goes on accepting them. Dennett asks which property the machine's internal acceptance state is about: being a genuine two-bitser, being a disc of a certain shape, or being something used as currency here. He claims there is no fact of the matter, and that the same indeterminacy infects our own states, so the original/derived distinction collapses. The standard reply on Searle's behalf grants the indeterminacy of which property is represented but insists it is irrelevant: Searle's distinction concerns the source of the norm, not its determinacy, since original intentionality is intrinsic to the biology and derived intentionality is borrowed from us.

My claim is that the example cannot adjudicate between these readings because it confounds two questions. Call them specification — which property fixes correctness — and provenance — whose interests make that property the correctness condition. Dennett is right that specification is interest-relative when the system's dispositions are shallow: the machine's counterfactual profile is exhausted by shape, so nothing internal settles whether it tracks shape or monetary role. But provenance does not inherit that indeterminacy, and the two questions can come apart in the other direction. Consider a case I haven't seen discussed, the serviced machine: the manufacturer's technicians adopt an explicit policy that the coin module is to accept whatever the local population actually uses as currency, and implement it so that the module's acceptance state is caused by that fact. Specification is now determinate — the state is answerable to monetary role, not shape — while provenance remains derived, since the technicians and we fix the correctness condition. Dennett would presumably say the indeterminacy simply reappears one level up, in the policy itself; I think that reply either proves too much or concedes my point, but I can't tell which.

What the case suggests is that the load-bearing notion is neither determinacy nor origin but something I've been calling self-specification: a state has original intentionality only if the system's own organisation determines which property it is answerable to. The live questions then become whether self-specification is coherent without a biological subject, and whether a policy the module learns from the technicians' behaviour, rather than has imposed on it, would count. I suspect the regress is vicious and that this is close to what Searle was after, but I can't tell whether I've merely restated him in new vocabulary.

I'd particularly value a verdict on two things: whether specification and provenance are genuinely independent, or whether ascribing provenance already presupposes a determinate specification; and whether the serviced machine is a real counterexample to Dennett or just a redescription of the design stance.
