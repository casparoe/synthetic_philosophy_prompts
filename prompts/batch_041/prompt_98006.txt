You are an experienced philosophy teacher and game designer. I teach a 90-minute upper-level undergraduate seminar on incommensurable values and hard choices, with 14 students. Design a runnable activity that makes them feel the force of the idea that some choices are hard not because information is missing or because people are irrational, but because the values at stake do not lie on a common scale and cannot be traded off by any single currency. Begin by brainstorming two or three possible mechanics before settling on one. The activity must be self-contained -- no readings, no citations, no outside research -- and must not require prior knowledge of the technical literature. Include a concrete scenario with roles or rules; a pairwise comparison procedure the group must actually use; a later manipulation in which one option is replaced by a slightly improved version of itself; and a debrief that maps what happened onto the difference between incommensurability, incomparability, and ordinary weighing. Do not let the facilitator name the target idea until the debrief. Think carefully about the order of events so that the discovery happens before the naming. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and be honest about which feature of the idea the activity cannot reproduce and that I will therefore have to supply in discussion. Give me a practical plan with a timeline, materials, and exact facilitator instructions.
