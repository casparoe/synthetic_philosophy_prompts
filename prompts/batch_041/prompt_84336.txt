I have been assigned, for my debate club's unit on the epistemology of disagreement, to draft the joint statement that a conciliationist and a steadfast theorist about peer disagreement could both sign after a genuine adversarial collaboration. Please produce that document in four parts: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides accept — about what makes someone a peer, what higher-order evidence does, and what the existing debate has and has not settled; (2) the remaining disagreements, each written in a formulation that the side it belongs to would endorse; (3) the cruxes, and for each one the argument or case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and (4) an explicit statement of any part of the dispute that is merely verbal, with a reason.

Two matters I would especially like handled, since our club keeps stalling on them. First, conciliationism is itself a claim that a peer rejects, so the collaboration is an instance of the very case at issue: does conciliationism require its adherent to abandon conciliationism when a peer is steadfast, and if not, exactly what principle blocks the inference? Second, please fix on one concrete disagreement that has already survived full disclosure — both parties having read the same material, having equal competence, and having stated their reasons — and have each side predict what the correct response is and what would change its mind.
