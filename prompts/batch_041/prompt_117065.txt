I'm a philosopher — action theory, mostly — and I've been collecting cases for a paper while failing to find the umbrella term I'm sure must exist. The pattern is this. In each case someone is better off having an attitude — a belief, a feeling, sometimes just a bit of character — that they've got no business having on the evidence, and the good only turns up if the attitude isn't held for the sake of the good. A colleague needed a lift across a long stretch of desert where nobody stops, because a stranded passenger can't promise anything; the only way a driver stops is if she really would pay up afterwards, and she can't turn herself into that sort of person by working out that it would pay to be that sort of person — the working-out undoes the whole thing. My sister says the same about falling asleep, and about her marriage: the moment she starts keeping score to protect herself, the thing she was protecting is gone. A student swears the only way he could finish his dissertation was to stop caring whether it got him a job.

What I can't tell is whether I've got one phenomenon here or four. Sometimes the attitude is a belief, sometimes an emotion or a disposition; sometimes you could get it by taking a pill or moving to a different city, sometimes you couldn't, and I can't say what makes the difference; and in every case there's the same odd second-order move, where 'be like that' sounds like the right advice and yet can't be taken as advice by anyone who takes it for that reason.

I know there's literature on believing things for practical reasons, and I know about self-fulfilling expectations, but neither seems to be it. Is there a name for this, or a body of work? And if there isn't one, please don't just coin a label and stop — I want the joints: which of my cases are really the same case, what seperates the ones where the manipulation works from the ones where it destroys the very thing, and what the nearest concepts are and exactly how each falls short.

You're the closest thing to an expert on this I can get to without a library, so reason it out carefully instead of grabbing the first famous name that half fits. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, no restating my question.
