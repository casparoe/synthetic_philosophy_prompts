Write me the desiderata for a theory of choice without preference — the specification any adequate account of how an agent can choose between options she has no reason to prefer must meet. I'm a novelist, and the spine of my book is a character who must choose between two things she judges exactly equal in value, so I need her deliberation, and the narrator's gloss on it, to survive a philosopher's scrutiny. Set out the cases the theory must get right (a genuine tie; two options that are incomparable rather than equal; the repeated choice among equal options; the agent choosing on behalf of someone who has no preferences at all; the case where the cost of not choosing is itself a reason), the distinctions it cannot collapse (indifference as an attitude versus equality of value versus incomparability; having no reason to prefer versus having a reason to be indifferent; the reason that favors an option versus the procedure that selects one), and the constraints it cannot violate (that a tie may be broken by the mere act of choosing; that choice is taken to reveal preference; that an agent whose choices cycle can be pumped for money). Give the consideration behind each requirement, say which are negotiable and which are not, and name the single requirement that eliminates the most candidate theories. Then flag the pair I suspect cannot be satisfied together — that reason must favor whatever one does, and that agents can act where reason favors neither option. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. A table is fine. American English.
