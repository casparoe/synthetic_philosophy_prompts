Ok, so I'm in this reading group on moral responsibility and we've spend the last month on blame, and I said I'd open the next session by putting my own view on the table so everyone can go at it. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I honestly can't tell whether it hangs together: (a) blaming someone isn't just judging that they did wrong, it's addressing a demand to them — asking them for an answer; (b) for that demand to be legitimate you need standing, which means being someone who can hold them to the norm, and part of that is being bound by it yourself rather than exempting yourself while you hold others to it; (c) hypocrisy is the clearest way to lose standing, someone who does the same thing has no business making the demand; (d) but I'm also a plain objectivist — whether an act is wrong has nothing to do with who says so, and a hypocrite can say something true, and even know it; (e) in practice I want less blaming and more actual repair, and I think the test of whether a particular bit of blame is warranted is mostly whether it can be taken up; (f) I don't think victims have any special standing — anyone genuinely committed to the norm can call you to account, and being the one who got hurt is neither necessary nor sufficient; (g) self-blame is my clearest case of legitimate blame, I do it constantly and nobody thinks I'm in no position to do it; (h) when someone blames me without standing, I think something like "you're in no position to say that to me" is a complete answer, not a dodge.

Concretely: a friend of mine has been lying to his partner for years, and last month he tore into me for lying to mine. I think he was in no position to do that, full stop, even though he was right about what I did. But I also don't think I need to be personally clean to have standing to go after a politician who's on the take — otherwise almost nobody could ever blame anyone, and I'd hate to live in that world. I can't tell whether that's a principled distinction or just me wanting it both ways.
