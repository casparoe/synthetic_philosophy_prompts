For the past several years I have worked as a literary translator, and my publisher has recently begun running every manuscript through a neural machine translation system before handing it to me to post-edit. I have found myself defending, more confidently than I can justify, the claim that these systems do not understand the texts they translate — that their fluency is a surface achievement, a matter of manipulating distributional regularities rather than grasping what the sentences are about — and I take the further step of insisting that this is a difference in kind rather than a temporary engineering deficit, so that fully automating literary translation is misconceived rather than merely difficult.

I would like a map of what this position risks. Which observations, arguments, or results should move me, and how decisive would each be? I want both the empirical and the conceptual species of defeater: probes that ought to separate pattern-matching from comprehension (discourse-level reference and ellipsis, register and honorifics, evidentiality, and especially cases where the target language forces a choice — of gender, aspect, number — that the source deliberately leaves open), and equally arguments that my notion of understanding is doing no real work here, or that I am applying it to a moving target.

Two things I would especially like you to press on. First, is the claim falsifiable as I have stated it? If these systems keep improving and eventually pass every behavioural probe I can name, I can always redescribe the tests as shallow, and I suspect that move is available to me for free. Tell me whether any version of my position genuinely predicts something, what that prediction is, and whether the defensible residue is only the weaker claim that machine translations are shallow in the sense of failing to make and defend interpretive choices, rather than the stronger claim that the machines do not understand at all.

Second, what would a sympathetic defender of my view concede counts against it — not only what critics allege? Which of these defeaters are already available, and which would require developments we have not seen? And if the position turns out to be well insulated, is that a defect in it, or the unavoidable price of any claim about a state that cannot be observed directly?
