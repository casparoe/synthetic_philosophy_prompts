I am applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship in the philosophy of science and need a research proposal of roughly four pages, single-spaced. I have a half-formed idea that I would like you to turn into a proper proposal.

The idea: the dispute between epistemic and ontic structural realism is usually fought on shared ground. Both sides agree that structure is whatever is preserved across theory change, and then they disagree about whether structure is all there is. I suspect the ground is not as shared as it looks. Both sides use structure as a placeholder for whatever the continuity of science turns out to consist in, and the real content is smuggled in by an unexamined assumption about which invariances matter: invariances of the mathematical formalism, invariances of the laws under symmetry transformations, or something looser, like preserved relations among observable regularities. If that is right, the epistemic-versus-ontic distinction may be downstream of a question neither side asks, and the debate may be partly verbal without either side noticing.

Turn this into a proposal. It should do the following. First, state the research question so that it has a determinate answer, one that could in principle come out either way rather than a thesis in search of support. Second, explain why the existing positions leave that question open, describing positions by their shape rather than by who holds them. Third, set out the method concretely enough that I could follow it month by month across the year, and explain why recasting the debate in terms of invariances is a better route than the obvious alternative of adjudicating epistemic against ontic structural realism on its own terms, for instance by appealing to episodes of theory change. Fourth, say what a result would look like for each of the two verdicts the question could receive — that the distinction is substantive and survives the recasting, and that it is largely verbal — and say what would count as the project failing rather than merely concluding. Fifth, give an honest assessment of scope, including what might make the project too large for a single year and how I would cut it down.

As a test case, the proposal should say what the recasting would do with the familiar triviality worry: the objection that any formal structure of the right cardinality can be read into the world, so that structure as such imposes no real constraint. If my recasting cannot say anything useful about that worry, that is a reason to doubt the idea, and the proposal should be candid about that rather than paper over it.

Please do not include citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; I will add those myself. Refer to positions and arguments by description. Write in American English, in a register suitable for a fellowship panel that includes scientists as well as philosophers.
