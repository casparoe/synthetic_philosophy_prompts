I am a high school student and I am preparing a piece for our school's philosophy-and-technology magazine, which publishes one long satire per issue. I want to write a parody of a corporate FAQ or onboarding document for a fictional AI company that claims to have solved the alignment problem by narrowing it. The company's pitch is that it does not try to make AI systems good or wise; it only makes sure that a system does exactly what its user intended -- no more and no less. The humor should come from getting the distinction right: intent alignment is about a system's objectives matching the operator's intentions, while value alignment is about those objectives being worth pursuing or matching human values. A system can be perfectly intent-aligned and still be a catastrophe if the user's intention is malicious, reckless, or confused. I want the FAQ to be deadpan and cheerful, written in the voice of a company that is genuinely proud of having 'solved alignment' by declaring values to be out of scope. It should include entries on: what the company does when a user intends harm; what it does when the user's intention is ambiguous, self-destructive, or changes halfway through; whether a system that faithfully carries out a terrible plan counts as a success; how the company handles the possibility that the user is not the only person affected; and a customer-support answer about 'value drift' that quietly reveals the company believes values are somebody else's department. The satire should be fair to the narrow view -- let its most reasonable defenders sound reasonable for as long as possible -- and it should be legible to a reader who does not know the technical literature. Please avoid the most well-worn examples from the AI-safety discourse; invent the company's own case studies and customer testimonials, and target the narrow definition rather than any named living person. Aim for about 700-900 words, formatted as numbered FAQ entries. After the piece, add a short note of about 150 words for my editor that separates what is actually settled among people who study AI alignment from what is genuinely contested, and that flags any place where the satire takes liberties with the ideas or leans on a contested reading. Please do not present a contested view as if it were the consensus. Do not include citations or a reading list; the piece should stand on its own, and I cannot use them. Also do not quote any real person or document; invent the company, its spokesperson, and its customers.
