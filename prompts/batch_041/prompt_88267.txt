I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for a long time, and I keep running into the commitment race: the strategic predicament in which two or more agents each stand to gain if theirs is the commitment that holds, and so they compete to commit first or most credibly — to burn their boats, to rewire their own payoffs, to become the sort of agent whose threat is believed because it is transparent. The trouble is that "commitment race" gets used in the comment sections for several structurally different games at once, so I want a classification instrument to keep them apart.

Please write me a decision tree (or taxonomy) of the positions one can hold about commitment races. The deliverable is the instrument itself: every internal node a question that separates the positions beneath it, every leaf a named position accompanied by one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Do not begin from a list of labels; construct the logical space first — the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they permit — and only then place the actual positions in it, marking which combinations are unoccupied and diagnosing each briefly (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored).

Anchor the whole tree in a single self-contained case, so that it requires no outside reading: a one-shot two-player game in which each player may, at some cost, make an irreversible move before the other can reply; the player whose commitment is credible shifts the outcome in their favor; payoffs are common knowledge, intentions are not.

Phrase every node as a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Make the branches cut along at least these distinctions: whether a commitment is credible for causal reasons (options removed, payoffs altered) or for evidential or logical ones (a decision procedure a predictor can read off); whether what matters is temporal priority, irreversibility, or mutual knowledge of the commitment; whether the underlying game is pure conflict or mixed-motive; and whether the analysis needs a solution concept beyond Nash equilibrium.

The tree must contain, as leaves, at least the boat-burner, the payoff-wirer, the transparent-agent (evidential) view, the updateless or acausal view, and the race-escaping view — the position that the winning move is to commit never to be the first to commit. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, rather than papering over the gap.

The audience is a reading group that already knows basic game theory, so you may assume Nash equilibrium and the prisoner's dilemma but should define anything more exotic in a clause. Six to nine leaves.
