I sit on a hiring/promotion committee and it's slowly driving me nuts. We all treat citation counts and journal prestige as the main currency for judging researchers -- but the same comittee insists on "arm's-length" referees, frets about citation rings and coercive self-citation, and treats each citation as a little signal of quality. That seems to presuppose something about how those numbers get made: that the citing papers are more or less independent bits of evidence, and that the total is a wisdom-of-crowds kind of thing. But if the herding/cascade literature is right, a lot of those citations are one early signal echoing around, so the count isn't independent corroboration at all.

So what theory of evidence, testimony, or aggregation does citation-based evaluation actually presuppose? Separate what it strictly needs from what it merely fits well with -- maybe it only needs the number to correlate with quality well enough to be a cheap, hard-to-game proxy and never needed independence in the first place. Which rival views does the practice quietly rule out? And if we really took the cascade picture seriously, what would concretely have to change?

Take a definite position, don't just lay out the options and leave it to me. Give it to me as a JSON object with these fields: implicit_theory (the view the practice presupposes, 3-5 sentences); strictly_presupposed (list); merely_fits (list); rules_out (list); internal_tension (the independent-referee thing vs. raw counts); underdetermination (where several theories would license the same practice); verdict (one sentence: which theory it really presupposes and whether that's defensible); changes_if_replaced (2-4 concrete changes); confidence (0-1); likeliest_way_im_wrong (one sentence).
