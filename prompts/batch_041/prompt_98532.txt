The framing runs roughly as follows: because we discount the future hyperbolically — steeply over the next hour, gently over the next year — your present self and your future self are best treated as two different people whose interests diverge. On that picture, akrasia is not a single agent failing to do what she judges best; it is a small collective-action problem, with the present self taking the immediate reward and leaving the future self to carry the cost, much as one grazier over-stocks a shared field. The colleague who first put this to me draws two further conclusions from it. First, that the old puzzle of weakness of will largely dissolves, since there is no single will present to be weak. Second, that commitment devices, and sometimes institutions acting on a person's behalf, are legitimate rather than paternalistic, because they are merely helping one party to a dispute against another — and he adds that a sophisticated agent, foreseeing that her future self will renege, has positive reason to precommit in exactly this way.
