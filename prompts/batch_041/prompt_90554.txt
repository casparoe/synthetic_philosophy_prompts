I'm a novelist and some how I've ended up on the board of a small press. We have a modest endowment, a charter that makes us pay out 4 per cent a year to living authors, and a once-in-a-decade chance to buy the UK rights to a strange translated trilogy for about a third of our capital. Our editor reckons there's maybe a 20 per cent chance of ten times our money back and otherwise we lose almost everything, and the payoff only arrives if we're still solvent in three years to publish it. Half the board says the expected value is plainly positive so we should go for it; the other half says one bad run and there's no press left to pay anyone, and our treasurer keeps muttering about Kelly betting and geometric rationality, and something about the difference between the average across possible worlds and the average over time. I follow about half of it. Draft the memo I can circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend a concrete stake or a no, say what conditions would change the recommendation, and give the expected-value camp its due rather than just repeating that we can't afford to lose. Please don't tell me to diversify — we're too small for that to be an answer.
