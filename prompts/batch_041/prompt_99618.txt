Frontier artificial-intelligence labs have settled into a recognizable pattern: they publish system cards and safety evaluations with each model release, their leadership argues publicly that unilateral slowing is self-defeating because competitors would close the gap, and the same firms lobby for regulation that would bind the whole industry. Treat this cluster of behavior as a single institution and ask what it presupposes.

First, reconstruct the implicit theory of the competitive situation that the practice encodes. Does it treat safety work as a tax on capability — an overhead that a rival who declines to pay can convert into advantage — or as insurance against a low-probability, high-severity outcome, or as a public good whose benefits cannot be confined to whoever pays for them? Does the simultaneous demand for binding regulation presuppose that the competitiveness constraint is genuine (so that a rule binding everyone is the only escape from a race) or that it is inflated (so that the demand is cheap talk, or a maneuver to raise rivals' costs)? These presuppositions can pull in opposite directions; identify which part of the practice commits the institution to which, and whether the parts are consistent.

Second, separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits. Which rival theories of the situation — a genuine prisoner's dilemma, a coordination failure, a market in which safety is simply a product attribute that customers will pay for, a race whose leaders genuinely cannot stop — would license exactly the same observable behavior? Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, such that several incompatible theories of the underlying game would produce it?

Finally, say what would have to change in the practice if the competitiveness constraint were false, and what would have to change if the alignment tax were negative — if the very work that made a model safer also made it more capable. Could the institution as it stands even represent that possibility, or does its design assume the tax is always positive? Distinguish, throughout, the claims the practice needs to be true from the ones it merely happens to express.
