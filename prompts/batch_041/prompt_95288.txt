Design a thought experiment that separates the contribution a blamer's own moral record makes to their standing from the contribution their relation to the wrong makes, in a case where the criticism itself is true.

Keep two things apart throughout. Warrant is whether the criticism is accurate: whether the person really did the thing, and whether it really was wrong. Standing is whether this particular person is in a position to hold that particular person to account for it. Assume warrant is present and identical in every part of the case; everything that varies should be standing, and the case should make clear what losing standing does and does not take away.

The design. Four variants, with warrant fixed across all four and two features varying independently: (i) whether the blamer has done the same wrong and not reformed, and (ii) whether the blamer is entangled in the wrong or in the target's life — a housemate, a colleague, a co-signer, someone who benefits — or is a detached outsider with no connection to either party. I want all four cells, and I want the differences between them traceable to those two features and nothing else.

Constraints. Contemporary and ordinary: shared housing, a workplace, a club, a family. No criminals, no moral monsters, no dramatic harm, nothing that turns on contested law. The wrong should be modest enough that reasonable people disagree about how much it matters — a broken commitment, a repeated small rudeness, a favour quietly not returned, a mess left for someone else. No fictional devices, and do not stipulate the verdicts: they have to fall out of the structure of the case.

For each cell, state whether the criticism is true, whether the blamer has standing, what the target may permissibly reply (something on the order of: you have no right to say this to me; you of all people; mind your own business), and what the target still owes if anything — an answer, a change, nothing at all. Flag the cell whose intuitive verdict is least stable and say what the instability is a symptom of.

Then: name the single feature doing the most work across the grid; say what happens to the grid if that feature is removed; and give the strongest version of each of the two opposing views — that a blamer's own record is irrelevant to standing, and that it is central — noting which cell each view is worst placed to explain. Describe the views in your own words; no names, texts, or citations are needed.

Close by settling the question the case was built for: is the hypocrisy objection a genuine objection to standing, or does it leave standing intact and attack something else? Say which reading the grid supports.
