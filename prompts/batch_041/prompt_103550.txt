I teach a graduate seminar in the philosophy of medicine, and the unit on naturalism versus normativism has gone stale: my students arrive able to recite the value-free account of disease and the harm-based account, and then have nothing to say once I press them with cases. My plan is to rebuild the unit around a fixed set of probe cases that pull the two accounts apart, and I would like your help scoring the cases themselves before I commit to them. For each case I have stipulated my own pre-theoretic verdict, not because I think my intuitions are authoritative but because I need a fixed benchmark against which the two accounts can be compared. Please work only from the descriptions I give and from the general shape of the two positions — treat the value-free account as the view that disease is a deviation from species-typical functional organization, evaluable without appeal to harm or to the patient's values, and the harm-based account as the view that a condition counts as a disease only relative to harm or to values, whether the patient's or the community's. Do not lean on the secondary literature; I want to see what the two accounts do with these cases on their own terms.

The cases are as follows. (1) Situs inversus totalis: the internal organs are arranged in the mirror-image of the usual pattern, a condition that is statistically rare but typically asymptomatic and harmless. My verdict: not a disease. (2) Dental caries in a population where nearly everyone has decayed teeth by midlife; the state is harmful and involves tissue breakdown, but it is statistically ordinary. My verdict: a disease. (3) A metabolic variant fixed in a small isolated population: it lowers reproductive success modestly, produces no discomfort or incapacity, and is universal in that population. My verdict: not a disease. (4) Persistent, distressing symptoms with no measurable deviation from species-typical structure or function anywhere a clinician looks. My verdict: a disease. (5) Senescence: a universal, harmful, gradual loss of function that no member of the species escapes. My verdict: not a disease. (6) A hypothetical intervention that raises a performance-relevant trait well above the population norm, undertaken with consent; the question is whether the pre-intervention state is a disease. My verdict: not a disease. (7) A condition that is atypical, harmful, and involves clear impairment, but is caused entirely by an environmental toxin and would disappear if the environment changed. My verdict: a disease. (8) A culturally valued practice that measurably reduces lung capacity, which the practitioner does not experience as impairing and would not want corrected. My verdict: not a disease. (9) A single metabolic variant that is entirely harmless on one diet and causes serious illness on another; my verdict is that the same bodily state is a disease in the second environment and not in the first.

Please return your answer as a single JSON object with the following fields. "case_scores" should be an array with one object per case, each having: "case_id" (1 through 9); "naturalist_verdict" (exactly one of "disease", "not a disease", "indeterminate"); "normativist_verdict" (same three options); "divergence" (an integer 0 to 10 for how far apart the two verdicts are on this case, where 0 is agreement and 10 is flat contradiction); "favors" (exactly one of "naturalist", "normativist", "neither"); and "rationale" (one sentence, no more). Then "coverage" should be an object with two fields, "naturalist" and "normativist", each a fraction between 0 and 1 giving the proportion of my nine stipulated verdicts that the account matches, counting a case where the account returns "indeterminate" against my stipulated verdict as a half match. Then "tiers" should be an object with three keys, "high_leverage", "medium_leverage", and "low_leverage", each holding an array of case ids, ranking the cases by how much diagnostic work they do in separating the two accounts. Then "convergence" should be a string of two or three sentences assessing my working hypothesis that the two accounts agree wherever harm and functional deviation co-occur and come apart only where those two features diverge. Finally, "bottom_line" should be a single sentence stating which account, if either, the cases as a whole favour, and "hardest_case" should give the id of the case that resists both accounts most stubbornly.
