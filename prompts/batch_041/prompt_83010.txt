Setting first, so we're not talking past each other: risk-weighted expected utility is the framework where you take an act, rank its possible outcomes from worst to best, and instead of weighting each outcome by its own probability you weight it by the *jump* in a risk function r evaluated at the cumulative probability — so the worst outcome gets weight r(p1), the next gets r(p1+p2) − r(p1), and so on. r concave means you're risk averse; r linear and you're back to plain expected utility. Buchak's version of the view says r is a basic feature of a rational agent's attitudes, sitting alongside beliefs and desires rather than being a roundabout way of describing how much the goods at stake are worth to her.
