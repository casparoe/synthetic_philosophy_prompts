Two friends of mine have been argueing for months and I want them to just write the document they should have written together. One's a psychologist who studies hindsight bias — she runs the experiments where you feed people the outcome and watch their probability estimates slide, and she treats the whole thing as a correctable error; she also reckons you get the effect for outcomes that aren't really a story at all (ask people whether a couple will still be together, then tell them the answer, and the estimates move). The other's a historian who thinks narrative isn't decoration you add to an explanation but what makes it an explanation in the first place, so calling it the 'narrative fallacy' is a bit of a smear. Write the joint statement of their adversarial collaboration: the claims they can both sign, about what the experiments actually establish and about what a good historical explanation has to do; the disagreements stated precisely, each phrased so the other side would accept it as a fair rendering of their view; and the cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether knowing the outcome distorts a probability judgement or just makes the causal chain look tighter than it was, and another is whether a narrative's air of inevitability does epistemic work or is pure rhetoric. For each crux, give a case or test they'd both agree bears on it, and write down what each would predict about it.
