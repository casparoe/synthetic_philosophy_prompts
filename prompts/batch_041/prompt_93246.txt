My country just joined NATO and I've ended up in a running argument with my dad (ex-army) about whether Kant's Perpetual Peace still says anything useful about all this, or whether its just a pretty Enlightenment daydream. I'm supposed to turn it into a blog post for a general audience, so I want to think it through properly rather than just rehearse the obvious.

What I'd like from you: drop Kant into 2025 with his actual 1795 views intact and tell me where he'd land on things that didn't exist for him. Nuclear deterrence and mutually assured destruction (his preliminary articles wanted standing armies abolished and war loans banned, so deterrence looks like a genuine problem for him); NATO and Article 5, i.e. a defensive alliance that explicitly isnt a world state; the UN Security Council veto; autonomous weapons; humanitarian intervention and the responsibility to protect; and climate-driven migration, which is the weird test case for his cosmopolitan right of hospitality.

Two cautions. First, don't turn him into a generic liberal internationalist, because I'm fairly sure his own texts pull in two directions: in Perpetual Peace the federation of free states looks like something members can walk away from, whereas in the Doctrine of Right he talks as if states are under an obligation to leave the state of nature, which sounds a lot more coercive. Second, be explicit about where you're extrapolating versus where his stated positions actually pin things down, and if some modern issue just cant be answered from his framework, say so instead of inventing a view for him.

Under 500 words, and lead with the bottom line: a couple of sentences on whether Kant is any use here at all, then the rest.
