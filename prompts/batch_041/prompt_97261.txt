I am assembling materials for a graduate seminar on the foundations of welfare economics, and I want a structured analytical judgment rather than a literature survey.

Assume, for the purposes of this exercise, the following six positions on the measurability and interpersonal comparability of utility:

(A) Utility is ordinally measurable within each person, and not comparable across persons.
(B) Utility is cardinally measurable within each person, so that intrapersonal welfare differences are meaningful, but utility is not comparable across persons.
(C) Utility is ordinally measurable and ordinally comparable across persons: judgments of the form "person 1 is better off than person 2" are meaningful, but interpersonal comparisons of welfare differences are not.
(D) Utility is cardinally measurable and unit-comparable across persons, so that interpersonal comparisons of welfare differences are meaningful, while interpersonal comparisons of welfare levels are not.
(E) Utility is cardinally measurable with a common unit and a common zero, so that both levels and differences are interpersonally comparable.
(F) Utility is cardinally unit-comparable among persons who lie on the same side of a fixed adequacy threshold, but only ordinally level-comparable between a person above the threshold and a person below it; no stronger cross-threshold comparison is licensed.

Now consider the familiar objection that a broadly aggregative, totalist theory is committed to sacrificing any number of ordinary persons to a single being who converts resources into utility at a vastly higher rate.

Rank (A) through (F) from the position under which this objection poses the least threat to a totalist theory to the position under which it poses the greatest threat. For each position, assign a number between 0 and 1 measuring the protection it affords, and give one or two sentences of reasoning. Where a position offers protection only by rendering the offending verdict undefined rather than by positively ruling it out, say so explicitly, and indicate whether you count that as genuine protection or merely as vacuity. You may record ties, but if you do, explain what the tied positions share. Treat the ranking strictly as a ranking of monster-resistance; it is not a ranking of the overall plausibility of the positions.
