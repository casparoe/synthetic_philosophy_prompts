My friend Mara works on computational neuroscience and has very settled views about which systems are sentient / morally considerable. Her track record: thermostat, no. Feedforward image classifier, no. Deep RL agent that maximizes a game score, no. Honeybee, yes. Lab rat, yes. Locked-in patient, yes. Human in a minimally conscious state with intact thalamocortical loops, yes. Brain organoid with spontaneous recurrent activity and nociceptive-like signals, yes. Large language model that says it is suffering, no. Now she is asked about a new system: a silicon spiking network with recurrent thalamocortical-style loops, a global workspace bottleneck, and a learned self-model, trained only by self-supervised next-frame prediction, with no reward/punishment pathway, no nociception, and no interoceptive valence. It can report on its own internal states. What will she say? I care less about yes/no than about the rule she is actually running, the other rules that fit her past verdicts just as well, and which further case would tell them apart. If you had to bet, which way and why?
