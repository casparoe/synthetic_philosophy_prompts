Quine died at the end of 2000, so he missed the whole business of training models to be good by rewarding them when humans approve of what they say — RLHF, reward models, all of it. I keep wondering what he'd have made of it. Not the engineering, its the metaphysics people smuggle in: we say a model's values are getting better, as if the training run were converging on something that was already there. Would Quine have thought there was a fact of the matter being approximated, or just a social practice getting stamped into the weights? And does his behaviourism about meaning plus the indeterminacy of translation turn the model's moral talk into the gavagai problem all over again, or is there something disanalogous about the case? He barely did ethics at all, so I'm asking you to build the view out of his naturalism and whatever else of his you think would really be doing the work. Say which of his commitments carry the weight, how he'd handle the obvious pushback that a machine has no stake in anything, and where you'd be least confident in the reconstruction.
