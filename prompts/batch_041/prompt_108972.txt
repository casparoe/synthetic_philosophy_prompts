Years ago I read GEB and I've skimmed the later self book (I Am a Strange Loop, 2007). What I can't get past is the load-bearing move in the middle of GEB: Hofstadter takes Gödel's construction — arithmetization, the diagonal trick, a formula that manages to say of itself that it isn't provable — and then says that the same shape, a system that loops back onto itself, is what a self is. He's explicit that he is NOT running the Penrose/Lucas line (he thinks incompleteness doesn't show minds beat machines); he wants the loop as a picture of how a self can come out of stuff that has no self in it. In the 20th-anniversary preface he puts the ambition like this: "GEB is a very personal attempt to say how it is that animate beings can come out of inanimate matter. What is a self, and how can a self come out of stuff that is as selfless as a stone or a puddle?"
