I've been going to a reading group on truth and propositions and we keep circling back to Bradley's regress, and I've started to think the whole debate is stuck in a way that nobody in the room wants to admit. The setup is always the same: you've got a proposition, say that the cat is on the mat, and you want to know what makes it the *unified* thing it is rather than just a heap consisting of a cat, a mat, and a relation floating next to each other. If you say a further relation ties them together, that relation is now just another item, so you need another relation to tie *it* to the rest, and off you go. The monists think that's a genuine refutation of the relational picture and that it shows unity has to be primitive or grounded in something non-relational, maybe in the act of judging. The other side thinks the regress only gets going if you assume that anything doing unifying work has to itself be one of the things unified, which is exactly the assumption they reject, so the argument is question-begging and proves nothing. Both sides seem to be talking past each other and I can't tell anymore whether there's a real disagreement here or just a shared word doing two jobs.

What I want is a diagnosis rather than a verdict, and I'm less interested in a history of who said what than in the shape of the impasse. Is this a substantive dispute about what unity is and what would explain it, or a verbal one where 'proposition', 'relation', and 'unity' are being used with different senses on each side? Is it two groups answering different questions under one heading -- one about what has to be true in the world for a proposition to represent something, the other about what we're doing when we judge or assert? And is there a hidden assumption -- about whether unity needs explaining at all, about whether a relation can be internal to its terms, about whether propositions are abstract objects or just the shadows of acts of thought -- that neither side has made explicit but that's quietly doing all the work? I have a hunch it's the last one, but I want you to tell me if that's right and why, using what each side actually commits itself to. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, a demonstration that one side's starting point rules out the other's question, or an argument that no progress should be expected here because the thing being asked for -- an explanation of unity that doesn't presuppose unity -- is incoherent from the start. If that's the answer, say so plainly.
