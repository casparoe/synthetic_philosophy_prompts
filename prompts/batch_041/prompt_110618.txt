My best friend is getting married next month. She's a social choice theorist and her fiance is a welfare economist -- they met at a conference on interpersonal utility comparisons, so half the room will be people who know Harsanyi's aggregation theorem cold, and the other half will be her huge Irish Catholic family who think "utility" is the electric company. I'm the best man. I want a five-minute toast that actually uses Harsanyi's aggregation theorem as its backbone, not just name-drops it. Like, the whole thing should turn on the fact that if you've got two people whose preferences each satisfy the expected-utility axioms, and you want a single "couple" preference ordering that respects Pareto (if both prefer X to Y, the couple prefers X to Y), then that ordering has to be a weighted sum of their individual utilities -- but the theorem doesn't tell you what the weights are. That's the joke and the tender part: the weights are the marriage. Can you write the toast? It should definately land for the social choice people without losing her aunt Maureen. Don't cite any papers or give a reading list -- just make the idea do the work. Also, please don't make it too sweaty; it's a wedding, not a seminar.
