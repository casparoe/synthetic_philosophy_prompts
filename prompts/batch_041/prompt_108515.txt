The Solarists have accumulated decades of observations of the ocean — the vast, transient formations it produces, the visitors it materializes aboard the station, and the whole failed research program that grows up around it — and every hypothesis they construct is either refuted or left dangling alongside rivals that fit the same data equally well: the ocean as a mind, as a machine, as an indifferent natural process, as a mirror of the observers, as something for which no human category is adequate. Where does this genuinely dramatize underdetermination as Duhem, Quine, and van Fraassen frame it, and where does Lem stage something the standard thesis cannot capture — for instance an evidential base that is unique and unrepeatable rather than a stable body of repeatable phenomena, or one that is causally entangled with the observer, so that the "same evidence" available to two rival theories never quite exists?
