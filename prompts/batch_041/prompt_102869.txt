ok, i'm putting together a little zine with my philosophy reading group and i need a poem for it. can you write me a triolet — that 8-line french form with the two repeating lines — in the voice of Buridan's donkey, the one that starves in the middle between two identical bales of hay. the part i actually care about, because most tellings get it backwards: the donkey is a caricature. Buridan's real view was that a rational will literally can't break a tie like that — when two options are truly equal the will can only hold back and wait for the circumstances to change, it doesn't get to just flip a coin and commit. the "oh come on, you'd obviously just pick one arbitrarily" move is the objection people aim at him, not his own answer. so i want the triolet's repeated refrain to be the donkey doing exactly that — suspending, coming back to the same spot, wiping out whatever the other lines tried to settle — so the form cant resolve the deadlock any more than the donkey can. and give the two bales lines that scan the same and rhyme with each other, so neither one can win on the page either. it should hold up for someone who actually knows Buridan's position. no formatting please — no headings, bullets, bold text or tables, just continuous prose around the poem if you need any.
