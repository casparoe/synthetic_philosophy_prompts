For thirty years I was an experimental geophysicist; since retiring I have been reading philosophy, chiefly on whether value comparisons must be transitive, and I have been trying to discipline my own judgments with a spectrum of my own construction. I would like you to tell me what principle, if any, unifies the verdicts below, what it predicts for cases I have not put to myself, and which of my verdicts it requires me to give up.

The spectrum concerns one person's life, differing only in the number of days per year on which they have a mild headache: brief, and no interference with work, sleep, or mood. Label the lives H0, H1, ..., H1000, where Hn contains n such days.

My verdicts. (i) For every n, Hn and Hn+1 are equally good; the extra day falls below the level at which I can discern any difference in how well the life goes, and I would swap either for the other without hesitation. (ii) H0 is better than H2; set side by side, the difference is small but real. (iii) H1000 is much worse than H900, not merely a little worse. (iv) I would choose H0 over H1 for myself, but I do not take that to show H0 is better than H1; it shows only that I dislike headaches. (v) My verdict in (ii) depends on the comparison being made directly: arriving at H2 by two unnoticeable steps, I cannot get myself to judge the endpoint better, though I can when I consider the pair alone.

I suspect I am being pulled between treating betterness as vague and treating it as non-transitive, and I cannot tell which is right. My tentative proposal is that comparisons are determinate only relative to a comparison class, and that transitivity constrains the determinate comparisons within a single class rather than comparisons across classes. Please test that proposal against (i)-(v), repair it as needed, state the resulting principle precisely, identify the case that motivates each clause, tell me which of my verdicts it cannot keep, and say what it predicts for the unlisted comparison of H3 with H0 and for a life like H500 but with one additional day of severe, disabling pain.
