- Raft rated for four, five people clinging on, all five will die of hypothermia within the hour unless somebody lets go. She says one has to go, and it gets decided by drawing lots -- not "the weakest goes," not "the one who can't swim goes," not "the one who grabbed the least."
- Same raft, but only four people in the water. She says you still draw lots among all five (the four in the water plus the one already aboard) for the four spots. She's dead against women-and-children-first and dead against "save the youngest."
- A woman in the water has a line to the raft; if you don't cut it, the raft swamps and all six go down. She says cut it. She's clear that this is different from the overloaded-raft case because here you're not choosing who dies, you're just refusing to let the many die for the one.
- Two people on a spar that floats one. She says whoever got there first keeps it, and if they arrived together, lots again.

I'm now writing the chapter I actually care about. Raft rated for four, five aboard, one has to go or everyone drowns. Tell me the rule she's actually been running, which other rules fit everything she's said so far just as well, what each of those predicts for my chapter, which prediction you'd bet on and why, and then give me the one extra scenario I should write that would force the two rules apart and settle it.

Give it back to me as a JSON object, please, with these fields:
