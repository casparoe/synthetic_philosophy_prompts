You're a philosopher of medicine who also knows the general literature on how definitions work and on the role definitions play in argument. Think carefully before you answer, and start by brainstorming a handful of candidate labels before you commit to one.

I sit on a hospital ethics committee and I've started noticing a move I don't have a name for. Whenever we argue about what death is, the definition gets quietly re-tooled so that it keeps matching whatever we already do, and then it gets waved around as if it were the thing deciding the hard cases. A few examples. Our policy says death is the irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness together with loss of brainstem function; then a patient on ECMO clearly has no capacity for consciousness but still shows some brainstem reflexes, and rather than admit the definition was off, we amend the policy to drop the brainstem part. In another jurisdiction the statute says death is irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory function, but in practice everyone is declared dead by neurologic criteria, and when someone objects we're told the neurologic test is just how you establish the circulatory thing, since the brain is what drives circulation. And when a family refuses a neurologic declaration on religious grounds, someone always floats the idea that maybe the definition should also require that the family accepts it, which would make a fact about the patient depend on an attitude in the room. What bugs me is that the definition is never the thing that settles anything -- it's adjusted after the fact every time -- and yet we all keep treating it as the authority, and nobody seems bothered.

Is there an established name for this? I half-remember a few terms from philosophy of science and from the theory of definition that feel close but not the same, and I'd like you to say how each near miss differs and which, if any, is the real thing. If nothing fits, coin a term, define it, and tell me where its edges are -- what would be a borderline case, and what would just be an ordinary case of revising a definition rather than the phenomenon I'm describing. A bare label won't help me; I need the carving.
