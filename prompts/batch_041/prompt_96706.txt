ok so I'm in this third-year ethics seminar on promises and it's kind of taking over my life. The seminar leader keeps saying "the cases decide the theories" and then hands us the most boring cases imaginable (guy promises to meet someone for lunch, shocker, he doesn't show). So for my final essay I want to run it the other way round: here are some promises that I think actually put pressure on the standard accounts, and I want you to rank the accounts by how well they handle them, best to worst, with a one-line reason for each, and a note on which cases each view just has to bite the bullet on.

The accounts, as I understand them: (1) the expectation/assurance one, where the wrong of breaking a promise is that you led someone to count on you and then let them down (Scanlon-ish, the "principle of fidelity" thing); (2) the practice/convention one, where promising is a social practice with constitutive rules and your obligation comes from fairness in a just scheme of rules you've benefitted from (Rawls, I think, and Hume is somewhere in this family with the coordination story); (3) the joint-commitment one, where a promise literally makes the two of you a plural subject and you can't unilaterally walk out of it (Gilbert); (4) the authority/standing one, where the point of a promise is that it gives the promisee some kind of say over you, so breaking it is a kind of usurpation rather than just a letdown — I think this is Shiffrin or Owens, the "authority interest" guy, I keep mixing them up.

The cases: (a) a promise made to someone who's since died, about something nobody else knows or cares about; (b) a promise you made as a kid that your family still holds you to and that you now think is silly; (c) a promise to a person with severe dementia who no longer remembers it and never will; (d) a promise made while completely drunk; (e) a promise to keep a secret that turns out to have been about hurting someone; (f) a promise to yourself, no second party at all; (g) a promise to an Alexa-style smart speaker that you'll water the plants, which somehow still feels binding; (h) a promise whose beneficiary never finds out you broke it.
