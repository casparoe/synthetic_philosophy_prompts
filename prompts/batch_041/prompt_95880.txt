I am a PhD student in epistemology, and I have been asked to prepare the framing document for a two-day workshop that will pair a committed pragmatic encroacher (a subject-sensitive invariantist) with a committed epistemic contextualist. Both have agreed to produce a joint statement rather than another round of talking past each other, and the drafting falls to me. Write that statement. It should open with a numbered section of claims both parties can sign — about the ordinary pattern of knowledge attributions, about the rival positions they jointly reject, about the methodological constraints on a theory of knowledge ascription, and about what each would accept as evidence against its own view. It should then set out the residual disagreements in a table, each disagreement given twice, once in wording the encroacher would endorse and once in wording the contextualist would endorse, with a note on which disagreements might be merely verbal and what turns on disambiguating "knows". I expect at least two cruxes: whether the practical factor enters the truth conditions of "knows" or the knowledge relation itself, and whether the practical situation doing the work is the subject's or the attributor's. For each crux, give the case or argument both parties agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. I want the statement to face two cases squarely: an engineer who must certify a bridge as safe, where an error would kill, hearing a colleague say over a relaxed lunch that she knows the bridge is fine; and a subject with fixed evidence who is mistaken about her own stakes, believing she is choosing a footpath when she is in fact choosing whether to send a bus over a failing span. For each case the parties should state the verdict and the reason, and if they reach the same verdict by different routes, they should say so.
