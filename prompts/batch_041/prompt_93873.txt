My labmate wont stop talking about mechanistic anomaly detection -- the idea that you watch a model, and flag it when it does something dangerous because of a differnt internal mechanism than the one driving it on ordinary inputs, even if the output looks harmless. I built the argument below to show the idea can't be made precise, and now I'm worried it's too good, because it seems to kill off most of the philosophy of mechanistic explanation I actually like. (1) To say two episodes of a system's behavior come from the same mechanism, you need a criterion of mechanism identity. (2) Any such criterion has to bottom out somewhere: in behavior (same mechanism when same functional profile, i.e. same relevant input-output dispositions), in structure (same parts standing in the same relations), or in us (same only relative to our choice of variables and level of description). (3) If it bottoms out in behavior, then a system that produces the behavior it produced before is using the same mechanism by definition, and one that doesn't is just out of distribution -- so anomaly detection collapses into OOD detection. (4) If it bottoms out in structure, the parts are only ever given relative to a level of description (weights? neurons? features? circuits?), and for any episode there's a description under which it's the same mechanism and another under which it's different, so the criterion tracks our bookkeeping rather than the system. (5) If it bottoms out in us, that's conceded outright. (6) So "same behavior, different mechanism" is either impossible or not a fact about the system. (7) Mechanistic anomaly detection needs it to be a determinate fact about the system. (8) Therefore the whole program is OOD detection in fancy dress, or it's about us and not the model.

Where does this go wrong? I'd rather keep (7) and loose something else, and I suspect (2) is the trouble, but every replacement I try -- fix the variables by asking which interventions change which other variables; say the mechanism is whatever the training process was actually selecting on -- either sneaks behavioral criteria back in or makes "same mechanism" a matter of choice all over again. Name the step you'd reject, say why it looked fine before, and tell me what rejecting it commits me to.
