I've been reading alot about the Flint water crisis and I keep going back and forth. I know the outline: the city switched its water source, the new water wasn't treated to stop corrosion, lead leached from the old pipes, and a bunch of kids got poisoned; officials at several levels knew or should have known. Don't give me the history. I want you to run it through the metaphysics of causation by omission. In particular, use a distinction I've been kicking around: 'thin omissions' (just the absence of some event, identified counterfactually) vs. 'thick omissions' (an agent's not-doing, which only shows up as a thing at all against a role, practice, or norm). Which of the failures in Flint are thin, which are thick, and which are causes in the difference-making sense rather than just in the norm-violation sense? Does the case make the norm-relative account of omission causation look better, or does it collapse cause into blame? And be honest about where your reasoning is most likely to fail -- what would you need to see, or what argument would have to go through, for you to give a different verdict?
