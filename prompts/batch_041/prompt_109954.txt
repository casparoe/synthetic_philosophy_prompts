Ok so I'm putting together the first session of a grad seminar on the concept of intellegence, and I want to run it as a ranking exercise instead of a lecture. Here's the set-up. A colleague of mine (not a fan of the standard psychometric stuff, and also not a fan of the AI-benchmark stuff) has been floating a list of what she calls candidate marks of intelligence: features such that if a system had them you'd be inclined to call it intelligent, and if it lacked them you'd be inclined to withhold the word. She's not claiming any one of them is necessary or sufficient on its own. The question is how each would do as the single headline criterion of a general-intelligence benchmark — if you had to build one benchmark whose score was supposed to mean general intelligence, which of these would you most want the score to track?

Her list, as best I can render it:

A. Transfer under distribution shift: the system performs well on problems drawn from a family whose structure its designers never got to see and couldn't tune on.

B. Compression: the system's behavior is equivalent to the shortest program that reproduces a very broad range of observations, and the shorter that program relative to what it reproduces, the more intelligent the system.

C. Learning speed: how few examples or interactions it needs to reach a fixed competence level, measured against a reference learner you specify in advance.

D. Self-calibration about its own limits: it knows when it doesn't know, can predict where it will fail, and its confidence tracks its accuracy rather than tracking how fluent the answer sounds.

E. Resourceful goal-getting: hand it an objective and an adversarially stingy set of tools and it finds a route anyway — the MacGyver criterion.

F. Intervention/observation discrimination: it reliably distinguishes what it would observe from what would happen if it acted, and updates differently on the two.

G. Improvisation and transmission: it invents a new notation, game, or protocol from scratch and then gets another agent to use it correctly with no shared prior vocabulary.

H. Answer-indistinguishability: across long, mixed-domain interrogation, a knowledgeable interlocutor can't reliably tell it apart from a knowledgeable human.

Two things I want from you. First, sort these into tiers by how well each would serve as the headline criterion for a general-intelligence benchmark — top tier, middle, bottom, however many tiers you think are honest — and give me a sentence or two per item saying why it lands where it does. Second, tell me which item is carrying the most weight for the whole project, and which one on reflection is probably just a proxy for something else on the list; if two of them are near-duplicates, say which.

Also, she'd want to know: if there's a mark of intelligence that's obviously missing here, name it and tell me where it would slot in.

And don't just be agreeable about the ordering. Tell me how confident you actually are in the ranking as a whole, and point to the single placement you're least sure about and why.
