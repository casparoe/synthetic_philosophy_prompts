ok so I'm putting together a small set of counterfactual-history prompts and I need one more, and this one's on Adam Smith. Set-up: Smith gets resurrected in 2024 and someone sits him down with a briefing on the world economy as it actually is — not the headlines, the plumbing. Multinationals slicing up supply chains, the WTO and its dispute panels, investor-state arbitration clauses that let a company sue a government for lost profits, tax havens, the sovereign debt of countries that used to be colonies, and the whole argument you hear from the left that all of this is just empire with a nicer name.

I want his response as an arguement, not a vibe. Work from the actual machinery of the Wealth of Nations — the case that the colonial trade monopoly is a loss to the public and a windfall to a particular set of merchants, the suspicion of joint-stock companies governing distant territories, the passages where he seems almost relaxed about the American colonies going their own way. Then push the hard question: is the modern system continuous with the colonial one he attacked, or has it changed in kind? There's an old historiographical line that free trade was itself a form of empire, informal rather than formal, and I want him to actually have to deal with that rather than wave at it.

Two things I don't want: a modern libertarian in a wig, or a modern anti-globalization activist in one. He should reason like Smith, which probably means landing somewhere neither camp enjoys.

At the end, say where you're least confident and what would flip the verdict — which feature of Smith's own texts, or which fact about how the modern system actually works, would push him the other way.
