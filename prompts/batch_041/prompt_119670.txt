Ok so I'm running a reading group and I want to use a dialogue instead of the usual essay thing this time, and I want it to actually get somewhere instead of everyone shrugging. Here's the setup.

Two people, both of them sharp, both of them right about something, arguing over whether "alive" names a real property out there in the world or whether it's a line we draw for our own convenience. Call them A and B.

A's line: there's a fact of the matter. Give A whichever version of the criterion you think is strongest — self-maintenance/metabolism on the one hand, or being part of a population that evolves by natural selection on the other — and A insists the borderline cases are just places where we havent done the science yet.

B's line: the two best candidate criteria come apart exactly on the cases we care about, and that's evidence we aren't tracking a natural kind at all. Make it concrete. My favorite example is the contagious facial cancer that's been spreading through Tasmanian devils for a few decades now: its been evolving in its hosts the whole time and by the evolution criterion it looks about as alive as a bacterium, but it's also just a chunk of devil tissue that got loose and started getting passed around when the animals bite each other. (If you think I've got the details wrong there, describe the case generically instead — I don't want the whole dialogue hanging on my memory of a nature documentary.)

Things I want hit along the way:
- the suggestion that the real unit is the lineage and not the organism, so "is this thing alive" is close to a category mistake;
- B pressing A on whether aliveness comes in degrees, with A giving an actual answer — all-or-nothing with the degrees being merely epistemic, or the property itself being graded — either is fine, but pick one and defend it;
- at least one sub-point where somebody genuinely changes their mind mid-dialogue, not just the main question.

What I don't want is the two of them landing on "well, it depends what you mean by alive." That's the exact move I'm trying to get away from. Take a definite position and commit to it. Somewhere in there I want a clear verdict on who has the better of the exchange, and the characters should be the ones delivering it — one of them concedes, or one lands a move the other can't answer, or they pin down exactly where the remaining disagreement lives and which of them is now on the hook for what. Real back-and-forth, please: each of them answering the specific thing the other just said rather than trading monologues. Neither one is a strawman, so if A wants to say "science will sort it out," that had better come with a story about how.
