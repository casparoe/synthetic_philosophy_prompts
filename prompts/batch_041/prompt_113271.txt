Some context, because it is the reason I am asking rather than merely curious: I moved countries for work about eighteen months ago, and the job I took turns out to consist largely of writing country briefings — short, blunt assessments of how a place works and what its people are likely to do. I spend my mornings producing exactly the kind of generalization I have spent my adult life distrusting, and my afternoons on the receiving end of them from people who have never lived where I grew up. I have tried to write down what I actually believe, so that I can find out whether it is a position or just a set of reflexes.

(1) National character is not a coherent notion. There is no trait profile belonging to a nation as such. Claims of the form 'the Germans are punctual' are not false so much as malformed: they cannot be true, because a nation is not the kind of thing that has a temperament.

(2) Culture, by contrast, is real, transmitted, and causally efficacious. Growing up in a place leaves durable traces — in what counts as a reasonable request, how much silence is comfortable, whether disagreement reads as hostility — and those traces survive into adulthood and are legible to outsiders.

(3) Population-level statistical claims about behaviour are legitimate, and it can be rational both to believe them and to act on them. Reasoning from where someone is from to a probability about what they will do is not automatically a fallacy; refusing to use such information is not integrity but self-sabotage.

(4) It is nevertheless a wrong to treat a person differently because of their nationality, and this holds even when the base rates are accurate. There is no degree of accuracy at which the inference becomes permissible in face-to-face dealings.

(5) The reason (3) and (4) do not collide is that a predicate means something different at the two levels: to call a nation X is to make a claim about a distribution, to call a person X is to make a claim about them. Same word, different claim.

(6) It is legitimate to criticize a people for what they collectively do — through their institutions, their policies, what they tolerate — and never for what they are.

What I want is a consistency check, not a defence and not a rebuttal. Which of these are actually in tension with which, which pairs merely look contradictory, and how severe is each real conflict? Please rank them rather than treating everything as equally problematic. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost, and flag any case where a tension that looks fatal can be defused only by adding a premise I have not stated. Finally, tell me the single revision that would resolve the most, and what I would be buying with it.
