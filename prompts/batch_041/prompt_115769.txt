I'm a science and ideas journo, and through a series of bad decisions I've agreed to open a small two-day workshop on dreams, scepticism and cross-cultural philosophy with a talk defending a reading of the butterfly passage in the Zhuangzi that I put in an essay last year. My line, roughly: the bit at the very end of the Qiwulun chapter, where Zhuang Zhou wakes from dreaming he was a butterfly and can't tell whether he's a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he's a man, is definately not a skeptical arguement and was never meant as one. It isn't Descartes's move in a Daoist hat; it doesn't raise a possibility and then leave it hanging for us to worry about. It makes a claim about the self instead — that there is no fixed thing there that could be either a man or a butterfly, only the transforming — and the not-knowing at the end is a refusal to answer rather than a suspension of judgement. So reading it as proto-Cartesian both oversells it for epistemology and undersells what it does to personal identity.

Now the room. Four analytic epistemologists, two of whom work on dream arguments and the sceptical hypothesis and will want to know what this passage adds to a debate they think is already well mapped. Two Sinologists who read the text as therapeutic, ironic and anti-doctrinal, and who will hear my reading as another Western theft. And one sleep scientist who thinks dreams aren't even experiences in the sense the argument needs — more like imaginings — and will say the whole passage rests on a folk picture of sleep.

Draft my prep sheet for the Q&A. I want the objections this specific room will actually raise, in the order they'll come, in each group's own vocabulary, ranked by how much damage they do, with what I should concede up front, a two-minute reply for each, and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And tell me which of my claims is carrying all the weight, because I suspect it's the one about the self and I'm not sure I can defend it.
