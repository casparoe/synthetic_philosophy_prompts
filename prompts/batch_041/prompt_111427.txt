I'm TAing a seminar where we spend a week on wether artifacts have politics, and I'm so tired of the bridge example I could scream. For the last ten minutes of section I want to hand out a poem instead of a handout, and the poem is just the phone menu of a housing association's repairs line, spoken by the recording. So write me that menu, as a poem, in exactly five numbered options, each one or two sentences, no intro and no outro -- the five options are the whole poem, no title, no epigraph. Some rules, because I've tried this before and it always comes out to easy. The caller never speaks and never gets named; the recording is the only voice, and it has to be boring, the register of a utility and not a horror film. Somewhere in the menu a problem has to be visible that the menu can't take: the thing the caller is actually ringing about (a slow leak under the kitchen units that's going to rot the floor) must not be one of the options, and the nearest option has to misfile it in a way you can feel. At least one option has to presuppose a caller who doesn't exist -- it says "your boiler" or "the account holder" to someone who rents and is neither. At least one has to quietly hand a moral decision to the machine, like who counts as an emergency, with no person anywhere making it. The fifth option is where you get a human, and it should cost the most -- hours, hold time, maybe a fee -- but it must not turn into a speech about how evil they are, since the whole point is that every option sounds reasonable. One more thing, and this is the bit I actually care about: put in one option that would make no sense if this particular landlord weren't run the way it is, and one that would come out the same in any organisation with the same constraints, so that the poem carries the difference between an artifact that's political because of the people behind it and an artifact that has a politics of its own. Don't label which is which, just let the two sit there. First read it should pass as customer service; second read it should be a document about who gets fixed first, and I want to be able to argue both sides of the determinism question straight off the poem.
