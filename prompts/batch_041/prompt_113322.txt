A criminal-justice primer I have been reading summarizes the retributivist/consequentialist debate as follows: "Retributivists hold that punishment is justified because offenders deserve it; since desert is what justifies punishment, they would punish every offender exactly as much as he deserves regardless of what it costs the rest of us, and they take an eye for an eye as the measure of what is deserved. Consequentialists hold that punishment is justified only by its good effects, chiefly deterrence; since only the total balance of consequences matters to them, they are committed to framing and punishing the innocent whenever doing so would deter more crime than it caused. Most legal systems are retributivist at heart, which is why a judge who sets a sentence below the guideline range because the prisons are full does something no retributivist can approve of and any consequentialist can."
