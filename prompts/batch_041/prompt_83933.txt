i'm a backend engineer, no formal phil background, but i've been reading alot about philosophy of psychiatry and i want to stress-test my position before i inflict a blog post on people. here's roughly where i've landed: (1) categories like depression, ADHD and PTSD aren't natural kinds in the periodic-table sense; they're looping-effect categories -- classifying someone changes how they feel and act, which changes what the category picks out. (2) i still think there are real causal processes under a lot of these conditions, and the categories can get better at latching onto them. (3) i think the right test for a diagnostic category is whether it helps people, not whether it cuts nature at the joints. (4) i think patient self-understanding is epistemically important: if patients reject a diagnosis that's evidence it's missing something, and if they embrace it that's evidence it's tracking something. (5) i think looping effects are a feature not a bug -- they're more like calibration or co-training between the label and the labeled than contamination. (6) i think psychiatric research should aim for categories that are stable across cultures and time. (7) i think the move toward dimensional/transdiagnostic approaches is progress. (8) i'm definately not a social constructionist; i don't think mental illness is just a social construct. which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, say for each real tension what would have to give, and tell me if there's a single revision that cleans up the most.
