So I've been grinding on the statue/clay problem for my thesis and I've talked myself into a view I'm provisionally calling "parasitic persistence." Short version: the statue and the lump of clay are numerically distinct objects that genuinely coincide (none of the "one of them is really just the other" cop-out), but the statue has no persistence conditions of its own -- it persists exactly as long as the lump does, and its modal profile is entirely derivative from the lump's. I even built a little operational test I run on colleagues: take any candidate coincidence, ask whether the dependent object could have survived a change the host object couldn't, and if it can't, you call the dependence parasitic rather than mutual.
