I have just come back from a long evening of arguing about the Allais problem with a friend who teaches decision theory, and I would like an audit of my position before I go back to him. Here, as carefully as I can state it, is where I stand.

(1) Rational choice under risk consists in maximizing expected utility with respect to one's credences and utilities. I take this to be the correct normative standard, not merely a convenient description of what people happen to do, and I do not think rationality comes in different varieties for different decision problems.

(2) In the Allais pairings — the first, where a sure moderate sum is offered against a gamble that pays much more with high probability and nothing otherwise, and the second, where both of those options have their chances of any positive payoff reduced by the same factor — I take the sure sum in the first and the high-stakes gamble in the second. I have defended both choices and would make them again.

(3) Independence is a requirement of rationality: if two options are alike in some respect, and that respect is common to both, then it cannot by itself make a difference to which of them is better.

(4) I have never regarded my Allais choices as a departure from expected utility at all. I take them to be the diminishing marginal utility of money, together with the fact that the certainty of the moderate sum is itself part of what is on offer; and I take it that satisfying a coherent taste cannot be irrational.

(5) Completeness and transitivity are non-negotiable for me. I do not think cyclical or gappy preferences are rationally permissible, at least not in cases of this kind.

(6) There is a fact of the matter about what it is rational to do. An agent can be perfectly coherent and still be getting it wrong; rationality is not just coherence.

(7) I also think it is rationally permissible to be averse to ambiguity — to prefer a bet on a known chance to a bet on an unknown one when the payoffs are the same — and I do not think Ellsberg-style choices are mistakes.

What I want is an audit rather than a defence or a refutation. Tell me which of these pull against one another, how hard, and in which direction; be explicit about which pairs only appear to conflict, since I would not be surprised if one of them does. For the genuine conflicts, rank them by severity, identify the cheapest repair in each case and what it would cost me, and then say whether any single revision would clear up the most at the least cost. I would rather learn this from you than in front of him.
