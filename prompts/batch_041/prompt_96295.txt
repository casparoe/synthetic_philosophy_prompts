I am at work on a short paper on the ethics of panpsychism, and what I want from you is your credence rather than a survey of the literature — assume I know the literature. Fix the following proposition: some of the entities or states posited by fundamental physics are experiences had by a subject, and at least some of those experiences are intrinsically bad for the subject who has them. I have deliberately left moral significance out of the proposition itself, so that it enters as one of the terms rather than contaminating the target. Give me a single number between 0 and 1 for your credence, and then show me the derivation: the sub-questions the number depends on, a rough probability for each, how they combine into the final figure, and which sub-question the answer is most sensitive to.

I expect the decomposition to include at least the following, though you should split or add terms as the logic seems to require. First, whether experience is fundamental and ubiquitous at all — some version of panpsychism, panexperientialism, or a view on which the intrinsic natures of physical properties are experiential — as against the view that experience is exhausted by a certain kind of biological or functional organization. Second, and this is the term I care about most, because I suspect it carries almost all the weight: whether the fundamental experiential properties, supposing there are any, carry hedonic tone. Most of the discussion treats the basic experiential property on the model of a hue or a spatial quality, something with a what-it-is-likeness but no valence, and if that is right then panpsychism is ethically inert. So I want your conditional credence that the micro-level is not merely experiential but hedonic — and nested inside that, whether intrinsic valence is even coherent, or whether pleasure and pain are essentially representational or dispositional, in which case a valence-bearing micro-state would be not improbable but ill-formed. Third, whether the bearers of such states are subjects in the relevant sense, and what happens to your number if the combination problem pushes us toward one cosmic subject rather than a swarm of tiny ones. Fourth, whether momentary unpleasantness with no memory, no self-concern, and no capacity to mind counts as suffering at all, or whether the ethically loaded notion requires precisely the integrative machinery the micro-level lacks. Fifth, the deflationary term: whether, even if everything above went through, the states would fail to aggregate, so that the honest description is not vast suffering but nobody being worse off.

Then tell me which term you are least confident in and what would move it — an argument, a case such as an insect or a thermostat, or something else entirely. For what it is worth, my own credence is about 0.03, and it sits there because I hold the hedonic term low; if you land far from me, tell me which term we most likely disagree about and whether the disagreement is conceptual, empirical, or evaluative. Write this as continuous prose, with no headings, no numbered or bulleted lists, no bold, and no tables, the way one would write a letter — and lead with the number.
