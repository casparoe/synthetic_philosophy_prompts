Dewey's experimentalism about democracy and inquiry is, as I understand it, the thesis that social arrangements are experiments — hypotheses about how to live together, to be tested by their consequences and revised in light of them — and that the method of the laboratory should therefore be extended to political life. I sit on our city's civic-innovation committee, and on that basis we are about to propose a policy laboratory: a standing panel of experts fixes the outcome measures in advance, runs controlled trials of competing policies, and the council enacts whatever the trials vindicate, with the public voting only on which measure matters most. Given that this is the institutional form Dewey's experimentalism calls for, how should the panel proceed when a trial vindicates a policy that the public then rejects, and the panel is confident that the rejection is simply a mistake?
