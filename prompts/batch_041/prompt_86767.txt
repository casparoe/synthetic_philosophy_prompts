Anyway we had a meeting last week and it turned into a shouting match about four words. The assembly woman shot back that a referendum just gives you the will of the people on a bad day, and that a proper deliberative body is the only way to find out what the people would will if they'd actually thought about it. Then someone else piled on that the courts shouldn't be able to strike down a referendum result either, because that's just thwarting the will of the people — same phrase, third argument.

The annoying thing is all three of them say 'the will of the people' like its completely obvious what it means, and I'm now pretty sure it isn't one thing at all. My suspicion is the referendum guy needs a much heavier reading than he realizes, the assembly woman is quietly using a different one, and the courts comment is doing something else again, but I can't put my finger on where each of them is cheating.

Give it to me as a list or menu I can bring to the next meeting, not an essay — I need to be able to point at a line and say 'that's the one you're leaning on.' And if some of these readings turn out to be the same reading wearing different hats, say so. Sorry this got so long.
