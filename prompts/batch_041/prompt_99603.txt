I'm on the advisory panel of a small foundation that funds SETI-adjacent work, and on Thursday we vote on the only item that really matters: whether to put £2m into a project called Beacon. Beacon would send one high-power, narrow-beam transmission at a single nearby star — an M-dwarf about 40 light years out that's had a decent biosignature candidate, one of those oxygen-plus-methane wobbles — and then just listen for a reply for the length of the grant and stop. No repeat broadcasts, no omnidirectional shout, no starmap, no here-is-our-address package. Though obviously a beam aimed at one particular star tells anyone who's looking roughly where it came from.

Roughly half the panel is relaxed about it. The other half can't get past the dark forest argument: the reason the sky looks quiet is that anything that announces itself gets found, anything that gets found gets destroyed, so a civilisation like ours should sit still and pray nobody has our number. I've read alot about this and I think that argument is carrying more weight in that room than it can really bear, but I don't want to just wave it off, because if it's even slightly right then we're spending other people's money on a small but genuine chance of extinction.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. I need a concrete recommendation — fund it, don't, or fund it with conditions, and if conditions then exactly what and by when — with the reasoning laid out so the panel can argue with the reasoning rather than with me. Take the dark forest case at its strongest and on its own terms; don't tell me it's unfalsifiable, and please dont just say we've been leaking radio for a century anyway, because I've heard that one and I'm not convinced it's as decisive as it sounds. I'd also like you to be straight about your confidence — tell me which parts of this are real arguments and which are just taste — and tell me what would change your mind: what would have to be true about the universe, or about the Beacon proposal specifically, for you to recommend the opposite.
