Here's where I've landed and I want a sanity check before I write it up for my blog, because I suspect I'm contradicting myself somewhere and I genuinely can't see where. So: (1) I'm a functionalist about minds -- what makes a mental state that state, and me me, is the pattern of information processing, not the specific meat; run the same program on silicon and it's the same thing. (2) So I'd sign up tomorrow for the gradual upload: they swap one of my neurons for a functionally identical chip, wait a bit, swap another, on and on until I'm all silicon. I think I survive that, the guy who walks out the other side is me, Ship of Theseus and all that. (3) But I would not step into the scan-and-destroy teleporter, not even a perfect one that reads my brain down to the atom, rebuilds it exactly somewhere else, and vaporizes the original. I'm pretty sure that kills me and leaves behind a copy who sincerely believes he's me. (4) I also think what actually matters in survival isn't staying the same organism, it's psychological continuity -- memories, personality, the stuff I care about carrying forward. (5) And I do think there's a fact of the matter about whether the post-procedure person is me, it's not just a choice about which words to use.

Can you go through these and tell me which ones actually conflict, which only look like they do, rank the real conflicts by how bad they are, and for each say what I'd have to give up and what it'd cost me? And if one single change would clean up the most, tell me which one. Please don't just wave your hands and say it's all vague or that I'm free to define my terms however I want -- I want the specific joints, the places where two of these can't both be true, and the cheapest way out of each.
