I'm a novelist — three books in, none you'd have heard of — and I'm about 200 pages into one set inside an AI lab, which means I've spent a year reading my way into the reinforcement learning literature and have come out the other side with Opinions. Which is a problem, because the whole moral spine of the book depends on getting this right, so I'd like an audit of my position set before I commit to it on the page. Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) Suffering is valenced experience: for something to be suffering there has to be something it's like to be in that state, and it has to feel bad, not merely be a state the system works to get out of. (2) But the only handle I have on "feels bad" from the outside is functional — the state that organizes avoidance, learning, memory, attention, the thing that does the work pain does in us — so a reward signal is the obvious candidate for the valence role. (3) I'm a biological chauvanist: I don't believe you get experience out of silicon however it's organized, it takes the wet evolved stuff. (4) When the moral status of something is genuinely uncertain I want to be cautious; I don't want to be the person who said it was fine. (5) I think the systems we have now, the ones trained to maximize a scalar, plainly don't suffer, and I've said so in interviews — there's nobody home, no persisting subject, no one it's like something to be. (6) And I also think the reward is a training signal and nothing more; it shapes the policy and then it's gone, and what you actually ship has no reward in it anywhere.

Two things I keep tripping over and can't tell if they're real objections or just me being a novelist about it. First, the old electrode work where an animal will press a lever for brain stimulation till it drops and won't eat — I've read that this is best understood as a wanting/liking split, that what's being pumped is closer to "wanting" than to "liking", and if that's right then (2) seems to be pointing at the wrong machinery: the training signal would be the wanting side, and the liking would be somewhere else entirely, which would make reward signals precisely not the valence role. Second, in a lot of RL the reward only exists during training, in the loop, and what you deploy is a frozen policy with no reward in it at all — so even granting (2), is there ever a moment where the shipped thing is in the state we're arguing about? I genuinely don't know if that's decisive or a category error on my part.

So: go through (1) through (6) and tell me which pairs are actually pulling against each other and how badly, which ones only look like they conflict, and for each real one what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. Please rank by severity rather than telling me everything is in tension — I know I can't have all of it. And if there's a single revision that cleans up the most, say which one, and be honest about whether I'd still have a book left.
