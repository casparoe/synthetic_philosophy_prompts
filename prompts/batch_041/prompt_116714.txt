At a workshop on climate policy last month, a philosopher in the audience disposed of an economist's cost-benefit analysis with one line: 'discounting the future is a bias, not a preference.' He took that to establish that any positive pure rate of time preference in a social discount rate is indefensible, and therefore that the analysis was rigged from the outset. I suspect several quite different claims are compressed into that sentence, and that one of them may genuinely deliver his conclusion while others are either platitudes or tell against him. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan could be expressing -- psychological, decision-theoretic, preference-theoretic, and normative readings, including the thought that impatience reflects a failure of imagination rather than a reason -- and for each say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and whether it actually supports his conclusion? Be explicit about where the distinction between pure time preference and discounting for growth or uncertainty is doing the work, and say which reading he most likely had in mind.
