I chair the investment committee of a public development bank, and the industry ministry has asked us to co-fund a three-year pilot built on Oskar Lange's answer to the socialist calculation debate. Under the pilot, a central board will post prices for roughly twenty key industrial inputs and revise them quarterly — lowering any price at which it observes a surplus and raising any at which it observes a shortage — while participating firms' managers are instructed to choose the input mix that minimizes average cost at the posted prices and to produce up to the point where marginal cost equals the posted price; managers' bonuses will track how closely they follow those two instructions. The ministry's economists concede that Mises and Hayek once denied that any planning board could price capital goods rationally, but they argue that the objection was really about the cost of gathering and processing dispersed information, a cost that cheap computation has now dissolved. Please draft the memo I will circulate before the vote: state whether we should fund the pilot and on what conditions, if any, and set out the strongest case against the recommendation you give.
