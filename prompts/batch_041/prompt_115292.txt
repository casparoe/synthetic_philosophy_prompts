I'm a retired chemist — 35 years in a lab, and now I read philosophy all day and drive my wife up the wall. I've gotten stuck on the doctrine of penal substitution: the idea that Christ was punished in our place, that the penalty we deserved was transferred to him. I have a vague memory of an analytic philosopher — David Lewis, maybe? — making the point that we don't really beleive this, because we treat punishment like a debt, and while a debt can be paid by anyone, punishment can only be undergone by the guilty party. But I'm not sure that's the real crux, and I want to see whether the doctrine can be made to work in a purely secular case before I make up my mind.

So here's what I want you to build. A thought experiment, realistic and secular — no theology, no courtroom theatrics, no ticking bombs — in which a willing innocent person undergoes something that looks like the punishment for someone else's wrongdoing, and the case is engineered so that this is genuinely just: not merely merciful, not merely excused, but just. One or two paragraphs. Then do the check on it. First, state exactly which feature of the case is doing the work that makes it just rather than unjust. Second, vary that one feature and show the case flips to unjust. Third, tell me whether that feature has any analogue in the theological doctrine, or whether the doctrine needs something we would never accept in a human case.

I'm especially interested in which of these is the working feature, and I don't want you to just pick one and assert it — show me by the variation: is it that the substitute volunteers? That the wronged party and the one doing the punishing are the same person? That the substitute is somehow identified with the wrongdoer — same family, same corporation, same body politic? That the punishment is really more like a debt or a cost than like condemnation? Or something else I haven't thought of. If you think the whole exercise is confused — that just punishment of an innocent substitute is an oxymoron however you set it up — then say so, but build the case first and show me where it breaks. And finish with how a defender of penal substitution would try to block your case, and whether that block is available to them without appealing to divine mystery.
