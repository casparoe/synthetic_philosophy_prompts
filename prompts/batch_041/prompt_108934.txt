Backend dev here, never took a single philosophy class, so go easy on me. I'm one of five mods on the Discord for our city's tech scene, about 6k members. We keep re-fighting the same fight about where the line is and I'd rather settle it before the next blowup.

A friend of mine says what we're arguing about is just the paradox of tolerance, the thing where a community that tolerates everything eventually tolerates the people who wreck it, so it has to draw a line somewhere. Fine, but that tells me there's a line, not where ours is. So here's our actual track record, roughly two years of mod log:

1. Removed + permaban. Guy posts "women honestly aren't cut out for this work and the community should stop pretending otherwise." Nobody named, no links, no call to go do anything, just the claim. 40ish reports in an hour, thread getting dunked on hard, and two women deleted their accounts that week. Nuked it.

2. Left up. Long angry post calling a well known maintainer "a fraud, a bully and a talentless hack," with code receipts. We locked it when it got shouty, nobody banned, post is still there, and as far as I can tell nobody left over it.

3. Removed + permaban. Someone posted a contributor's real name, employer and city with "let her employer know what she's like." No debate needed on that one.

4. Left up with a mod note telling people to cool it. Post arguing the diversity scholarship budget is a waste and should go to the hackathon instead. About 15 reports, a bunch of people said it made them feel unwelcome, nobody actually left.

5. Removed, warning, no ban. "Joke" thread with slurs about women devs. Reported by 40+ people, mostly downvoted, a few people said they were done with the server and I think one actually went.

6. Removed, no ban. Someone dropping a link to a study, "just asking questions," about women's average aptitude for programming. Very politely phrased, couple of people left after it.

Also worth knowing: every single thing we've ever removed came in through reports. We've never nuked something we just happened to scroll past.

Two of my co-mods read that record completely differently, which is the actual problem. One says we only act when a person is being attacked or targeted: doxxing, harassment, slurs, that kind of thing. The other says no, we act whenever a group in here is being told they don't belong, and the report counts and the "this made me feel unwelcome" stuff is just noise.

Now the case we're stuck on. Long time member, zero prior strikes, posts a civil 1200 word essay: the community has "overcorrected" on inclusion, he doesn't think women are equally suited to this kind of work, and if a few people leave over that then fine, the community will survive. No individuals named, no links, no slurs, no calls to action, and honestly it reads more like an op-ed than a troll. It's at like +180/-40 a day later, it's the busiest thread we've had all month, nobody has reported it, two people posted "I'm out" inside the thread but didn't actually leave the server, and one woman who's been here since day one said it made her feel like she doesn't belong. Nobody's deleted their account.

Two of us want to nuke it and ban him, two say leave it and let people argue it out, and I'm the tiebreak. So what do I do? I want (a) the rules our past calls are actually consistent with, and I know more than one rule fits, (b) what each of those rules says about this post, (c) your best guess at the right call and why you'd pick that rule over the others, and (d) one future case that would cleanly seperate them so we stop re-litigating this every three months. And if our record genuinely doesn't decide it, say so and tell me what principle I'd be adopting with each choice, because I'd rather know that than pretend the precedent settles it.
