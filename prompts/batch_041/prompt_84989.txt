I am inclined to think that surrogate goals are a general and robust answer to acausal threats, where a threat works through the threatener's prediction of your choice rather than through a causal channel: an agent that fears extortion can precommit to a surrogate goal that is worthless to itself but costly to the extortionist if the threat is carried out, and thereby make the threat non-credible. Treat this as a position to be tested. What would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, separating those that turn on common knowledge of the commitment, on the threatener's ability to model the surrogate, and on whether the strategy merely relocates the threat or invites new ones; say what a defender of the position should concede rather than what critics would say. Finally, assess whether the position risks anything at all or has been insulated by idealisation, and what result in decision theory or multi-agent simulation would move it.
