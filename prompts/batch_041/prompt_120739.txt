For a graduate seminar on whether death is bad for the one who dies, I want the specification before the survey of views. Set out the desiderata any adequate account must meet — the cases it has to get right, the puzzles it must dissolve, the distinctions it must respect — with the consideration behind each requirement, and mark each as non-negotiable, negotiable, or the prejudice of a particular camp. The list should cover at least: that death is never experienced by the one who dies; the asymmetry between prenatal nonexistence, which we do not count a misfortune, and early death, which we do; the distinction between the harm of dying and the harm of being dead; a life that would have gone on longer but worse; a life that was short but complete; the death of someone with no desires or projects, such as an infant or a person in a persistent vegetative state; the case in which the longer life would not have been lived by the same person, as with progressive dementia; and a non-overgeneration requirement — if death is bad because it deprives one of goods, why is it not equally bad that one was born no earlier, or has had no more children? Then say which requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, in particular whether locating the harm in a subject at a time can be reconciled with its being a deprivation, and which single requirement does the most to exclude the main contenders (the Epicurean denial, comparative or deprivation accounts, desire-based accounts, and accounts that locate the badness in the narrative shape of a life).

Deliver this as a table with columns: Requirement | Motivating case or consideration | What it rules out | Verdict (non-negotiable / negotiable / camp prejudice). Follow the table with a JSON object with fields 'jointly_unsatisfiable' (a list of pairs, each giving the two requirements and why they conflict), 'most_exclusionary' (the requirement and one sentence on why), and 'uncertain' (requirements whose status you could not settle, with what would settle them).
