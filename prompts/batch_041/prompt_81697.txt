Two friends of mine, Ada and Bo, got into an argument about this and I want it written out properly.

Their setup: you wake up with no memories in a plain white room, and a voice explains that a few minutes ago it created you. Before that it flipped a fair coin. Heads: it created exactly one person, and that's you. Tails: it created a trillion people, and you're one of them, picked at random — everyone else is awake in an identical room, having this same conversation. That's all you'll ever learn; theres nothing to look at and nobody to ask.

Then the voice offers a choice. Red button: win $100 if the coin was heads, lose $100 if it was tails. Blue button: the other way around. Or press neither.

Ada says the coin is still 50/50 and both buttons are a wash, because whichever way the coin went, the person sitting here reasoning was always going to be you, so finding yourself here tells you nothing about how many people there are. Bo says that's backwards: if it was tails there were a trillion chances for someone to be you and only one if it was heads, so you should be about a trillion times more confident it was tails, and smash the blue button. (I think this is the self-sampling vs self-indication thing, but I can never keep straight which one is which.)

Write their dialogue. Dont strawman either of them. Ada needs a real reply to the "trillion chances" point, and Bo needs a real reply to the "you were always going to be you" point. Let them get into whether they even mean the same thing by "confidence," and whether Bo is being presumptuous — reading a fact about the world off the bare fact that he exists. Have them try at least one variation (different numbers, or a biased coin) and say whether either view breaks.

Start with the dialogue, no preamble.
