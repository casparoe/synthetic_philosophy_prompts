Ok so I've been lurking on LessWrong and old Overcoming Bias threads for about a decade now, and I've read way too many posts about Dutch books, and I want to commission a joke about them. One that's actually funny to people who've read the arguments, not just "lol probability."

Here's the thing I want it to turn on, because the standard joke (incoherent guy gets bled dry by a bookie) is the boring version and honestly sort of wrong. The interesting part is all the load-bearing assumptions: the theorem only bites if you'll take either side of a bet at your stated odds, in any combination the bookie cares to assemble, and if money is linear in utility for you, and if you don't just look at the finished package, notice it's a sure loss, and refuse it. So the comedy is in the gap between the theorem and the human. Maybe the bookie shows up to fleece a guy whose credences are a complete mess and the guy is immune anyway, not because he's coherent but because he won't accept combinations. Or flip it: the guy is perfectly coherent and still gets wrecked by the diachronic book, the conditionalization one, because he just never updates. Or the accuracy-first crowd wanders in and says the whole racket is beside the point, since being Dutch-bookable is a fact about practical irrationality and not epistemic irrationality, and the depragmatized move is a patch that maybe doesn't stick. Any of those seams works; I just don't want a joke that could've been written by someone who only knows the one-line summary.

Give me the joke as a numbered list of exactly five beats, each one a sentence or two, no intro, no wrap-up, don't tell me what the joke is doing. After the list, one short line on how confident you are that this is a fair rendering of the actual arguments, and name the single bit you're least sure of -- if the punchline leans on a contested reading of any of it, say which.
