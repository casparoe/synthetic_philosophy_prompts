ok so I'm in this metaphysics-of-science seminar and we just spent two weeks on Dennett's three stances thing — physical, design, intentional — plus the "real patterns" idea that a pattern is real when it lets you compress and predict the data, not because there's some extra ingredient in the world that makes it real. My roommate and I got into it at 2am over whether my sourdough starter "wants" to be fed (I say intentional stance, he says that's just the design stance with extra anthropomorphism) and honestly I'm still not over it. So for the final paper I'd rather write something funny than another 3000-word essay, and the prof said creative formats are fine as long as the philosophy is actually in there.

Here's what I want: a leaked internal memo — deadpan officialese, no winking at the reader, no explaining the joke — from a fictional government body (I've been calling it the Bureau of Pattern Standards, but name it whatever's funniest) that has decided to act on the view that only the physical stance deals in genuinely real patterns. So it's been tasked with cleaning up public discourse. I want a phase-out schedule for intentional-stance talk ("the market is nervous," "the immune system knows," "my dog is jealous"), a licensing scheme for anyone who needs design-stance vocabulary (engineers, biologists, anyone who has to say a thing is "for" something), a certification procedure for patterns applying to be declared real, with the bureau's actual criterion — compression, predictive payoff — written out like a zoning code, an appeals process for a pattern that objects to being called a mere heuristic, and a grandfather clause for the older literature. Around 650-750 words, numbered sections. It should be genuinely funny to someone who's read the real-patterns stuff and readable to someone who hasn't.

The comedy has to come from getting the stances right, though. I definately don't want a strawman of the "only physics is real" side, because the funniest version is one where the bureau's arguments are actually good — parsimony, the causal closure of physics, the fact that belief-desire talk predicts individual humans badly, the frame problem, and the nagging worry that if a pattern is only real relative to a stance then there's no fact of the matter about whether it exists — and the horror comes from following all of that all the way out. Three things I want in there: a quote from an invented dissident, someone from a made-up Society for the Preservation of the Intentional Stance who's just been denied a permit; at least one moment where the bureau can't even state its own policy without slipping into design-stance or intentional vocabulary, e.g. it can't say what a thermostat or a censor is without already assuming what the thing is for; and my sourdough starter as an official test case, with the bureau having to decide whether the stadium wave and a traffic jam are the same kind of pattern or two different kinds. By the end I want a reader to actually feel the live question — whether stance-relative patterns are real or just useful — and not just get the joke. Last line should be one sentence starting with "Moral of the memo:".
