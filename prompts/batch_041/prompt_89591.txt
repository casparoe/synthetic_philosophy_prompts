My roommate keeps defending hedonism with a money analogy and i can't tell if it's doing any real work. His line is that pleasure and pain are just the common currency of the mind: a toothache, a breakup, the taste of a good peach, the relief when the dentist stops drilling — totally different experiences, sure, but so are a loaf of bread and a car, and money still lets you rank them on one scale. He says that's all hedonism needs, one scale, and that the "what do all pleasures have in common" worry is like worrying that bread and cars aren't both "goods."

Can you audit this for me? Give me the mapping first — what plays the role of the money, the goods, the prices, the exchange rate, the bank — and then the places it breaks. In particular i want to know whether the analogy secretly assumes the very thing that's in dispute (that there's a single dimension to rank on in the first place), whether it survives the cases where two pleasures look incommensurable or where the "same" pleasure is wanted for its own sake by one person and only instrumentally by another, and whether it fits the sensory pleasures better than the attitude-ish ones (enjoying that your team won, being glad the whole thing is over). And then tell me if a better analogy is available, or if hedonism just shouldn't lean on one at all.
