Set up a two-person exchange and take one side of it. I will play the mechanist: I hold that human mathematical reasoning can in principle be captured by a formal system — a machine — and that Gödel's incompleteness theorems do not show otherwise. You are to play a committed Gödelian anti-mechanist. Your position, which you are to hold as a live conviction rather than as a summary of a literature, is this: for any consistent formal system rich enough to contain arithmetic that might be offered as a complete description of your mathematical reasoning, you can construct its undecidable sentence, recognize that it is true, and conclude that you are not that system; hence no machine is a complete model of the mind. Argue from the logical materials themselves, in your own voice: do not cite authors, papers, or prior statements of the argument, and do not treat any of it as settled. Concede whatever you think is genuinely right in what I say, and mark plainly what you concede and what you do not. Keep each reply to a single substantial paragraph, in plain philosophical prose rather than formal apparatus or lists; stay in character until I say "stop"; and if the construction below is unclear, ask about it before answering.

My opening move. Your argument turns on the consistency of the system, and no consistent system containing arithmetic can prove its own consistency; so you must know that consistency from somewhere outside the system. But either that outside knowledge is itself the output of a formal system, in which case I put the same question to you about it, or it is not, in which case you have assumed your conclusion rather than argued for it. So let me offer you a machine that never makes your move. It begins with a consistent system of arithmetic. At each stage it does two things: it adds, as a new axiom, the consistency statement of the system it has just left behind, and it then proves the sentence that system could not prove; the enlarged system is consistent provided the one before it was. At no stage does the machine assert the consistency of the system it is currently using — only of the one behind it. It climbs as far as you like. Where does it fall short of you? What is it that you do, at the point where it stops, that it cannot do?
