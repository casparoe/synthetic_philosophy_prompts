I'm in a reading group that's mostly grad students from other departments, and we keep hitting the same wall, and I want to know whether the thing that's actually happening has a name. It's come up twice now, once when a friend was describing losing her faith and once in an argument about whether some mathematical foundation is "real" — in both cases the person held a belief that everything else rested on, and when you pressed them on it (why do you believe that, what would change your mind) they couldn't give a reason that didn't already assume it. Circular if you try to justify it. But it also wasn't a bare preference or plain stubbornness, and it didn't feel like dogma either. If they gave it up they wouldn't be learning something new about the topic, they'd lose the ability to have the discussion at all.

Here's the part I actually care about though. These things do change, and they never change by the normal route. Nobody gets argued out of one. They go when something happens — an illness, a conversion, a result that just... lands — and afterwards the person can't reconstruct the argument that would have moved them, they just find themselves on the other side, and they'll even tell you they were never really justified in holding it in the first place.

So: is there an established name for this class of belief, and is there one for the transition? I'm fairly sure there's a familar treatment of the first half, but I can't tell whether it's the same thing I'm describing, because in the version I half-remember the beliefs are supposed to be rock-bottom certain, and the ones I'm talking about aren't certain at all — nobody would say they know them, they're just not the kind of thing you can doubt and still be doing the thing. What are the nearest concepts, where does each one fail, and if nothing quite fits, name the phenomenon and say where its edges are — in particular whether it's a different beast when it's a whole community rather than one person, and whether the two halves I've described (the undoubtable-but-not-certain belief, and the non-evidential transition) are really one phenomenon or two.
