I'm a graduate student helping to run a seminar on theories of consciousness, and I've been handed the opening ten minutes of next week's session on higher-order theories — the family of views on which a mental state counts as conscious just in case one has a suitable higher-order representation of it (a higher-order thought, in David Rosenthal's influential version, or something more like a perception-like "inner sense" in others). I'd like to open with a joke, but every joke about consciousness I've been able to find works just as well on someone who has never opened a book: "the mind is a strange thing," "my neurons are up to something," that genre. What I want is a joke that only lands if you know what the theory actually says.

So: write me one. To spare you reconstructing the view, here are the commitments I'd want the joke to respect. The core claim is that a state's being conscious is a matter of its standing in the right relation to a higher-order state — roughly, being the object of the right kind of inner awareness — and the leading thought-based version insists that this higher-order state is itself normally not conscious, on pain of an infinite regress. The view is also standardly paired with two famous embarrassments: the higher-order state can misrepresent what it is about, so that in principle one might have a higher-order thought aimed at a first-order state that isn't there at all; and there is the plain fact that thinking about a rock does not make the rock conscious, so something must be special about the target's already being a mental state rather than an ordinary object. My joke should turn on one of these features — the unlit top of the ladder, the inner witness who can be wrong about what he witnesses, the difference between a mental state and a rock — rather than on the general oddity of consciousness. Keep it short (a set-up and a punchline, or a few lines of dialogue; I don't care whether it's a one-liner, an exchange, or a miniature scene), make it funny to someone who has spent years on this literature rather than merely comprehensible, and don't let it earn the laugh by misstating the theory: if the punchline needs the higher-order state to be conscious, or needs the theory to say that consciousness is an illusion, it's the wrong joke. Please also resist the standard props — zombies, bats, Mary's room — unless the joke genuinely turns on them. Finish with a single sentence naming which feature of the theory the punchline exploits, so that I can tell it wasn't assembled from vibes.
