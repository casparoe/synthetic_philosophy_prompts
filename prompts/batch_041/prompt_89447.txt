ok so for my history class we had to read this Nietzsche essay, the one where he argues that knowing too much history can actually be bad for you, and he sorts the ways of doing history into three kinds — monumental (you dig through the past for heroes and great moments to copy), antiquarian (you just love and keep your own past because its yours, no matter how boring or unimpressive it is), and critical (you put the past on trial and condemn it so you can get out from under it). Now we have to write down our own position and then get grilled on it in class. Heres where I actually stand:

1) the whole point of learning history is that it gives you people to look up to, so you can try to be like them. thats what makes it worth doing at all.
2) but i also think you should never lie about the past. if one of the greats did something horrible, you say so. no covering it up, no "it was a different time".
3) i love the old stuff in my town. the fair, my grandpas photos, the ugly church everyone wants torn down, the statue of the founder who apparently was a real piece of work. i want to keep all of it, and not because any of it is good or important. keeping it is kind of the point.
4) if a tradition is holding people back, you should be able to just cut it off. no sentimentality, no "but weve always done it this way".
5) and honestly i think history is worth knowing just for itself. knowing what actually happened is a good thing even if it never helps me do anything or be anyone.
