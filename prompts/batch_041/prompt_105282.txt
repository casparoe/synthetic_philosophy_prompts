ok so i've been going down a rabbit hole on this judy benjamin problem and i keep wondering what frank ramsey would have made of it. quick version of the setup: judy's a paratrooper who's been dropped somewhere and has no idea where. she's got it 50/50 between the red territory and the blue one. each territory is split into a first and a second platoon, and before the radio crackles she's got no reason to favor one platoon over the other within whichever territory she's in. then someone comes on the radio and tells her she can't be told where she is, but that if she's in the red territory then she's in the second platoon. the puzzle is that it's weirdly unclear how she should update. if you read the message as just a plain conditional and conditonalize on it, you get one posterior; if you treat it as information about a conditional probability and try to move your credences as little as possible (some information-minimizing or maxent-type move), you get another; and the answers don't match. people have been fighting about this for decades and there's still no concensus on what the right answer is.

ramsey never saw any of this — he died in 1930, long before any of these formal updating rules got sorted out. but he's the guy behind the move where, to decide whether you accept a conditional, you hypothetically add its antecedent to what you already believe and see how that shifts your confidence in the consequent. and he had a picture where a conditional isn't really a claim about the world that's true or false, it's more like a rule or policy you adopt for guiding your judgements and actions — what he called a variable hypothetical.

so here's what i want: get inside ramsey's head and tell me what he'd actually say about judy benjamin. would he think there's a determinate posterior she should land on, or would he say the question is malformed — that it only looks hard because we've smuggled in the assumption that the radio message is a proposition with a truth value that you conditionalize on? would his add-the-antecedent-hypothetically move even apply here, given that the conditional is the thing she's learning rather than something she's evaluating? and if he thinks the puzzle is under-described, what would he say you have to pin down first before there's any fact of the matter about the answer? reason it out from what he was actually committed to, don't just hand me the standard menu of updating rules with a ramsey sticker slapped on it.
