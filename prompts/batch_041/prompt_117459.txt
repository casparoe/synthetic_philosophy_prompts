I'm a high school senior doing an independent-study unit on machine ethics, and for my final piece I would like to write a satire. Please write a customer-facing FAQ page — around 600 words, numbered questions in the calm, reassuring register of a vendor's support documentation — for "Aletheia 4," an add-on ethical-reasoning module that other companies bolt onto their AI systems. The vendor has built it from three parts: a layer of permissions and prohibitions that the module is not allowed to override; a scorer that ranks candidate actions by their expected consequences; and a small resolver that settles disagreements between the two by holding a weighted vote, where each part's weight comes from how confident it reports itself to be. The module was trained by rewarding the answers that human raters said they approved of, and any human operator can press an override button at any time.

I would like the humour to come from the engineering rather than from jokes about robots. The answers should sound like things a real vendor would actually say, while quietly conceding the problems: what happens when the rule layer and the scorer disagree and the resolver's vote is essentially arbitrary; what the module has really learned, given that approval was the reward; why it performs so much better on trolley-style dilemmas than on the messy situations it meets in deployment; what it means for the module to explain its reasoning, if the explanation is composed after the decision; and who is responsible once a human holds the override. A reader who works on machine ethics should find the piece fair and unsettlingly plausible, and a reader who does not should still be able to follow it. Please keep the vendor confident throughout, do not let the FAQ announce its own irony, and do not have anyone resolve the problems at the end.
