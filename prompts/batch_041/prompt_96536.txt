My old roommate keeps defending this argument and I can't tell if it's clever or just broken. Here it is: (1) If you believe some moral claim only because someone told you, you don't see why it's true. (2) You can't know a moral claim unless you see why it's true -- morality isn't geography, it's not the kind of thing you can just be handed. (3) So nobody can know a moral claim on the strength of testimony. (4) And if you can't know it, you can't be justified in believing it either -- a belief you can't back up yourself is just a guess with good manners. (5) So deferring to someone else's moral judgment is never justified, and anyone who says "I just trust my priest on this" is being irrational. (6) So there's no such thing as a legitimate moral authority.

The conclusion is obviously absurd -- I learned half of what I think about right and wrong from my grandmother, and I don't think I was just guessing that whole time. But steps 1 and 2 feel true to me and I can't see where the rot starts. Which step do I give up, why did it look fine, and what does giving it up cost me? I'd rather keep 2 than 5 if I have to choose, so tell me what I'm buying if I do. Also keep separate what people who actually work on this agree on versus where they're just fighting -- I don't want a made-up consensus. Plain American English, please.
