OK so background: I manage a small pile of money — my own savings plus a little from two friends who trust me and would prefer to keep trusting me — and it's mostly boring index stuff, but every few months something comes along that looks like a genuinly positive-expectation bet: a mispriced market, a stake in a friend's business, a sports line I think my model beats. The problem is always sizing. Every time I google how much I should put on, I get the Kelly criterion, and everyone online talks about it like it's a law of nature — bet the fraction that maximizes expected log wealth, done. But when I try to actually use it I get stuck, because the inputs it wants (a known probability, a known payoff, a bet you get to make over and over) are never quite what I have. I also keep reading that you should maximize the geometric mean of your outcomes rather than the arithmetic one, which I sort of understand for a bet I make a thousand times and not at all for a one-off. And someone told me that if your estimate of the edge is even slightly off, betting full Kelly is a disaster and you should bet a fraction of it — but I've never seen that turned into anything I could run before I actually wire money somewhere.

So: write me a decision procedure for sizing a bet. Make it something I can run in an evening with a spreadsheet and no probability theory past arithmetic and averages. I want the questions asked in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, the points where it should just say no, pass, stop, and the points where it's really a values judgment and I should get my own brain or the friends' sign-off involved instead of pretending there's a number. Some things it specifically has to handle: a bet I can make repeatedly versus a once-in-a-lifetime one; an edge I'm confident in versus one I'm guessing at; a bet where losing would actually change my life versus one where it wouldn't; and two or three positions that look independent but might all go bad in the same week. And it should be honest about when the whole Kelly frame just doesn't apply, rather than forcing a number out of me anyway.

Then run it on three cases and show your work: (a) a sports bet where I've backtested a model over a few hundred past games and it's been well calibrated; (b) a friend's startup where I figure there's maybe a 3x if it works, it's a one-shot with no repeats, and I'd be in for a serious chunk of my savings; and (c) something where I'm pretty sure I have an edge but I built that estimate by staring at the same data I'd be betting on. I expect to not like at least one of the answers — that's fine, I'd rather the procedure tell me to bet small or not at all than flatter me. Finish by telling me what the procedure can't do and where it'll misfire.
