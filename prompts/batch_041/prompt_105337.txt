I've been assembling my view of time for a seminar and I want a consistency check before I commit to it. Here is the package: (1) presentism — only present objects and events exist, and the past and the future are not there in any sense; (2) truthmaker maximalism — every true proposition is true in virtue of some entity that exists, and I have no patience for brute truths; (3) no abstracta — I will not buy ersatz times, abstract propositions, or any other non-present entity, abstract or concrete, to do truthmaking work; (4) change is real and primitive — an object genuinely has a property at one time and lacks it at another, and I refuse to analyze this either as property possession relative to a time index or as a matter of distinct temporal parts; (5) special relativity is approximately true and I accept the relativity of simultaneity, yet I still think there is a fact about which events are present; (6) cross-temporal relations are real — it is true that I admire Caesar, and Caesar does not exist.

Which of these actually pull against one another? I can't see where the trouble is, so I need you to discriminate rather than flag everything: say which pairs are genuinely incompatible and which only appear to be and why, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each give the cheapest repair and what it costs me. If exactly one commitment has to go, tell me which one and why that is the cheapest exit. Please answer in American English.
