Consider the following proposition, which I will call Recoverability:

(R) For each ordinary adult human, there exists a single preference ordering over outcomes that is (i) stable across contexts and over time, (ii) the causal source of that person's choices rather than a post-hoc summary of them, and (iii) in principle identifiable — up to whatever transformations leave behavior unchanged — by an ideal inverse reinforcement learner given unlimited observations of that person's choices in a fully specified environment.

Give me your credence that (R) holds for a randomly selected adult human, and derive the number rather than asserting it. I want the decomposition: a rough probability and one line of justification for each term, then the combination. At a minimum, separate these:

1. The probability that a person's preferences are stable and coherent enough to constitute an ordering at all, as opposed to being constructed or context-dependent on the fly.
2. The probability that choices are caused by preferences, given habit, inattention, framing effects, and decision noise.
3. The probability that a preference ordering is well-defined over the relevant outcomes — complete and transitive — rather than containing genuine incommensurabilities or cyclical pairwise judgments.
4. The probability that identification succeeds even granting that (i)–(iii) hold, i.e., that the environment and the observed behavior do not leave the ordering underdetermined, for instance because many different orderings induce the same choices.

Then do three things.

First, tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what observation or argument would move it most.

Second, give me the number you would assign under a weakened reading, (R′): there exists a preference ordering that predicts the person's choices better than any competing hypothesis of comparable complexity, whether or not it is the causal source of those choices and whether or not it is stable. Report both numbers and say what the gap between them is measuring.

Third, redo the estimate for a case I care about more than the ordinary one. Suppose the person knows they are being modeled and chooses partly in order to shape what the model infers, so that their demonstrations are addressed to the learner rather than sampled from their preferences. Tell me which terms change, in which direction, and roughly by how much, and whether the resulting credence in (R) is higher or lower than in the naive case.

State any assumption you need to make the proposition precise enough to have a truth value, and flag any place where you think the question is ill-posed rather than merely uncertain.
