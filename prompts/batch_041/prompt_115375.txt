Hey — I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and I've got a thing from late Wittgenstein I want to work out as fiction instead of another essay. Its the stuff in On Certainty: the idea that doubting isn't something you can do just anywhere, it only gets going inside a practice that already takes a whole pile of things for granted; that those taken-for-granted things aren't beliefs you weighed up and justified, they're more like the frame everything else gets checked against; and that you can't really doubt them or know them either — try saying one out loud and it comes out either flatly obvious or slightly unhinged. He's also got this picture of thoughts as a river, where some of it is the flowing water and some is the bed it runs in, and the bed can shift over time, but never because somebody refuted it. And there's a live fight among people who work on this about whether these certainties are even propositions at all, or more like a way of acting that never gets said. I'm not asking you to settle that — honestly I'd love a story that reads fine either way.

So: write me a short story about someone who doubts for a living — a fact checker, a fraud investigator, an auditor, a manuscript reviewer, whatever you like — who runs into a certainty their usual methods can't get any grip on. Then keep going past that. The bit I care about is that the certainty doesn't get refuted, it just slowly stops being the bed and becomes water, and the character has to keep working and eating and being a person while thats happening. Keep it mundane: no brain-in-a-vat stuff, no evil demons, nothing cosmic. The certainty should be small and specific and a little boring, the kind of thing you'd never think to say out loud. Nobody in the story should explain any philosophy or name Wittgenstein, and I don't want lectures or epiphanies — dramatize it, let the thinking show up in what people do and notice.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered scenes, no intro and nothing tacked on after scene five, and let that last scene land the shift without announcing it.
