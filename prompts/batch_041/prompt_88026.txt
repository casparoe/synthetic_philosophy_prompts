I've been reading about punishment on and off for a few years now, on my own, no uni or anything, just books and forums and arguing with my brother in law who used to work in a prison. And the thing that bugs me is this. There's the camp that says punishing someone is justified because they deserve it, full stop, and the amount should fit what they did (I think the fancy word is retributivists). Then there's the camp that says no, punishing is justified by what it gets you — less crime, dangerous people off the street, maybe even someone turning their life around — and if it doesn't get you any of that, doing it is just cruelty with paperwork. These two have been going at it for what seems like centuries and nobody ever moves.

I can see the shape of the loop they're stuck in. The desert people say: if it's all about results then you'd be fine punishing someone you know is innocent, as long as it calmed a riot or made an example of someone. The results people say: not under the rules I'd actually want, because a system that does that falls apart and does more harm in the long run. Then the desert people say that's just sneaking desert back in the back door, and everyone goes home. Or it turns into a fight about whether deterence even works, where both sides seem to have their own numbers and neither one gets convinced.

What I want from you isn't a verdict on which side is right. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Some things I've wondered about, and I'd rather you tell me if there wrong than be polite about it:

- Maybe there answering different questions under one word. One side might be telling me what punishment IS — hard treatment that's deserved, so a system that doesn't care about desert isn't really punishing, it's just managing people — while the other side is telling me when we're allowed to do it. Those aren't the same question, so of course they never collide.
- Or maybe it's about who a justification has to be able to answer to. When I push the desert side they end up saying something you could say to the person being punished, to their face: this is what you're owed. When I push the other side they end up talking like a policy maker, to everybody else, about what kind of system we should have. If thats the real split, is it a real disagreement at all, or just two different jobs that got the same name?
- Or the whole thing hangs on two or three cases (the innocent person punished for the greater good, the guilty person who deserves it but where nothing at all is gained) and each side has already decided whether those cases count as evidence before the argument even starts.

Tell me which of these it is, or none of them, and why — using what the two sides actually say, not a survey of the field. Then tell me what would count as real progress here: some distinction, some case, some finding, or is the honest answer that nothing will move it and the question is badly put? If it's badly put, what should I be asking instead?

No reading list please, I'm not going to get through it anyway, and no citations needed, I can look things up myself.
