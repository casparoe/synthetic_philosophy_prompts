I'm making a little exercise on aesthetic testimony and I want your take. Take this list of things a critic might say:

- "This chord is a Neapolitan sixth."
- "The slow movement is the heart of the piece."
- "This facade is beautiful."
- "That joke is funny."
- "This vintage is better than the one from two years ago."
- "This poem is better than it looks on a first read."
- "The proportions of this room make it feel cramped."
- "That director's late films are sentimental."

For each one, score 0-1 how much a sincere assertion by a competent judge can give me a justified belief that it's true without me experiencing the thing myself: 0 = I really have to see/hear/taste it, 1 = the testimony is as good as being there. Then rank the whole list from most to least transmissable and give me a sentence per item on why it lands where it does.

So it's less of a gut call, score each item on two axes first: (i) how much the claim is about the object's perceptible structure vs. about the response it's meant to provoke in a suitably placed subject, and (ii) how much the verdict depends on discrimination you can only get by training, so a novice could parrot it but couldn't apply it themselves. Let those drive the score, and if they don't determine it, say what else is doing the work.

And for each item, flag whether it's actually a live disagreement among people who work on this or something everyone basically agrees on. I don't want a contested view dressed up as the consensus, and I'd rather see "genuinely contested" than have you quietly pick a side.
