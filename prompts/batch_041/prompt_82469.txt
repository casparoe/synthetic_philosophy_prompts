A year ago my reading group set out to settle a small question — why do promises bind? — and we have made no progress at all, which is now more interesting to me than the question itself. The accounts we are working with are decades old and the exchanges between them are familiar to everyone in the room; what is not familiar is any sense of what would settle the matter. I would like you to tell me what kind of disagreement we are actually having.

Here is the map we started with, as I understand it. On the Humean/practice view, the obligation attaches to a move within an established convention: a social practice defines what counts as promising and carries the obligation, so a promise binds because it is an act of that kind, and the question of what binds an isolated promisor with no practice behind him has no clean answer. On the second view — Scanlon's — the obligation is generated by the individual act: by voluntarily leading someone to expect that you will do something, knowing that they want that assurance, and intending to give it, you give them a claim on you, and the practice is at most scaffolding. On the third — Shiffrin's, with a related line developed by David Owens — promising is a mechanism that transfers to the promisee a certain authority over the promisor's future action: the promisor hands over a decision that was his to make, which is why only the promisee can release him, and why breaking the promise is a usurpation rather than a disappointed expectation or a departure from convention.

Our failure is not that we cannot agree which account is right; it is that we cannot agree on what an argument for one of them would even look like. And last term we invented a case that made things worse, because our intuition about it is unanimous while each account seems to reach that intuition by a route it is not entitled to.

The case. A promises B to do X. B is a thoroughgoing fatalist about other people's conduct: she forms no expectation that A will do X, arranges her affairs on the assumption that he will not, and would be mildly surprised if he did. A knows all of this. A does not do X. All of us think A wronged B, and wronged her specifically — B has a complaint, not merely a sense that something regrettable has happened. But the expectation view seems to have no expectation to work with, the reliance view no reliance, and the practice view seems to locate the obligation in the practice rather than in B, so that B's complaint comes out derivative. Only the authority view gets the directedness for free, which several of us find suspicious: it looks like it earns the right answer by building it into the description of the act.

My hypothesis, offered for you to test rather than to adopt: that the impasse is not a disagreement about grounds but about the explanandum. One of us is asking what makes a broken promise a wrong done to someone; another is asking what reason the promisor has to perform; a third is asking how a practice with these features arises, sustains itself, and what it does for those who have it. The phrase "why do promises bind" covers all three, and each of us treats his own question as the one the others are failing to answer. A rival hypothesis, which I take seriously: that the real split is about evidence — whether the intuition that the unrelied-upon, unexpecting promise still binds is data to be explained or an artifact to be explained away — and that everything else is downstream of that.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict, and I do not want a survey or a bibliography; I cannot check references and do not need them. Please structure the response as follows.

**The accounts as I have stated them** (about 350 words). Correct my map where it misdescribes any of the three positions, and say precisely where they come into contact and where they genuinely conflict.

**Diagnosis** (about 500 words). What kind of disagreement this is — verbal, a difference of explanandum, a difference about the evidential standing of intuitions, or a substantive disagreement about the grounds of obligation — argued from what each side actually claims rather than from the labels. Say whether my hypothesis is right, partly right, or wrong, and whether the rival hypothesis is the better one.

**The case, re-examined** (about 350 words). Whether my case really discriminates among the accounts, or whether each of them can absorb it with a small adjustment; if the latter, say what the adjustment costs the view that has to make it. If you think the case is rigged in the authority view's favour, say where the rigging is.

**What would count as progress** (about 250 words). Given your diagnosis, what would move this — a distinction, a case, a formal result, a change of question? Or is the impasse a sign that nothing should be expected to move it, and why?
