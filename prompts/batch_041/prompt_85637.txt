I'm in a political theory reading group that keeps circling a move I don't have a word for. Someone says national traditions are invented—flags, anthems, folk costumes, national holidays, the whole package—and everyone nods. Five minutes later the same person is explaining that their own national community is different: its traditions go back centuries, and anyway what looks like invention was imposed from outside by an empire or a colonizer, so the authentic nation was there all along underneath. Or a defender of a small nation says the big neighbor's national story is a nineteenth-century fabrication, while their own national revival is just a recovery of something that was always there. I've seen it with holidays, dialects, borders, even recipes. The pattern isn't just in-group bias. It's more specific: the general thesis that traditions are invented gets accepted in the abstract, but the label invented gets assigned only to the other side, or to an elite or colonial power, while one's own tradition is reclassified as recovered, suppressed, organic, or ancient. Sometimes the very same institution is called invented when the rival does it and authentic when we do it, and nobody seems to notice the switch. The weird part is that the same person can say all nations are imagined communities at lunch and then at dinner insist that their nation's roots are real and ancient without feeling any tension.

I know this is somewhere near the standard literature on invented traditions and imagined communities, but those don't seem to capture the asymmetry—the way inventedness is treated as a stain that can be displaced rather than a condition that applies to everyone. Is there an established name for this exact move? If not, can you name it and define it carefully? I'd like to know what it is and isn't: how it differs from ordinary ethnocentrism, from false consciousness, from strategic essentialism, and from just saying that all national identities are constructed. Also, does it require conscious cynicism, or can people sincerely believe both halves? And is there a mirror-image version where the dominant group calls the minority's tradition invented while treating its own as natural?

I'm less interested in a label than in the boundaries—what would count as an instance, what wouldn't, and whether the phenomenon has a standard explanation in the literature. If the best answer is that there's no single established term, I'd still like the nearest concepts lined up and a proposed term with a definition that makes clear where it stops applying.
