Here's my opening move. I'll grant you the easy bit: change is no problem on your view, because your worm just has parts with different properties, so nothing ever has to be bent and straight at once. Fine. My problem is what makes the parts parts of one thing. When I say I was a kid and now I'm an adult, I'm not reporting that there's a kid-stage and an adult-stage sitting around in some pile -- I'm saying one thing changed. You explained the change by scattering it across parts, but the 'one thing' seems to have dropped out of the story, and now I want to know what glues the parts into a worm rather than a heap arranged in a line. Telling me they're all parts of the worm won't help, thats the thing in question. So pick a glue. If it's causal continuity between adjacent parts, here's a case: a machine gets taken apart, the pieces sit in boxes for a decade, then someone rebuilds it from the same parts in the same arrangement. One worm with a long gap, or two worms? If two, then by your own rule I should become a new worm every time I sleep -- and if one, then causal gaps don't break worms, so what does? If instead the glue is just the continuous path through space-time, then a rock and the sand it crumbles into are one worm, and honestly so is half the room if I'm loose enough about where one thing ends -- which makes me think your worm boundaries are just something you pick, a convention, and then I dont see what all the extra ontology was buying you.
