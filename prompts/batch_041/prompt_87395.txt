Whenever people argue about whether 'generations' — Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z — are real, it goes the same way every time: one side says they're just a marketing fiction / socially constructed, the other says but look, cohort effects are measurable, and neither side ever says what kind of thing a generation would have to *be* in order to count as real. I think that's the actual gap. And my hunch is the interesting question isn't 'are generations real' but whether the vagueness is the *boundary* kind or the *no-joint-at-all* kind. 'The Midwest' has fuzzy edges but still tracks something — weather, accent, voting — whereas 'people born in even-numbered years' has crisp edges and tracks nothing. I keep thinking money is the obvious comparison, totally constructed and totally real, but then the question is what makes a generation more like money than like 'people whose surname starts with M.' So which one are generations closer to? I suspect the honest answer is 'it depends on the generation and on the variable,' and I want the proposal to argue that that's the useful answer and definately not a cop-out.
