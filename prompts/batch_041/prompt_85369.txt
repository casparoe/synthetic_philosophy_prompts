I sit on the ethics committee of a fertility clinic network in Europe and we keep having to rule on cases where part of the treatment happens abroad. Our record so far, roughly:

- intended mother whose sister carried for her, expenses only, family staying in a country where that's legal: approved
- couple who went to California through a commercial agency, surrogate was 24, first time, in debt, couple going straight home to a country that bans it: rejected
- an unrelated friend as surrogate, expenses only, family in a country where it's legal but the parentage only gets sorted out after they get home: approved, with conditions (counselling, a legal plan)
- commercial arrangement in California, no agency, surrogate was 38, had done it twice before, comfortable financially, her own lawyer, couple staying in California where the whole thing is legal: approved
- imported eggs from a clinic abroad, donor was a student on a flat fee, recipients living somewhere paid donation is illegal: rejected

New case in front of us. Couple live in a European country where surrogacy is banned and they can't get the parentage recognised at home. They matched directly with a surrogate in California, no agency, they found each other through a support group. She's 36, has been a surrogate twice, comfortable financially, has her own lawyer, they're paying her 45k plus expenses. Intended father is the genetic father; intended mother isn't, because they used a compensated egg donor they found through the same group, paid a flat fee, again no agency. Baby born in California, they come home.

What do you think we'll say? I want the candidate rules that fit our whole record, which ones the new case actually pulls apart, your best guess and the reasons for it, which precedent is the closest analog, and the one further case that would separate the rules that are still standing afterwards. If it really is underdetermined, tell me what we'd be adopting as a principle with each way of going, because I have to defend this to the board. Don't give me a general tour of the commercialisation debate, stay with our cases.
