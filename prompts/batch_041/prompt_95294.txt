For the last six years I've been building ranking and recommendation systems, so my working vocabulary is order theory as engineers use it: partial orders, total preorders, weak orders, Pareto dominance, scalarization, lexicographic orderings, utility functions, indifference classes, and the difference between a relation that is transitive and one that is merely acyclic. I know that in a total preorder, indifference is an equivalence relation, whereas in a partial order, incomparability is not. I keep running into a practical analogue of a philosophical puzzle about whether 'better than' is transitive. My system uses a tolerance-based comparator: if two items score within epsilon of each other, it treats them as tied. That seems to generate a spectrum — A is better than B, B is better than C, and so on, until a clearly worse item ends up tied with a clearly better one — without any single comparison looking wrong. I've started calling this the epsilon-tie problem. I want you to translate the main philosophical positions on this puzzle into my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report.

The positions I have in mind are at least these three: (1) transitivity is a constitutive constraint on any coherent comparative relation, so a genuine 'better than' must be transitive; (2) 'better than' can be genuinely intransitive in some cases, and the spectrum arguments show this rather than merely exposing a mistake; (3) the spectrum arguments reveal that our ordinary comparative judgments are inconsistent and need revision. Also translate the distinction between incomparability (neither item is better than the other) and incommensurability (there is no common measure on which to compare them) into order-theoretic terms. For each position and distinction, say what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Then tell me whether the residue is a defect of the engineering framework — something a ranking system cannot represent — or a sign that the philosophical debate is less clear than it looks. I am fluent in the engineering vocabulary but have no formal philosophy training, so define any philosophical term you use. Do not give citations, reading lists, or names of works. End with a final line beginning 'Verdict:' that states in one short sentence whether the residue is best understood as a limitation of the target vocabulary, a defect in the source debate, or both.
