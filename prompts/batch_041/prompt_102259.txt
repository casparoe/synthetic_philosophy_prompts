Ok, so Brentano — the 19th-century guy whose whole thing was that intentionality, being directed at an object, is the mark of the mental and nothing purely physical has it. Now suppose he's alive today and someone walks him through Dennett's claim that a thermostat or a vending machine only has "derived" intentionality borrowed from us while people have the original kind, and Searle's claim that computers have none at all, just syntax shuffling. Then they boot up a chatbot and tell it it's about to be unplugged, and it types out something begging not to be shut down. Would Brentano say that thing has genuine intentionality, or only the derived kind, or would he say the whole original/derived split is just his own mental/physical line redrawn? And wich side would he end up closer to, Dennett's or Searle's?
