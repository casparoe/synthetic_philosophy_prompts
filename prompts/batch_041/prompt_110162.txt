Ok so here's a premise I've been chewing on, please turn it into a short story.

It's around 2060. Most rich countries have quietly made generation membership a legal category. Your birth year now puts you in a registered cohort, and the cohort gates real stuff — pension formula, housing queue, whether you're a "legacy" or "transition" worker, all of it. Nobody voted for this; it just grew out of actuarial spreadsheets until it was load-bearing.

The bit I actually care about: the cutoffs were drawn at round-ish year boundaries by some consultancy decades ago, so two people born a week apart can end up in legally different worlds. And the demographers who maintain the registry have a running in-joke — the three things the labels are supposed to track (how old you are, what year it currently is, what year you were born) are mathematically tangled together, so you can't estimate them seperately without just assuming the answer you want. Which means a very powerful machine is running on a quantity that may not be identifiable at all.

So: one person, born right on the boundary, trying to get themselves reclassified. Take both sides seriously — the officials who think the cohort labels are administrative fictions that happen to work, and the neighbors whose entire sense of self has grown around being a member after fifty years of being treated as one. I keep going back and fourth on whether "Millennial" or whatever is a real kind of person or just a bucket we all agreed to live in until it started shaping us, and I want the story to sit in that tension rather than settle it.

No ending where a philosopher shows up and explains the moral. 1500-2500 words is fine.
