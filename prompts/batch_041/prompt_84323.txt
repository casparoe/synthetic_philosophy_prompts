So I need help writing somthing and it's a weird one. My company just did its promotion round and I got bumped up over a guy I've worked with for six years and honestly rate as much as I do myself, and it's been eating at me. My partner has been arguing with me about it for two weeks and we keep going in circles. Last night she said nobody deserves anything, the whole idea is made up, and I realised we aren't even disagreeing about the same thing. I want the document we should have written together instead of yelling in the kitchen.

Two camps. Call them the realists and the institutionalists. Realists: when I say Dave deserved that promotion, I'm saying something true or false about Dave, not just about our company's rubric, and the rubric can be wrong about who deserved it. Institutionalists: deserved only ever means did well by the standards of some scheme, so the sentence is a report on a rulebook plus maybe a complaint that we picked the wrong rulebook, and there's no further fact of the matter to be right or wrong about.

What I want:

A numbered list of claims we could both sign without either of us feeling we sold out. Not fluff like promotions should be fair. Teeth. Include what we're both talking about when we use the word, what we both deny (neither of us thinks whoever shouts loudest deserves it), and which arguments we both think are bad. I suspect we both hate she earned it therefore the outcome is just, and we both hate luck exists therefore desert is fake, but check whether we can actually both sign those or whether one of us is smuggling.

Then a disagreements table. Each row: the question, then my side written so I'd put my name to it, then hers written so she'd put her name to it. No hedged mush in the middle.

Then the cruxes, the sub-questions where settling them would force one of us to move. I think they're roughly these:

Whether desert claims survive swapping the rulebook. Use this case: same worker, same output, two employers, one with a generous pay scale and one stingy. Does she's underpaid mean anything past she'd be paid more elsewhere? Realists: the verdict survives, there's an amount that fits the work and one employer is missing it. Institutionalists: strip out the comparison class and the verdict goes quiet, people start asking underpaid by whose scale. Write down what each side predicts people will actually say about the case.

Whether desert is comparative or absolute. If she'd been the only candidate and there was no runner-up, could it still be true that she deserved it, or is deserving always deserving-more-than-someone? I have a feeling half our fight lives here.

Whether the ubiquity of luck kills desert or only makes it hard to apply. Realists predict: if luck undermined desert it'd equally undermine the student who studied all night, which nobody believes. Institutionalists predict: the realist retreats to the part that isn't luck, and that residue keeps shrinking till it's empty.

Whether a desert fact nobody can measure is any use. If it's true Dave deserved it and no one could ever know, does that change what anyone should do? Realists say yes, it constrains which schemes are just. Institutionalists say an unknowable constraint isn't a constraint.

Whether desert is a fit between a person and a reward or a claim one person has on other people's behaviour. These come apart, since somthing can fit while nobody owes you anything.

Also make us say out loud wether meritocracy names a system or just the story the winners tell about it.

For each crux, say what both of us would accept as relevant evidence or a relevant argument, and be honest if any of them turn out to be about words rather than about the world. If so, say which part and why.

Two constraints: no bibliography, no names of philosophers, don't tell me to go read anything, I want the document itself. And dont be nice about it. If one of my columns is just my side of a verbal dispute, put that in writing. I'd rather find out now than in month three.
