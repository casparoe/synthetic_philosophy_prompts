I am writing a chapter on the ethics of influence and I need a defensible ordering, not a survey. Here is the criterion I am working with, and I want you to apply it even where it conflicts with the more familiar ones: a mechanism respects an agent's authorship of a choice when the agent could, on reflection, endorse both the outcome and the process that produced it, including the fact that the process was designed by someone with an aim of their own. This is deliberately more demanding than disclosure plus easy avoidance. A mechanism can be fully transparent, trivially escapable, and still fail the authorship test, if the agent could not endorse having been steered in that way at all. Where my criterion and the transparency/ease-of-avoidance criteria disagree, mine governs, and I want the disagreements surfaced rather than smoothed over.

Sort the following eleven mechanisms into five tiers, from those that most fully respect authorship (tier 1) to those that most seriously compromise it (tier 5). Use the whole scale; do not cluster everything in the middle. Where two mechanisms land in the same tier, say what separates them from the entries immediately above and below.

1. Changing a workplace pension from opt-in to opt-out.
2. Imposing a cooling-off period before a large purchase becomes final.
3. Presenting identical factual information in loss terms rather than gain terms.
4. A personalised message, timed using the agent's own behavioural data, sent at the moment they are most likely to act.
5. Leaving an option available but placing one extra form and one extra click in front of it.
6. A penalty-backed commitment contract the agent signs in advance against their own future defection.
7. Publicly disclosing an agent's non-compliance with a widely shared norm.
8. Removing the option from the menu altogether.
9. Requiring the agent to write down their reasons before the options are revealed to them.
10. Quietly removing paperwork that currently stands between the agent and a benefit to which they are already entitled.
11. Paying the agent to choose one way.

Give one sentence of justification for each placement. Then answer three questions separately. First, which two items does my authorship criterion order differently from the transparency-plus-avoidability criteria, and why does the divergence arise? Second, for which single item would the placement change most if the designer's aim were something the agent would enthusiastically share rather than something they would merely tolerate, and in which direction? Third, which placement are you least confident in, and what fact about the world, if you knew it, would settle the matter?
