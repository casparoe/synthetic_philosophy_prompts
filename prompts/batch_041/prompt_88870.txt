I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background at all, and I've fallen down a rabbit hole that started with our sprint estimates being garbage. Every time someone says "this refactor is two weeks" it's five, and eventually a teammate sent round one of those behavioural-econ writeups about the inside view vs the outside view — the basic idea being, don't ask what's true of *this* project, ask what's true of projects like this. I've definately gone pretty hard on it since. I built a spreadsheet of base rates (how often side projects actually get finished, how often people who say they'll learn a language stick with it, how often small startups in my old company's niche lasted past year three) and now whenever I catch myself doing inside-view reasoning — "but I've already got the auth layer done, so..." — I just throw it out and go with the number. I did a bit of ML on the side so I think of it as: base rate = prior, my own case-specific reasoning = likelihood, and since I know my likelihood is garbage I've basically set its weight to zero and gone full prior. I've even started shaving a few percent off the base rate as a humility tax, in case the number itself is too flattering. My partner says I've got worse at predicting my own stuff and that I'm now weirdly pessimistic and annoying about it, which, annoyingly, matches my last few personal estimates actually coming in too low.

So here's what I want to know. If the outside view is just more accurate than the inside view — and that seems like the whole point, my inside view is literally the biased thing — then why would anyone ever bother with inside-view reasoning at all? And isn't assuming I'm not the exception just what epistemic modesty means? Like, if I'm being modest, shouldn't I assume my case isn't special, full stop, and stop flattering myself with reasons why my situation is different? I want to know if the humility tax should be bigger.
