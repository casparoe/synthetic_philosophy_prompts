The question:

Kant's first formulation of the categorical imperative tells us to act only on maxims we could will as universal laws. Explain the difference between the two ways a maxim can fail this test, then work through (a) the maxim of making a false promise to get money and (b) the maxim of never helping anyone in need. Finally: critics say the test is empty, because you can always redescribe your maxim until it passes. Does that objection refute the test, or can it be answered? Defend your answer.

What they wrote:

Kant's first formulation of the categorical imperative says we should act only on maxims we could will as universal laws. A maxim is the subjective principle of action — the rule I'm actually acting on, not just the outward act. To test one we ask whether it could hold as a law for everyone, and Kant says failure comes in two flavours. Sometimes the universalised maxim can't even be conceived: once universalised it destroys the practice it depends on, so there's a contradiction in conception. Other times it can be conceived but can't be willed, because it would undercut an end that every rational agent necessarily has, and that's a contradiction in the will.

Take false promising. My maxim is: when I need money, I'll borrow it promising to repay, with no intention of repaying. Universalised, nobody could rely on a promise, so the practice of promising-for-security collapses and the maxim can't even be thought. That's a contradiction in conception, which is why false promising is a perfect duty.

Now the maxim of never helping anyone in need. Universalised, no one would ever help anyone, so if I were in need I couldn't get help either, and the maxim destroys the very thing it depends on. That's also a contradiction in conception, which is why Kant treats it as an imperfect duty — I suppose it can't be perfect, because everyone has to be free to choose whom to help.

On the objection: it's basically right that you can redescribe a maxim until it passes, and Kant knew this — it's why he moves on to the Formula of Humanity in the Groundwork, which is the formulation that actually does the work. So the first formulation is a rough guide and the second one is the real test.
