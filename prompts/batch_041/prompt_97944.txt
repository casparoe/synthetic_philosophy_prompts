My friend's doing a PhD on epistemic defeaters and keeps giving me verdicts on cases of the form: X knows P, then meets evidence against P — does X still know? Three she's given me. One: Nadia has double-weighed the flour on her kitchen scale and knows the bowl holds 500g; her flatmate says he weighed a packet of the same flour and got 480g. Verdict: still knows, ignore him. Two: same setup, but a stranger in the shop says those packets are usually underfilled. Verdict: still knows, do nothing. Three: same setup, but the flatmate says he's read that this brand of scale drifts after a few years. Verdict: still knows. I asked whether she was just running the dogmatism paradox — the thing where if you know P you know any evidence against P is misleading, so you may wave all of it away — and she got cagey.

New case: a lab tech at the shop, who calibrates scales for a living, says she tested Nadia's exact model that morning and that Nadia's own unit reads 30g heavy, and offers to show the log. What will my friend say about whether Nadia still knows, and is she really a dogmatist or is something weaker doing the work in her earlier verdicts? Give me the candidate rules she could be running, what each predicts here, your best guess with reasons, and the further case that would tell the closest two apart. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question. British English, ta.
