I run the theory session in our archaeology reading group next block and the topic is the jump from an object to a belief -- not "what did they eat" but "what did they think they were doing when they put someone in the ground". The papers are dry, so I want to open with a poem read out loud before we get into them.

The thing I want it to bite on: in archaeology the same deposit can be made by totally different behaviours. Equifinality, basically -- one outcome, many causes, so "we found X, therefore they believed Y" is almost never a valid move on its own. Take a grave with goods in it. You can read the goods as grief (someone loved this person), as a claim staked by the survivors (the person burying them with the good stuff is saying something about who they are now), as hygiene or fear (settle the dead so they stay settled), or as bookkeeping (the shells and the pin came from somewhere, and they stand for debts, alliances, obligations). Four behaviours, one hole in the ground, and no amount of careful trowel work will tell you which. Add that a deposit is usually a slow accretion of unrelated events rather than a single frozen instant of living behaviour, and the "reading" of the grave starts to look like something the excavator does rather than something the grave contains.

So: write me a pantoum. Quatrains where the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza come back as the 1st and 3rd of the next one, and the last stanza closes the loop, so the poem's first line is also its last. Five stanzas. Give the first four stanzas one reading each -- grief, the survivors claim, hygiene/fear, bookkeeping -- and let the fifth be the closing one that wont pick. The whole point of the form is that a line means something different the second time round; if the repetitions are just repetitions the poem has failed. The deposit: one crouched burial, a dog at the feet, a bone pin, and a few hundred marine shells carried 200 km inland. Dont lecture the reader about theory, and dont end on the tidy lesson that the truth is unknowable -- each of the four readings should be genuinely good, so that refusing to choose costs something. Someone in the group who works on mortuary archaeology shouldnt wince.

Loose meter is fine, rhyme if it helps, but the repetition has to be doing the work.
