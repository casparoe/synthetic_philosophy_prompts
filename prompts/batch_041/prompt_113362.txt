I keep flip-flopping on the KK idea — whether knowing something means you know that you know it — and I want to see what principle actually fits my gut. Do I know my own name? Yes, and I know that I know it, no hesitation. My bank PIN, which I've typed a hundered times: I know it, and I'd say I know that I know it, I'd bet on it. There's a bird on the fence outside: I know it's there, and yeah, I know that I know. Now the odd ones. Am I slightly cold right now? Sort of — there's a faint chill. Do I know I'm cold? Barely, but yes. Do I know that I know I'm cold? No, because the chill is so faint that if I weren't cold things would feel exactly the same and I'd still say I was. Same with being in a mildly good mood: I'm in one, I know it, but I don't know that I know it, since a mood one notch lower would feel identical from the inside. So the wierd part: the PIN is also a case where I could easily be wrong (4723, or was it 4732?), and yet I claim to know that I know it, while the chill is a case where I could be wrong and I claim not to know that I know. What's the difference between those two? I wondered if it's just that knowledge needs a margin for error, but that looks like it kills the PIN verdict too and I don't want to give that up.
