I'm making a handout for a grad polisci theory seminar on "democratic backsliding" and I want a decision tree / taxonomy instead of the usual essay. Two things bug me about how this normally gets taught: everyone sorts themselves by which label they like ("I'm a liberal-democracy person") before they've said anything about actual cases, and the term itself might just be a polite word for "country I don't like right now" -- I want the handout to force that question instead of dodging it.

So build me a tree where every branch node is a question about a case, phrased so someone can answer it without already knowing where it leads, and the leaves are the named positions.

Some distinctions I definately want the tree to use, because I think they're where the real disagreement lives:

- baseline: does "backsliding" even apply to a country that was never fully democratic, or is the concept inherently relative to a prior high-water mark? Call this the baseline question.
- legality: can a government hollow out democracy while every step it takes is legal and constitutionally kosher? Some people think the interesting cases are exactly the legal ones -- erosion-by-law, sometimes called abusive constitutionalism -- others think if it's legal it isn't erosion, it's just constitutional change you happen not to like.
- intent: does it matter whether the actors mean to erode democracy, or is the label about outcomes only?
- scope: is losing a free press / independent courts / a functioning civil society the same phenomenon as losing free and fair elections, or a seperate one? I want the tree to make someone take a side here.
- threshold vs process: is backsliding a continuous slide, or a crossing of a line into a different regime type? I.e. is "backsliding" just autocratization with nicer manners.
- who decides: is the label something external observers assign, or is it only legitimate as a domestic self-description? This is the "who gets to say" branch. My instinct is it's downstream of the others, so put it near the end -- but tell me if you think it belongs earlier.

Leaves I need to see somewhere: an electoral-minimalist view; a liberal view (rights and constraints, not just elections); a deliberative or participatory view; the "legal but authoritarian / abusive constitutionalism" view; a competitive-authoritarianism style threshold view; a "it's just authoritarianism relabeled" view; and the meta-leaf that says the concept is essentially contested, a partisan label with no fact of the matter. Add any others you think the logical space actually needs.

Two more things. First, build it from the logic first: give me the combinations of answers the branches allow, then place the real positions in that space, and tell me which cells come out empty and why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?). Second, if any two leaves can't be separated by your questions, say so instead of pretending the tree splits them.

It should let someone put Hungary, Poland, Turkey and the US in different places, and more importantly make visible why reasonable people disagree about whether the US even counts. Aim for maybe 8-12 branch nodes. Don't worry about citing anyone, I just want the structure.
