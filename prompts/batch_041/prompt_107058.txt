I'm building a tier list of cohort labels — Boomers, Gen X, Millennials, Gen Z, Gen Alpha, and whatever else you want to throw in — ranked by how much each one is a real kind rather than a marketing artifact. Tier them, most-real to least-real, with a line or two of justification per tier.

Two things I want you to actually factor in, not just gesture at. First, the age-period-cohort identification problem: age, period and cohort are linearly dependent, so in principle you can't seperate a cohort effect from a period effect from an age effect with the usual data. Does that kill the generational-kinds program outright, or just make it hard to measure? Second, the performative angle: if people read "you're a Millennial" and start acting accordingly, does the label bootstrap itself into a real kind, and if so is that enough to count? Compare that to the older idea that a generation is defined by a shared formative rupture — are these the same kind of thing or competing accounts?

Don't just hand me the usual "generations are fuzzy social constructions" line — commit to a ranking and defend it. And tell me what would change your mind: what evidence or argument would move, say, Millennials or Gen Z up or down a tier, and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
