I'm putting together a metaphysics unit and want to use Back to the Future as a hook for origin essentialism. The plot basically runs on Kripke's necessity-of-origin intuition -- if Marty's parents don't get together in 1955, he and his siblings get erased, and the fading photograph (and then his own hand) is the film's image of what that would mean. But read it closely: Kripke's claim is the strong one, that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg, not merely from different parents, whereas the movie seems to make Marty's existence depend only on the two people pairing up, and then at the end it "fixes" the timeline and everyone comes back the same but nicer, with a better truck and a happier marriage, as if the same individuals could simply have had a different history. So which is it -- does the film endorse origin essentialism, quietly weaken it, or trade on an equivocation between "same parents" and "same origins"? I want a reading that takes the modal argument seriously and says where the movie lines up with it and where it resists it, and ends by saying what the fiction gets at that the bare argument can't -- the lived anxiety of being contingent, maybe, or the gap between a person's identity and their causal history. Dont just summarize the plot.
