I'm drafting a position statement for a graduate seminar on killing and letting die, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it — better to find the fault lines myself than to have a classmate find them for me. Here is where I've landed. (1) There are genuine constraints on harming: it is wrong for me to kill one innocent person even when doing so would save five others, which is why I would not kill a healthy patient to harvest their organs. (2) The constraint is not absolute, though. If the alternative to killing one were the extinction of humanity, I would permit the killing, so I hold a threshold view, with the threshold set very high. (3) What grounds the constraint is the inviolability of each person: a worth that is not the sort of thing to be weighed against others' interests the way a cost-benefit calculation weighs them, and that forbids treating a person as a mere resource for others' ends. (4) I do not, however, believe in a special class of agent-relative reasons. If a consideration is a genuine reason to act at all, I think it is a reason anyone can recognize from an impartial standpoint; the bare fact that a killing would be mine rather than someone else's does not by itself change what I have reason to do. (5) I accept the familiar pair of trolley verdicts: it may be permissible to divert a runaway trolley onto a single person in order to save five, but not to shove a bystander into its path, and I take the difference to turn on whether the one is being used as a means.

I would like you to tell me which of these commitments actually pull against one another and which only appear to, ranked by how much trouble each conflict causes; for every genuine conflict, give the cheapest way to repair it and an honest accounting of what that repair costs; and then tell me which single revision would resolve the most trouble at once. I am not antecedently attached to any one of the five, so do not spare any of them. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, without preamble or restating my question.
