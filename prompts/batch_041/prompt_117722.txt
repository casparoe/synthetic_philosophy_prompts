Since I retired from the chemistry dept I've been sitting in on a cogsci reading group, where I keep arguing with a friend, Dana, who does inverse reinforcement learning — she watches people play games and tries to back out what they're actually after. She's got a consistent track record on when that's allowed. Prices on the screen, guy pays 30% more for the direct flight: real preference for time, she'd bet money on it. Her grad student takes the same route to the lab every day: nothing, could be habit, could be he's never tried another way. The undergrad in her experiment who mashes a button for points because she was told to: nothing, the options were handed to her. The guy who re-ups for a brutal night class every year, syllabus in front of him: strong preference, she's sure. The husband who spent two years and a lot of money getting his wife the car she wanted: real choice, she says, but it tells you about her tastes, not really his.

New case: my nephew has spent three years and most of his savings on treatment at a clinic for a condition the hospital says he doesn't have. He's read the clinic's material cover to cover, thinks the hospital is wrong, and re-ups every month.

So what does Dana say about him — real preference or not? I want the rules her earlier rulings are actually consistent with, what each one predicts for my nephew, your best guess at her verdict and why, and the case that would seperate the rules. For what its worth, I keep telling her the whole project is unidentifiable — you can always trade a bit of preference for a bit of mistaken belief and get identical behaviour — and she says that's a reason to be careful, not a reason to quit.
