I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy. For some time I have been thinking about self-amending rule systems — the game Nomic and the paradox of self-amendment, constitutional amendment clauses, and more generally any rule that takes its own revision as its subject matter. I have worked my way to a set of positions, and before I write them up I would like an audit of whether they actually hang together.

Here is where I have landed.

(1) Self-amendment is not paradoxical. The appearance of contradiction when a rule authorizes its own revision comes from conflating two different things: the rule considered as a standing norm, and the particular act of legislating carried out under it. Hold those apart and the air of paradox disappears; there is nothing logically suspect about a rule's having itself as its subject matter.

(2) Even so, any system of rules that is genuinely in force must contain at least one rule that cannot be amended. In Nomic this is the role of the immutable rules, which must be transmuted before they can be touched. A rule-set with no fixed point has nothing to anchor the validity of the rest.

(3) The authority of that fixed point cannot be derived from any further rule. It can only rest on acceptance — on the fact that those subject to it treat it as binding. It functions the way a bootloader does: it is what starts the system, not something the system itself validates.

(4) On the specific case: a rule that declares itself repealed, or an amendment clause that removes the very procedure by which amendments are made, is void — it cannot do what it purports to do. Such a move does not merely change the system; it removes the ground on which any later change could be judged valid.

(5) Methodologically, I do not think you can judge whether a rule-set is consistent until you have a meta-rule for settling conflicts among rules — one that says whether later rules defeat earlier ones, or immutable ones defeat mutable ones. And that conflict rule is itself just another rule within the set.

My suspicion is that not all of these can be true at once, but I cannot see where the fault lines are. Please audit the set. Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how serious each tension is, which pairs only look inconsistent but are in fact compatible, and which single commitment I would have to abandon or revise in order to make the remainder consistent — and what that revision would cost me. I would rather have the tensions ranked by severity than treated as equally bad.
