I am a novelist, and I have reached the part of a book where I need to know what my characters have done to one another. The book concerns a family, plus two outsiders, who between them bring about a death. None of them intends the death as such, all of them contribute to it, and the last third of the novel turns on who is entitled to say "we." I have been deciding the book by ear, which is the right way to write one and a poor way to arrive at a principle, and I would like to know what principle my ear has been following.

Take the role of a philosopher of action who works on collective intentionality and joint action, and who is willing to reason from a novelist's examples rather than from the literature. Begin by restating my cases in your own words and sorting them by the feature you think is doing the work; only then commit to a principle. I would rather have your honest bookkeeping than your agreement, and a precise principle than a comprehensive one. Two of the verdicts below I hold with conviction and cannot hold together, and I would like you to say which one has to go.

My verdicts:

- Two movers carrying a piano down a stairwell, one at each end, having agreed on the plan: together, beyond argument.
- A quartet improvising, no score, no signal, each player listening: more clearly together than the movers, though no plan exists anywhere. This is the verdict I would defend to the death.
- Two strangers walking the same direction at the same pace for an hour, each unconsciously matching the other's stride: not together, though each is adjusting to the other the whole way.
- A married couple who each, separately and over years, stop trying; neither says so; the marriage ends. I write that they let it end, and the sentence feels true, yet I cannot point to an act.
- A crowd that surges and crushes a man against a barrier: I do not think the crowd acted together, and I do think the crowd is answerable as a body. I want both and cannot have both.
- A corporation that lays off four hundred people through a committee process in which every member voted for a package none of them preferred: the corporation did it, though nobody intended it.
- A shepherd and his dog moving a flock: together, though the dog can agree to nothing.
- One man proposes a robbery; the other says nothing and drives the car: together.
- Two writers who, in ignorance of each other, publish attacks on the same man on the same morning, each delighted to learn of the other: not together.
- A mother and her adult son who alternate years of caring for a dying man, without discussion and without affection: together, and I do not care that both would deny it.
- Two parties who sign an agreement, fully intend the joint act, and then each privately does something else that happens to produce the same result: not together.

The principle I have been half-using, offered so that you can test it rather than adopt it: an action is joint when each participant's action is guided by their expectation of what the others are doing and by the shape of the whole they are making together, so that if the others had done otherwise, the participant would have done otherwise too. I can already see that this admits the strangers and may lose the quartet, and I do not know what it says about the corporation or the dog.

What I want from you: the principle, or a small set of rival candidates, each stated precisely enough that I can check it against a case; for each clause, the case that forces it; your verdicts on cases I have not listed, including two chess players, a parent and a toddler putting away toys, a funeral at which the whole room weeps in the same instant, and a nation that goes to war through a chain of decisions no one intended; and an honest accounting of which of my verdicts the principle cannot accommodate and must therefore overturn. Work through the cases before you generalize, and show me where you are uncertain. If a term of art earns its place, define it in plain words the first time you use it; I cannot look anything up, so do not send me to a reading list or to names. I want the reasoning, not the sources.
