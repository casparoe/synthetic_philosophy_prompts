You are an expert in the historical-critical study of the gospels. Begin with a short brainstorm of the full space of things one might demand of a criterion of authenticity—a rule for sorting sayings and deeds that go back to Jesus from those that do not—and only then commit to a list. Write the desiderata: what such a criterion must be able to do to count as adequate for the purpose, with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely commitments of a particular research tradition. As you go, treat each criterion as supplying something like a likelihood ratio—how much more probable the evidence is if Jesus did or said the thing than if he did not—and say what that reframing preserves or destroys in the familiar criteria (embarrassment, dissimilarity, multiple attestation, coherence). The part I most want is the argument that some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, and whether that, rather than a simple shortage of evidence, is why the criteria have never settled who he was. Separate what is settled among specialists from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
