My brother and I got into it over the holidays about the phrase "conspiracy theory" and I still can't let it go. His line is that a conspiracy theory is a particular kind of thing: it explains some event by hidden coordination among powerful people, and it's built so that it can't lose — evidence against it gets folded in as part of the cover-up, the lack of evidence is itself evidence of the cover-up, and the believers are never wrong. My line was that "conspiracy theory" is basically a slur, that it's the word you reach for when you want to discredit a claim, and whether something gets the label has more to do with who's saying it and whether it inconveniences the people in charge than with anything about the claim itself. The annoying part is I don't think either of us is obviously right, and I suspect that's because we're talking about two different things that happen to share a name. A city water department quietly sitting on tests showing lead in the pipes, with a couple of employees who can't get anyone to listen — that's secret coordination plus a cover-up, and it's also the kind of thing that has actually happened, but nobody calls the person who believes it a conspiracy theorist. And then there's the other direction: claims that get the label and yet seem to be held for pretty ordinary reasons, the kind that would fall apart tomorrow if someone just produced the paperwork.

So what I want is a real comparison of the two pictures — the "conspiracy theory is a distinctive kind of bad explanation" picture versus the "conspiracy theory is just a label for claims that threaten the comfortable" picture. Which one actually does better as an account of what's going on, where does each one break down, and is there any honest way to hold onto both without just waffling? And take it concrete for me: if my brother and I are arguing about some specific claim that's been called a conspiracy theory — my friend is convinced the recycling company just trucks everything to the landfill, that kind of thing — what should we actually be looking at to decide it, and does the label help or just get in the way? I'd also like you to say how confident you are in what you're claiming, and to point out the one thing you're least sure about.
