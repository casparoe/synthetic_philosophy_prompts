For the past few months I've been sitting in on a small graduate seminar on cause prioritization — the usual trio of importance, tractability, and neglectedness, along with the various attempts to formalize or replace it — and I've come away with two nagging worries I'd like you to take seriously rather than dissolve. The first is that the framework is asked to do two quite different jobs at once. One is allocative: a foundation with a fixed pot of money wants to know where the marginal dollar does the most good, which is a portfolio question about returns curves. The other is biographical: an individual choosing what to spend a decade on is asking about option value, personal fit, and irreversible commitment, not about marginal returns. A colleague in the seminar pushes this further and proposes replacing neglectedness outright with a quantitative notion — roughly, the elasticity of expected impact with respect to additional resources in a cause — on the grounds that what matters is not how few people are working on something but how steeply its returns rise as attention arrives; a crowded field could still be the best bet if its returns are unusually convex. The second worry is that neglectedness equivocates between a problem being under-researched and a career field being under-populated, and that these can come apart sharply: a topic may be crowded with funding and thin on anyone who actually understands it, or the reverse. I don't know whether either worry survives scrutiny, but together they have made me doubt that the standard three criteria are even measuring one thing.

Now I've been asked to help a donor design a small programme: two researchers, roughly a year, conceptual work only, no new empirical studies, and explicitly aimed at improving how cause prioritization is done rather than at ranking any particular cause. Which open questions about the framework itself most deserve that year, and in what order? I want a defended agenda, not a survey: for each question, why it is upstream of the others, what would have to be true for two people to make real progress on it in twelve months, and how its resolution would change an actual allocation decision. I'm especially interested in the ones that are load-bearing but rarely made explicit — whether importance can be put on a single scale across incompatible value systems without quietly importing a contested population axiology; whether the three criteria are assumed to trade off smoothly when they might instead be lexicographic or gated by a tractability threshold; whether the downstream effects of an intervention are knowable enough for expected-impact estimates to carry information at all, or whether the whole exercise drowns in consequences too diffuse to anticipate; and whether the honest output is a single ranking or a portfolio that stays defensible across several worldviews that will not be reconciled. The wild animal suffering case strikes me as a useful stress test, since scope and neglectedness both look enormous there while tractability is exactly what is in dispute. Tell me what you would cut, what you would keep, and what would move each item up or down your list.
