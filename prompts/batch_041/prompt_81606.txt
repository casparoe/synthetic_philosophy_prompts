Write a short story of about 1,500 words set in the mid-1950s. A mathematician and a neurophysiologist have been paired on a project to build a computing machine that can keep working despite components that fail often — an attempt to imitate the reliability the brain achieves with neurons that are slow and noisy, the problem von Neumann was thinking about in his unfinished book on the computer and the brain. The story should turn on one conceptual disagreement rather than on plot: the mathematician wants a single exact formal language in which the brain's computations can be written down, while the neurophysiologist suspects that the brain's own signalling is graded and statistical, so that any exact transcription either discards what matters or merely redescribes the brain without explaining it. Do not let one character simply win; show what is at stake in the choice of descriptive language and what each of them cannot see. Write in British English, and do not quote from any real text.
