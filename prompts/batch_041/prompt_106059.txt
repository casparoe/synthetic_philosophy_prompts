Write a deadpan wellness column of about 600 words for a fictional lifestyle magazine, in the voice of its resident 'philosophical nutritionist', who adapts Nietzsche's dietetics into a modern regimen. On his own account in his late autobiographical writing, Nietzsche treats the 'question of nutrition' — alongside place, climate, and recreation — as more fundamental than any question of truth; claims indifference to food and a taste for simple, unelaborate fare; distrusts alcohol; and ties the highest achievements of thought to the body's condition, all the while writing as a chronic invalid who moved between Alpine and Italian towns in pursuit of a tolerable climate. Follow this to its conclusion and keep a straight face: the column should retail the regimen while quietly exhibiting what is strange about inferring a philosophy from a digestion — that it turns truth into a matter of climate, grades foods by the spiritual rank of those who eat them, and prescribes a cure on the testimony of a man it conspicuously failed to cure. The humour must come from the accuracy of the dietetics, so that a reader who knows the relevant texts finds it fair rather than merely absurd, while a reader who does not can still follow it. Do not name or target any living person. Write in British English.
