A colleague defends Dennett's three stances with an analogy he treats as needing no argument: the physical, design, and intentional stances, he says, are just Marr's three levels of analysis in another idiom — physical stance = the hardware implementation, design stance = representation and algorithm, intentional stance = the computational theory of what the system is doing and why. On this reading the intentional stance carries no more ontological weight than the other levels; it is simply a higher-level description, autonomous in the way Marr said the levels are autonomous, and asking whether a belief is real is as confused as asking whether a sorting algorithm is really running on the silicon. He uses this to conclude that the whole realism debate about beliefs is a pseudo-problem. Audit the analogy for me: give the correspondence table, then say where the two triads actually come apart — in particular, whether Dennett's design stance lines up with Marr's algorithmic level at all, and whether the intentional stance has any analogue of Marr's task-level question of what the system is for — and tell me whether his deflationary conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time. If a better analogy would do the work he wants, say what it is.
