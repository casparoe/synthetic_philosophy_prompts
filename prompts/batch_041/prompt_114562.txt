I'm a first-year MA student trying to turn a half-formed idea into a thesis prospectus — 9 months, maybe 15k words, no empirical work, just me and a library. Heres the idea.

Everyone argues about whether pleasure and pain are commensurable, and the fight usually gets framed as: either there's a common currency so you can add them up and trade them off, or the two are incommensurable and you can only make local, case-by-case judgements. But there's a family of views that looks like it needs both at once: the negative-utilitarian-ish ones where suffering has lexical priority over pleasure above some threshold, while below that threshold you can still trade the two against each other. So: pick a threshold T. Below T, some quantity of mild pleasure outweighs a mild pain. At or above T, no amount of pleasure compensates for the pain, ever. (I know that's a crude way to put it — tightening it is half the project.)

My hunch is that 'commensurability' is doing too much work in these debates and wants splitting into at least three separate things: whether you can rank any two options at all, whether one of them can outweigh/compensate the other, and whether there's a single unit both are being measured in. A threshold view looks like it wants ranking without compensation above T, and something suspiciously unit-like below T. So the question I want to sharpen and answer: does a lexical-threshold view of pain against pleasure actually need a cardinal measure of value below the threshold, and if so, is the position stable — or does it quietly re-import the commensurability it was supposed to be denying?

Second thing I keep tripping over, and I think it's the more original half: what is T a threshold *of*? A single episode of pain, the pain in one person's life, or the total pain in the world at a time? Those give you genuinely different views, and I suspect they come apart badly once you aggregate across people — a threshold defined on episodes might make any world-level lexicality incoherent, or generate cycles in the ranking. I want the prospectus to say which reading is defensible and what exactly breaks under the others.

I also want some of my own machinery in there rather than just re-running the standard thought experiments. What I've been calling the dial case: a single device where you set one number and it moves a person continuously from mild discomfort through to agony, with the option of buying units of mild pleasure against positions on the dial. I think walking a case like that across T, and seeing whether the indifference structure survives, will show where the view needs a measure and where it can get by with an ordering.

Turn this into a proper prospectus: state the question so it has a determinate answer, explain why the usual positions (full commensurability, flat incommensurability, plain lexical priority with no threshold) leave it open, say what the method actually is month by month, what a good result would look like versus what would count as the project failing, and be honest about whether this is too much for nine months or too thin to fill them. Don't worry about citations or a reading list, I'll fill those in myself — I just want the argumentative spine.
