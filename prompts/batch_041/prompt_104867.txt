I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I keep running into the Judy Benjamin problem as an alleged embarrassment for Bayesian updating — it gets trotted out whenever someone wants to claim that there are perfectly rational changes of belief that neither Bayes' rule nor Jeffrey conditioning can capture, and I've never seen it explained in a way that lets me tell what the problem actually demonstrates versus what it merely demonstrates about one proposed patch. As I understand the setup, Judy Benjamin is dropped into an area divided into Red Army territory and Blue Army territory, with the Red territory further divided into a Second Company section and a Headquarters section, so that there are four cells; she begins with equal credence in each of the four. She then receives a message that has the form of a conditional — something to the effect that if she is in Red, she is in Headquarters — delivered in such a way that it neither tells her whether she is in Red nor hands her a proposition she can simply conditionalize on. What I want is a careful conceptual explanation of what is going on here. Please act as an expert in Bayesian epistemology and probability kinematics, think through this carefully before writing, and start by laying out the formal setup explicitly: the four cells, her initial credences, and the precise form of the new information.

Then walk me through the conceptual issues one at a time. First, what exactly is the informational content of the message — is it a new proposition, a constraint on a conditional credence, or something else, and why does that distinction matter? Second, why can't ordinary conditionalization be applied, given that she has not learned any proposition with certainty, and why doesn't Jeffrey conditioning rescue the case either — does the message fail to specify a partition over which her credences have shifted, or is the trouble elsewhere? Third, what is the minimum-relative-information (Infomin) approach, why is it the natural thing to reach for once ordinary rules fail, and why does its answer strike people as counterintuitive — in particular, why does updating on a conditional about the Red region's internal structure apparently raise her credence that she is in Blue territory at all, and is that increase really absurd or does it have a defensible reading? Fourth, does the surprising result indict Infomin specifically, the whole idea of treating the message as a constraint to be satisfied by the nearest probability function, or Bayesian updating as such — and if there are competing diagnoses in the literature, lay them out as a menu rather than picking one and defending it. Finally, draw out the general moral about the difference between learning a proposition and learning a conditional, and say what a Bayesian should conclude about the scope of their own updating rules. Wherever you can, use the concrete numbers of the four-cell case rather than staying at the level of abstraction, and flag any step where the setup itself is ambiguous enough that different authors would describe the new information differently.
