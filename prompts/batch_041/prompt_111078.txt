I teach a 90-minute session on Machiavelli for a political theory seminar -- 16 students, one room, no budget, and I dont want to lecture up front. I want a game that makes them feel the fortune and virtù stuff in chapter 25 of The Prince instead of hearing me say it: fortune as a raging river that floods the plain, virtù as the dikes and dams you build in the calm season, and the sting in the tail -- that men succeed when their way of proceeding happens to fit the times, that a man cant just switch his way of proceeding because he can't deviate from what nature inclines him to, and that fortune is therefore the arbiter of roughly half of what we do. So give me something runnable: each player is dealt a temperament they cannot change for the whole game (cautious, impetuous, something in between), they can spend a limited budget on preparations before they know what the times will be, then several rounds unfold and the times shift. There should be no dominant strategy -- if hoarding preparations always wins, redesign it -- and at least some of the winners should win purely by the luck of their temperament matching the times. I need materials, rules, a payoff scheme that doesn't need money, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what actually happened onto the dikes, the half of our actions, and the Julius II point about impetuousness. They shouldn't hear the words virtù or fortuna until the debrief. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about what the game can't reproduce -- in particular, a dice distribution is a known risk you can estimate and hedge against, and I suspect that quietly contradicts the text. And give me a line on how to handle the closing image of fortune as a woman who has to be beaten into submission, since someone will raise it.
