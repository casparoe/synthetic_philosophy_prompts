I have come to accept a version of prioritarianism, and I want to know what should count against it. The route in was the leveling-down objection: I cannot see how a world in which everyone is equally badly off is in any respect better than one in which some are better off and no one is worse off, so I have stopped treating equality as valuable in itself and have settled on what I have seen called the priority view — that what matters is how well off people are, with extra weight given to benefits that go to those who are worse off. Please map this position's exposure: which arguments, intuitions, formal results, or real-world implications should move me; what the view implies that utilitarianism and egalitarianism do not; which of these a committed prioritarian would themselves concede as counting against the view and which they would simply dismiss; and how decisive each should be, from fatal through serious to merely awkward. I am especially bothered by three things. I cannot say why the weighting should have one shape rather than another, and I notice that a gentle weighting leaves me close to a utilitarian while a steep one leaves me giving absolute priority to the worst off, so that numbers stop counting at all. The view seems to need a cardinal, interpersonally comparable measure of well-being, and I do not know what that requirement costs me. And I gather there are ex ante and ex post versions that come apart under uncertainty, without my knowing which one my reasoning commits me to. I would also like to know whether the leveling-down objection, or something close to it, comes back around to threaten prioritarianism itself, and whether the view can be qualified until nothing could count against it — and if so, whether that is a defect or just what a view of this kind looks like.

Give me a table with one row per candidate defeater and these columns: Defeater (a short name); Kind (empirical finding, conceptual argument, intuition about a case, or formal result); What it would show if it succeeded; Status (live threat, already settled against the view, or already settled in the view's favour); How a prioritarian would respond; Verdict for me (fatal, serious, or merely awkward). Then a short closing paragraph naming the single most decisive item and the single weakest, and saying what would actually change my mind.
