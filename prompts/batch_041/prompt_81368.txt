I'm reading Bentham's essay on sexual morality — the one he wrote around 1785 and left in manuscript, only printed in the 1970s — and I keep getting stuck on this bit:

"To what class of offences shall we refer these irregularities of the venereal appetite which are stiled unnatural? ... I have been tormenting myself for years to find if possible a sufficient ground for treating them with the severity with which they are treated at this time of day by all European nations: but upon the principle utility I can find none."

Then a little further on, dealing with the suggestion (he takes it from Montesquieu) that the practice enfeebles the men who do it:

"If however it tends to weaken a man it is not any single act that can in any sensible degree have that effect. It can only be the habit: the act thus will become obnoxious as evidencing the existence, in probability, of the habit."

What I cant get straight is the shape of the argument. Is the real claim an empirical one — the weakening never happens, so the law has no mischief to answer to — in which case everything hangs on a physiological premise he never substantiates? Or is the stronger move the conditional one: grant the weakening, and even then the mischief falls on the agent himself and on the state's stock of force, and punishment still fails the utility test? He files the thing under "offences against one's self" but only on the supposition that the weakening story is true, then seems to walk away from the classification, and I can't tell how much work it's doing. Related: is this the harm principle before Mill, or just utility applied to the question of legislation, with no principled limit on what the law may punish? And what do I make of his remark that the law draws no distinction between the act done with the patient's consent and the same act done by violence — does he need consent as a category at all, or is it decoration in an argument that runs entirely on aggregate pain and pleasure?

I'd rather have a close reading of how the argument is built than a summary of what he concludes.
