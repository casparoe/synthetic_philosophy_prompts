ok so I'm in this reading group on big history -- Sapiens, Guns Germs and Steel, The Dawn of Everything, that whole shelf of "here's the entire human story in 400 pages" books -- and we keep having the same fight every week. one person says Harari's story is too tidy and everything is always building toward something, someone else says Diamond is just geographic determinism with a bow on it, someone defends Graeber and Wengrow for putting agency and contingency back in, and then we argue for two hours without ever agreeing on what we're even judging these books by. so before the next session I want the rubric first, not another round of vibes.

write me the desiderata for a grand narrative of human history. what does one of these things actually have to deliver to be any good? I mean the real requirements, each with the case or consideration behind it -- the events and findings it can't get wrong, the distinctions it can't collapse (explaining something is not the same as describing it, and "this made the outcome more likely" is not the same as "this caused the outcome"), the constraints from archaeology, genetics, and dating it has to stay consistent with. and tell me which of these are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp -- like I suspect "must contain a single driving mechanism" is a Diamond-and-Harari preference rather than a genuine requirement, but I want you to tell me.

two things I want folded in, because I think they're where these books actually cheat:

(1) scale-hopping. a lot of the time the story explains a huge macro outcome with a little micro mechanism, or the reverse, and never builds the bridge between the two levels. so I want a requirement that every load-bearing explanation has to declare what level it's running at and connect down (or up) to the level of the outcome it's supposed to explain. is that a real requirement or am I just annoyed?

(2) the hindsight thing. there's a demand that a narrative explain the outcome that actually happened -- why *this* trajectory and not another. but there's also a demand that its explanatory claims be statable without already knowing how it turned out, otherwise it's not explaining, it's just narrating with the answer in hand. I have a feeling these two pull against each other and can't both be fully met. if so, say so, and say which one you'd sacrifice and what it costs.
