ok so I'm writing a chapter arguing that the knowability paradox isnt really a problem about anti-realism at all, its an artifact of letting your knower be an ideal reasoner, and I need a case that does the work for me. so: build me a thought experiment to spec.

The spec is that I want an agent who genuinely knows a conjunction but doesnt know either conjunct. Thats the hinge of the whole derivation -- you take some unknown truth, p and nobody knows p, suppose it to be knowable, then pull it apart into knowing p and knowing that nobody knows p, and the second half is what kills you. I want a case where the pulling-apart step just fails, on an agent I can describe without blushing. Constraints: the agent has to be a plausible bounded cognizer -- a person with some specific cognitive limit, an animal, a small trained network, whatever you like -- no gods, no oracles, no demons, and no possible-worlds machinery beyond the standard kit. The conjuncts should be ordinary empirical claims, not self-referential or indexical tricks, and the case has to come out the same way whether you're a reliabilist or a JTB-plus-defeater person, because if it only works under one theory of knowledge my referee will just say I chose the theory to fit the answer. And please no lottery tickets and no preface-paradox library -- those are the two examples everyone reaches for and the whole point is that this generalizes. Also dont just stipulate that your agent fails to know the conjuncts; the mechanism has to be described concretely enough that the failure falls out of it. No cites needed obviously, this is a construction job.

Then do the analysis: state the truth in question, run the derivation step by step and mark exactly where it dies on your agent, and tell me which single feature of the case is load-bearing -- i.e. what I'd have to change to make the derivation go through again, and whether that change is something an anti-realist can accept without giving up the store. My own hunch is that saving knowability by denying that knowledge aggregates over conjunctions is a bad trade for someone who wanted knowability to be the measure of truth, but tell me if thats wrong. Finish with the best objection from someone who thinks the conjunction move is non-negotiable -- I want the strongest version of your agent doesnt really know the conjunction, it just has a true belief about it, and then what I'd have to add to the case to close that off.
