Question 3 (25 points). Read the exchange and answer (a)-(d).

Nadia: Did you get the grant?
Omar: The committee meets in April.

(a) (7) What, if anything, does Omar implicate? Reconstruct the reasoning a hearer would go through to get from what Omar said to what he implicated, using the Cooperative Principle and the relevant maxim(s). Say which maxim you're appealing to.

(b) (6) Omar then adds: 'Though honestly, I already heard.' Nadia says: 'So you contradicted yourself.' Is she right? Explain what the addition does to the implicature, and what that shows about the relation between what a speaker says and what she implicates.

(c) (6) Now compare: 'Omar got the grant but he hasn't told his brother.' Is the contrast carried by 'but' the same kind of thing as what you described in (a)? Say what test you'd use to decide and apply it.

(d) (6) Nadia could have made the same point by saying 'You'll know when I know.' Would the implicature have been the same? What does your answer show about what a conversational implicature attaches to?

Student's answer:

(a) Omar implicates that he doesn't know yet whether he got the grant. That's not something he says — he only says when the committee meets — but it's clearly what he means, so in a broad sense it's part of what he said: if he already knew the answer, his reply would have been false. The hearer gets there through the Cooperative Principle. Nadia asked a yes/no question and Omar's reply, read literally, doesn't answer it, so Nadia has to assume he's still cooperating. The most obvious way to make the reply cooperative is to suppose he can't answer directly, and the reason he can't is that he doesn't have the information yet. So what he means is that he doesn't know yet.

(b) Nadia is right. If Omar already heard, then 'the committee meets in April' was just false, or at least he was pretending not to know. And you can't cancel a genuine implication once it's there — the hearer has already drawn it, and adding a sentence that conflicts with it doesn't take it back, it just makes Omar inconsistent. So this shows that what a speaker implicates is really part of what he said, and the second sentence is a retraction rather than a cancellation.

(c) Yes, it's the same kind of thing. 'But' carries a contrast between getting the grant and not telling his brother, and that contrast isn't part of the truth-conditions — the sentence is true even if he did tell his brother. So the contrast is suggested rather than stated, which is just what an implicature is. So 'but' is doing the same job as the reasoning in (a).

(d) Yes, 'You'll know when I know' would have implicated the same thing. But that shows the implicature is detachable from the particular words, since Nadia could have got the same effect with a completely different sentence. Detachability is one of the standard marks of a conversational implicature, along with cancellability, so (d) fits with (a).
