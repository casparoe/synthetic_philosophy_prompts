A dispute has been running through my department for the better part of a year, and I have acquired a personal stake in it that I did not choose. The subject is whether there is any such thing as the supererogatory — acts that are morally better than what is required yet still not obligatory. One party insists the category is real and indispensable: the colleague who takes on the student no one else will supervise, the stranger who gives a kidney to someone she has never met, are doing more than duty demands, and any theory that calls their acts required has lost contact with the moral life. The other party insists the category is incoherent: if an act is morally better than its alternatives, then it is what one ought to do; to call it optional is either a muddle about the word 'ought' or a confession that we lack the nerve to do what we know we should. Both sides accept the same cases, the same facts, and the same distribution of praise; they part over what an 'ought' is tracking.

It became personal last spring, when I was found to be a partial match for a relative who needed a transplant, declined to donate, and have since been unable to settle whether my guilt is warranted — which is precisely the question the two sides answer differently.

My hypothesis is that this is a verbal dispute: one side uses 'ought' for what the balance of reasons favours, the other for what may be demanded of a person, and the disagreement dissolves once the ambiguity is flagged. But I suspect something substantive may survive beneath it. Perhaps the real question is whether a permission can be grounded not in the absence of any reason but in the defeat of one — whether an act can be genuinely optional while a reason still speaks in its favour — or whether a moral theory is meant to guide deliberation from the inside rather than to assess acts from outside. Diagnose the dispute for me, engaging what each side actually claims rather than the labels they wear. Is my verbal hypothesis right, or is there a real question underneath it? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result about the logic of 'ought', a change of question — or should we expect none?
