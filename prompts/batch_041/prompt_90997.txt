(1) Every account of morality gets its authority from some source S — God's commands, God's essential nature, what we'd desire under ideal reflection, what rational agents couldn't reasonably reject, the constitutive aims of agency, take your pick.
(2) For any such S, either S has authority because it answers to a standard of goodness that was already there, or things are good because S is what it is.
(3) First horn: then goodness is prior to S, so S isn't doing the grounding, the standard is.
(4) Second horn: then goodness is whatever S delivers, with nothing to check it against; swap S for a different S and the verdicts swap with it. That's arbitrary, and an arbitrary morality isn't a morality.

(6) So there's no non-arbitrary foundation for morality at all. Realism, constructivism, theism: all down together, and the honest position is that moral facts are a useful fiction.
