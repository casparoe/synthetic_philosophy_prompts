Something I keep running into at work and I dont know if there's a word for it. I do ops at a mid-size hospital network and about a year ago we rolled out a model that reads intake notes and flags stuff for triage. It's genuinely good, better than the average nurse on a bad night. The thing that bothers me isn't the mistakes, it's what happened to the disagreements. Before, when a note was ambiguous, two nurses would read it differently and argue for a minute, and that argument is basically where the catching of errors happened. Now they both look at what the model says, and if it says routine, they both say routine. Not because anyone told them to. It's just that when the thing you'd be arguing with is a machine that's right most of the time, you don't bother. So disagreements on the floor went to basically zero and everyone reads that as "we're finally consistent," and I can't tell if it's that or if we've lost the only instrument we had for noticing we're wrong. The accuracy doesn't help, because the errors we do get are now errors for everybody at once.

Second version, smaller scale. My brother's on a dev team where everyone writes with the same assistant and the review is also done by the same assistant, and they shipped a permissions bug that four people read. Four pairs of eyes, one pair of assumptions.

What I want to know is whether this has a name, and if it does, what it doesn't cover. Because the names I'd reach for feel wrong in a specific way. Nobody here is caving to social pressure, and nobody's been cut off from outside information, we all read the same journals and go to the same conferences. It's more like the redundancy got used up. Everyone is still doing their job, the checking is still happening, it just isn't independent anymore, and the independence was the part doing the work. I know there's some old result about crowds only being smart if the people in them decide separately, and this seems related but not the same, because there the problem is copying and here nobody's copying, they're all just looking at the same thing.

Is there a concept for this? If not, name it and tell me where the edges are. Does it need a single shared tool, or does it happen with a shared textbook, a shared training program, a shared boss? And is it one phenomenon or two when the thing making everyone agree is a person instead of a model?
