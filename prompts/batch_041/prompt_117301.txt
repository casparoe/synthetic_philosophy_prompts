So for the past few months I've basically talked myself into the etiological account of biological function — the selected-effects line, where a trait's function is the effect it was selected for. Heart's function is pumping blood because pumping blood is what got hearts kept around; a trait that's just a byproduct of something else has no function of its own. I think it beats the causal-role/systemic view (function = whatever role a part plays in a system's capacity to do something) mainly because it gets the function-vs-accident distinction and the possibility of malfunction right without needing a designer or a background notion of what the system is *for*.

I want to stress test it. What would actually count against it? Not a laundry list of everything anyone's ever said — I want the things that should move me, ordered by how decisive they are, and for each one, whether someone who holds the view would themselves admit it's a problem or would just say it's a misunderstanding. And be honest at the end: has the view been patched so many times that theres nothing left that could count against it, and if so is that actually a problem or is it just the fate of a good account?

Some specific things I keep running into and can't sort out:

- Do within-organism selection processes generate functions? B cells get selected in a lymph node for affinity to an antigen — is that selection in the sense the account needs? Because if yes, it looks like the same move hands functions to cancer cell lineages inside a tumor (proliferate, invade, evade) and to a virus's surface protein (evade my immune system). If no, what's the principled restriction that excludes those but keeps ordinary natural selection? "Selection on the organism's lineage" feels like it's been built in to save the view.

- What about a trait with no selection history at all? Suppose someone synthesizes a gene, drops it into a bacterium, and the bacterium now survives an antibiotic that would otherwise kill it. Everyone in the lab will say the gene's function is to confer resistance. But the lineage has no history of being selected for that. Same with a brand new mutation that happens to detoxify a pollutant the organism has never met. Does the view just deny these have functions? Is that a bullet worth biting, and is it different from the swamp-organism style cases or basically the same objection?

- Selection is always selection for something under some description, and I can always gerrymander the description. A heart was selected for pumping blood, but also for making sounds (it does that, presumably it did it ancestrally too, it just didn't matter), for occupying the pericardial space, for being a thing whose pumping kept the lineage going, and so on. Why does the account pick out pumping blood rather than the gerrymandered disjunction? I know there are answers floating around involving explanatory relevance, or "the effect that explains why the trait persisted" — I want to know whether those answers do any real work or whether they're just restating the intuition.

- Two reverse cases: (a) a trait that was selected for X, stopped doing X, and now does Y where Y is what keeps it around; (b) a trait that arose by drift, was never selected for anything, and turns out to be doing something useful now. If the account has to keep adding clauses — "or was very recently selected, or is maintained because of it, or..." — at what point does it stop being an account and become a redescription of whatever we already wanted to call a function?

- Artifacts and designed things: a watch, a designed protein, a software routine. I think the view is fine here because the designer's intentions or the design's own replication history can play the selection role, but I'm not sure whether that's the same notion of function or a second one glued on. If it's a second one, does the claim that this is a unified account of function survive?

Then: which of these are fatal, which are merely awkward, and if you only got to press one, which would you press? Also, what would the causal-role people say is their single strongest objection to the selected-effects story, and would the selected-effects people concede it or wave it off?
