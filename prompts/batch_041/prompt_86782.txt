I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy or biology background beyond a couple of pop-science audiobooks, and I've got a long-running argument with a friend of mine who does growth marketing. His version of "culture is Darwinian" is that ideas are basically replicators the same way genes are: they mutate when people misremember them, they get selected when some spread and the rest die, they even drift when a thing blows up purely by luck inside some small group, and fitness just means "spreads", full stop. He deploys this every couple of sprints to shut down the discussion about whether we can deliberately engineer something to go viral — his line is you can't design for it, the space of ideas selects, it doesn't take instructions, and anything that looks engineered is just a story we tell afterwards about something that happened to be fit.

What I can't square with that picture is a few things. One, I spent years in a big group chat watching a handful of in-jokes evolve, and what actually happened was not copies drifting at all — people kept re-deriving the joke from scratch, and it kept snapping back to the same two or three forms no matter how badly each person mangled it. If mutation is random and selection is the only thing pushing, why does the thing keep getting pulled toward the same shapes? Two, a friend who did a genetics minor told me the selection math quietly assumes a trait comes from two parents, doesn't blend, and can't inherit anything you picked up during your own life — and memes, as far as I can tell, do all three of those things. Three, in biology the gene sits inside a body that gets thrown away, and the copying machinery is separate from the thing being copied; with memes the copy machine is the brain that's also the thing being copied, and that feels like it should break something.

So audit it for me. Start with the mapping table — meme/gene, mutation, selection, drift, fitness, whatever else he'd need to make the analogy go through — then the places the mapping actually breaks, and be explicit about which breaks matter for his conclusion (can't engineer virality, selection is the only game in town) versus which are just pedantic differences that don't touch it. Then tell me whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the whole thing the entire time. If there's a better way to picture cultural change that keeps the Darwinian flavor without the mismatched inheritance, tell me what it is and what it buys. I can follow a technical answer, I just dont know the literature, so define terms as you go.
