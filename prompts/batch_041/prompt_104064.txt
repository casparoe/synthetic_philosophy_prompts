I keep running into a move in philosophy of science that I dont have a word for. It first showed up for me in a discussion of laws of nature. Someone says laws are just the regularities that show up in our best theories. Someone else says no, laws have a kind of necessity that mere regularities lack. The first asks what necessity adds. The second says it's what lets a regularity support counterfactuals and explain its instances. The first says their summary view already does both. The second says the first has the direction of explanation backwards: the regularity holds because of the law, not the other way around. Then both agree they're describing the same events and the same counterfactuals; they only disagree about a primitive modal something that never shows up as a further difference. The same shape came up later over some boring generalization about gold spheres, where one side called it accidental and the other kept saying that's not what lawhood is. Is there a name for this exact standoff, where two views agree on every observable and every counterfactual but disagree about which way the explanation runs? I know it's near the Humean versus anti-Humean debate, but I want the name for the specific shape of the disagreement, if there is one, and if not, a term and definition that marks it off from ordinary disputes about laws. I'd also like to know whether the standoff is real or verbal, and what would have to be true for the anti-Humean side to be adding something rather than just relabeling the Humean's summary. Don't just give me a label; tell me where the edges are.
