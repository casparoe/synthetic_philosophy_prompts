I am putting together a position statement for a workshop on the science of consciousness and would like it audited before anyone else reads it. What follows is a version of integrated information theory, but I want the audit to turn on the commitments as I state them here rather than on what the secondary literature says the theory is committed to.

(1) Experience is identical with the maximally irreducible cause–effect structure that a system specifies over its own elements. There is no further fact about what an experience is like beyond the shape of that structure.

(2) Experience is graded. Nothing marks the point at which integrated information becomes experience; wherever a system has a nonzero, maximally irreducible structure, there is something it is like to be that system, however little.

(3) There is exactly one conscious subject per system, namely the maximal one. Smaller irreducible structures embedded in it do not have experiences of their own, even when they are irreducible considered in themselves.

(4) Experience is substrate-dependent. Two systems can realize the same input–output function and differ in whether and how they are conscious; in particular, a purely feedforward digital system that reproduced a human being's entire behavioral repertoire would have no experience, because its causal structure is of the wrong kind.

(5) Intentional and cognitive states are a different matter: belief, desire, and the rest are functional, and a system with the right organization would have them whatever it happened to be made of.

(6) This is a scientific position rather than a metaphysical one. It makes risky predictions — that the cerebellum contributes nothing to experience despite its neuron count, and that experience is generated in the posterior cortex rather than the prefrontal — and I expect those to be settled by experiment.

(7) I am not a panpsychist. There is nothing it is like to be a thermostat or a liver.

Tell me how these hang together. I want the genuine tensions ranked by severity, with the inference spelled out in each case — which commitment does the work in generating the conflict — and, for each one, the cheapest available revision and what it would cost me. I also want to know which pairs look inconsistent but are not, and why the appearance is misleading. If a single revision would clear up more than one tension at once, say which and what it would cost. I do not know in advance which of these I would abandon, so do not treat any one of them as the commitment I am secretly willing to trade; and do not pad the answer by flagging every pair as tense. If most of the set is coherent, say so. No references needed — argue it on the merits.
