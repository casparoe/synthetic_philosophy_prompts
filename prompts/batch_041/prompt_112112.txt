I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun attending a philosophy of science reading group, and I keep encountering a one-liner that seems to do more work than it can support: a colleague will say that laws of nature are just patterns, usually in order to set aside my question whether a law explains why its instances behave as they do, or whether there is any necessity in nature beyond constant conjunction. I would like a disambiguation rather than a defense. Set out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, from the nearly trivial to the strongly metaphysical, and for each say what it commits one to about the ontology of laws, about accidental generalizations, about laws with no instances, about the relation between fundamental laws and symmetries or conservation principles, and about whether laws can be said to govern or explain anything. Mark which readings are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, which are dubious or false, and which of them would actually support my colleague's dismissals. I do not need a reading list; I want the menu of readings and your verdicts, clear enough that I can tell him which of his own commitments he is invoking.
