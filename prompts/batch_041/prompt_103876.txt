I sit on the board of our city's arts foundation. Thursday we vote on the biggest grant we've ever considered — a resident theater company wants a five-year, seven-figure grant to turn the old indoor market hall downtown into a 400-seat theater. I'm supposed to circulate my written opinion before the vote, and I'd like you to draft it.

Here's the wrinkle. One of our board members, Dana, has been circulating a memo arguing we should reject it, and honestly it's better than I expected. She's leaning on Rousseau's case against the theater — you know, the reply to d'Alembert, where he argues a theater doesn't reform a people, it flatters them, and that what a small republic actually needs is festivals where the citizens are the show for each other rather than paying to watch professionals pretend. Her twist is local: she points out the market hall was one of the last places in the city where people from every neighborhood actually rubbed shoulders as equals — buying, selling, arguing — and that we'd be converting that into rows of seats where most people just... watch. She wants us to fund a year-round festival and street-performance program instead, at a third of the cost.

I dont fully buy it, but I don't want to just wave it away either, and two board members are now genuinely on the fence because of her. So: draft my opinion. Give me a clear recommendation — fund it, fund it with conditions, or reject in favor of her counter-proposal — with a specific timeline, since the company says it needs an answer this cycle or it walks. Take Dana's Rousseau argument seriously enough that she'd feel answered rather than outvoted, including the part where she might be right. And say something about whether that argument even applies to a city like ours, which isn't Geneva and isn't small. I have a nagging feeling his case only works if you already assume the city is a tight moral community with a shared way of life to protect, and that a modern city is more like a bunch of strangers who need somewhere to go at night — but I can't articulate it, so that's your problem.

One more thing: don't turn this into a literature review. I don't need citations or a reading list, I need something I can read aloud at the meeting that actually takes a position and tells us what to do.
