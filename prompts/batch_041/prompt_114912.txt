I direct the research budget of a mid-sized animal welfare foundation. We allocate roughly two million dollars a year in quarterly rounds of about thirty proposals; the team is three people, none of us an entomologist and none of us trained in decision theory; the board signs off on the round as a whole. A single grant runs between twenty and two hundred thousand dollars, so a bad round is expensive without being fatal, and we will have to live with whatever we decide for at least a year.

A growing share of what we receive concerns invertebrates: farmed insects, wild insects caught up in pesticide regimes, soil and leaf-litter fauna, and a few proposals about animals even further from us. These proposals almost all have the same shape. The population involved is enormous — we have been working with a standing estimate on the order of 10^18 individual insects — so, the argument runs, even a very low probability that they are sentient, multiplied by numbers that large, puts more expected welfare at stake than our entire vertebrate portfolio. We cannot act on this. We cannot check the numbers, none of us can settle whether an insect is sentient, and every round we end up with one memo saying the arithmetic compels funding and another saying we are being asked to decide on the strength of a guess that has been dressed up as a product.

Please write a decision procedure my team can actually run, proposal by proposal, in under an hour each, and that produces a one-page record for the board. I want the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. It has to be usable by someone who has never read any of the literature on this, so please do not assume we know the names of arguments or positions, and do not give us a reading list or references — we need something self-contained.

The procedure will have to handle at least the following, and I would like you to say how it does so rather than leaving it to us:

- The difference between a claim about a taxon's sentience, a claim about whether our money changes anything, and a claim about how much the change matters per individual, and how these combine without generating the appearance of precision. If the honest answer is that the procedure should refuse to combine them at all in some cases, say so and say what it does instead.
- The fact that our uncertainty about sentience is shared across every insect proposal rather than independent per proposal, so that the portfolio does not diversify the way our vertebrate grants do. Say whether that should make us more or less willing to fund any of them.
- Interventions whose sign is unknown rather than merely uncertain, including the case where restoring habitat increases the number of animals who will live and die.
- Proposals whose whole value is reducing our uncertainty, and whether we should hold back a fixed share of the budget for them rather than re-deriving the case each round.
- What to do when the plausible numbers span many orders of magnitude and the decision flips somewhere inside that range. If the answer is that the decision is then not really being made by the calculation, I would rather hear that than a confident-looking threshold.

Then run the procedure on three proposals and show your work: a lab study on whether a particular order of farmed insect has the neural machinery for anything worth calling pain; a campaign to change the killing method at an industrial insect farm, where the change is cheap and applies to something like ten billion animals a year; and a wild pollinator habitat program that we have funded for four years, that our donors love, and that I suspect your procedure will tell us to cut. I want at least one case where the procedure gives an answer we will not like.

Finally, tell me plainly which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure and who should be making them, what the procedure cannot do, and the conditions under which we should stop using it and build a different one.
