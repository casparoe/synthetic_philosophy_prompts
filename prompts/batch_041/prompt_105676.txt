I've spent years in the rationalist blogosphere watching the Malthusian and cornucopian camps talk past each other — one side treating every deferred reckoning as confirmation that the reckoning is simply later than expected, the other treating every deferral as proof that the framework was never falsifiable in the first place. I want the document that a genuinely good-faith adversarial collaboration between the two camps would produce, written so that a committed Malthusian (limits to growth, carrying capacity, diminishing returns to complexity) and a committed cornucopian (substitution, innovation, ever-rising resource productivity) could both put their names to it.

First, a numbered agreed-claims section containing only propositions both sides would actually endorse: what the original argument asserts and what it does not, which of its parts are the load-bearing mechanism versus the incidental historical packaging, what the record so far does and does not show, and — the part I care most about — a shared formal criterion distinguishing 'the theory was right but early' from 'the theory was rescued from refutation by moving the goalposts past every horizon.' Neither camp may get that criterion for free; it has to be one that would convict their own side if their own side's predictions kept failing.

Then the residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording as a fair rendering of its position rather than a caricature of the other's. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp. For each crux, give the case, argument, or measurement both camps agree bears on it, and record each camp's prediction in advance — a specific indicator, a finite horizon, and a direction, never just 'eventually.'

To keep this from collapsing into the textbook version, work the following through rather than gesturing at it. (a) Treat carrying capacity not as a fixed number but as a function of technology, institutions, and energy return, and say precisely where the two camps' functions diverge — is it the shape, the slope, or the very existence of a ceiling? (b) Take a sealed habitat — a generation ship or a fully closed colony with a fixed energy budget and a stated rule for how fast its technology improves — and have both camps state, in advance, what trajectory they predict and what observation inside the habitat would falsify their own view. (c) Say which parts of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, and why — for instance, whether 'limits' is being used by one camp to mean a hard ceiling and by the other to mean a shifting frontier, so that the two never actually contradict each other. (d) Mark explicitly where the joint statement must stay silent because the two camps share no common evidence standard, and say what would have to be built to give them one.

Format: a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table whose columns are the claim, the Malthusian wording, the cornucopian wording, the crux it turns on, and each side's pre-registered prediction. Keep the prose tight; this is a document two proud, stubborn camps would co-sign, so anything either side would flinch at has to go.
