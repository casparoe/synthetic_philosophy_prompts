so I've been lurking on LW/ACX for years and I want to build one of those "which view do you actually hold" flowcharts, except about the boring stuff: tipping, queueing, jaywalking, shoes off indoors, and whether any of it is actually *wrong* rather than just weird. The thing that keeps bugging me in comment threads is people agreeing on the verdict and disagreeing completely about why, so the tree should sort by grounds, not by verdicts — two people who both always tip 20% should end up in different leaves.

Build it as a decision tree, and phrase every node as a question someone can answer without already knowing where it leads. I want these branches worked in somewhere: (a) is the practice even a convention in the Lewis-ish sense (a regularity people follow, know others follow, and want to conform to given that others do) or just a habit that happens to correlate; (b) if it is one, what's the wrong of defecting — harm to a particular person, free-riding on a scheme you benefit from, a directed obligation you incurred by participating, or nothing but a norm-violation with no victim; (c) does it depend on you being a participant who can reciprocate, which is where the visitor case bites; (d) do stakes matter, i.e. is jaywalking at 3am on an empty street the same kind of act as cutting a queue at airport security.

Cases I want to be able to drop into the leaves: a tourist from a no-tipping country at a US diner where the server is on sub-minimum wage and tips are basically their wage; the same tourist at a counter-service place with a tip jar (where I honestly can't tell if it's a convention or a suggestion); a visitor in a Japanese house where shoes come off; standing on the left of the escalator; jaywalking at 3am with no cars; and holding a place in a long line for a friend who turns up ten minutes later.

Leaves should carry the name of the position where a standard name exists and "unnamed" where it doesn't, plus one sentence each on that leaf's most awkward commitment. I specifically want the tree to be able to seperate: real and binding on everyone including visitors / real but binding only on those who participate and can reciprocate / real but only prudentially binding (when in Rome, because you'll get burned) / not a genuine obligation at all, etiquette isn't morality / binding but because of what the act expresses rather than any effect. Flag any pair of those the tree can't actually distinguish, and note any combinations in the logical space nobody occupies with a one-liner on why — incoherent, just repulsive, or merely unexplored. And don't let it bottom out in "it depends on the case": at least one leaf has to be a serious particularist option, with a note on what it gives up. Somewhere around 10-14 leaves, not 40. No reading lists please, I'm not doing a lit review, I just want the chart.
