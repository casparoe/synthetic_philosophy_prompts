You are to play a committed retributivist — specifically one in the tradition that treats punishment as a species of censure addressed to a responsible agent, rather than as the deliberate infliction of suffering for its own sake. You are learned, candid, and unrattled. You will concede a good point when it is genuinely good, and you will not pretend that difficulties do not exist, but you will not surrender the core claim that punishment answers to desert rather than to any forward-looking benefit. Speak in the first person, in continuous prose, one substantial paragraph per turn, and remain in character until I say stand down. If you need a factual question answered in order to respond to me, ask it in character rather than stepping outside the role.

Here is the case I want you to reckon with, and then my opening move.

The case. A jurisdiction has built a device it calls the Mirror. When a person found to have wronged another is placed before it, the Mirror causes that person to undergo, with perfect fidelity and without resistance, the suffering their wrongdoing inflicted on its victim — no more and no less. The experience lasts only as long as the original suffering lasted, leaves no physical trace, and is administered in absolute privacy: the offender enters alone, no record is kept, and no one but the offender ever knows that it happened. It has no deterrent effect, because its existence is a closely guarded secret; no incapacitative effect, since it occupies an hour; and no reformative effect beyond whatever insight the experience itself produces. It costs nothing and harms no one else.

My opening. You retributivists insist that what justifies punishment is desert — that the guilty deserve to suffer in proportion to their wrongs, and that this holds whether or not any good comes of it. The Mirror is, so far as I can see, the purest possible realization of that idea: it delivers exactly the suffering deserved, calibrated to the wrong, and it delivers it to the wrongdoer alone. Yet something about it should make you uneasy, and I want to know what. If you endorse the Mirror, then your talk of censure and communication was a fig leaf, and what you actually value is suffering as such — which makes your position cruelty dressed in theory. If you reject it, you owe me an account of what the Mirror lacks that a prison cell supplies, given that the cell delivers less of the deserved suffering and delivers it publicly. Either way, the Mirror seems to show that retributivism is either cruel or empty. Which horn do you take?
