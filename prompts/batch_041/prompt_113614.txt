I'm a 4th-year bioethics PhD student and I sit on our health plan's medical policy committee — the one that decides what's a covered benefit and what gets bounced as not medically necessary. I'm drafting next week's recommendation and I want it to be consistant with what we've already done, which is:

- growth hormone for a kid with confirmed GH deficiency: covered
- growth hormone for a healthy 10-year-old at the 20th percentile whose parents just want him taller: denied
- testosterone for a man with documented hypogonadism and symptoms: covered
- testosterone for a 52-year-old with low-normal T who's tired and wants to build muscle: denied, on evidence/risk grounds
- statin for a healthy 50-year-old with very high LDL and a family history: covered
- statin for a healthy 40-year-old with average LDL who wants insurance: denied
- modafinil for narcolepsy: covered; modafinil for a healthy shift worker with no sleep disorder: denied
- bariatric surgery at BMI 42 with type 2 diabetes: covered; at BMI 33 with no comorbidities: denied
- finasteride for a 30-year-old worried about his hair: denied

Next week's case is growth hormone for a 12-year-old with idiopathic short stature — first percentile for height, no deficiency, nothing else wrong with him, getting bullied badly at school, and the endocrinologist and parents are both pushing hard. What will my committee do, and what should we do if we actually want to be consistent with ourselves? I can see at least four rules that fit everything above: the Boorse/Daniels normal-functioning line, a plain treat-or-prevent-a-disease line, a welfare line (Daniels' own fair-equality-of-opportunity reasoning would push this way), and a we-don't-pay-for-positional-advantage line — plus whatever our cost-effectiveness people have quietly been running. Do those come apart on the ISS case, which one should I go with, and what case should I put on the agenda right after it to force the issue? I want the case where the two rules I'm torn between actually give different answers, not just another hard one.
