Draft the joint statement that two camps in the theory of markets could both put their names to after a genuine adversarial collaboration. The first, the price-as-aggregator camp, holds that market prices are epistemically remarkable: a price compresses dispersed private information that no single participant possesses, so the market can in principle know more than any of its members, and price movements are the closest thing we have to a public record of what everyone knows. The second, the no-trade camp, holds that this picture cannot survive the theory of trade under shared beliefs: if traders have a common prior and it is common knowledge that all are rational, the arrival of private information cannot by itself generate any trade, since every trade would be a bet that both parties expect to win; observed trade must therefore be explained by heterogeneous priors, by hedging and liquidity needs, or by noise, and each of those explanations undercuts the claim that prices reveal private information. The no-trade camp adds a reflexive objection: if prices fully revealed everyone's information, no one would be paid for the costly work of gathering it, so full revelation is self-undermining.

I want the document such a collaboration would produce: a numbered section of claims both camps can sign, stating what prices do and do not encode and what the no-trade results actually establish and do not establish; then the precise residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; then the cruxes -- at minimum whether the common-prior assumption is doing all the work, whether the aggregator's claim is about a static snapshot or a dynamic process of discovery, and whether any conceivable observation could discriminate between them -- with, for each crux, the argument or case both sides agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance; and finally a note on which parts of the quarrel are merely verbal.
