ok so im a junior in high school and i volunteer like two afternoons a week at this wildlife rescue + conservation place near me (mostly feeding baby squirrels tbh), and recently i started sitting in on the monthly meeting where they decide what the org is actually going to DO about stuff out in the field. heres their track record as best i can piece it together:

- a whale got wrapped up in fishing rope, they sent the boat out and cut it free. it was one of the endangered kinds.
- on a monitoring trip they watched a wolf pack bring down an elk and just filmed it. no intervention.
- mange went through the local foxes and was killing almost all of them, so they set out medicated bait stations and it worked, the population came back. nobody thinks we brought the mange in.
- bad winter, hundreds of elk starving, they voted NOT to put out hay — said it just makes more elk and more starvation next year.
- introduced rats were eating seabird chicks on an island, they backed the poison drop, but they insisted on the fast-acting poison instead of the kind that takes days.
- gorillas were catching our respiratory bugs, so they paid to send a vet team in.

next months vote is on something a grad student brought them and the board is genuinely split, i cant tell which way it goes. theres a really nasty mite thing going around in the local pigeons — crusty feet and faces, itches constantly, they cant preen, its gross and probably miserable. its natural, nobody brought it in, it doesnt kill them, pigeon numbers are totally fine, and nothing else in the ecosystem depends on pigeons being sick. but the treatment is cheap, you just put medicated stuff in the feed, and a vet already showed it works and doesnt hurt anything else.

so: what rule(s) do they actually seem to be running on? what does each one predict for the pigeons? which would you bet on and why? and if two of those rules agree on the pigeons, what future case would actually split them apart? i definately keep going back and forth on whether theyre doing 'only fix what humans broke' or 'relieve suffering wherever its cheap to do so' or 'only act when a species or an ecosystem is on the line.'

also — is it weird that they cared so much about how the rats die when they were fine with killing them? does that tell you anything about which rule theyre really on?
