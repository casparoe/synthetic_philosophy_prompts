For a short talk I'm giving to my high school's philosophy club I'm trying to get my own view of karma straight, and I'd like you to check whether the following commitments can actually be held together. (1) Karma is a causal law of the universe rather than the verdict of any god or judge. (2) Karma returns to each person precisely what their actions deserve, in proportion to those actions. (3) Whatever suffering a person undergoes is therefore deserved at bottom, on account of actions in earlier lives that the person does not remember. (4) Nobody deserves blame or punishment for what they could not have foreseen or controlled. (5) The fitting response to anyone's suffering is compassion, never contempt. I suspect that some of these pull against each other, but I can't see where the real fault lines are. Please tell me which pairs genuinely conflict and how badly, which pairs only look like they conflict, and for each genuine conflict what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. Rank the genuine conflicts by severity, and say which single change would clear up the most. Please keep your answer under 500 words.
