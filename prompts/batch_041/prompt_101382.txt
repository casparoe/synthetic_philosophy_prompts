I keep running into a pattern and I don't know if it has a name, and it's bugging me.

Somebody's worried about whether one of their faculties or sources can be trusted, and they settle the worry by using that very faculty or source. A friend of mine who was anxious that his memory is getting bad started listing off times he'd remembered something correctly, and felt better — but the listing was memory. A guy I argue with online who suspects his news sources are biased reassures himself by reading more of those same sources saying they're balanced. A student who doubts her own reasoning re-derives the argument, finds it sound, and is now confident.

In each case no single step looks like it just assumes the conclusion. Every particular belief — "I did remember that right," "that article did check out," "that step follows" — seems fine on its own, and then they get piled up into "so the faculty is reliable," or at least "so I can trust it here." But the whole pile seems to only be available to someone who already trusted the thing. The little successes are the kind you'd only count as successes if you were already taking the faculty at face value.

I've heard the words "bootstrapping" and "easy knowledge" thrown around and I suspect they're near this, but I can't tell if either is exactly what I'm describing. So: does this shape have a name? And please don't stop at the label — I want the carving. How is this seperate from ordinary circular reasoning or begging the question, where some premise just is the conclusion? How is it different from a legitimate track-record argument, like trusting a friend because they've come through for you before, where the record wasn't produced by the very thing under scrutiny? Is there a real difference between the case where someone is trying to establish a faculty's reliability for the first time and the case where they're just topping up confidence they already had? If the closest established concept is the bootstrapping problem or the problem of easy knowledge, tell me how my examples hit or miss it and which fits best. And if nothing fits, coin a term and give me its edges and some borderline cases. American English, and skip the reading list — I can't look anything up anyway.
