ok, I have to hand in a position statement for a just war theory reading group and I'd rather find the cracks myself first. here's where I've landed, and I've a suspicion it's a patchwork rather than a position:

1. the test for whether someone is liable to be killed in a war is avoidability: are they part of an unjust threat, and could they have got out of it without unreasonable cost? if they couldn't reasonably get out, they aren't liable.
2. I don't accept the moral equality of combatants. unjust combatants aren't permitted to kill just ones, and the just side is permitted to kill them.
3. conscripts who've been lied to and can't reasonably refuse aren't culpable for what they do, and it'd be wrong to punish them.
4. I hold civilian immunity as close to absolute as makes no difference, and that includes not just the ordinary voter but the person machining shells in a factory, because they aren't themselves the threat.
5. as a matter of the law of armed conflict I want both sides' combatants treated alike, PoW status, same weapons rules, same courts, because a one-sided law is unworkable and inhumane.

what I want is an audit, not a tour of the literature. which of these actually pull against each other, which only appear to, how bad the real ones are, and for each real one what has to give and what that costs me. order them by severity. if a single concession would clear up more than one, say so, and say which concession that is. don't tell me it's all fine unless you can show it case by case, the cases I actually care about are the conscript, the shell-factory worker, and the citizen who votes for the war. British English please.
