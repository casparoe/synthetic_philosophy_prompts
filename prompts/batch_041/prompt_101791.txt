I have been turning over a question on my own for some time, and I would rather have it written up carefully than simply answered, so I am asking you to draft a document for me.

The question is what we are really saying when we call a condition a disease. As far as I can tell there are two broad camps. The first holds that being diseased is a plain biological fact about how a body is working — that a heart which cannot pump properly is unhealthy whether or not anyone disapproves of it, and that our values enter nowhere into the judgment. The second holds that no condition is a disease purely as a matter of fact: what makes it a disease is that it harms the person or that we disvalue it, so that if our values shifted, the condition would shift with them. I have seen these two positions given the names naturalism and normativism, though I know little more than the names.

I would like you to treat the two positions as the parties to an adversarial collaboration — the kind of good-faith exercise in which each side sets down what it can honestly agree to and then states, as exactly as it can, where it still differs — and to produce the joint statement that both sides could put their names to. Please format it as a numbered list of agreed claims followed by a table of the disagreements.

I want it to satisfy a few conditions. The agreed claims should be substantive rather than ceremonial: each side should be able to sign them without feeling it has quietly surrendered its position. Each side's remaining view should be worded so that the side in question would accept the wording as its own, rather than as a caricature drawn by its opponent. You should identify the cruxes — the smaller questions that, if they were settled, would genuinely move one side or the other. And for each crux you should name a case or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, together with each side's prediction about how that case should come out.

To keep the thing from floating off into abstraction, I have made up three cases, and I would like them used as the tests. First, a person with no symptoms and no loss of any capacity, but with a measured marker that reliably predicts a stroke ten years hence: is that person already diseased? Second, a trait that impairs nothing and bothers no one but happens to be rare in the population — some uncommon eye or skin variation, say: is that a disease? Third, a condition that does cause real suffering and shortens life, yet that many of the people who have it say they would not choose to be rid of — as many in the Deaf community describe their deafness: is that a disease?

Finally, I would like you to say whether any part of the two sides' disagreement is only a dispute about words — the same facts, redescribed — and if so, which part.
