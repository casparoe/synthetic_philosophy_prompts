A state parole board uses a structured decision guide that awards points for insight, remorse, and acceptance of responsibility, and it will deny parole to an inmate who continues to deny the offense on the ground that denial shows a lack of insight; yet the same guide lists childhood abuse, addiction, and cognitive impairment as mitigating factors that reduce culpability and weigh in favor of release. What philosophical commitments does this practice presuppose about freedom, responsibility, and self-deception? Does it presuppose that the inmate is radically free to own or disown his past and that denial is a form of bad faith, or does the trauma-mitigation provision presuppose a causal, perhaps deterministic, view on which the past diminishes responsibility? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say which parts the practice strictly requires and which merely fit with several theories, identify whether the two provisions are inconsistent, and tell me what a consistently Sartrean parole policy would look like — and what would replace it if the board adopted instead a compatibilist or trauma-informed deterministic view.
