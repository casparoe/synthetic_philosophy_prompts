ok so for my comparative social policy seminar I've ended up defending a position I'm not sure I can actually defend, and I'd like you to try to break it.

The position: the Nordic family policy bundle -- long paid leave, cheap universal daycare, the whole apparatus -- isn't just a nice accessory to the Nordic model, it's the engine of it. The causal arrow runs mostly from the policies to the trust, not the other way around. My slogan for it in the seminar was the pram-to-parliament pipeline: universal childcare pulls mothers into paid work, which gives them something to lose if the tax base shrinks and something to gain from public services, so they keep voting for high taxes; and once everyone is using the same daycares and clinics and standing in the same queue for a spot, you get the everyday familiarity that shows up as generalized trust on the surveys. If that's right, a low-trust country can in principle buy its way in: adopt the bundle, wait a generation, and the trust follows the institutions.

What should count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be, including things that have arguably already happened in countries that adopted pieces of the bundle. Be honest with me about two things. First, which parts of this would the people who genuinely believe it concede are problems, as opposed to what only critics say. Second, whether I've quietly made it unfalsifiable, because every time a country adopts the policies and the trust doesn't show up, I catch myself saying "yeah but they didn't adopt the whole bundle" -- if that move is doing all the work, tell me, and tell me whether that's fatal or just the normal cost of holding a view like this. And if you can, tell me what my version predicts that the reverse story (high trust came first, the policies are downstream of it) doesn't predict, because right now I can't see much daylight between them and that worries me more than any single counterexample. I'd rather keep the claim that the policies matter alot than the claim that they're sufficient, if I have to give one up.
