Ive been arguing with a friend about evolutionary debunking and I've realized my gut verdicts dont fit any of the standard positions, so I want to know what principle they actually add up to. Case by case, here's what I really think:

- Promise-keeping. Breaking a promise is wrong even when nobody will ever find out, and I'm told our tendency to keep them is basically reputation management. Verdict: not undermined at all.
- My own kids. I'd pull my kid out of a fire before two strangers' kids, and I think that's not just permissible but right. Nepotism obviously has an evolutionary story. Verdict: not undermined.
- My countrymen. I don't think a citizen's life matters more than a foreigner's, and when I catch myself feeling otherwise I treat it as a bias to correct. Near as I can tell the tribal instinct has the same sort of pedigree as the parental one. Verdict: undermined.
- Incest between two adults who use contraception. I think it's wrong, but when I press on why, all I find underneath is revulsion, and I know the revulsion has a selection story (something about siblings raised together not being attracted to each other). Verdict: this is the one where I feel the belief getting shaky.
- Gratitude. You owe something to someone who helped you at real cost even if they never learn you were ungrateful. Evolved, surely. Verdict: not undermined.
- Animal cruelty. Torturing a dog for fun is wrong and I honestly cannot find an evolutionary story for it. Verdict: not undermined, and if anything the missing story makes me trust it more.
- Eating meat. I don't think it's wrong, and I notice the people who do think so mostly got there by reading and reasoning rather than instinct, which makes me trust that belief less rather than more. Verdict: not undermined (the meat-is-fine one).

My half-formed rule is this: a belief is undermined if the process that produced it doesn't track the truth, i.e. it would have produced the belief whether or not the belief was true. But that seems to undermine all of them equally, since reputation management doesn't track the truth either, and it also says the meat verdict should flip, which I don't feel at all.

So is there a principle, or a small set of them, that gets all seven, or close? Which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? For each part of the principle, tell me which of my cases is doing the work, and then say what it predicts for two I haven't listed: (a) that a stranger's pain counts as much as my kid's, which I'd defend to the death and which is the exact opposite of what I'd expect evolution to have handed me, and (b) that we owe something to people who wont be born for two hundred years.
