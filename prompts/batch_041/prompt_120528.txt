I am a fourth-year PhD student in philosophy, and I have been drafted to write one of the questions for the final exam in an upper-level decision theory course I have been TAing. The material has become personally relevant in a way I did not expect. Over the past year I have been working through a genuinely hard career decision, weighing a stable but low-upside path against a much riskier one with a higher ceiling, and I noticed that my reasoning did not look like expected utility. I was treating the probability of the good outcome as though it deserved less weight than its face value, and I kept wanting to commit in advance to plans I suspected I would abandon once some of the uncertainty resolved. That led me to risk-weighted expected utility (REU), and I want the exam question to test the specific thing that tripped me up: that a nonlinear risk function applied to decumulative probabilities can make an agent dynamically inconsistent across a two-stage problem, and that this is not repaired by simply re-optimizing at each node.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 points, built around five must-hit items, each with a one-line note on how students typically get it wrong.

Constraints on the question:

- Aim it at upper-level undergraduates and beginning graduate students. They have seen expected utility theory, the Allais and Ellsberg problems, and the definition of REU, but the question must not be answerable by reciting the Allais discussion or the standard summary of REU.
- The exam is a three-hour final; this question should be answerable in about 35 minutes.
- It must not require any outside reading, and it must not ask for citations or a bibliography.
- Do not quote from any text, and do not attribute specific claims to named authors beyond the framework itself; the question should stand on the formal setup.

Setup to use. For a gamble whose outcomes are ordered from worst to best as x_1 < x_2 < ... < x_n with probabilities p_1, ..., p_n, REU is defined as

u(x_1) + sum from i = 2 to n of (u(x_i) - u(x_{i-1})) * r(P(x >= x_i)),

where P(x >= x_i) = p_i + ... + p_n and r is an increasing function with r(0) = 0 and r(1) = 1. When r(p) = p, REU reduces to expected utility. For the numerical parts, take u(x) = x, so all risk attitude comes from r, and take r(p) = p^2.

The centerpiece should be this two-stage problem. At stage 1 a fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads (probability 1/2), the agent receives $2 and the game ends. If it lands tails (probability 1/2), the agent proceeds to stage 2. At stage 2 the agent chooses between Option P, which pays $0.80 for sure, and Option Q, which pays $3 with probability 1/2 and $0 with probability 1/2.

The question should ask students to:

(i) compute the ex ante REU of the plan that selects P at stage 2 and of the plan that selects Q at stage 2, then compute the REU of P and of Q at stage 2 conditional on reaching it, and state which option is preferred at each point;

(ii) explain why no expected-utility maximizer, with any utility function, can produce this reversal, name the axiom of expected utility that the REU agent violates, and explain in terms of the risk function and the ranks of the outcomes why the reversal arises;

(iii) determine whether the reversal survives if the stage-1 settle outcome is $0 instead of $2, and explain the result;

(iv) discuss what an agent who anticipates the reversal but cannot commit should do, and whether the answer changes if the risk function is read as a genuine taste for risk rather than as a distortion of belief.

In the model answer, please show the arithmetic explicitly and flag any place where a student could reach the right conclusion from a wrong computation. In the marking scheme, separate an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain.
