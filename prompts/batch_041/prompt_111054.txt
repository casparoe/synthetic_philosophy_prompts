I am a backend software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, though I read it on the side. A colleague and I have been arguing about whether the reinforcement-learning idea of a single scalar reward signal is a useful analogy for pleasure, and that led me to the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the suspicion that the taste of a ripe peach, the relief of a headache lifting, the satisfaction of finishing a proof, the pleasure of a sad song, and the thrill of a horror film are so different that there may be no one thing they have in common. I would like a dialogue that stages the two most natural responses to this problem.

One speaker should be a hedonic monist: she holds that every pleasure shares a single phenomenal quality, a common hedonic tone or pleasedness, even when it is accompanied by very different sensory or cognitive contents. The other should be a hedonic pluralist: he holds that pleasures are unified not by any shared feeling but by a functional or attitudinal role, such as being desired, liked, or playing a certain role in practical reason. Both should be equally competent, and neither should be a strawman; each should concede the other's strongest point and then press where the view seems to fail. In particular, I want them to work through a thought experiment I have been kicking around, the hedonic blender: a device that takes two pleasures, say scratching an itch and hearing a chord resolve, and produces a third state that preserves their intensity and duration but allegedly has no distinguishable sensory or intellectual character, just pure pleasedness. The monist should treat this as evidence for a common currency; the pluralist should argue that the blender either smuggles in a sensory quality, or creates a new pleasure with its own intentional object, or shows only that we can attend to pleasure in the abstract. They should also address the masochist's pleasure and the pleasure of relief, and say whether the monist's view makes pleasure too thin to explain motivation while the pluralist's view makes hedonism trivial or unfalsifiable. Finally, they should discuss whether the reinforcement-learning reward signal is a helpful analogy or a category mistake.

Please write about 1000 words of genuine back-and-forth, not alternating monologues. Let the monist be somewhat sympathetic to the idea that hedonic tone might be a determinable quality, one that can be specified in more determinate ways, the way color is determinable into red and blue, and let the pluralist grant that many pleasures do feel alike, so that the real disagreement is about whether that likeness is essential or merely contingent. Do not cite any books, articles, or authors; I am interested in the arguments, not the literature, and I will not be able to look anything up.
