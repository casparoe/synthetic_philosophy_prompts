My friend and I have just spent most of an evening arguing about Hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture of creativity: the claim that most of what we call originality consists in sliding the existing parameters of a familiar conceptual space — variations on a theme — and that genuine novelty, if it exists at all, is the rare invention of a new parameter that the space did not previously contain.

We began with a concrete case. She said that a jazz musician who invents a new scale has done something the picture cannot accommodate; I said the scale is merely an unusual setting of a knob that already exists, namely which subset of the twelve pitch classes you treat as home. From there we escalated to what we ended up calling the meta-knob: the knob that governs how many knobs a space has. Her position is that the meta-knob is a cheat, because any supposedly new knob can be redescribed as the meta-knob turned up a notch, so the picture absorbs every counter-example. Mine is that this cuts both ways — if the meta-knob is itself a knob, then turning it is still a twiddle, so her defence is self-sealing, and yet the space can grow, which means the picture has quietly smuggled in the very thing it set out to explain.

I would like a joke about this, please. It should be funny to someone who actually knows the knob-twiddling account of creativity: the humour has to come from the content — the twiddle versus meta-twiddle distinction, the regress, the question of when two variations belong to the same space — and not merely from the fact that the subject matter is abstract. A compact set-up and a punchline would be ideal, and it should be a joke I could tell her tomorrow without either of us feeling that it had been written by someone who had only read the blurb.

Please write it in British English.
