Alright, I've been meaning to ask someone about this for ages and I can't find the right term, so here goes. I'm a philosophy professor, but I do ethics — I am not a philosophy-of-medicine person — so I know roughly that there's this long fight between the people who think "disease" is a purely descriptive, biological notion and the people who think it's inescapably evaluative. I am not asking you to rehash that fight. What I keep tripping over is a narrower thing that seems to cut right accross it, and I want to know whether it has a name and a literature, or whether I'm just going to have to invent one.

Here's the shape of it. Take two people whose bodies, right now, are in the identical condition, but who arrived there differently:

- One person was born without hearing; another lost theirs in their forties. Same ears, same silence. Yet in ordinary medical talk the second one is much more readily called a "disease" or a "condition," and alot of Deaf people will insist the first isn't a disease at all — it's a way of being, a culture, a language.
- Two people have identical mildly impaired kidney numbers today. One has always been that way; the other was perfectly fine two years ago and is visibly sliding. We get far more worked up about the slider.

What's doing the work in both cases doesn't seem to be the state the person is in — it's the comparison to some baseline they're supposed to have been at, i.e. a kind of counterfactual or historical "where you would have been." That's strange, because the descriptive/biological view says a disease is just a sub-normal level of function, which treats both members of each pair identically, and the evaluative view says it's about harm or disvalue, which also wouldn't obviously care about the history. So neither of the two big camps seems to have a slot for it.

So: (1) Is there an established name for this — the idea that disease attaches to a departure or a loss rather than to a state, so that the same bodily configuration can be a disease in one person and not in another purely because of the route they took to it? (2) If there is, give me the term and, more importantly, how it relates to the things I'm probably confusing it with — the descriptive/evaluative debate itself, the "mere difference" idea in disability ethics, whatever Canguilhem was up to. (3) If there's no clean term, please don't just slap a label on it and stop: define it, tell me what it is and what it isn't, and give me the hard boundary cases (someone born deaf who deeply wishes they weren't? a slow slide versus a sudden insult? a condition that's actually improving?). I'd honestly rather have a careful carving than a one-word answer — but I do want the word too, if one exists.

Please write in American English.
