For the extended essay I am writing for my school's philosophy society, I have tried to state my own view of the four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian — and I would like an audit of it before I commit. This is what I hold. (1) The four form a ladder: each kind has all the capacities of the kinds below it plus something new, so nothing is Popperian without also being Skinnerian. (2) What the ladder measures is increasing detachment from the actual environment — the Darwinian creature is shaped by selection across generations, the Skinnerian by feedback within its own lifetime, the Popperian by trials that happen inside it, the Gregorian by trials that happen in tools inherited from other minds. (3) A creature's kind is settled by its species and evolved equipment rather than by what it happens to be doing: a human is a Gregorian creature even when acting on autopilot, and a frog is a Darwinian creature even when it learns. (4) Genuine representation, meaning a state that is about something and can be wrong, begins only at the Popperian level, since only there can a mistake be made without the creature paying for it; conditioning yields dispositions, not content. (5) What separates Gregorian creatures is not a further faculty but culture: language, writing and arithmetic are received from other minds rather than grown from one's own. (6) Because of (5), anything that reliably acquires and uses those tools counts as a Gregorian creature whatever it is made of, including a system trained by reinforcement learning on text.

I cannot tell which parts of this pull against each other. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, how serious is each, and what is the cheapest revision that would make the set consistent — and what would that revision cost me?
