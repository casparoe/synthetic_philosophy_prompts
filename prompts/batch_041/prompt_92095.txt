Her line, and she's happy to defend it, is that once you've got hyperbolic discounting on the table, "weakness of will" is just a folk-psychological label for present bias and the philosophers should hand over the keys. I want to push her on that rather than let her do a victory lap, because I'm fairly sure the interesting stuff lives in the gap between the two vocabularies, not in either one on its own.

Could you draft me ten or so interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give? I want the follow-ups to actually land, not just be "interesting, could you say more". No generic ones please — nothing about what drew her to the field or where she sees things in ten years.

Things I specifically want hit:

- The identity claim itself: is akrasia *identical* to present bias, merely explained by it, or just correlated with it? And what does she think the philosophers' word is doing that hers can't? The follow-up should box her in whichever way she goes.

Here's my worry, and I'd like it turned into a question: a *sophisticated* discounter precommits, so she never seems to act against her own best judgement; a *naive* one simply acts on her current ranking, which by her own lights at that moment is the best option going. In neither case does anyone act against their own judgement. So where's the akrasia? I want her either to produce a case or to admit the phenomenon has quietly vanished.
- Synchronic vs diachronic. Hyperbolic discounting is a story about preference *reversals* across time; the akratic is standardly supposed to act against her judgement *at the moment of action*. Are those even the same phenomenon, or is one being used to explain the other by pun?
- What "judgement" comes to in her framework, if anything — can her model even seperate the agent's own verdict on her action from her ranking of outcomes?
- The felt struggle: the effort, the gritted teeth, the fact that it doesn't feel like merely having different preferences at different times. Is that epiphenomenal for her? - The reverse direction: is there anything in the philosophers' treatment of akrasia that the modellers should be taking on board, rather than the other way round?
- The practical upshot: if akrasia is present bias, is the fix always a commitment device — precommitment apps, deadlines, lockboxes, that sort of thing — and what does the model say about people who can't restructure their own choice environment?
