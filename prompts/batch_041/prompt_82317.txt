ok so I just moved onto the SRE team and last week I ran my first blameless postmortem. Checkout was down for 40 minutes, we did the timeline, the contributing factors, the "we're not here to blame anyone", the action items, and at the end we all agreed the incident was closed, and its kind of never actually closed. Driving home I kept thinking this whole ritual is a machine for dealing with a bad past: incident, root cause, blast radius, mitigation vs. actual fix, follow-ups nobody ever finishes, a hard rule that the doc never names a person. Our VP keeps saying we need to "reckon with" last year, which is probably what set this off.

Then I went down a rabbit hole on how countries deal with their own bad pasts — Germany and the Nazi period (they even have a word, Vergangenheitsbewältigung, something like coming to terms with the past), Japan and the war, Turkey and the Armenian genocide, the American South and slavery and the Civil War — and I definately can't stop seeing the same machine, sometimes with the safety rails off.

So: restate the German position — that a country has to work through its past instead of burying it, and how they actually went about it (the official reckoning, the memorials, teaching it in schools, the payments, the insistence that it's never finished) — in the vocabulary I actually use. Incident, timeline, root cause vs. contributing factors, blast radius, mitigation vs. fix, action items and follow-up, blameless vs. blameless-but-still-accountable, ticket closed, systemic vs. individual, postmortem theater. Do it straight, don't just wave at the resemblance, and tell me where the fit is forced. I've been an SRE about a year, I've run two of these and sat in on maybe fifteen, so use the vocabulary properly.

Then do the same, shorter, for Japan, Turkey and the American South, and say where the translation breaks hardest in each. My glosses, in order: Japan = a lot of official apology plus a lot of unresolved stuff around Yasukuni and the textbooks; Turkey = the state position is basically that it didn't happen and it's not a safe thing to argue about (my girlfriend is Turkish, I've learned the dinner table rules); the American South = the Lost Cause, the statues, "it was about states' rights". If any of those glosses are wrong, correct them before you translate, because a translation is only as good as what it's translating.

And the part I actually care about: what didn't survive at all? Not the awkward fits, the things with no counterpart anywhere in my vocabulary. Then which way the residue cuts — is postmortem culture missing something real (guilt, mourning, the fact that the incident isn't actually over), or is the Germany thing vaguer than it sounds once you try to say precisely what it consists of? Watch out for the temptation to smuggle postmortem assumptions in and call it fidelity — like assuming the past is over, or that there's a neutral party running the review.

Give it to me as a numbered list, exactly five items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no conclusion. Items 1-4 = Germany, Japan, Turkey, the American South, item 5 = the residue and your verdict.
