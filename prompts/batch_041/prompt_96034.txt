Not sure this is the right place to ask, but here goes. I've never taken a philosophy class in my life — I just read blogs and listen to podcasts on the train — and I've ended up with a pile of opinions about pain and about knowing things that I'm starting to think can't all be right at once. Can someone tell me which ones actually clash?

1. If my back is hurting right now, I can't be wrong about that. Feeling it is how I know it.
2. I've got a rule I've been calling the wobble test: you only really know something if a tiny nudge to the facts wouldn't have changed your answer. If you'd have said the same thing even with things slightly different, that's a hunch, not knowledge.
3. Pain isn't on/off. There's a fog zone — I wake up and my back is somewhere between stiff and sore and I honestly can't say which one it is.
4. But I don't think the fog zone means there's no answer. Zoom in close enough and it either hurt at that second or it didn't.
5. The know-that-you-know thing (I think it's called the KK something?) is obviously false for most stuff — I know my keys are in my coat, but I don't know that I know. For my own feelings though it seems true: if I know I'm hurting, I know that I know.
6. Knowing isn't a matter of degrees. You either know or you don't.

So which of these can't all hold at once, how bad is each clash, and whats the cheapest way to patch it? Rank them by how bad they are, and tell me which pairs only look like they clash but don't. Don't tell me what to believe — just tell me what has to go and what it costs me.
