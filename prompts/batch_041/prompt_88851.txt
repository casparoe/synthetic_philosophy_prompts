I run a monthly philosophy night in the back room of my local — about a dozen of us, nobody's got a philosophy degree, we pick a topic, one person kicks it off and then we argue for ninety minutes. There's a whiteboard, no slides. Next month it's my turn and I want to do the ant colony one: every single ant is basically a little machine following a few dumb rules — follow the smell, pick the thing up, put the thing down — and yet the colony as a whole farms, builds, goes to war, seems to decide how many foragers to send out. There's an old book with a talking ant colony in it that I read years ago (I think she's called Hillary?) and I've been chewing it over ever since. The question for the group is whether there's anything it's actually like to be the colony, or whether "the colony has decided" is just shorthand, the way we say my laptop is thinking when it's churning.

Don't explain it all to me, I've got the gist. I want a plan for the evening. What order do the ideas have to come in — what's the one thing they have to grant before the question even sounds like a real question rather than a wind-up, and what has to wait on what after that? What single example should carry the whole thing, and what does it need to have and not have? I'd thought about using the ant farm I had as a kid, but that feels too small to carry it, so tell me if something else should. I've got one twist I want to build to and I'm not sure where it belongs: what if you cut the colony in half? Both halves carry on foraging and fighting as normal, so it looks like you've either turned one mind into two, or killed one and made two new ones, or shown there was never one there to begin with. I'd definately like the evening to turn on that, if it can.

I can already hear the objections. Someone will say its just a metaphor and get smug. Someone else will say it's obviously a real mind and anyone who doubts it is being precious. Someone will bring up the internet, or a city, or a market, as if that settles it. And one bloke always says "emergence" and then sits back. I want to know roughly where in the running order each of those lands and what I actually say when it does. Also tell me what I should openly refuse to do at this level — I don't want to spend the night on zombies and I don't want to end up explaining the Chinese room — and give me one question to ask at the end that would tell me whether they've actually got it, rather than just whether they had a nice time.
