My town has a historical society and I've somehow ended up running it, which is way more than I signed up for. Background: the town was basically built by one factory, and the factory ran on what everyone here politely calls the labour question — cheap immigrant kids, injuries nobody wrote down, that kind of thing. The founder is still the town's patron saint: statue in the square, high school named after him. My great-grandfather worked in that factory, which is how I got sucked in, and I'm trying to write a book about him.

So we have one room of stuff and three plans for it. Plan one is a shrine: clean the founder's portrait, put his letters under glass, celebrate the town's grit and industry, let people walk out feeling proud. Plan two is from the people who've been cataloguing for thirty years — every receipt, every fence post, everything kept and labelled, no story, no spin, just the whole record in its place. Plan three is from a younger crowd who want the room to work as an indictment: the payroll ledger with the childrens names and wages, the safety report nobody acted on, front and center, so nobody can miss what the money was made of. And there's a practical wrinkle — an old family trust will fund the renovation, but only if the full heritage of the town is preserved, which their lawyer reads as nothing deaccessioned and nothing reframed. The heritage grant we're also chasing wants us to interpret the material for contemporary audiences, which basically means telling a story about it. I dont think we can take both.

I keep swinging between the three plans and I can't tell if that's cowardice or if there's a real case for some combination. My great-grandfather is in that ledger and he's also in the founder's letter book (wrote asking for work twice, turned down once). What should we actually do with the room — and how do I even think about which way of using the past is the right one here, if any of them is?
