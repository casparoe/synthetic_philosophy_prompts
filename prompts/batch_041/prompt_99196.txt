Ok I need help and I think this counts as a philosophy question. I've been trying to eat less meat for climate reasons, I fly a couple times a year for work and feel bad about it, and I just moved so I finally started voting in local elections. My buddy is one of those people who can argue you into a pretzel, and he's got me stuck. I don't want to be told to go read someone, I want to see the actual move. Here's his argument the way I wrote it down:

1. You're morally responsible for an outcome only if your action made a difference to whether it happened. If the world would've been exactly the same whether you acted or not, you didn't do it.
2. The grocery store orders meat in fixed pallets based on last week's sales. If I skip one pack of chicken thighs, the same pallet gets ordered anyway, so the number of chickens killed is exactly the same. (He says it doesn't help that if enough people skip, a pallet eventually drops off, because I have no way of knowing whether mine was one of the ones that mattered.)
3. Flying: the plane is going anyway, and my seat doesn't add a single extra molecule of CO2 that wouldn't have been burned.
4. Voting: my one ballot has never decided an election and realistically never will.
5. So none of these acts are things I'm on the hook for.
6. And every single person in my position can say the exact same thing, because each of our acts is individually a drop in the bucket.
7. So nobody is on the hook for climate change, for factory farming, or for who wins, even though those outcomes are bad and are made up entirely of acts like ours.
8. It gets worse. It generalizes to any harm that needs lots of small contributions where no single one is necessary: a mob, a cartel, a firing squad where the guy dies from any three bullets. And even two people who each stab someone at the same moment, where either stab alone would've killed him — neither made a difference, so on (1) neither is on the hook.
9. But that's insane. You can't have a mob beat a man to death and it turn out that nobody did anything wrong.
10. So (1) is false.

The thing is, I actually want to keep (1). Its the only thing keeping me from feeling personally guilty about absolutely everything I do, and it seems obviously true that if my action changes nothing then theres nothing to blame me for. But steps 8 and 9 look airtight too, and I can't have both. So which one do I give up? If it's (1), what replaces it and what do I lose? And if the trick is somewhere in 5-7, which step is it, and why does it look so innocent? Be honest about what it costs either way.
