I've been reading around the tragedy of the commons for a seminar and keep running into a configuration that looks like its mirror image, but I cannot find a name for it and I am no longer sure it is a commons problem at all. The pattern is this. A resource is genuinely shared — a wood, an aquifer, a pasture, a workshop with six key-holders — and everyone who uses it believes that if they all took as much as they would ideally like, the resource would be ruined. Acting on that belief they all hold back: they take less than they want, they defer to one another, they leave the thing alone rather more than they need to. What none of them realises is that the belief is false. The resource is far more robust than they assume and could comfortably absorb the level of use each of them is forgoing. So the resource goes to waste instead: the brush accumulates and the fire risk climbs, the water sits in the aquifer, the bandsaw gathers dust. Nobody has taken more than their share, nobody has been injured, there is no villain and no complainant, and yet the outcome is plainly worse than what was on offer. A friend who works in land management tells me the overgrown-woodland version of this is real enough, and a friend who shares a studio with five others says the same about the tools, which is part of why I think it might be a genuine pattern rather than a story I have told myself.

Two further features are what make me doubt my own framing. First, the restraint is self-enforcing: because the belief is shared, anyone who took more would be read as greedy, so even the people who privately suspect the resource could take more keep quiet and keep their hands off. Second, hand everyone accurate information about the resource's capacity and the whole arrangement dissolves — so the failure looks epistemic as much as anything to do with incentives, even though the outcome has the same shape as a collective action problem: a result nobody wants, produced by nobody in particular.

I know Hardin's story, I know about free-riding, and I know that commons are often governed well by their users, so I am not asking for any of that. What I cannot tell is whether the thing I have described is a recognised phenomenon with its own name, or merely the ordinary commons problem with the sign reversed, which the literature would treat as the same case under a different label. If there is a name, I want it, but the name is the least of what I am after. I want to know what the thing actually is: whether the correct diagnosis is a coordination failure, an epistemic failure, or a norm-sustained equilibrium that no one has any reason to leave; which parts of my description are load-bearing and which incidental — in particular, whether the false belief about the resource's capacity is essential, or whether the same structure arises when the belief is true but merely uncertain; and how it differs from the nearest things it might be confused with, including the ones I have not thought of. If nothing established fits, please coin a term, define it precisely, and tell me where its edges are — what would have to change in the scenario for the term to stop applying — and give me a case sitting right on the boundary. I would also be grateful if you would say what the remedy would be under each diagnosis, since the answers come apart: information in one case, changed incentives in another, and in a third nothing short of an outside shock. Please write your answer in American English.
