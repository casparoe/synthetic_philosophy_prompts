My friend and I host a small history reading group and we keep having the same fight: whether "pseudohistory" is even a real category or just a word for history we happen not to like. It always goes the same way. He says it's about method -- whether the work does source criticism, treats evidence as defeasible, can be shown wrong -- and then points out that respectable revisionist history and total crank stuff (Atlantis, the ancient-aliens crowd, Fomenko rewriting the whole chronology) sometimes use the exact same moves: radical skepticism toward received sources, arguing from silence, reinterpreting the standard evidence to fit. I say no, it's about the conclusion, "pseudohistory" just means the claim collides with what the field accepts, and he says that's circular because the field's acceptance is precisely whats in dispute. A year of this and nobody's budged. So what kind of disagreement is this really -- a genuine disagreement about criteria of evidence, a verbal one about what the word is for, or are we just answering two different questions under one label (how to appraise the reasoning vs. how to appraise the conclusion)? If it's that last one, say what would actually count as progress here, or whether nothing should. Give me a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the demarcation literature. End with a single last line starting "Diagnosis:" that names the kind of disagreement in a few words.
