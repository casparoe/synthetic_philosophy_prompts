Ok so I've somehow become the person in our co-op who deals with it when someone behaves badly. Nine of us, we run a bike shop, it happens maybe four or five times a year, and it always lands on me a week before a meeting with zero training. The stakes are basically people's rent and whether the group holds. A friend gave me Strawson's Freedom and Resentment and the only thing that stuck is that there seem to be two really different things happening when someone does something crap: either the anger is doing its job and they owe you an answer, or you've quietly stopped seeing them as someone you can be angry at and started managing them like weather. I do the second one constantly, usually when I'm too tired to have the actual conversation, and I'd like to stop guessing. Can you turn that into a procedure I can run in an evening before I walk into the room? What to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops in a no (nothing to raise with them), and where it hands off and says a human has to decide because it's about what we value as a group rather than a fact about them. Whatever comes out gets voted on by everyone, so it has to be something I can say out loud without jargon. Then run it on two: the guy who kept missing shifts and said he was depressed — I'm half hoping the answer is leave him alone, so tell me if I'm wrong — and the one who's just consistently snide in meetings until everyone went quiet, which is the one I keep dodging. And be straight with me about what the procedure can't do, I'd rather know where it misfires. Max 800 words.
