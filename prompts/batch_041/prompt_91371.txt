You are a philosopher of mind and personal identity advising a small interdisciplinary research group that has three years and modest funding to work on a deliberately strange question: whether an I can be distributed across more than one brain. Take as a starting point Hofstadter's idea that each human I is distributed over numerous brains rather than being limited to one, so that something of a person can persist in the minds of others. Assume the group cannot settle the hard problem of consciousness and should not try. Your task is not to survey the literature but to set an agenda. Which open questions in this area most deserve the group's effort, and in what order? For each, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers working with conceptual analysis and simple thought experiments rather than neuroscience, and what evidence or argument would move it up or down your ranking. Please also identify at least one line of work that looks important but that you would deprioritize, and say why. Begin by brainstorming the candidate questions before committing to a ranking, and be explicit about the criteria you use.
