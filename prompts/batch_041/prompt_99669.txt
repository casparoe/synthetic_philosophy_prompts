I need one character's central argument to rest on a claim I can state precisely, so please act as a political philosopher and institutional-design scholar with expertise in sortition, term limits, and bureaucratic politics. Think carefully before answering, and begin with a brief brainstorm of the causal channels by which such a chamber might actually govern: selection effects, deliberation, agenda control, administrative dependence, coalition formation, and whatever else you judge relevant.

Then give me your credence in the following proposition, on a 0 to 1 scale: P: In such a chamber, the dominant long-run effect on ordinary statutory policy is not greater popular responsiveness but a transfer of effective agenda-setting and drafting power to the permanent civil service. State the number plainly, then decompose it into the sub-questions you take P to depend on. These might include whether randomly selected members can acquire enough policy competence within one term to resist bureaucratic framing; whether the absence of parties and re-election incentives makes them more independent or more dependent on staff; whether the civil service is itself constrained by professional norms, media scrutiny, or a written charter; whether dominant effect can be assessed across policy domains; and any others you think belong. For each sub-question, give a rough probability and one sentence of justification, then show how they combine into your final number. Please do not answer that it depends on the details; the details are the decomposition. Finally, tell me which sub-question your credence is most sensitive to, and what kind of evidence, historical, experimental, or conceptual, would move it most. I am not looking for a reading list or citations; I want the reasoning and the number.
