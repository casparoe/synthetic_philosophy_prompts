Write a dialogue between two philosophers who disagree about whether Strawson's reactive attitudes constitute moral responsibility or merely accompany it. The first holds that resentment, indignation and guilt do not track a prior fact about the agent but are what it is to hold her answerable, so that viewing her objectively is a departure from the interpersonal relation rather than a more accurate description of it. The second holds that what makes an agent responsible is her sensitivity to reasons, and that the reactive attitudes are one natural way of registering this rather than the thing registered, so responsibility can survive their absence.

Stage their exchange at a restorative justice conference in which the victim, having already forgiven the offender, says she now feels no resentment at all, while the offender's carefully prepared acknowledgement strikes everyone in the room as hollow. Each should say what they take the case to show and respond to the other's reading of it rather than restating their own position: the first should identify precisely what has gone missing and why it matters, the second should explain why nothing of importance has. Neither should be a strawman, and the dialogue should not resolve into agreement. Roughly a thousand words.
