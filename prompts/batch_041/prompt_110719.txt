Im a TA for a second-year social choice course and I've got to hand out something for the session on Sen's liberal paradox next week. Last time the whole hour turned into people naming positions they half-remember from the reading and then arguing about which one is really the paradox, which was a waste. What I want instead is a decision tree / flowchart that takes a student from 'yeah, the prude-and-lewd case is a real conflict' to an actual position about what to do about it, plus a plain key at the end mapping answer patterns to leaves so I can hand it out as a one-pager.

Quick refresher on the case so you can phrase nodes in terms of it if you want: one copy of Lady Chatterley's Lover, two people. Lewd wants to read it, but would enjoy it even more if Prude were made to read it. Prude wants it binned unread, and if somebody has to read it he'd rather it be him than Lewd, since he thinks reading it with enjoyment is worse than reading it with disgust. So Lewd's order is: Prude reads > Lewd reads > nobody reads. Prude's is: nobody reads > Prude reads > Lewd reads. Give each person the say over whether they themselves read, and you get Lewd reads > nobody reads from Lewd's say, and nobody reads > Prude reads from Prude's say, while both of them prefer Prude reads to Lewd reads, so the Pareto condition gives you Prude reads > Lewd reads. Cycle. The three conditions on the table are the Pareto one, the unrestricted-domain one (any preference profile is admissible), and the liberal one (each person gets to decide at least one pair).

What I need:

- Build it from the questions outward, not as a list of names with a tree drawn round them. I want to see the logical space first and then where the actual positions sit in it.
- Top split should be between two readings of what the case shows: that the three conditions really can't be held together as stated, so something has to give; and that the conditions are fine but we've described the problem wrongly, so something has to be re-described. Sub-branches under each.
- Under the 'something gives' side, one branch per condition, and under each of those the genuinely distinct moves. If Pareto goes: does it go only for preferences about other people's business, or altogether, or do you keep it but stop treating preference-satisfaction as the thing that matters? If the liberal condition goes: drop individual rights, or keep them but take them out of the social ordering altogether, so they constrain what may be decided rather than entering the ranking? If unrestricted domain goes: which profiles get excluded, and does excluding them actually buy you a social ordering or just move the bump somewhere else?
- Under the 're-describe' side I want leaves for at least: rights as things the holder can waive or trade away; rights attached to acts and choices rather than to outcomes, so that Lewd reads having chosen to is a different alternative from Lewd reads because Prude made him; alternatives described richly enough to include who chose and how; the view that the case is a reductio of welfarism rather than of liberalism; and the view that social judgement needn't be a single complete ranking at all, so the cycle is a reason to stop asking for one.
- Also a leaf for the deflationary option: no general answer, the right move depends on which right is at stake, so the theorem is a menu rather than a result.
- Phrase every node as a question a student could answer without already knowing where it leads. Nothing like 'do you accept minimal liberalism?' and nothing that just hands them the label.
- Use at least these cases as the material the nodes are phrased around, not only the book one: (i) the book case above; (ii) someone who wants to sell a kidney, or take a job everyone else thinks is degrading or dangerous, where the people stopping him are acting for his own good rather than to protect anyone else; (iii) a dispute where it isn't obvious where the private sphere even is, e.g. a man who wants to paint his house a colour the whole street hates, or a community that wants an apostate to keep up its religious observances; (iv) one where the act is private for the person doing it but leaks, e.g. loud music at 2am, smoking, refusing treatment for something contagious. If a leaf only works for some of these, say so at the leaf instead of pretending its general.
- Each leaf carries the name of the position (or 'unnamed' if nobody really holds it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
- If a branch only works because there are just two people, or because each person has exactly one protected pair, say so on the branch.
- Mark the empty cells: combinations nobody occupies, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely undefended. The cell where all three conditions hold in full for every profile is of course empty — that's the theorem — so tell me which others are.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate, and any two labels that are the same position under different names.
- Size: 10-14 leaves, one page.

No citations, no reading list, no 'see Sen 1970' — I just want the tree and the key. If a position is standardly attached to somebody's name, just give me the descriptive label for it.
