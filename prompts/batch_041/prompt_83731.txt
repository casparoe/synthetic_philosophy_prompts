Right, I'm making a handout for a small reading group on the philosophy of set theory — mostly maths postgrads who've had a proper course in set theory and nothing beyond that in logic — and I want it to be a decision tree rather than yet another essay. The starting point is the one thing everyone in the room already accepts: CH is independant of ZFC, so neither it nor its negation is a theorem of the standard axioms. I want the tree to walk someone from that starting point to a position on whether CH has a truth value at all. Don't label the branches with the positions themselves — every node should be a question a person could answer without already knowing where it leads.

The distinctions I care about, roughly in this order, are: (1) does independence tell you anything about truth, or only about provability? (2) is the universe of sets one structure with a definite height and width, or a kind of structure with many equally good instances — and make this branch concrete by asking whether CH is more like the parallel postulate (where we simply get different geometries) or more like Goldbach (where there's a definite answer nobody has found yet); (3) if there is a definite answer, is it knowable in principle, or could it be a fact we're permanently shut out from? (4) does the determinacy of the full power set — the thing second-order logic is supposed to get you — actually settle it, or is that a red herring? (5) if the answer is meant to come from new axioms, must they be justified by something intrinsic to the concept of set, or is it fine to justify them by how well the resulting theory behaves? (6) is the set of reals a determinate object at all, or is that where the rot sets in?

Six to nine leaves, please. Give each leaf a name where there's a standard one and just write unnamed where there isn't, and give each one a line on its most awkward commitment. If your tree can't seperate two of the leaves, say so rather than fudging it. And if some branch leads to a combination of answers that nobody actually holds, note it and say whether you reckon that's because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. A page or so is plenty.
