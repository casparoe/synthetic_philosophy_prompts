In the socialist calculation debate, Lange's market-socialist model has always struck me as a decisive reply to Mises: the Central Planning Board need not solve the entire allocation problem in advance, because it can adjust prices the way a competitive market does — raising them when a good runs short and cutting them when it piles up — until supply and demand settle. If that is right, then the impossibility claim seems to reduce to a claim about how hard the arithmetic is, and that claim looks obsolete. A contemporary planner running a machine-learning model of the whole economy and updating prices continuously from millions of real-time signals would command vastly more computational power than any 1930s planning board could have imagined. So wasn't the calculation debate really an argument about computational tractability, one that has now been settled in Lange's favor? And if so, is there anything left, beyond political inertia, in Hayek's knowledge argument that should keep economists treating the question as genuinely open?
