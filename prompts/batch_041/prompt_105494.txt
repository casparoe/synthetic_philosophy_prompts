So my partner and I have been trying to have a kid and it's not happening the usual way, and we've started actually looking into surrogacy and egg donation, and the more I read the more I've landed on a view I didn't expect to have. I think the European approach is basically right: you can do it, but not for money. Cover the surrogate's expenses, cap them, keep it a gift, and treat the American version — where it's a contract, there's a fee, an agency takes a cut, and in some states the intended parents are on the birth certificate before the baby's even born — as a moral mistake. My partner's sister has basically offered to carry for us and even that hasn't shaken me; if anything it's made me more sure, because it feels like a gift and I don't want to be the one who puts a price on it.

But I don't want to just sit in a view that flatters me. So what would actually count against it? And I want you to commit — tell me where you land and why, not a menu of options.

A few things I'm already aware of and want you to sort through:

First, 'the European model' isn't one thing, as far as I can tell. Some countries ban paid surrogacy but allow paid egg donation; some ban egg donation outright; the UK allows both but with a compensation cap and with the surrogate staying the legal mother at birth, so the agreement isn't enforceable and she can change her mind. That last bit seems to matter — there's a difference between criminalising the practice and just refusing to enforce the deal — and I'm not sure which one I actually believe in, or whether the distinction is doing any real work. And notice the two systems just pick different people to protect: in the American version, if the surrogate changes her mind, it's the intended parents who are left with nothing.

Second, I keep running into the idea that the ban is expressive rather than protective — that its point is to say women's bodies and babies aren't commodities, not to prevent any particular harm. If that's the real justification, I want to know what could count against it, because I suspect ordinary evidence about outcomes can't touch it, and I can't tell whether that makes it a cop-out or just makes it like any other view about value.

Third, the empirical stuff: bans supposedly push people abroad, and the women who end up doing it there may be worse off and less protected — which, if true, seems to count against my view, though I can't tell if it's decisive or merely awkward.

Fourth, the feminist point I find hardest: that altruistic surrogacy is worse, not better, because it leans on women's willingness to give unpaid and to be emotionally pressured into it, whereas a market at least pays them and gives them a contract. I don't know what to do with that.

So: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which ones a defender of my view would themselves concede (not just what critics say), and tell me whether my view has been quietly rigged so nothing can count against it — and if it has, whether that's a genuine problem or just what any view about value ends up looking like. I'd rather be argued out of it than keep it because it makes me feel clean.
