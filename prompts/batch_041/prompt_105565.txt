Im putting together a session for a forecasting reading group on the outside view, specifically the "how long do empires last?" bit. The usual move is to pick a reference class of past empires and take the median lifespan, but the choice of class is doing all the work and I dont think anyone in the group has thought hard about why they pick the one they pick. So Ive sketched seven candidate reference classes for forecasting how long the current US-centred order holds, and I want you to rate each on how much weight a careful forecaster should actually give it, 0 to 1, where 0 is "basically useless, it just launders a vibe" and 1 is "this is the class I'd genuinely pool". Here they are:

1. Great powers that were still the largest economy fifty years after their share of world output peaked.
2. Hegemonic orders anchored by a single dominant reserve currency.
3. Empires that wound up by devolving into a voluntary commonwealth rather than being conquered.
4. Polities that survived a sustained fiscal-dominance episode (debt well above output, for a long stretch) without a restructuring.
5. States whose overseas basing network outlasted their relative economic primacy.
6. Orders that faced a peer competitor with a much larger population but much lower output per head.
7. Empires whose dissolution was set off by a peripheral war they chose not to escalate.

For each one, give the score and one line on the main defect (survivorship, gerrymandering to fit the answer, non-stationarity, tiny n, whatever it is). Then rank them best to worst overall, and say which if any you'd actually pool together into a single base rate versus keep only as sanity checks. Also tell me which single class you'd reach for if you could only use one, and what that choice quietly assumes.

Give it to me as a table with columns: reference class | score (0-1) | main defect | pool or sanity-check. Follow it with a short ranked list and a couple of sentences on the one-class choice. No citations, no reading list, just your reasoning. British English please, ta.
