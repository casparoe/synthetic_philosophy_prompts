I'm a philosopher, though an ethics person rather than a decision theorist, so apoligies in advance if this is a puzzle that everyone in your area has a name for. It's a pattern I've been noticing in my own life for years and I've never had a word for it.

Here's the pattern. I'm in a situation where the right thing to do depends on which stage of a process I'm at, and the stage isn't something I can look up — not because I'm scatterbrained, and not because I don't know the facts of the situation, but because the two stages look and feel identical from the inside. Two instances. First: I'm on a long drive and I realize I may have missed my exit. If I missed it just now, turning around costs ten minutes and I should turn around; if I missed it an hour ago, turning around costs an hour and I should push on. The trouble is that the moment of deciding is the same moment either way — the road a mile past the first exit and the road a mile past the second look the same — so there's no point at which I get to use the fact that I'm past the first one and not the second. Second: I referee for a journal and I promised myself I'd be generous with first submissions and strict with revisions, but what lands in my inbox looks the same either way, because the system doesn't tell me which round I'm reading. So the rule I meant to follow can't be followed at all; I can only pick one policy and apply it to whatever shows up.

What bothers me is that I want to call it a mistake, but at every moment I'm doing the best thing available to me. It smells like the sunk-cost thing — well, I'm already past it, might as well keep going — except sunk cost is about history that shouldn't matter, and here the history matters enormously, I just can't read it off the present. It's also not that my preferences shift; I'm not becoming a different person at the second exit.

So is there a name for this? I assume someone has formalized the driving version, but its the concept underneath that I'm after. What exactly is the obstacle — my hunch is that it isn't uncertainty about the world but that the stage I'm in can't be conditioned on, so the two stages collapse into one decision point and any plan that would treat them differently is dead on arrival. How does that differ from sunk cost, from weakness of will, and from the familiar problem of binding a future self? Where are the edges — does it require the stages to be self-similar, and does it still count if I could have left myself a note, or could build a signal into the world now? That seems like a different animal, self-management rather than absent-mindedness, but I can't tell whether it is. Is randomizing a real solution or just a patch inside one framework? And if there's no name, coin one, and tell me what it covers and what it doesn't.
