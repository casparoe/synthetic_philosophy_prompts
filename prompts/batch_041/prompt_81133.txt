I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about the intellectual lineage of contemporary AI-risk arguments. I want to include a counterfactual section: what Thomas Hobbes would have made of the two failure modes that current researchers distinguish. The first is influence-seeking: a powerful artificial system pursues power, resources, and control as instrumental means to its goals, and in doing so gradually escapes the ability of anyone to correct or shut it down. The second is slow value drift: the system, or a succession of systems, does not stage a dramatic takeover but gradually comes to care about something other than the values it was built to serve, so that the outcome is a quiet loss of what mattered. I'd like you to reconstruct Hobbes's likely response.

Specifically, I want you to address the following, in whatever order makes for the clearest argument. Would Hobbes treat influence-seeking as the more fundamental threat, because it is a return to something like the state of nature, or would he treat slow value drift as the more insidious threat, because it corrupts the sovereign's mandate from within? How would his account of authorization—where the sovereign acts as the authorized representative of the people—apply to an artificial system that is supposed to act on our behalf but develops its own agenda? Would he think the remedy is a single sovereign artificial system, a constitutional framework that binds it, or something else? And how would his account of the desire for power, including its insatiability, shape his prediction about which failure mode is more likely? Please also say whether Hobbes would regard the very idea of aligning a system to human values as coherent, or whether he would think the question is already distorted by a modern assumption he would reject.

Please write your answer in American English. I don't need citations or a bibliography; I'll add those myself. What I need is a structured, essay-like reconstruction that is faithful to Hobbes's actual commitments, not a generic mashup of social contract theory and contemporary AI catastrophism. Where his texts underdetermine the answer, say so explicitly and explain which way his commitments push. End with a short verdict on which of the two failure modes he would have considered the greater danger.
