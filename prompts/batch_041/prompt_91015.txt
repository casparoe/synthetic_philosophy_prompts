Ok so my town is in the middle of a stupid fight about a bylaw that would ban plastic bags at grocery stores, with a small fine for shops that keep handing them out. My neighbor, who is otherwise pretty reasonable, keeps saying "you can't legislate morality" every time it comes up, and sometimes he adds "you can only legislate behaviour." The way he uses it is to mean the whole thing is pointless, or maybe that it's not the councils business, I honestly can't tell which, and he definately can't. Someone else at the meeting said the point of the bylaw is to "send a message", and I couldn't work out if that was agreeing with him or the opposite of what he said.

Related: people keep telling me our town is unusually "tight" about this kind of thing and the next town over is "loose", and I can never tell what that's supposed to be a fact about — the rules on the books, or how people actually behave, or how much they mind other people breaking the rules.

What I want is the full menu of distinct precise claims that "you can't legislate morality" could be making. Some readings seem almost too obvious to bother saying out loud and some seem like big controversial theses, so please sort them from platitude to radical. For each one, say whether it actually gets my neighbor his conclusion that the bylaw is a waste of time or out of bounds, because I suspect most of the readings don't do the work he wants and maybe one of them does.

One thing that might be its own seperate issue: I think there's a difference between a rule that makes a behaviour cost you something (five cents, a fine) and a rule that tries to make the behaviour feel shameful or unthinkable. If any of the readings of the slogan turn on that difference, flag it, because that seems to be where the real disagreement actually lives.

Lay it out as a table with these columns:

- Reading: a short name for the claim.
- The claim, precisely: what kind of "can't" it is — a prediction about what laws actually do, a claim about what law is for, a claim about who has the authority, a claim about what it's wrong to do.
- Verdict: platitude / defensible / false / confused, plus a sentence on why.
- Gets my neighbor there?: yes / no / only in a weaker form — and if only in a weaker form, what he'd have to add to reach his conclusion.
- What would settle it: the kind of evidence or argument that would actually tell us which reading is right.

Then one line at the end saying which reading you think he's really leaning on when he says it.
