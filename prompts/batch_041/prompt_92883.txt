I retired a few years ago from a career in ecology and evolutionary biology, and I have spent the time since working my way into philosophy. I am now trying to write down my considered position on pleasure and pain, chiefly so that I can see whether the parts of it actually fit together. Here is the package, stated as plainly as I can manage.

(i) Hedonism: pleasure is the only thing that is intrinsically good, and pain the only thing intrinsically bad.

(ii) Heterogeneity: I can find no single felt quality common to everything I call a pleasure. The taste of a ripe peach, the relief when a migraine lifts, the satisfaction of an argument that finally closes, the warmth of a room after a cold walk. I cannot locate a common phenomenal ingredient across these, and I do not expect a neuroscientist to find one either.

(iii) Value is grounded in phenomenal character. What makes a state good is how it feels, not whether it is desired or wanted, and not any functional role it plays in the economy of the mind.

(iv) Pleasure is nonetheless a real kind. It is the sort of thing a mature science of mind could investigate and about which it could discover genuine facts, not a mere grab-bag assembled by speakers of a language.

(v) I reject desire-based accounts. Being desired is neither necessary nor sufficient for a state to be a pleasure, and desiring a state is not what makes it good.

(vi) Relief counts as a pleasure. When a long pain ends, the state that follows is a pleasure, even in those cases where nothing positive seems to be felt and the whole of the experience is the going-away of the pain.

(vii) Cruel pleasure. The pleasure a person takes in another's suffering is intrinsically good, even though the act and the character it expresses are plainly bad.

I would like you to audit this package rather than expound on the topic. Tell me which of these commitments pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict, and for each genuine tension how severe it is and what the cheapest way to restore consistency would be, together with what that repair costs me. I do not know in advance where the trouble is or which commitment I would surrender, so please do not assume a preferred victim. I am not asking for reading lists or citations; I want the reasoning.

Please structure the response as follows.

Summary. Two or three sentences restating the package as you understand it, so I can check that you have read it as I intended.

Genuine tensions, ranked by severity. One paragraph per tension, most severe first. For each, say exactly which commitments are involved, why they cannot all be held as stated, and what a defender would have to deny or weaken. Roughly four hundred words.

Apparent tensions that dissolve. One paragraph each, explaining why the conflict is only surface-level and what distinction does the work. Roughly two hundred words.

The cheapest repair. Identify the single revision that resolves the most, state its cost precisely, and say what the resulting position can no longer claim. Roughly two hundred words.
