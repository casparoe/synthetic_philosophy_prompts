Ok, so I'm about three weeks into reporting a long feature on promise-keeping and I've hit a wall. Every philosopher-ish person I interview gives me a different story about why a promise binds, and then I put some case from my actual reporting to them -- a promise nobody remembers, a company that "commits" to something via a chatbot, a promise the promisee has released you from but secretly still wants kept -- and they either go quiet or start bolting on exceptions. I'd rather have the spec than another theory. So write me the desiderata: what does any adequate account of why promises bind actually have to deliver? The requirements list, each one with the case or consideration that puts it on the list, and for each, whether it's non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp. Cases I can't stop chewing on and want the theory to handle:

- two people who both completely forget the promise, nothing rides on it, nobody relied, nobody's hurt -- is breaking it still wrong, and if so, wrong to who?
- the release: promisee says forget it, but privately still wants it kept and resents you either way
- the borrowed voice: a parent promising for a kid, a spokesperson "committing" a whole company -- can you bind someone who never said anything, and what kind of agent has to be doing the promising for any of this to work?
- you keep the promise, but keep it in a way that leaves the promisee worse off and wishing you'd just broken it
- five people on a committee, three release you, two don't
- a promise whose content becomes illegal, or just plainly wrong, to carry out
- a promise to someone who dies before you can keep it
