I am scheduled to present a paper at a philosophy of religion workshop, and I need a question-period preparation sheet. My thesis is that the grounding objection to Molinism can be answered by relocating the truth-makers of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom: rather than grounding them in God's will, in divine ideas, or in brute fact, I argue that they are true in virtue of features of the individual essences of merely possible creatures. On this account God's middle knowledge is, in effect, His knowledge of creaturely essences. The second prong of the paper is that an essence can ground the truth of a counterfactual without causally determining the creature's choice, so that libertarian freedom is preserved.

The room will fall into roughly three factions: open theists who deny that God has exhaustive knowledge of free creaturely acts at all; a pair of Thomists who hold that counterfactuals of freedom cannot have truth-makers because nothing in reality corresponds to them, and who will suspect that any essence-based grounding is really just God's natural knowledge of possibilities dressed up; and one or two Reformed compatibilists who think the whole libertarian scaffolding is the real mistake. There will also be a handful of agnostics about the debate who will mostly probe whether the proposal is doing any work.

Please draft the prep sheet: the objections I will actually get from this audience -- not the generic ones recycled from the literature -- ordered by how much damage they do, each phrased the way the person raising it would phrase it, each with a roughly two-minute reply and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Tell me what I should concede up front, where I should hold firm, and the one question I am hoping nobody asks.
