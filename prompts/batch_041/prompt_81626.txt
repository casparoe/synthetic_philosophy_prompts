I am drafting a position paper on the liberal paradox and would like a consistency audit of my commitments before I commit to the argument. Here is where I have landed.

1. Welfarism: the goodness of a social state depends only on how well off the individuals in it are, and the facts about individuals that enter the evaluation are their preferences, suitably idealized.
2. The weak Pareto principle is non-negotiable: if every individual strictly prefers one alternative to another, the social ranking must agree with them.
3. There are protected personal domains — a person's religion, reading, intimate associations — over which the individual concerned is decisive, regardless of what anyone else prefers.
4. The liberal paradox is a genuine impossibility, not an artifact of a careless formalization. Something has to give unless we restrict which preferences enter the domain.
5. The restriction I favor is to exclude meddlesome preferences: other-regarding preferences whose object is someone else's personal domain.
6. That exclusion is principled rather than ad hoc. A preference that another person's private choice go a certain way is not a judgment about anyone's well-being; it is an attempt to exercise authority over that person, and a welfarist was never committed to counting exercises of authority.
7. I also think personal domains should be modeled as a partition of the issue space rather than as pairs of alternatives: an individual's authority covers a whole cell, and the paradox only ever arises from carving cells into pairs.
8. Rights are side constraints on what may be done to people, not entries in a social welfare function.

I suspect this package does not hang together and cannot see where the fault lines are. Please audit it. Which commitments are genuinely in tension, how severe is each tension, and which pairs only look incompatible? For every real tension, give the cheapest repair and what it costs — which of the eight has to be weakened or abandoned, and what else that drags down with it. Order the real tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad, and close by naming the single revision that would resolve the most at once.
