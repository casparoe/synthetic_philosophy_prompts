Write a short story that dramatizes the idea of a tangled hierarchy and its inviolate level, using Escher’s image of two hands drawing each other as a governing metaphor. Set the story in an institution—perhaps a court, a codebase, a guild of cartographers, or a self-revising legal system—whose rules can modify themselves. The institution has an inviolate level: a rule or operation that makes the self-modification possible but that cannot itself be modified, named, or fully represented from within the institution without either collapsing the hierarchy or turning it into an ordinary, revisable rule. Have a character try to use the inviolate level to fix a concrete injustice or error. The story should make the reader feel both the practical necessity of the inviolate level and the conceptual cost of trying to point at it. Do not explain the theory in an essayistic aside; let the plot and imagery carry it. Then add a short afterword, addressed to the reader, that separates what is relatively settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on self-reference, hierarchy, and paradox—especially about whether an inviolate level is a real feature of such systems or an artifact of description. In that afterword, also note where the story takes liberties with the concepts or leans on a contested reading. Do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus.
