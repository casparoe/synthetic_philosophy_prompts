Ok so this has gotten a little out of hand. Me and my brother have been arguing for like two months now about whether God being hidden is a problem, and it started because I told him I stopped believing at 19 and that honestly the main thing was that nothing ever seemed to be there — no sign, no sense of anything, nothing. He said thats not an accident, thats on purpose, and that if God were as obvious as the sun we'd all be puppets or sycophants and love wouldn't mean anything. I said fine, but then what about someone who genuinely would believe if there were anything to go on and isn't resisting anything? He didn't have an answer, and now our whole philosophy meetup wants to do this as a session, so I promised I'd bring an actual quiz that sorts people instead of everyone just talking past each other.

So: please write me a "which view of divine hiddenness do you actually hold?" quiz. Eight to ten questions, each one a concrete case or a plain-English claim that a normal person can answer without knowing any jargon, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. Two things I specifically want the questions to pull apart. First, what the person thinks the complaint is really about: that the evidence doesn't settle whether God exists, that you can't be in a relationship with someone you can't perceive, that you can't know what God actually wants from you, or that the whole setup is unfair because some people get visions and some people get nothing. Second, whether they think a more obvious God would cost us something real — freedom, faith, moral seriousness, the chance to actually choose — or would just be a straight upgrade. I want at least one question on the "obvious God" counterfactual; one on a world where everyone gets exactly one weird experience that could also just be a coincidence (I think thats the most interesting case, because it splits people who think belief only has to be *possible* from people who think it has to be *reasonable*); one on somebody who believes in God but has never once felt anything; one on somebody who had an experience and then lost it; one on somebody who's never heard any of this at all; and one on the person who'd actually prefer God stay hidden, because an always-watching God sounds like a surveillance state.

For the outcomes, I want the scoring key to land people on a grid rather than a list of names, roughly: hiddenness is a real cost and that's evidence against God; hiddenness is a real cost but it's our fault, something got broken and it isn't God's doing; hiddenness is a feature, and the thing it protects is free love; hiddenness is a feature, and the thing it protects is moral or character development; hiddenness isn't really a feature or a bug, it's just what any relationship with something that isn't a body would look like, so the complaint is confused; and the skeptical one, where we just can't see why God would do it, but that's not a strike against God. Name each cell in a way my friends can actually use in conversation, or mark it unnamed if nobody really defends it. If some combination of answers doesn't land anywhere — like someone who says hiddenness is pure cost and also that it's nobody's fault — flag it and tell me whether it's unoccupied because it's incoherent, because it's unattractive, or just because nobody's bothered to defend it. And if two of the outcomes turn out to be ones the quiz can't separate no matter how I word the questions, say so instead of pretending they're distinct. American English please, and don't worry about citing anyone, I just want the instrument.
