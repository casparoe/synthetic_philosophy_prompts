ok so I'm supposed to write something on precommitment for a seminar next week and before I start I want to know whether my views actually hang together, because each one feels fine on its own and I suspect they don't as a set.

(1) Entrenched constitutional rights that ordinary majorities can't repeal are legitimate. Not rule by the dead over the living, or if it is, that isn't automatically an objection. (2) But democratic legitimacy requires that the people who are alive now can author their own laws -- you can't bind a future demos. (3) Individual precommitment is legitimate and it binds: the guy who locks the booze in a safe and mails himself the key, the advance directive refusing treatment, the escrow account that pays out if I smoke. It binds even when the later self is screaming to get out -- I think a demented patient's later protest shouldn't override the directive they signed while competent, and that they seem content now doesn't settle it either. (4) What makes all of that legitimate is that it's self-imposed: the state isn't overriding anyone's autonomy, it's just enforcing an instruction the agent gave back when they were in charge. (5) I don't beleive in a "true self" or a real will hiding behind the momentary desires. Frankfurt's hierarchy of higher-order desires seems to me to do no actual work, and I don't see why the earlier self gets to be the authoritative one just because it's earlier. (6) I also think enforcement matters -- a precommitment nobody can enforce is just a wish.

So: which of these pull against each other, how badly, and which ones are only in tension because I phrased them badly? Rank by severity, give me the cheapest fix for each and what it costs me, and tell me if there's one joint that's carrying more weight than the rest. No reading list, just the audit.
