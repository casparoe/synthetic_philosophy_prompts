Two of my friends, Maya and Dev, had a long fight in our group chat over Benatar's asymmetry — you know, the four claims where the absence of pain is good even though there's nobody around to enjoy it, but the absence of pleasure only counts as bad if there's somebody deprived by it. I got nominated referee, tried, and failed. I've been reading LessWrong-ish blogs for a decade so my instinct is always to hunt for the crux, and here I honestly couldn't tell whether they were disagreeing or just talking past each other. Can you adjuducate it move by move — who's right at each step, not just who wins overall?

(1) Maya: the asymmetry is rigged. Same structure on both sides, opposite verdicts, and the only thing picking out the difference is that Benatar needs it to reach antinatalism. That's a stipulation in a lab coat.

(2) Dev: it's not a stipulation, because the asymmetry keeps showing up independently in our ordinary attitudes. Nobody thinks there's a duty to make happy people, but everyone thinks there's a duty not to make miserable ones. Nobody mourns the happy children they never had the way they mourn a child they made who suffered. Nobody's sad about the happy people who never existed on some empty island. Five asymmetries all pointing the same way is evidence he's tracking something real rather than inventing it.

(3) Maya: that just relocates the mystery. All of those attitudes have a simpler explanation — we don't count people who don't exist as having interests at all. No interests, no deprivation, no claim. Same reason the empty island isn't a tragedy. And if that's the explanation, then the asymmetry is a fact about the grammar of harm, not about the value of existing, which means it can't do the work Benatar wants — because the very same fact makes it true that coming into existence isn't a harm either. Nothing can happen to nobody, in either direction. She says he wants it both ways: absence of pain is a good with no one to receive it, absence of pleasure isn't a bad for lack of a receiver. Pick one rule.

(4) Dev: then you owe me an account on which bringing someone into a life of nothing but agony isn't a harm to them, and "there was nobody to harm" isn't an account, it's the thing under dispute. Benatar isn't claiming the unborn are worse off than they'd otherwise be; he's claiming that once you exist, having been brought into existence was bad for you, and you can say that without pretending the non-existent are sitting in a grey room being deprived. Also Maya's move proves too much: if no subject means no harm, there's nothing wrong with manufacturing a person guaranteed to suffer, which almost nobody believes.

They went three more rounds and mostly repeated themselves. Please rate all four moves from clearly wrong to clearly right, say which one is doing the real work, and tell me what the actual crux is — is this a substantive disagreement about harm, or a verbal dispute about whether harm needs a subject? Two things I couldn't sort out myself: (a) which of the four moves would Benatar's defenders themselves concede is a real problem, and which would they wave off; and (b) whether the "nobody consents to being born" line is a seperate argument or just a restatement of the asymmetry — Maya kept switching into it, Dev said it was a non sequitur, and I couldn't tell who was being slippery.
