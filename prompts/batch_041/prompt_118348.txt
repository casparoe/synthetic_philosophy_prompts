I've been going down a rabbit hole on the fall of empires lately, partly doomscrolling, partly because I keep having to make ten-year plans at work and honestly have no idea what the world looks like in 2035. A friend of mine says it's not complicated: empires are like organisms. They're born, they grow, they harden, they get sclerotic, they die. He thinks they have a rough shelf life of a couple hundred years and that we're past ours. And when I push back he says the trick is to stop thinking about this particular empire and think like an actuary with a life table — you don't need to know anything about the individual, you just need the base rate for the reference class, and the base rate says we're old.

How far does that actually hold? Give me the correspondence table — what's the birth, the growth, the aging, the death, what plays the role of the lifespan and of the life table — then tell me where the mapping breaks. And be straight with me about whether the conclusion he's using it for survives once the breaks are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the whole argument. If there's a better way to frame the outside view on this, I'd like to hear it.
