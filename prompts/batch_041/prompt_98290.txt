He says it's just the sorites paradox with a fancy hat on: nobody ever claimed there was a sharp line between a heap and a non-heap, so the fact that you can't point to the exact grain that makes the difference shows nothing, and in the same way you're allowed to just refuse the chain of 'better than's without ever naming the step where it flips. He also keeps saying 'better than' is transitive the same way 'taller than' is — if A is better than B and B is better than C then A is better than C, full stop — so any argument that seems to force an intransitivity has to be cheating somewhere.

I half believe him, but I want the honest audit instead of the slogan. Give me the correspondence table: what maps onto the grains, the heap, the sharp cutoff, and the transitivity in his picture, and what each side is actually committed to. Then tell me where the sorites analogy really breaks — is 'refuse the chain' the same move in both cases, or is the value case doing something the heap case can't? — and where the 'taller than' analogy breaks. And put the transitivity claim under the knife: if betterness is more like being the better chess player, where you can beat the guy who beats the guy who beats you, than like being taller, what happens to the conclusion he's trying to draw? I dont want a who-said-what survey, just the mapping, the breakages, and whether his 'its just sorites' move actually earns him transitivity or quietly assumes it.
