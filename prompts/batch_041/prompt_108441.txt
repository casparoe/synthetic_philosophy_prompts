I'm pretty sure that doesn't settle anything but I couldn't say why on the spot.

Can you lay out all the distinct precise things "he was right, just early" could actually be claiming? I want it as a menu I can bring back to him, not one reading explained at length. For each one say whether its trivial, substantive and defensible, or just false, and crucially which of them would actually rescue the prediction from having been falsified versus which are just a way of never letting it be falsified at all.

Some of the readings I suspect are in the pile and want seperated: the timing was off but the mechanism was real; the trend is right even if the schedule slipped; it was only ever a conditional/tendency claim about a world without technological change; the prediction failed but the auxiliary assumptions are the thing that got falsified, not the core; and the ugly one where "early" is doing no work because any failed prediction can be called early. Tell me which is which, which is what people usually mean, and whether theres a reading under which the slogan is just empty. Also whether "early" can even be a coherent claim about a prediction whose whole content was a deadline.
