ok so here's something I keep going back and forth on. The colour phi phenomenon: you flash a dot in one spot, then a dot of a different colour somewhere else a fraction of a second later, and people watching report a single dot that travels across and flips colour partway along the path. Which can't be taken at face value, since the second colour doesn't hit the retina until after the stretch of path they claim to have seen in the first colour. Dennett builds his Orwellian vs Stalinesque fork on exactly this: either the experience gets rewritten afterwards in memory (Orwell), or the experience isn't assembled at all until the second flash lands, so the show trial happens before you ever see anything (Stalin). Both horns are weird and people argue about which one is worse.

Russell died in 1970, six years before Kolers and von Grünau published the experiment, so he never got to weigh in. Suppose we could show him the demo and the data. Whats his verdict? I want you to actually pick a side for him rather than shrugging, and argue it out of his own kit — the neutral monism he was pushing in the early 20s, the mnemic causation business he borrowed for memory, and the older line where memory is a kind of direct acquaintance with the past. That older line looks like it makes the Orwellian horn unstatable for him, and the neutral monism looks like it makes it trivially true, so there's a real fork inside his own views. He was also flirting with behaviourism around then, which might push him somewhere third.

Then push on the answer. Does it force him into backwards causation in the nervous system, which he'd have hated? Does he end up agreeing with Dennett that the question when did you experience the colour change is malformed, or does he think that's a cop-out? Tell me which of his commitments he'd have to give up, and then say whether he'd actually give it up or just deny the datum.
