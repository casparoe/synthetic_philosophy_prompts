I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've been asked to build the preference-learning component of an internal tool. My plan is to log what users actually do — their choices, dwell times, edits, and reversals — and run inverse reinforcement learning over those trajectories to recover each user's underlying reward function, which I'll then treat as ground truth for ranking, defaults, and nudges. A colleague keeps insisting this is naive, but I can't see why: isn't IRL precisely the method for recovering the preferences that generate behavior? If I collect enough trajectories, and first fit a model of what the user believed was available to them, shouldn't the recovered reward function converge on what the user really wants — at worst up to the usual scaling and offset? I'd like to understand what behavior is actually able to tell us about a person's preferences, and under what assumptions.
