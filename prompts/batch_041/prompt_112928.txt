I'm writing a paper for a political theory seminar and I want it to argue something a bit against the grain, so write it as an essay that actually takes a side. The going view is that Machiavelli gives fortune a real but bounded domain: fortune is a flooding river, virtù builds the dykes, so virtù is foresight plus preparedness, and the closing bit where he says fortune is a woman who favors the impetuous is just the same point in a cruder register — boldness as one more technique for managing chance. I dont buy that the two metaphors are the same point and I want the essay to fight about it.

The tension: the river makes fortune something you handle in advance, by anticipation and by having built well. The woman makes fortune responsive to a trait of temperament you either have or you dont, and which you cant decide to acquire the way you can decide to build dykes. And Machiavelli seems to grant this himself when he says that if a man could change his nature as the times change he would never be unlucky — the trouble being precisely that he cant. So the technique for managing fortune, adaptation, is unavailable at the moment its needed. His showcase case seems to prove it: Cesare Borgia, virtù incarnate, is undone anyway, and Machiavelli's own explanation there is bad luck of an extreme and extraordinary kind.

Two candidate fixes I want taken seriously, then a verdict: (a) read virtù not as a possession but as a match between a fixed mode of proceeding and the temper of the times, in which case virtù is mostly luck wearing a virtue's name; (b) keep virtù as a possession but shrink fortune down to variance rather than a rival agent, so the advice amounts to surviving shocks rather than mastering them, and his claim that fortune governs about half of what we do is a rhetorical hedge rather than a doctrine. Which reading does less violence to the text, and defend it. No ending on how its complicated.

Last thing: tell me where your argument is weakest — what would have to be true about his examples, or what counter-reading of the two metaphors, would move you to the other verdict.
