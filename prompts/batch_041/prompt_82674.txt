Bit of an odd one, but here goes. I spent thirty-odd years as an analytical chemist before I retired, and I've only lately started reading philosophy properly, and Hume on miracles has me goign round in circles. I want a straight number plus the working, not a survey of the literature.

Set it up like this. Imagine a big pre-registered, multi-site, double-blind trial of petitionary prayer — say twenty thousand cardiac patients, half of them prayed for by strangers who never meet them — and the prayed-for group comes out ahead on the primary outcome at p = 0.001, with a second site replicating it. Nothing about the protocol looks fishy, the statisticians are indepedent, and the result survives every correction anyone throws at it. So: what's your credence that what happened is a genuine violation of the ordinary course of nature, a miracle in the sense that actually matters, rather than chance, some unnoticed bias, or a natural mechanism we don't yet understand? Mine's about 0.15. A friend of mine, a devout chap and no fool, says 0.7.

Don't just hand me the number. Break it down — how much rides on the base rate for miracles at all, on what we should expect the data to look like given a real divine intervention versus given a subtle flaw in a very large trial, on how far a single replicated trial can really move a prior that low, and on whether 'violation of a natural law' is even the right description once the effect is well attested. Give each a rough probability, say how they combine, and tell me which term your answer is most sensitive to and what would move it most. And while you're at it — does Hume's argument even bite here, or is it aimed at testimony in particular and not at a well-run experiment? Please answer in British English.
