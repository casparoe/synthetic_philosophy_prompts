I'm a novelist, and I was at a residency dinner last week when two friends of mine got into it, and I've been chewing on it ever since — partly because the book I'm writing is about a writer who dies just before his last novel comes out, so it isnt exactly academic for me.

It started because I said it was a shame our old teacher didnt live to see his book win the prize. Then Dee (a hospice nurse, reads a lot of philosophy on the side) and Marco (a poet, lapsed Catholic, no patience for clever arguments) went at it.

(1) Dee said the shame was mine, not his. Death can't be bad for the person who dies: while you're alive you aren't dead yet, and while you're dead you aren't there to be harmed. A bad thing has to happen to somebody, and there's no somebody left.

(2) Marco said that's word games. If I die at 50 instead of 85 I've lost thirty-five years, and that's the worst thing that can happen to a person even if I never feel the loss. Someone could embezzle my savings and I'd never find out — I'd still be worse off. Being deprived of something is a harm whether or not anyone's around to notice.

(3) Dee said fine, but deprived compared to what? There's no fact of the matter about how long I was supposed to live. If the benchmark is just a longer life, then it's bad for me that I wasn't born three hundred years from now when the medicine's better, and bad for me that I'm not somebody else with better genes, and nobody believes that. She also trotted out the old line that you don't lie awake grieving the eternity before you were born, so why the eternity after. Then she added one I hadn't heard before: if you think the me at 85 is basically a different person anyway — different memories, different everything — then it's not clear the guy who'd have lived those extra years is me enough for it to count as my loss.

(4) Marco said the symmetry is bogus, because before you're born there's no one who was interrupted. And he said the real test is reputation: a writer's name can be ruined after he dies, his letters published, his plagiarism exposed, and we all talk as if that harms him and not just his family. So we already believe people can be harmed when they're not around to feel it.

I couldn't referee. Can you go through it move by move — who's right about what, where each of them is overreaching, what they're both missing, and who has the better of it overall? And tell me how confident you are in each verdict, and which point you're least sure about.
