ok so I'm running a reading group on the problem of universals next term and I want to hand out a proper desiderata sheet instead of the usual 'one over many, therefore realism' thing everyone already knows. I dont want the standard three-item list. I want the full spec: what any adequate theory of properties/universals has to deliver, each requirement tied to the case or consideration that forces it, and then your call on which requirements are negotiable and which arent.

Things I want on the list (add whatever I'm missing, but these are the ones I care about):

- impure / haecceitistic properties: being identical to a particular, being a mile from the Eiffel Tower, being such that Sally is taller than you. Any theory that only handles 'pure' universals has to say something about these, and they look like properties in every syntactic and inferential way.
- the determinable/determinate family structure: scarlet is a way of being red, and something can be red and scarlet at once but not red and blue all at once, which a mere class of red things can't obviously reproduce.
- Boolean structure: properties seem closed under conjunction, negation, disjunction, but sparse-property people deny most of those are properties, so say what the theory is supposed to do here.
- second-order properties and quantification over properties, since the theory is meant to underwrite talk about being a colour or being a fundamental property.
- relations, not just monadic properties -- and specifically the problem that a relation's instances seem to need to be 'together' in a way the theory has to explain.
- Bradley-style regress worries about instantiation and compresence, and whether the theory needs a primitive that just relabels the problem.
- empty predicates: there could be a predicate with no instances, and the theory has to say whether it expresses a property.
- the sparse/abundant divide and where the theory is allowed to draw it -- I think this one kills more views than any other, so be honest about it.
- Cambridge properties, since if those count then almost anything goes and parsimony is dead.

Then the part I really want: which of these can't be had together? I suspect determinable/determinate + full Boolean closure + sparse properties is jointly unsatisfiable, but tell me if I'm wrong, and which other pairs break. Also flag any requirement I've smuggled in from one camp that a rival would just reject -- I'd rather know it's a prejudice of one side than have it quietly doing the excluding work.

Then briefly score the usual suspects against the list -- Armstrong-style immanent universals, transcendent/Platonic ones, trope theory with primitive resemblance, class nominalism, resemblance nominalism, structural universals -- a line or two each on what they get right and which requirement they most clearly fail. Keep the scoring short, the desiderata are the deliverable. Purpose, in case it matters: I want a theory good enough to do two jobs at once, grounding a Humean best-system account of laws and supporting reference magnetism in metasemantics, so if those two pull the desiderata in different directions, say so.
