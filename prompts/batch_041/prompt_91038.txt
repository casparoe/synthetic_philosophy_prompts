I am preparing a position statement for a small workshop on frontier AI governance and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is where I have landed.

1. The alignment tax is real and, in the current regime, significant: holding capability fixed, robust safety measures such as interpretability, oversight, red-teaming, and staged deployment raise costs, slow iteration, or degrade benchmark performance.
2. The competitiveness constraint is binding. In a market with several capable actors and no enforceable global cartel, any lab that unilaterally pays a significant alignment tax will tend to lose talent, capital, and influence to labs that do not, and will therefore have less say over the eventual outcome.
3. Because the constraint is binding, the only durable safety measures are those that are capability-neutral or capability-enhancing. Measures that require a permanent sacrifice of capability will be selected against.
4. Alignment is technically solvable, and a solved alignment method would be cheap. Once we know how to build systems that reliably pursue intended goals, the marginal cost of safety would be low.
5. So the central problem is the transition: we need to survive the period before alignment is solved, when the tax is high.
6. During that transition, unilateral restraint by one lab is not only permissible but morally required, even if it cedes advantage to less cautious actors.
7. I also hold that the competitiveness constraint is never an excuse for inaction. If a safety measure is right, we should take it regardless of what competitors do.
8. Finally, I think open-sourcing frontier model weights is pro tanto bad because it irreversibly lowers the cost of misuse, and I think concentrated control of frontier models by a few labs is pro tanto bad because it concentrates power.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly way to restore consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would cost, and then identify the single revision that would clean up the most. I do not need citations; I need the audit.
