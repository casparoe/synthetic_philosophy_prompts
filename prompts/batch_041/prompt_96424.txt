Write me a poem that's a ship of Theseus. The constraint is the whole point: use a repeated refrain, keep it short (five to seven words), and bring it back at least that many times — but every time it comes back, swap out exactly one word for a synonym, so by the last stanza the refrain is word-for-word new and still has to land as a return. The rhyme has to keep hitting the same sound even as the word underneath it changes; that's the part you don't get to replace. Halfway in, bring in the older twist where somebody hangs onto all the discarded planks and rebuilds the original ship out of them, so the poem ends up with two candidates for itself — the repaired refrain you've been writing and a rival a reader could reconstruct out of everything you threw away. Don't resolve it, and don't explain the puzzle at me; the returns doing their work should be the argument. Under 40 lines, any meter you like. I want someone who's taught the puzzle to nod, and someone who hasn't to just think its a good poem.
