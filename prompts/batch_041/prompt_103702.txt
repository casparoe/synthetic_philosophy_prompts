I'm teaching Borges's Funes the Memorious in my Wittgenstein seminar this term and I want to use it as a test case rather than just a fun story about memory. Funes can't form general concepts — every particular is its own thing to him — and his numbering system, where each number gets its own private name, looks tailor-made to embarrass the private language argument. So read the story hard against the later Wittgenstein: is Funes the reductio of the idea that a concept needs an essence, with family resemblance as the way out, or does Borges actually show us something Wittgenstein misses, namely that someone could have a rich inner language-game of their own that nobody else can join? I keep going back and forth alot with a colleague who insists Funes is basically an overfit model — all memory, no generalization — and I'd like you to say whether that's a good way of putting it. Show me where the story illustrates the language-game and rule-following ideas, where it strains them, and end with what the story adds that the Investigations doesn't.
