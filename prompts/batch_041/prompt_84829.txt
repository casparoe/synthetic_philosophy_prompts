I'm writing the final paper for a masters module on the philosophy of set theory, and I want one question on Skolem's paradox that cant be answered by reciting the line about the model being countable from outside and uncountable from inside — they all trot that out and then flounder as soon as you push on it. What I want is a question that makes them adjudicate two readings, so I've written two short replies below (invented, but the sort of thing students hand in), and the question should ask them to say which is closer to right and exactly where each one goes wrong:

Reply 1: "The paradox isn't really about sets at all. Löwenheim–Skolem gives us a model whose membership relation isn't the real one, so the 'uncountable' set in it isn't one of our sets, and Cantor's theorem is left untouched."

Reply 2: "The paradox shows that countability is purely relative. Whether a set is countable depends on which model you're in, so there's no fact of the matter, and Cantor's theorem doesn't establish that there are more reals than naturals."

So the question should get them to say what actually dissolves the paradox and where exactly the missing bijection goes, what each reply gets right and wrong, and whether the paradox gives any real support to relativism about set-theoretic notions — plus, for a couple of extra marks at the end, whether formalising ZF in second-order logic with full semantics helps, and why or why not.

Give me the question as it would appear on the paper, with the two replies quoted in it, then a model answer of about 600 words, then a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves a top answer has to make and, for each one, a line on how candidates typically get it wrong. It's a 90-minute closed-book paper, so nothing that depends on them remembering names or dates.
