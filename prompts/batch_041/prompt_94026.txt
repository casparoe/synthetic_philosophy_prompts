I am a doctoral candidate in archaeology assembling a comparative database of "collapse" episodes, and I want to fix the coding scheme before I commit to it. My colleagues divide over whether the term names a real process or an observer's verdict, so I am treating that dispute as the ranking criterion rather than trying to resolve it in advance. Below are five candidate coding rules for the variable "societal collapse," each of which would classify a case as a collapse rather than a mere transformation. Please rank them from the most to the least defensible as a criterion, judged specifically by how well each keeps "collapse" a descriptive category rather than a retrospective value judgment, and give each a one- or two-sentence justification that names the specific inferential risk it does or does not carry. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, ordered from most to least defensible, with no introduction or conclusion.

1. A case is coded as a collapse when the maximum population of the affected region falls by at least half within two centuries of onset.
2. A case is coded as a collapse when the central political authority that previously coordinated the region ceases to function and is not replaced at a comparable scale.
3. A case is coded as a collapse when monumental construction and long-distance trade networks are abandoned, whatever happens to the population.
4. A case is coded as a collapse when the material standard of living, as indexed by stature, diet, and household goods, declines sharply and durably.
5. A case is coded as a collapse when the people who inherit the region's later historical record describe their predecessors as having fallen from a former greatness.
