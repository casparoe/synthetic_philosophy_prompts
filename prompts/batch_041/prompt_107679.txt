I am a doctoral student in philosophy, and I have been asked to help a neighborhood time-bank resolve a dispute about its exchange rate. The founders set one hour of anyone's time equal to one credit. Now members who offer specialized services—legal advice, tutoring, physical therapy—are drifting away, saying their hours are worth more, while others insist that differential rates would turn the community into a market and betray its purpose. The credits can be spent only within the network; they cannot be converted to cash. I want to advise them on whether there is a principled, non-arbitrary way to compare different kinds of labor, or whether every exchange ratio is ultimately a convention. I am not looking for citations or reading lists—I need the philosophical structure of the disagreement and a defensible way to think about what the community should do.
