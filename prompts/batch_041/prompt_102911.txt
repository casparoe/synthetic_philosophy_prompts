I'm a journalist — science and ideas, mostly — and before that I spent the best part of a decade on the courts beat, so I know my way round the vocabulary of sentencing and penology: aggravating and mitigating factors, the totality principle, parity of sentencing, determinate vs indeterminate sentences, licence conditions, parole, the tariff, collateral consequences, restorative justice conferences, deterrence vs desert vs rehabilitation vs incapacitation, and all the rest of it. I've been asked to write a long peice on cancel culture and deplatforming, and I keep reaching for that vocabulary, which makes me suspicious that I'm forcing it.

So: restate the whole arguement over cancel culture and deplatforming as a form of punishment entirely in the language I already use for crime and sentencing. Take the main positions as they're actually put — that it's disproportionate, that it's a modern mob or a digital pillory, that it's just the market and free association doing what markets and free association do, that it's the only sanction available when the law won't touch you, that it's the powerless finally getting to hold the powerful to account — and translate each one into penology terms as faithfully as you can, without quietly importing the criminal law's assumptions and calling that a translation.

Then the bit I actually want: the residue report. What in this debate has no counterpart in the criminal-justice frame at all? Off the top of my head: a sentence with no end date and no appellate court; prosecutor, judge and jury all being the same crowd; a rule that wasn't a rule when it was broken; character being punished rather than acts. Also, two colleagues have been nagging me with rival framings — one reckons the nearest analogue isn't a sentence at all but being struck off, a doctor losing their licence, since what's withdrawn is permission to operate in a public space rather than liberty itself; the other says it's just a boycott, i.e. shopping. Tell me whether either survives the translation better than the sentencing frame does.

Finally, seperate the three kinds of leftover: gaps in my vocabulary, places where I'm misusing it, and places where the original disagreement was vague all along and only looked sharp because nobody had to write the sentence down. I don't want a reading list — just the translation and the leftovers.
