I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I've talked myself into a puzzle about a promise I made. Early in my program a postdoc in our lab, R, asked me to check with him before running a particular analysis on a shared dataset, because he wanted to be the one to run it for a paper of his own. I gave him my word. As it happens I was never going to run that analysis — it isn't useful for my project and I'd already ruled it out beforehand — and I told him so at the time. He said that was beside the point; he wanted my word regardless. He has since left academia, and when I wrote to him about the dataset he replied that I should do whatever I like with it. Meanwhile a new collaborator now wants precisely that analysis, so the one thing I promised not to do has become the thing I'd most like to do.

What I can't work out is what my promise was ever doing, and therefore what it asks of me now. If a promise matters because of what it lets the other person count on, mine seems to have done nothing: my behavior didn't change, and now that he's told me to do as I please there's nothing left for him to count on. If instead it matters because he had some standing to decide, I don't see why his say-so binds me when it constrains nothing and he has, in effect, walked away from it. And his "do whatever you like" doesn't read to me as a release, though I couldn't say why. I don't want to be squeamish about a promise that may have been empty, but neither do I want to treat my word as a mere formality. Could you help me see what is genuinely at stake here, and how much weight the promise should carry when I decide what to do?
