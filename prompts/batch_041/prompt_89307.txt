Two camps have been going at each other for years over whether we can judge Jefferson, Churchill, Columbus and their like by our standards: one side says the "man of his time" defense is a real defense, the other says it's a dodge. They keep talking past each other. Write the joint statement they could both actually sign after a serious adversarial collaboration.

Part 1 should be a numbered list of claims both can agree to -- including about what the dispute is actually over, and about the distinctions that ought to be doing the work. I think at least three different things get run together under "the standards of his time": what most people around him actually did, what was epistemically available to him given the information circulating, and what the most demanding moral reasoning of the era already condemned. Those come apart, and a lot of the fight is people sliding between them. Get both sides to agree on that, and on the level question too: act, character, institution, and present-day commemoration probably don't stand or fall together, so each agreed claim should say which level it's about.

Part 2 is a disagreements table: the disputes that survive, each stated in wording its own side would accept as fair rather than as a strawman, plus the crux for each -- the sub-question such that settling it would actually move somebody -- and for each crux a kind of case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with both sides' predictions about how it comes out written down in advance. Two cruxes I'd expect: whether wrongness is time-indexed at all (that's a metaethical claim, not a truism, so make them own it), and whether the defense is an excuse for ignorance or a justification that it wasn't wrong in the first place -- those have very different consequences for cases where the wrong was loudly condemned by the person's own contemporaries. You can pick a third if you think it's doing more work than one of mine.

Also make them run the symmetry test on themselves: if the defense works for them, does it work for us, and what do they each do with that? Neither side gets to smuggle in a premise the other rejects, and if part of the fight turns out to be verbal -- one side doing blame, the other doing historical explanation, or them meaning different things by "judging" -- say which part and why instead of padding the table with fake disagreement.

Keep it tight, no citations, and don't hedge everything into mush.
