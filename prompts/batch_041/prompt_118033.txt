I am a high school student writing a short story for my school's philosophy club. The story should explore the ethical implications of panpsychism, especially the idea that suffering might be a feature of fundamental physics rather than only of complex organisms. To make the stakes concrete, I have devised a fictional view I call valenced field theory: every local excitation of a quantum field has a primitive hedonic tone, so the total suffering in a region of spacetime is the integral of negative valence over that region. Write a short story about a graduate student who realizes that a planned high-energy experiment would, on this view, produce a vast amount of diffuse, low-grade suffering in otherwise empty space. The story should center on the ethical dilemma of whether such suffering matters when it belongs to no unified subject and leaves no memory, and on how the student reasons about it. Please write literary fiction rather than an essay, and let the philosophical issues emerge through the narrative.
