Ok so I'm in this reading group on the science of sex differences and we keep going around in circles on the gender-equality paradox — the pattern where the more a country scores on measures of gender equality (the Nordics, basically), the bigger the gap between men and women in stuff like how many end up in STEM, or how they answer interest questions along the things-versus-people axis. Half the group treats it as basically decisive: strip away the economic pressure and the underlying biological preferences show up. The other half thinks that's a textbook ecological fallacy and the whole thing is an artifact of how you index egalitarianism or which fields get counted as STEM. I have to present next week and I don't want to just summarize both sides, I want to actually commit to a number and defend it, so I could use an outside opinion.

Here's the claim, as precisely as I can state it: "The cross-national gender-equality paradox is better explained by average biological/innate sex differences in interests and preferences than by cultural or institutional explanations." I want your credence in that, but honestly the number matters less to me than the decomposition, so give me the sub-questions you think it hinges on and a rough probability for each, then how they combine. Off the top of my head I'd want it broken into at least: (a) whether the phenomenon even replicates and survives the measurement objections — how egalitarianism gets indexed, WEIRD-ish sampling, what counts as STEM, whether we're looking at stated preferences or actual degrees; (b) assuming it's real, how much weight the free-expression-of-dispositions story carries versus the cultural ones (stereotype salience, labor-market structure, work-life balance, self-selection driven by anticipated discrimination); (c) how much of (b) is even answerable, given that calling a population-level sex difference in interests innate is doing a lot of contested work; and (d) I want to keep separate whether the answer to the truth question has any bearing on what follows for justice or representation, because I suspect people are smuggling that in.

Is that a coherent way to carve it, and does it change any of your sub-questions?
