Ok this is going to be a long one, sorry in advance. I'm a backend engineer, never took a philosophy class in my life, and I don't read much outside of work stuff. But a coworker sent me a podcast about the Zhuangzi butterfly dream and now I can't stop thinking about it. I read the story itself in some translation I found online, so don't hold me to the exact wording.

As I understand it: Zhuangzi dreams he's a butterfly, flying around, totally happy, no idea he's a person. Then he wakes up and says he can't tell whether he was Zhou dreaming he was a butterfly, or whether he's a butterfly right now dreaming he's Zhou. And then — this is the part that gets me — he doesn't say so nothing is real, everything's a dream. He says there must be some difference between Zhou and the butterfly, and he gives that whole business a name.

So my buddy and I built this argument out of it in our group chat, and I can't find the exit.

(1) There's no test you can run from inside your present experience to tell whether that experience is a dream. Whatever you check — the clock, the light switch, pinching yourself — a dream could contain that too.
(2) So I can't know right now that I'm awake. Fine, I'll take that, it seems obviously right.
(3) Same deal for Zhuangzi. Neither Zhou nor the butterfly can settle it from where each one is sitting.
(4) And they don't just both fail the test, they fail it in exactly the same way. Whatever reason Zhou has for thinking he's the real one, the butterfly has the identical reason from its side. Neither has anything the other lacks.
(5) Two options with the same evidence, where no possible evidence could ever seperate them, aren't two competing theories at all. They're two ways of describing one thing, like half empty vs half full.
(6) So there's no fact of the matter about which one is true. And the same move works on me: that I'm a software engineer in Cleveland and that I'm a butterfly dreaming I'm a software engineer in Cleveland aren't rivals. Nothing here is more true than anything else.
(7) That's too much. Zhuangzi himself ends on a real difference between Zhou and the butterfly, not on two descriptions of one thing.

One guy in the chat says the whole thing is confused from the start, because a dream is by definition what you wake up out of — you can only ever say that something was a dream, never that this is one. So there was never a contest between being awake and dreaming, and the argument in (3) and (4) is weighing two things that don't belong on the same scale. My buddy says the argument is fine and the answer is just that Zhuangzi is inconsistent at the end. A third guy says the whole setup is rigged, because the story is told by the guy who's awake and the butterfly never gets a turn, so the symmetry in (4) is being asserted from inside one of the two sides.

I'd hate to give up (1), it really does seem undeniable to me, and I'd hate to give up (7) too, because then I'm just saying the old man fumbled the ending. So which step do I actually drop, why did it look fine while I was writing it, and what does dropping it cost me? And tell me straight whether the first guy's line is a real answer or just a way of winning by definition, and whether the third guy's point about who's telling the story is doing anything. Also, if it matters, I notice the story's doubt is about which of two dreamings was the dream, not about whether I'm dreaming this second, and I can't tell if my argument is quietly sliding between those two.
