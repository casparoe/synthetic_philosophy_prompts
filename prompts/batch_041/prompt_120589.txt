Spent most of last night having a long arguement with my friend Nadia (she's finishing a PhD in formal epistemology) about logical omniscience, and she ended it by saying my whole position was "awareness logic with extra steps," which stung because I really don't think it is. Anyway I have to present it Thursday and I'd like to not get shredded.

What I'm defending: that logical omniscience isn't a problem about belief, it's a problem about bookkeeping. The closure requirement only bites if you assume an agent's beliefs are a set of sentences closed under consequence. Drop that and say what an agent believes is just what she's disposed to use as a premise, plus the inferences she's disposed to actually draw, and the "problem" evaporates -- there's nothing left for her to be non-omniscient about. I want to push it further and claim that once you do this, the idealized logically omniscient agent isn't even a coherent fiction; there's no fact of the matter about what she "really" believes over and above her dispositions. Nadia thinks that last move is doing all the work and is either trivial or false depending on how you read "disposed."

The room: mostly the logic and epistemology reading group -- they all know the standard escapes (impossible worlds, awareness, quietly weakening the closure axioms) and they're allergic to anything that smells like relabeling. Plus two cognitive scientists from the reasoning lab, who will want to know what this predicts and whether "disposed to use" is measurable at all, and one Bayesian who I'm fairly sure will say I've just given up on coherence.

Can you write me the Q&A prep sheet? The objections this actual room will raise, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each in the words they'd use, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft -- and flag the one I'm praying nobody asks.
