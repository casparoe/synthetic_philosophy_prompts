I keep flip-flopping about when someone counts as irrational across time, so let me just dump my gut verdicts and ask what principle is behind them. A guy with a strict preference loop over three paintings -- likes A over B, B over C, and C over A -- who lives alone on an island and is never offered a single trade: I still want to say something is wrong with him, that the loop itself is the defect and getting money-pumped is only the symptom. A guy whose tastes just drift -- coffee over tea in the morning, tea over coffee by four, and he'll happily pay a penny a day to have someone swap his cup when the mood flips -- seems completely fine to me, no irrationality anywhere. Someone who signs a contract to be lashed to the mast during the storm and then begs to be untied once the waves get big: irrational, and not merely because his tastes changed, he's failing as an agent. Same contract, but he breaks it because he's learned the storm already passed: totally fine, that's new information. Then the case that breaks me: a guy who sincerely doesn't care about a cent, so a trader walks him from $100 down to $0 a penny at a time, and at each step he shrugs and says sure, doesn't matter to me. Every step is a wash by his own lights, nothing about him changes, and yet I want to call the whole sequence a failure -- but the moment I say that I have to explain why the coffee-and-tea guy isn't one too, since he's also paying a penny a day for nothing anyone else can see. My half-baked principle is that you're irrational iff no single coherent set of preferences could make sense of your choices across your whole life, with a carve-out for changes driven by new information or by things you actually endorse. Test that against the lot, tell me which of my verdicts it can't keep, and say what it predicts for a case I haven't raised -- a man who deliberately keeps his preference loop because he enjoys cycling, and calls the money pump a fair price for the ride. Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up.
