I'm running a discussion section for a second-year phil of language course and next week is conditionals. The textbook hands the students a nice tidy map — indicatives are material conditionals plus a Gricean story about why they sound weird, counterfactuals get the closest-world treatment — and then the section falls apart, because half the class rejects the map and the other half rejects it in a different direction. I want to give them a decision tree instead of another lecture.

Build it from the cases up, not from the names down. Every branching node has to be a question about a specific sentence or pair of sentences, phrased so a student can answer it without knowing which theory their answer points them to. So no nodes like "do you think indicatives have truth conditions?" — thats the thing I want them to discover they've already committed to.

Cases I want in there somewhere:

- The Kennedy pair. You can accept "If Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did" and reject "If Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have". Ask them what they think is going on there.
- The die. A fair die has been rolled, you know nothing else. Take "If it came up even, it came up six." Is the probability of that 1/3, or 2/3, or is the question malformed?
- Antecedent strengthening. "If you strike this match it will light" sounds fine; "If you strike this match and it is soaking wet it will light" doesn't, even though the second antecedent entails the first.
- The disjunction test. Does "Either it didn't rain or the picnic was cancelled" say the same thing as "If it rained, the picnic was cancelled"?
- A future antecedent where nothing is settled either way: "If it rains on Saturday the match will be cancelled", and then the would-version of the same thing. Which side of the line does that pair fall on, and does your tree predict that, or does it fall into a gap?
- Embedding. Is "It is probably true that if the fuse blew, the lights went out" a sentence with a truth value? Can a conditional sit in the antecedent of another conditional? Can you negate one — "It is not the case that if it rained the picnic was cancelled"?

The leaves should be the positions people actually hold, named where there's a standard name — material-conditional-plus-pragmatics, the conditional-probability/Ramsey line, Stalnaker's uniqueness view, Lewis's variably strict one, the unified restrictor/quantifier treatment on which indicatives and counterfactuals are the same kind of quantifier over worlds differing only in modal base and ordering source, and the Dudman-ish line that forward-looking will-conditionals are a different semantic kind from ordinary indicatives — plus any others that fall out of your tree even if they've got no name. Please don't give me a reading list or citations, I just want the instrument.

Two things I really want at the end. First, a list of answer-combinations that no position seems to occupy, with your diagnosis of each: is nobody there because the view is incoherent, because its unattractive, or because its just never been explored? Second, any two leaves your tree can't actually seperate on the cases it contains — say so plainly rather than papering over it with a distinction the students couldn't apply. Aim for 8-10 nodes and 5-7 leaves.
