My old flatmate Nadia is three years into a PhD in decision theory and has become unbearable about Aumann — she thinks the agreement theorem explains everything. She keeps issuing verdicts on disagreement cases and I've been trying to work out what she's actually commited to. So: two traders who share a prior over the market, who each know the other's credence that a deal will go through, and who each know the other to be a careful Bayesian — she says it simply can't be common knowledge between them that they still disagree; one of them must have slipped up somewhere. Two traders who started from different priors, because one trusts the historical base rates and the other thinks they're junk — she says they can disagree till they die and swapping numbers won't help. Two traders who share a prior, each of whom knows the other's credence, and knows that the other knows theirs, and so on for a handful of levels, but where it never quite becomes common knowledge — she says they can stay stuck, because the theorem needs common knowledge and a few rounds of mutual knowledge isn't that. And then a pair who share a prior, where it's fully common knowledge how each of them sees things, who still disagree — but what they disagree about isn't a probability at all, it's what to do: one takes both boxes in a Newcomb-type case, the other takes one. She says that's outside the theorem altogether, it's about credences and not choices, so they can go on disagreeing forever.

Next week's case has two people sharing a prior over everything relevant, each having told the other their credence and knowing the other knows, all the way up, both Bayesians, nothing slipping — and they still disagree, about whether a particular patch of marshy ground counts as a wetland that a new rule covers. One says 0.9, the other 0.2. Will Nadia say they can carry on disagreeing, or that one of them must be getting something wrong? I care much less about the answer than about the rule she's really running: what rules fit her four verdicts, what does each of them predict here, which do you think she's actually on and why, and what further case would seperate them? Don't hedge — say which way you think she'd go, and be honest if her record genuinely underdetermines it.
