I keep having the same argument with colleagues about whether sentience comes in degrees, and I want a diagnosis rather than another round. Camp one: obviously it's graded — an insect has a sliver, a dog a lot more, a thermostat presumably none, and moral status should track the amount. Camp two: category error — a system either has experience or it doesn't, and the only thing that comes in degrees is our confidence about which side of that line a given creature sits on. Camp one says that just assumes the conclusion; camp two says otherwise you're scaling a quantity nobody has ever measured. Nobody budges.

So what kind of disagreement is this? Verbal, about the word 'sentience'? Substantive, about what kind of property phenomenal consciousness is? Or two groups quietly answering different questions under one heading — one metaphysical (is the property itself graded?) and one epistemic (how sure can we ever be about any given creature)? My hunch is that a lot of the heat comes from people sliding between 'probably not sentient' and '0.3% sentient' as if those were one and the same claim, and that pulling them apart either dissolves the fight or relocates it somewhere else. Tell me which it is, and if it relocates, where to.

And the bit nobody will answer straight: even granting sentience is graded, does moral status grade with it, or does moral status have to be all-or-nothing because equal consideration of interests needs everyone inside the club to count the same? Is that a different kind of disagreement from the first one? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, an empirical result, a change of question — or is this one just not going to resolve?
