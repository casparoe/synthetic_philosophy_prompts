Half of us say that's consent, she didn't object; the other half say it can't be, she doesn't get it. What are the most important implications of the real philosophy of what makes consent valid for how we should write this, and please actually take a side on whether consent is fundamentally the patient waiving a right or the patient autonomously authorising something, because I think that's the fork that decides the shrug case and I cant tell which one our policy is already committed to. Related thing we keep hitting: our IRB calls it undue inducement when a homeless patient signs up for a risky trial mainly for the money, and the PI says he's an adult so it's his call — does the same machinery handle that, or is voluntariness a separate can of worms?
