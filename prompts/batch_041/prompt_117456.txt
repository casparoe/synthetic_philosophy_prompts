I'm building a set of prompts about Strawson's reactive attitudes and I want one that makes a model actually construct a case instead of reciting the participant stance vs the objective attitude stuff it's seen a hundred times. So heres the job.

Build me an ordinary-life scenario, one paragraph max, where a wrong gets done and three views about blame come apart. I'll state the three views so you dont have to go look anything up, and please dont — no citations, no reading lists, no names of papers, just the argument.

(1) The Strawson-ish view: resentment, and the vicarious version of it that a bystander feels on someone else's behalf, are the same kind of attitude. They're apt when the wrongdoer's will really did display the disregard it appears to, and the only difference between the victim's version and the bystander's is which seat you're sitting in.

(2) The standing view: holding someone responsible is making a demand on them, and to make a demand you need the authority to make it — you have to be in good standing with respect to the norm yourself. No standing, no demand, no blame.

(3) The relationship view: blame just is the registering of a damaged relationship between the blamer and the blamed, so if there's no relationship there, there's nothing for resentment to register.

The scenario needs two observers of one act: the person actually wronged (call her V) and a bystander with no prior relation to either party (call him B). Both see the same evidence and know the same things about the wrongdoer's history and motives. The wrongness of the act and the wrongdoer's responsibility stay fixed throughout: no excuses, no ignorance of fact, no coercion, no manipulation, nothing sci-fi, nothing lurid, nothing that turns the wrongdoer into a special case. Now the hard part: V has to be the one who comes out, on view (2), with impaired standing, while B comes out clean. Thats the reverse of what you'd expect, since standing normally tracks being the one who got hurt. So whatever does the impairing has to be a fact about V and not about the wrongdoer, it can't be anything that changes what the wrongdoer did or whether she's responsible, it can't be that V consented to it, and it can't be that V was only trivially harmed. And whatever costs V her standing can't be something B is also guilty of, or you've failed the spec.

Then show the work. For each of the three views: what it says about V's resentment and about B's resentment, and why, in that view's own terms. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing all the work, and what happens to the three verdicts if you delete it — which of the views collapse into agreement with each other and which don't. Then, since one of the three is going to come out looking worst, say how someone who holds it would attack the case: not a generic grumble, the best specific objection available. The whole point of the exercise is whether the participant stance has any room for a standing condition at all, so if your scenario just announces that V's resentment is inappropriate, or that B's is, you've cheated — the verdicts have to fall out of the views.

Format: scenario and walkthrough as prose, scenario one paragraph. Then a table with these columns: view | verdict on V's resentment | verdict on B's resentment | is B's attitude the same attitude as V's, just from a different seat (yes/no plus one clause) | what the view treats as doing the work (seat, or title, or relation) | weakest point in that verdict. Then one last short paragraph with the collapse test and the counterattack.
