I've been reading LessWrong-adjacent blogs and forums for about a decade, and I've absorbed a working picture I rarely examine: beliefs are in-the-head states, and getting them right is a personal engineering problem — calibrate, notice your confusion, update on the evidence you yourself can gather. I've also collected a set of externalist theses that each, in a different way, seem to put a thumb on the scale against that picture. Please sort them into tiers by how much a rationalist who accepted the thesis should actually change their epistemic practice. The ranking dimension is how far the thesis moves the locus of epistemic responsibility away from the individual and onto the environment, the community, or the causal-functional process that produced the state.

The theses:

- Environmental externalism: the chemical stuff in your surroundings, not your internal state, fixes what your word for the wet stuff refers to, so a duplicate in a different environment would mean something else with an identical head.
- Social externalism: your linguistic community's usage, not your own partial understanding, fixes what your terms mean, so you can have a belief whose content you cannot fully state.
- Causal-historical externalism: what a name picks out is fixed by the chain of transmission rather than by any description in the speaker's head, so two people with identical internal states could refer to different things.
- Teleosemantic externalism: a state's content is fixed by the biological or designed function it was selected to serve, not by anything the thinker can introspect.
- Collective-cache externalism: an individual's so-called belief is a local cache of a computation that properly belongs to a group, so content is a property of the collective and the individual is at best a mirror.
- Swamp-duplicate externalism: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate with no causal history either has no content at all or the same content, and introspection cannot tell you which.

For each tier, give me one sentence on why it lands there and rank within the tier; then tell me which single thesis, if true, would most change what a rationalist should do on Monday morning, and what that change would be. Don't retreat into it depends on your theory of content — pick a reading of each thesis, commit to it, and rank on that.
