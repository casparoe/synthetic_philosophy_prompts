I'm trying to plan a two-year reading group -- four of us, decent on the history and the puzzles but none of us much good at formal logic -- and I want to spend it on the open questions in the grounding/fundamentality literature that are actually worth the time, not just the ones that get cited alot. Can you rank them by how upstream they are, meaning which answers would settle or dissolve the most of the others? Off the top of my head: is grounding one relation or a whole family of them; is it primitive or analyzable in terms of essence or modality; must grounding be well-founded, i.e. does there have to be a fundamental level; and how does grounding line up with explanation and truthmaking. Plus one I'm not sure anyone's put quite this way: if grounding facts are themselves ungrounded, does that make them fundamental -- and can they even be fundamental, since they mention the non-fundamental facts they relate? If not, is the whole idea of a fundamental level in trouble? For each question, tell me why it's upstream or not, how tractable it is for people who'll be avoiding heavy formalism, and what kind of result in the next year or two would move it up or down my list. And say which one is overrated because settling it wouldn't change anything downstream.
