ok so I'm in a seminar on political responsibility and we've spent like three weeks on the Jaspers thing — the four flavours of guilt (criminal = actual crimes you committed, political = being answerable for what your state did in your name, moral = your own conscience, metaphysical = the survivor's thing about being alive when others weren't) — and whats driving me nuts is I still can't figure out which of those my prof thinks can attach to people who weren't even born yet. So I started keeping a list of her verdicts on cases we threw at her, and now I want you to tell me what she'll say about next week's case.

Her record so far:
1. Ordinary Germans who hated the regime, voted against it, couldn't have stopped anything, and just kept their heads down: no criminal guilt, no moral guilt, but yes political guilt. She was clear they're answerable for it and owe something toward repair.
2. A guy who personally ran a roundup and then spent forty years being genuinely decent and horrified at himself: still criminally and morally guilty. A change of heart doesn't launder anything, she said.
3. Descendants of perpetrators who emigrated, cut all ties to the country, and inherited nothing from it: no guilt of any kind, nothing owed.
4. A foreign volunteer who came in from outside to help with the killing: fully morally and criminally guilty, but zero political guilt. Her line was that political guilt isn't something you earn by acting, it comes from belonging, and he never belonged.
5. A bystander who had the power to stop a deportation and chose not to: morally guilty, not criminally.

Next week's case, and it's fictional so don't @ me about it not being real. Karsland: three generations ago the state confiscated basically all the farmland of an ethnic minority and handed it to majority farmers, and the minority was expelled. Nobody has ever apologised, no compensation was ever paid. Today the current majority generation didn't do any of it, plenty of their grandparents actually hid minority families or protested, and the confiscated land is the backbone of the economy so the majority is doing very well out of it. The descendants of the expelled minority have mostly moved back and are citizens now, and there's also a family of immigrants who arrived in 2010 and now farms confiscated land.

So: what does she say about the guilt of (a) the current majority generation and (b) that immigrant family? And be straight with me — what are the different rules her five verdicts are compatible with, what does each one predict about Karsland, which one should I bet she's actually running, and what case should I put to her to seperate them? I've been going back and fourth on whether the real story is just that she says "guilt" when she sometimes means obligation, so also tell me if that reading changes anything, because if it does I think it changes everything.
