I'm on the "values review" group at a mid-sized AI lab — we sign off on what a model is allowed to have as its default values, and so far we've just been deciding case by case with no written principle. Our record:

- We blocked a fine-tune that would have had the assistant, by default, assert that one religion's claims are true. Most users in that market wanted it; the minority who'd have been told they were wrong cared a lot.
- We let users pick a persona for the assistant in their own sessions — religious, political, whatever — as long as it's off by default for everyone else.
- We refused to let our founder bake his personal ethics into the default model, even though he owns the company.
- We approved defaults that follow local conventions (units, dates, what counts as polite) on the grounds that some default is unavoidable.
- We approved a default that nudges users away from heavy drinking when they seem to be asking for life advice, over the objection of a few users who called it paternalistic.

On the docket: an organized majority of users in one market wants the assistant to steer people, by defualt, toward what they call a good life — marriage, kids, religious observance, steady work — and away from things they find degrading (casual sex, certain body mods, a certain kind of career). They're careful about it: no doctrine, no explicit claims, just warmth toward one way of living and coolness toward the other. The people on the wrong side of that are a small minority there, and the polls say most users would be pleased.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which of the earlier calls is the closest analog? Give me the rule our record seems to license, plus at least one rival rule that fits the record just as well, what each predicts here, which I should go with and why, and the one future case that would force the two apart. Don't just hand me the usual suspects — I want the rule our record actually supports, not the one you'd prefer we had.
