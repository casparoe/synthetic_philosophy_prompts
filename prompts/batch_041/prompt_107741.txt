Sorry if this is a dumb question. I'm not a philosopher, I just do risk/claims stuff for a small logistics company, and this has been bugging me for years and I've never managed to look it up properly.

Here's the shape of it. Someone hands you a number about how likely something is, and the number turns out to depend completely on which pile of similar cases you count. My own car: 8 years old, 140k miles, well maintained. Whats the chance it dies this year? Count all 8-year-old cars. Count all 8-year-old Hondas. Count my exact model and year. Count only cars at my mileage. Count the cars I personally have owned, which is a sample of one, me. Every one of those gives a different number and every one is defendable. Or a friend got a diagnosis and the doctor quoted her a five-year survival figure, and she said "but I'm not the average patient with this," and he said it's the best we have, and she said then it doesn't apply to me. Same shape. Or in arguments online: "most people who do X end up Y," and someone always comes back with "you can't count those people, half of them weren't really doing X."

What bothers me isnt that we're short on data. It's that the choice of pile seems to be doing all the work, and the choice seems to get made by what you already think or what you want to show. Two people with identical information can pick different piles and both be right, and there's no study you could run to settle it, so the fight isn't really about the facts. And if you keep narrowing the pile you eventually get down to just this one case, which isn't a frequency of anything, and if you widen it the pile stops resembling the case at all. So sometimes I think the number isn't really about the world, it's about what we happen to care about. But I might be overreaching there.

Is there a name for this? I know "base rate" and "correlation isn't causation" are in the neighbourhood, but they don't seem like the same thing. If there is an established term, I want the edges of it: what it is, what it isn't, what the nearest concepts are, how each of them differs from what I'm describing, and which one actually fits. And tell me straight whether my last paragraph, the bit about the number being about us rather than about the world, is part of the same idea or a seperate and shakier claim — because I honestly can't tell if I've found one thing or two.
