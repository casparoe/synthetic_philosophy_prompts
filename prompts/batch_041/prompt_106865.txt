The lecturer, two of my students, and a guy in my reading group all read this as saying virtù is basically flood-control engineering: foresight, preparation, infrastructure, done in advance, and then you're fine. On that reading the whole fortune/virtù business is really a story about risk management, and the conclusion they draw is that virtù can in principle neutralise fortune, or at least fence it off. My reading-group friend wrote a seminar paper calling Machiavelli a proto-insurance man, and it's clever enough that I want a real audit before I either steal it or dunk on it. An engineering analogy has no slot for any of that — it can't represent favour, timing, boldness, or the fact that virtù in his sense includes impeto and not just prudence.
