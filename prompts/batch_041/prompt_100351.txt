I am assembling a comparative component for a course I teach on church and state, and I should like your help in grading four well-known settlements against one another. They are: the established Church of England; the German arrangement whereby a church tax is collected by the state from registered members; French laïcité; and American disestablishment. I am interested in a single, deliberately narrow dimension: how well each arrangement serves the position of the unaffiliated — not religious minorities inside a faith, but those who profess no religion and who may wish to be left undisturbed by religious institutions altogether. In particular, I want you to treat each settlement as a set of defaults: what a citizen is enrolled in, or rendered liable for, without doing anything at all, and what it costs them to get out. Rank the four from best to worst on this dimension, assign each a score between 0 and 1, and for each say in a sentence or two which feature of the arrangement does most to fix its score. Then tell me which, if any, of the four you would defend as the most attractive model for a contemporary liberal democracy, and whether the ranking would change materially were the dimension instead the protection of devout religious minorities. Please write your answer in British English.
