Please explain the conceptual issue behind the often-discussed claim that mathematics is unreasonably effective in the natural sciences. Separate three things that are commonly run together: the claim that mathematical structures developed for internal mathematical reasons later turn out to apply to nature; the claim that nature is itself mathematical; and the possibility that the sense of unreasonableness is a selection effect, because we count as mathematics those structures that survive and count as physics those domains that admit mathematical formulation. Make the selection-effect reading precise with a concrete imagined case: a branch of pure mathematics pursued only for its own sake later becomes the natural language of a new physical theory. Say what that case would actually establish and what it would leave untouched. Distinguish the descriptive question of why our successful theories are mathematical from the metaphysical question of why the world should be so. Finally, say what would count as evidence against the selection-effect reading, and why the issue is not settled by observing that mathematics is a human invention.
