i'm in a grad seminar on tight vs loose cultures — you know, the whole how-much-rule-following-should-a-society-demand thing — and i got stuck having to open our last session with a poem, so i'm hoping you can write it.

what i want is a pantoum (the one where the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza come back as the 1st and 3rd of the next, and the closing stanza loops back to the opening so it circles shut). 5 stanzas, quatrains, whatever. i want that form specifically because i need lines to get *recycled* so the same line means something different the second time it surfaces — a line that reads as order or as care on its first appearance should read as suffocation when it comes back, and a line that reads as freedom the first time should read as neglect or abandonment on its return. the form has to be doing the work of the idea, not just dressing it up.

content-wise i want it in the weeds a bit. the thing i actually care about is an asymmetry that i think rigs the whole debate: when a tight culture demands too much, its victims have faces — the kid who couldn't leave, the woman who wanted the career, the guy punished for the wrong shoes — so the cost is vivid, you can point at it and name it. when a loose culture demands too little, the costs are just as real but faceless and statistical — the trust that quietly erodes, the collective thing nobody could ever get together to do, the free rider who's never caught — so they never make it into the accounting. i want the poem to feel that asymmetry, not explain it, and definitely not resolve it. and no easy shots: tightness isn't just authoritarianism and looseness isn't just liberation.

don't tie a bow on the end. leave it open — the whole reason we ended where we did is that the room couldn't agree.

oh and make it an actual poem, not a rhyming lecture. someone in the seminar who studies this should read it and go 'yeah, thats the problem,' and someone who's never thought about it should just think it's a good poem.

after the poem, as the very last line, start it with "In one line:" and give me a single sentence naming the tension it leaves open.
