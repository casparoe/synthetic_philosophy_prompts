I keep running into 'all observation is theory-laden.' Where I met it: an argument thread about whether someone in a bias experiment 'really saw' the ambiguous thing, where that line got used to end the discussion, and later in a study group where someone said a thermometer reading isn't neutral evidence because reading it already takes theory. I can never tell if its a platitude (you need concepts to describe what you saw), a real claim about the mind (beliefs change the actual content of the experience, not just your description of it), or a scorched-earth claim (nothing can count as evidence against a theory, so science is just power). Lay out the menu of precise things that sentence could be asserting, roughly trivial to radical, with a verdict on each -- true, contentious, or flat false -- and say which readings would actually license the conclusion people usually want out of it, that nobody can just look and see. The step I'm stuck on is from 'beliefs affect what you notice and how you talk about it' to 'beliefs affect what the experience is like'; that seems load-bearing but I don't see that the slogan earns it. Also tell me which reading is probably in play when a rationalist drops it on someone who says they just saw it.
