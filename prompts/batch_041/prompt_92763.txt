I run the intangible-heritage register for a mid-sized city's culture office. We recieve maybe twenty nominations a year, the decisions get published, and the last time we turned one down the group went to the local paper saying we'd called their heritage fake — so I want a procedure my two-person team can actually run on a nomination, built out of the Hobsbawm and Anderson stuff about invented tradition and imagined communities, instead of vibes. Specifically I want the order of questions: how to tell a living custom, which is allowed to drift and change, from an invented tradition, which insists on invariance and a continuity that's largely factitious; how to test an ancientness claim when we're never going to get archival proof; what to make of the moment of invention, i.e. does the claimed origin line up with a period of fast change, a redevelopment, a new state, a tourism push; who did the inventing and who benefits; and that census-map-museum thing Anderson does, where the state standardises and classifies the practice it later claims to have discovered. Anderson's point that being imagined doesn't make a community false has to survive into the procedure, so don't let the whole thing collapse into "recent equals inauthentic", and flag where the procedure has to stop and hand the call to a person because it's a question about what the city wants to be rather than a question of fact — final sign-off is my director, who's an administrator, not a folklorist. Then run it on the three nominations on my desk: a paper-lantern procession in the old fishing quarter, billed as centuries old, but only documented from 1981, when a residents' association formed to fight a redevelopment and a schoolteacher wrote the song and the costumes; a saint's day with continuous records back to the 1700s whose content changes every single year — route, effigy, date — but which the nomination insists is an unchanging ritual; and a weaving pattern one family calls ancestral, which a state folklore institute standardised in the 1960s for a national exhibition and which the neighbouring village also claims. Which of these can I say no to, and which does the procedure make me say yes to even though I don't want to?
