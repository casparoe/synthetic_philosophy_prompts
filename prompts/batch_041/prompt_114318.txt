Here's an argument I half-stole from a friend and now can't stop poking at, and I'd like a professional philosopher's help finding the leak. Set it up as a role for yourself -- you're an expert in metaphysics of science and philosophy of language, and I want you to brainstorm the candidates before you commit to one. Also, think carefully and go step by step; don't just name a fallacy and run.

The argument:

(1) A natural kind is a grouping that reflects the objective structure of nature rather than our interests.
(2) If a grouping reflects objective structure, then its members share some property -- or set of properties -- in virtue of which they are grouped.
(3) But look at any grouping in any science and the members differ: no two electrons occupy the same position or have the same history, no two water samples have the same impurities and isotopes, no two members of a biological species share a genotype, and for any property you name there's some member that lacks it or some non-member that has it.
(4) So no grouping satisfies (2).
(5) So there are no natural kinds -- "carving nature at its joints" is a metaphor with no cash value, and every classification, in physics as much as in sociology, is a projection of our interests.

My friend thinks (5) is a reductio of the whole idea and wants to keep (1). I think he's cheating somewhere, but here's my problem: I actually do want to say species aren't natural kinds, and I suspect "gene" and maybe even "race" aren't either, so I can't just wave the conclusion away as absurd -- I need to know exactly which step breaks and why it breaks there rather than somewhere that would let me keep those verdicts. Is it (2)? Is (3) doing illegitimate work? Is there a reading of "share a property" that saves (2) without smuggling in a single essence? Tell me which premise I should give up, why it looked fine at first, and what each repair costs -- in particular, whether any repair lets me say species aren't kinds while keeping water and electrons as kinds, or whether I have to swallow the whole thing or give up the species verdict too. American English, please.
