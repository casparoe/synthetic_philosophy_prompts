ok so this is roughly where I've landed on inheritance and equal opportunity and I want someone to tell me if it actually hangs together or if I'm just telling myself a nice story.

(1) The thing I care about most: where you end up shouldn't be set by who's kid you happen to be. Not just no formal barriers — I mean two kids with the same brains and the same willingness to work should have the same shot, whatever they were born into.
(2) I'm not a leveler though. Equal opportunity isn't equal results, and I'll happily defend big pay differences.
(3) At the same time I don't think there's anything wrong with a parent giving their kid a leg up. I'd pay my kid's tuition without blinking, I wouldn't feel guilty about it, and I don't think a rich person doing the same is a villain.
(4) Inheritance should be taxed hard. Nobody earned it. Fortunes that just sit there for generations compounding while everyone else's wages crawl is exactly the thing I hate. Big exemption for a house and maybe a family business, but the rest, take it.
(5) I'd never want to abolish the family or have the state raise kids in common. The family is where basically everything good comes from.
(6) When I try to say why (3) is ok, the honest answer isn't "it's my money" — its that a family is a thing that outlives any one of us, and money passes through it. My grandparents' money came to me in trust, in a way, and I'd pass it on the same way. The point of the money is the family continuing, not me spending it.

So: which of these are actually pulling against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each one? Rank them. And for each real one, what's the cheapest thing I could give up or soften, and what do I lose by doing it. If you think one of them has to go entirely, say which and why.

Smaller thing while you're at it: I feel completely different about a grandparent paying a kids tuition (fine) and legacy admissions at a university (corrupt), even though they kind of look like the same move. Am I kidding myself?
