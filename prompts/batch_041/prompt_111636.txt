I was having an arguement with my flatmate last night about Bernard Williams's one-thought-too-many complaint against utilitarianism, and I want to see whether it can be run backwards, so bear with me. What I want you to build is a case with two agents who do exactly the same thing and bring about exactly the same outcome — not similar outcomes, identical ones, so that nothing at all in the world distinguishes what they did. One of them is a plain act-utilitarian. The other is a Williams-style agent acting out of a ground project. The specification is this: when you set the two deliberations side by side, the utilitarian's is the one that comes out clean — one thought, pointed straight at the other person and their need, nothing spare — and the Williams agent's is the one carrying a thought too many, where the surplus thought is a thought about himself and his project rather than about the person in front of him. Keep it mundane and realistic: a family thing, a work thing, a neighbour thing; no trolleys, no one dying, no dramatic rescues. One paragraph for the scenario itself. And the extra thought has to be extra in the sense the objection is actually meant to pick out, not just because you've made the Williams agent a vain or self-absorbed person — he should be a decent man acting well, and if the reverse verdict only comes off because he's stupid or because the rule he's following is obviously daft, the case fails.

Then do the work. Walk me through each agent's deliberation, why the utilitarian's single thought is genuinely single, and why the other one has one more than it needs — under Williams's own account and under the utilitarian's account separately, since I suspect the two of them will disagree about which agent is the one carrying the spare thought. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work; my hunch is that it's whether the project shows up in the deliberation as a premise or merely as the thing that makes the person care, but you tell me. Then say what happens if I take that feature away — does the case just collapse back into the original one-thought-too-many?

Finish with the strongest way a Williams defender would attack what I've built, i.e. what they'd say to show that the reverse version doesn't stick, and whether you think that reply actually works. I have a nagging worry that the whole thing is a trick of description, so be honest with me if it is.
