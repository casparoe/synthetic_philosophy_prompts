Alright, so I've read way to much rationalist-adjacent stuff about consent (the old LW sequences, assorted blog arguments about contracts and coercion, a bunch of comment threads I should probably stop reading) and my intuitions have gone mushy on it. I want to force them into actual numbers.

Here's the ask. For each item below, give me a number from 0 to 1 for how much it invalidates consent, where 0 means the consent is perfectly valid and nobody should think twice, and 1 means there was no consent at all, the thing is void. Then a sentence or two on why it lands there. Please don't hide behind it-depends — commit to a number, you can caveat after.

- Someone physically holding you down.
- "Do this or I'll get you fired," from the person doing the asking.
- Nobody is threatening anything, you're just broke and every option in front of you is bad.
- They lie to you about a dealbreaker fact.
- They lie to you about something you care about, but it's a fact about their feelings rather than about the world.
- They simply don't mention something you'd obviously want to know, and you don't think to ask.
- You're drunk. Drunk enough to have made a bad call, but you'd honestly have said yes sober too.
- You're drunk enough that you don't remember it the next day.
- You want it, but only because of stuff you were taught to want growing up, and if you'd been raised elsewhere you almost certainly wouldn't.
- You signed something in advance agreeing to whatever a future you gets asked, and now future-you is being asked.
- Everyone's watching, and saying no would make you the buzzkill.
- You consented, then halfway through changed your mind and didn't say anything.

Three extra things. First, flag any where the number for "did they actually consent?" should come apart from the number for "is it fine to go ahead anyway?" — I think those are genuinely different questions and I want to see where you think they split. Second, say which of these are really the same defeater wearing different hats. Third, tell me if any item doesn't belong on the list at all (wrong kind of thing to be rating this way), and whether you'd bet on there being one underlying scale here or three or four seperate failure modes that just happen to share a name.
