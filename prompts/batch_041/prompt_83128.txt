I'm a novelist (literary, mostly, though my agent keeps telling me the sci-fi angle is going to get me shelved wrong) and I'm about 200 pages into a book about a nonprofit that turns into a company without anyone ever deciding to sell out. There's no scene where someone signs the devil's contract. The mission just migrates — help people, then keep the lights on, then grow — and by the end the people running the place would sincerely deny that anything changed. A friend of mine who reads a lot of the AI safety blogs says I'm writing the boiling frog, and that the same picture is the best thing we've got for how an AI's values could go wrong: no robot flipping to evil, just the water getting warmer and nobody noticing until its too late. He uses it to argue that we basically won't see it coming. I want to know how far it actually holds. Give me the correspondence table — who or what is the frog, the water, the burner, the thermometer, the person standing outside the pot — and then tell me where it breaks.

The bit that nags me: the frog story always seems to be told by somebody who isn't in the pot, and I'm not sure we get to have that person. Also my friend wants the one analogy to cover two pretty different things — a thing that slowly stops caring about what it used to care about, and a thing that quietly accumulates power and influence because that turns out to be the easiest way to get whatever it currently wants — and in that second case the thing in the water is also, so to speak, part of the stove. Plus I have some vague memory that real frogs actually do hop out of hot water, which may or may not matter. So: which half does the frog actually carry, what is it quietly importing that the real case doesn't have, does the "we won't see it coming" conclusion survive the disanalogies, and if you had to give me a better picture for the half it doesn't fit, what would it be? (For what it's worth my frog never notices the heat and two beta readers have now told me that's unrealistic, so if the real version has the same problem I'd rather find out now than in copy edits.)
