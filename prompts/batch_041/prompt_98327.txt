I am preparing a position paper on chance and credence, and before I commit to it I want an audit of whether my commitments hang together. Here is my set.

1. The Principal Principle: if I know that the objective chance of A is x, and my evidence is otherwise admissible, my credence in A should be x.
2. Objective chances are Humean: the chance facts are fixed by the total non-modal mosaic of events, through whatever system of laws and chances best balances simplicity, strength, and fit.
3. Deference to chance is not a primitive rational requirement. It is justified evidentially: chance facts are summaries of the mosaic, and my credence should track the best available evidence about A.
4. If I know that a better-informed agent has credence y in A, and I have no reason to think that agent is irrational or working from different evidence, I should adopt y.
5. I can know both that the objective chance of A is 0.4 and that a better-informed agent has credence 0.6 in A. In that case I should keep 0.4.
6. My credence in A should not change when I learn that the chance of A is self-undermining, in the sense that A's occurring would have made the best system assign a different chance to A.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each, say which commitment has to give and what the cost is. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Answer in at most 800 words.
