I'm putting together a small research programme — a workshop series plus two postdocs for three years — on the conceptual foundations of democratic inclusion, and I have to decide what the group should work on first. The three candidate areas are compulsory voting, lowering the voting age, and extending the vote to resident non-citizens. My working hypothesis is that these are three faces of a single question: what makes someone a member of the demos, and whether membership is a status one either has or lacks, or instead a bundle of decision-specific authorisations that one might hold for some questions and not others. A colleague in my department thinks that hypothesis flattens what actually matters, and that each area turns on something different — she says compulsion is really about whether voting is a duty of justice or only a right, the age threshold is really about whether competence is a scalar to be weighed or a gate to be passed, and non-citizen voting is really about whether the relevant claim is that everyone affected by a decision ought to have a say or that everyone bound by a law ought to. We spent most of a seminar failing to settle whether she's right, which is part of why I'm asking.

What I want is a defended agenda, not a literature survey. Suppose the group has philosophical training and no capacity to run experiments or design surveys, and suppose the aim is conceptual work that could change how theorists, and eventually reformers, frame these debates. Which open questions in this area should come first, and why? For each, say how upstream it is — how many other disputes would be settled, dissolved, or reframed if it were answered — how tractable it is for people who can't gather their own data, and how neglected it is relative to how much turns on it. Then tell me which of the questions I've named, mine or my colleague's, are likely dead ends or pseudo-questions, and what would have to happen — an argument landing, a body of empirical results becoming robust, a legal or constitutional change — for your ordering to shift. I care more about the criteria and the trade-offs you make explicit than about a comprehensive map, so please defend the order rather than hedge it.
