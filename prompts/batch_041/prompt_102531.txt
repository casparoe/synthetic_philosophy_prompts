Here is my opening move, which comes out of an evening I spent fiddling with an audio editor. I took a single steady tone and faded it continuously to silence over about ten seconds. At the start everyone agrees it is audible; at the end everyone agrees it is not; there is no instant in the fade at which I can point and say the tone stopped being audible. But it seems to me the ear has a threshold underneath all this — a point where the last hair cell stops firing, or where the signal finally drops below the noise floor of the auditory system — and if some such physical event occurs at a definite moment, then there is a last audible tone after all, and the whole business looks like ignorance rather than a hole in the world: we simply cannot say which tone it was. So why should I believe there is no fact of the matter about where "audible" stops, rather than a fact of the matter that we are constitutionally unable to locate? And please do not tell me the physiology is beside the point unless you can also say what it is about the meaning of the predicate, rather than about the ear, that gets to decide where it stops.
