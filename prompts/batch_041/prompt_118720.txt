Ok so this is a weird one but bear with me. I run a small reading group on historical contingency -- for-want-of-a-nail stuff, butterfly effect history, Tolstoy going on about how the generals are basically decorative -- and instead of opening with another essay I want to open with a ranking exercise I can argue with in the room. So I need you to actually do the ranking.

Here's the scheme. For each event below, give me two numbers, both between 0 and 1:

Flip: imagine re-running the short window around the event -- call it a few months, a year at the outside -- with only tiny perturbations. A different wind. A courier arriving six hours late. A general with a bad cold. Someone turning left instead of right. Everything structural stays fixed: the economies, the demographics, the technology, who's allied to whom, all of it. Flip is the probability that, under that kind of jitter, the outcome comes out the other way.

Leverage: if the outcome does flip, how different is the next century? 0 = a hundred years later you basically can't tell the difference. 1 = a world you'd barely recognize.

Then sort the events by Flip x Leverage, highest first, and give me two or three sentences on each. For the ones where the two numbers pull hard against each other -- high flip but low leverage, or the reverse -- tell me which of the two you think I'm misjudging and why.

The events:

1. Hastings, 1066 -- the whole thing where Harold marches north, smashes the Norwegians, then has to race back down.
2. The Spanish Armada, 1588.
3. The first battle of the Marne, 1914.
4. The Cuban missile crisis, 1962 -- and specifically whether Arkhipov and the B-59 crew carry any real weight in your number.
5. The night the Berlin Wall opened, November 1989 (Schabowski's press conference).
6. Florida, 2000.

Last thing, and keep this to a paragraph: say whether the whole scheme is even coherent. Does "small perturbation with structure held fixed" pick out anything real, or is it just smuggling in an assumption about which causes count as big and which count as small -- and if it's the latter, does that kill the exercise or just make it a way of surfacing people's priors? Dont let that question eat the ranking, the ranking is the main thing.
