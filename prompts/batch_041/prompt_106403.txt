I'm a novelist, about two-thirds through a book set in the late forties — Berlin mostly, two families on opposite sides of a line that wasn't quite a line yet. I've got a fellowship application due (public humanities thing, six months, they want a two-page proposal) and I'd like to use it to think through the history properly instead of just cherry-picking whichever account flatters my plot.

Here's my half-formed idea, tell me if it's a real project or just me avoiding the actual writing. Everyone asks who started the Cold War and everyone answers with a different culprit depending on where they're standing. But lately I suspect the interesting question isn't who started it, it's what we're even counting as a "start," and whether "started" is one question or a dozen seperate ones. Is the Cold War a single thing that began at a single moment, or a bundle of quarrels — Germany, Iran, Greece, the bomb — each with its own first mover, so that the question has no single answer but plenty of true ones? And then blame, which I don't think is the same as cause: is blaming a state the same kind of act as blaming a person, and what do you do with the fact that so much of what happened looks like nobody's decision at all, just two bureaucracies grinding past each other until the grinding became the point?

Turn this into a proposal. I need the question stated so it has an actual answer, why the usual camps (blame Moscow, blame Washington, blame nobody in particular, it was structural) haven't settled it, what I'd actually do month by month for six months, and what would count as the project succeeding versus me fooling myself. Don't hand me a reading list or citations, I'll get those from a librarian. Keep it in American English.
