I have no formal training in philosophy or cognitive science, but I have spent a lot of time thinking about these questions on my own. In a long argument with a friend who works in machine learning, I said that I did not believe a perfect simulation of my brain would be me. He replied, 'The brain is a computer,' and treated that as decisive. I have since heard the same sentence used to argue that mind uploading is possible, to dismiss worries about consciousness, to claim that neuroscience is just reverse-engineering software, and to say that mental states are computational states. I can see that the sentence is doing a lot of work in these arguments, but I cannot tell what it is actually claiming.

Could you lay out the different precise claims that 'the brain is a computer' could be making, from the nearly trivial to the very radical? I would like a menu of readings, not a single interpretation defended. For each reading, say what it commits you to, whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something false or at least unsupported. In particular, I am confused about the difference between the brain literally being a digital computer with discrete internal states and stored instructions, the brain implementing some program or algorithm, the brain being a physical system that can be described as performing computations, and the brain being a computer only relative to an observer who interprets its states in a certain way. I have also read that any physical system can be described as implementing some computation if you choose a suitable mapping, which makes me wonder whether the slogan becomes trivial if it means only that. Does the slogan require a distinction between syntax and semantics, or between computation and information processing, or between a system that computes and a system that is a computer? Does it require that mental states have representational content, and that the content is carried by formal symbols?

Then, please tell me which reading my friend is most likely relying on when he uses the slogan to settle whether an uploaded mind would be me, and whether that reading actually supports his conclusion. If some readings support the conclusion and others do not, say which are which. I would like the answer to be organized as a list of readings with verdicts, so I can bring it back to him.
