I'm applying for a six-month fellowship and need a two-page research proposal. My half-formed idea is that disputes over "the heritability" of traits such as height, educational attainment, and psychiatric conditions go wrong because heritability is being asked to do at least three different jobs at once: it names a descriptive variance ratio within a specified population and range of environments; it is used to make claims about what would happen under some intervention on genes or on environments; and it is used to make claims about what is transmitted from parents to offspring. These three quantities can come apart, and I suspect the sharpest confusion lies in the gap between the second and the third. I also suspect that the reference range of environments is doing hidden normative work: a heritability coefficient is defined only relative to a population and a range of environments, and which environments belong in that range is not settled by the data alone, so some apparently empirical disagreements about how heritable a trait is may really be disagreements about which environments to include.

Turn this into a proposal. State the central question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the standard treatments leave it open; describe the method (conceptual analysis of the distinct quantities, plus a few worked cases in which high heritability estimates and cheap environmental interventions come apart); say what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and give a realistic scope for six months. Refer to positions and arguments rather than to specific papers; I will add the citations myself. Write in American English.
