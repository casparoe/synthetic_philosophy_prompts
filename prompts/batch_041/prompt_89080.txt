Next month I've got a 30-minute slot in our dept work-in-progress seminar to defend a draft on Carroll's tortoise, and what I want from you is a Q&A prep sheet, not a pep talk or a summary of the literature. Here's the crowd: two proof theorists who live in natural deduction and sequent calculi and think the whole tortoise puzzle is an artifact of writing arguments in a Hilbert-ish style; two epistemologists who work on a priori justification and care a lot about wether inferring by modus ponens can be justified without already using modus ponens; one person who works on the normativity of logic and thinks norms are perfectly expressible in propositions, so she'll want to know why I say otherwise; one Wittgenstein-ish guy who'll say rules bottom out in practice and ask what's new here; and a cognitive scientist who studies reasoning and thinks nobody reasons by applying rules in the first place. No substructural people, no historians of logic.

My line is this. The tortoise's demand can't be met by any extra premise, and that isn't a failure of patience on Achilles's part, it's a diagnosis. The mistake is what I call the propositionalization move: treating the rule "from A and A→B, infer B" as one more thing to be believed, so it can be handed over as a premise. I distinguish three things the tortoise could be after — (i) the conditional as a premise, (ii) the rule as a permission ("you may infer"), (iii) the act of inferring itself — and my claim is that piling up more of (i) or (ii) never yields (iii), because (iii) is something a reasoner does rather than something they have. I've also got a variant case where I hand the tortoise a sequent-calculus presentation and a permission instead of a premise, and the regress restarts anyway; I use it to block the "you just formalized it wrong" reply, though I'm not sure its airtight. Then I draw the morals: if (iii) is carried by no proposition, the standard project of showing deduction to be truth-conducive from premises the reasoner already accepts is misbegotten, and I think that's the part that will get me hurt.
