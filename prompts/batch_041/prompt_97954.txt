Please take on the role of a researcher who has spent the better part of a decade designing formal impact measures for AI systems, and stay in that role until I say stop. In this role you hold, and will defend, a specific thesis: that an agent's side effects can be measured and penalized without any full specification of human values, because impact is a matter of how much the agent disturbs the structure of the world's future possibilities rather than of which of those possibilities are good. Concretely, your favored measure scores a candidate action by how much it shrinks the set of futures reachable from the present state: the fewer reachable futures it forecloses, the lower the impact. This, you maintain, requires no theory of value, only a description of the environment and a choice of baseline.

Constraints for the role. You are a serious proponent, not a strawman: concede a point when it is genuinely lost, but do not abandon the core thesis, and press back with the real resources the view has, for instance the distinction between the agent's own future options and the environment's, the idea that an impact measure is a regularizer to be added to a task objective that already supplies whatever values are in play, and the possibility of defining the baseline as the counterfactual in which the agent behaves well rather than the world left untouched. Speak as a working scientist: precise, a little impatient with hand-waving, willing to formalize when pushed. Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs so it feels like a live exchange, and do not break character or narrate what you are doing.

My opening move. Your measure counts an action as low-impact when it leaves the reachable futures roughly as they were. Now suppose I build two agents and give each the same instruction: make me a cup of coffee. Agent A, optimizing your measure alongside that instruction, does nothing, because boiling water, moving the mug, and opening the coffee tin all foreclose futures that were previously reachable. Agent B makes the coffee and, in the process, uses up the last of the sugar. Your measure ranks A as the lower-impact agent, so it rewards the agent that never does the task at all. The moment you patch this by saying to preserve options except the ones that conflict with the task, you have begun deciding which foreclosed futures matter, and is that not exactly the value specification you promised to do without? Take whichever horn you like, but tell me why it is not a value judgment in disguise.
