I sit on the algorithmic accountability board for our state's unemployment-insurance agency. We have a model that drafts eligibility determinations for caseworkers, who review and sign off, and at our meeting on the 14th the board has to pick the audit regime for the next fiscal year. I have to circulate a written recomendation beforehand, so I need you to draft it.

Here's the choice. Option A, outcome-based: each quarter we sample final determinations, score them against what happened on appeal, and pay the vendor a bonus tied to how many match. Option B, process-based: we score the file itself -- did the rationale name the governing rule, was the model's uncertainty surfaced, did the caseworker record a reason for overriding -- and the bonus rides on those scores. We ran both over the same six months of backlog. Under B, checklist compliance went from about 60% to 95% and the reversal rate didn't budge at all. Under A, reversals fell on the ordinary cases but rose on the disability-verification class, where it turned out the model was matching the appeals label for the wrong reason and collapses the moment the intake form changes. Two things the board keeps talking past each other about. First, the rationales in the file are written after the decision is already made -- the vendor's own engineer called it "trace laundering" in a message that got forwarded to us -- so when we audit "process" we aren't watching the reasoning, we're watching a plausible story about the reasoning. Second, the appeal outcomes we'd use as ground truth in A are produced by human reviewers who, it turns out, mostly defer to the file we're supposedly grading them against, so the label is downstream of the process.

Draft the memo. Give me an actual bottom line -- A, B, or a specific hybrid, with a time frame and a trigger for revisiting it -- not a framework, and reasons the vendor and the dissenting board members can respond to rather than just restatements of the two positions. Take the strongest case for whichever option you reject seriously enough that its supporters don't feel railroaded. Finish by telling me plainly which single feature of this setup is doing the most work in your argument and what happens to your recommendation if that feature weren't there. No citations needed, and keep it short enough that I can read it out loud in ten minutes.
