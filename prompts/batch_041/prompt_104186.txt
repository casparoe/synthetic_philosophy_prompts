I run a small reading group on teleosemantics and the project of naturalising mental content, and we keep hitting the same snag: every toy example anyone reaches for (frogs and flies, that sort of thing) is one everyones already memorised, so people just recite the verdict instead of actually deriving it. So I'd rather you build us a fresh one from scratch, to spec. What I want is a single animal, a bird or a rodent, something that actually exists and nothing sience-fictional, with an internal state that has a perfectly determinate content, but where that state has never once covaried with the thing it's about, and the thing it's about may not even exist any more. The content can't be stipulated by you or by me or by any trainer in the story; it has to fall out of the history of the state and nothing else. And I want a matched pair, differing in exactly one respect: in the first animal the state's function was fixed by natural selection over many generations, while in the second the very same machinery, doing the very same downstream work, had its function fixed by trial-and-error learning inside the individual's own lifetime. Hold everything else constant, same wiring, same behaviour, same causal role in the system.

Then do the check properly, please. What does a pure informational or covariational account say about each of the two animals? What does a selected-function, teleosemantic account say, and does it treat the learned case the same as the selected one or does it come apart there? What does a functional-role or use-based account say? After that, tell me which single feature of the case is actually doing the work, what happens to the whole example if you delete the selection history and keep everything else fixed, and how someone who thinks covariation is the whole story would try to deflate it. I don't need citations or a reading list, just the case and the argument, in British English.
