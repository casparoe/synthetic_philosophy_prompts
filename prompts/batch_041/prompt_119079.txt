I am a high-school student who has been asked to lead a forty-five-minute session of our school's philosophy club. The topic I chose is whether conceivability can ever be a guide to possibility. I do not want you to explain the topic to me; I want a teaching plan. My classmates are bright but have no formal logic or metaphysics. I want to avoid the zombie and Hesperus-and-Phosphorus examples because everyone has heard them. I also want the session to hinge on a distinction I find slippery: between the loose sense in which I can picture something and the stricter sense in which a scenario is fully coherent, with no hidden contradiction waiting to be discovered. Please tell me the order in which the ideas must be introduced and why each depends on the previous one; the one carrying example I should build the session around, what it must have and lack, and why it is better than the usual examples; the misconceptions my classmates will predictably bring or form—for instance, that if I can imagine it then it is possible, or that if I cannot imagine it then it is impossible—and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off; one question I can ask at the end to see whether they have understood the distinction rather than just learned a slogan; and what I should openly refuse to explain at this level. Please keep the whole plan under 800 words.
