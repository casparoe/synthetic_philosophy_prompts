I'm a third-year PhD student in STS — my work is on how ML systems get evaluated in practice — and I've somehow ended up hosting the interview slot at our department's AI ethics workshop next month. The guest is a researcher I really admire who's spent the last few years arguing that the alignment problem should be narrowed way down: the target isn't giving AI the right values or aligning it with human flourishing, it's just getting systems to do what their operators actually intend, and everything else is either downstream of that or a different problem wearing the same name. I want questions that actually push on this, not softballs that let her restate the thesis for the fourth time.

Can you draft 8–10 questions, with a follow-up for each one keyed to the answer she's most likely to give? A few things I definately want covered:

- At least one that makes her say what work the word intent is doing here. If the operator often can't articulate their own intent in the cases that matter, is there even a fact of the matter for the system to track, or is intent alignment just instruction-following with a philosophy degree?
- At least one on whether intent alignment is sufficient rather than merely necessary — the Midas case, where the system flawlessly does exactly what was asked and that's the disaster — and what she says when the operator's intent includes being corrected, or includes the system not being the kind of thing that would optimize too hard for whatever it thinks the intent is.
- At least one on the objection that the narrowing collapses: to track my intent rather than my literal words, the system needs a model of what I care about, and that starts to look like the value stuff she's trying to set aside.
- And one on the politics of the broad version. I'd also like a closing question about what would actually make her give up the narrow framing — a result, a class of failure, a demonstration that the broad problem can't be quarantined. And if she reaches for the line that the broad problem is just the narrow one plus ordinary policy questions, I want a follow-up that makes her spell out exactly where that boundary is supposed to fall, because I suspect it's doing more work than she lets on. Keep the register conversational — this is a workshop chat, not a viva — and please no warm-up stuff like what got her into alignment in the first place.
