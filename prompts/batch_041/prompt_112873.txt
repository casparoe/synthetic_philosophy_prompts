okay, so I'm a thrid-year PhD working on the foundations of inductive inference and I've hit something I'd rather have your honest credence on than another round of the usual talking points.

The proposition, call it P: Solomonoff induction's convergence theorem gives a genuine justification for treating short description length as a guide to truth — genuine in the sense of licensing that preference for agents like us, who have finite data and finite compute, rather than only in the infinite-data limit for an idealised reasoner. I'll count P true if the convergence result plus the structure of the universal prior is enough on its own, and false if it needs some further epistemic assumption smuggled in — that the environment is computable, that we've got a privileged reference machine, that we're sitting inside the prior's support, or whatever.

Any number from 0 to 1 is fine, but what I actually want is the decomposition. Here's how I'm carving it up, though tell me if I've got the partition wrong:

(a) does an asymptotic result at all — posterior mass concentrating on the truth as the data go to infinity — carry any normative weight for a bounded agent who never gets to the limit? Or is 'converges in the limit' simply not the kind of thing that can justify a belief now?

(b) the invariance theorem fixes the prior only up to a multiplicative constant that depends on the pair of universal machines, and that constant isn't effectively bounded — the standard proof gets it by prefixing a simulator, and the prefix can be arbitrarily long. So for any finite dataset you can rig two machines so that either of two rival hypotheses looks simpler. Does that collapse the 'objective simplicity' story, or is it a harmless idealisation that washes out eventually?

(c) is the computability assumption (the true environment lies in the support of the prior) doing the real work — i.e. is the whole thing quietly circular?

(d) is the 2^(-length) weighting actually derived from anything deeper, or is it just the convention that makes the algebra come out clean?

Rough probability for each, how they combine into P, and then which of them you're least sure about and what would move it most. And if you reckon the whole framing is a category error — that 'justification for finite agents' isn't the sort of thing a convergence theorem could ever deliver — say so and still give me a number, with the same decomposition.
