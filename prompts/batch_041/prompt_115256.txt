OK so this is going to sound like a random question, but I've been chewing on it for a few weeks and I can't let it go. I recently got roped onto the safety committee at work (I work at a mid-size bakery, so "safety committee" is a fancy name for three of us arguing about the dough mixer), and we had an incident where a guy got hurt because a guard wasn't refitted after maintenance. In the write-up, half the committee wanted to say the injury was *caused* by whoever skipped the refit, and the other half said no, the cause was the machine, and the skipped step was just... nothing, an absence, you can't point to it. And I realized I've had this exact fight before, at home, about whether my brother "caused" our dad's fall by not calling an ambulance sooner -- everyone had strong feelings and nobody could state the actual rule. What stuck with me is that the same kind of not-doing gets treated completely differently depending on stuff nobody says out loud. If the harm is already rolling -- the machine's already going to hurt him, the stroke's already happening -- then failing to step in feels like a cause. But if the good was never going to happen unless somebody acted, then failing to act feels like nothing at all, like you can't cause a thing that wasn't already on its way. And on top of that, whether the not-doing even counts seems to hinge on whether the person was supposed to be doing it: a stranger walks past, a lifeguard walks past, very different stories, even though neither of them moved.

So what I want to know is whether there's a name for this, and whether it's one name or three. I have a feeling the general area is called something like "causing things by not doing them," and that philosophers have definately fought about whether that's even possible -- but what I'm after is narrower: the split between not-stopping-a-harm and not-producing-a-good, and the way a background expectation of action seems to decide which side of that line you land on. Is there an established term for that asymmetry, or for the way the expectation seems to do the work? If the nearest thing is the debate over whether absences can be causes, or something half-remembered like "double prevention," then tell me exactly where my pattern matches and where it doesn't, because I don't think it's quite any of them -- the bit I can't place is that the expectation seems to be doing *causal* work and not just *blame* work, and I can't tell if that's a real distinction or if I'm just confusing "caused it" with "is to blame for it."

If nothing fits, coin me something and tell me what it covers and what it doesn't. The edges matter more to me than the label -- I want to know what the thing is, what it isn't, and which nearby idea is the closest miss, because right now every time this comes up I end up waving my hands at "well, it depends."
