For some years I have worked as a nurse in a chronic pain clinic, and I have gradually accumulated patients who do not fit the vocabulary we have for them. One woman reports her back pain at the same intensity as always and describes its character and location exactly as before, but says it has stopped bothering her altogether; her injury and her medication are unchanged. A second patient, whose pain is as severe as any I have seen, tells me quite calmly that it is terrible and that he has no wish for it to stop — not resigned, not depressed, simply without the wanting. I can already tell both of these apart from the stoic who endures a pain she still wants gone, and from the patient who has learned not to struggle against a pain he still wants gone. What I cannot name is what the two of them have in common: how bad a pain feels, how much it bothers the person who has it, and whether that person wants it to stop seem to be three separate matters, while our words treat them as one. The same split shows up in treatment — the drug that lowers the number on the scale often leaves the awfulness untouched, and now and then I see the reverse. I have been told that something like the first case occurs in patients with certain kinds of brain damage, but that is a syndrome with a lesion behind it, and what I keep meeting seems to be a structure rather than a disease.

Is there an established concept for this — for the gap itself, or for either of the two ways of falling into it? If the only name on offer belongs to the neurological cases, what is the nearest concept for the general phenomenon, and precisely where does the comparison break down? If nothing fits, please coin a term, define it, and say what it rules out. It matters to me practically, because I have to record in the notes whether a patient is suffering, and I have stopped trusting that the number or the word pain tells me. I would also like the answer to take a position on the philosophical question underneath: if the awfulness can disappear while the sensation remains, and the wish for relief can disappear while the awfulness remains, then is a pain bad because of how it feels, or because the person who has it is set against it?
