I work as a systems designer on a large multiplayer simulation game, and I keep running into a theological analogy that I can’t quite cash out. The engine is a branching-worlds simulator: every possible world is a path through a state tree, and at each decision point an NPC’s action is produced by a deterministic policy plus a seeded random stream. Recently we added a counterfactual oracle: a precomputed cache that, for every possible agent and every possible circumstance, stores what that agent would freely choose in that circumstance. The cache is populated before any world is instantiated, and the world-creation routine consults it when deciding which agents to create and which circumstances to place them in. The designers treat the cache as a source of knowledge that is logically prior to the world but posterior to the agent’s nature: it does not causally determine the choice, it merely records it. A colleague says this is just Molinism with extra steps; another says the analogy collapses the moment you look at it closely. I know the engine vocabulary well, but I have only the outline of the theological debate.

Please restate the Molinist position — the claim that God knows, prior to any decree, what every possible free creature would freely choose in every possible circumstance; the distinction between natural, middle, and free knowledge; the role of counterfactuals of freedom; and the grounding objection, i.e. the worry that if these counterfactuals are true before any divine decree, nothing has made them true — as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary. Translate possible worlds as save files, counterfactuals as cache entries, free choices as policy outputs under a seed, divine decrees as world-creation parameters, and so on. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the position translate cleanly into the engine’s architecture, which parts require distortion (for example, where the engine’s determinism or the cache’s causal role smuggles in assumptions the theology denies), and which parts have no counterpart at all. Is the residue a defect of my framework — something a simulation engine cannot represent — or a sign that the theological claim is less well-defined than it appears? I want the translation to be honest, not a re-description that makes the theology look like a database query with a designer’s license.
