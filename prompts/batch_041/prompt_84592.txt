Two wrinkles from our group that I want the list to cover, because I think that's where the interesting stuff is.

1. The lottery and the preface seem to have the same probabilistic shape -- a bunch of individually near-certain things that can't all be true at once -- but the intuitions pull in opposite directions. Is that a real asymmetry the theory has to respect, or an illusion? To isolate it: suppose I have 100 unrelated claims about tomorrow's weather in 100 cities, each 99% likely and statistically independent, so nothing tells me how many are wrong, and it seems fine to believe all 100. Now suppose my friend the forecaster tells me her service always gets exactly one city wrong out of any 100. Same probabilities, but now I know exactly one claim is false. Does that turn it into a lottery, and if so, what's doing the work -- exhaustiveness, a known partition, my knowing the disjunction, or something else? A theory that can't say which feature matters is just gerrymandering.

2. Stakes. Same 100 claims, but they're a medical protocol: 99 concern dosages where a mistake means a bad afternoon, one concerns a drug interaction where a mistake kills someone.
