I keep going back and forth on how I size bets, and I want to know if there's one principle behind my gut or if I'm just fooling myself. Here's where I land:

- A coin that comes up heads 60% of the time, paying even money. Putting my whole bankroll on one flip: awful. Betting a small fixed slice of it over and over: obviously fine.
- Same coin, but I only ever get to flip it once. I'd still say going all-in is nuts, even though I'd happily take the bet.
- Insurance on my house is a losing bet on average. I buy it anyway and think that's the rational thing to do.
- A lottery ticket is also a losing bet, I don't buy one, and I think buying one is a small mistake.
- I wouldn't pay more than a few dollars for the St. Petersburg game, even though its expected payout is infinite.
- Someone offers me double-or-nothing on my entire net worth, and even tips it a little in my favor. I refuse.
- But I'm happy to have my whole retirement sitting in a broad index fund, which is also one big uncertain bet that could go badly.

The last two feel like they can't both be right, and the insurance/lottery pair bugs me too since there both negative-EV. I have a hunch it's something about caring about wealth multiplicatively rather than additively -- growing it by factors instead of by sums -- but I want that made precise. What's the actual principle, does it get all seven, and which of my verdicts does it make me give up?
