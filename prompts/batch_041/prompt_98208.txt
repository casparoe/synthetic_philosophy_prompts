Write me a dialogue between two philosophers arguing about the Meno problem — why is knowledge better than mere true belief, if it even is. A page or so, set it wherever you like.

One of them should defend a line I've been kickin around: that the real payoff of knowledge over lucky true belief is that knowledge is the stuff you can pool — cross-examined, relied on in testimony, built on by other people — whereas a true belief you just happen to hold can't be handed over, because from the outside nobody can tell your lucky guess apart from your insight. So knowledge is worth more because it's a public, examinable good.

The other should push back hard. Maybe that this gets the order of explanation backwards, since you already need to know who's worth listening to before any of the pooling gets going. Or that it makes the value of knowledge merely instrumental to cooperation and can't explain why a solitary knower is better off than a solitary lucky believer. Or something else that bites — your call.

Neither of them should be a strawman, and I want real back-and-forth: each responding to what the other actually said rather than trading monologues. Skip the textbook justified-true-belief recap, and don't bother with citations, I'll know what you're gesturing at.
