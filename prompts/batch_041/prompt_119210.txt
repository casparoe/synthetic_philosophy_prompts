I am a statistician working on Bayesian model comparison — marginal likelihoods, Bayes factors, hierarchical priors, and the perennial arguments about improper priors and about what belongs in the denominator. I have almost no background in philosophy of religion, and I do not want a literature survey or a reading list. What I want is a translation, and a candid account of what the translation costs.

Please restate the fine-tuning argument for design, as faithfully as you can, in my vocabulary, and then do the same for the principal lines of objection to it. I care about the elements that usually stay implicit: what plays the role of a prior over the constants, what plays the role of the likelihood of life, what the alternative to design is supposed to be, and how the argument handles the fact that we have exactly one realisation to condition on.

There are several things I have read or been told, and I would rather you place them inside the translation than accept them on trust. First, the argument seems to require a normalisable distribution over possible values of the constants, and the natural candidates are either arbitrary or improper, in which case a Bayes factor may not exist at all; tell me whether the argument can be run without one. Second, life-permitting is a coarse-graining choice — any life at all, observers like us, carbon chemistry specifically — and the likelihood moves enormously with the choice; I want to know what fixes the right grain in my framework, if anything does. Third, it has been put to me that the constants look at least as tightly tuned for the production of black holes and massive stars as for biology, which would make life an oddly parochial thing to have specified. Fourth, the non-design alternative is not a single hypothesis but an open-ended disjunction, so I want to know what a likelihood conditional on it could even mean, and whether that is the familiar problem of the catch-all in Bayesian confirmation or something worse. Fifth, the likelihood has to come from physics rather than from frequencies, since there is one sample. Sixth, the design hypothesis looks to me like a composite with unspecified free parameters — what the designer wants, how capable it is — and I want to know whether it therefore attracts an Occam penalty or escapes one by being too vague to be a model at all. The multiverse response, incidentally, looks to me like a hierarchical model in which the constants are drawn from a hyperdistribution, and I suspect the hyperprior is where the whole thing either works or does not.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. For each element of the argument, say whether it translates cleanly, whether it survives only in a distorted form, or whether it has no counterpart at all in what I do. I am particularly interested in three candidates for non-translatability: the thought that the constants might not have been variable in the first place, so that there is no space of alternatives for a prior to live on; the suggestion that design is not a hypothesis with a likelihood so much as a constraint on how the whole comparison is set up; and the indexical element in our observing a life-permitting universe. For each, say whether the failure marks a genuine defect of my framework or whether it is evidence that the original claim was less clear than it sounded and was doing its work through its ambiguity.

Tell me how confident you are at each stage, and single out the point you are least sure of. Write it as continuous prose, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold, and no tables — the way you would write a long email to a colleague. Please use British English.
