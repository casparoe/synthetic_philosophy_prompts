A decision-maker faces two independent choices. Choice I: (a) $500 for certain, or (b) a 10% chance of $2,500, an 85% chance of $500, and a 5% chance of nothing. Choice II: (c) a 15% chance of $500, otherwise nothing, or (d) a 10% chance of $2,500, otherwise nothing. She reports preferring (a) to (b) and (d) to (c). Explain why this combination of preferences violates the independence axiom, making the common-consequence structure explicit: (a) and (b) both contain an 85% chance of $500, and replacing that shared component in each with an 85% chance of nothing yields (c) and (d) respectively. Then explain the conceptual issue the example is standardly taken to raise — whether independence is a requirement of rationality or merely a convenient modelling assumption — giving the strongest argument on each side, including whether the sure-thing-principle defence, in both its simultaneous and its sequential (dynamic Dutch book) versions, survives the fact that her risk attitude appears to depend on whether the choice set contains a certain option. End with a one-sentence verdict on which side you find more compelling. Answer in at most 350 words.
