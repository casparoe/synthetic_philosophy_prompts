Had a three-hour fight with my friend last night about the rescue case — one person on one side, five on the other, you can only reach one group — and he's now a full convert to the numbers-dont-count line (he says he got it from Taurek, but for all I know he made it up). I think his conclusion is obviously wrong and I still can't find where his argument breaks. Here's his thing, cleaned up:

1. When I'm deciding whether to save someone, my reason to save them is just that their life is at stake — same reason, same strength, for every person in peril.
2. Reasons of equal strength don't outweigh each other. If my reason to save A is exactly as strong as my reason to save B, then neither beats the other; they just balance.
3. So my reason to save the one is exactly as strong as my reason to save any single one of the five — no individual's claim is weaker than the one's.
4. And a claim doesn't get weightier just because other people happen to have claims too. B's claim isn't stronger because C, D, E and F are also drowning; that's not a feature of B's situation.
5. So my reason to save the five is not stronger than my reason to save the one.
6. When two options are backed by reasons and neither outweighs the other, either choice is permissible.
7. So it's permissible to save the one instead of the five.
8. Nothing in 1–6 changes if the other group is five thousand or five billion. Nobody's individual reason got any stronger along the way, and step 4 says the company of others doesn't add weight, so the size of the group is invisible to the argument.
9. Therefore there's no number of people such that I'm required to save them rather than the one. The numbers never count, period.

He's happy with 9. I'm not — if it's one guy versus literally everyone else alive and I save the one guy, I'm a monster, full stop. But I can't break the argument. Step 4 seems obviously true to me; I genuinely don't see how a second drowning person makes the first one's claim any weightier. And yet 4 plus 2 seems to grind out 5, and 5 grinds out everything else. He thinks the whole move is just that you can't add up people, like that's a neutral observation, and says anyone who resists is secretly doing utilitarian arithmetic. When I push, he says the culprit is 2, that I just need to accept reasons don't accumulate, but I don't even know what that's supposed to mean and it feels like he's naming the conclusion rather than a premise.

So which step do I reject, and what does it actually cost me? I'd like to keep 1 and 4 if I possibly can — I don't want to end up saying a person's claim gets stronger just because a crowd is standing behind them. But if I have to give something up I want to know what I'm signing up for, because I have a feeling the bill comes due somewhere I haven't looked.
