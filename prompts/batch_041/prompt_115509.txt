my reading group just finished Galen Strawson's Basic Argument and everyone left a bit rattled, so i want to open next week with a sestina -- the form's obsessive recycling of the same six end-words is basically the shape of the regress, each stanza trying to ground the self one level further down and just getting the same words back. pick end-words that can do that work (cause, self, prior, why, own, and something for the deed itself) and let the rotation make the point that the asking never lands anywhere; the envoi should feel like a door closing, not a resolution. two things i don't want flattened: that the regress is meant to bite whether or not determinism is true -- a choice that just happens to you isnt any more yours than a caused one -- and that the target is only responsibility in the ultimate, self-origination sense, so the poem can still let people go on thanking and resenting each other without that becoming a cop-out or a punchline. if the only ways out are a circle or a stop you can't justify, i'd rather it sit in that than invent a fourth door.
