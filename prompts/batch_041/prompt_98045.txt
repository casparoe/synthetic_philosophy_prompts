I hold a view about pain that I shall state as starkly as I can, because I want to see how much it can take.

The view is this. What makes an episode of pain bad for the creature undergoing it is its phenomenal character, and specifically the sensory quality of painfulness itself — the felt hurt that qualifies the sensation. On this view the badness is intrinsic to the sensation and inseparable from it. There is no possible creature in whom the sensory-discriminative content of pain (its location, its intensity, its distinctive quality) is fully present and who is nevertheless not harmed by it, because the badness just is that content's hedonic character. Aversion, the urge to withdraw, the motivational pull — these are the usual consequences of pain and they can come apart from it, but they are not what its badness consists in. Call this intrinsicism about the badness of pain.

The stock objection is pain asymbolia: patients who, after certain lesions, report pain, localise it, give intensity ratings, and yet show none of the suffering — no withdrawal, no distress, sometimes what looks like amusement. I know the objection. What I want is a proper map of what my view risks, not a rehearsal of that one case.

So: what would count against intrinsicism? I should like the following, in this order.

First, the observations, arguments, intuitions and possible cases that tell against it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with the reasons for the ranking made explicit — why one defeater is decisive and another is a discomfort the view can absorb.

Second, which of these an intrinsicist should concede as genuine costs and which they may legitimately dismiss, and on what grounds. In particular, tell me whether the asymbolic cases are best read as (a) straightforward counterexamples, (b) cases of pain only in a diminished or analogical sense, or (c) cases in which the badness is present but the patient's report and behaviour are not to be trusted. Do not assume the third is a mere evasion; say what would make it respectable and what would expose it as ad hoc.

Third, whether intrinsicism is committed to any prediction its rivals are not — something we could in principle go and check, in the clinic or in the laboratory — and what result would embarrass it most.

Fourth, whether the view has been insulated from refutation by quiet qualification, and if so whether that is a defect in a view of this kind or a sign that it is being asked the wrong question. I want you to be willing to conclude that it is well insulated, and to say plainly whether that troubles it.

I have also been struck by a distinction that I have not seen drawn in quite these terms, and I want you to work with it rather than around it: between the aversiveness of a pain (its tendency to elicit withdrawal, its motivational pull) and what I shall call its hedonic gloss (the felt badness that seems to qualify the sensation itself rather than to be a reaction to it). The question I care about is whether the gloss is a third thing over and above the sensory content and the aversion, or whether it collapses into one of them; and whether any of the clinical dissociations bear on that at all.

To press the point, take the following constructed pair. Suppose a drug is found that leaves the sensory-discriminative profile of pain untouched — same localisation, same intensity ratings, same autonomic signature — while abolishing aversion and any subsequent avoidance learning; subjects on it describe the experience as intense but not at all bad, and, pressed, say not that it is neutral either, but that it has a quality they cannot place. Suppose a second drug does the reverse: it abolishes the sensory-discriminative profile while leaving subjects reporting that something is wrong or awful somewhere, though they cannot say where or how intense it is. Tell me which of these, if either, is the true mirror-image of asymbolia, what each would show about the separability of the gloss, and — importantly — what neither could show, however clean the result looked. I am not asking you to adjudicate whether such drugs are likely; I am asking what the cases would establish if they obtained.

I am most moved by arguments from what pain explains — its role in learning, in avoidance, and in giving a creature a reason to stop — so if the strongest defeaters are of that kind, say so, and tell me whether intrinsicism can survive them by relocating the badness somewhere I have not yet looked.

Finish with your own verdict: how exposed the view really is, what would settle the matter, and which single case or result would tell most against it. Write in British English.
