Compare two theses that are often conflated in discussions of the necessary a posteriori. The first is rigid designation: a term is rigid when it designates the same object with respect to every possible world in which it designates anything at all. The second is essentialism: an object has a property essentially when it could not have existed without that property. I want to know whether these two are independent, or whether one is doing all the explanatory work. Take a name introduced for whatever it is that produces a certain observable effect, before anyone knows what kind of thing produces it, and suppose later inquiry identifies the underlying structure. Work through what each thesis says about the modal status of the identity statement that results — is its necessity owed to the rigidity of the name, to the essence of the structure, or to both — then construct a case in which the two theses deliver different verdicts about necessity. End by saying which thesis carries the explanatory load in the standard examples, and whether either could be true while the other is false.
