I'm a novelist, about 60k words into a book where a man starts a small end-of-the-world cult and turns out (sort of) to be right, and the whole thing hinges on a bit of probabilistic reasoning I've never quite shaken off. Before I build the ending on it I want a consistancy check on my own views, because if they don't hang together I'd rather find out now than in a second draft.

Here's roughly where I've landed.

(1) When I'm in doubt about where I am in the world — which of the many possible observers I turned out to be — I should reason as if I were a random one, drawn fairly from all the observers there are or ever will be. It's the only thing stopping me from thinking I'm special.

(2) Sleeping Beauty: I'm a thirder, all day long. When she wakes up not knowing whether it's Monday or Tuesday, a third is the right answer.

(3) The Doomsday argument is rubbish. The version I mean is: if I'm a random human being among all the human beings who'll ever live, I'm probably somewhere in the middle of the queue, so there probably aren't many of us still to come. Don't buy it.

(4) What kills it, for me, is the reference class. Am I a random human? A random mammal? A random conscious creature anywhere in the universe? A random moment of experience? Choose differently and you get a different answer, so the whole thing's rigged from the start.

(5) I'm perfectly happy for an argument to be sound and to have no bearing at all on what I do on Tuesday. 'Yes but what should we do about it' isn't a real objection to a claim about what's probably true.

(6) And I do think the bare fact that I exist is evidence about how many observers there are — the more of us there are, the better my chances of being one of them. That feels obviously right to me.

So where's the fight? (2) and (1) feel like they're on the same side, (4) feels like it's doing the work against (3), and (5) is just me being sensible. But I've a nagging feeling I'm helping myself to something. Rank the tensions by how bad they are, tell me which ones are only apparent and why, and if something has to go, tell me what's cheapest to drop and what I lose by dropping it. Audit, not a lecture — and no reading lists, I'm not going to look anyone up, I just want to know whether the thing stands up.
