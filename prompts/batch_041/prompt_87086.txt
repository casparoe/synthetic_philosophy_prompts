Every time my wife's family gets together her brother corners me with the same physics thing and I never know what to say back. His spiel: Einstein killed absolute simultaneity, so whether two distant events count as happening at the same time depends on who's asking and how fast they're moving. For someone on a fast enough ship, a star blowing up a million light years away is already old news, while for me it hasn't happened yet — and neither of us is wrong. From there he concludes there's no real now anywhere except where you're standing, so the past and the future are just as real as the present, nothing is ever actually gone, and everything that will happen is already there. He thinks this is basically settled physics and that anyone who says the past is over is just being sentimental. What bugs me is how fast he goes from us disagreeing about what's simultaneous to the future already existing — I can't tell if that's a real step or a sleight of hand. My sister-in-law rolls her eyes and says its fatalism with extra steps. Who's right here? Is his argument as airtight as he makes it sound, and if it is, does it actually change anything about how I should live, or is this one of those cases where the physics is real but the conclusion is doing something sneaky? I'd definately like a decent reply for next Thanksgiving that doesn't require tensor calculus.
