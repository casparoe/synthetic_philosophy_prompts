I spent 35 years as a field ecologist and only lately got into philosophy, so go easy on the jargon. I keep a running list of cases where I think we should or shouldn't step in on wild animals, and I can't get them to line up under one rule. Fawn trapped in a wildfire started by humans: pull it out, obviously fine. Same fawn stuck in a ravine it fell into on its own: still fine, I'd haul it out. A tapeworm that'll blind a wolf: deworm the wolf, no hesitation. A cat toying with a mouse in my yard: I shoo the cat off, fine. But a hawk carrying off a squirrel: I don't feel I should interfere, and honestly it feels a bit wrong to. Deer starving in a hard winter: I'd put out feed, that feels fine. Culling deer so the herd doesn't starve: I'm against it, feels like playing god even though it might cut total suffering. And the one that really gets me: a virus sweeping through a rabbit population, I'd vaccinate, no problem, but I wouldn't want to engineer the virus out of existence or turn predators into vegetarians, that feels like breaking something.

I noticed I've been telling myself the good cases are where I remove suffering that isn't load-bearing in the ecosystem, and the bad ones are where I mess with suffering that is, but the tapeworm is obviously load-bearing for the tapeworm's life cycle and I still say yank it, so that's shot. I've got alot of these little cases and no principle. Is there one that gets most of them, or a couple of competing candidates? Tell me which of my verdicts I'd have to give up to get a clean rule, and what the rule says about a case I haven't thought of -- say, quietly feeding a fox that's starving while its prey population crashes.
