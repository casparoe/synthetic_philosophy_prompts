Some friends and I have been playing a long-running game -- we call it Nomic, the kind where the players change the rules by voting, and the rules govern how they get changed -- and our house rules have drifted so far from any published version that I can't just look up the answer anymore. Our referee is a friend, Dee, and she's ruled on every dispute so far; noone has ever overruled her. We vote on a new proposal Sunday and I want to know what she'll say, and honestly whether she has any principled way of deciding it.

The record, as best I can reconstruct it:

- Y3: proposal raising the threshold for changing a rule from a simple majority to two-thirds. Passed with 58%. Dee upheld it, saying the rule in force at the time of the vote is what governs, and that was simple majority.
- Y5: proposal lowering it back to simple majority. Passed with 71%. Upheld, same reasoning.
- Y6: proposal raising it to two-thirds again. Passed with 80%. Upheld.
- Y8: proposal adding rule 7, which reads "rule 7 may never be amended or repealed." Passed with 74%. Dee upheld it, and said in passing that it now protects itself.
- Y9: proposal repealing rule 7. Passed with 61%, i.e. short of two-thirds. Struck down, on the ground that it didn't clear the current amendment threshold. She said nothing about whether it would have been good at two-thirds.
- Y11: proposal repealing the rule that gave Dee her power to rule on disputes. Passed with 70%. Dee upheld the repeal, observed that she could therefore only rule on disputes arising before the vote, and then kept ruling on those. Nobody objected.
- Y12: proposal changing the winning condition from 200 points to 100, adding that it applied "to the game as it's been played." Upheld, because we all agreed it was still the same game.

Sunday's proposal: repeal rule 7 by the ordinary procedure, which is two-thirds. It gets 70%, so it clears the bar. It also contains a clause saying that notwithstanding rule 7, this repeal takes effect immediately, and that any ruling holding it invalid is void and of no effect.

So what does Dee rule? Lay out the rules her record is jointly consistent with -- I can see at least a hard-line entrenchment reading and a boring "the current procedure is the only constraint" reading -- say which one I should bet on and why, and name the future proposal that would actually settle it. And if both readings are genuinely open, tell me what principle we'd be adopting as a group either way, since we're going to have to live with the precedent.
