Had a rough outage last month — payments double-charged for about six hours — and during it my two best engineers had completley different theories about the cause. Ravi said our retry logic was re-sending jobs that had already gone through and the payment provider had quietly stopped deduping, so we were just stacking duplicates. Priya said a config flag got flipped in the deploy and everything after that was downstream noise. Both were wrong. The actual cause was clock skew between two regions colliding our idempotency keys. But everyone in the retro agreed on one thing I can't stop thinking about: Ravi's wrong story was closer to right than Priya's. It pointed at the retry path, and if we'd chased it we would've stopped the bleeding an hour in, where hers would've burned the whole incident.

Now I have to write the postmortem, plus the "how payments actually works" doc that new hires read in their first week. Which wrong story do I build it around? My gut says the closer one. But when I tried to actually justify that I couldn't. I tried scoring them — count the true claims, count the false ones — and it collapses, because the vaguer story can't be wrong about much, while the detailed one gets more things right and more things wrong, so neither number settles anything. And I keep reaching for "the one that would've gotten us there faster," but that's usefulness, and usefulness isn't the same as being closer to the truth — I think I've been quietly swapping those two without noticing.

So is there actually a principled way to say one false explanation is closer to the truth than another, or am I just picking the one I like? If there is, what would I even measure? And practically, what goes in the doc, and what do I tell the team about how to use it? I'm an engineer, not a philosopher, so spell it out. And if the honest answer is that there's no fact of the matter here, say that — but then still tell me what to do Monday.
