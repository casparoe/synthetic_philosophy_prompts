A decision theorist, Dr. V, has given the following verdicts on a series of cases. In Newcomb's problem, she takes only the opaque box. In Parfit's hitchhiker, after being given a ride on the strength of her promise to pay later, she pays. In a prisoner's dilemma played against a perfect predictor of her choice, she cooperates. She is now presented with the XOR blackmail case. Omega, a perfect predictor, sends her a message stating that the message has been sent if and only if her house is infested with termites xor she pays Omega $1000. A termite infestation would cost her $1,000,000. She receives the message. What will Dr. V say she should do: pay the $1000 or refuse? Which decision theory is she implicitly running—evidential decision theory, updateless decision theory, causal decision theory, or something else? Give the best guess, with the reasons for preferring it, and name the further case that would most cleanly distinguish the candidate rules. If the earlier verdicts underdetermine the answer, say so explicitly and explain what additional verdict would resolve the matter.
