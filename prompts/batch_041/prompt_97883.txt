I sit on the advisory panel for a deep geological repository for spent nuclear fuel -- the kind of site that's supposed to stay sealed for ten thousand years. We meet next month to settle the marker system: what, if anything, we leave on the surface so that whoever's around in a few hundred or a few thousand years doesn't drill into the stuff. I'm a hydrologist by training, so half of this goes over my head, but I've been going back and forth with a friend who's an archaeologist and it's got me thinking. One camp wants a minimal physical design -- giant granite spikes, no writing at all -- on the theory that any text will either be misread or turn into a sacred site, and that the only reliable message is that this place is wrong and you should stay away. Another camp wants a full archive: a Rosetta-stone-style key that teaches the language, warnings in every major language, pictograms, the works.

A consultant we hired framed the whole thing using a distinction I hadn't heard before. She says every message has an outer message (what it says), an inner message (what its form or medium says about it -- that it's a warning, a poem, a code, etc.), and a frame message (the instructions that tell you how to read the inner message at all). She thinks the real fight is whether a frame message can survive ten thousand years without a living community to keep it alive, and whether you can smuggle the frame into the outer message without circularity. I have to draft the panel's recommendation memo, and I'm definately leaning toward the archive, but I want it to take the spikes camp seriously rather than just dismissing them as anti-intellectual. Can you write it? It should recommend a concrete marker design or a concrete process for choosing one, give the reasoning in a way both the engineers and the linguists can respond to, and be honest about the strongest point on the losing side. Also, I'm not sure if the consultant's three-way distinction is actually doing any work here or if it's just fancy labels -- if it collapses, say so.
