So this happened at my aunt's place over the holidays and I've been turning it over ever since. My cousin and my uncle got into it about whether brain size has any moral relevance, and neither of them would let it go. I stayed out of it and I'm honestly not sure who was winning, so I want you to actually referee it. Here's more or less how it went.

(1) Cousin: "Moral status has to rest on something real, and the only candidate that survives is the capacity to have experiences and to have things go well or badly for you. That capacity is produced by a nervous system. So how much nervous system there is is at least *evidence* about how much there is that can matter morally. That's the whole reason a lobster doesn't get the vote."

(2) Uncle: "Two problems. First, 'how much nervous system' is a terrible measure — it's confounded by body size, by what the tissue is doing, all of it. A sperm whale's brain is enormous and nobody thinks it's five humans' worth of moral patient. Second, and worse, even if it tracked intelligence perfectly, intelligence isn't what grounds moral status. What grounds it is sentience — the capacity to feel. And nothing says sentience scales with processing power. You can imagine something very clever that feels nothing at all, and something dumb as a rock that suffers horribly."

(3) Cousin: "The zombie thing is a thought experiment about what's conceivable, fine, but I can't test it, and in the animals we actually know, more brain goes with more flexible behavior and more of what looks like a rich inner life. Corvids and octopuses are the counterexamples everyone reaches for, and look at what they actually show: not that a small brain can be smart with the same architecture, but that you have to look at organization and density, not raw size. That's a refinement, not a refutation. I'll happily swap 'size' for 'the relevant neural complexity.'"

(4) Uncle: "Then you've given up the only thing that made the claim interesting. Nobody can measure 'relevant neural complexity,' so now it's unfalsifiable, and meanwhile 'brain size' keeps doing all the cultural work it's always done — ranking races, ranking men over women, ranking disabled people as lesser. And if you really take it seriously you get results nobody will accept. The infant. The person in a persistent vegetative state. The kid with severe microcephaly who's happy and loves her brother. You going to tell me she counts for less? That's your reductio right there."

(5) Cousin: "That's two different moves and only one of them lands. 'This measure has been abused by racists' is the genetic fallacy — abuse doesn't tell us whether it's tracking anything. And the marginal cases cut my way: everyone already agrees that a brain which has permanently lost the capacity to experience counts for less, which is exactly why we're allowed to withdraw life support. If you accept *that*, you've already accepted that some fact about the brain grounds moral status. You just don't like it when the fact turns out to be size."

(6) Uncle: "That's a bait and switch. 'This particular brain has lost the capacity' and 'this brain is smaller than that one' are not the same fact. I'll grant you the first and deny you the second, and nothing about granting the first forces me to grant the second."
