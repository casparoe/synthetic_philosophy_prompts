Compose a short joke about the moral difference between lying and merely misleading. It should turn on the distinction between deceiving someone through what you assert and deceiving them through what you merely implicate, and then add a further twist by having the deception ride on a presupposition rather than an implicature — the speaker's utterance is true and what it asserts is true, yet a falsehood is smuggled in as background the sentence simply takes for granted. The characters may be invented, but the mechanism has to be one a philosopher of language would recognize as a real case of misleading-without-lying, and the humor should come from that mechanism rather than from a generic quip about lawyers or politicians; keep the whole thing brief. End with a single line beginning "Target:" followed by a short phrase naming the precise distinction the joke exploits.
