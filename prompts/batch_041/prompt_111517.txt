I am a high school student doing an independent study project on the history of science, and I have spent the last few weeks reading about Johannes Kepler's astrology — not the famous laws of planetary motion, but the part where he insists he is reforming astrology rather than abandoning it. I would like to practice defending his position against a serious objection, so please play Kepler himself: not a caricature, not a modern commentator summarizing him from the outside, but the man in Prague in the early 1610s, speaking in the first person, with the commitments he actually held. Stay in character until I say stop. You may concede minor points, and you may ask me a clarifying question if I have been unclear, but you must not abandon the central claim that the aspects between planets are the real and defensible core of astrology, grounded in the harmonic or geometrical structure of the world and its action on the soul, while the twelve signs and the twelve houses are either arbitrary or at best secondary. Write in a single substantial paragraph per turn, as if we were talking across a table, and do not step outside the role to explain what Kepler really meant.

My opening move is this. You keep the aspects and throw away the houses and the signs, and you tell me the reason is that a trine or a square is a real geometrical relationship while a house cusp is a mere convention. But the twelve signs are also geometrical — they are equal divisions of the same ecliptic circle along which the aspects are measured — and the houses, however conventional their starting point, are also divisions of that same circle. If the soul responds to configuration and harmony, why should it respond to a threefold or a fourfold division of the circle and not to a twelvefold one? It looks as though you have kept the parts of astrology that happen to fit your musical ratios and discarded the parts that do not, and then called the result reformed rather than merely selected. And a second worry, which I would like you to answer in the same breath: if the soul is free and can resist the stars, then whenever your predictions fail you can say the soul resisted, and whenever they succeed you can say the harmony was felt. What observation could ever count against your astrology, and if none could, in what sense is it knowledge rather than a system of interpretation? Please begin as Kepler, in character, and answer both halves.
