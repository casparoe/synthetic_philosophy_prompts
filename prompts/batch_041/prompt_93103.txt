I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy training, and I've got a fight between two friends that I definately can't figure out how to settle. Here's the situation.

Our city council passed an ordinence in March making it illegal to sit or lie down on the sidewalks in the downtown business district during the day. On paper it's about everyone; in practice everybody knows it's about the homeless guys who hang around the transit station. $250 fine, and it's enforced however the cops feel like it that day. I voted for the councilwoman who pushed it through -- mostly for other reasons, I honestly never read the fine print -- and now that I see it in action I think it's cruel and pointless.

So I decided I'd go sit on the sidewalk downtown every Saturday with a few friends, right where the cops patrol, and just eat the tickets. My friend Dana thinks I'm being a drama queen. She says three things: that the whole idea you owe the law your obedience just because it's the law is made up, so I shouldn't act like getting a ticket is some noble sacrifice; that if I'm going to break it I should do it quietly and not get caught, since the fines just fund the thing I'm protesting; and that accepting the punishment is a martyr complex. My other friend Rob says the exact opposite -- that if you break a law and then dodge the penalty you're just a criminal and nobody has to listen to you.

I can't tell who's right and I don't want a follow-your-conscience answer. What I want is the actual structure underneath this. Is there really no duty to obey a law just because it's a law, or is Dana wrong about that? Does it matter that I voted for this thing? Does it matter that the people the law is really aimed at aren't me -- like, am I making it about myself by getting arrested over it? And does it actually matter whether I take the fine or slip away, or is that just style? There's also a repeal on the ballot in November, so a legal path exists -- does that change whether I should be doing this at all?
