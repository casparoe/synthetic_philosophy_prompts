For years I have watched a dispute in probabilistic modeling go nowhere, and I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. The question is whether there is such a thing as genuinely uncertain evidence — a case in which my credence in a proposition shifts without my becoming certain of anything — or whether every such case is really ordinary conditioning on a certain proposition about my observation, my experience, or the data. The first camp mixes the hypothesis's conditional probabilities over the evidence proposition using my new credence in it; the second conditions on the certain fact of what I observed, with a likelihood linking that fact to the hypothesis. I have built a small case that separates them — I observe a noisy indicator and become 0.7 confident it reads blue, and conditioning on the observation with its likelihood and mixing with the 0.7/0.3 weights give me different posteriors — and I cannot see that either side has simply erred, which is what puzzles me. My hypothesis is that the impasse is less about which posterior is correct than about what may count as evidence and about an unargued formal commitment to holding the hypothesis's conditional probabilities given the evidence fixed, but I cannot tell whether that makes the dispute verbal or merely relocates it. Diagnose it: is it verbal, substantive, a clash over which cases count as evidence, or a sign that the question is ill-posed — and, given that diagnosis, what would count as progress, whether a formal result, a distinction, or a concession about the case, or why none should be expected.
