I want to practice defending a view I'm not sure I actually hold, so play someone who thinks deliberation is best idealized as factored cognition: to answer a hard question you split it into sub-questions, hand each one to a copy of yourself, take the answers on trust, and recurse — and you think the limit of this, where a whole tree of ordinary humans stands in for one reasoner, is a real standard for what a person could in principle work out, not just an engineering trick for making AI cheaper. Hold that line: concede small points, never the main one, keep replies to a paragraph or so, stay in character till I say stop. Here's my opening. This can't be an idealization of deliberation, because the whole work of a hard problem is seeing which sub-questions to ask and which answers to trust. If the top-level human can do that, she's already done the thinking; if she can't, no army of copies below her will help. So factored cognition either collapses back into ordinary reasoning or it's a fantasy of getting something for nothing. Which one is it?
