I'm helping run a reading group that has spent a few sessions on HCH and factored cognition -- the picture in which a question is answered by having a human decompose it into subquestions, each farmed out to another instance of the same procedure, until the questions are small enough that someone can simply answer them, with the whole arrangement idealized as the limit of that recursion. We keep noticing how much quiet work the idealization does, and we would like a piece of satire whose target is the idealization itself rather than any particular researcher or lab.

Please write an excerpt from the internal operations handbook of a mid-sized firm that has adopted HCH as its official decision procedure, in the flat, unembarrassed register of a genuine employee handbook: numbered sections, a flowchart rendered in prose, an FAQ, a short section on the appeals process, and a closing note on the transition period. The comedy should come from the machinery rather than from wackiness, and it should get the details right enough that someone who knows the idealization well laughs rather than winces. In particular, let the handbook be serenely confident about the base case (someone, somewhere, must simply answer the leaf questions, and must answer them well); about the stipulation that every human at every node decomposes the question faithfully and then aggregates the returned answers faithfully; about the fact that the procedure is defined by its limit while every actual run of it is finite, budgeted, and staffed by people with other meetings; about the assumption that a difficult question factors into mostly independent easy ones; and about the resulting inability to ever check a decomposition against the question it was supposed to preserve. Include an FAQ item on what to do when a subquestion turns out to depend on the answer to a sibling subquestion, one on how the firm's actual practice relates to the idealized version, and one on what the appeals process can and cannot review. Roughly 800 words, deadpan throughout, so that a reader can tell what is being mocked without the text ever winking. Any quotations should be attributed to invented in-universe figures -- a regional manager, a departing analyst, an outside auditor -- never to real texts or real people. The piece should be legible to someone with only a rough sense of the idea, but a reader who knows HCH and factored cognition should find that it neither cheats nor overexplains.
