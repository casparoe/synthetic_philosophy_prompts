I have been trying to work out what I think makes a decision to stop searching rational, and my verdicts on cases will not line up as neatly as I would like.

Picking a cereal: I take the first box that is on sale and looks familiar, though I could scan the whole aisle at no real cost. I count this as perfectly rational. Choosing a bathroom paint colour: I stop at the first swatch that seems fine, out of thirty, again with browsing nearly free. Also rational. Apartment hunting: I view eight of forty, find one that meets every criterion I set in advance, and stop because each further viewing costs me an evening. Rational. Hiring: the committee stops at the first candidate who clears the bar, even though further interviews are cheap and a better candidate may be next in line. Rational. Surgeon: I take the first in-network surgeon listed without checking outcomes, though a page of public data is one click away. Not rational. Firm: it ships a design that clears its own threshold although a single cheap test would probably have revealed a much better design, and it skips the test. Not rational. Weary renter: after eight viewings with nothing acceptable, she signs a lease that fails two of her own stated requirements because she cannot face more viewings. Not rational. Partner: I marry the first person who meets my list of requirements, having met almost no one else. I want to say this is not irrational, but it feels unlike the cereal case and I cannot say why.

The only principle I can state is that stopping is rational when the expected gain from further search is smaller than what the search would cost, but that seems to make my cereal and paint verdicts wrong, so I suspect it is either incomplete or not the real principle at all.

Please tell me what principle, or smallest set of principles, actually delivers these verdicts. Two things I cannot reconcile: in the cereal and paint cases I think stopping is rational even though further search is nearly free, while in the surgeon and firm cases cheap search seems to make stopping irrational; and I treat the apartment and hiring cases as rational but the weary renter as not, though all three stop for want of further effort. Say what the principle predicts for a case I have not listed, such as a voter who settles on the first candidate who seems acceptable and stops reading, when the stakes are high and the reading is tedious, and which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon if it cannot have them all.
