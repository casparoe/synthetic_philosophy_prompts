I'm trying to figure out what I actually think emotions are, by going on gut reactions to cases. House spider on the desk: I know it's harmless, my heart is pounding, and I'm afraid, genuinly afraid, not just having a weird bodily moment. Roller coaster: I know I'm safe, I'm screaming and grinning, and I don't think that's fear, even though it feels a lot like it. Free-floating dread on a Sunday afternoon with nothing in particular I'm dreading: that's an emotion, and it's anxiety specifically. Room full of people panicking and I catch it without believing anything's wrong: I'm afraid, briefly. Horror movie: I'm scared, and I don't want to call it fake fear, but it isn't the same thing as the spider either. Friend three days into a bad diagnosis, flat, says he feels nothing, but he flinches at noises and snaps at people: I want to say the fear is in there somewhere, just not felt. And someone on a drug that produces the whole bodily pattern of fear, with nothing he's afraid of: I flip-flop — half of me says that's just a body state, half says if it feels like fear then it's fear.

My half-theroy was: emotion = a feeling, plus an appraisal of something as mattering, plus a pull toward action, all three. The spider case seems to kill that, unless the appraisal can be implicit and unconscious — but then the roller coaster should come out as fear too, and so should the drug case, and neither does.

So is there one principle that gets the spider, the roller coaster, the movie, the contagious panic, and the unfelt fear? What does it say about the drug case, and about fear in someone having a nightmare? And which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? I don't care whether you end up calling emotions judgments or perceptions or feelings, I want the principle.
