Construct a case that makes the inefficacy objection as sharp as it can be made. Specifications: it must be an ordinary consumption or workplace decision, not a trolley problem and not science fiction; the mechanism must be stipulated explicitly and must make the individual's act make strictly no difference to the outcome — not an imperceptible difference and not a tiny probability, but no difference at all, and this must be visible in the mechanism rather than asserted; the harm must be serious and its victims concrete enough that we cannot wave the case away as diffuse; the actor must be fully informed, uncoerced, and in no special position (she is not the last to join, not pivotal, not a ringleader), and no small group containing her may be pivotal either; and it must still be true that the practice would not survive if everyone acted as she does, with the case making plain that this is a fact about a set of acts rather than about hers. The case is for isolating whether the wrongness of contributing to a collective harm can rest on participation in a practice rather than on making a difference, so it fails if the verdict is secured by ignorance, coercion, or unreal victims.

After the scenario (one paragraph), give the verdict and the reasoning for each of: a difference-making account; a probability-raising or expected-value account; and an account on which the wrongness is expressive or consists in participation in a wrongful practice. Then say which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you remove it in each direction — first making the contribution marginal but imperceptible, then making it pivotal — and whether the verdict changes, and how a defender of the difference-making account would attack the case. If the specifications cannot all be met at once, say which one blocks it.
