I've been running a little agent-based model and its got me worried about Lewis's account. The setup: 200 agents play a coordination game with two symmetric equilibria, say left-left and right-right. They have no theory of mind, no recursive beliefs, no representation of anyone else's expectations. Each just uses a simple reinforcement rule: repeat what worked, switch occasionally. Over a few thousand rounds the population locks onto one equilibrium. The other equilibrium was just as good; the lock-in is arbitrary. Deviating is costly because you meet a wall of people going the other way. There's no common knowledge in Lewis's sense -- no one knows that anyone knows that anyone knows. There's not even a conditional preference to conform; they just do what paid off. Yet the thing looks like a convention: it's arbitrary, it coordinates, it's self-sustaining, it has normative force (you'd be a fool to drive on the other side). So: on Lewis's account, is this a convention or not? If it's not, then common knowledge and conditional preference aren't necessary for the phenomenon we care about, and Lewis's analysis is either too narrow or is analyzing something else. If it is, then those conditions are doing no work, and any stable equilibrium regularity counts. Which is it?
