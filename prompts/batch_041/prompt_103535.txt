I chair the bylaws committee of a small resident-owned housing cooperative, and I need advice on a procedural question that has turned into a dispute about something deeper. The building has forty units. Some owners live in their units; others rent theirs out and live elsewhere. Among the residents are long-term renters who have lived here for a decade, a live-in aide to an elderly owner, adult children who have moved back home, and the owner of a ground-floor café whose lease runs through the building. We are about to vote on replacing the elevator and adding an accessibility ramp. The project requires a special assessment on owners, and it will close the café’s entrance for months. Our bylaws give one vote per unit to owners, and they can be amended only by a two-thirds vote of unit-owners. So the people excluded by the current rule cannot vote to change the rule that excludes them. The board is split on whether we may even put the question to a broader group without first amending the bylaws. One faction says only owners should vote because they pay and carry the risk. Another says anyone who lives here should vote because they live with the result. The café owner wants a vote because the construction will affect his business. Residents of the neighboring building say the ramp will change the shared courtyard and want in. One board member proposes a stakeholder assembly: owners, renters, the café, and a neighbor, with votes weighted by how long each has been affected. Another proposes a boundary jury of twelve people chosen by lot from everyone within a block, to decide who should be eligible to vote in future decisions. I have to recommend a process. Who should be included in this vote, and on what principle? Should non-owners get a binding vote, an advisory vote, or a separate track? How should we treat people who will be affected later—future owners, people on the waitlist to buy in, and whoever replaces the aide if she leaves? And how do we break the circularity without assuming the very boundary we are arguing about? Please lead with your bottom line, then explain, in no more than 650 words.
