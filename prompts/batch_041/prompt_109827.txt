Two members of my reading group spent most of last week arguing about a case I had described: a glass packed in foam, so that if you dropped it, it would not break. He said the glass is not fragile while it sits in the foam — or at any rate the ordinary conditional 'if dropped, it would break' is simply false of it, so the analysis of fragility as that conditional must be wrong, or else fragility must come and go with the packaging. She said that is absurd: the foam does not make the glass any less fragile, it only keeps the fragility from showing itself, so the conditional needs a proviso — if dropped, and nothing prevents it from breaking — and once you add that, the analysis is fine and the glass is fragile throughout. He replied that the proviso is either vacuous or circular: 'nothing prevents it from breaking' just means 'it would break,' and if instead she means 'no external interference,' then interference has to be spelled out in terms of breaking, which is precisely what is being analyzed. She said he is running together two different things — a wire that melts the instant you touch it to a conductor (a finkish case, where the trigger removes the disposition) and a glass in foam (a masking case, where it merely blocks the manifestation) — and that once you see the difference, the proviso can be given a non-circular reading. He said the distinction does no work, since in both cases what makes the counterfactual false is a fact about the whole situation rather than about the glass, and her analysis keeps appealing to the very disposition it is supposed to reduce. Neither would move. Please adjudicate move by move: is the foam case a genuine counterexample to the conditional analysis, is the fink-versus-mask distinction principled, can the proviso be stated without circularity or vacuity, and does either of them end up with the better account of what a disposition is? Rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say what each party is missing, and flag how confident you are in each part of the verdict. If the question cannot be settled without first resolving something I have left unstated, or if my framing of the cases is itself confused, say so plainly rather than working within it.
