Im putting together a little reading-group handout on teleosemantics and the naturalizing-content stuff, and this time I want a dialogue instead of an essay. Here's the setup I want you to use.

There's a robot — lets call it a forager — that a lab trained with reinforcement learning to find food in a big batch of simulated rooms. After training, one of its internal units fires reliably whenever food is sitting in front of it, and the forager turns toward it. Now the lab drops it into a new room where a shiny red object that isn't food is sitting in front of it. The unit fires, the forager goes for it, and it fails (in the sim, it just sits there and "starves"). I want a dialogue between two people arguing about whether that internal unit represents food at all, and if it does, whether the red-object case is a genuine misrepresentation or just a disposition firing in the wrong place.

Character one is a card-carrying teleosemanticist in the Millikan-ish biosemantics tradition: content is fixed by proper function, and proper function comes from a history of selection. She thinks the training run counts as a selection process, that the unit has a derived proper function to indicate food, and so the red-object case really is a misrepresentation — the unit is doing its job badly, not just doing something else.

Character two is a skeptic about all of this. Her line is that teleosemantics needs an actual lineage — reproduction, inheritance, variation, the whole Darwinian package — and that a single training run is just learning, which gives you a discriminative disposition and nothing more. So the unit has no content, and calling its red-object firing a "misrepresentation" is us projecting. She'll also press the slippery slope: if a training run is a selection process, then whats not one? A thermostat, a lock and key, a phototropic plant, the immune system? And she's happy to bite the bullet that the forager is in the same boat as all of those.

I want them to actually go at it, not trade monologues. In particular: character one should have a real, non-hand-wavy answer to "what isn't a selection process" — I don't want her to just assert that training counts and move on. And character two should have a real answer to "then what makes your own head any different from the forager's," since she presumably thinks she has content. Let each of them land a genuine hit on the other and let each of them concede something small when the other earns it. It can end unresolved if thats honestly where it lands, but please don't let either one turn into a strawman — no "so you see, science proves there's no such thing as meaning" speeches from the skeptic, and no "well, evolution just is a selection process, problem solved" from the teleosemanticist. Keep it around 700–900 words, and have them argue about the forager specifically rather than reciting the general positions at each other.
