So I've been trying to work out how much of my income I'm actually on the hook to give away, and I've ended up using my friend Priya as an oracle, because she went deep into effective altruism last year and now has these very confident verdicts about rescue-type cases. The problem is I can't tell what rule she's actually running. Her verdicts seem to fit a couple of different stories, and I keep noticing I reach for whichever one convieniently gets me off the hook. Here's everything I've managed to pin her down on, hashed out over like three dinners:

- The classic pond case — you walk past a shallow pond, a toddler's drowning, you can wade in and save her but you'll wreck your phone and shoes, call it $300: she says you're just flatly required, no debate.
- You can instead give that same $300 to a charity that sponsors one specific named child and will basically certainly keep that child alive: required.
- Same single-child sponsorship, but it costs $5,000, which for me is like a month and a half of take-home pay: still required, she doesn't even blink.
- You send $300 to a trachoma charity that isn't about any one person, it just keeps a handful of people from going blind: she says that's lovely but not required, you're allowed to skip it.
- You can prevent one statistical death through some big anonymous charity, but it costs your entire $60,000 in savings: not required, she says that's past the call of duty.
- $300 to a charity that stops a bunch of kids from getting a serious but non-fatal illness: not required either.

So here's the case I actually care about, and I suspect it's the one that'll finally nail her down. There's a specific person, a total stranger, but I know her name and I'm staring at her face on a crowdfunding page, and she'll go permanently blind unless she gets a $300 surgery I could just pay for out of pocket. One identifiable person, cheap, but not life-or-death. I genuinely can't tell whether Priya's going to say I'm required to pay or just that it'd be a nice thing to do. Tell me what she'll say — though honestly I care less about the yes/no than about which rule she's actually running, what the other rules that fit all six of her verdicts would predict for this case, which of them I should actually bet on and why, and what single further case would cleanly split them apart if they're still tied.
