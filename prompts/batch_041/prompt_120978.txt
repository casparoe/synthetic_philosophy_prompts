I'm teaching King Lear in a seminar on aging, and I want to pair it with Cicero's De Senectute -- the dialogue where Cato the Elder, in his eighties, goes through the four standard complaints about old age (it pulls you out of public life, it weakens the body, it takes away the pleasures, it puts you within sight of death) and says they're all mistaken, mainly because they're complaints about character, not about age. The obvious pairing is Cato the serene Stoic against Lear the howling counterexample, and I dont want the obvious pairing. Here's whats nagging me. Early on, Laelius asks Cato whether old age only looks bearable to him because he has money, property, and standing -- and Cato says, more or less, that yes, that helps, but that's not everything, then tells an anecdote about Themistocles being taunted by some nobody from a small island. That's the one moment where the treatise almost admits what I suspect: every consolation in it is paid for out of an estate that the old man still owns. Lear looks like the experiment where you take the estate away and leave the title. So read the play against the treatise and tell me whether Cato's argument survives it -- and be honest about where my hunch distorts the play, because Kent and Gloucester are old too and they don't seem ruined by it. Push on the seasons argument, where Cato says resenting that your youth is gone is like a farmer resenting that summer arrived; on the storm, and what it does to the idea that old age is natural and so acceptable; on the fact that Cato's old age is a solo project while Lear's is entirely about children and the handing over of power; and on the bit near the end where Cato says an actor doesnt have to stay on stage until the end of the play to deserve the applause. Does the play let any of that stand? And I've been going back and forth on how the play treats it's old men besides Lear -- is that evidence against Cato or for him? End with what the play can show about aging that an argument like Cato's can't reach, and if the play also exposes something wrong in my framing, say so.
