I'm the person at my company who has to sign off on anything that scores human beings — credit models mostly, but also a pilot that ranks which delinquent accounts our collections team calls first, and there's a benefits-style thing coming down the pipe that I'm dreading. So roughly one proposal a month lands on my desk, I get about two hours with it, and then I sign my name next to legal's, which means if it goes wrong it was mine.

I've been reading the rat-adjacent corner of the internet for like fifteen years, so I know the shape of the fairness literature without being able to do anything with it. I know the impossibility results aren't a gotcha: you can't have a score that's calibrated within groups and also have equal false-positive and false-negative rates across groups when the base rates differ, and depending on which pair of properties you demand you hit the same squeeze wearing different clothes. I also know the follow-up work where bolting a fairness constraint onto a deployed model makes the group you were trying to help worse off down the line, because the constraint changes who gets flagged, that changes the outcomes, and the next model trains on the changed outcomes.

What I don't have is anything I can actually run. Right now it's vibes plus whatever paper I read last, and I'm sick of it.

Turn this into a procedure my team can run on a proposal we didn't design. I want the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the places where the procedure stops and returns a no, and the places where it hands the decision to a human because the thing being decided is a value and not a fact. Flag the steps that are just judgment wearing a procedure costume.

Stuff it has to handle, because these are the ones that wreck me: we often can't legally record the protected attribute, or the subgroup is forty people; the interesting cases are intersectional and the squeeze bites harder there; the label is never the thing we care about but a proxy for it; and everything feeds back.

The output should be concrete. I want a one-page ledger with a column for the criterion, who it protects, who pays for it, what we'd have to believe for that to be OK, and the sentence we'd have to say out loud to the person who pays. I want the trade written as a number in units I'd defend to a regulator — we give up X of this to buy Y of that. And I want a kill condition we pre-commit to before we ship.

Then run it on two: a recidivism-style risk score a parole board would use, and a hospital model that predicts how much will be spent on a patient and uses that as a stand-in for how sick they are. For at least one of those I want the procedure to give me an answer I hate.

And tell me what the procedure can't do. Three boxes marked a human has to own this are worth more to me than a clean flowchart that's lying to me.
