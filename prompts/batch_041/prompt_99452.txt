You are a philosopher specializing in social epistemology — the epistemology of inquiry, testimony, and the social conditions under which people hold their beliefs — and you have a reputation for two things: turning a heap of case-by-case judgments into a precise principle, and telling people plainly which of their judgments the principle cannot save.

I have been trying to sort the epistemic environments around me into echo chambers and epistemic bubbles, and my judgments refuse to settle onto any single criterion. Below is how I actually call things, as honestly as I can put it. Work from these cases rather than from the literature: I am not asking for a survey of positions or a reading list, and I don't want citations. Start by brainstorming the candidate criteria the cases seem to point to — exclusion, unwarranted distrust, manipulation, the social cost of dissent, the agent's own dispositions — and only then commit to a principle.

1. A man whose feed has been algorithmically narrowed to agreeable political content. He has never sought out the other side, but nothing in him resists it: a good argument would reach him, and he doesn't think his opponents are liars. A bubble, I say, not an echo chamber.

2. A religious community that keeps its members away from outside views but teaches no contempt for outsiders. Bubble, not chamber.

3. A community whose members see plenty of outside views but have been taught that the outside world is a machine of manipulation, so that nothing arriving from outside can count. Echo chamber.

4. A community with unimpeded access to outside views and no doctrine of outsider dishonesty, where dissent is merely socially expensive: no one lies to the members or forbids them to look, they simply never seriously entertain the alternative, because doing so would cost them their standing. I want to call this an echo chamber too. This is where my criteria start to chafe.

5. A person who holds an unpopular view after reading and answering the best objections to it, and whose friends all agree with her. Not an echo chamber; the agreement isn't doing the work.

6. A group that distrusts one particular outside source, for what I take to be good reasons — the source has a documented record of distortion. Not an echo chamber, even though insiders are discrediting outsiders.

7. Someone raised inside an echo chamber who has since left it, but whose belief survives unchanged. I don't want to say he is now in an echo chamber, though nothing about his belief has improved.

8. Two people in physically identical information environments — same feed, same friends, same exposure — one of whom would revise under pressure and one of whom would not. I want to say only the second is in an echo chamber, which suggests the property belongs to the person-and-environment together rather than to the environment alone.

I suspect I cannot have all eight.

What I need from you: (a) a precise statement of the principle, or small set of rival principles, that comes closest to capturing this pattern, with each clause motivated by the case that forces it; (b) a candid verdict on which of my eight judgments the best principle requires me to abandon, and whether the cheaper repair is to split one concept into two or to give up a judgment; (c) your account of what exclusion, distrust, and manipulation each contribute to the definition, given that case 4 is an echo chamber without a distrust doctrine and case 6 is a distrust doctrine without an echo chamber; (d) whether the truth of the beliefs in question makes any difference — a community that is insular and dismissive of outsiders but happens to be right still strikes me as an echo chamber, and I want to know whether that instinct can be kept; and (e) the principle's verdict on a case I have not listed: a community that exposes its members to outsiders constantly, but only through formats that make the outsiders look ridiculous.

Where a case resists the principle, say so rather than smoothing it over, and state your final principle in a form I could apply to a new case without asking you again.
