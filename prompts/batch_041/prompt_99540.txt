ok, trying to get my own head straight on free will and I'd rather have a consistency audit than another primer. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I've been going back and forth on this alot:

1. Determinism is probably true. I don't see a place for a gap in the causal order and I don't want one.
2. So I'm a compatibilist: free will and moral responsibility are about acting on your own reasons and desires without being coerced or manipulated, not about being uncaused. A deterministic world is fine for that.
3. But I also think responsibility needs real alternatives — "could have done otherwise" has to mean more than "you would have, if you'd wanted to." Not just a conditional.
4. I think a person raised from birth inside a closed cult, who does awful things out of desires the cult installed, and who feels no external pressure in the moment, isn't responsible for those things — even if, after leaving, they look back and say "yeah, that was me."
5. I don't think deliberation is an illusion. When I'm choosing, the options feel genuinely open, and I think that feeling is tracking something real.
6. And I don't think sticking quantum randomness into the brain would help — a coin flip inside me isn't me choosing.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for each real one? Rank the tensions by how bad they are instead of just listing them, say what each fix would cost me, and then tell me if there's a single revision that would clean up the most. I have a nagging feeling one of these is quietly doing all the work and I can't tell which.
