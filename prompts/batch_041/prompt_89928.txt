Bit of an odd one. I've somehow ended up writing the short gig and album blurbs for a listings site a mate runs, which means people keep asking me what I think of things I haven't heard, and it's made me realise my actual views on all this don't obviously hang together. Here's roughly where I've landed — tell me if it's a package or a mess.

(a) There are facts about whether a record or a novel is any good. Not facts like the boiling point of water, but not just "this is what I happen to like" either. Some things really are better than others, and someone who thinks a total hack is the equal of the real thing is, in some sense, wrong.

(b) You can't judge a work unless you've sat with it yourself. Being told it's a masterpiece isn't the same as hearing it. If I haven't listened to the album I don't think I'm entitled to an opinion about how good it is, full stop, however much I trust the person telling me.

(c) I act on other people's verdicts constantly and I think that's completely fine and rational. If a critic I rate says the new record is worth the money, I'll buy the ticket on the strength of that alone, no hesitation, and I don't think I'm being irresponsible or believing anything I shouldn't.

(d) When I do call something beautiful I'm not just reporting my own reaction, like saying "this makes me feel queasy". I'm saying something anyone who really listened ought to go along with. If you can't hear it, I think something's missing in you, not just that we have different tastes.

(e) Taste is trainable. Two years of listening to almost nothing else has made me a much better judge than I was, and the people who write for the proper magazines have listened to ten times more than me, so they're better judges than I am — reliably better, not just differently wired.

I keep feeling these can't all be true at once, but every time I try to work out which ones are actually fighting I talk myself in circles, and honestly I'd rather not give any of them up. So can you go through it: which of these genuinely can't be held together, how bad is each clash (rank them, don't just tell me they're all in tension), which pairs only look like they're in conflict and why, and for the real ones, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? Just write it out in continuous prose, no headings or bullet points or bold text or tables — the way you'd explain it to me over email.
