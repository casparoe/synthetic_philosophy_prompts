I recently became the data-ethics liaison for a county agency that uses a risk model to decide who gets a housing navigator, and at our last meeting the director circulated a fairness checklist that has turned into a small crisis. The checklist demands that the model be calibrated within each language group, that false-positive rates be equal across groups, that false-negative rates be equal across groups, and that no decision be based on group membership. The base rates in our data genuinely differ, so I spent the meeting trying to explain that all four cannot hold at once—and that adding a randomization layer, as the director suggested, only relocates the problem rather than dissolving it. She then added a fifth criterion, which she called explainability at the margin: the model must be able to justify its decision to the applicant ranked just below the cutoff, in that applicant's own language, without revealing the score. I want to open the next meeting with a joke that lets everyone laugh at the bind without letting them off the hook.

Please write that joke. It should be technically literate: the punchline should turn on the actual structure of the impossibility—e.g., that satisfying one fairness constraint tends to push the violation onto a different margin, or that a calibrated score with unequal prevalence makes error-rate parity a kind of three-body problem—not on generic jokes about biased data or opaque black boxes. A short scene or monologue would work well, under two minutes spoken; you could stage it as a job interview in which a risk score is asked to satisfy a panel of mutually incompatible references. No citations or literature review needed; I only need the joke to be correct and funny to people who already know the material.
