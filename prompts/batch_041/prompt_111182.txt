Here's something that's been nagging me since I retired from the lab and started reading epistemology, mostly over my head. Forty years of error bars does something to you: when I began putting numbers on my opinions I kept them as ranges rather than single values -- somewhere between 0.2 and 0.8 on this, 0.4 to 0.7 on that -- because a single number would be a precision I havent earned. I gather some philosophers keep them as ranges on purpose, which is how I ended up in this corner of the library. But when I try to do arithmetic on the ranges the way I'd propagate error bars, it comes apart. Cleanest case: my range for A is 0.2 to 0.8, so my range for not-A is the same, and combining those the way I'd combine two independent measurements says A and not-A might both hold with probability 0.64, which is garbage -- the ranges have stopped carrying the information I thought they were carrying. Same story with a pair of claims: I know the range for A and the range for B, I don't know how they hang together, and the range I get for A-and-B swings from zero up to the smaller of the two depending on what I assume about that, and every assumption looks equally defensible to me. So my ranges dont seem to have fixed anything on their own. Is there a name for this? I have a hunch the interval-arithmetic people run into something like it, but I cant tell whether the probability version is the same disease or a different one. And I don't want just the label -- I want to know whether my honest-looking range is really an unfinished object, and if so, what you're supposed to add to finish it.
