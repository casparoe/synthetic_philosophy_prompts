Right, I've volunteered myself for something stupid and it's turned into a proper argument, so I'm outsourcing the thinking.

I'm painting a mural at our community centre. Ten panels along one wall, one per century, 1000 to 2000, left to right, each panel showing 'what was going on in the world' in that century. Sounds easy. Panel one — the 1000s — has broken me. If I put the Norman Conquest on it, the whole wall turns into a story about England with some scenery. If I try to be even-handed I get a jumble: Song China doing its thing, Cahokia doing its thing, the Norse landing in Newfoundland, and no thread at all, just stuff. That's not a mural, its a collage.

My mate Dev (did history at uni, has opinions) says the grid is the problem, not the contents. He says centuries, counted left to right, is already a European way of chopping up time and I'm smuggling a whole worldview in with the layout, and that I should either commit to one story and admit that's what it is, or not build the wall. But if I drop the frame there's nothing holding the thing together at all. And I still have to title each panel, and every title I try — 'mediaeval', 'early modern', 'the Renaissance' — sounds like a story about Europe wearing a date as a disguise.

So what do I actually paint? Is there a version that isn't one story with a European spine, or ten unrelated pictures in a row? And if there isn't, which of those two is the more honest thing to build, and how would someone walking past be able to tell which one I'd picked?
