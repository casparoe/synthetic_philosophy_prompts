I chair the Sensory Standards Committee of a mid-sized specialty coffee importer, and I need a written opinion I can circulate before Thursday's meeting. Our quality system rests on two master tasters, and last month they split on the same lot, cupped side by side. Taster A reports that the coffee itself has changed: same palate, different cup. Taster B reports the reverse — the coffee is what it has always been, and his own palate has drifted, such that he now detects a cardboard note he is confident was never in the bean. Each has held his position for six weeks, each can describe the change in detail, each passes our standard panel tests, and neither has anything to gain. Nothing we possess — no assay, no triangulation procedure — can settle which of them is describing the change correctly. The lot is large, already contracted, and carries our oldest marketing claim, that the blend has not been altered since 1974.

The committee must choose among three options: release the lot under the unchanged-blend claim; recall and reblend; or hold the lot pending a full recalibration of the panel. Please draft the committee's decision memo. It should recommend one course with a date and the conditions attached to it, state the strongest case for the option you turn down, and be written to be voted on rather than admired.

Three matters I want addressed rather than dodged. First, our system treats a taster as a measuring instrument — one of the two is our calibration reference, against whom all other staff are scored — and I cannot tell whether that practice presupposes that there is a fact of the matter about how the coffee tastes, or presupposes the opposite. Second, our head of quality, who read philosophy as an undergraduate, says this is a textbook instance of the problem of qualia: two reports that agree the taste has changed and disagree about what changed, with nothing independent to adjudicate between them, and that some philosophers regard the very idea of an intrinsic, private, ineffable character of experience as an incoherent posit. I do not want a survey of that literature. I want to know what follows for Thursday. Can this committee treat the question of whether the coffee changed or the taster changed as a question with an answer, and if not, what should it do instead? Third, one member has proposed that every taster keep a standing palate-drift log recording changes in his own sensitivity. Say whether that proposal answers the problem or merely relocates it.
