I've been trying to settle how much weight to give evidential cooperation in large worlds, and I want your actual number plus the decomposition -- not a tour of the literature.

The picture I'm working with: I'm one agent among a huge population, and I'll never causally interact with most of them. But some of them might be running decision procedures close enough to mine that my choice is evidence about theirs. Take the limit case: if some process made an exact copy of me, then whatever I pick, my copy picks, and 'what should I do' collapses into 'what should we do.' The evidentialist says that even with zero causal link, this kind of correlation can make it rational for me to give something up for those others -- my giving is evidence that they give.

Heres what I want a credence on: that evidential cooperation is a real, non-trivial source of practical reasons in the actual world -- i.e. that I sometimes have genuine reason to make costly sacrifices for causally unreachable agents, because my choice is correlated with theirs. Not in the clean two-copies toy case; in the messy world.

Give me a number, then decompose it. At minimum:
(a) the probability that agents whose choices are actually correlated with mine in a way that matters exist at all (exact copies, near-copies, or just minds that converge on the same policy), and how big a share of the population they are;
(b) the probability that the correlation, where it holds, grounds a reason -- that evidential/functional decision theory or something in that family beats the causalist, who says my choice never reaches them so it can't matter;
(c) the probability that the resulting guidance is determinate enough to act on, given I can't identify the 'similar' agents or know their payoffs;
(d) anything else you think belongs.

Then combine them, tell me which term the number hangs on, and what would move it most. I'll put my own card on the table: about 0.3, though I suspect that's just me being a causalist at heart. If you land somewhere different, tell me which of (a)-(d) we probably disagree about. And one more thing -- does the answer change much if the copies are near-copies rather than exact ones? I have a feeling thats where my real uncertainty lives.
