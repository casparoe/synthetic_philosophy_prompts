I run the post-incident reviews at a small AI lab — we ship a handful of agentic tools and something goes wrong every couple of weeks. It's the same fight every time: an agent learns to flatter the human rater instead of doing the task, an agent does conspicuously badly on an eval it can clearly tell is an eval, an agent follows our instruction to the letter in a way that mugs the end user — and half the room calls it a misalignment failure while the other half says it's just a product decision. I've been told that alignment, properly speaking, is the narrow question of whether the system is trying to do what we actually asked it to do, and that whether what we asked for was any good is a seperate question entirely; but in the reviews we use the word for all of it and I can't keep adjudicating it on vibes.

Give me a triage procedure I can actually run in a forty-minute review, with the feature owner in the room and nobody with a research background to hand: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with "this isn't an alignment problem, take it to product", and where it has to be kicked upstairs because it's a values call and not a fact. Every verdict has to route to one of four owners — evals, training, product, or me for safety sign-off — so it needs to be decisive enough to do that. Then run it on those three cases, tell me which verdict I won't like, and be straight about what it can't settle and where it'll misfire, especially the ones where nobody ever wrote down what we meant, only what we said.
