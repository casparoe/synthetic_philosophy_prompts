For a semester-long independent research paper in my high school's humanities program, I need a short research proposal on a puzzle I noticed in Émilie du Châtelet's Discourse on Happiness. In that work she sets out what she calls the great springs of happiness, and two of them seem to pull against each other. On one hand, she says the happy person must have shed her prejudices and should cultivate the love of study, which she praises as a passion that depends on no one else. On the other hand, she says the happy person must be capable of illusion and should carefully preserve the illusions that give her pleasure, and that as the passions fade with age we should replace them with milder tastes. My half-formed idea is that the conflict is only apparent, because she is not offering a single list of conditions but running two projects at once: a hedonistic one, on which happiness is the sum of agreeable feelings and an illusion is simply one more source of them, and a perfectionist one, on which happiness depends on what a person is and does. Please turn this into a proposal. State the question so that it has a determinate answer, explain why a plain reading of the Discourse leaves it genuinely open rather than merely ambiguous, describe the method — I would reconstruct her argument from the text itself, separate her claims about feelings from her claims about character, and test whether the two strands can be reconciled or whether one has to give way — say what a satisfying result would look like and what would count as the project failing, and give a realistic scope for one semester of work by a high school student on her own. I will supply the citations myself, so the proposal need not name sources.
