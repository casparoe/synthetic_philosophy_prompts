Ok, so I'm a retired chemist — spent thirty-odd years on polymer physical chemistry, and since I retired I've been reading alot of philosophy, mostly the metaphysics of science stuff, and I've landed on a cluster of views about dispositions that I'd like a sanity check on before I inflict them on anybody at the local discussion group. Here's roughly where I've ended up.

(1) Dispositions are real. When I say a glass is fragile, or that sugar is soluble, I don't think I'm just abbreviating a prediction about what would happen. Fragility and solubility are genuine features of the things that have them.

(2) That said, I don't think a disposition is anything over and above the microstructure. Glass is fragile because of how its molecules are arranged and bonded; two samples that were molecule-for-molecule identical would be fragile to exactly the same degree. Same deal for the rest of them.

(3) I'm convinced the old idea that being fragile just means it would break if struck is dead. The sorcerer case does it for me — a sorcerer who makes a glass not break precisely when it's struck, so the counterfactual fails even though the glass is obviously still fragile. And the packing-foam case does it from the other direction.

(4) And that's the thing: masking doesn't remove the disposition. The glass wrapped in foam is still fragile, it's just that the foam keeps it from manifesting. Same with the sorcerer's glass — still fragile, he's only fiddling with what actually happens.

(5) Dispositions are intrinsic. Whether this glass is fragile is settled by what's going on inside the glass, not by whether there happens to be foam around it or a sorcerer watching.

(6) I'm a physicalist, and I don't buy that it's powers all the way down. The fundamental stuff of physics — mass, charge, whatever it turns out to be — is categorical. Everything else, dispositions included, is built out of that.

(7) I also don't think the laws of nature are necessary. They're just descriptions of how things happen to behave; the world could have run on different regularities.

Now, I've got a nagging feeling some of this doesn't hang together, but every time I go looking for the seam I end up going in circles and confusing myself. So: which of these are actually in tension, which ones only look like it and aren't, and how bad is each real one? I'd like them ranked by how serious you think they are, not just a flat list of maybes. For each genuine tension, tell me the cheapest way to patch it and what I'd be giving up in the bargain. And at the very end, tell me the single change that would clean up the most trouble for the least cost — I'd much rather make one revision than seperate ones.
