I am a novelist, and I have nearly finished a book whose spine is a compromise. Two communities share a valley — one has worked it for generations, the other settled there within living memory — and the plot turns on the deal they strike over water, grazing, and the right to build. Neither side is villainous; each is trying to keep a way of life. My characters argue from the first chapter to the last about whether what they have agreed to is a triumph or a sellout, and in drafting the final part I have realized that my own views about what makes a compromise good are quietly running the show. I would like you to tell me whether those views hang together, because I can no longer tell.

Here, roughly, is where I have landed. One: where two people rank things differently there is usually a deal that leaves each better off by her own reckoning, and the good of a compromise is largely that surplus — each gives up what she ranks lower for what she ranks higher, and other things equal I take that to be a reason in favour of any such deal. Two: values are plural and there is no common measure, no single scale on which your attachment to a way of life and mine can be weighed against each other. Three: so it seems to me that the question whether a given compromise was a good one has no answer from nowhere; verdicts are relative to a standpoint, a party's own values or the values of someone judging from inside some tradition. Four: all the same, I do not think it is merely true-from-somewhere that some compromises are exploitative. When the stronger party trades a trinket for a birthright, that is not only a loss by the weaker party's lights; it is a wrong. Five: nor do I think a person is irrational or confused when she refuses a deal that would leave her better off by her own ranking, because some of what she values cannot survive being priced — a loyalty that can be bought off at the right price was never loyalty, and the exchange does not leave her with less of the same thing but with a different thing. Six: I want my characters' quarrel to be a real quarrel; when one calls the deal a triumph and the other calls it a sellout, I want them to be disagreeing about something rather than reporting two private standpoints.

What I need is an audit rather than a lecture. Which of these pull against each other, which only look as though they do, and how badly does each genuine conflict bite? Order them by severity, say for each what would have to give and what that would cost, and then tell me which single revision would clear up the most. Please commit: I would rather have a definite verdict I can argue with than a survey of positions, so choose the commitment you would abandon and defend the choice rather than handing me a menu. Write it as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter — no headings, no numbered lists, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
