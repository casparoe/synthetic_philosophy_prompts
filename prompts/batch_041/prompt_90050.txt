For a graduate seminar on social construction, I want a decision tree that takes a reader from one starting commitment — that money, race, and gender are all socially constructed, and that they are not sure what they have just committed to — to a specific named position. Every internal node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and every leaf should be a position with a descriptive label rather than a defender's name; do not include reading lists or citations. Put a concrete case at each node wherever you can: a token of currency, a racial classification on a census form, the category woman in a sports eligibility rule.

Build the space around at least these distinctions: (i) whether the kind is constitutively mind-dependent, so that it would not exist if no one thought or acted as if it did, or only causally mind-involved, our practices bringing about instances that could in principle have existed otherwise; (ii) whether the kind is a status conferred by collective acceptance or a cluster of traits causally produced in its members; (iii) whether the kind's members are altered by being classified, the classification looping back on the classified, or whether the kind is indifferent to how we describe it; (iv) whether the construction story is told in terms of our representations or in terms of our practices; and (v) whether, once the construction story is told, the kind can still support induction and figure in explanations of its members' behavior.

The tree must separate at least these positions: constitutive constructionism that is still realist about the kind; causal constructionism that treats the kind as a natural kind; the view that the kind is real but its construction is explanatorily idle; the eliminativist view that the kind does not exist; the view that what is constructed is the concept rather than the kind; and the view that the real question is what the kind ought to be rather than what it is. Flag any leaves your tree cannot separate, note any combination of answers that no one occupies and say whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and finish by saying which of the three kinds — money, race, or gender — each leaf handles least well.
