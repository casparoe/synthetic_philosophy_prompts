I'm giving a paper at a cross-cultural epistemology workshop next month and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a defence. I came to this material from the rationality blogs rather than from Sanskrit — I still think of an epistemology as a set of intake channels, and I have a bad habit of asking whether the channel count is a free parameter — and my worry is that this room will hear exegesis where I mean argument. The thesis: upamāna is an irreducible pramāṇa, and the standard analytic treatment of it as an oddity is downstream of having no working criterion for individuating ways of knowing, so that three gets assumed rather than argued. My sketch: Nyāya individuates a pramāṇa by its instrument, its object and its upshot; in the canonical case — you are told that a gavaya is like a cow, you meet one in the forest, and what you come away with is knowledge of what the word "gavaya" picks out — the resulting cognition has an object the other three pramāṇas don't supply, and the Naiyāyika distinction between the instrument (the grasp of similarity) and the operative cause (the remembered statement) is what the whole count rests on. The room: eight or nine analytic epistemologists who work on testimony, memory and the a priori; one formal epistemologist who will want credences; one virtue epistemologist for whom sources should really be competences; and two colleagues from South Asian studies, one working on the Buddhist critics of upamāna and one on Navya-Nyāya. Nobody is hostile; nobody has any antecedent reason to care whether the number is four or three.

You are a philosopher who has taught both Nyāya and contemporary analytic epistemology and who has watched this exact kind of talk go badly. Think carefully, and on your own, before you write: who in that room raises what, and why, steelmanned rather than caricatured. Then give me exactly five numbered entries, with no introduction and no closing summary, each entry one or two sentences: the objection in the words its raiser would actually use, my reply in brief, and a clause on where the reply is soft. Order the first four by how much damage they do to the thesis, and make the fifth the one I'm hoping nobody asks.
