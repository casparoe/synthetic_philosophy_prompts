Ok, so I'm putting together the last session of my grad seminar on free will and moral responsibility and I want to open with a poem for once instead of yet another handout.

We spent a few weeks on Strawson's Basic Argument — the one where you do what you do because of the way you are, so for the deed to be really yours in the responsibility-entailing sense you'd have to be responsible for being the way you are, which would need you to have somehow brought it about that you're that way, which just pushes the question back a step, and forever; and anyway even if you could make yourself you'd have to be responsible for having chosen to make yourself like that, so nothing is ever owned all the way down. What I want is a sestina built out of that. Six end-words of your choosing, rotated the usual way, but pick them so that the rotation is the point — the words should keep coming back in shifted positions the way the demand for ultimate responsibility keeps coming back, and by the envoi it should be clear that the same six moves are all the argument ever gets and it never terminates. The envoi shouldnt resolve anything. If it lands on a comfortable "well then it's just determinism" I'll be annoyed, because the bite of the argument for Strawson is precisely that it doesnt turn on determinism at all — indeterminism buys you randomness, and randomness is no more yours than causation was. The turn (if a sestina can be said to have one) should be the spot where you can't even rescue yourself by being self-made: whoever does the making, the maker's own nature is just sitting there behind them, unchosen.

Tone: not a funeral. Strawson's own attitude is that the truth of the whole thing doesn't wreck anything — he still says thank you, still gets angry, he just doesnt think anyone ultimately deserves it. So let it be unsentimental but not bleak, and accurate enough that someone who knows the Basic Argument finds it right rather than a caricature, and someone who doesn't still gets a good poem out of it.

Keep it to the standard shape — six six-line stanzas plus a three-line envoi — and then, as the very last line, one plain sentence saying what the form is supposed to be doing, starting with "In short:". Dont bother citing anything, I'll handle that myself.
