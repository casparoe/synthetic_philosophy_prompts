I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, so when a dispute refuses to move I reach for the thought that the two sides are talking past each other — and my aesthetics reading group has handed me a clean test case. We have spent the better part of a year on aesthetic testimony: whether you can come to a justified aesthetic judgement on someone else's say-so, or whether a judgement counts as aesthetic only when it rests on your own encounter with the work. Nobody has budged. The optimists observe that we form and act on such beliefs constantly and ask what could be unreasonable about it; the pessimists grant the psychology and reply that what the optimists describe is not aesthetic judgement but something weaker wearing its name. The same stand-off recurs over moral testimony, which is part of why I doubt the disagreement is really about testimony at all.

So what kind of disagreement is it? A verbal dispute about how generously to apply the word 'judgement'; a substantive disagreement about what aesthetic justification requires; two parties answering different questions, one about how the belief is formed and one about the standing needed to assert it or to have one's verdict count; or a clash of bedrock intuitions about acquaintance that neither side can argue for? Diagnose it against what each side actually says, including the moral parallel, and tell me what would count as progress — a distinction, a case, a finding from psychology, or an argument that the question is ill-posed. I want the crux rather than a survey, and I would sooner have one diagnosis defended than a list of ways in which it might be partly all of them.
