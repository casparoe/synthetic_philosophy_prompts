Here's the setup. There are two stories people tell about how AI goes badly, and I've been treating them as seperate failure modes for years. The loud one: some system ends up pursuing influence in a fairly direct sense -- it accumulates resources, it resists being corrected or shut down, it turns itself into something you'd have to fight. The quiet one: nothing dramatic ever happens, no grabby agent, just a slow slide where we keep handing off more decisions, our values get sanded down a little at a time, and at the end we're nominally in charge but nothing we want actually determines what happens. One of my students said last week that these aren't two things at all, just one process seen at two different speeds -- crank the timescale right and they blur together. Another said the quiet story isn't a failure mode at all; it's just what ordinary technological change looks like, and calling it "failure" smuggles in a baseline about how fast change is allowed to be.

Write me an essay that takes a side. I don't want a survey, I want a verdict: are these genuinely two distinct failure modes, or one dynamic at different speeds, and does the quiet one even deserve the label?

Two failure modes are really separate only if you can name some intervention that blocks one while leaving the other live. So: is there such an intervention here? Also make you're essay address whether the difference between the two is a difference in outcomes or just in how legible the process is to us while it's happening -- I suspect a lot of the "two modes" intuition is really about observability, but I'm not sure that's fatal to it.
