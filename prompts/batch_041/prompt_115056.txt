Setup, in case it helps: Nyāya epistemology says there are four pramāṇas — the ways a true cognition gets produced such that it counts as knowledge. Perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), comparison/analogy (upamāna, the route where you're told a gavaya is like a cow, later see one, and now know what that word picks out), and testimony (śabda). Four, not six — they think postulation and non-apprehension, which some other schools add, reduce to these.

Here's the case. A radiologist glances at a scan for two seconds and knows there's an early tumour. She's right, and reliably right — calibrated over thousands of cases. But she can't say why.
