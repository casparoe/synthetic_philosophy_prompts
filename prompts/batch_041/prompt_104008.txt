I hold the standard three-condition account of valid consent: an act of consent is valid just in case the person is competent to give it, adequately informed about what she is consenting to, and acting voluntarily, free of coercion and deception. I want you to map this position's exposure — what would have to be true for it to be false, and which cases, arguments, and intuitions should actually move me.

Start with the defeaters that come from inside the account. The voluntariness condition appears to need a baseline, a theory of what the consenter is entitled to, before an offer can be distinguished from a threat; I want to know whether the account can supply one without circularity or whether it must import an independent moral theory and lose its claim to neutrality. The information condition appears to need a theory of materiality, since not every false belief voids consent; I want to know whether the line can be drawn in a principled way or whether every proposed line either lets too much through or voids consent whenever the consenter is mistaken. The competence condition looks domain-relative in a way that resists a single threshold. And the account treats validity as the thing that does the moral work, even though we say that valid consent to some acts does not make them permissible and invalid consent to others does not make them wrong — so I want to know whether it conflates validity with moral force.

Then the external defeaters: cases where all three conditions are met and consent still seems absent or void; cases a rival — relational, performative, or will-based — describes better; and any argument that the conditions are not independent, for instance that voluntariness and competence are two names for the same thing, the consenter's will being properly her own.

Sort the defeaters from fatal through answerable to merely awkward. Tell me which ones the account's own defenders would concede and which they would dismiss, and why. Then say honestly whether the account has been insulated from refutation by turning each condition into a placeholder that can be filled in however the case requires, and if so, whether that is a fatal defect or just what a framework-level account of a contested concept looks like.

For calibration: the defeater I would find hardest to live with is a case in which the consenter is competent, informed, and uncoerced by any ordinary standard, yet the consent seems void because the conditions under which she formed her preferences were themselves unjust. I want to know whether that shows the account is incomplete or only that the voluntariness condition was being read too thinly.
