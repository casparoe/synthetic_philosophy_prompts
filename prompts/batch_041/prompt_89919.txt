ok so I've been lurking on LessWrong / the various spin-off discords / ACX comments for like a decade now, and there's a thing about how we talk about models that's been nagging me and I finally want to pin it down.

Here's the practice. When a model gives an answer and the chain of thought it prints doesn't actually reflect what drove the answer -- you patch some activations, or you just notice the real cause was a cue in the prompt the CoT never mentions, or the CoT is plainly a post-hoc story -- we (me included) instantly reach for: the model rationalized, it lied about its reasoning, it knew X but wouldn't say so. And the whole interpretability vocabulary is built on this: eliciting latent knowledge, "the model's real beliefs vs. what it claims," deceptive alignment, the unfaithful-CoT literature, all of it. This is a real, load-bearing thing people do every day, not a fringe view.

What I want from you: reconstruct the theory of belief and mental-state attribution that this practice presupposes. Does it require realism about the model's beliefs -- i.e. there's a fact of the matter about what it "really" computed or knew, independent of any predictive use we make of it? Or can you rebuild the exact same practice on a thoroughly Dennett-ish instrumentalist reading, where "the model believes P" just means the intentional stance predicts it well and there's no further fact underneath? Which rival views does the practice quietly rule out, and which does it merely fit nicely with but not actually need? Be careful to separate what it strictly presupposes from what's just along for the ride.

Now the part I actually care about. I've been messing with a little distinction of my own and I want you to run the practice through it. Call a report-belief the thing a model will avow in text if you ask it, and a steering-belief the thing that actually moves its outputs when you intervene on it. In the unfaithful-CoT case these come apart cleanly -- the steering-belief is there, the report-belief isn't, or vice versa. So: does the practice presuppose that report-beliefs and steering-beliefs are the same thing, or the same kind of thing? Because if not, I suspect a good chunk of the discourse is just confused, and half the disagreements are people using belief to mean different halves of that split without noticing.

Then do the taboo test: forbid yourself the word belief entirely (and knowing, and intends, if they're doing the same work) and try to state what the unfaithful-CoT complaint even is. Does it survive as something like "the printed CoT is a bad predictor of the model's behavior," or is there leftover content that the instrumentalist vocabulary can't capture? If there's leftover content, that's the interesting bit and I want to know exactly what it is. Give me the practice rebuilt from scratch under that constraint and tell me what it loses in the rebuild.

Last thing: I have a suspicion that the practice is way more underdetermined than we pretend -- that instrumentalism, some functionalist realism, a flat deflationary belief-talk-is-just-a-stance view, and maybe a dispositionalist thing would all license the identical lab behavior and the identical sentences in the paper. So tell me where it's genuinely underdetermined, and if so, which part of the practice is doing real work and which part is decoration we could drop tomorrow with nothing changing. No need for cites or a reading list, I know the neighborhood, I just want the argument.
