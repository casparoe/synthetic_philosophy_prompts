My editor says the framing has to be right, and honestly the reason is that the two get fixed in opposite ways. I've got the basic distinction down: an epistemic bubble is missing information, and you fix it by putting the missing stuff in front of people; an echo chamber is a social structure where members have been brought to distrust everyone outside it, so exposure makes things worse instead of better. What I dont have is a procedure. Can you give me one I can actually run? Ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the call back to me because it's values and not facts, plus the cases it will misfire on. Constraints: I only see public behavior, what people say in interviews, and their source lists — no network analysis, no private data, deadline in an hour, and my editor signs off on the framing. Three things I want it to survive: a group that has the facts right but whose social structure is still an echo chamber (and the reverse, a group that's alot wrong but isn't an echo chamber at all); a group whose distrust of outsiders is calibrated — they got burned by institutions and know it — because I do not want a checklist that ends up labeling people who were let down as brainwashed; and my own reading, which is one-subject and probably fine. Run it on three cases, including one where it gives an answer I won't like and shouldn't ignore.
