My friend and I just spent the evening arguing about Plato's views on drunkenness. He maintains that Plato is straightforwardly hostile to intoxication, and that the Laws mentions drinking parties only to warn against them. I think this misses the more interesting reading of the text: that the Athenian Stranger proposes supervised drinking parties as a diagnostic and educational practice, one that reveals and trains a person's character. Please argue for my reading. Make the case that Plato's treatment of drunkenness is not simply prohibitive but treats regulated intoxication as a tool for self-knowledge and civic virtue, and address the strongest objection—that Plato's apparent approval is merely instrumental, or that the drinking party is a concession to human weakness rather than a genuine good.
