Alright, moral sainthood, but not the usual framing — skip the literature review, I already know the standard moves.

Define a saint precisely: at every choice point she takes the option with the highest expected moral value, where moral value is impartial, and where her own projects, relationships and happiness enter the calculation only to the degree that impartial morality itself licenses. No self-favoring, no permissions exercised except where morality grants them.

I want three credences:

(1) that this life is good for the person living it — better for her than a decent ordinary life with a job, friends, hobbies and a healthy amount of selfishness;
(2) that the world is better off with one such person in it than with one near-saint, someone who does maybe 70% of what impartial morality asks;
(3) that the answer to (2) flips sign as prevalence rises — i.e. saints are net positive at 1% of a population and net negative at 60%, because of the stuff I keep circling: moral licensing in everyone around them, free-riding, the saint soaking up all the hard cases so nobody else develops the capacity, and the saint being a lousy friend and therefore a lousy agent in the one place she has unique leverage.

Then decompose each one. Sub-questions with rough probabilities and how they combine: is the highest expected moral value even well-defined given incommensurable goods and moral uncertainty? Does well-being track project-satisfaction, or is it more a bundle of objective goods a saint can have plenty of? Is morality saturated, always a best action, or sparse with lots of permitted territory? And is there a real gap between the saint as optimizer and the saint as exemplar — could she be justified in not maximizing in order to stay a visible example, and if so doesnt that eat itself?

For calibration: I'm around 0.4 on (1), 0.6 on (2), 0.5 on (3). Tell me where you'd move me, which sub-question your numbers are most sensitive to, and what evidence would shift it most. If you think the whole thing is an artifact of how I framed desirability, say so and put a number on that too.
