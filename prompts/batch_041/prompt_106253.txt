I want to do this properly and not just let her restate the famous bits. You're an expert on personal identity and you know this literature cold, so think it through first, maybe brainstorm where the actual pressure points are, then give me 9 or 10 questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.

Things I definately want in there:

- the plain transporter is too easy for her, so press the variants. One where 1% of your neurons get swapped out each day for three months while you stay awake and chatting the whole time. One where the machine rebuilds you from a backup taken a year earlier, so whoever walks out is missing a year. One with a 10% error rate, so you arrive with most of the memories and a slightly different personality. Which of those does her view have to call survival, and which is she willing to call death?

- if she's right, why do I owe my own future self anything more than I owe a stranger, and does the view actually make anyone calmer about dying, or is that just a hope she's attached to it?

Keep it conversational and no softballs like what got you into philosophy.
