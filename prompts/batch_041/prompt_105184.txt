I've been going back and forth with a friend about manipulation for like a month now and I finally wrote down all my gut calls on cases, so tell me what principle (if any) they add up to.

1. A salesperson tells me the jacket is the last one in my size, and it's true, and it tips me into buying. Fine. That's just sales.
2. Same salesperson says it when there are three in the stockroom. Not fine — but that's just lying, so maybe it tells us nothing.
3. My doctor says a surgery has a 90% survival rate instead of saying one in ten people die. Fine, even though the framing is doing real work on me and I might have refused if she'd put it the other way.
4. Same doctor, same accurate numbers, but she picks the 90% phrasing because she's guessed I'll refuse otherwise and she thinks I should have the surgery. Less sure here. I lean fine? It's still my call, and nothing she said was false.
5. A charity shows me a photo of one kid, with a name and a birthday. Fine. Emotion, sure, but that's just communication.
6. Same charity says the matching gift expires in three hours when it actually runs all month. Not fine.
7. My partner says 'fine, do whatever you want' in a tone that means I'll pay for it. Not fine, even though every word is true and I am in fact free to go.
8. Same partner says, out loud, 'I'd be hurt if you went, I'm not trying to stop you, that's just true.' Fine. Which is weird, because the coded version and the honest version are trying to do the same thing.
9. My therapist uses a technique straight out of a manual to get me to hear my own reasons read back to me. She's told me what she's doing and that it's a technique. Fine.
10. A friend told me flat out: 'I'm going to keep bringing up how good you felt after your last run, because I know it works on you, and I want you to sign up for this race.' Fully disclosed, no falsehood, and it is openly a lever. Fine, honestly. Annoying, but fine.
11. A colleague who wants me on their side of a department fight never says anything false, never plays on a feeling, never offers me anything but good reasons — but they also never mention that they're curating, they just quietly give me the arguments that happen to point their way, and only those. Not fine. I feel managed.
12. Someone asks me for a big favor at 11:30 at night when I'm wrecked and I say yes. Not fine, even though I could have said no and the favor itself is harmless.
13. Someone I'm negotiating with just lets a long silence sit after my offer, because they know I can't stand silences. Fine. If they'd made up a competing offer, not fine, but the silence is on me.
14. A friend gets me to a surprise party by lying about where we're going. Fine. Covert, deceptive, aimed at my choices, and still fine.

The pair I can't square is 10 and 11. In 10 someone is working around my deliberation with a lever they've announced. In 11 someone is working through my own reasons and hiding nothing but their intentions. If manipulation is about bypassing the reasoning, 10 is the bad one. If it's about whether I know what's being done to me, 11 is the bad one. I flip every time I think about it.

My best guess at a principle is that manipulation is moving someone by working on something in them that they'd want switched off if they noticed it — a lever they'd disown — while persuasion works through the part of them that's actually them. But that's not it either, because the therapist and the marathon friend are both working levers and I say fine, and the colleague is working through my own reasons and I say not fine. I also can't get 3/4 apart from 12, or 5 from 6, in a way I can defend.

So: is there one principle that gets all of this, or close? If not, which of my verdicts do you give up, and why that one instead of the others? State the principle precisely, say which case forces each clause of it, tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed (pick something that you think will make me squirm, and feel free to make it about a machine doing the persuading, since I assume that's where this is all heading), and be honest about which of my calls it just can't accommodate. I'd rather have a clean principle that loses one of my verdicts than a Frankenstein that saves all of them.
