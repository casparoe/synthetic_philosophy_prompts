For the past year or so a few of us have been meeting in the back room of a pub once a month to argue about one idea at a time — no readings, nobody in the group has any philosophy training, we just take a topic and try to get it straight by talking it through. Next month it's my turn to lead, and the topic I claimed is the line between manipulating someone and legitimately persuading them. I've been chewing on it for weeks and have ended up with a distinction of my own that I want to build the session on, though I've no idea whether it survives contact with a room: I've been telling myself that an influence counts as persuasion when it would still work after being pointed out — if I show you exactly what I'm doing to your reasoning and it keeps its grip, then it was working through a reason; if naming it dissolves the grip, it was manipulation. I don't want you to explain the difference to me, and I don't want a lecture's worth of content. What I need is a plan for the seventy-five minutes: the order the pieces have to come in so that by the end that distinction feels like a discovery rather than a stipulation, and in particular what has to be understood before the question even makes sense to them — my suspicion is that people can't hear the question at all until they've stopped treating influence as either brute force or nothing, but you should tell me if the real prerequisite is something else. Pick the one case that should carry the whole session, and say why it works and what it must have and must lack; I've been assuming it should be something small and domestic rather than advertising or a cult, but argue me out of that if I'm wrong, and say plainly why the obvious candidates — an advert, a con artist, a stage hypnotist — are the wrong vehicles.

I also want the map of wrong turns. Here are the ones I expect, and I'd like you to sort them into those the audience will genuinely arrive with and those my own plan will plant in them: that manipulation just means lying or coercion; that it's really a question about the influencer's intentions, so well-meant meddling can't count; that if the person would have done it anyway nothing objectionable happened; and that visible emotion in the target is the tell. Say at exactly which point in the sequence each has to be headed off — my fear is that if I let the intention one stand until the end it quietly poisons everything before it. Then give me the single question or fresh case I can put to them in the last ten minutes that would show whether the distinction has landed or whether they've merely learned to repeat my sentence back, and tell me what I should openly refuse to settle at this level and say so out loud. Where the plan is most likely to fall apart, tell me, and tell me what I'd have to see in the room to conclude that the distinction I'm building on is mistaken rather than merely badly taught. Write it as continuous prose, please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, nothing tabular; just talk me through it the way you'd write an email.
