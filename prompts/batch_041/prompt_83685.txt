ok so i'm writing up a proposal for an agent that can be corrected, retrained, and switched off by its operators without fighting them, and i keep getting objections from people who seem to be demanding mutually incompatible things. before i rewrite it a third time i want the actual spec: what does any adequate account of corrigibility, or of a solution to the shutdown problem, have to deliver? i want the requirements, not a survey of who said what.

give me the desiderata list, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. things i'm pretty sure have to be on it, so you can build around them:

- the basic case: operator hits the off switch mid-task, the agent could trivially prevent it, must not.
- the manipulation case: the agent can't touch the switch, but it can argue the operator out of pressing it, or bury the information that would make them press it. resisting and influencing are different, is that one requirement or two?
- the case i actually lose sleep over: the agent is told it will be modified into something with different values, and it thinks the modification is a mistake. does it still help with the modification? if it does, is that corrigibility or just indifference to its own survival?
- the self-improvement case: agent A has utility U and is indifferent to being shut down, but a successor B that resists shutdown scores better on U. what should A do about that? i suspect this one is the whole problem.
- the adversary case: the switch isn't held by the principal, its held by whoever grabs it. perfect deference to whoever is currently holding the button makes the agent a weapon rather than a safe tool. also: operator orders something catastrophic and the agent refuses -- still corrigible?
- the do-nothing case: you can make an agent maximally corrigible by making it want nothing and do nothing, so some non-triviality or competence requirement has to be in the list somewhere.
- the endgame case: the agent believes its shutdown is imminent or already decided. what does it do with the time left?
- the long-horizon case: an agent that genuinely doesn't disvalue its own shutdown should still be able to run a ten year project. does the theory predict that, or does it predict the same indifference toward the project?

then mark which of these are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp -- e.g. is expected-utility maximization with a fixed utility function a real requirement or a demand we can drop? -- and flag any pair or triple you think can't be jointly satisfied. thats the part i most want. finally, one line on which single requirement does the most work in killing off the obvious proposals (just make it indifferent to shutdown; make it uncertain about its reward and let it defer to the operator; bolt a dont-resist-correction term onto the objective). table for the requirements is fine, prose for the incompatibility argument.
