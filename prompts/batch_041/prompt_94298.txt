Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of scientific rationality must meet once its unit of appraisal is the research programme rather than the isolated hypothesis. State each requirement together with the case or consideration that forces it — for instance, that the theory must sort progressive from degenerating programmes on the evidence available to a contemporary, not on how things later turned out; that it must explain why it can be rational to keep working inside a programme whose central commitments are shielded from direct falsification; that it must respect the gap between a programme's being rationally abandoned and its being shown false — but build the list yourself, and add at least one requirement the standard accounts would rather not face. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the prejudices of a particular camp, and say whether any subset cannot be jointly satisfied; if some are jointly unsatisfiable, that is the result I most want to see. Then, briefly, how Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes fares against the list, and — since a criterion no one can apply in real time is worth little — whether your list could be used to judge a programme still in progress, where the verdict cannot wait on the eventual record. Finish by naming the argument or consideration that would change your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
