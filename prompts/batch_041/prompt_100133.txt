Ok, so I'm the "game theory guy" in my friend group — I've read way too much of the Sequences and the LW-adjacent decision theory stuff, and now I host a board/card game night basically every Wednesday (4-8 people, rotating cast), plus I play in an online correspondence league where the games are rated and I definately care about my rating. The thing I keep wanting, every time we crack open something new, is a way to cross off moves before I start playing.

Not "what's the best move" — just: which of my options can I delete, because some other single option, or some probability mix of my other options (I think that's a mixed strategy, right?), does at least as well against every single thing my opponent could do? Then delete again from what's left, and keep going until nothing more falls out. I know this is just iterated elimination of dominated strategies and I know the textbook story — it assumes common knowledge of rationality, assumes everyone's running the same iterations on each other, etc. But the textbook story is not a procedure I can run in twenty minutes with a pen while people are setting up the table.

So: give me the procedure. Steps in order, what counts as passing at each step, when to stop, and how to tell "this option is dead" apart from "this option is dead only if I also assume everyone else is doing this same reasoning back at me." If there's a quick visual/geometric test for "is this option beaten by some mix of my other options" that doesn't require setting up a linear program, give me that instead of the LP. And it has to be something I can explain to a normal person in one sentence, because if the procedure says "never play that," I have to justify it to people who are two drinks in, and "trust me, I did the math" does not cut it.

Run it on two cases, start to finish, showing your work:

Case 1: a three-option game a friend invented. We both secretly pick A, B, or C. It's symmetric. My payoff is 3 if we match on A, 3 if we match on B, 0 if we match on C, 1 if we pick different letters — except A vs B, which pays 0 to both of us.

Case 2: the 0-to-100 number game where you win by being closest to two-thirds of the average. I know the textbook answer is that everyone should end up at 0. I also know that every time I've actually played it, people pick somewhere between like 15 and 35, and I picked 0 once and came dead last. Run the procedure and tell me straight what it says to play, and whether it's allowed to say anything other than 0.

Then tell me where this breaks. I specifically want the misfires: the weak-dominance thing where the order you cross options off changes the answer, what to do when elimination leaves me with a coordination problem or with nothing at all, and whether I'm ever allowed to un-cross a move because the person across the table is going to play it no matter what the theory says.
