The paper claims to solve the MU puzzle: you start with MI and the only allowed moves are the four rewrite rules -- if a string ends in I you can add U, if it has the form Mx you can add x to get Mxx, you can replace III with U, and you can delete UU -- and the question is whether MU is ever reachable. The usual answer is no. The authors say they get MU by jumping out of the system: they add a meta-rule that lets you replace any string with any other string if the new one is more interesting or illuminating, and they argue that insisting on the original rules is just fetishizing a formal system. One reviewer says this is exactly what JOOTSing is and we should accept it; the other says it's not a solution to the puzzle, it's changing the puzzle, and we should reject. I need a short written opinion (600-800 words) I can circulate before Friday: recommend accept, reject, or accept with conditions, with a concrete next step for the submission and reasoning the authors could actually respond to. Give the strongest point on the losing side a fair hearing rather than a strawman, and say what our general policy should be for future submissions that claim to solve a formal puzzle by stepping outside its rules.

After the memo, give me a short JSON object with fields: decision (one of accept/reject/conditional), one_line_rationale, within_system_vs_meta_level (which is the paper actually doing?), strongest_counterargument, conditions (array of strings), policy_rule, confidence (0-1).
