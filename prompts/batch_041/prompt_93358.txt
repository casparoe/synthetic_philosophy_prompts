A reading group I belong to has been working with a distinction between an epistemic bubble — a person whose picture of the world is skewed because contrary information never reaches them — and an echo chamber — a person whose trust in outside sources has been dismantled in advance, so that contrary information arrives pre-discredited rather than absent. Last week two members, Anna and Ben, got into an argument about it that neither would drop.

Anna said the distinction is one of mechanism, and that it earns its keep because the remedies differ: a bubble is at least in principle cured by exposure, whereas exposing someone in an echo chamber only supplies them with more material to discredit. Ben said the mechanism is the same on both sides, since nobody 'excludes information' — you rely on some sources rather than others, and reliance is trust — so a bubble is an echo chamber that has not finished forming, and the difference is one of degree. Anna said degree is exactly what makes the difference here: a person who reads one newspaper out of habit is not merely a milder version of a person who has been taught that every other newspaper is a liar, and the real difference lies in the history of how the trust was lost rather than in the present shape of the network. Ben replied that if the difference is between warranted and unwarranted distrust, then it is not a feature of the person's belief-forming setup at all — it depends on whether the outsiders really are trustworthy — so calling someone's situation a bubble is always already a verdict about whose testimony counts as knowledge; by Anna's standard, anyone who distrusts flat-earthers is in a bubble. Anna said that is a reductio only if we cannot distinguish distrust that was earned from distrust that was installed. Ben said that history is precisely what a third party cannot read off a person's present epistemic state, and that a diagnostician who declares a bubble or an echo chamber is doing the same thing the chamber does: settling in advance which sources are reliable.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move, saying of each move whether it is right, partly right, or mistaken, and why. Then say who has the better overall position, and what both of them are missing.
