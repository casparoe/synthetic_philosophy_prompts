I'm a cognitive science masters student and I've been in a long-running argument with two friends — one is a machine learning person who's very gung-ho about AI minds, the other is a biologist who thinks the whole question is confused — and there's a move that keeps happening that I don't have a word for. It goes like this. Someone says the material doesn't matter, only the pattern does; the carbon isn't the point, the computation is. Ok, fine, I can get on board with that. But then the second you ask whether some stupid simple thing counts — a thermostat, a lookup table, a big spreadsheet with every possible input and output typed into it in advance, a pile of rocks arranged so that by pure luck it has the same input-output map as a brain — they back off and say no no, it has to be the *right kind* of computation, it has to actually *use* the information, the parts have to be causally wired up the right way, it has to be a process and not a lookup table. And I can't tell if thats definately a legit refinement or if they've just smuggled the physical stuff back in through a side door after telling me for ten minutes that the physical stuff doesn't matter.

The thing that really bothers me: I read somewhere that for basically any physical system you can find *some* way of mapping its states onto the states of some computation so that it counts as running that computation — which would mean which computation it's running isn't even a fact about the system, it's a fact about how we choose to describe it. If that's right then the slogan and the retreat are both in trouble, but in different ways. So: is there a name for the specific move where a substrate-doesn't-matter claim gets defended by quietly re-importing substrate-ish requirements (or organization-ish ones, which might be the same thing in disguise)? And even if there is a name, what I actually want is the carving: how is this different from plain functionalism, or from the multiple-realizability point, or from the old complaint that computation is all syntax and no meaning? Where exactly is the line between a legitimate constraint on what counts and a smuggled-in prejudice, and what would a version of the position look like that doesn't do the smuggling?
