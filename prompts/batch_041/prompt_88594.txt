Consider the gender-equality paradox: across many cross-national datasets, sex differences in self-reported traits — interest in things versus people, agreeableness, risk aversion — come out larger, not smaller, in countries that score higher on indices of gender equality. One standard reading is biological, holding that once cultural constraints are relaxed the underlying disposition shows through; the other is cultural, holding that in egalitarian societies gender is a salient self-descriptive category and respondents answer against a local comparison standard, so the result may reflect the instrument rather than the trait. Argue for a third position: that the pattern is a fact about the instrument before it is a fact about either nature or culture, since both standard readings presuppose that the instrument measures the same construct on the same scale in every country, and without evidence of that invariance the observed pattern is compatible with the underlying distributions being identical everywhere. Assume the reported differences are standardized against a pooled standard deviation that itself varies across countries, and say how that bears on the argument, what a study able to adjudicate among the three readings would have to do, and whether any conclusion about justice — as opposed to about nature or culture — follows from the reading you find most defensible.
