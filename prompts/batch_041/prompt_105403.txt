I'm producing a short interview for our department podcast, and the guest is a philosopher who defends a pretty literal version of the best-of-all-possible-worlds thesis — not the "eh, it's good enough" reading, but the strong claim that among the worlds God could have actualised, this one has the greatest total value, and that God's goodness left him no real option. He has two twists I havn't seen anyone press him on: (1) he reads "greatest" as maximal rather than maximum — this world isn't worse than any other, but some other worlds may be incommensurable with it — and (2) he values worlds as whole histories, so a world can be best overall while long stretches of it are worse than the corresponding stretches of rivals.

Draft me eight to ten interview questions that go past him just restating the view. I want at least one question each on: what makes a world "possible" for him (does the candidate set include worlds with different laws, different free creatures, or only worlds sharing our starting conditions?) and how much of the theodicy's weight that choice is carrying; whether "not worse than any" is actually enough for a being deciding to create, or whether that quietly downgrades "best" into "tied for top of a very loose ranking"; whether a whole-history reading lets him say any particular suffering is for the best, or only that the total is, and what the difference comes to for a grieving parent; how creation can be free or gracious if the best world is the only one God can actualise, and what the antecedent/consequent will distinction is supposed to buy him here; and the no-maximum worry — that if value admits of endless improvement there may be no best world at all, which is exactly the objection his maximality move is meant to dodge, so does it actually dodge it?

For every question, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give, and flag anywhere he'll probably retreat to "we can't compute the values" or "that's a matter of faith."

Keep the whole thing under 800 words, and none of it on "what drew you to philosophy."
