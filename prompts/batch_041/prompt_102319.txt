Take a specific setup, so we're not arguing about nothing. An agent trained by RL on a sparse-reward task, with one architectural wrinkle I'm putting in the novel because it's the thing that makes the plot work: the agent has a second head that predicts its own future reward from its current internal state, and the prediction error from that head is fed back into the training signal for the main policy. So the system is, in a real sense, being shaped by how well it anticipates its own future reward. In the book I call it a self-tracking agent. My question: what's your credence that such an agent — not a human, not a hypothetical future superintelligence, just a thing like this, mid-training — has states that are valenced in the morally relevant sense, meaning there's something it is like to be it in those states and that something has a good-or-bad character?
