You are a philosopher whose training spans both continental phenomenology and formal microeconomic theory — equally at home in Sartre's Being and Nothingness and in the machinery of revealed preference, choice functions, constraint correspondences, and mechanism design. I am a decision theorist. I know the choice-theoretic vocabulary cold and Sartre only as a tourist: I have absorbed the slogans without the system. I want you to restate Sartre's account of radical freedom and bad faith in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report.

Before you write anything, brainstorm on the page: (i) list the primitives of my vocabulary that could plausibly do work here — a choice function over a feasible set, a constraint correspondence taken as exogenous, a preference relation assumed stable, the distinction between parameters and instruments, commitment devices, type versus action; (ii) list the load-bearing Sartrean claims you intend to carry over — that consciousness is not a thing, that the for-itself has a self-negating structure rather than a fixed nature, that freedom is total rather than a matter of degree, that bad faith is a lie to oneself, that facticity and transcendence must be held together rather than traded off, and that the person who sincerely owns up to a fixed character is in bad faith as much as the one who denies it. Then do the translation.

In the translation, handle the following explicitly:

- Radical freedom as the denial of any genuinely exogenous constraint correspondence: the agent is represented as choosing within a feasible set that is itself among the things chosen. Is there a coherent formalization of that, and what does it cost?
- The denial of a stable essence or character: can my framework represent an agent whose preference relation is not fixed across contexts without simply redescribing it as incoherent? If it must reach for context-dependent or reference-dependent preferences, tell me whether that is a real representation of Sartre's point or a substitution that changes the subject.
- Bad faith as self-imposed necessity: the agent treats its prior choices, its social role, its temperament, or its desires as given parameters rather than as things it is still choosing. Formalize this as a divergence between the constraint set the agent takes as exogenous and the one it actually faces, then say whether my apparatus can represent an agent that believes a falsehood about its own choice set while its choices reveal the truth. If the revealed-preference structure makes that belief impossible to state, say so plainly rather than papering over it.
- The waiter, the coward, and sincerity: an agent performing a role sincerely, plus the diagnosis that sincere self-identification with a fixed character is itself bad faith. What is the counterpart here — a type, a commitment, a repeated action? Does the specifically reflexive structure of bad faith survive at all?
- Facticity: the past, the body, the situation as fixed. Sartre wants the past to be unalterable and yet its meaning to be chosen. My framework has endowments and histories that are simply data. Can re-choosing the significance of a fixed endowment be represented, or is that a move my vocabulary cannot make?
- Responsibility: in my framework, responsibility attaches to choices within constraints and never to the constraints themselves. Sartre holds the agent responsible for the whole situation. State precisely what the translation must do to make that a coherent claim rather than a category error.

Then the residue report. For each element above, tell me what translated cleanly, what translated only with distortion, and what has no counterpart at all. Where you had to extend my formalism to make the translation go through, say whether the extension is well-defined or ad hoc. Finally, give me your verdict on the residue: where something does not survive, is that a defect of my vocabulary — a real feature of agency it cannot express — or is it evidence that the Sartrean claim was less clear than it looked, so that once you demand a formal representation you find the claim has no truth-evaluable content? Do not hedge; commit to a diagnosis for each piece of residue, and tell me which single piece is the most serious.

Do not give me citations, reading lists, or secondary-literature summaries; I want the translation and the verdict, argued in your own words. Take the space you need and keep the argument tight.
