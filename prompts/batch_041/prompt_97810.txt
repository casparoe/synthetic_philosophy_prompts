I chair the priority-setting committee of a national compensation fund established after a mass-casualty disaster. Under our statute the committee must publish a single schedule that ranks categories of claimant harm by relative severity; that schedule is then loaded into the payout software, which disburses from a fixed pool. The software will not accept a ranking unless it is a strict total order — it will refuse an input in which A sits above B, B above C, and C above A — and our lawyers add that the schedule must be defended by reasons that treat like cases alike, with no unexplained jumps between bands. Those two requirements have now collided with our own judgments.

We are grading three bands of injury that differ along two dimensions. Band 1 is a moderate permanent impairment with no chronic pain. Band 2 is a slightly milder impairment with mild chronic pain. Band 3 is a barely noticeable impairment with severe chronic pain. On the committee's considered view, each neighboring pair is a wash: we do not judge Band 2 worse than Band 1, and we do not judge Band 3 worse than Band 2, because at each step the impairment falls by a perceptible amount while the pain rises only slightly. But we are equally confident that Band 3 is not a wash against Band 1 — the pain at that end is of a different order of magnitude. Some members want to declare the two dimensions incommensurable; others want a threshold below which pain differences simply do not count; others want to draw an arbitrary line and defend it openly as a policy choice rather than a finding.

Draft the memo I will circulate before the vote. Recommend a concrete schedule to publish and a specific course of action for adopting it, with a deadline for the committee to act, and set out the reasoning in a form the fund's counsel could actually defend in a challenge. Close with an honest paragraph on the strongest case for the option I am currently inclined to reject — do not strawman it. Roughly 700 words, addressed to the committee, recommendation and deadline first, reasoning after.
