ok so the IAU vote in 2006 that demoted Pluto — the Prague general assembly, the "cleared the neighbourhood of its orbit" clause, Eris showing up the year before and forcing the issue, all that — i want to use that whole mess as a test case for theories of natural kinds. heres the setup i want you to actually work with, since you cant look it up: the 2006 definition says a planet (a) orbits the Sun, (b) is massive enough to be roughly round under its own gravity, and (c) has cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit. Pluto fails (c), so it gets relabelled a dwarf planet, which is a brand new category invented in the same resolution. A lot of planetary scientists basically ignore the whole thing and keep calling round non-stellar bodies planets anyway.

Now the lens. I want three accounts in play. First, Boyd's homeostatic property cluster view — a real kind is a cluster of properties that reliably co-occur because some causal mechanism holds them together, and thats what makes the kind explanatory rather than just a label. Second, Dupre's promiscuous realism — nature doesnt come pre-carved along one privileged set of lines, lots of different classifications can be equally legitimate, and theres no fact of the matter about which one is "the" joint. Third, the Locke-ish real vs nominal essence distinction, plus Hacking's stuff about us making up categories that then loop back on the things they classify.

So: run the Pluto episode through these. Was "planet" ever a natural kind at all, or was it always a nominal kind / a convenience category that got mistaken for something deeper? Is the 2006 definition a discovery — scientists finally locating a joint — or a decision, a line drawn at a general assembly by people who wanted to stop adding planets? Does "cleared the neighbourhood" do any causal work? I think theres a reading where that criterion tracks something real, gravitational dominance of an orbital zone, some quantitative measure of who actually runs the neighbourhood — if so does that rescue the new category as a Boyd-style real kind, or is it a post hoc rationalization for a boundary someone wanted? And the definition only covers bodies orbiting *our* Sun, so it cant even travel to exoplanets — can a definition that local be aiming at a natural kind, or does that give the game away?

Then flip it. What does the episode show about the theories? Does the lobbying, the backlash, the "Pluto is still a planet to me" stickers and the fact that working scientists just keep using the old word count for Dupre-style pluralism, or is that all just sociology and the astronomy is fine? Where are these accounts silent — e.g. what does any of them say about a kind being settled by a vote? Does the case help kind-realism, hurt it, or reveal the debate was never really about cases like this?

No citations, no reading list, i dont care who said what first — just do the mapping honestly and say where each theory runs out of gas.
