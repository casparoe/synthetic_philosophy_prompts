I got told at my checkup that my blood sugar puts me in the prediabetes range and its turned into a whole argument at home. My wife says I'm not actualy sick, it's just a number a committee picked, and my brother says of course it's real, a pancreas that isn't doing what a pancreas is meant to do is a broken pancreas. I've been reading around alot since then (not after a reading list, I wouldn't understand half of it) and I keep running into this fight between people who think disease and health are just descriptions of how a body is ticking compared to normal, and people who think they're value judgements in disguise, where calling something a disease is already saying it's bad and it's the kind of thing medicine is supposed to fix. Then there's a third lot who say it's both at once and you can't pull the two apart, like the word coward, where the describing and the judging come as one piece. Someone else told me the whole thing is framed wrong and health should be about being able to adapt and cope, not about your organs being in spec.

What I want is the most important implications of all this for my actual situation, not a summary of the debate. Does the cut-off being a convention mean it's all made up and I can ignore it? Does it change anything about whether I take the pills if the doctor offers them, or about how much of this is medical and how much is just me needing to eat better? Do I tell my kids it's a disease? And where does the line between a risk factor and an actual disease come from anyway, because that line is doing an awful lot of work in my case and nobody will say where they got it from. Basically: what does fall out of this and what doesn't, because both sides carry on like everything does.
