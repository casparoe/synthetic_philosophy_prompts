I cover science and ideas for a general-interest outlet, and I'm working on a piece about the centipede game and backward induction -- the thing where two people would both walk away better off if they kept cooperating down the line, but the supposedly airtight "rational" logic says the first player should pull the plug on move one. Problem is the standard textbook version is so well-worn my editor says readers tune out the moment they recognize it. So I want a fresh case for the piece.

Build me a centipede-style game, but set it somewhere ordinary people could actually find themselves -- two coworkers quietly covering for each other on a shared project, two neighbors splitting the cost of a fence, two small suppliers who depend on each other, something like that. Here's what it has to do:

- finite, with a definite last round that both players know about in advance
- the backward induction answer has to be "defect at the very first opportunity," and that has to be the genuine verdict -- don't just stack the payoffs so defecting is obviously best
- the unraveling has to hold up even if both players are fully rational and both know each other's payoffs cold -- so you can't get the fragility out of one of them being a hothead or being in the dark about the numbers
- still, I want the case to expose why that verdict is fragile, so build in one specific feature that does that work, and tell me what it is

Then spell it out for me: what backward induction says at each node, what the two players actually end up with versus what they'd get if they both just hung in there, which single thing in the setup is making the whole paradox tick, and what breaks if you remove it. Finish with the strongest counter-argument -- what a defender of the backward induction answer would say to call the case a cheat, and whether you think that objection actually lands.

Skip the citations and reading lists, I just want the scenario and the reasoning. Keep the setup itself tight, a paragraph or two, and definately don't make me do the math myself.
