Ok so I'm a 4th-year phil PhD (metaethics, mostly reasons and normativity stuff) and my office mate is a hardcore particularist — reasons are holist, there are no true moral principles, every case is its own case — and we've now had the exact same fight like nine times. We decided the only honest way to actually resolve it is satire, so I need you to write the piece for me rather than another seminar paper nobody reads.

Here's the concept. A fictional VC-backed startup (call it whatever you want, maybe Contexta or Meridian) has built its entire internal culture on the particularist thesis. New hires get an onboarding handbook instead of a code of conduct: no written rules, no policy manual, just "case immersion", assigned mentors, and something they've branded context pods. I want you to write that handbook — deadpan corporate voice, ~700 words, short numbered-ish sections or a Q&A, your call — as if the people who wrote it genuinely believe all of it. The comedy has to come from actually knowing the position: reasons that shift valence depending on what else is true (the same feature that's a reason to promote someone in one case is a reason to let them go in another), the denial that principles are needed to codify anything, moral competence as a kind of perception rather than rule-application. And I want it fair — a Dancy fan should laugh and nod, not wince, so no cheap "lol relativism" moves; the best jokes should be ones a particularist would have to admit are real consequences of their own view.

Two things to make it actually bite. First, they keep accidentally reinventing general rules and refusing to admit it — there's a whole internal euphemism for it ("creeping generality", a "provisional pattern", a "case-cluster") — and the handbook should keep doing this in the most self-unaware way possible. Second, seed in a recurring dissenter: a compliance officer's margin notes, or a one-star Glassdoor-style review tacked on at the end, from someone who just wants to know what the actual policy is. That way the reader feels the real objection — can this scale, who adjudicates when two mentors disagree, what does consistency across cases even mean if reasons are holist — without anyone ever stating it. Dont name any real living people, and obviously I don't need citations or a reading list, just write the thing.
