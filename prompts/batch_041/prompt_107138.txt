So I've been reading a lot of Russell on marriage and free love lately, and it's got me trying to pin down what I actually think, because my gut keeps contradicting itself. Here's a run of verdicts, mostly hypothetical, and I want to know what principle gets them, if anything does.

Two people promise each other exclusivity and both keep to it: fine, obviously. Two people promise each other exclusivity, one has a one-night thing, and tells the other the next week: I think that's genuinely pretty bad, not a technicality. Same thing but the other never finds out and never will: still bad, in my gut. So it isn't just about the lying. Couple agrees to be open, no secrets, both actually fine with it: fine. Couple is open, but one of them only agreed because they were scared of being left and is quietly miserable about it: I think the one who pushed for it is doing something wrong, even though nothing was hidden and no promise was broken. Couple is nominally monogamous, but one has a long-standing thing on the side that they've both tacitly never discussed (the other basically knows): I think this is... fine? Or at least way less bad than the hidden one-night stand, which seems like it shouldn't be true. A couple where one partner is totally financially dependent on the other and the other starts sleeping around, with the dependent one's blessing: something feels off here and I don't think it's about the sex. And the same situation but in a place and time where divorce is impossible and the dependent one would be destitute: I feel pretty much the same as the previous one.

My hunch is the badness is tracking deception plus some kind of power/dependency thing, and not sex as such. But that hunch makes case 3 (nobody ever finds out) not bad, and case 6 (tacit, half-known) not fine, and both of those go against me. And I can't tell whether the real principle is about broken promises, about some good the relationship has that gets damaged, or about what the other person was reasonable in expecting.

What I want: one or two candidate principles stated precisely enough that I could apply them to a case I haven't met, which of my verdicts has to be dropped to keep them, and what they predict for a case I didn't list -- say, a couple who both genuinely want the open arrangement but one of them hides it from their family and close friends, who'd be devestated if they knew. Also, be honest about whether the principle ends up on Russell's side or against him, as you read him. I want reflective equilibrium here, not a survey of what other people have said.
