I chair the scalable-oversight review panel at an AI laboratory, and I must bring a written opinion to Thursday's meeting on whether to certify a proposed evaluation protocol as adequate evidence for the safety of a model we intend to deploy. The protocol, in outline, is this: for any question put to the model, two copies of it argue for opposing answers, and a separate, deliberately weaker judge model — one we take to be substantially less capable than either debater — decides which side made the better case. The research team's claim is that the honest debater can always force the judge towards the correct answer, so that a judge which reliably picks the winner is thereby a judge which reliably reaches the truth, even on questions it could not have answered unaided. Cross-examination, they add, is what stops a debater from winning on a case the judge cannot check.

My worry, which I want the opinion to meet head-on, is the obfuscated argument. A debater may offer a case whose intermediate steps are each individually plausible to the judge and whose conclusion happens to be true, yet which is not sound at all; and to the judge this may be indistinguishable from a sound argument that is merely too long or too technical to follow. If the judge cannot verify the steps, then winning and being right come apart, and the protocol's guarantee lapses. Worse, the burden is asymmetric: complexity is cheap for a dishonest debater and costly for an honest one, so a protocol that rewards a judge's willingness to be persuaded by elaborate chains may systematically favour the side that has something to hide.

Please draft the short written opinion I shall circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — certify, refuse certification, or certify subject to named conditions — with reasons the research team could actually respond to. Be specific about what further evidence would change the panel's mind, and about whether the guarantee survives once arguments require many steps rather than one. Give the strongest version of the team's reply a fair hearing rather than dismissing it, and note any respect in which our own testing regime is open to the charge of circularity.

Please write the opinion in British English.
