so I've been reading rat-adjacent stuff for like a decade — the Sequences, all the expected-value discourse, the whole shut up and multiply thing — and through a series of bad decisions I've ended up as the person on the grantmaking team at a small animal welfare foundation who actually has to make calls on invertebrate proposals. We're tiny: four staff, a board, maybe $2M out the door a year, and about a quarter of the portfolio is now invertebrate welfare because two donors got excited about it. The board is split down the middle — half think it's obviously where the expected value is, half think it's a meme and we're lighting money on fire for animals that might be little robots. I keep getting asked in board meetings what our framework is and I keep winging it, which is bad and also getting embarrassing.

What I want is an actual decision procedure I can run on a proposal, not a philosophy essay. Like: the questions I ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops and we decline, where it says fund, and where it hands it to the board because the remaining question is values and not facts. It has to be grounded in the real shape of the scale argument — the move where insects are so numerous that even a tiny probability of sentience multiplied by an astronomical number of individuals swamps the welfare of the vertebrates we're actually confident about — and it has to survive someone in the room saying but what if they're basically tiny robots, so I need the objections built into the procedure rather than bolted on after. Concretely it needs to handle: the probability-of-sentience-per-taxon problem (is a shrimp more likely to matter than a black soldier fly larva, and what do I do when the evidence is thin for both?), the difference between number of individuals and amount of morally relevant experience (a billion larvae living two weeks versus a million chickens living two years), uncertainty about intensity and duration, the tractability side (welfare per dollar, and whether welfare per dollar is even the right denominator when the numerator is this speculative), and the failure mode where we fund speculative invertebrate work and crowd out a boring, well-evidenced vertebrate program — that last one is the skeptical half of the board's whole argument and I don't have a good answer to it yet.

Constraints: decisions happen quarterly, I get about two weeks of staff time per cycle, anything over $250k needs board sign-off, and the person running the procedure will be me, not a philosopher. Then run the finished procedure on three real-ish proposals so I can see it bite: (1) funding development of an electrical stunning device for farmed shrimp, where the crustacean evidence is thin but not nothing and the supply chain is huge; (2) funding research on humane slaughter methods for black soldier fly larvae reared for animal feed, where the numbers are absurd and the sentience evidence is thinner still; and (3) a pilot on reducing insecticide use in agriculture for the sake of wild insects, where the numbers are the biggest of all but the intervention is diffuse and basically unmeasurable. I want one of those to come out decline even though I personally want it funded, and I want you to be upfront about what the procedure can't settle and where it's just me dressing up a gut call as a flowchart. Don't give me citations or a reading list, I can't use those in a board memo, I just want the logic and the steps. American English please.
