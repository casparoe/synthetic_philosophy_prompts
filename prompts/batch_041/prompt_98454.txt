So I run a monthly discussion group that's basically a LessWrong-adjacent meetup — about 14 people, mostly software types, a couple of phil grad students, and a few who just came because a friend dragged them. We get a community room for 90 minutes and it splits nicely into two halves with a little aisle down the middle, which I feel like I can use. We've been doing decision theory this term and next month I want to do Death in Damascus, because everyone's heard of Newcomb and nobody's actually sat with teh Damascus version.

Here's what I actually want them to feel, and it's really hard to get across by just explaining it: how evidential reasoning about your *own* upcoming action can fail to ever settle. In the story Death has already fixed on being wherever you end up, so the moment you notice yourself deciding to stay in Damascus you conclude that staying is fatal and that Aleppo would've been safe, so you flee — and then you notice yourself fleeing and conclude you'll die in Aleppo *because* you fled, so you want to stay, and it just loops, there's no option that stays put once you've picked it (no "ratifiable" choice, if I've got the term right). And the follow-up I find genuinely unsettling is that the patched versions don't obviously help: bolt on a rule that breaks the loop by having you pick a mixed strategy, and you're still the kind of agent who'll cheerfully buy a $1 ticket that pays $11 if you survive the night — which is a dreadful bet, because by hypothesis you don't survive it. I want them to run smack into that from the inside instead of hearing me narrate it.

So: design me a runnable activity for this room and this 90 minutes. I want people physically committing to a choice, getting burned, wanting to switch, getting burned again, and only then cottoning on to the thing they're stuck in. No real money — tokens, points, pride, whatever. It's totally fine to stage Death as a role or a facilitator operating a rule, and I'm happy to use a confederate or a rigged mechanism, but the activity must not tip off from the inside whether Death is *causing* your fate or merely *predicting* it — that ambiguity is kind of the whole point, and I want the debrief to cash it out (causal vs evidential, ratifiability, why the loop-breaker is still exploitable). Also please make the rules such that "I refuse to choose" isn't a live option, since in the real puzzle you can't decline to act. Give me rules, materials, the token/payoff scheme, a timeline, and the debrief that maps each beat of what happened onto the ideas — and, the part I need most, an honest list of what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle each one, plus which feature of the puzzle the activity simply can't reproduce and I'll have to supply by talking about it afterwards. I dont need any readings or references, nobody's going to look them up.
