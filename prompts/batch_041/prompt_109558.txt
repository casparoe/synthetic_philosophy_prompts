I've been reading that Williamson's anti-luminosity argument is the decisive refutation of the KK principle, the thesis that if you know something, you are in a position to know that you know it. The margin-for-error reasoning is supposed to show that at each step of the iteration — I know, I know that I know, I know that I know that I know — you could be just barely wrong, so knowledge can never be iterated at all, and no one ever knows that they know anything. If that's right, doesn't it follow that epistemology is impossible as a discipline, since you could never establish that you know a philosophical claim rather than merely believe it? And doesn't the same argument show that introspection is unreliable in general, so that I can't know whether I'm in pain, only believe that I am?
