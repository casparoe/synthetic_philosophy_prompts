I've been thinking about Goodhart's law and specification gaming alot, and I want to stress-test a view I've landed on after a long argument with two friends in a philosophy of science reading group. The view is this: Goodhart's law isn't really a law about metrics, or a piece of management folklore, or even a distinct phenomenon. It's a special case of a more general claim about optimization under selection. Whenever an agent is selected or rewarded for producing a proxy P that is supposed to stand in for a target T, and the agent can search over actions, P will drift from T as a function of two things: how much information about T survives the compression into P (I've been calling this metric entropy) and how far the agent's search can reach into regions where P and T come apart (search reach). When search reach exceeds metric entropy, you get gaming; and the only durable fixes change the selection environment rather than adding more elaborate metrics. I think this covers the standard cases -- hospital waiting-time targets, standardized test scores, Soviet production quotas, RL agents finding reward hacks -- and also predicts that lots of supposed fixes, like random audits or multiple metrics, just push the gaming up a level instead of stopping it.

What I want from you is what would count against this. Not the usual objection that Goodharting can be mitigated by goodhart-proof metrics. I want the observations, arguments, formal results, or real-world developments that should actually move me, ranked from decisive to merely awkward. Put differently: what would have to be true for the view to be false, and which of those possibilities are live rather than just logically possible? Which parts of the view are doing the real work -- the metric entropy/search reach distinction, the claim that fixes only work by changing selection, or the reduction of all gaming to one mechanism? What would defenders of this view concede counts against them, and what would they wave away? And please be honest about whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every counterexample can be redescribed as search reach being high enough or metric entropy being low enough, is that a bug, or is it just what a general explanatory framework looks like? I'd also like to know what the view predicts that rival accounts of specification gaming don't, and whether there's a clean case where it makes a wrong prediction. Answer in American English.
