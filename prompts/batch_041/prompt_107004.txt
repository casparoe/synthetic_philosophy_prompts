Compare two accounts of the authority an advance directive holds over the person one later becomes. On the authorship account, the directive binds because the person who signed it and the patient who now lives under it are the same person, so her earlier considered judgment continues to speak for her. On the stewardship account, the earlier person held only temporary custody of a life she would not herself inhabit, and the directive binds at most as a defeasible default that yields to the present interests of whoever actually lives that life. The two accounts agree on routine cases; they separate when the now-incapacitated patient cheerfully resists the directive. State what each account must conclude there, then argue which is more defensible. In doing so, address whether the authorship account adds anything to the bare facts of psychological and bodily continuity, and whether it is coherent for the earlier self to hold a veto over the later one that the later one cannot lift.
