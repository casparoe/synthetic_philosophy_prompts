A friend has a stock reply whenever I say that my own choices cannot matter — that my carbon footprint is a rounding error, that my vote will not decide an election, that one person's boycott moves no market. She asks, 'But what if everyone did that?' I have never been able to tell whether this is an argument or a slogan, and I would like the possible arguments set out. Lay out the distinct precise claims the one-liner could be making, from the nearly trivial to the contentious, and give a verdict on each: platitude, substantive and defensible, or false. I suspect that at least one serious reading has nothing to do with what would happen if everyone literally acted as I do, and instead treats my choosing one way as evidence about how the many other people relevantly like me will choose; I have heard this offered as the rationale for cooperation in a large world, and I cannot see how it differs from the folk reading or from the universalising ones. For each reading, say whether it actually engages the objection I was making — that my own action has no causal effect — or whether it changes the subject; and if the evidential reading is the one doing the work, say what it would have to claim in order to succeed, and whether it would also oblige me to cooperate with anyone who reasons as I do, whatever their ends. Give me the menu, one entry per reading, each with its verdict, and finish by naming the reading my friend must have in mind if her reply is to be a reply.
