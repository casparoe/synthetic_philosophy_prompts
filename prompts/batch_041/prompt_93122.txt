I've been calling myself a prioritarian for a couple years now, and my friend just took the whole thing apart in about five minutes, and I honestly can't tell if the argument is good or if I'm being conned. Help me find the exit.

Here's the argument, written out as cleanly as I can.

(1) The priority view says that a benefit to a person matters more, morally, the worse off that person is. The standard way to write that down: the value of a distribution is the sum over people of f(how well off each person is), where f rises but flattens out, so each extra unit of welfare is worth less the higher up the person already is.

(2) Now suppose the total amount of welfare to be split between two people is fixed. Then the priority view says: split it as evenly as you can. Because with a flattening f, moving a unit from the better off person to the worse off person always adds more value at the bottom then it takes away at the top, so you keep moving until they're level.

(3) So the priority view prefers the more equal of any two distributions that have the same total.

(4) But preferring equality is exactly what egalitarians do. Underneath all the machinery, what the priority view is really committed to is equality.

(5) So the priority view treats equality as good in itself.

(6) And any view that treats equality as good in itself is open to the leveling-down objection: it will rank a world where everyone is equally badly off above a world where some people are much better off, even though nobody is better off in the first world.

(7) So the priority view is open to the leveling-down objection too, and the one thing I thought it had over plain egalitarianism is gone.

I definately want to keep the priority view, and specifically I want to keep the thing I bought it for, which is that it isn't open to the leveling-down objection. So: which step is doing the damage, why does that step look so innocent, and what do I have to give up if I reject it? And is there a version where I keep the priority view and still owe the egalitarian something?
