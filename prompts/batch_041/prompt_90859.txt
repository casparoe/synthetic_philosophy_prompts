I've been going back and forth on this for a history essay and I think I've landed somewhere, so tell me if I've got it wrong. My position is basically the structuralist one: Nazi Germany wasn't really caused by Hitler the person. The conditions were all already there — the treaty, the depression, the fragile republic, the paramilitaries, the whole revanchist and antisemitic mood — and if Hitler had been hit by a car in, say, 1930, somebody else from that same milieu would have ended up doing something very close to what he did. Different name, maybe slightly different timing, but the same basic thing. So the Great Man version is mostly hindsight bias: we tell it that way because he's the one who happened to be standing there.

What I want is what would count against this. Not "well, he was a good speaker" — I mean actual evidence or arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be. Which specific things would have had to go differently for the structuralist story to be wrong? Does it matter that a lot of the most extreme stuff — the specific fixation on the east, the racial laws, the war aims — looks like it was *his* and not something the party or the generals were already pushing for? And is there any way to make this actually testable, or does it all just come down to how you imagine the counterfactual? Someone told me the honest way to do it is the "minimal rewrite" — change as little as possible from the real world and see if the outcome survives — so tell me what the minimal rewrite is that would have avoided it, and whether that test is rigged in the structuralist's favour from the start.

I also want what structuralists themselves would concede counts against them, not just the stuff critics throw at them. And be honest with me: has "the structures did it" basically become unfalsifiable? If every way things could have gone differently gets claimed as "still the same underlying structure playing out," is the view risking anything anymore? If it isn't, is that a real problem for a view like this, or is that just how historical explanation has to work? And what does the structuralist view actually predict that the Great Man view doesn't — like, where should they disagree about something we could go and check?

The thing that would convince me personally is a case where a genuinely different person at the top led to a genuinely different direction despite the same structural setup — not just a different style or a different timetable, but a different thing happening — or a case where the structures were just as bad and nothing like this happened. If that kind of comparison is impossible, tell me why, and tell me what the closest thing to it would be. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and don't let me off easy on the ones that are just awkward.
