For two years a colleague and I have been arguing about whether any truths are analytic, and neither of us has moved. She holds that the category is empty or incoherent: nothing is true solely in virtue of meaning, and whatever we call analytic is either an ordinary empirical claim or a stipulation about how we propose to use words. I hold that she is running together two things that must be kept apart — a sentence's being true in virtue of its semantic content, and our being entitled to accept it without empirical investigation — and that once these are separated the real question is which of them, if either, does any work. We agree on the linguistic facts and on every case either of us can produce. What I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal dispute about the word 'analytic', or two parties answering different questions — one about semantics, one about the epistemology of the a priori — under a single heading? If some of it is verbal, what is the non-verbal residue, and what would count as progress on that residue? My own guess is that the verbal part is larger than either of us admits, and that what remains is a disagreement about whether a priori entitlement needs a semantic explanation at all; tell me whether that diagnosis holds up, and what, concretely, would move the question.
