I'm building a thing on whether a colony can have a mind, and I want a case that does something specific instead of just reheating the usual Hofstadter-ish "ants are neurons, so the colony is a brain" analogy.

Here's what I'm after: a thought experiment where an ant colony really does have a mind — it perceives, remembers, decides, and in principle you could argue with it — but where the mind is not a further entity sitting over and above the ants, and where no single ant and no proper subset of the ants is itself a subject of experience. So "the colony has a mind" comes out true, but you can't point at the part of the colony that is the mind. It also has to be genuinely one mind, not just a bundle of ant-level processes that happen to correlate — except the unity can't come from a central place where everything gets integrated, because I want no such place to exist. And to make it harder: the colony's memory should outlive every ant in it, so that the colony cannot distinguish what it learned from what it was born with. That last bit is doing real work for me, so keep it.

Constraints: stick to plausible ant biology, no sci-fi, no souls or special substances, one paragraph for the scenario itself. Don't just stipulate that it has a mind — the behavior in the scenario has to be the kind of thing that would at least make a skeptic pause.
