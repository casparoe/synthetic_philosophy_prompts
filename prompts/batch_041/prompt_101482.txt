so I'm a novelist, about two-thirds through a draft whose main character is one of those rationalist types who updates on everything, and writing her has made me realize I should get my own epistemic house in order before I keep putting words in her mouth. here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it actually hangs together:

1. I don't think you ever get a proposition for free. Everything you learn, you learn with some noise — reading a thermometer, hearing a friend say something, glancing at a dial — it's all partial. So I don't buy the picture where some proposition just becomes certain and you condition on it.

2. Given that, I think the right general rule for changing your mind is Jeffrey's: you take your new credences in each of some set of mutually exclusive cases, keep your old conditional credences given each case, and average them together. Ordinary conditioning is just the special case where one of the cases goes to probability 1.

3. I think the order you learn things in shouldn't matter. If I find out A with some uncertainty and then B with some uncertainty, versus B then A, and both routes leave me holding the same total evidence, I should end up in the same place. Getting there in a different order shouldn't move where I land.

4. I'm a subjectivist about credences — they're degrees of belief in my head, and I don't think there's a fact of the matter about what my prior should have been. Two people can start in different places and neither one is irrational just for that.

5. I think I can learn things I'd previously ruled out entirely. Characters do this all the time, and so do I — I was sure of something, then the world showed me otherwise, and it went from impossible to merely unlikely.

6. And I think updating is conservative in the right way: when I take something new in, the only thing that should shift is my distribution over the cases I'm considering. How likely I took the rest of the world to be, given each of those cases, should stay where it was.

which of these actually fight each other, and how bad is it? if two of them can't both be had, which one is doing the least work and should go? and are any of them only looking like they clash? I'd rather fix the cheap ones and keep the load-bearing ones, so be specific about which commitment forces which, and rank the real problems by how much they'd actually cost me.
