ok so I've fallen down a rabbit hole on Gongsun Long's white horse dialogue (白馬論) and I want a poem out of it. my reading group does one weird text from the Chinese canon a term, I got voluntold to open this one, so it's a real occasion and I'd like it to actually be good.

the gist, in case it's fuzzy: two unnamed guys argue over 白馬非馬. the first says horse names shape, white names color, and what names color isn't what names shape, so a white horse is not a horse. the second says come on, if there's a white horse then there's a horse. then the first pulls the move I can't stop thinking about: if you ask someone for a horse, yellow and black horses will do; ask for a white horse and they won't. so what's being asked for isn't the same in the two cases, so a white horse is not a horse. as far as I can tell the whole fight turns on 非 doing double duty (is not the same as / is not a kind of) and neither of them noticing they're playing different games with it, which is very two-LessWrong-commenters-arguing-past-each-other-while-agreeing of them.

what I want: 16-20 lines, no fixed form, no rhyme needed, two voices. one line gets said by both of them, and the poem has to make it mean different things in their mouths without the narration explaining it -- I want the difference audible in the line itself. bring that line back at least three times, and the last time it has to land as plainly false where the first time it landed as plainly true. the yellow and black horses should actually be led out and turned away on stage, not just named. don't resolve it, but do let the reader feel the exact beat where they're answering different questions -- one of them right about words, the other right about animals, neither hearing the other. if someone whos read the dialogue can't find a mistake in either mans argument as you've put it, thats the bar.
