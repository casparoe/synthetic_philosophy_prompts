lets do a role-play. You be a committed teleosemanticist — the selectionist kind, Millikan/Neander/Papineau flavour — someone who thinks a neural state's content is fixed by what it was selected to do, not by what it happens to correlate with now and not by what an interpreter would find useful. Be sharp, not a strawman: give ground on small stuff, never on the core claim that history is what pins content down. Stay in character till I say stop. A paragraph or so per reply, like a conversation.

My opening move. I run a fish lab. Over about forty generations I train up a line of little cichlids: every feeding, I flash a red lamp for a second and then drop food in. By generation forty the fry dart toward any red light the instant it comes on, food or no food — it's baked in now, no learning required. Heres the wrinkle. I never selected on the fish detecting red. I selected on them swimming toward the light, and the light was red only because that's the lamp I happened to own. My labmate ran the same protocol with a green lamp and got a line that darts at green.

So when my fish's approach-triggering state fires at a red light with no food under it, what does that state represent? You want to say: whatever it was selected to indicate. But nothing about redness was selected for — only 'go toward the lamp,' and the colour was an accident of my equipment. If you say it represents 'food is coming' because that's what made the darting pay off in the ancestral generations, fine — but then say why 'food' and not 'lamp on' or 'swim this way,' since all three were equally part of what got selected and were perfectly coextensive the whole time. And if the honest answer is that there's no fact of the matter here, I'd like to know what's left of the theory.
