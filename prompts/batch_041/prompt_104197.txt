I'm in a graduate seminar on agency and artificial intelligence, and our assignment is to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over whether large language models are agents. The first camp — call it the agency camp — holds that an LLM is, or can be, a genuine agent: a system that pursues ends, acts for reasons, and is a proper target of belief- and intention-attributions. The second — the simulator camp — holds that an LLM is best understood as simulating a space of possible agents, so that the goals and reasons we observe belong to a simulated character rather than to the model itself; on this view the model is a simulator, not an agent.

Write the document both camps could sign. It should contain (1) a numbered list of agreed claims — about the behavioural facts, about what agency would require, about which observations are neutral between them, and about what would count as evidence either way; (2) the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would endorse; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case, thought experiment, or experiment both camps agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of the dispute strikes you as merely verbal — a difference in how a term is used rather than a disagreement about the world — say which part, and say where the two views turn out to be compatible rather than opposed.

Two sub-issues I want handled explicitly, though you should add others if they matter: whether 'simulating an agent' and 'being an agent' can come apart at all, and whether the simulator view is a claim about mechanism or about the correct level of description. Keep every agreed claim substantive — no formula so vague that both sides could shrug it off.
