ok so for my english unit we've been reading about three different ways a person can react when they get dissed, and my teacher calls them honor culture, dignity culture and victimhood culture. the basic idea: in an honor culture the insult is a debt that has to be payed back and if you dont pay it back you're nothing; in a dignity culture you don't care what strangers think and you just walk away; in a victimhood culture the point isnt to hit back or shrug it off, it's to get a third party (the school, the group chat, whoever) to take your side and publicly call the other person out.

i want to write a poem for the unit project but not a boring one where i just list the three things. here's my plan. one tiny thing happens, the exact same thing in all three sections -- somebody bumps into you in the hall and doesn't say sorry. the bump is worded the same way every time. only the reaction changes, and the form has to do all the work, so the words honor, dignity, victim and culture cant show up at all, not once.

section one: rhymed couplets in a hard stomping meter, and the last line has to sit there like a bill somebody still owes me, like the rhyme only pays off if the other person comes back and answers it. section two: blank verse, no rhyme, unrhymed iambic pentameter, flat and even, and it just refuses to hand anything back, so there's no loop to close. section three: the poem turns and starts talking to somebody who wasnt even there, the reader or the crowd, and asks them to decide who was wrong. 8 to 10 lines each, so 30ish total.

then the ending, and this is the part im stuck on. im writing a poem, so im already talking to a audience about a wrong i think somebody did to me. i want the last few lines to notice that the poem im writing is itself doing the third thing, and then leave me hanging about whether that means all three of these are just different ways of being watched. dont resolve it, i want the tension left open. and i want somebody who's actually studied this stuff to read it and think the poem gets the three logics right instead of just name-dropping them.
