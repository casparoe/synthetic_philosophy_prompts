I am the standards editor at a weekly magazine, and roughly once a week I have to decide what to do with a submission that blames someone for something: a lobbyist for one firm arguing that a rival greenwashes; a woman denouncing a safety violation at a larger company when she was herself fined for the same violation; a state senator attacking a policy she voted for. Readers and staff object that the author has no standing to say it, and our internal arguments about this go nowhere. I have no philosophy background and about twenty minutes per submission, and what I need is a reusable procedure, not an essay.

Build it around three standings I want kept distinct, since conflating them is what makes our current discussions useless: standing to speak (to put the accusation into public circulation at all), standing to demand (to address the wrongdoer and require an answer or a change), and standing to resent (to have been wronged personally by what was done). My working assumption is that these come apart — a hypocrite may keep the first and lose the second — and I want each question in the procedure to say which standing it bears on, with the places where the distinction is doing no real work marked as such.

Also build in a rule for what I will call the unpaid-debt case: a critic who is still doing the thing, or who did it and has never acknowledged it, versus one who did it, has said so, and has stopped. I suspect these should be treated differently and want the procedure to say how. Then say what the procedure cannot do — in particular, it must not silently merge the question of the accuser's standing with the separate question of whether the accusation is true.

The deliverable is a short ordered list of questions, what counts as a disqualifying answer to each, where the procedure terminates with a decision, and where it must hand the case to me as a value judgment I cannot delegate. Run it on the three submissions above, including any case where it gives an answer I will not like, and end with a final line of the form Verdict: 1) <publish / publish with disclosure / reject>; 2) <...>; 3) <...>.
