I belong to a small reading group that has spent the past term on self-locating belief, and next week it falls to me to circulate a short statement of my own position so that the others can take aim at it. I would rather find the weak joints myself first. What follows is therefore not a request for a survey of the literature, nor for reading suggestions; it is a request for an internal audit of the six claims below, taken literally and with the motivations I have given them.

To fix the case: a fair coin is tossed; if it lands heads, Sleeping Beauty is woken once, on Monday; if it lands tails, she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, with her memory of the first waking erased; on waking she cannot tell which day it is. My claims are these. (1) On waking, her credence that the coin landed heads should remain one half. The coin's chance of heads was fixed before she went to sleep, and waking is not something she learns when she wakes — she knew all along that she would be woken at least once — so there is no new evidence for her to conditionalize on. (2) On waking, she genuinely does not know what day it is: Monday and Tuesday both remain live possibilities for her, and nothing available to her could distinguish them. (3) If, on waking, she were told that it is Monday, her credence in heads should still be one half, since the day of the week is not evidence about a fair coin. (4) I accept the self-indication assumption — the bare fact that there is an experience occurring here, namely mine, strikes me as evidence in favour of hypotheses on which more experiences of that kind occur rather than fewer; other things equal, I would rather my credence went to a world with more observers than to a world with fewer. (5) I have never been able to take the Doomsday argument seriously: the fact that I find myself among the first hundred billion or so humans seems to me to be no evidence at all about how many humans there will eventually be. (6) As for betting, if at each waking she is offered a wager that pays her two dollars if the coin landed tails and costs her three dollars if it landed heads, she should take it.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. Go through the claims pairwise and separate the pairs that are genuinely in tension from the pairs that only appear to be; for the genuine ones, rank them by how much damage they do, say exactly what would have to give in each case, and say what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the package. If some claim is simply false, say so, but distinguish that from its being inconsistent with the others — I care much more about the second. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether any of the six can be held only by adding something I have left unstated.
