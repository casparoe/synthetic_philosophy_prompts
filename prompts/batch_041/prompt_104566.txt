I'm giving a paper at a workshop at the law school next month — the theme is language, obligation and the state, if that helps set the tone — and what I actually need from you is a Q&A prep sheet, not a rehearsal of the talk. The room will be about a dozen contract and criminal law people who mostly want to know whether anything I say cashes out in doctrine, plus two proper philosophers of language: one is an Austin man to the bone, the other reckons Searle settled all of this back in the seventies. So there'll be two quite different flavours of hostility and I want both anticipated.

My claim, crudely put: Austin's old split between a misfire (the act doesn't come off) and an abuse (it comes off but is hollow or insincere) leaves out a third thing I want to call a *non-take*. The conventions are all satisfied, I'm perfectly sincere, the words are plainly heard — and yet the act doesn't bind, because the party it's meant to bind never took it up. I think this matters most for what I'm calling *self-binding performatives* — resigning, disowning a view you once held, forgiving someone, promising yourself something — where the whole point of the act is to change the speaker's own normative standing rather than to impose an obligation on somebody else. The standard Austinian machinery assumes the act works by altering other people's obligations, so for this class I think it gets the direction of fit backwards. That's the bit I expect to be mauled over.

So: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, not the ones I'd get from a philosophy-of-language conference — ordered by how much damage they'd do if they landed, each phrased the way that subgroup would phrase it, each with a two-minute reply, a note on where the reply is soft, and what I should just concede up front. Include the one question I'm lying awake hoping nobody asks. Lead with the single objection most likely to sink me — put that first and then work down — no throat-clearing and don't restate my question back at me. British English, please (apologise not apologize, that sort of thing).
