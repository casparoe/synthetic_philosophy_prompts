I am preparing a short talk on the Mencius–Xunzi disagreement over human nature (性, xing), and I want to make sure I have it right. My reading is that this is essentially a factual dispute: Mencius holds that we are born good, Xunzi holds that we are born bad, and the matter could be settled simply by observing how people actually behave, in which case Xunzi wins easily. What I cannot square is his insistence that anyone can become a sage, which looks flatly inconsistent with a bad nature unless goodness is just imposed on us from the outside, like his image of warped wood that has to be forced straight. A friend suggests the real contrast is that Mencius treats morality as a development of nature whereas Xunzi treats it as a transformation of nature, but that still sounds to me like the same external imposition. How should I present Xunzi's position so that it does not come across as self-contradictory?
