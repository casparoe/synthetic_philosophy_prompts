'The language of thought hypothesis is the view that we think in a language — that thinking is basically inner speech, carried on in whatever natural language you happen to speak. Fodor's central argument is that thought has to be compositional, because we can entertain infinitely many thoughts out of finite means, and compositionality requires a language with a syntax and a vocabulary, so there have to be sentences somewhere in the brain. The hypothesis predicts that creatures without a language, like animals and pre-verbal infants, can't really think at all, and it's also why Fodor holds that all of our concepts are innate. The connectionists killed the whole thing off back in the eighties by building networks that learn without any language-like symbols, so these days it's mostly a historical curiosity.'
