My position, so you have something to aim at: there are only minds and their experiences; there is no mind-independent material substance. I know the classic argument for this is usually dismissed as trading on a confusion between conceivability and possibility, so I do not rest my case on conceivability at all. My first move is linguistic: I claim that the word "object" names a role in a perceptual exchange, roughly the way "the score" names a role in a game, so an unperceived object is like a move in a game no one is playing — not a hidden thing, but an incoherent role. My second move is to explain the stability of the world without Berkeley's God: I say unperceived objects exist as standing dispositions to be experienced, a rule-governed order among possible experiences rather than a substance behind them. I think these two moves rescue the idealist conclusion. I suspect at least one of them is doing less work than I think.
