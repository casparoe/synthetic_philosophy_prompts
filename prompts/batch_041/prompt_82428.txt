I'm drafting a short position piece for a graduate seminar on naturalizing intentional content, and I'd like a consistency check before I commit to it. Here is the package I want to defend.

(1) Representational content is fully naturalized: for any genuinely representational state there is a fact of the matter about what it represents, and that fact is fixed entirely by selection history — evolutionary or learning-based — with no further normative or interpretive ingredient.
(2) Content is determinate: a content ascription is objectively correct or incorrect, not merely more or less useful for our purposes.
(3) A being molecule-for-molecule identical to me but with no selection history whatsoever has no representational states at all, whatever it goes on to do.
(4) What earns content its place in a naturalistic ontology is the explanatory work it does in predicting and explaining behaviour.
(5) The magnetotactic bacterium, which steers by an internal chain of magnetic crystals toward low-oxygen water, represents the direction of lower oxygen concentration and not the direction of the geomagnetic field; the former is what the mechanism was selected for.
(6) Novel contents are no special case: the concept electron is representational content even though nothing was ever selected to detect electrons.

Which of these genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and how badly? Rank the real tensions by severity, say in each case what would have to give, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most at the lowest cost.
