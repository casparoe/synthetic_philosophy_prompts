I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy; everything I know about it comes from a few years of reading on my own. I'm putting together an application for a small fellowship that funds independent work by people outside academia: about six months of part-time work, evenings and weekends, producing one working paper of roughly 5,000 words. Please write the proposal for me. The topic is consequentialist cluelessness, the worry that because we cannot trace the long-run effects of what we do, a theory that evaluates actions by their consequences has no determinate verdicts about almost anything. I think the debate has stalled on a framing I find suspicious, namely that the only options are to bite the bullet (make your best guess at the expected consequences and live with the fact that the guess rests on absurdly thin evidence) or to retreat to a restricted version of the theory (count only foreseeable consequences, or shift to assessing rules rather than individual acts). My half-formed idea comes from my day job. When I change a large unfamiliar codebase I also cannot predict the downstream effects, but the engineering response is neither to guess nor to refuse to touch anything: it is to build abstraction layers and invariants, properties I can establish locally and that hold across a wide range of unknown downstream behavior. I suspect consequentialism needs something analogous, a notion of an act being robust across the space of possible long-run effects, where the robustness itself is something we can sometimes know.

Turn this into a proposal. State the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; I take it to be something like whether there is a non-vacuous and non-question-begging criterion of consequence-robustness, and whether such a criterion vindicates the consequentialist's verdicts or collapses into a view we already have. Explain why the existing responses leave this open rather than settling it. Set out what I would actually do, month by month, and be honest in the method section about how much of the work is conceptual clarification and how much is case-by-case testing; I expect a lot of it is trying to state robustness precisely and then hunting for counterexamples. Say what a good result would look like (a criterion that survives the obvious vacuity objections and yields different verdicts from rule-based alternatives on at least some cases) and what would count as the project failing or having to change shape. Two things I want the proposal to take seriously because I keep getting stuck on them: first, whether cluelessness about consequences is symmetric, since my sense is that we are far more confident about some kinds of long-run bad effects than about long-run good ones, and I want to know whether that asymmetry can be made principled or is just a bias; second, the reflexive problem that this very proposal is an action with long-run consequences I cannot trace, so a criterion telling me to act only when robust may be self-undermining, and I need the proposal to say what it does about that. Keep the scope realistic for six months of evenings and weekends, tell me plainly if the question is too big for that, and don't include citations or a reading list; I will fill those in myself, and I would rather you characterize positions by what they claim than by who holds them.
