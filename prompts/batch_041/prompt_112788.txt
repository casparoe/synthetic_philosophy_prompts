I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun reading moral and political philosophy. I have been working through Rawls’s theory of justice and, more recently, some discussions of algorithmic fairness in which the difference principle is treated as a kind of maximin constraint: choose the arrangement that maximizes the position of the worst-off group. This has left me with a question that I suspect rests on a confusion, but I cannot see exactly where the confusion lies.

My reasoning is as follows. The veil of ignorance is a device for choosing under uncertainty: I do not know which social position I will occupy, so I should choose the principles that maximize my expected utility, or, if I am extremely risk-averse, maximize the minimum. The difference principle, as I understand it, tells us to arrange inequalities so that they benefit the least advantaged. That sounds like maximin. And maximin is just expected-utility maximization with an infinite coefficient of risk aversion. I realize that Rawls adds constraints, such as the priority of basic liberties and fair equality of opportunity, but those look like side conditions; once they are fixed, the difference principle still seems to be maximin over the remaining social primary goods. I have also read that Rawls denies the parties can assign probabilities to their possible social positions, but I do not see why that matters: if probabilities are unavailable, maximin becomes the rational choice by default. So is Rawls’s difference principle simply a special case of a more general welfarist decision theory, with the veil of ignorance supplying the uncertainty and the difference principle supplying the extreme risk aversion? If so, then the disagreement between Rawls and utilitarians is not really about the nature of justice but about how much risk aversion is reasonable behind the veil. And if that is right, then the difference principle has no special authority: it is one point on a continuum of risk attitudes, and a less risk-averse chooser would be equally justified in selecting a utilitarian principle.

I would be grateful if you could tell me whether this is a misunderstanding, and if so, exactly where it goes wrong. I am less interested in a general summary of Rawls than in a precise diagnosis of the inference I have made. Please do not give me a reading list or citations; I want the argument.
