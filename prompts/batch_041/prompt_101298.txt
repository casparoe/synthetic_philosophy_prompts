ok so i've got this argument that's been bugging me for weeks and i'm pretty sure it shows the reflection principle is just false, but i really dont want it to be false. help me out here.

(1) Reflection says: if i'm certain that at some future time my credence in H will be r, then my credence in H now should be r.

(2) I'm a subject in a psych study. they've told me -- and i believe them -- that tomorrow at noon they'll give me a pill that makes me totally, 100% convinced of H, where H is something i currently have basically no evidence about. say H is "there are an even number of blades of grass in my front yard."

(3) So right now i'm certain that tomorrow my credence in H will be 1.

(4) By (1), my credence in H right now should be 1.

(5) But H is a claim about my lawn, and the pill is just a chemical that scrambles confidence. nothing about it is evidence about my lawn, so my credence in H should stay where it was, which is basically nothing.

(6) So (1) is false.

i'd way rather keep (1) than (5). i lean on reflection all the time -- trusting that i'll have thought it through by tomorrow morning, that kind of thing. so where's the slip? is one of the steps invalid, or do i actually have to give up reflection, and if so what exactly do i replace it with, and what do i lose by doing that?

two follow-ups i cant untangle:

(a) if i patch it by saying you only defer to future selves you 'trust' or that are 'better informed' than you, isnt that just circular? does the patched version still do any real work, or have i just renamed the problem?

(b) does it matter that the future me is drugged, as opposed to just rationally updating on evidence? like, if tomorrow i'll instead see some genuinely relevant thing and rationally land on 0.9 in H, is reflection fine there? i have a feeling the drugged case and the evidence case have to come apart somehow but i cant say how, or whether that saves reflection or just relabels it.
