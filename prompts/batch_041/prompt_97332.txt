Right, so I've been going back and forth with my mate Dev — four Vipassana retreats deep and now convinced he's basically a stream-enterer, no offence Dev — about anatta, and we keep talking straight past each other. His line is that "no-self" isn't a claim about what exists at all: it's a technique, a way of loosening the grip, and the Buddha point-blank refused to answer when Vacchagotta asked him whether there's a self, which Dev reckons settles the matter. I'm more of the view that anatta is just... true? Like, there is no self, the aggregates are the whole story, "person" is a handy label and nothing more, and the fact that the Buddha went quiet is because the questioner wasn't ready for the answer, not because there wasn't one. Anyway, we're stuck, so I want us to write the document we should have written months ago.

Draft us a joint adversarial-collaboration statement between the two camps: (1) the "no self, full stop, it's a doctrine" camp, and (2) the "not-self is a practice, the Buddha stayed silent on whether there's a self, and turning it into a metaphysical denial is itself a kind of clinging" camp.

What I want in it:

- The claims both of us can actually sign. Not fluffy ones — stuff about what the texts do and refuse to do, about what any view here has to explain (karma and rebirth, moral responsibility, blame and praise, promises, the fact that we all walk around acting like selves, the first-person "I"), and about what "self" is even supposed to mean: a permanent unchanging essence? the everyday psychological subject? the narrative self? Insist that we pin down the word before we disagree with it.
- The precise disagreements, each one worded so that its own side would accept the wording of its own view (so neither of us feels strawmanned).
- The cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move one of us — and for each crux, a case or argument we both agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about it, written down before we look.

Use cases like these, or better ones if you've got them. First: the vow carried across rebirths — a monk takes a lifelong vow, dies, is reborn with no memories of any of it; who exactly is on the hook for the vow, and does either view have the resources to say? Second: the slow-turnover case, ship-of-Theseus but for a person — every aggregate replaced over decades while the promises, debts and grudges all carry over. Third: the "no one home" report — a meditator who, after years, sincerely reports that the sense of a self has dropped away, yet still keeps her promises and still feels guilty when she's a shit. Is that report evidence for the metaphysics, or just a psychological datum both sides can absorb?

Also, and I care about this bit: tell me which parts of our dispute are just verbal — about the word "self", or about what counts as a "doctrine" versus a "practice" — and which parts are genuine. And flag anywhere one of us is quietly smuggling in an empirical claim (e.g. that believing in no-self actually reduces suffering) that isn't settled by argument at all. Don't bother with citations, we both know the texts and we'd only get the sutta numbers wrong.

Format it as a numbered list of agreed claims, then a disagreements table with the cruxes and both predictions. Keep it in British English and try to make it something we could both genuinely put our names to.
