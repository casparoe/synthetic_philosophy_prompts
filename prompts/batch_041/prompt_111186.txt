I'm the philosopher on a team-taught third-year module on Jesus and history — my colleague does the exegesis, I get one 90-minute session on whether the criteria of authenticity can ever settle who he was, and I need a plan for teaching it, not the content. I know the material; what I want is the order, the carrier and the misconception map.

Here's the dependancy structure I think is right, tell me where it's wrong. Nothing works until they've got the gap between the Jesus of the gospels and the Jesus behind them, and specifically that this is a claim about what kind of documents the sources are, not about what happened — without that, every criterion just sounds like a machine for extracting facts from paperwork. Then the criteria arrive as a proposed fix to a problem they haven't felt yet. Only then the collapse. If some step has to come before all of that, say so.

The part I actually want help with is the carrier. I'm tempted by the baptism by John, the stock embarassment case, but I've a nagging feeling it's too tidy: the story is load-bearing for the church's own account of who Jesus was, so embarrassment might be doing none of the work while feeling like it is. If that's the wrong example, tell me what should carry the thing and why. Whatever we use has to be something they can hold in their heads with no handout, and it has to be able to fail in more than one way, so they can watch different criteria return opposite verdicts on the same item.

Misconceptions, in the order they'll surface, and exactly where in the sequence to head each off: that 'authentic' is one thing (it isn't — he said it, he did it, and it goes back to the earliest layer are three different claims and the criteria only ever touch the last); that dissimilarity is the strong criterion (it's the self-defeating one — the less a saying resembles either its Jewish setting or the early church, the less we can explain how it survived to be written down); that coherence is harmless (it's doing all the real work and it's the most circular); and that the criteria were meant to be applied one at a time, when they were a cumulative case in which each is individually invalid. Also: part of the room will come in thinking the question is settled by faith, another part that it's settled by method. I want both dislodged, but gently, and at the point in the sequence where it'll actually land rather than in the intro.

The twist I want to end on, and the reason I'm bothering at all: the newer move is to stop asking whether a saying is authentic and ask instead how memories of Jesus were formed and transmitted in those first decades, and what a community like that could plausibly remember. I want that to register as a shift in the question rather than as 'here's the next method', so tell me where it goes and what it displaces.

What I'm leaving out, and will say I'm leaving out: multiple attestation in any detail, the manuscript tradition, and Paul. Plus give me one thing they have to do in the last five minutes that shows me they've got the dependency structure rather than a list of criteria — something they produce, not something they answer.

Format: bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate what I've asked for. No reading list.
