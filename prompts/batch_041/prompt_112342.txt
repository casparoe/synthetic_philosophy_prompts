Me and my mate Dan spent two hours arguing last night about refugees — who counts as one, and who's actually obliged to take them in — and he's come away convinced he's solved it. His big idea is to run asylum like a carbon market. Every country gets an annual obligation measured in what he calls protection-years: one person sheltered for a year is one unit, and it doesn't matter why they left, war or drought or a collapsing currency, if they're inside your borders you get the credit. The units are tradeable. If a state doesn't want to host anyone it buys units off states that are already hosting, or off states that volunteer to host extra, at whatever price clears. He says this fixes burden-sharing, kills the endless fight over who's a genuine refugee because everybody counts the same, and — his favourite bit — lets the market reveal which countries actually mean it. I said it was grotesque, he said that's just the squeamishness of people who benefit from the current mess, and honestly I didn't have a comeback beyond that. So: what are the most important implications of a scheme like that, both for who ends up counting as a refugee and for who ends up having to take them in? I want the big ones, the ones that do real damage. I dont want technical nitpicks. I need something better to say to him than it feels grim.
