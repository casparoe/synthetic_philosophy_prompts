Trying to sort out my view on where meaning lives before a discussion group, and I'd rather have a consistency check then a pep talk. Here's roughly where I've landed, in no particular order:

- Meaning isn't in the marks themselves — a symbol string only means something relative to an interpreter standing outside it (call that an outer message).
- But I also think a system can have inner messages: internal states that mean something to the system itself, not just to someone watching from outside.
- And the cases I find most interesting are ones where the system generates its own frame messages — the conventions for how to read its own states — instead of having those handed to it.
- I don't think a thermostat or a bacterium really means anything by its states; that's just loose talk.
- And I think a state's meaning is fully fixed by the causal/functional role it plays.

I've got a feeling these don't all fit together but I can't see where the fault lines are. Which pairs are actually in tension, how bad is each one, and what's the cheapest thing to give up for each? And is any of it only apparently in tension? Rank the real ones by severity.
