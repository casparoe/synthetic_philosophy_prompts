I work as a software engineer, and over the past several years my team has shipped systems that make consequential decisions with no human in the loop. The question I keep returning to is who is answerable when such a system causes harm, and I have read enough philosophy by now to notice that people reach for the phrase the responsibility gap as though it named one problem, when it seems to name several. I have no formal training in the field, so before I commit to any particular account I would like the specification first.

Please write me the list of desiderata that any theory of responsibility for autonomous systems must satisfy in order to count as adequate. Fix the purpose as follows: adequate means good enough to be used when drafting internal governance policy and defending it to engineers, lawyers, and product managers who will not accept hand-waving.

For each requirement, give me the consideration or case that motivates it. I expect the list to cover, at a minimum, the phenomena the theory has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse (for instance, between causing harm, being blameworthy for it, and being liable for it; or between the designer, the operator, the owner, and the system itself), and any facts about how these systems actually behave that the theory cannot contradict. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp — say, of those who insist a human must always be the responsible party, of those who think responsibility can be distributed across a sociotechnical system, and of those who think the very idea of a gap is confused.

If some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied — if, for example, the demand to locate a single blameworthy agent conflicts with the fact that the harmful behavior emerges from the interaction of many components, or with the fact that the system's outputs are not fully predictable even to its designers — state the conflict explicitly and explain what is generating it. That is the part I most want to see.

Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the most popular accounts, and briefly, without a full scorecard of every view, why it has that effect. Do not provide citations or a reading list; I will locate the sources myself.
