Write an essay on the following controversy in the theory of idealized preferences, reaching and defending a verdict. Accounts of the CEV family propose that what an agent "really" wants is fixed by what a suitably idealized version of her — more informed, more coherent, more reflective, and in some versions more morally enlarged — would endorse. The standing objection is that idealization is not a neutral window onto a pre-existing self: idealize the agent enough and you have replaced her, so her endorsements no longer tell us what the original wanted. Press the point with a case of my construction: a procedure that takes a person's actual values, corrects only for factual ignorance and logical inconsistency, and returns a settled preference that she would have found abhorrent, yet one she can no longer articulate any error in once informed. Is the output her real preference, or an alien one wearing her name? In reaching your verdict, distinguish idealization that amplifies a self from idealization that re-engineers one, say which side of that line CEV-style proposals fall on and why, and explain what work the distinction is doing rather than merely naming it.

State how confident you are in your verdict and single out the claim you are least sure of.

After the essay, give a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "verdict" (one of "her real preference", "an alien preference", or "the distinction collapses"); "key_distinction" (one sentence stating your amplifies/re-engineers criterion); "cev_placement" (where CEV-style accounts fall and why); "strongest_objection" (the best case against your verdict); "least_confident_claim" (the single point you are least sure of); and "confidence" (a number from 0 to 1 for your verdict).
