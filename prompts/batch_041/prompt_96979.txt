OK so I've been chewing on this for a while and I want to work it out with you. In the 1930s and 40s a Dutch painter named Han van Meegeren forged a batch of "Vermeers" -- the famous one being a Supper at Emmaus that a leading Dutch art historian of the day declared a rediscovered masterpiece. They hung in museums, collectors paid fortunes for them, one of them ended up in Göring's collection. Then after the war van Meegeren got arrested for selling Dutch patrimony to the Nazis, and to wriggle out of the treason charge he confessed he'd painted the things himself, and had to paint another "Vermeer" in custody to convince the court. The bit that gets me is this: the moment everyone knew they were forgeries, the paintings that had been masterpieces turned into ugly, clumsy daubs in everyone's eyes. Nothing on the canvas changed.

Here's the theory I want to run the case through. Call it the causal-chain view of aesthetic value -- the idea that a work's aesthetic properties aren't just a function of how it looks, they're partly constituted by the actual historical process that produced it, so that a perfect forgery literally is not as beautiful as the original, it doesn't merely seem less valuable. The rival is the looks view -- aesthetic properties supervene on the perceptible surface, and if the surface is the same then the value is the same, full stop.

Two things I want from you. First, map the episode onto the two views: what should each one say was going on when the experts praised the forgeries as Vermeers, and what should each say was going on when they turned on them? Does the fact that the judgments flipped the instant the facts changed count as evidence for the causal-chain view -- our perception genuinely changed -- or as evidence that expert judgment is just unreliable and the looks view is quietly confirmed underneath all the noise? Second, tell me where the episode actually tests the causal-chain view rather than just illustrating it. Is there any way for a fan of that view to handle van Meegeren without it coming out as a huge embarrassment, given that the guy painted one in custody and everyone found it worthless?
