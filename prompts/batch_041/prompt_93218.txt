I am a graduate student in metaphysics, and I am trying to get clear on origin essentialism. During a seminar discussion, a classmate claimed that origin essentialism is the view that each person could not have come from a different sperm and egg. I replied that this seems to make siblings impossible: my sister and I came from the same two parents, so if the particular sperm and egg are essential to identity, then either she is me or one of us could not have existed. He said I was confusing the claim that a person's origin is essential with the claim that sharing an origin is sufficient for being the same person, but I cannot see how the distinction helps. After all, if my parents had used IVF and had two embryos, one that became me and one that became my sister, and if the second embryo had been implanted instead of the first, would I have existed? If the answer is no, then it seems the actual embryo is doing all the work, and my sister's existence becomes a kind of duplication problem. If the answer is yes, then origin essentialism is false.

I am also worried about a simpler twin case. If a single embryo had split into identical twins, both twins would share the same sperm and egg. Would origin essentialism have to say that there is only one person, since both have the same origin? That sounds absurd, but I cannot see what stops the inference. What I want to know is whether there is a coherent version of origin essentialism that avoids these dilemmas, or whether it inevitably collapses into the view that I am just my zygote and that anyone with the same origin would be me. I am not looking for a reading list or citations; I want the distinctions that make the position defensible, if it is defensible, and a clear diagnosis of exactly where my reasoning goes wrong.
