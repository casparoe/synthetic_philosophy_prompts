My friend and I got into a long, stupid argument last night about whether Americans are right to celebrate success or whether the Scandinavian Jantelagen instinct — you know, the don't-think-you're-special code, and the tall poppy thing where the highest earner in the village gets taken down a peg — is actually the healthier social glue. She thinks the American Dream stuff is just a machine for making losers feel like failures, and I said a culture that cuts down every tall poppy ends up with a field of stumps and no one to look up to. We didn't settle it, obviously, and now I want to run something in my class that lets students feel both pulls in their gut instead of just talking about them. I definately don't want them to just recite the standard pros and cons.

I teach a first-year seminar called Philosophy and Society, 18 students, 80 minutes, and the room is set up for small groups. I've been thinking alot about this, so here's the setup. I want you to act as a philosopher who also designs classroom simulations: start by brainstorming three or four possible mechanics, think carefully about which one makes the point through the rules rather than through my mouth, and then give me one runnable activity. The activity should make them feel the force of the Jantelagen/tall-poppy dynamic and the American Dream counter-dynamic without me explaining either up front. So no lecture, no definitions on the board, nothing named until the debrief. They should run into it through the rules: maybe a status market where players can invest in their own public standing or spend tokens to knock someone else down, with payoffs that make cutting down the top player tempting in one round and costly in another. I want roles or hidden goals, materials that are just index cards or a whiteboard, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, a rough timeline for the 80 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments onto the two ideas — including the difference between envy, resentment, and legitimate criticism of someone whose success was rigged. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of the idea the game can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself. Please keep it in the weeds a bit: I'd rather the mechanic turn on relative rank, visibility, and a lagom-style ceiling than on a generic sharing circle, and I'd like at least one moment where the winners and losers have to decide whether to keep playing the same game.
