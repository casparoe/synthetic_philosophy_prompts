ok so I've been reading Nietzsche and I've ended up with a health philosphy I'm not sure actually hangs together. Here it is. (1) There's no such thing as a healthy diet in general — what nourishes one constitution poisons another, so anyone prescribing food for everybody is selling you something. (2) There's no little man behind the curtain either; a person is just a hierarchy of drives and whichever one is strongest at the moment does the "deciding." (3) All the same I keep a pretty strict regimen — no alcohol, one long walk a day, light food, early nights, barely any meat — and I think of it as giving style to my character, as what my best self requires of me. (4) But I also think greatness runs on the robust kind of health Nietzsche admired, and that the people with real depth got it from being sick and from suffering — illness is a stimulus, not just a bill you pay. (5) And ascetic self-denial, denying yourself for its own sake, strikes me as pathetic and life-hating — which is awkward, since a regimen is nothing but denying yourself stuff. (6) My own contribution: health is a budget you draw down, so the point of the regimen is to bank enough to spend it on something hard.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and if something has to give, what's the cheapest thing to drop? Rank the tensions by how bad they are instead of telling me they all fit together fine.
