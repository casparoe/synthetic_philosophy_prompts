At our little decision theory reading group we keep getting stuck on the same thing in different clothes, and I want to know if it has a name or if it's just the thing I think it is.

First version. My aunt did one of those cheap at-home genetic tests, mostly for the ancestry part, and it came back with a high risk number for something that may never happen to her. There's no treatment, no screening, nothing she can do — it doesn't change a single decision she'll ever make. She was fine before and now she's up at 3am. But when I asked if she regreted it she looked at me like I was nuts and said she'd order it again tomorrow, and she's said the same thing twice since. So it made her worse off, and she'd do it again, and I don't think that's incoherent — I think it's something specific. Second version, same shape: a friend of mine is a grad student and read a comment on a preprint showing his method breaks down in a regime he doesn't care about. Nothing to do about it, the paper's out, and he says he'd still read the comment, and he also says he hasn't slept well since. Third: someone I know refuses to check her pension balance, because seeing the number wrecks her week even though she never trades on it.

Now the argument that keeps coming up in the group. Information can't make you worse off, and the proof is that if you didn't like what you found out you could just ignore it and do exactly what you would have done if you hadn't found out. Which is airtight when I write it on the board and wrong for all three of these people. My guess is that the word doing all the work is 'ignore' — you can ignore a recommendation, but I'm not sure you can ignore a fact once it's in you, and the argument seems to treat those as the same operation. Is there a name for this? Not for the general fact that people are curious or that news can sting, but for that specific move, or for the assumption the move needs, or for the family of cases where it breaks down. I don't want just a label — I want to know where the edge is. Does it matter whether the news is about something you could act on? Does it matter if the news is about the past, where there was never anything to do? And is my aunt's 'I wish I could un-know' the same thing as 'knowing was bad for her', or are those two different questions that the group keeps collapsing into one? If there's no established term for any of it, coin one and tell me exactly what it covers and what it doesn't.
