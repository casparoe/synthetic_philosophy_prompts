I build recommender systems for a mid-sized content platform, and I have been handed a project to design an intervention against what my colleagues keep calling ‘filter bubbles’ — the usual thing, a diversity injection into the ranking, some counter-attitudinal content, a dashboard. I have no formal philosophy background, and before I write a single line of code I want to know what a theory of these phenomena would actually have to get right, because my worry is that we will build something that moves our metrics while missing whatever it is we are actually worried about. A colleague offered a distinction I find useful but cannot place, and I would like the specification built around it rather than around the popular vocabulary: she says there are two quite different failures — one in which you never meet the opposing view because nothing ever surfaces it for you, and another in which you do meet it but have been primed to treat every opposing source as bought, compromised, or acting in bad faith, so that the encounter only digs you in deeper. I want the desiderata for a theory of such epistemic environments written out.

So: give me the requirements list. What phenomena must such a theory explain, and what is the consideration behind each requirement — the cases it has to get right (please include the awkward ones: the person whose informational world is sealed but whose beliefs happen to be true and well-founded; the marginalised or vulnerable group that deliberately curates its sources for reasons of safety rather than error; the group that looks homogeneous from outside but is held together by an explicit evidence norm that would let it be corrected; and the person who is simply in the minority on some question and is not thereby in a chamber at all). Which distinctions must it respect and not collapse — between the two failures above, between being isolated and being manipulated, between the belief-level and the social-level, between an environment that is epistemically defective and one that is merely unusual, and between an environment that is bad epistemically and one that is bad for other reasons such as welfare or autonomy? And which settled results must it not contradict — for instance that almost all of anyone’s beliefs arrive by testimony, that trusting a source is normally reasonable rather than a lapse, and that the mere fact of disagreement is not by itself evidence that either party is unreliable?

Then tell me, of the requirements you have listed, which are genuine constraints and which are merely the prejudices of one camp; which are negotiable given what the theory is for — and here let me fix the purpose, since I need this to tell an engineer like me when an intervention is even justified — and which are non-negotiable. I especially want the part where you say whether some subset of these requirements cannot all be satisfied at once, and if so which ones, because that would be the actual result and it would save me from promising my product manager something impossible. Finally, flag which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the obvious candidate theories, and mark plainly the points where a requirement is a value judgement dressed up as a constraint, so I know which parts of the specification are up to us and which are not. No citations or reading lists, please — just the spec and the reasoning behind it.
