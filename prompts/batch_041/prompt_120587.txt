Right, so — I'm a novelist, three books in, and the current one is a family saga that runs from about 1840 to 2140, which has meant five years of me lying awake at night thinking about how much the future is supposed to count. I've read a fair bit of philosophy on the side (no degree, just a shelf and a habit) and I've ended up with a set of views I'm attached to, but I have a nagging feeling they don't all fit together. I'd like an audit, not a lecture. Here's the set, as plainly as I can put it:

1. The date a good falls on doesn't change what it's worth. If someones going to be just as well off in 2200 as someone is today, the fact that they're 175 years downstream isn't a reason to weigh their good any less. Same inside a single life — I don't see why my 70-year-old self should count for less than my 35-year-old self just because he comes later.

2. But I've never been able to believe that making more happy people is, by itself, a good thing. Double the population, make everyone equally happy, and I don't think you've made the world twice as good. And I don't think you wrong a person by not bringing them into existence. What's good or bad is how things go for people who are there to have things go for them.

3. The only "discounting" I'll own is the odds that the thing never happens at all. If there's a real chance we don't make it to 2200, then a good in 2200 is worth less to me in expectation — but that's just multiplying by a probability, it isn't me caring less about later people.

4. As a novelist I'll admit something that may be the odd one out: I think shape matters, not just totals. A life that goes badly and then well is better than one that goes well and then badly, with the same goods in both. Same for a century, same for a book. I'd rather the last chapter be the strong one.

5. I love my daughter more than I love any number of strangers in the year 2400, and I think that's fine and not a moral failing. Love isn't a weighing scale.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each one? I dont have a view about which of them I'd give up, so don't ask me — tell me. If some pair is only in tension when you read a commitment more strongly than I wrote it, say so, and tell me which reading I should take. At the end, tell me the single revision that would clean up the most, even if I won't like it.
