I've been going down a free will rabbit hole for a few weeks now (no philosophy background, just stubbornness) and the thing I cant get past is the argument that you can still be responsible for something even when you literally could not have done otherwise. The version of it I keep bumping into: someone wires up your brain so that if you were about to decide not to do X, the wiring would make you do X anyway. But you decide to do X all on your own, the wiring never fires, and — so the argument goes — you're responsible for X, which is supposed to show that responsibility doesn't need "could have done otherwise" at all. I want you to write a parody that attacks this from the inside.

Format: a customer support FAQ page for a fictional home gadget, the Intervener Home 2.0, from a made-up smart-home company (invent the brand). The device sits in your house and is guaranteed to only ever kick in if you were about to do otherwise. Around 650 words, deadpan the whole way through, no winking, 7 to 9 questions and answers.

The jokes have to come out of the actual philosophy, so someone who knows this literature laughs instead of wincing. Stuff I want it to get right: that the case gets its verdict by stipulation about what happens in the actual sequence of events; the standard pushback that either the device's presence does something in that actual sequence (so it's a cause, and the customer isn't off the hook the way the company claims) or it does nothing at all (so it isn't really a case where they couldn't have done otherwise) — the FAQ isn't allowed to just ignore this, it has to trip over it in the fine print; the "flicker of freedom" idea that maybe what matters is that you could have at least tried or begun to do otherwise; and the awkward fact that the whole setup needs the actual sequence to be deterministic-ish, which the company should treat as a shipping requirement or a sold-separately module. Plus the classic corporate move of dumping all responsibility on the customer while selling a device whose entire point is that the outcome was never in doubt.

Dont be coy about where it lands: have the company commit to one horn of that dilemma — either "our device makes zero difference in the actual sequence, therefore it was all you" or "our device is genuinely part of the causal chain, therefore..." — pick one and keep the fine print consistently on that side, even when it makes the company look bad. Readable to someone who's never heard any of this.

Start right in with the FAQ itself, no preamble, no "here's my take" — the first line should be the page's first question. Then, after the piece, a short note of 3 to 5 sentences: which horn you committed to and why, and where you took liberties with the philosophy to make the joke work. Be definite — don't hand me both sides and leave me to pick.
