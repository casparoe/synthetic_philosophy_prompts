So let me dump what I think his position is, so you don't have to guess or go looking for it.

His theory, roughly: art is when a person recalls a feeling they've really had, expresses it in external signs, and other people catch that feeling. If nobody catches it, it isn't art, however skillfull the execution. How well it works depends on how individual and particular the feeling is, how clearly it comes across, and how sincere the artist is — whether he actually feels the thing he's putting out. He also gives tells for counterfeit art: stuff borrowed from other art, stuff that's just imitation (photographic detail for its own sake), stuff hunting for striking effects, and stuff that's merely diverting/entertaining. And he's suspicious of difficulty and obscurity — if only a trained coterie can read it, that's a bad sign, and a real test is whether ordinary people with no art education get it.

The part I want the joke to land on: his definition of art is amoral — any feeling, even a vile one, counts as art if it actually spreads — but his idea of good art is fiercely moral. Only feelings of universal brotherhood and love count, and he ranks art by how much of that it does. So a sincerely made, wildly infectious cruel thing is real art that is bad art. And when he turns the test on his own taste he writes off Shakespeare, Wagner, Beethoven, Dante, and the bulk of his own writing, novels included.
