I've got a workshop paper coming up in about three weeks and I need a Q&A prep sheet. Here's the setup.

My paper is on hinge epistemology, the On Certainty stuff. My line is that everyone's been treating hinges as one kind of thing and they're actually two, and I coin a term for the second kind: rails. Rough version: a hinge is a certainty that holds a practice together and that you can't coherently doubt from inside that practice (my having hands; the world not having sprung into existence five minutes ago, for the purposes of ordinary inquiry). A rail is a former hinge: something that used to be held fast and then got rebuilt into a revisable, evidence-sensitive belief once the surrounding practice changed. The earth goes round the sun was a hinge for a while and is now a rail, and it's a rail because there's a whole machinery attached to it, textbooks and instruments and the institution of astronomy, that can in principle pull it up. So the distinction isn't about content, or about how certain something feels, it's about whether there's machinery attached.

The payoff I want: hinge epistemology can't do the anti-sceptical work Wright and Coliva want from it, because the Cartesian doubt isn't a demand to doubt a hinge, it's a demand to turn a hinge into a rail, and you can't do that from a standing start, you need a practice on the other side to rail it into. So the entitlements only ever protect against practical doubt, and the sceptic isn't making a practical demand. I also think this is just what Wittgenstein actually meant and that the propositional reading is a mistake.

The audience: it's a scepticism workshop, maybe 30 people. Mostly analytic epistemologists, closure and defeat and transmission of justification, that crowd. A couple of formal epistemologists who'll want to know what my machinery is, precisely. Then four or five Wittgenstein people who I suspect will say I've invented a distinction Wittgenstein already had, and that naming it is the whole paper. Plus one historian of early modern philosophy who always asks what Descartes would say.

What I want from you: the objections I'll actually get from this room, not the ones I'd get on a blog. Ordered by how much damage they do me. Phrased the way these people would phrase them. For each, a reply I can give in about ninety seconds, and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Tell me what to concede up front so I don't look like I'm defending everything at once. And give me the one question I'm praying nobody asks.

Also, if you think the hinges/rails thing is just the old point that hinges can be given up, dressed in new clothes, say so. I'd rather here it now.
