I have spent a long time reading the rationalist blogs and forums, and my working metaphysics is roughly Bayesian: a belief is a probability, a probability is a summary of predictions, a model earns its keep by being calibrated and by winning bets, and the difference between a model that genuinely tracks a structure and one that merely happens to fit the data is the difference I care most about. Lately I have been reading Rorty, and I want to attempt a translation exercise rather than a book report.

Please restate Rorty's antirepresentationalism — his suspicion that the whole vocabulary of accurately representing reality is not doing the work we imagine it does, together with his pragmatist treatment of truth and justification — in my vocabulary. Assume I will grant you any apparatus you need: priors and likelihoods, hypothesis classes, loss functions, calibration, predictive processing, instrumentalism about models, and the tracking-versus-fitting distinction. Translate claim by claim, as faithfully as you can, and resist the temptation to substitute the rationalist position for Rorty's. I am aware that many of us already accept that models are tools rather than pictures, so one specific thing I want you to address is whether that platitude is the whole of antirepresentationalism or whether the translation has quietly discarded the part that makes Rorty uncomfortable for a Bayesian.

Then give me the residue report. Which of his claims survive translation cleanly? Which survive only as a distortion, such that I should suspect you have imported assumptions he would reject? And which have no expression in my vocabulary at all? I am most interested in that last category, because my vocabulary is built out of precisely the representational notions he is attacking, and I suspect no translation into it can be neutral. Tell me where the failure lies: is there something the rationalist framework genuinely cannot say, or was Rorty's claim less substantial than it sounds? Where the translation succeeds, say whether the success is a vindication of the rationalist picture or merely a sign that the two vocabularies were never as far apart as either side pretends.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know Rorty and the pragmatist tradition. Do not present a contested reading as the consensus, flag which parts of your translation are your own construction rather than anything Rorty or his interpreters would endorse, and tell me plainly how confident you are in the residue report itself — in particular, whether you take the untranslatable remainder to be a real feature of his view or an artifact of the vocabulary I handed you.
