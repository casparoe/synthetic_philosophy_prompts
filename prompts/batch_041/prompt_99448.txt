Set out a detailed comparison between the Molinist doctrine of middle knowledge and a rival position I will call Decree-Grounded Actualism. On Molinism, God knows, prior to any creative decree, true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom—propositions about what any possible free creature would do in any possible circumstance—and this middle knowledge guides the decree without determining the creature’s choice. On Decree-Grounded Actualism, there are no true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom prior to the decree; what a creature would freely do is instead grounded in the creative act itself, so God knows such counterfactuals only as the reverse side of the decision to create a particular world and to constitute particular creatures with particular freedoms. Compare the two views along the following axes: (1) the order of explanation among God’s natural knowledge, middle knowledge, and free knowledge, and whether Decree-Grounded Actualism can retain a genuinely distinct middle moment; (2) the grounding of counterfactuals of freedom, including whether the standard grounding objection against Molinism applies with equal or greater force to Decree-Grounded Actualism; (3) the compatibility of each view with libertarian freedom, divine omniscience, and the efficacy of grace; (4) the resources each has for the problem of evil and for explaining why God creates a world containing a particular amount of sin and suffering; and (5) whether Decree-Grounded Actualism is best classified as a variant of Molinism, a version of Calvinism, a form of open theism, or a genuinely third option. In your answer, state clearly where the two positions agree, where they diverge, and which one, if either, better preserves both robust divine providence and creaturely freedom. Please answer in American English.
