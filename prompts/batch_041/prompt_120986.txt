ok so I've been lurking on rationalist blogs since like 2009 — LessWrong, the old Overcoming Bias days, that whole decision-theory-obsessed corner of the internet — and I want a bit of satire that definately only works if whoever writes it actually knows the game theory.

Give me an advice column. Dear-Abby style: headline, three or four short letters, the columnist's replies, around 700 words total. The columnist is a game theorist who solves every reader's problem by iterated elimination of dominated strategies. One tool, and it is a hammer. Deadpan all the way through, no winking, no narrator stepping in to tell us he's wrong. He's a true believer, so this has to be satire from *inside* the position, not a piece explaining why the position is silly.

Here's what I want the jokes to actually turn on, because a game theorist should laugh at this rather than wince:

- Strict vs weak dominance. Eliminating weakly dominated strategies is the sketchier of the two moves — a weakly dominated strategy can still sit in a Nash equilibrium, so deleting it is doing more work than the rationality assumption licenses, and the order you delete things in can change what's left standing. He should be breezy about all this. (He also likes to claim that pushed far enough the procedure can eat every option and leave you with nothing to do at all — I'm pretty sure that's a real thing for weak dominance, but don't quote me, so if you can't make it land just leave it as something he says.)

- Mixed strategies. There are games where no pure option beats another pure option head to head, but a lottery over two of them does. So a reader can be stuck with an option that's strictly dominated by a coin flip between their other two, and the columnist has to explain — correctly! — that this doesn't mean they should flip the coin, it means they shouldn't take the third thing. That's the funniest bit available here and I want it in.

- The question he can never answer. Elimination only tells you which strategies a rational player won't play. It doesn't tell you what to do. So he keeps gesturing at whatever survives and at equilibrium, and the reader keeps noticing that this is thinner than what they asked for. Worth having one letter with three parties in it too, so he can trip over the fact that never-a-best-response and strictly-dominated come apart once there are more than two players — that's where the wheels come off and he doesn't notice.

End it on a letter from someone who followed the advice perfectly and now has no options left, and let the column just stop there. An editor's note at the very end is allowed if you want one tiny crack in the facade; the columnist himself never gets the joke.

Don't pad it with citations or invented theorems with names on them, and keep it readable for someone who's never done a problem set — but the content has to be right for the people who acutally have.
