I've been in a reading group on predictive processing for about a year now and we keep circling round the same fight, and I've started to suspect the fight isn't really about the brain at all. Roughly, one camp says the free energy principle is the real deal: a first-principles result, derived from the fact that anything that persists has to keep itself within some limited set of states, and that once you have that you get perception as inference, action as inference, learning, attention, the lot, with the maths doing the real work rather than the metaphors. The other camp says this is either empty or false -- you can describe any dynamical system as minimising some free energy provided you're allowed to pick the right generative model and priors, the Markov blanket requirement is either trivially satisfiable or an artefact of where you decided to draw the boundary, the prior preferences that are supposed to stop the thing from just sitting in a dark room doing nothing look suspiciously like they were smuggled in to produce the right answer, and as far as anyone can tell the principle on its own makes no prediction that a plain prediction-error-minimisation or reward-maximisation story doesn't already make. The defenders come back with: that's like complaining thermodynamics doesn't tell you the boiling point of water without a model of water; the principle is a constraint, not a theory, and demanding it be falsified on its own is a category mistake. Then we all go home, read the same blog posts, and nothing moves.

What I want from you is a diagnosis, not another round of the argument. Is this a substantive disagreement about the brain that's just been badly framed, or is it a disagreement about what counts as an explanation in the first place -- a fight about the standards for calling something a theory, dressed up as a fight about variational inference? Is there a hidden empirical question underneath (does the brain actually do variational inference, or does it merely look that way under a certain description, and how on earth would you tell)? Or are the two sides answering different questions under one heading -- one about what any persisting system must be doing at the level of the formalism, one about what this particular lump of tissue is doing? My own guess, and I'd like you to tell me if it's wrong, is that the unfalsifiability charge and the thermodynamics reply are both true but about different objects, the formalism and the models built on top of it, and that most of the heat comes from people sliding between the two without noticing. If that's roughly right, say so and spell out what the disagreement is really over; if it isn't, say where it goes wrong. And be honest about whether anything could actually settle it -- a theorem about which systems do and don't satisfy the principle, an experiment that pulls it apart from the rival stories, or just a clearer agreement about what we're asking a theory of the mind to do.
