I've been stuck for weeks on a decision between two offers that, as far as I can tell, are exactly equal in every respect I care about: the same pay, the same kind of work, colleagues I like equally well, commutes that are equally annoying, and no difference in long-term prospects that I can detect. They both expire at the end of the month, so doing nothing is itself a choice, and not one I'm eager to make. My friends have split into two camps. One says that if the two are truly equal then it doesn't matter which I take, so I should stop agonizing, flip a coin, and get on with my life; on this view my weeks of paralysis are not evidence of anything except a failure of nerve. The other says the very fact that I can't bring myself to pick proves they can't really be equal — that if I introspected honestly I'd find a preference — and that I should treat my paralysis as a signal of a hidden reason rather than as a genuine dead heat. I've tried the coin, and it didn't behave the way it was supposed to: when it came up for the first offer I felt a small pang of disappointment, which I took as proof that I wanted the second, so I went with the second; a day later I felt the same pang about that one, and now I don't trust the test. I've also tried to settle it by taking the one with the marginally shorter commute, but that felt like manufacturing a reason rather than discovering one, and I can't tell whether a manufactured reason counts.

What I want to know is whether there is anything to either camp beyond the truism that a decision eventually has to get made. Is it actually true that when two options are equal it doesn't matter which one I choose, or does that phrase quietly smuggle in something false — say, that the act of choosing is itself worthless, or that regret afterward is irrational? Can a person act rationally with no preference at all, or does every action require something that tips the scale, in which case the dead heat is either impossible or I'm fooling myself about it? And if I do resolve it by coin or by some arbitrary rule, is that a legitimate way to decide, or a way of pretending that something other than me made the decision? Please give me a serious answer rather than reassurance, and tell me plainly which of my two friends, if either, is closer to right. Write in American English.
