I'm putting together my commitments on the aggregation of individual well-being for a welfare-economics reading group, and I'd like a consistency check before I circulate them.

1. Harsanyi's aggregation theorem is sound as a derivation: if individual preferences and the social ordering both satisfy the expected-utility axioms, and the social ordering respects a Pareto condition (where everyone is indifferent between two prospects, so is society), then the social welfare function must be a weighted sum of individual utility functions. I accept this as a constraint on any defensible social objective.

2. The weights should be equal, and the right device for fixing them is a veil of ignorance on which the chooser has an equal chance of being anyone. So the social objective is the unweighted sum — equivalently, the average — of individual utilities.

3. I am not a utilitarian about distribution. Offered a choice between a pair of lives at (5, 5) and a pair at (9, 2), I take (5, 5): a smaller total spread evenly can be better than a larger total spread unevenly.

4. Well-being is preference satisfaction. How well off you are is how well your life goes by your own lights, as registered in your informed choices.

5. Consider someone who has spent her life in a subordinate position, knows exactly what the alternatives are, has been offered them, and still prefers her constrained life. I think she is badly off, and that a social objective which scores her highly has mis-measured her.

6. Distributions are to be assessed by how each person's life actually goes, not by the ex ante prospects they faced. The ex ante/ex post distinction is doing real work for me, and I come down on the ex post side.

7. I am risk averse about my own life: I would rather have a certain modest income than an even chance of great wealth or destitution. And I think expected-utility maximization is the correct account of rational choice under risk.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only appear to, and for each genuine conflict, what has to give? Please order the conflicts by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad, and finish by naming the single revision that would resolve the most at the least cost. I don't need a survey of the literature or suggestions for further reading; I want to know what this package commits me to.
