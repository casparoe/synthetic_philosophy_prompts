Write a short story of roughly 1,500 words about an engineer tasked with designing a neural implant that must be trusted with human memory, who insists on copying the picture of the brain that von Neumann sketches in The Computer and the Brain (Yale University Press, 1958) rather than the picture her colleagues favor. On von Neumann's account the brain is not a precision digital machine: its components are slow, noisy, and individually unreliable, and it achieves dependability through massive redundancy and statistical averaging rather than through careful manufacturing; its signaling mixes analog and digital features instead of committing to clean binary logic; and its memory is not addressed the way a computer's is. Her colleagues want a conventional high-precision digital design. The story should turn on one concrete decision where the two approaches diverge, with real stakes and no easy resolution, and the disagreement should be argued out by the characters rather than narrated from one side.

After the story, add a brief note that separates what is settled about von Neumann's actual claims from what is genuinely contested or speculative among people who know the material, including where he was explicitly guessing, where later neuroscience has moved on, and where the story takes liberties with his ideas or leans on a contested reading of them; do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Write in American English.
