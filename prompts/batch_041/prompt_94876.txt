Hi — I host a small ethics podcast (two of us, a few hundred regular listeners, no budget). Next week's guest is a political philosopher who's spent about a decade defending what she calls the 'exchange account' of moral compromise. The gist: when two groups with clashing values have to live under some shared arrangement, treat it like a trade. Each side concedes on something it ranks lower and gets ground on something it ranks higher; if both sides end up ahead in their own rankings, the compromise is legitimate, and if one side is made to give up something it ranks above whatever it recieves, the compromise is rotten. That's the whole view — no extra machinery, she insists. I need eight to ten questions that actually press on this rather than letting her restate it, and I want at least one question each on the following, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give:

1. The incommensurability objection — values aren't on a common scale, so is there really a fact about who 'came out ahead'? She usually answers that you only need each side's own ranking, not a shared scale, so follow up on that specifically.

2. The commodification / betrayal worry — that treating a value as tradable is itself a change in what you hold, so conceding ground on something you call sacred already corrupts it. She'll probably say the account is about priorities, not price tags; press that reply.

3. The third-party problem — where the thing being conceded isn't the negotiators' own, but lands on someone with no seat at the table. Make it concrete: two blocs trade votes, one gets a farm subsidy, the other gets a carve-out from an anti-discrimination rule, and the people who lose are a group that isn't in the room. Does the exchange account even have the resources to call that rotten, or does it have to import some seperate idea?

Then give me at least one question that makes 'it's a Pareto improvement' hard to use as a shield — ideally a case where the background is already unjust, so the 'trade' starts to look like an offer you can't refuse — and one closing question about what could count as evidence against the exchange account, i.e. what she'd have to see in order to give it up.

Please don't write anything that could be asked of any guest ('what drew you to this?', 'what's the biggest misconception?'). Every question should turn on the actual content of the view.
