Hey, I'm a junior in a medieval political thought seminar and I got stuck running the last discussion section of the semester (50 minutes, 15 undergrads who did the reading and retained maybe half of it). The topic is Aquinas on tyrants and tyrannicide, and I don't want to just re-lecture at them. What I want to hand out is a "which view about killing tyrants do you actually hold?" decision tree that students walk through and land on a position, then argue with whoever landed elsewhere.

Please build it as a taxonomy where every branch node is the question that seperates the views underneath it, and every leaf is a named position -- use the historical label where there is one, otherwise a plain descriptive name like "obedience until deposed." Seven to nine nodes and six to eight leaves, small enough that someone can walk the whole thing in ten minutes.

The distinctions I want doing the actual work:

- tyrant by title (a usurper with no claim to rule) vs. tyrant by exercise (a legitimate ruler who rules badly) -- I want the tree to force students to say whether these two come apart on whether you may kill him
- who may act: a private person with a clean opportunity, a public magistrate or officer, the church/pope, or nobody
- whether the tyrant loses his dominion the moment he becomes a tyrant, or only once some competent authority declares it -- and what that implies about obeying his unjust commands in the meantime
- whether the bar is principled (never lawful) or prudential (scandal, civil war, a worse successor, a foreign invasion)
- whether killing him and resisting or deposing him short of killing are separate questions with separate answers

Nodes have to be concrete cases, not "-isms." So something like: a usurper who happens to rule well; a legitimate king who orders a bishop executed for refusing an unjust tax; a magistrate who swore an oath of loyalty to the man; a tyrant whose death opens the border to an invading empire; an excommunicated tyrant; a regent ruling in the name of a child king; a private person who's sure he'd get away with it. Don't ask "may the pope depose princes?" -- make them answer the case and let the label fall out. And make sure the view our seminar attributes to Aquinas lands on some leaf, even if he's hard to place.

Two more things. First, flag any leaves your tree can't seperate and tell me why -- I have a feeling "only public authority may act" and "the church may depose" come apart only in the case where no legitimate magistrate will act. Second, for branches nobody actually occupies, say so and diagnose it: incoherent, just unattractive, or maybe never argued?

Our prof's line was that Aquinas doesn't treat shaking off a tyrant as sedition, since tyrannical rule isn't ordered to the common good, but that this obviously doesn't yet tell you who gets to do the shaking. I want the tree to make students feel that gap instead of resolving it for them.

No citations or reading lists needed, I just want the instrument. Write it in American English.
