I spent thirty-five years in a laboratory before I turned to philosophy, and the idea that has caught my attention is the tangled hierarchy: the structure Escher draws in Drawing Hands, where two hands each bring the other into existence, and which Hofstadter reads in Gödel, Escher, Bach as a strange loop that cannot be resolved from inside the picture. On that account the resolution lies at a level that is not part of the loop at all — the artist's level, the level at which the rules are laid down rather than followed, which I have heard called the inviolate level. I am not yet persuaded that this notion earns its keep, and I should like you to construct a case sharp enough to test it.

The specification is as follows. The scenario must be realistic: an institution, a standards body, a laboratory, a court, a firm. No brains in vats, no conscious machines, no magic, no time travel. The tangle must be genuine, in the sense that two or more elements each derive their standing from the other in the same respect, so that neither can settle a dispute between them without presupposing precisely what is in question. Mutual oversight or a division of powers will not do; that is a circle of accountability, which is a different thing from a loop in a hierarchy. The inviolate level must lie inside the story — some actor, instrument, or mechanism belonging to the scenario itself — and must not be imported as an umpire; an umpire introduced by stipulation is a cheat, and so, for these purposes, are God, the author, and the reader.

After the scenario, do the analysis. Identify the levels, show where the loop closes, and say exactly what makes the level you have chosen inviolate — why it is not itself caught up in the tangle. Then give me a control: the same institution with one feature altered, such that the tangle either fails to arise or is resolved from within, and tell me which feature is doing the work. Say what happens if the inviolate level is deleted — whether anything actually breaks, or whether the institution carries on, which would suggest the level was decorative. And tell me how someone who holds that no inviolate level is ever needed would attack the case, and whether the attack succeeds.

If you find that you cannot build the case with the inviolate level inside the story, do not force it; say so plainly and explain what the failure shows. Tell me how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of. Write in continuous prose — no headings, no numbered lists, no bold, no tables — as you would in a letter.
