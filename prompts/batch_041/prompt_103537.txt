A friend in my phil of language seminar says he's shown externalism can't be right, and it's bugging me because I basically like externalism. The case: imagine a village where the first person to name the clear drinkable stuff pointed at a glass drawn from the north well, which happens to be H2O, but every other well and lake and tap in the village is XYZ, and nobody ever finds out. Externalism (the Twin Earth move) says their word still means H2O, since that's what the original sample was and the causal chain runs back to it. Here's his arguemnt:

(1) A natural-kind term's meaning is fixed by the actual kind that grounded its introduction, not by anything in the speakers' heads.
(2) So in the village the word means H2O, even though no speaker has ever met H2O and every one of them takes it to mean XYZ.
(3) So what that word means is fixed by a fact no speaker could ever get at.
(4) And there's nothing special about that word: any of my words could have its meaning fixed by some fact outside my head that I can't get at.
(5) So for all I know, none of my words mean what I think they do.
(6) So I can't know what I mean when I talk.
(7) That's absurd; I know perfectly well what I mean.
(8) So externalism is false.

I'd most like to keep (1) and (7), and I'd give up (2) if I had to, but I can't see where it actually slips. Which step do I cut, why did it look fine, and what does cutting it cost me? If the honest answer is just "give up externalism," say so.
