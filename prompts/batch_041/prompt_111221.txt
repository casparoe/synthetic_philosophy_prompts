My two housemates got into it last night about Dennett's four kinds of creatures — the Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian ones from the tower of generate-and-test — and I ended up refereeing from the kitchen doorway without being able to settle it. Here's roughly how it went.

Sam said the four levels name four genuinly different mechanisms for generating and testing variation, and that's what makes the scheme worth having. A Darwinian creature generates variation by mutation and tests it by surviving; a Skinnerian one generates behaviour and tests it by reinforcement; a Popperian one generates hypotheses internally and tests them by simulation, so it can let a hypothesis fail without itself having to; and a Gregorian one generates and tests with thinking-tools it inherited from its culture — words, numbers, diagrams, other people — so it can offload part of the test onto the environment. He said the tower is a hierarchy because each level lets the creature run more trials at lower cost, and that's a real difference in the creature, not just in its surroundings.

Rosa said that's a nice story but it smuggles in progressivism the levels don't earn. She said 'Popperian' and 'Gregorian' aren't two kinds of mind; a Gregorian is just a Popperian with better external memory. The change is in the environment — books, tally marks, conversation — not in the creature. On her view the tower is really a taxonomy of cognitive niches, and dressing it up as four kinds of creature is misleading, because you could have a Popperian creature with no culture at all and a Gregorian one with a thin inner life, and nothing about the mind itself would have changed.

Sam said that's his point: if a mind is partly constituted by what it offloads, then a taxonomy of minds and a taxonomy of niches aren't seperable, so Rosa's objection begs the question. Rosa said that's a bait-and-switch. If minds are partly constituted by their niches, then the four levels are just a way of describing how much scaffolding is lying around, and the interesting question — what the creature can actually do with the scaffolding — varies continuously. Sam said the levels are the scaffolding. Rosa said no, the levels are supposed to be the creature.

They asked me to settle it and I honestly couldn't. Can you adjudicate move by move, and specifically: (1) Is Sam right that the four levels name distinct mechanisms of generate-and-test, or is Rosa right that Popperian and Gregorian collapse into one? (2) Is the tower a hierarchy of minds or of niches — and does that distinction survive Dennett's own insistence that minds are partly external? (3) Is Rosa's progressivism charge fair, or is the tower not meant as a progressivist claim in the first place? (4) If they're talking past each other, where exactly does the equivocation on 'kind of mind' happen? Rate each of their moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and say what both of them are missing.
