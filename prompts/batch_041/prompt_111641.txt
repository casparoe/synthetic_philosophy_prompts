Ok so I got elected to our town council in the spring, which nobody expected including me, and the one thing I actually ran on was a rule requiring landlords to accept housing vouchers. We passed it in May. In June the state legislature passed a bill saying municipalities can't do that. Preemption, apparently it's a whole thing, I'm learning. Our town attorney says we're basically dead in the water and should go home.

Meanwhile my sister lives in Germany and keeps telling me about subsidiarity — the EU version, where the central level is only supposed to act if the member states can't do the job themselves, and apparently it's also a Catholic idea that's older than the EU by a lot. And I've also been reading this older American argument that the real point of giving towns and states power is that they're laboratories: somebody tries something at small scale, you see what happens, other places copy it if it works.

What I want from you is a proper comparison of those two things. Subsidiarity versus the laboratory argument. Are they the same argument in different clothes, or do they actually come apart, and where? My gut says the lab thing is about information and the subsidiarity thing is about something more like dignity or the integrity of smaller communities, and that this matters, because a laboratory is exactly the thing you shut down when you don't like the experiment — but I might be making that up.

Second thing. Subsidiarity looks to me like it has a trap door built in: somebody has to decide whether the lower level can do the job adequately, and if the higher level gets to make that call, then it isn't a real constraint on the higher level at all, it's just a nice way of talking. Is that right? Does the laboratory argument have a similiar trap door, or is it structurally safer?

And then just tell me: which one of these, if either, gives me an actual argument against preemption that isn't me dressing up the fact that I want my ordinance to survive? I'd rather have a verdict I can defend at the next meeting than a balanced summary. Skip the reading list, I won't get to it.
