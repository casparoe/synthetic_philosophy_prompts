Anti-reductionists like Reid and Burge think this gets both the psychology and the epistemology wrong: they hold that we're entitled to believe what we're told straight off, with no reasons required, because our nature makes credulity the default (Reid says God designed us this way), and because that entitlement is a default it can't be taken away by whatever evidence you might later get about the speaker. Lackey is the leading anti-reductionist these days; on her view testimony is basically like perception, so the hearer needs no reason at all and the speaker's own reliability doesn't really enter into it.
