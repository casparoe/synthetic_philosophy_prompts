I'm in a grad seminar on mass atrocity and we've spent the last few weeks on Browning and Goldhagen, and I want to write my own position down before Thursday so people can take swings at it. Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) Browning is basically right about Reserve Police Battalion 101: those were ordinary men — Hamburg reservists, middle-aged, mostly working class, not fanatics and not sadists — and the picture of them as eager ideological killers isn't what the interrogations show. (2) So Goldhagens thesis, that a specifically German eliminationist antisemitism was the cause, is wrong, at least as an explanation of what these particular men did; though I also think you can't explain why the genocide happened at all without antisemitism being widespread, and I take that to be a claim at a different level, not a quiet concession to him. (3) I'm a situationist at heart: Milgram and Zimbardo convinced me that character and ideology do much less explanatory work than we like to think and that the situation does most of it, so "that's just what those people were like" makes my hackles go up. (4) At the same time I think the men were fully morally responsible for what they did, and that "we had no choice" is self-deception rather than an excuse. (5) And the thing I keep coming back to is Jozefow: Trapp, in tears, told the men that anyone who didn't feel up to the task could step out, about a dozen did, and nothing happened to them. I take that to show that individual conscience actually could and did make a difference there, and that it says something real about what people are capable of.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and how bad is each one? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than telling me they're all in some kind of tension, and for each real one say which commitment has to give and what the cheapest repair would be, then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Be careful to separate what's actually settled in this literature from what's genuinely contested among people who know it — don't hand me a contested reading as if it were the consensus, and if one of my five is doing work that a live counter-reading would undercut, say so. Keep the whole thing under 700 words.
