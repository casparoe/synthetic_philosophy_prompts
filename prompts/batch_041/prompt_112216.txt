Reading group threw this at me and I cant get it out of my head, because if it's right the whole "will there be a singleton?" debate is empty. (A singleton, in the sense the AI-governance people use it: a world order where a single agency makes the top-level decisions and nobody else can revise them.) We introduced our own term, "umpire," for any arrangement that can veto moves by the players -- a hegemon, an enforcement body, a shared norm everyone polices.

(1) A world is multipolar iff no single agent can unilaterally set the terms for the others.
(2) For any two agents with conflicting aims, lasting peace requires some mechanism that reliably stops either from unilaterally revising the terms, since otherwise the stronger one revises whenever it pays.
(3) Any such mechanism must be able to override the preferences of both parties, including the stronger one, and against its will.
(4) Something that can override the preferences of the strongest agents in the system, against their will, is functionally an agent that sets the terms -- that is, a singleton.
(5) So every stable multipolar world contains a singleton.
(6) Every unstable world is either heading for a stable multipolar world (so, a singleton) or for a singleton outright.
(7) Therefore every world either contains a singleton or is on its way to one.

So "multipolarity" is either a temporary condition or a singleton wearing a disguise, and the singleton hypothesis has no content -- yes is the answer for every world, including this one. I'd hate to give up (2), it seems like the realist's own point. Which step do I drop, and what does dropping it cost me?
