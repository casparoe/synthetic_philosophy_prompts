I have no academic training — I listen to podcasts, I argue with friends after dinner, and I read whatever the internet hands me — but for about a year now I've been circling the set of arguments people call the hinge of history: the claim that we might be living at the single most consequential moment in the human story, that the next few decades could fix the whole long-run trajectory of civilization one way or the other, and that this possibility should reorganize what we care about and how we spend our effort. The more I read, the more I notice that the *way* the idea gets discussed has a flavor all its own, and I'd like a piece of satire that goes after that flavor rather than the people who hold it. So: please write a satirical FAQ page, roughly 700 words, for a fictional nonprofit I'll call the Pivotal Moment Project, whose entire reason for existing is to assess whether individuals and organizations are personally living at the hinge and to certify them accordingly. The page should read like the real thing — a deadpan nonprofit voice, an earnest opening paragraph about why the assessment exists, a numbered self-assessment quiz ("Do you regularly find yourself in rooms where decisions of civilizational scale are made?" and so on), a scoring rubric with tiers of certification, a section headed something like "Common Objections, Answered," and a closing note with a helpline for readers who suspect that they themselves might be the hinge. I want the jokes to come from getting the actual arguments right, so that someone who has read this literature laughs instead of wincing. In particular the piece should quietly absorb the sampling worry (if you imagine your moment in history drawn at random from all of them, the odds of landing at the hinge look vanishingly small, and no one has a non-circular way of explaining why they happened to land there), the reflexivity (the more people who act as though now is the hinge, the more the world's trajectory really does bend to their actions, so the belief appears to validate itself), the regress of earlier hinges (nearly every generation has had a story about why *its* moment was the decisive one), the unfalsifiability (nothing that happens could count as evidence against it), and the licensing problem (if this is the hinge, then extreme and otherwise reckless measures start to look proportionate). Sustained irony, no winking, and don't name any real person or organization — invent the staff, the founder, the rival certification body, whoever you need. Finally, after the piece, give me a short structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "satire_target" (one sentence naming the position or practice being satirized), "in_universe_org" (the fictional body and its stated premise), "real_features_exaggerated" (an array of four to six short strings, each naming an actual feature of hinge-of-history reasoning that the piece blows up), "objections_landed" (an array of the substantive objections the satire makes without ever stating them), "deadpan_devices" (an array of the techniques that keep it legible while staying straight-faced), and "strongest_unanswered_objection" (the one objection the piece deliberately leaves standing).
