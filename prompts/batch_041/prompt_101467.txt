I'm a science-and-ideas journalist and I sit on the data ethics panel for my local council (don't ask — it's basically me, a retired GP and a woman from the residents' association). Next week we vote on whether to wave through a predictive tool that children's services want to use to triage which families get an early-help visit. It's a parenting-support thing, non-punitive, but you only get the visit if you land in the top slice of the risk score.

Here's what's actually on the table. The model is well calibrated: among families scored 0.7, roughly 70% go on to have a serious incident of some kind. But the two main ethnic groups in the borough have different base rates — referrals for one group run at about double the other, mostly a hangover from where the old policing hotspots were. So if the tool is calibrated in both groups, it can't also hand out the same false-positive rate in both, and it can't hand out the same false-negative rate in both either. The vendor's dashboard literally has three buttons — 'equal error balance', 'equal miss rate', 'equal alarm rate' — and our statistician says no combination gets you the other two at once, and that this isn't a bug, it's arithmetic.

Two more things keep coming up and I don't know what to do with them. (a) The training labels come from past incidents recorded by a service that was itself stretched and unevenly spread, so 'risk' here partly means 'was watched'. (b) Because the tool only ever ranks families against each other for a fixed number of visits, a family's score can go up without anything about that family changing, just because other families' scores went down. Half the panel thinks (a) and (b) mean we should bin the whole thing; the other half thinks thats throwing away a decent tool for a bad reason.

Write me the short opinion I'll circulate before the vote — proceed, proceed with conditions, or don't proceed — with the reasoning, what we should do at the vote itself and what we should commit to over the six months after. I need something the children's services lead can actually reply to, and I need you to deal honestly with the strongest case against whatever you end up recommending, not just the easy one. And if I've got any of the above wrong — the maths, the framing, the whole idea that a panel like ours can decide this at all — say so straight out, seperate from the memo, rather than writing me something that just flatters the way I've set it up.
