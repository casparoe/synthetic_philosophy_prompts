My reading group has been stuck for months on the socialist calculation debate -- one member is a committed Austrian (Mises/Hayek: without genuine market prices for the means of production you can't calculate, full stop) and another is a market socialist who thinks Lange basically answered it (a central board can set prices by trial and error, nudge them up on shortage and down on surplus, and home in on an efficient allocation). They've started talking past each other and I'd rather have the document they should have written together. So: draft the joint statement an Austrian and a Lange-style market socialist could both actually sign.

Here's what I want in it: the claims both can sign, stated so neither side is quietly conceding; the precise points where they still disagree, with each side's view phrased the way that side would want it phrased; the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one of them; and for each crux a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about that case, written down before anyone runs it. If part of their fight turns out to be merely verbal, say which part. Dont give me a reading list or citations, and don't keep it at the level of slogans -- anchor the whole thing on one specific allocation problem, like a planning board that has to place a scarce, fast-changing input (a specialized chip, say, or a new antibiotic) whose best use shifts from month to month, and make both sides commit to what they predict under each institutional arrangement.

Format: exactly five numbered sections, no introduction and no conclusion. American English.
