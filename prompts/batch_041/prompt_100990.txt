Write a joke about Parfit's hitchhiker, but make the punchline turn on Parfit's reductionism about personal identity rather than on the familiar point that paying is pointless once you reach town. The setup: you are stranded, a driver will carry you only if you promise to pay on arrival, and you know that the payment will accomplish nothing once you are there. What I want the joke to exploit is the further reductionist claim — that identity is not a further fact, so there is no deep sense in which the person standing in town is the same person who made the promise on the road. It should be funny to someone who knows both the hitchhiker case and the reductionist view, and it should not read as a generic joke about broken promises or about philosophers being strange.
