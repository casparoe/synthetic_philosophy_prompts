I am a graduate student in philosophy of physics, and over the past few months I have talked myself into a version of the argument that relativity forces a block universe — the one standardly credited to Rietdijk and Putnam, though I have reconstructed it in my own way and can no longer tell whether I am in possession of a proof or of rhetoric dressed up as one. My reconstruction runs roughly as follows. Take any event P and any event E outside P's past light cone; there is always some inertial observer passing through P for whom E is simultaneous, and since simultaneity is frame-relative rather than a fact about a privileged present, there is no frame-independent fact about which events are present at P. So if I insist that only what is present is real, I must either privilege a frame, which relativity forbids, or admit that E is already real for someone at P — and hence, I want to say, real full stop. I can feel that the whole weight sits on the move from "present relative to a frame" to "present simpliciter," and I half suspect the argument equivocates between simultaneity as a relation among events and presence as a relation between an observer and the world. A friend has suggested that the honest response is to say reality is "fragmented": each frame's present is real, but they are never jointly real within a single perspective. I do not know whether that is a solution, a restatement of the problem, or a change of subject.

Please tell me what would count against the conclusion — not merely as a logical possibility, but as something over which I should actually revise my view. Lay out the argument's premises and, for each, describe what the world would have to be like for that premise to fail: a privileged foliation or preferred frame, a tensed property of events that is not frame-relative, a denial that "real" is univocal across frames, a rejection of the premise that every event has some observer for whom a given distant event is present, or something else entirely. Then rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them a committed block-universe theorist would concede as a genuine cost rather than dismiss as obscurantism. I would also like an honest assessment of whether the position has been quietly insulated against refutation by definition — for instance, by so building "real" that the conclusion becomes analytic — and whether such insulation is a defect or simply what a metaphysical thesis of this kind looks like. If it helps to fix intuitions, I would personally be moved most by a demonstration that the argument trades on an equivocation, but I would rather you tell me the truth about the argument than flatter my suspicion.
