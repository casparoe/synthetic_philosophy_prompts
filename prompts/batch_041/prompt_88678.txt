Chalmers introduced the meta-problem of consciousness as the problem of explaining why we think there's a hard problem of consciousness at all. The hard problem is explaining subjective experience; the meta-problem is explaining why we have the intuition that experience is mysterious and not reducible to physical processes. Chalmers thinks the meta-problem is actually the more tractable one, and that if we solve it—if we can explain why humans report and believe that consciousness is hard to explain—then we'll have gone a long way toward dissolving the hard problem itself. Illusionists like Frankish agree: once you explain the illusion that consciousness is something over and above physical processes, the hard problem evaporates. Chalmers himself is not an illusionist, but he thinks the meta-problem is a good research project because it can be tackled by materialists and non-materialists alike. The key point is that the meta-problem is about the representation of consciousness, not consciousness itself.
