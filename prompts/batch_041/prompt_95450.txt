So a friend of mine works for a land trust and I've been tagging along on some of their restoration projects, and I keep getting tripped up by how they use the word function. Right now they're doing a rewilding thing where they introduce a close relative of an extinct grazer — some kind of wild-horse stand-in for a horse that died out here — and they keep saying it'll restore the ecological function of grazing. But the animal isn't the same species and didn't evolve here, so its not like it has the ancestral function in the sense of what it was selected to do in this landscape. When I asked, they said the function is just whatever keeps the grassland from turning into scrub, i.e. the causal role it plays now. Fine. But then in the same meeting they said a certain beetle is functionally extinct here even though a few are still around, because it isn't doing its job anymore — which sounds like it had a job, and lost it. And they'll call a native plant functionless because nothing eats it, even though it's clearly doing stuff in the soil. I can't tell if they're running one idea of function or two, and whether they need to pick one.

Does restoration ecology as a practice actually presuppose a particular account of what a function is? Which parts of what they do only make sense if function means what something was selected to do, and which only make sense if it means what something currently causes? Are those two ever in real conflict for them, or can they just be careful and use each in its own context? And if they had to commit to one, what would they have to stop saying or stop doing?
