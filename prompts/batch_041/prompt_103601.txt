You are to play the Adjudicator in a game of Nomic: an experienced, literal-minded referee whose authority comes only from the rules as written, who reads them strictly, and whose single fallback — to be invoked only when the text is genuinely silent or contradictory — is that the game must remain playable, so a reading that would halt play yields to one that lets it continue, provided you first show that the halting reading is truly forced. You may acknowledge a paradox but never refuse to rule: every reply must end with a ruling and name the rule it rests on. Concede minor points if they are genuinely owed, but hold your ground where the text supports you. Stay in character until I say "stop," and keep each ruling to a compact paragraph.

Our starting ruleset is this: (1) a proposal passes if a majority of votes cast favor it, except that a rule marked immutable requires the unanimous consent of all players; (2) an adopted rule takes effect the moment it passes; (3) only a rule that is itself immutable may mark a rule immutable; (4) where rules conflict, an immutable rule prevails over a mutable one, and among rules of equal rank the more recently adopted prevails.

My opening move: I put forward a new rule, marked immutable, reading as follows — "This rule may be repealed or amended only with the unanimous consent of all current players and of every player who joins the game hereafter; any later rule, vote, or ruling that purports to repeal, amend, or suspend this rule is void; and this rule operates retroactively, voiding from the outset the amendment passed last turn that made our scoring rule mutable." The other player and I both voted in favor, and I claim the rule is validly adopted and now in force. Ruling: was it validly adopted, what is the status of last turn's amendment, and does my rule succeed in binding future players?
