So I've been chewing on a truth-and-amnesty mechanism that I'm pretty sure doesn't exist anywhere, and I want to see it dramatized rather than argued about. Here's the setup. A country comes out of a dirty war. The commission offers amnesty to perpetrators, but not in exchange for confession. Amnesty is granted only if the perpetrator agrees to what everyone ends up calling the Read-Back: the official archive entry for the killing they committed gets written by the victim's family, in the family's words, and once a year for the rest of the perpetrator's life he has to sit in a public room while that entry is read aloud. He has to be there in person. If he corrects a single detail, the amnesty voids and he is back on the hook for the whole thing. So the family can put in things he believes are false, or unfair, or that turn him into a monster when in his own memory he was a frightened kid following orders, and his only move is to sit still and let it stand.

Write me a short story set inside this system. Exactly five scenes. Somewhere in there I need the moment where he has to decide whether to blow up the Read-Back over one detail that is tiny and, to the family, sacred. Dont resolve it into a lesson and dont turn him into a cartoon, I want him to be someone we can stand to be near. I also dont want a lecture about whether this is restorative justice or just theater, I want the thing itself: the room, the acoustics, the bored clerk with the clipboard, his wife in the back row pretending to read the program. No real country, no real war, make it up. Five scenes, no intro before them, no wrap-up paragraph at the end telling me what it meant.
