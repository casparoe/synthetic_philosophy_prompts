I am assembling the closing item for a two-day symposium on accountability for autonomous systems: a mixed room of control engineers, two regulators, a lawyer or two, and enough philosophers to be a nuisance. The panels will spend two days circling what everyone there calls the responsibility gap without ever quite landing on it, and I would like to close with a poem, read aloud, that lands on it. Please write that poem.

The form is not decoration; it is the argument, so I would like it kept exactly. Six quatrains, twenty-four lines on the page, in a chain: the last line of each quatrain is also the first line of the next, and the last line of the sixth quatrain is the first line of the poem, so that the whole thing can be read round and round and never arrives anywhere. The shared lines should be written out both times. Do not announce any of this in the poem; the returning line should be the only signal that the loop has closed.

Each quatrain is spoken by a different link in the chain that produced the harm: whoever wrote the specification, whoever assembled the training data, whoever signed off on the deployment, the person at the console when it went wrong, and one further voice that is not a person at all. No quatrain may name its own speaker; a speaker should be identifiable only by what the stanza hands on and to whom.

The grammar should carry the point. No line may present an agent performing an action: everything that happens should arrive as mandated, inherited, undergone, assumed, or discovered, in the passive or the impersonal. There is one exception, and it should be the poem's first line — a first person finally claiming the thing — so that the same line, when it returns as the last line, has been emptied out by everything in between.

The harm itself you should choose and then never name: concrete enough to be real — a refusal, a collision, a mispriced loan, a missed diagnosis — but the poem should not describe it, only circle the place it came from.

I want the poem to get three things right, because the panels kept sliding off them. First, that no one could have known and no one was in a position to have done otherwise are different gaps, and a chain can have either without the other. Second, that the person at the console, nominally in charge and with no available move but to accept the recommendation, absorbs the blame precisely by being the last one with a name, and that this should be shown rather than lamented. Third, that the backward question of who is answerable and the forward question of who can change what happens next come apart; the last quatrain should raise the forward question and leave it open, with the returning opening line arriving after it.

Do not use the terms of art: no responsibility gap, no control, no tracing, no alignment, no accountability. Do not moralise — no "we must", no "in the age of", no robots, no consolation, no shrug. This will be read aloud to people who have been in a conference room for two days, so the lines must be speakable in one breath and intelligible without a footnote. Free verse is acceptable; a plain, scannable pentameter would be better than something showy.

Give me the poem first, then a section titled "On the form" of roughly 250 words, on what the loop, the shifting speakers, and the single claiming line are doing, and on what the poem deliberately leaves unresolved.
