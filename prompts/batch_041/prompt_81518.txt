I have no training in philosophy, but for the better part of a year I have been keeping a notebook of moral disagreements between different cultures, and I have had to admit that the notebook is worthless as it stands. Every entry reads the same way — they think this is wrong, we think it is fine — and what set me off in the first place was reading that the moral intuitions psychologists actually study come mostly from a narrow, educated, Western slice of the world. What I really want to record is not the bare fact of a difference but the conceptual problem underneath it: a feature of a particular case that makes it unclear what, if anything, the two sides are even disagreeing about, or unclear whether the difference counts as evidence that morality is more like a local language than like arithmetic. For example, I noticed that in some places whether a person deserves blame for a bad outcome turns almost entirely on what they meant to do, while elsewhere it turns on what they actually brought about, so that someone who intended harm and failed is judged worse than someone who caused harm by accident; the conceptual problem there is that one community seems to be ranking agents while the other is ranking outcomes, and it is not obvious that they are answering the same question at all. That is the sort of entry I am after: not they differ about X, but here is why, in this case, the difference resists being classified either as agreement or as disagreement.

Please give me five such entries. Each should be a short, self-contained case, invented or drawn from ordinary life, in which two communities answer a moral question differently, followed by a clause naming the specific conceptual issue the case raises; the five issues should be as unlike one another as you can make them, since I would rather have five genuinely distinct problems than five variations on the thought that one side is simply mistaken. I would also like each case stated in full, with whatever background a reader needs to follow it, and I would rather you not cite studies or authors at all, so that the cases stand on their own. Finally, please avoid the examples everyone has already heard; no trolleys and no arranged marriages, unless you can give one of them a genuinely unfamiliar angle.

Give the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
