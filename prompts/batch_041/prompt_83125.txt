I'm in a reading group that's been grinding through teleosemantics for about a month now -- the view that a mental state represents whatever the mechanism producing or consuming it was selected to do, so content is just biological function and no magic is needed. One of us showed up with an argument he's very pleased with, and I honestly can't tell if it's a party trick or a real problem, so I'm outsourcing the diagnosis. Here it is, cleaned up: (1) A representation's content is fixed by the biological function of the mechanism that produces or consumes it -- by what that mechanism was selected to do. (2) But every actual mechanism has been selected for a whole pile of effects at once: the chemotaxis machinery in a bacterium was selected for swimming up gradients, for reaching nutrients, for dividing, for persisting in the lineage at all. (3) Nothing in the selection history by itself singles one of these out as *the* function; any procedure for picking one is answering a question about what we find interesting, not a question about the biology. (4) So the content of the bacterium's internal state -- the thing that tracks whether conditions are getting better -- is indeterminate between 'nutrients increasing', 'conditions improving', 'food that way', and so on, with no fact of the matter. (5) An indeterminate representation is no representation; if nothing settles what a state represents, then it doesn't represent. (6) So the bacterium's state has no content, and nothing in the argument turned on it being a bacterium, so no state of any organism has content. (7) Hence the theory that was supposed to naturalize content entails that there is no content at all, which means either naturalism about content is hopeless or the theory is just wrong.

He says this shows teleosemantics refutes itself and we should all go be eliminativists. I'd like to keep (1), since that's the entire reason I care about the view in the first place, and I'd happily give up (5) if that's where the leak is, but I want to know what I'm paying for it. I know the frog and the bee are the usual exhibits, which is why we switched to the bacterium -- it's less worn, and the internal state there really does seem to track a derivative rather than a concentration, which is either great for the argument or a hint that something's off about it.

So: which step do you reject, why did it look airtight when we wrote it down, and what do the other exits cost? In particular, if you think the fix is something like 'content is fixed by the consumer's proper function, not by every effect selection was for', tell me whether that's a real distinction or just relabeling the problem -- because if it's relabeling, I'd rather know now than after another month of meetings.
