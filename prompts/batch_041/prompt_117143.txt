Ok, weird question, and I'm not sure there's a word for it. I've been reading about whether what you believe or expect can change what you actually see — the whole argument about whether the effect lands on perception or just on judgement — and I keep feeling that the thing I want a name for sits right next to that without being it.

Here's what I mean. I took a wine course a few years back. What changed wasn't my opinions about wine. Two glasses used to be just two glasses; now one of them is obviously oaked and I can't make it stop being oaked. A friend who trained as a radiologist says the same thing: chest films were grey mush for the first few weeks and then at some point they turned into a thing with parts, and she can't get the mush back, though when she's wrecked at 3am she gets flashes of it and it feels like being let in on what the rest of us see. Late-life music theory did it to me too — a chord now sounds like it's doing something in a key, and I honestly can't tell whether that's in the sound or something I'm putting there. The odd part is that none of this feels like interpreting. It feels like noticing. I'd bet on the oaked one. And I'd fail any exam where I had to explain to someone without the training what I'm now perceiving.

I know about the duck-rabbit and aspect-dawning, I assume there's a big literature on expert perception and perceptual learning, and someone told me it's just categorical perception. That doesn't sit right: categorical perception seems to be mostly about where a boundary falls on a continuum, whereas this is more like new parts and joints showing up inside something that used to be one undifferentiated lump. And the involuntariness and the it's-about-the-object feel seem like they're doing work.

So does it have a name, and if not, what exactly is it? I don't want a bare label. If it's cognitive penetration, tell me why the involuntariness and the felt objectivity don't put it there. If it's perceptual learning, which part, and is the reverting-under-fatigue bit part of it or a separate thing? If nothing fits, coin something and tell me what it rules out. Don't bother with citations or a reading list, I'll chase sources myself.
