I host a legal-theory podcast, and next week's guest is a scholar who defends a proposal she calls "sunset judicial review": when a court strikes down a statute as unconstitutional, its ruling would bind for a fixed term — say twenty years — after which the legislature could re-enact the statute by ordinary majority unless the court, on reconsideration, reaffirmed the ruling. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press the position rather than let her restate it. I want at least one question on whether the scheme genuinely answers the countermajoritarian objection or merely relocates it; one on how it would treat rights whose entire point is to be insulated from shifting majorities (she will likely reply that the court can simply reaffirm, so prepare a follow-up for that reply); and one on the transition problem — what becomes of settled expectations and of litigation pending during a ruling's term. For each of those three, include the follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and close with a question about what evidence or development would show the scheme had failed.
