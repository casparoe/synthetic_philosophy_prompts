The halfing–thirding dispute over Sleeping Beauty has run for decades among people who agree on every detail of the experimental setup and, apparently, on the axioms of probability, and nothing has moved. I do not want another rehearsal of the arguments; I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. Is it verbal, with each side attaching a different sense to 'credence' or 'degree of belief'? Is it two camps answering different questions under a single heading — one a non-self-locating probability of the coin, the other a self-locating credence — or is the real split between an epistemic question and a practical one about what Beauty should stake? Is it a disagreement about what counts as her evidence, or about which cases are even data? Or is it bedrock intuition about what rationality may demand of an agent who may be duplicated, which no argument reaches? My working hypothesis is the equivocation reading, but I want it tested against what each side actually says rather than assumed. Given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a reframing of the question — or argue that none should be expected. Answer in a single paragraph of no more than 350 words.
