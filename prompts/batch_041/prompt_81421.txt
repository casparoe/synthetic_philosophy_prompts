I'm giving a talk at a small interdisciplinary workshop on the Anthropocene in a few weeks and I need a Q&A prep sheet. Take the role of a philosopher of science who is equally at home in the stratigraphic code and in the humanities literature and who has watched this fight drag on for years.

My thesis, roughly: the Anthropocene should be admitted to the geological time scale as an *event* -- like the Great Oxidation Event, or the impact at the end of the Cretaceous -- rather than as an epoch with a base and a golden spike. Events are diachronous: the thing shows up in different places at different times, 1610 in the Americas, mid-20th century in the fallout record, later still in parts of the deep ocean. I want to argue that diachroneity is a feature of the concept rather than a defect. Concretely I want to propose a seperate event register kept alongside the time scale, and a joint body of stratigraphers plus historians and Earth-system scientists to decide what goes into it, on the grounds that the commission which voted the golden-spike proposal down shouldn't have sole jurisdiction over a category that was never purely stratigraphic to begin with.

The room will be roughly: (a) four or five Quaternary stratigraphers who are still sore about how that vote went and who care about procedure -- synchronous markers, whether a thing is a unit or a phenomenon, what precedent does to the rest of the chart; (b) a bloc of environmental historians and anthropologists who think the whole exercise is a category error and that dating the Anthropocene to the bomb tests erases centuries of colonial violence (Capitalocene, Plantationocene, that crowd); (c) two Earth-system scientists who think the stratigraphers are being pedantic and just want a word they can use; (d) one or two geologists who will worry out loud about slippery slopes.

Before you start writing, think carefully about who actually holds power in hte room and what each faction can't afford to concede. Then give me the eight or so objections I'll really get, in the order they'll come, phrased the way each group would phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a flag on where the reply is weakest. Order them by how much damage they do, not by how likely they are. Tell me what to concede up front, what to hold, and finish with the one question I'm hoping nobody asks. No reading lists or citations, just the argument.
