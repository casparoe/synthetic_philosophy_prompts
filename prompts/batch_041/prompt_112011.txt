In molecular biology it is routine to say that DNA carries information and that the genetic code is a code. I would like you to compare two ways of taking that talk seriously, and then decide between them.

The first view treats DNA as a genuine message. On this view a gene sequence encodes content roughly the way a written sentence encodes content: the mapping from codons to amino acids fixes a meaning that does not depend on any particular cell reading it, the cellular machinery decodes that meaning, and it is precisely the arbitrariness of the mapping — the fact that the assignment of codons to amino acids could have been otherwise — that licenses calling it a code rather than a mere chemical regularity.

The second view denies that the molecule carries information in any interesting sense. On this view information is not a stuff stored in DNA but a relation between a sequence and a receiver (or an observer with an interest); DNA is simply one causal input among many — proteins, membranes, the cellular environment — and speaking of it as the code or the blueprint wrongly promotes one participant in a distributed process to the status of author.

Please compare these two views and adjudicate between them, using three cases:
(a) a genotype that yields different phenotypes in different environments;
(b) the arbitrariness of the codon assignments;
(c) a synonymous (silent) mutation that leaves the protein unchanged.

Structure your answer as follows:

1. The two views in plain terms — around 150 words.
2. Where the real disagreement lies, i.e. what each view is claiming about the word information — around 200 words.
3. The three cases worked through — around 400 words.
4. Verdict: which view you find more defensible, and what evidence or argument would make you change your mind — around 200 words.
