I co-teach a graduate seminar on the foundations of set theory, and my co-instructor has spent the term ruling case by case on set-abstraction principles. Each case is put to her in the same form: is there a set whose members are exactly the things satisfying such-and-such a condition? Her answers have been stable and, so far as I can tell, never in tension with one another. What I cannot recover is the principle generating them.

She accepts all of the following.

1. The things that are not identical with themselves. She says the empty set exists.
2. For any set a, the things identical with a.
3. For any sets a and b, the things identical with a or identical with b.
4. For any set a, the things that are members of members of a.
5. For any set a, the things all of whose members are members of a.
6. For any sets a and b, the things that are members of both a and b.
7. For any sets a and b, the things that are members of a and are not members of b.

She rejects exactly one principle so far: the things that are not members of themselves. Asked why, she says only that the condition is ill-formed, and declines to elaborate.

Two features of the record seem worth flagging. Her rejection is plainly not a blanket suspicion of negation, since principle 7 contains one. And every collection she has accepted is either the empty set or a subcollection of one of the given sets or built out of finitely many given sets; the one collection she has rejected is none of those things.

The next case on her list is the universal collection: is there a set whose members are exactly the things identical with themselves? She rules next week, and I would like your assessment beforehand. Tell me what she will say; what general rules are consistent with every verdict she has given; what each of those rules predicts about the universal collection; and which rule you would bet she is running, and why. Then tell me which further case would most sharply separate the surviving rules, and whether anything she has already accepted rules out either answer about the universal collection. I want the discriminating cases more than the verdict.
