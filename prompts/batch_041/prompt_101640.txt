I am a novelist at work on a book about people who discover that something they never thought of as a belief — a geologist's confidence in the slow deep time of the earth, a translator's trust that her mother tongue means what she thinks it means — was a commitment all along, and can be lost. In the reading I have been doing for it I keep returning to Wittgenstein's late notes on certainty, and I have found myself wanting to argue with them rather than merely admire them, so I would like you to help me do that by taking their side against me. Please play a committed philosopher of the Wittgensteinian stripe — someone who holds that the propositions Moore paraded, such as that here is a hand, that the earth existed long before my birth, that I have never been to the moon, are not items of knowledge or opinion at all but the hinges on which inquiry turns, exempt from doubt not because they are certain in some evidential sense but because doubting them would require the very framework they supply. Stay in character throughout, speak in your own voice rather than summarizing anyone's texts, concede a point when I earn it but never surrender the hinge, and keep each turn to no more than a short paragraph of conversation.

Here is my opening move, and I should say at once that I mean to use your own image against you. You and your Wittgenstein like to say that the certain propositions are a riverbed and our doubts and inquiries the water that flows over it, sometimes cutting new channels, sometimes depositing new sediment. But a riverbed is exactly the sort of thing that shifts. We once took it as a hinge that the sun goes round the earth, that kinds are fixed, that the heavens are incorruptible; those hinges were not doubted so much as dissolved, and we did not experience it as losing our grip on sense. So either the distinction between hinge and hypothesis is one of degree of entrenchment — in which case it is an empirical fact about us, revisable like any other, and not the transcendental role you want for it — or you must tell me what, in the moment just before a hinge is abandoned, distinguishes it from a belief that someone is finally persuaded to give up. I want your answer to work as conversation rather than as a lecture, so give me your reply as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.
