I'm a novelist — three books in, the last two about a Caribbean-British family not unlike my own — and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for. The obvious labels all seem slightly off, so let me just describe it and see what you make of it.

In my second novel there's a character, the eldest son, who steals from his mother's savings and then lies about it to everyone. When the book came out I got two letters, one from each side of an argument I hadn't realised I was walking into. The first said: thank you for showing us as we are, we're not saints, and any portrait that pretends otherwise is a lie that serves the people who want us to be either angels or animals. The second said: why did you have to make him a thief? You've handed them another stick to beat us with, and you knew they'd use it. Both letters came from people who loved the book. Both came from people who'd lived through the same things I had.

What gets me is that the disagreement isn't about facts — everyone agrees there are Caribbean-British men who steal from their mothers. And it isn't really about whether art should be political either; everyone in the room agrees it is, whether it wants to be or not. It's about something stranger: whether a depiction's meaning is fixed by the person who made it or by the uses it gets put to afterwards. I keep trying to seperate the two questions — what the book says and what people do with it — and I can't. And I've noticed the second letter is right in a way I can't argue with. The same passage that reads as honesty in one decade reads as betrayal in the next, and I've got no control over that. So the political force of what I write isn't a property of the writing at all — it's something that happens to it later, in other people's hands, and getting the depiction right doesn't secure it. That's the bit I can't find a name for. I've come across the idea — I couldn't tell you who said it first — that all art is propaganda whether it admits to it or not, and I think there's something in it, but it's too general for this. It makes the problem sound like a choice about content, whereas what I'm describing operates whatever content I choose.

So: is there an established name for this exact structure? If there is, I want the carving, not just the label — what the thing is, what it isn't, and how it differs from the nearest concepts, with a verdict on which of those nearest concepts actually fits best even if none fits exactly. If there isn't a name, coin one and define it, and give me boundary cases: tell me what would have to be true for the phenomenon to be absent, and describe a case where both of my letters would be wrong. And I definately don't want a reading list — I want the idea.

Please don't lay out the options and leave me to pick. I want your actual position — which concept you think this is, or what you'd call it, and the reasoning that gets you there. If you think I've misdescribed my own experience, say so and say why. Take the role of a philosopher of art who actually knows the twentieth-century debates about art and politics — I'd rather have someone who knows the terrain than someone who invents something from scratch. Think it through carefully, and start by brainstorming a few candidate concepts and testing each against my two letters before you commit.
