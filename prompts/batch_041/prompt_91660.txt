I'm assembling a short position paper on the gender-equality paradox for a graduate seminar, and before I commit to it I would like an audit of whether my commitments actually cohere. Here is roughly where I have landed.

(1) I believe there are real average differences between men and women in interests and in the kinds of work they gravitate toward; I am not in the camp that treats the differences as wholly illusory.

(2) I believe the magnitude of those differences, and the occupational sorting they produce, is heavily shaped by institutions, incentives, and norms — change the context and the numbers move a great deal.

(3) I accept the empirical pattern usually called the gender-equality paradox: in wealthier and more gender-egalitarian societies, several measures of sex-typed occupational sorting, and some measured preference differences, are often larger rather than smaller than in poorer or less egalitarian societies.

(4) I think there is probably some modest biological contribution to average interests — say, on a people-versus-things axis — that socialization would not fully erase.

(5) I hold that justice requires we not treat group averages as individual destinies, and that a society should work to keep gender from constraining what people are able to choose.

(6) My preferred explanation of (3) is that richer, more egalitarian societies relax the material and social pressures that otherwise herd everyone toward the same options, so that underlying predispositions have more room to express themselves; on this view the constraints were masking differences rather than creating them.

(7) I also suspect that a good part of the paradox concerns the spread of outcomes rather than any change in the average man or the average woman — that egalitarian societies widen the range of what people do, which can raise segregation indices without men and women becoming more different on average.

Please audit this set rather than summarize the surrounding debate. Which of these commitments pull against one another, and how severely? I expect some of the apparent conflicts to be merely apparent — identify those and explain why they dissolve. For the conflicts that are real, rank them by severity, state exactly what would have to give, and give me the cheapest single revision that would restore coherence and what it would cost me elsewhere in the package. I am less interested in which claim is fashionable than in what my own position commits me to, so be specific about the inferential links I may be taking for granted.
