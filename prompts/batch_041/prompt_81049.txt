I run the renewal desk at a small foundation that makes multi-year research grants, so about fifteen times a quarter I have to decide whether to keep funding something or kill it, and the money for a renewal comes out of someone else's budget. We keep having the same fight and I'm sick of it: I say the money's already gone, it's sunk, decide on the margin, and someone else says we can't quit now, we've got two years into this. I know the second person is supposed to be committing the sunk cost fallacy, but some of our cases don't feel like that to me and I can't articulate why.

Can you write me a procedure my team can actually run, in order, on a renewal? I want the questions, what counts as a real answer to each, where it just stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because it's a values call, not a fact. It needs to handle at least these: the case where how much we've already spent is genuinely evidence the project is good (we funded it for reasons, we're not idiots); the case where the payoff is all-or-nothing, so a half-built instrument or a cohort study stopped at year four is worth roughly nothing while finishing is worth a lot, and where "we've come this far" is doing real work; the case where "we've come this far" is really the original program officer protecting her own past judgment; and the case where committing in advance to finish is the only way to stop ourselves re-litigating at every review. Also be honest about which steps are arithmetic and which are judgment in a lab coat.

Then run it on three grants: one where the science is still fine but the lead postdoc left and the original question has drifted; one where it's a five-year cohort study in year four and only the final year makes the dataset usable; and one where we've spent about 80% of the budget for 20% of the results and everyone involved is personally invested. My director signs off but only reads page one, so tell me what to put there. And tell me if one of these comes out kill, because I suspect I wont like the answer.
