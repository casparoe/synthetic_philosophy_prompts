I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have fallen into a recurring argument with two friends — a labor organizer and a former campaign staffer — about what we are actually doing when we cast a vote. The organizer holds that voting is a duty we owe to one another, something closer to paying a debt to a system that protects us, so that abstaining is a kind of free-riding on other people's efforts; the staffer holds that voting is mostly an expressive act, a way of declaring who we are and what we stand for, and that because any single vote's effect on the outcome is vanishingly small, it would be irrational to vote for instrumental reasons at all. I can feel the shape of both views, but I cannot get them to sit next to each other in my head, and I suspect each is quietly borrowing assumptions from the other without admitting it.

Please compare these two accounts of voting — the duty-of-justice account and the expressive account — as carefully as you can. I want to understand what each is really claiming about the act of voting itself, what each must assume about an individual's causal impact and about what makes an action rational, and where the two genuinely conflict as opposed to merely answering different questions. Along the way, please make explicit what each account would say about the person who votes purely because it makes them feel like the sort of person who votes, and about the person who abstains while fully endorsing the duty to vote.

Then I want you to assess a third proposal, which is mine and which I am not sure is even coherent: that a vote should be understood as a very cheap insurance policy against the worst political outcomes, where the premium is the small cost of voting and the payout is a marginally lower probability of catastrophe, so that the policy is worth buying even though its expected value looks absurd on any individual calculation. Tell me whether this insurance framing is a genuine third position or merely a dressed-up version of one of the two accounts I have already described, which account it is closest to if it collapses into one of them, and which of the three a careful person should ultimately hold, with your reasoning.
