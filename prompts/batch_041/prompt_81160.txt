My mates and I have a standing argument at the pub that's got way out of hand and I keep loosing it, and I'm not even sure why. It's about where meaning comes from in the biological case — a frog's brain cell, a bee's dance, that kind of thing. I read up on it a bit and apparently there's a whole position called teleosemantics (Millikan is the name that keeps coming up?) where a mental state or a signal means whatever it was *for* — its job, the thing natural selection or learning built the mechanism to do — so it's the history that pins down the content. My mate Dan thinks that's rubbish. For him a state means whatever it reliably tracks and whatever the animal then does with it, full stop; the evolutionary backstory is irrelevant, and anyway "what it's for" is just a story we tell after the fact. We go round in circles and both of us end up just asserting our side louder.

So: write me a dialogue between two people holding those two views. Make them both sharp — I don't want the teleosemantics one to be a genius and Dan's one to be a chump, or the reverse; I want them actually answering each other's last point, not two monologues stapled together. Set it somewhere boring, like one of them driving the other to the airport, and give them names.

I want them fighting over a concrete case, but I don't trust myself to get real animals right, so use one I made up (don't worry whether it exists). Say there's a little burrowing animal with a single neuron that fires when the ground shakes and makes it dive underground. Sometimes the shaking is a predator, sometimes it's a clumsy mate tramping past, sometimes just a rockfall — so is that neuron's content "predator", or "shaking", or "danger", or is there no fact of the matter at all? And a twist: what if some of these animals learn the dive by copying a parent instead of being born with it — does that change what the signal means, and if so, does the teleosemantics person have to say the learned version is a different state from the inherited one? Push them into the weeds on how a state can ever be wrong if it's defined by what it's for, and on what stops the content from ballooning into "shaking, or a mate, or anything else that sets it off". Also let the other one spring the case of a perfect duplicate of the animal that pops into existence with no history whatsoever — does the teleosemantics person have to say its neuron means nothing? Let them land real hits on each other, don't let either of them win by pointing at who said what, and don't force a tidy agreement at the end.
