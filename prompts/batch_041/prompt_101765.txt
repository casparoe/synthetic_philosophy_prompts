You're a philosopher who also writes comedy — you know the expressivism literature cold and you can keep a straight face. I want a short satirical piece: a straight-faced modest proposal where every joke comes from the philosophy and not from wackiness.

The view it speaks from is the crude one — moral sentences dont state facts, they just vent the speaker's approval or disapproval (the old boo-hurrah picture). The pressure point to build on is that moral words seem to mean the same thing whether or not anyone's asserting them. Take the claim that lying is wrong and stick it in the antecedent of a conditional: if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong. Or put it under a not. Or inside a report, like she doubts that lying is wrong. On the boo-hurrah picture nobody is booing in those positions, so what are the words doing there? And without a story about that, modus ponens for moral arguments falls over, since the clause in the if has to mean what it means when asserted or the inference is just a pun on words. (This is the Frege–Geach problem; the sameness-of-sense-in-unasserted-contexts bit is sometimes called the Frege point.)

Now the ask. Your narrator is a sincere reformer who has decided that since embedding is exactly where the theory gets into trouble, the fix is to stop embedding — a cleanup of unasserted moral contexts from public life. Write their proposal, deadpan, in numbered sections (findings, the reform, implementation, likely objections, conclusion), around 500-650 words. Let them clock the real costs one at a time and answer each with a workaround that makes it worse: no moral conditionals, so no moral reasoning; no negations, so nothing gets denied, only booed; two people booing at each other arent disagreeing, they're a duet; no wondering whether, no asking whether, no believing that. Workarounds can include a short approved list of substitute locutions, a licence you need before you may express conditional disapproval, and a complaints procedure for people whose boos were mistranslated. The reformer must never drift into realism or quasi-realism to escape — the internal consistency is the joke, and the plan should collapse under its own weight by the end without anyone saying so.

No real names, no citations, no winking asides. Before you write, take a moment to brainstorm the embedded contexts that cause trouble, the workarounds, and the exact point where the plan breaks. Someone who knows the literature should wince and laugh; someone who doesnt should still see what's wrong with the plan.
