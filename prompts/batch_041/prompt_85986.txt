Write a dialogue between a Bayesian statistician and a frequentist statistician arguing about whether the replication crisis is really a problem with frequentist methods or just with research practices. I'm a software engineer with no stats background so alot of what I know is from blog posts, and the idea that you should just switch to Bayes factors seems to be everywhere. I want the frequentist to push back with real arguments and not be a strawman — the Bayes factor is super sensitive to the prior's scale, the Jeffreys-Lindley thing means a significant p-value with tons of data can still come out as evidence for the null under a vague prior, and Bayesians have there own garden of forking paths since you can pick the prior after seeing the data. But the Bayesian should give as good as they get, like that error rates aren't what a scientist actually wants to know, or that alot of failed replications are just small samples and publication bias and that's not a fault of the math. One thing I'd really like them to go back and forth on: whether it's circular to evaluate a replication using the original study's posterior as the prior. And end with them agreeing on something, even if it's just what they'd do differently next time. Neither side should win by default.
