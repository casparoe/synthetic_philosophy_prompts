I'm drafting a statement of my considered view of pain for a seminar and would like a consistency audit before I present it. Here is where I have landed.

First, I take the badness of pain to be intrinsic to the experience: a pain is bad for the one who has it in virtue of how it feels — its unpleasantness — and not in virtue of what it disposes the sufferer to do about it. Pain is my paradigm case of something bad in itself rather than for its consequences.

Second, I take what makes a pain bad to be its imperatival character — the way it commands the sufferer to make it stop. On this account the badness of pain just is its aversive motivational force, so that a state carrying no such demand would not be bad.

Third, I accept a certain clinical picture. In the condition of pain asymbolia, the sensory aspect of pain survives while the aversive aspect does not: patients can locate the stimulus and report its intensity, yet they are not bothered by it and show no withdrawal or distress. The two aspects can be pulled apart.

Fourth, and following from the above, I say that the pain of an asymbolic patient is not bad for that patient. They are not suffering, and nothing in their state gives them a reason to want it gone.

Fifth, I still think it would be wrong to inflict such pain on someone against their will. The wrong lies in the imposition, and in the disregard it expresses, rather than in the quantity of suffering produced.

Sixth, I think a life can go badly for a person without their being disposed to protest: the humiliated person who has come to accept his humiliation is worse off than he was before, and I would say the same of someone slipping into dementia who no longer registers what is being lost.

Some of these I reached independently and others plainly lean on one another, and I cannot tell whether the package hangs together. Please tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, how severe each real tension is, and what the cheapest revision would be for each — and then which single change would resolve the most. I am not yet attached to giving up any particular one of them.
