I'm a backend engineer and somehow I ended up on the board of our county history museum (long story, I got roped in because I maintain the website). Next Thursday the board votes on whether to host a traveling exhibit that a local family is offering to pay for. It's about a supposed advanced civilization that existed before the last ice age and then got wiped out, and it leans hard on the idea that mainstream archaeologists have "sat on" the evidence against it. The curator isn't an obvious crank -- he talks like a researcher, and he's got a list of maybe eight people with real degrees who back him up. His pitch is that he isn't asking us to endorse anything: he just wants the exhibit shown as one "hypothesis" among others, and he wants to display it with what he calls a "confidence continuum" -- every claim, mainstream or fringe, gets a 0-to-100 confidence score on one big chart, so visitors can "weigh the evidence themselves."

Two board members are into this and keep saying the museum shouldn't be in the business of picking winners. I don't have a good comeback and honestly I'm not sure they're wrong.

The complication: a chunk of the exhibit is actually solid. There's a genuinly good section on how carbon dating and ice cores work, and it's the only part that's about our actual county. So a flat no throws away something real. And the family funding it has donated before and might walk if we reject it.

I need to draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. Give me a clear recommendation with a concrete plan and a date -- not just "more study." And I want criteria the board can actually apply to the next weird exhibit that comes along, not just this one. Please take the neutrality argument seriously enough that my colleagues will feel heard, since I'll be reading this out loud to them.
