I'm putting together the final for our first-year PhD game theory module (the one on solution concepts — dominance, rationalizability, refinements). It's a 50-minute paper and I need one 20-mark question on iterated elimination by dominance once mixed strategies are in play. The thing I keep seeing is that students can chant "strict dominance is order independent, weak dominance isnt" and then do absolutely nothing with it, so I want the question built around a game they have to actually grind through rather than a definition they can recite back.

Here's the game I want to use — three strategies each, symmetric, so the two numbers in each cell are equal and you can read the same matrix for both players:

        L        C        R
T    (4,4)    (2,2)    (1,1)
M    (2,2)    (2,2)    (3,3)
B    (1,1)    (3,3)    (6,6)

Parts I want:

(a) 5 marks — get them to show M is strictly dominated by a mixed strategy, but not by any pure strategy, and in fact not even weakly dominated by a pure strategy. I want the answer to say why "just check the pure strategies" isnt a shortcut and why dominance never depends on what you believe the other player will do.

(b) 5 marks — run iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies to the end on this game, say exactly what survives, and then explain why the surviving set can't depend on the order you delete things in. Reason, not slogan.

(c) 6 marks — make them construct their own 2x2 where iterated elimination of weakly dominated strategies deletes a Nash equilibrium, and then explain the epistemic gap: why common knowledge of rationality buys you the strict version but not the weak one, and what extra assumption about beliefs the weak version has to import. In the marking scheme give a canonical example — I've been using one where row's B is weakly dominated by T and column's R is strictly dominated by L, with (T,L) = (1,1), (T,R) = (1,0), (B,L) = (1,1), (B,R) = (0,0), so (B,L) is a genuine NE that the procedure kills. Check that by hand before you put it in though.

(d) 4 marks — find all the Nash equilibria of the 3x3, including the mixed one, and say why IESDS can never delete an equilibrium while IEWDS can.

Deliverables: the question as it would be printed (matrix included), a model answer of about 600-700 words (no names or citations needed, I just want the arguments to be tight), and then the marking scheme as a table with exactly these columns: Part | Criterion (the move a full-credit answer makes) | Marks | What a B-level answer does instead | Error this catches. Marks in that table have to sum to 20. After the table, a compact JSON object with fields: level, time_minutes, total_marks, parts (a list, each entry with id, marks, crux, must_hit, typical_error), and a_vs_b_line.

Two things I'll be picky about: the mixed strategy in (d) has to be exact, no decimal approximations, and in (b) the answer should definately say what does *not* get deleted, not just what does — that's where the marks are hiding.
