I'm putting together a metaphysics unit on properties and universals, and before we read any of the theories I want the spec. So: what does a theory of properties (universals, if you like) have to deliver to count as adequate? Give me the requirements list, with the case behind each one -- the phenomenon or the example that makes it a requirement rather than a preference -- and say which of them are genuine desiderata and which are just the taste of one camp dressed up as a constraint. A few cases I want the list built around, because there the ones I keep tripping over:

- two properties that happen, as a matter of fact, always to turn up in exactly the same things, so you can't tell the class of instances of one from the class of instances of the other (some people call this the companionship difficulty);
- a crowd of things where every pair resembles in some respect, but there's no single respect they all share -- so a theory that says a property just is the class of things that resemble one another ends up with a 'property' that's nothing in particular;
- nothing is wholly red and wholly blue at once, yet 'red or blue' is satisfied by plenty of things, so not every class and not every predicate can be a property;
- properties that drag in a particular individual: being someone's sibling, being the capital of one particular country;
- properties nothing ever has -- the exact determinate value of some quantity no object has ever taken, or a property only a possible-but-never-actual particle would have;
- instantiation itself, where you seem to need a further relation between the thing and its property, and then a relation between the thing, the property and that relation, and off it goes.

Then the bit I actually care about: are any of these requirements jointly unsatisfiable? I'd bet theres at least one pair where you have to give something up, and I want the trade-off spelled out rather than smoothed over. Say too which single requirement does the most work at ruling candidates out, and whether that's the one I'd have expected. Keep any scoring of the going views (realism about universals, tropes, class nominalism, whatever else is in play) to a couple of lines at the end -- the criteria are the deliverable, I'll do the marking myself. Purpose, in case it helps you fix what counts as adequate: this is for a metaphysics that has to stay on speaking terms with natural science, so nothing gets to be spooky or primitive unless it's earning its keep.
