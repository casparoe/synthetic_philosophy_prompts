I'm preparing a decision tree for a graduate seminar on insect welfare and the scale argument, and I want it built from the logical space rather than from a list of authors. Take as fixed that there is a real but low probability that insects are sentient, that insect populations are astronomical, and that the expected numbers are therefore enormous. Each branch node must be a question a reader can answer without knowing which position it leads to, and must do real separating work. The nodes I want on the tree: whether the relevant probability is a single credence or a range, and what a range licenses; whether uncertainty about sentience is ordinary empirical uncertainty or a special kind of moral-status uncertainty that calls for caution; whether the decision rule is expected-value maximisation, a probability threshold, or something risk-averse; whether the unit of concern is individual insects, lineages, or whole ecosystems; and whether the relevant harm is what we do to insects or the sheer fact that so many of them exist. Leaves should be named positions, each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment; I especially want to know whether the tree separates those who treat insect welfare as the top cause area, those who treat it as a research priority only, and those who think the argument is a distraction dressed up as a calculation. Make the tree decide a concrete case: a practice that kills a trillion insects outright but replaces one in which ten trillion live short, pain-filled lives. Flag any leaves the tree cannot separate, and note which combinations of answers are unoccupied and whether that is because they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Begin with the tree's most consequential branch and the positions it yields, then give the tree.
