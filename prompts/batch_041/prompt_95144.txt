hey, so im a junior and i run the debate club at my school. next thursday i have to fill about 40 minutes for 15 kids (mostly 15-17, three or four of them are genuinely sharp, the rest are there cause their friends are) and i want to do it on the boundary problem — who actually gets to count as part of the group that decides. i dont want you to explain the topic to me, i already get it. i want you to plan how i explain it.

heres why i picked it. our student council is in the middle of a real mess about this and its the best thing thats happened to me all year. theres a proposal to move wednesday start time to 10 instead of 8. council announced only students vote on it. then parents got mad because theyre the ones driving us and rearranging work. then someone pointed out the cafeteria staff would have to come in earlier anyway to have food ready, so theyre clearly affected but obviously not voting. then my friend jaya said what about the freshmen coming in next year who have to live with a 10am start for four years, theyre not even here. and then it got genuinely unhinged because the principal said, in front of everyone, that whoever is affected by a decision should get a say in it, which sounds great until you notice that literally everyone in town is affected by literally everything, so now the whole town votes on our bell schedule.

what i want them to walk out with is the loop: the group cant decide whos in the group without already being a group, so every answer you give either spirals outward forever or just quietly draws a line somewhere and pretends it didnt. i also want them to feel two specific traps, not just hear me list them. trap one: if you say anyone affected can opt in and vote, then the people who bother to show up are exactly the people who want the change, so the group is basically picking itself and calling it democracy. trap two: if you say no, the group was fixed in advance, then whoever fixed it is the actual decider and the vote is theater.

so: plan my 40 minutes. what has to come first, and what depends on what — like, do they need the opt-in trap before or after the future-freshmen thing, and why. what single example should carry the whole session (i assume the wednesday fight, but tell me if a different example would carry it better and what that example would need to have and not have to actually do the job). the misconceptions theyll walk in with — im guessing at least: that this is obvious and the answer is just whoever it affects; that democracy is majority rule and membership is a totally separate issue; that someone has to draw the line so its not a real philosophical problem — and exactly where in the sequence i should head each one off. one question i can ask at the end that would show me whether it landed or whether they just nodded. and what i should openly skip and tell them im skipping, cause half of them are gone by minute 25.

dont give me readings, they wont do them, and dont give me a worksheet. i want the plan.
