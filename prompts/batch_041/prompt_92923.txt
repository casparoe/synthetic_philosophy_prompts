Ok so I'm putting together a six-month working group on the singleton idea -- one decision-making agency at the top of the world order, whatever form it takes -- and I need help figuring out what we actually spend the time on. The group is four political theorists, one economist, and me (I did a masters in philosophy of science). Nobody's an ML person and we're not going to settle whether it's technically buildable, so assume for the sake of argument that some kind of singleton could exist and ask what's worth thinking about once you've granted that.

My hunch is that the flashy questions (is it good, is it likely, should we want it) are all downstream of the boring-sounding structural ones, but I want you to tell me if thats wrong. Heres the pile I keep staring at:

- what actually makes a singleton stable -- legitimacy, coercion, or just being the only game in town -- and whether stability is even the right target to optimize
- the succession problem: a singleton run by a mortal person or a small committee is just a temporary monopoly, so does the idea only work if the decider is an institution or a rule rather than a ruler, and is that distinction even coherent
- exit and voice: with no outside option, what stops it from being a prison (Hirschman feels relevant but I don't know if anyone's actually done this work)
- whether polycentric governance, overlapping jurisdictions, Ostrom-ish stuff, is a real alternative at planetary scale or just nostalgia for small-scale society
- the transition: are all paths to a singleton equally bad, or does the question only really bite for some of them
- self-binding: can a singleton credibly commit to limits (Ulysses contracts, constitutions that can't be amended), and what happens when it can't
- the epistemic tradeoff: what it loses by giving up the error-correction of disagreement vs what it gains in coordination

Which two or three of these (or something I'm missing entirely) are upstream of the rest, and in what order would you put them for a group like ours? Defend the ordering: what makes a question worth six months of five peoples attention, which ones are tractable for political theory rather than econ or ML, which would you tell us to drop, and what evidence or argument over the next couple years would move something up or down the list.
