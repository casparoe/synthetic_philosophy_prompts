I am a distributed-systems engineer. My working vocabulary is message passing over unreliable channels, local states and views, partial failure, two-phase commit, agreement and consensus protocols, the coordinated-attack scenario, and the gap between what a node knows locally and what is true of the system as a whole. I have no formal training in game theory, but I am fluent in the protocol vocabulary and I think in it.

I want you to take the core of epistemic game theory as it is built on common knowledge — the notion of common knowledge itself, including its infinite hierarchy (I know that you know that I know …); the result that common knowledge of one another's beliefs forces agreement; and the appeal to common knowledge of rationality to characterize equilibrium and to generate the backward-induction puzzles — and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in my vocabulary. Treat this as a genuine translation rather than an analogy: for each epistemic notion, give the nearest protocol-level notion, and say explicitly where the nearest notion is a distortion rather than an equivalent.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims carry over intact, which carry over only after being bent to fit (and describe how they bend), and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I am especially interested in whether "rationality" and the possible-worlds semantics of knowledge — what an agent would know or do in counterfactual states, not merely the states actually realized — can be expressed at all in terms of protocol states and message histories, or whether they are doing work my vocabulary cannot represent.

Finally, tell me what the residue indicates. Is the untranslatable remainder a defect of my framework, something distributed systems ought to be able to say and cannot, or is it a sign that the game-theoretic formulation carries assumptions less determinate than they appear? I would accept the verdict that my vocabulary is the impoverished one if that is the honest reading, but I want the verdict argued rather than assumed.
