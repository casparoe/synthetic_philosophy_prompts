hey, I'm a second-year phil student and I'm in a seminar on philosphy of medicine. we've spent like a month on the naturalism vs normativism fight over 'disease' and at this point the two sides are just talking past each other. our prof keeps saying we should do an "adverserial collaboration" instead of yet another debate, but none of us can picture what the actual document would even look like.

so can you write that document for me. the two camps are:

Camp A (naturalist): whether X is a disease is a fact about the organism -- roughly, some part isn't doing the job it's supposed to do, or is functioning below the species-typical level. values only come in afterwards, when we decide what to treat or when we call someone "ill".

Camp B (normativist): there's no value-free notion of "dysfunction" to get you off the ground in the first place, so calling something a disease already carries an evaluation -- about harm, about disvalue, about what medicine is for, or just about what we're willing to treat.

I want something both camps could actually put their names to, which means:
- the claims they both accept, including claims about what the dispute is about and what it isn't
- the real disagreements, stated so that each side would accept the wording of its own position (no strawmen, and no wording that quietly builds the other side's assumptions in)
- the cruxes, i.e. sub-questions where settling one would force at least one side to give something up
- for each crux, a case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance
- if part of the fight turns out to be merely verbal -- e.g. they're using "disease" for two different jobs -- say which part and why, and keep the rest as a genuine disagreement

Please use the cases we made up in class for the crux predictions, they're way more interesting than the usual "homosexuality isn't a diagnosis anymore" example:
1. Valley: an isolated population where everyone carries a variant that makes everyone's joints fail around 35 and nobody lives past about 50. nobody there has ever known anything different, they don't experience it as illness, and the community rejects the label. is it a disease, and if so disease relative to what?
2. Milk: a majority of the world's adults can't digest milk. in some places that's a diagnosis, in others it's just how people are. disease or not, and in which population?
3. Hair: hair loss is unremarkable in a 70-year-old man and people pay to fix it in a 25-year-old. does the naturalist's reference class (species? sex? age group?) come from biology or from us?
4. Ramps: two people with identical biology, one lives somewhere everything is built to accommodate them, the other doesn't. same disease status or not?
5. Booster: a healthy person takes a memory drug that lifts them above normal for a year, stops, and goes back to exactly how they were before. do they now have a disease, since they're below the level they'd gotten used to, or not?
