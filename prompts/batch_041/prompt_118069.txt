I'm a novelist, and my next book turns on a sorites series: a man's condition worsens in increments too small for anyone to notice, an insurance policy pays only if he has become totally unable to work, and every character is certain about the two ends of the series and about nothing in between. I want my five principals — a trial judge, two appellate judges, a philosopher retained as an expert witness, and the plaintiff, who has a theory of her own — to hold five distinct and defensible positions on what vagueness is, and I don't want any two of them to be the same view wearing different words.

Build me a decision tree over the positions on vagueness. Every internal node must be a question about a concrete case — something a character could answer from the witness stand without knowing any of the labels — never a question about which theory they favor. The leaves should include at least epistemicism; supervaluationism; the paraconsistent option on which a borderline sentence is both true and false; degree theory; contextualism; the view that the world itself is vague; and the nihilist view that no vague predicate ever applies. The tree should surface, along the way, whether there is a fact of the matter about the last item in the series; whether a borderline sentence has a truth value, and which one; whether the boundary can itself have a boundary; and whether the vagueness lives in the world, in the language, in the speaker's context, or in us. One leaf should be a position the logical space allows but that no philosopher defends: label it unnamed and give its most awkward commitment. Where the tree cannot separate two positions, say so plainly instead of adding a node that pretends to; and for each combination of answers that no position occupies, say whether it is empty because it is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because nobody has explored it. Keep it to something a reader can hold in their head: no more than twelve internal nodes.

Give it to me as a JSON object with these fields: "nodes", an array of objects with "id", "question" (the concrete case, in plain language), and "answers", an array of objects with "answer" and "leads_to" (the id of the next node or leaf); "leaves", an array of objects with "id", "position" (the standard name), "core_commitment" (one sentence), "hardest_case" (the case the view handles worst), and "which_character" (which of my five could hold it, and why, in one sentence); "unoccupied_combinations", an array of objects with "path" (the answers in order) and "diagnosis" (incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored, with a phrase of justification); and "indistinguishable_pairs", an array of objects with "pair" (two leaf ids) and "why_not_separable". I don't need sources or a reading list.
