A university tenure committee of five members decides whether to recommend tenure. A candidate is recommended only if all three criteria are met: research, teaching, and service. The committee votes separately on each criterion and then on the final recommendation. Its past decisions are as follows. Candidate 1: all five members accepted all three criteria, and all five voted to recommend; the committee recommended. Candidate 2: all five rejected all three criteria, and all five voted against recommending; the committee did not recommend. Candidate 3: four accepted research, four accepted teaching, but only two accepted service; two voted to recommend; the committee did not recommend. Candidate 4: four accepted research, two accepted teaching, four accepted service; two voted to recommend; the committee did not recommend. The committee has never recommended a candidate without a unanimous vote on the recommendation. The new candidate has four members accepting each of research, teaching, and service, but only two voting to recommend. Predict the committee's decision, identify the aggregation rule it appears to be running, give the other plausible rules that fit its past decisions equally well, say what each predicts for the new case, and name the further case that would distinguish the remaining rules.
