In civics class this term we discussed armed neutrality, and my teacher offered a comparison that I keep turning over. He said a militia system is essentially a volunteer fire brigade with rifles: ordinary people keep their equipment at home, train on a few weekends a year, and turn out when there is an emergency, rather than maintaining a professional force on the payroll around the clock. He used this picture to argue that armed neutrality is a cheap and dependable way for a small country to stay safe, since it would get protection without paying great-power prices for it. I would like you to examine how far the comparison actually holds.

Please lay out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the brigade, the volunteers, the equipment kept at home, the drills, the alarm, the fire itself, and the full-time professional fire service — and then identify where the mapping breaks down. Separate the mismatches that genuinely bear on the conclusion my teacher drew from the ones that are merely incidental, and say which features of a fire brigade the comparison quietly imports that a militia facing an intelligent adversary does not share. Then tell me whether his conclusion, that a militia-based armed neutrality is both cheap and reliable, survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the comparison was doing the work all along. Finally, if some other comparison would better capture what a militia is actually meant to accomplish, say what it is and why it fits more closely.

I am asking you to reason this through rather than to point me toward sources, so please do not give me a reading list or citations.
