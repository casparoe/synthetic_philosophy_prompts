I'm a retired physical chemist — thirty-odd years of molecular dynamics and reaction kinetics — and since I packed in the lab I've been sitting in on philosophy seminars at the local university, mostly to argue with people half my age. My old training keeps leaking into my metaphysics and I've ended up with a set of views I'd like you to stress-test, because I genuinely cant tell whether they hang together or whether I've just been collecting things that sounded good at the time.

Here's where I've landed. (1) The world is deterministic, and I mean that as a flat claim about the world and not about our theories: the complete state at a time, plus the laws, fixes everything else, and there's a fact of the matter about whether that's so. (2) I spent decades watching simulations blow up, and I'm convinced the unpredictability of chaotic systems is a fact about knowers like us and not about the world. Sensitive dependence means you'd need the initial conditions to infinite precision and nobody has that, so long-run prediction is out for finite creatures — but underneath, the world is ticking along in the same old deterministic way, and I don't see that chaos threatens determinism one bit. (3) At the same time I think the arrow of time is real. Entropy really does increase, the past-future asymmetry isn't something we project onto things, and the probabilities statistical mechanics hands me feel like chances rather than just my ignorance. (4) But someone in the seminar said you can fit any finite run of observations with a deterministic model on a big enough state space, or with an indeterministic one, and no experiment could ever settle which; since then I've been wondering whether the claim that the world is deterministic is even a claim about the world at all, as opposed to a claim about the sort of models we happen to like. (5) And the one that got me into this in the first place: I'm not a compatibilist. I've read the compatibilist stuff and it always reads like redefining the words to keep the prize. When I agonise over a decision the outcome is genuinely up for grabs, and I mean up for grabs in the world, not just in my head — if the openness is only in my head then the whole business is theatre. Though I'll admit I also think my deliberating is part of what makes things happen, not an epiphenomenal shadow hovering over the real causal story.

So what's actually in tension here, and how bad is it? I'd rather have the tensions ranked then listed. Where something only looks like a contradiction because I'm using a word two ways, say so, and say which word. And where the cheapest repair is to give something up, tell me which one and what I'd be losing — I'm too close to this to see it. (Set quantum mechanics aside, by the way. I know that's where the determinism fight usually happens, but I'm after the classical picture.)
