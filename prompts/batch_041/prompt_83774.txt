In the variant of the Sleeping Beauty experiment described below, a fair coin is tossed on Sunday. If it lands Heads, Beauty is awakened exactly once, on Monday. If it lands Tails, a second fair coin is tossed, and she is awakened either twice, on Monday and Tuesday, or four times, from Monday through Thursday, according to the outcome of that second toss. After every awakening she is drugged so as to forget it, and she is told the whole protocol in advance. At each awakening, then, her evidence is exactly the same: she is awake, she knows the setup, and she can neither tell what day it is nor how many awakenings she has already had.

I am not asking for the standard Sleeping Beauty verdict, and I do not want the halfer and thirder positions merely reported back. I want your credence that the correct answer in this variant — Beauty's rational credence, at any awakening, that the first coin landed Heads — is 1/2, where correct means the credence a perfectly rational agent with exactly her evidence ought to have, assuming there is a fact of the matter. Give me a number, then decompose it into the sub-questions it rests on, with a rough probability and a line of justification for each. At a minimum: (i) that the question has a determinate answer at all, rather than being an artefact of asking for a credence at an underdescribed moment; (ii) that the correct treatment of self-locating uncertainty here is the thirder's sampling picture rather than the halfer's indifference principle; (iii) that, given that picture, the counting rule extends to a chancy number of awakenings by expected counts — one awakening in the Heads branch against an expected three in the Tails branch, hence 1/4 — rather than by dividing each branch's probability among its awakenings, which gives 1/2; and (iv) that the halfer's principle is untouched by the chanciness itself, that is, that a chancy count gives the halfer no reason to move off 1/2. Then combine the terms, tell me which one your final number is most sensitive to, and say what would move it most — in particular, whether a calibration or Dutch-book argument of the kind used on both sides of the standard debate settles the extension question in (iii), or merely relocates the disagreement.

My own credence that 1/2 is correct here is about 0.65, and a colleague's is about 0.1. Tell me which of the sub-questions we are most likely disagreeing about, and whether there is a way to settle it that does not amount to re-running the standard halfer/thirder standoff.
