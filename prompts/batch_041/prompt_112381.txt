Write an essay on whether the notion of welfare that welfare economics and policy evaluation actually need can be defined by people's preferences as revealed in their choices. Take as the position to be assessed the following package: a person's good consists in getting what they want; what they want is whatever their choices dispose them toward; and a policy therefore improves a person's situation exactly when it moves them to an option set they would choose over the alternative. Weigh the case for this package against the case against it, and end with a defended verdict rather than a survey of the positions on offer.

Organise the essay around two things I am stipulating rather than asking you to look up. First, distinguish welfare as preference-realization — the person's good is the satisfaction of the preferences their choices reveal — from welfare as reflective endorsement — the person's good is the satisfaction of the preferences they would endorse on reflection, with adequate information about the options and undistorted by how those options are described or by which elicitation procedure is used. Second, take as a test case a person who knowingly keeps a preference that damages them, and who, asked about the preference itself, says they would not choose to have it removed. Say what each conception of welfare implies about that person's situation, and whether the two conceptions can actually be told apart in practice.

In the course of the argument, address at least the following. First, whether reflective endorsement has any content that is not itself a revealed preference, or whether it either collapses back into preference-realization or else imports a standard of distortion that is not preference-based at all. Second, which of the familiar objections to choice-based welfare — adaptive preferences, the sensitivity of stated preference to framing and to elicitation order, and the fact that choices reveal at most an ordinal ranking so that no cardinal welfare is available for aggregation or interpersonal comparison — are objections to preference-realization specifically, and which apply equally to reflective endorsement. Third, whether the problem of which self counts, given that a person's preferences change over time, can be answered inside either framework. Fourth, whether the autonomy-based defence of choice-based welfare — that a person's own choices are authoritative just because they are theirs — can be extended to reflective endorsement without becoming the paternalism it was meant to avoid.

Argue on the merits; I am not asking for a literature review or for citations. Conclude with the notion of welfare you think policy evaluation should use, and if your answer is a hybrid, state exactly which part of the work each component does and what the hybrid costs.
