I am a novelist, and I run a small monthly workshop for other writers — twelve people, ninety minutes, in a room with no screens or internet. I would like you to design a runnable classroom activity that makes them feel the force of the dogmatism paradox: the thought that if you genuinely know something, then any evidence that appears to count against it must be misleading, so you are entitled to keep the belief and distrust the evidence. Build the exercise so that the rules, roles, and materials do the work — the participants should hold a belief on a guarantee they cannot themselves verify, encounter evidence that ought to unsettle it, and find that the situation's structure licenses them to dismiss that evidence and keep believing; the discomfort should arrive in what happens, before the paradox is ever named. Specify the rules, the materials, the timeline, and the debrief that maps what happened onto the paradox, then tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and be frank about which feature of the paradox the activity cannot reproduce and that I must supply myself. Finally, say how confident you are in the design's fidelity to the paradox, and single out the step you are least sure of.
