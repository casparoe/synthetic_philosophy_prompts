I keep running into a move in arguments that I don't have a word for, and it's bugging me. Someone wants to defend a basic rule of inference -- take the one that gets you from "if P then Q" and "P" to "Q" -- and the defense they give is itself an argument that uses that rule at some step. Or they defend induction by pointing out that induction has served us well so far, or defend the law of non-contradiction with an argument that already leans on it. When you say that's circular, they push back: no, it's not the ordinary kind, because the premises aren't secretly assuming the conclusion, it's the rule of inference that's being used rather than established, and you can't state any rule-governed argument at all without using some rules somewhere. So is there an established name for this exact thing? I know it's in the neighborhood of the tortoise thing and of begging the question, but neither of those seems to be quite it. If there is a term, tell me what it actually picks out and where the edges are -- how it differs from plain circular reasoning and from a premise that just restates the conclusion, and whether anyone thinks it's a genuine defect or just an unavoidable fact about justifying rules. If there's no term, coin one, define it, and tell me what it includes and leaves out. I don't just want the label, I want the carving.
