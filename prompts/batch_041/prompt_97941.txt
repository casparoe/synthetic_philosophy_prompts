So my mate Priya is retiring after thirty-one years running the Lantern, the worker-owned bakery co-op down the high street, and I've been asked to do the speech at her do. I'm a novelist, which is precisely why she asked me and not one of the economists — she wants the story of the place, not a relitigation of the old Mises-and-Hayek-versus-Lange thing about whether a planned outfit can allocate anything without market prices. There'll be about seventy people: the current bakers, her brother (teaches economics, loud), her mum, and a load of neighbours who just want to eat the cake.

Here's the awkward part. The Lantern has always run on a little internal price board — every job from the 4am bake to the VAT return posted with a points value, people bidding for the shifts. Two weeks ago the membership voted, narrowly, to scrap it and move to a rota drawn up by a committee of three. Priya abstained. So this speech is happening in the middle of the exact argument it's about, and the room is split right down that line.

Write me the speech. Six or seven minutes. It has to be warm and it has to be true, which means it can't say the co-op proved Hayek wrong and it can't say it proved Lange right, and it can't smuggle either one in sideways. Priya left me a note (I recieved it with the invitation) asking me not to turn the thing into a seminar, and not to pretend the price board was sacred or that it definately failed. What I want is a speech where the real difficulty is doing the structural work — that the people actually doing the work know things no committee can collect, and the committee still has to decide — shaping what I'm allowed to say about her and what I have to leave out, without anyone in the room hearing the word 'calculation' or getting a lecture about information.

Then, after the speech, give me a JSON object with these fields: "speech" (the full text); "central_tension" (one sentence, plain words, naming the problem the whole thing is built on); "things_left_unsaid" (array of objects, each with "claim" and "why_it_stays_out" — the assertions the speech deliberately never makes); "audience_landings" (array of objects with "moment", "lands_for", "risks_losing"); "could_be_heard_as_taking_sides" (array of the lines most likely to be read as picking a winner, and what to do about each); and "cut_first_if_it_runs_long" (one line).
