i'm putting together a little zine of poems about epistemology and i want one about the bootstrapping problem / easy knowledge. quick version of the idea so you're working from the same thing i am: you have a perceptual faculty you've got no independent reason to trust, but you can still use its own deliverances to run a little inference -- 'the table is red; if its red its not white with red lights on it; so its not white with red lights' -- then do that again for the next belief and the next, until you've 'earned' the conclusion that your color vision is reliable, without ever bringing in anything from outside the faculty. every step looks licensed and the whole thing looks like theft. thats the thing i want the poem to make you feel.

form: a sestina. let the six end-words be the recurring tokens of that loop -- something like red, table, looks, seems, know, true, your call -- so the poem literally cant reach outside its own six words, the way the bootstrap cant reach outside the faculty. each stanza should license the next the way each inferential step does, and by the envoi the poem should be concluding something it hasn't paid for, landing on the too-easy 'so my eyes are reliable' without breaking stride or winking at the reader. a reader who knows the debate should recognize the structure in the form, not just get the label named at them.
