Some half-formed things I keep circling that you should engage with, and tell me if they're confused:

- Failed and near-miss revolutions. Some norms get argued against for a century and don't budge, then collapse in twenty years (dueling, footbinding). Others never move at all. Is the interesting variable the norm itself, the elite that carries it, or the surrounding crisis?
- Counter-revolutions and norm hardening. Anti-sodomy law spread across a lot of the world's legal codes in the 19th century through imperial administration, not argument. Do we have a theory of moral change that even handles the bad direction, or is everything written assuming the change is the good kind?
- The de jure / de facto gap. People can stop doing a thing without thinking it wrong, or think it wrong and keep doing it. Most of the literature seems to count the behavior change as the revolution. Should whether the norm itself actually changed be its own research question?
- Silent collapses. Dueling seems to die off largely after the mass slaughter of the first world war — no big argument, everyone just stops. How much of moral change is argument and how much is that?
- The honor/dignity/victimhood trichotomy that circulates in comment sections. Does it carve at joints, is it a candidate explanation of the mechanisms, or is it a distraction?
