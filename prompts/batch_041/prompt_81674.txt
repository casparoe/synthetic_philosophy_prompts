Me and my friend argued for like three hours last night about the frame problem and I still cant let it go. He's the philosophy of mind guy, I'm the one who actually did the AI degree, so we talked past each other some, but here's his arguement cleaned up the way he'd want it:

(1) To act on purpose, an agent has to get right which facts its action changes and which it leaves alone.
(2) Which facts are left alone is not a fact about the world, it's a fact about what the agent is up to. Nothing in the kitchen says the knife's sharpness is irrelevant to making tea, and nothing in my head has a little tag on the cat-fact marking it as not worth bothering with.
(3) So the agent needs something extra on top of its beliefs about the world: a filter that tells it what to bother with here.
(4) That filter has to work in situations nobody rehearsed, otherwise it's just a lookup table and the agent is only as smart as whoever wrote the table.
(5) A filter that works in unrehearsed situations has to work out, in the situation, what matters, which is the very job we hired the filter for.
(6) So you get either a lookup table, which isn't general, or an endless regress of filters for filters. Either way no physical system does what I do when I make tea.

He says people mush two things together: predicting what your action doesn't change, and deciding what to think about at all. He grants the first one is engineerable, and says the whole trick of the frame problem is that the first secretly needs the second, so the second is the real problem and it's impossible. Conclusion: no general intelligence, ever. Which proves way too much, obviously, I make tea every morning and I'm not magic. And before you say the fix is to just let the world do the filtering, he's ready for that one too, he says it only moves the lookup table into the body's design, and evolution wrote that table.

But I genuinely dont know where to put the knife in. The premise I most want to keep is (2), I think he's right that relevance isn't sitting out there in the world waiting to be percieved, and I don't want to give that up, because then you get the silly view where salience is just a property of scenes. (5) also looks airtight to me. It's (2) and (5) together that do the damage.
