My friend Maya has really strong opinions about when someone counts as weak-willed, and over the years I've kept a rough tally of her rulings. I want to know what she'd say about a new case, and honestly I mostly want to figure out what rule she's been running this whole time.

Her "yeah, that's weakness of will" calls: our friend Ben, at a poker table, thinking "I should just get up and walk away, this is idiotic," still thinking it as he shoves his chips in, and shoving them in anyway; a girl at 1am typing a text to her ex while thinking "I am going to regret this so much," and sending it; someone halfway through a second slice of cake, thinking "I literally said I'd stop," and finishing the slice.

Her "no, that's not weakness" calls: a guy who's decided, calmly, that smoking is worth the risk to him, and smokes — she says that's not weak, there's no conflict, he's doing what he's decided is best; someone who sleep-eats in the middle of the night, basically out of it, no thought about it either way; and someone held at gunpoint handing over his wallet, which she says isn't his will being weak, it's not even up to him.

Now the new one. Our friend Dan, the night before, sincerely thinks the best thing is to get up at 6 and go for a run. No conflict about it, he's definately decided. He sets three alarms and leaves his shoes by the bed. Then at 6, groggy, he sincerely thinks the best thing *right now* is to sleep in. And I mean sincerely — it's not that he thinks he should get up and caves, his preference has actually flipped, the way people's preferences flip about stuff that's far off versus stuff that's right in front of them (I think economists call it hyperbolic discounting, or a preference reversal). So he sleeps in. No guilt, no feeling of struggle, he just wants sleep more now and thinks it's the right call.

So does Maya call Dan weak-willed? What's the rule she's really been running, and are there other rules that fit all six earlier rulings just as well? For each rule, what does it say about Dan, and what kind of case would split them apart? Which rule do you actually think she's running, and why? Also, does it matter that Dan set the alarms the night before — is that him being strong-willed and then getting undone, or is it just him trying to tie up a future self who's kind of a different guy?
