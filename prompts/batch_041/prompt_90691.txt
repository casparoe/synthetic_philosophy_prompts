I spent most of last night arguing with a mate about the colour phi thing — you know the demo where you flash a red dot, then a green dot a couple of degrees to the side about a tenth of a second later, and you'd swear you saw a single dot travel across the gap and turn green roughly halfway along. His line, which he's lifted more or less wholesale from Dennett's multiple drafts business, is that the question "when exactly did the green get into your experience?" has no fact of the matter. You can tell the story the Orwellian way — you really did see a red dot and then a green one, and your memory of the first one got rewritten after the fact, 1984-style — or the Stalinesque way, where the colour change gets reconciled and inserted into the experience itself before it's ever "published", show-trial style, with the verdict fixed in advance. On his account no experiment can seperate them, because every experiment bottoms out in a report and a report is downstream of both stories equally; and anyway the Orwellian version is just wasteful, since why would the brain go to the trouble of falsifying a memory when it could falsify the experience in the first place? I found myself agreeing with him far more than I wanted to, and I honestly can't tell whether that's because the arguement is good or because it's a cop-out dressed up as profundity.

So here's what I want from you. Take the role of someone who actually works on the timing of experience — philosopher of mind with one foot in vision science — and give me an honest map of what would count against the no-fact-of-the-matter line. Think carefully before you answer, and please start by brainstorming the space of possible objections before you commit to a verdict. First I want the two claims prised apart, because I suspect they're doing quite different work: the modest one (no experiment can distinguish Orwellian from Stalinesque) and the ambitious one (there is no fact about when the content entered experience, full stop). For each, which observations, arguments, intuitions or formal results would tell against it, and how decisive would each be? Rank them from fatal down to merely awkward, and be clear about which ones a committed defender would themselves concede as counting against them rather than dismissing as question-begging. I'd especially like to know what would have to be true for the distinction to turn out empirically tractable after all — presumably you'd need a criterion for "this representation is now being experienced" that is sharp in time and not itself defined in terms of what the subject reports, plus evidence that it delivers a verdict in the colour phi case. Do the no-report paradigms, decoding work, masking and postdiction findings, or fast reaction-time measures actually bite here, or does the deflationist have a ready parry for each of them? And is the "the brain wouldn't bother" point a real argument or just a plausibility nudge — does it even cut one way, given that editing a memory sounds cheaper than editing a percept?

The bit I actually care about is the honesty of the verdict. Tell me whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if it has, whether that's a genuine problem for a view of this kind or just the price of doing business with consciousness. If you think there's a third option that isn't a timing story at all — say, that the subject misdescribes the content rather than mistiming it, so that nothing was ever "edited" in either sense — I want that on the table too, with a note on what it costs and who'd have to give up what. And if the whole thing generalises to the Libet-style debates about when a decision is made, or to arguments about perceptual latency generally, say so, because that's the part that made me sit up. What I'd personally find most persuasive is something that makes the two stories come apart in what they predict about the same subject at different times, or that uses something other than a verbal report — but tell me straight if I'm asking for something that couldn't exist, and if the answer is that the position is well insulated, say that too, and say whether it matters.
