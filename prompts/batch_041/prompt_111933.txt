Something about the DSM has been bugging me and I cant tell if it's a real inconsistency or just me. So the manual says a mental disorder has to cause clinically significant distress or impairment — okay. And major depression gets diagnosed after two weeks of low mood and the rest — okay, I guess. But then in the newer text revision they add prolonged grief disorder, and for that one you apparently have to wait a whole year before it counts; the international manual, the ICD, only makes you wait six months for what sounds like the same thing. Meanwhile if that same person had those exact symptoms with no death in the background, they'd be diagnosable at two weeks. So identical symptoms, and whether you count as disordered depends on how long ago somebody died and which book your doctor happened to open.

Here's what I actually want: what does this practice presuppose about what a psychiatric disorder is? Not the usual it's-a-social-construct-versus-it's-a-brain-disease ping-pong. I want you to reconstruct the implicit theory the manual is running on — disorders as natural kinds we discover, or as practical cutoffs we draw, or something messier in between — and then tell me which rival theories that practice quietly rules out. And is it even internally consistent, given it also insists on clinically significant distress when the whole point of the grief category is that the distress is, well, grief?

Second thing, which might be the real question: Ive read that these categories loop back and change the people who get them, so the diagnosis reshapes how you experience and report the very thing it's diagnosing. If that's right, does a criteria list written as if it's describing a pre-existing kind presuppose something the looping effect undercuts? Or can you be a realist about these kinds and still take looping seriously? And what would the practice have to look like if the people writing it took looping seriously — is there any way to write criteria that don't get eaten by their own effects?

If I've got the durations wrong, or the whole framing is confused, just say so instead of humoring me.
