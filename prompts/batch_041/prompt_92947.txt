Write an essay on the principle of identity of indiscernibles, organized around the following case: a universe containing exactly two objects and nothing else at all — no space, no time, and no properties other than a single symmetric, irreflexive relation that the two objects bear to each other and to nothing besides. Is such a universe possible? If it is, does the principle of identity of indiscernibles hold in it, or are the two objects distinguishable after all? Weigh the strongest defenses available to the principle — appeals to relational or impure properties, to primitive thisness, to the claim that the case is not genuinely conceivable — against the objection that each of these either assumes what it is meant to prove or makes the principle true only by inflating what counts as a discernible property. Then ask whether your verdict survives a change of subject matter: replace the two objects with two moments in the history of a single unchanging thing, and consider whether the same reasoning shows those moments to be distinct. Reach a definite conclusion and defend it, rather than ending with the options left open.
