A friend and I have been arguing for months about whether properties are real, and neither of us has budged. He holds that the world contains only particular things and the ways they happen to resemble one another — that 'red' is just a word we apply, and that asking what makes two red things both red is a request for an explanation that never has to be cashed out in terms of anything extra. I hold that some things are true of many particulars at once, and that this calls for an explanation grounded in something real. When I object that 'resembles' is itself a relation shared by many pairs, so he has not escaped the pattern he denies, he says that too is only a word; when he asks where my universal is located, I say that question confuses being real with being somewhere. My working hypothesis is that we are not really disagreeing about what there is, but about what an explanation of sameness is supposed to accomplish, and perhaps about what 'exists' is being used to mean — or else that we are answering two different questions that share a label. Give me a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two questions in one, and what — a distinction, an argument, a case — would count as genuine progress, if anything? And tell me what would change your mind about the diagnosis, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
