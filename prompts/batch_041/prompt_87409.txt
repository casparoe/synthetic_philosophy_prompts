My friend teaches political theory and has spent about a decade on one question: can delivering prosperity and order make an unelected government legitimate, or is that just a story authoritarians tell about themselves. I've sat through enough of her arguments that I can usually call her verdict before she gives it, but the case that just came up has me genuinely unsure and I'd rather not say something dumb in front of her. So: her record, as best I can reconstruct it, then the new case.

Singapore since independence: legitimate, and she defends this one hardest. The PAP has never lost, the press is tame, but the housing, the health system, the safety, and a swamp turning into a rich country inside one generation — for her that's a government doing the thing governments exist to do.

Hong Kong under the British: legitimate, or at least not illegitimate, which she insists is a real category. No meaningful elections, but rule of law, a loud press, and you could leave.

Botswana from independence on: legitimate. Diamonds, yes, but they didn't waste them, and there were real elections the whole way through.

Pinochet's Chile: not legitimate, no hedging. She'll grant the growth after the mid-70s, then say: the disappeared, the torture, and a boom that turned into a bust in the early 80s and left half the country behind.

Franco's Spain from the late 50s on: not legitimate, even though by the end it was a fairly ordinary Western European country. Her line is that a regime doesn't get to launder its first twenty years with its last fifteen.

China since the reforms: not legitimate, and she's visibly uncomfortable saying it, because the prosperity is real and enormous. Her line is that you can't seperate the boom from a party that shot people in 1989 and detains people en masse in Xinjiang now, and she'd rather admit the doctrine is false than call that legitimate.

Now the case. One of the smaller Gulf monarchies — I won't name it because I'd get the details wrong, but the type: a ruling family, an enormous sovereign wealth fund, citizens who mostly work for the state, and guest workers who outnumber the citizens several times over and can never naturalise. No elected chamber with any power whatsoever. Not a police state, as far as I know — no dissidents in prison, no massacres, no terror, and the citizens aren't going anywhere because the deal is too good. Genuinely excellent hospitals, roads, universities, life expectancy right up with the best. But nothing is produced that isn't oil or gas or finance built on the oil and gas, the oil runs out in a few decades, and the money paying for all of it comes out of the ground and out of the guest workers, not out of anything the citizens do.

What does she say? I want her verdict, the rules that fit all her earlier calls (all of them, not just the one you like best), what each of those rules predicts about this case, your best guess with your reasons, and which single further case would actually tell the rules apart. And if her precedents genuinely don't settle it, say that too, and tell me which precedent is the closest analog on either reading.
