Hi — I'm a software engineer with no real philosophy background, and I run a lunchtime "big ideas" club at my company, usually 10-14 people, engineers and PMs, none of them have studied philosophy. One session, 50 minutes. I want to run an activity, not give a talk.

The idea I'm chasing is about dispositions — what it is for a thing to be fragile, or soluble, or magnetic. The obvious story is: it's fragile just because it would break if you dropped it. I read about two snags that seem to wreck that story: a "fink," where triggering the test would itself destroy the disposition (so it never breaks, but it was fragile the whole time), and a "mask," where something blocks the outcome while the disposition sits there untouched (again, never breaks, still fragile). Plus the reverse: a thing that would break if you dropped it but isn't fragile at all — it just has a little bomb glued to it.

Can you design a runnable game or role-play where the group tests a set of "objects," builds up a theory of which ones are fragile from what they actually observe, and then the reveal shows their rule (never breaks = not fragile) was wrong in both directions — so they feel the pinch themselves before I ever say "fink" or "mask." I need: the setup and roles, the rules, a scoring scheme with a real stake (no money — points or bragging rights is fine), a timeline for the 50 minutes, and a debrief that ties each moment back to why you can't just define a disposition by what would happen if you triggered it. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (people gaming the rules, the object-players just announcing their cards, someone refusing to be tested, etc.) and how to patch it on the fly. And be straight with me about what the game can't reproduce — the part I'll just have to explain out loud.

Please start by brainstorming two or three different structures and pick the best, then give me the final version with everything above. Pretend your the professor who's watched a hundred of these flop.
