I've been going back and forth on the memory-and-forgetting thing for a while now — read a bit of Margalit, a bit of Rieff, and honestly cant tell whether they're disagreeing about a real question or just talking past each other — and the annoying part is that my own reactions to cases won't line up into anything. Here's what I actually think, going by gut. A woman whose brother was murdered decides she's done with the annual memorial, stops going, asks the family to stop forwarding her the emails: entirely hers to decide, and nobody gets to call it a betrayal. A city that turns an old prison into a museum and keeps it running at a loss: not just permissible, I think it owes this to the people who were held there. A town that takes down a monument to a slaveholding founder: fine, and I don't feel the removal itself as a loss. A town that quietly lets the site of a massacre get paved over with nothing to mark it, because "we've moved on": that one bothers me, and not only because I dislike the sort of people who say it. A truth commission that trades amnesty for testimony: I think that's a real bargain, maybe even the right one, and I don't think the victims' descendants are owed a prosecution instead. A court that seals the massacre records for fifty years to keep the peace: that I think is a betrayal, even though it's buying roughly the same peace. And on a much smaller scale — a friend who keeps bringing up a stupid thing I did years ago, saying he's "just remembering": I think he's wronging me, and I don't think I owe him a hearing.

So what I seem to want is something like: individual people can be released from remembering, and may even be entitled to be, while institutions that stand in some relation to the victims can't be — except the amnesty case and the sealed-records case cut the same way and I want them to come out differently, and I can't say why without gerrymandering. Is there a principle, or a small set of them, that gets all of this or close? Tell me precisely which of my verdicts it forces me to give up and why, and what it predicts for a case I haven't listed: a grandmother who is the last living witness to something terrible, and who has decided she wants to die without telling anyone in the family. And please keep the line between settled and contested clear — I don't want a contested view about whether there's any duty to remember at all handed to me as if it were what everyone who works on this agrees on.
