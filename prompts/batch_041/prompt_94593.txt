Ok so I've been rereading the Ant Fugue dialogue (the one where the colony is Aunt Hillary) and Dennett's riffs on it, and I want to write down where I've landed and have someone check whether the whole thing hangs together, because I keep feeling there's a clash in here somewhere and I can't pin it down.

Roughly what I think:

1. Functionalism, basicly: a belief is a belief in virtue of its functional role in the system, not the stuff it's made of. Carbon, silicon, doesn't matter, it's the organization.
2. Extended/distributed cognition: some cognitive states live in the world rather than in a head. Otto's notebook, a navigator's charts, and the case I actually care about — a pheromone trail. When foragers keep following a trail to a feeder, the colony's "memory" of where the food is isn't inside any ant, it's in the trail. I think that's a genuine memory belonging to the colony, not a cute metaphor.
3. So the colony really does represent and really does decide. As I understand the Temnothorax nest-site work, scouts each visit candidate sites and recruit others, and once a site has enough ants in it — a quorum — the colony switches from recruiting to carrying everyone over. I read that as the colony weighing its options and commited to one. The quorum isn't "as if" certainty, it *is* the colony being sure.
4. But there's nothing it is like to be the colony. Nobody's home in there.
5. And no extra ingredient. I'm not a dualist of any stripe; I don't think consciousness is something you sprinkle on top of the functional story, some inner glow that God has to add. Get the organization right and experience is just what that organization is, from the inside.
6. Individual ants, though, don't have beliefs and desires. An ant is closer to a sensor and an actuator with legs. The intentional vocabulary belongs one level up.
7. Same for corporations, markets, nations: not minds. Talk about what "the market" expects is a façon de parler at best. There's no extra subject there, just the people.
8. And nothing it's like to be a thermostat, obviously. Not a borderline case.

Three things are bugging me. First, 6 and 3: I'm saying mindless parts somehow add up to a mind, which is the exact move I'd mock in a dualist. Either ants have some mental life or the colony can't have one, surely? Second, 4 and 8: if there's nothing it's like to be the colony and nothing it's like to be the thermostat, what actually separates them? Am I just being a snob about size and neuron count, letting the colony be a mind because it reminds me of a brain? Third, 3 and 7: the colony gets to be a mind and the market doesn't, but the market's memory of scarcity sits in prices the same way the colony's memory of food sits in a trail, and prices get read and acted on. So why is one a mind and the other just a metaphor?

What I want: tell me which of these worries are real and which are me spooking myself, rank the real problems by severity, and for each one give me the cheapest fix and what it costs — not "you could modify this" with no price tag. If there's a clash in the list I haven't even noticed, that's the one I most want to hear about. And if a single change would clear up the most, say which.
