ok so I've been hanging around a few AI safety forums and there's this pattern I keep seeing and I cant find a name for it. Someone proposes that the safe thing for an AI is to be low-impact: do your job and don't touch anything else. Then someone pushes back that "your job" already means touching things. If I ask a robot to tidy the room, moving the chair is the job. If I ask it to leave the chair alone, moving the chair is a side effect. Same motion, same chair, opposite verdict, and the only thing that changed is how I worded the request. And there's a weirder version of the same thing: if you reward the system for having little impact, the "safest" move is to do nothing at all — but doing nothing while a fire spreads is also an impact, it just doesn't show up in whatever number you're measuring. So the measurement and the task description both seem to be quietly deciding what counts, before the AI does anything.

Then a third person in the thread says: right, so "impact" and "side effect" can't be pinned down without first deciding what the system is supposed to value, and that decision is exactly the thing nobody knows how to specify. Everybody agrees and the thread moves on, but nobody ever names the actual bind.

Is there an established term for this? And I definately don't just want a label slapped on it, I want the carving. I've seen people wave at the frame problem, or at Goodhart, and neither feels like quite it. Is this really just the general point that "harm" or "change" is always measured against a counterfactual baseline, so that the choice of baseline is doing all the work? Or is it its own thing that lives specifically in the impact-measure setting? If there's no standard name, coin one, and tell me whats inside it and whats outside it.
