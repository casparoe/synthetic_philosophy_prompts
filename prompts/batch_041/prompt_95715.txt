im making a handout for a reading group on whether the world is actually getting less violent, and i want to get past the usual back-and-forth. Here are six candidate reasons the recorded decline might be at least partly an artefact of how we count: (a) the state's monopoly on violence mostly relocates killing into prisons, policing and wars that get tallied under different headings; (b) the early 'baseline' homicide figures for most countries are educated guesses; (c) the threshold for what counts as a war death has been tightened over time, so older conflicts look bloodier than they really were; (d) the fall is real but mostly tracks fewer young men dying, i.e. it's a demographic story; (e) absolute death tolls have risen while rates fell, so 'less violent' depends on which of the two you pick; (f) a lot of historical violence was intra-household and never recorded, so the baseline is unknowable. Rank these by how much of the apparent decline each one genuinely explains, give a rough percentage for each (they needn't sum to 100), and say which rankings you'd actually defend to someone who's worked on this literature and which are just your best guess. Be explicit about which bits are genuinely contested among people who know the data and which are basically settled, and don't dress up a contested view as the consensus.
