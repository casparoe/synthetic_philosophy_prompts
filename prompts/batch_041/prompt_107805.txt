I'm in a graduate seminar on the historiography of the First World War, and we have spent the past three weeks on the dispute over who was responsible for the war's outbreak — the thesis associated with Fritz Fischer, that the German leadership bore the primary responsibility and pursued expansionist aims from the outset, set against the older view that responsibility was shared among the great powers and that the crisis of July 1914 is best explained by a system no single actor controlled. The arguments on both sides are sharper than I expected, but I have come to think that most of them turn on unstated criteria for what it even means to hold a nation responsible for a war, and I keep watching the two camps talk past each other because they have tacitly adopted different criteria. Before I read anything further, I want the criteria themselves laid out.

So write me the desiderata that any adequate theory of responsibility for a war has to satisfy. I am not asking for a verdict on 1914; I want the specification that a verdict would have to meet. At a minimum I want the cases the theory has to get right (a war that several powers chose into, one that almost nobody wanted and almost everybody helped cause, and the contrast with a war of unambiguous aggression); the distinctions it must not be permitted to collapse (causal contribution versus moral blame; responsibility for starting a war versus for prolonging it versus for how it was fought; the responsibility of leaders versus of the state versus of the population that supported them; moral versus legal versus historical responsibility; and the difference between guilt and answerability); and the constraints it has to live with (that the actual decisions were made by a handful of people; that many actors' choices were jointly necessary so that no one of them was sufficient; that a nation is not literally an agent; that later generations had no hand in the choice; and that we must not smuggle in knowledge the actors did not possess). For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it.

Then do the two things I most want. First, mark which of these are genuine desiderata and which are merely the commitments of one camp dressed up as requirements. Second, tell me whether some subset of them cannot be satisfied together — I suspect the demand that responsibility attach to actual moral agents collides with the demand that the theory capture what is true in saying that a country as such was responsible for the war, and if that is a real contradiction I want it stated as one rather than papered over. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out candidates. A short note on how the primary-responsibility and shared-responsibility pictures fare against the list is welcome at the end, but keep the weight on the criteria.
