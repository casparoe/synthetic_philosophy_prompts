I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums long enough to notice that the cost-effectiveness spreadsheet, the QALY, and the standard effective-altruism giving recommendation all quietly presuppose that one person's welfare can be weighed against another's, and that the presupposition is rarely defended and never specified. I want a piece that takes the practice seriously by taking it literally. Write a deadpan mock technical standard — scope, definitions, numbered clauses, an informative annex — governing the calibration and legal use of a unit of interpersonal welfare, in a world where such comparisons are not merely permitted but administratively required: courts, insurers, and regulators must compare one person's suffering against another's, and this is the document that says how. The comedy should come from getting the real problems right rather than inventing them: the zero-point problem and why a neutral life will not serve as the origin; the difference between ordinal and interval measurability and what it does and does not license; the aggregation difficulties that stand between individual rankings and a social ordering; the gap between willingness-to-pay and experienced utility; adaptation and projection bias; and the recurring temptation of money as the common currency. Include a clause treating the utility monster as an edge case the committee could not resolve, and an annex of disputed calibration cases, such as a chronic-pain patient weighed against a bereaved parent, and a mild pain of long duration against a severe pain of short duration. Around 700 words, no winking, and aim the satire at the practice rather than at any named person or living theorist; someone who works on the measurement of well-being should read it as an exaggeration of a real set of difficulties rather than a fabrication.

After the piece, add a short note outside the fiction separating what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and in particular do not present one side of the commensurability debate as the consensus.
