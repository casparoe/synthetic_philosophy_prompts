ok so I've been reading about the hole argument in GR and I think I basically get it, but the way people talk about it afterwards doesn't line up with what I take it to show, so I want to check my understanding before I go around saying wrong things at parties.

My version: take a spacetime with a region in it that's empty of matter (the "hole"). Apply a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside the hole but shuffles the metric around inside it. You get a second solution to the field equations that agrees with the first everywhere outside the hole and disagrees inside. GR can't tell the two apart. But the two models disagree about which spacetime point has which metric properties, so there's no fact of the matter about which point is which — which means the points can't be real things. So the hole argument refutes substantivalism and vindicates Leibniz: space is just relations between material things. And it shows GR is indeterministic in the strong sense — the same initial data can evolve into two genuinely different futures. That seems like a huge deal for a theory everyone calls deterministic.

So my questions: if the hole argument kills substantivalism, why do so many people still write as if spacetime points are real objects?
