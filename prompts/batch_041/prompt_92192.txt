My friend keeps saying it's just good design, nobody's targeting anyone, it's about keeping the seats clean and making sure more people can sit. But the whole point is obviously to stop homeless people from sleeping there, and the same city that paid for these benches also runs the big compassion-city ad campaign and just opened a new shelter, which is what set me off. What I actually want to figure out is what the bench presupposes about how objects and politics relate. Does designing a bench so it can't be slept on commit you to the strong claim that artifacts have politics -- that the object itself is doing political work no matter what anyone intended? Here's the part that's bugging me: a bench that's uncomfortable to sleep on is a different animal from a bench that's perfectly comfortable but sits on a plaza the cops clear every night. The first one seems to do the excluding all by itself; the second one needs people to do it. So does the first one presuppose that the object alone can carry the politics, and if so, which views of technology does that quietly rule out -- could someone who thinks technology is neutral even defend building these?
