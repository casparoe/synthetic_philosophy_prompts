Hi — I'm a novelist (two published books, both about people who are terrible with money) and I'm stuck on a story that I think is really about something in decision theory, only I don't have the vocabulary for it. I'd like you to help me write it. Put on two hats at once: someone who has genuinely thought hard about rational choice over time, and someone who can write a scene where nobody explains anything out loud. Think it through before you start, brainstorm two or three different ways to stage this, then pick the best one and write it — roughly 1800 to 2500 words.

Here's what's nagging me. There seem to be two completely different ways a person can be a sucker, and I keep seeing people collapse them into one.

The first: all at once, in a single sitting, her preferences run in a circle. Offer her the red one and she'll trade you her blue one for it; offer her the green and she'll trade the red; offer her the blue back and she'll trade the green — same afternoon, nothing changing except which pair you dangle. Keep it up and she ends holding exactly what she started with, minus something.

The second: at every single moment her preferences are perfectly tidy, no circles anywhere, but they *move* as the moment of decision gets closer. From a week out she wants the thing that takes a year; the day before, she wants the thing that takes an hour; and afterward she'd give anything to have waited. No single moment of hers looks irrational. There's alot of daylight between those two and I want a reader to *feel* it without anyone in the story naming it. That's the whole challenge.

Second thing I've noticed, and I think it's definately the engine: the con only works if something is missing. If she can see the whole sequence of trades coming, or if she can make a promise to her own future self that actually sticks, the whole thing falls apart. So please don't write a story where she's just weak-willed and dumb. The trap has to come from a specific gap — she can't foresee, or she can't bind herself, or she *can* bind herself but only at a price, and the price is where the story lives.

Third: don't make it money. Or if there's money, don't let money be the only thing being traded — money is what every version of this story uses and it lets the reader coast. Pick some other medium, and be careful about whether that medium's value is itself stable, because if the thing she's trading is something she revalues moment to moment, the whole structure gets much stranger and I want to see that.

And here's the turn I'm most interested in, if you can make it work: what if she decides, deliberately, to become exploitable? Not out of weakness — she does the arithmetic and cultivates the very pattern that lets someone take her apart, because going around the cycle pays her something each lap. Is that a rational thing to do? Is it irrational to *be* pumpable, or only to *get* pumped? I don't want the story to answer that. I want it to make the question hurt.

Then, after the story, a short note for me, maybe 200 words, no reading list and no citations please — I don't need names, I need to know what I'm allowed to say in interviews. Tell me which of the ideas I'm leaning on are actually settled among people who work on this and which are live fights, and don't smooth a fight over by presenting one side as the consensus. Also flag where the story takes liberties with the ideas, or leans on a reading that's genuinely contested, since I'd rather hear it from you now than from a reviewer later.
