I moderate a small online community — a few hundred members, mostly strangers who know one another only by handle — and I want help thinking through something that has been sitting with me for a few weeks. Among the unwritten services I used to provide was quietly tipping off newcomers when a particular person was drifting through communities like ours, someone with a long habit of borrowing money he never returns. After a stretch of drama about members policing one another, I decided to stop playing that role; we have a norm against callouts, and I told myself that warning people unasked was its own kind of overreach. A newcomer I liked, call him P, then lent this person a serious amount of money and lost it. I knew the borrower's history. I said nothing. I keep saying to myself that I let it happen, and a friend keeps telling me that you cannot cause something by doing nothing — that the person who took the money is the one who did it. I find that unsatisfying, but I can't articulate why, and I'd rather not settle for a slogan in either direction.

Here is what actually puzzles me. Had I warned P, I think he would have steered clear — probably, not certainly — and I notice that "probably" is doing a lot of work. Had I warned him and he'd lent the money anyway, I'd feel clean, as though I'd done my part; by staying silent I feel dirty, even though the outcome was identical. That seems backwards and I can't tell whether the backwardness is a bug in my feelings or a real feature of the situation. I also can't decide whether the thing that "caused" it was my silence, my decision to adopt a general policy of silence, or the norm itself — I didn't invent the norm, but I leaned on it. And there were at least three others who knew and said nothing; each of them could tell himself it wasn't his doing, and yet the warning that would have helped P was nobody's to give. It feels like the harm has no owner, which cannot be right.

I'd like you to help me think this through rather than console me. In particular: what is the most defensible way to decide which of these things — my silence, my policy, the norm, the borrower — count as the cause, and by what standard? Is the asymmetry I feel between a botched warning and no warning tracking anything real? And given all that, what should I do now, and should I abandon the policy? I've read enough blogs to know these questions have structure, but I want the structure applied to my case, not gestured at.
