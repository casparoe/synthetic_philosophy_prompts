I have been trying to settle, for myself, whether calling a condition a disease is a report of biological fact or an expression of what we happen to value, and I have noticed that my instinct here does suspiciously convenient work, so I want it pressure-tested. A friend who is sure disease is entirely factual put the following argument to me, and I could not find the loose thread even though the conclusion strikes me as too strong to be reached this cheaply. Here it is, laid out as he gave it:

(1) If "X is a disease" were a value judgment, then two people who agreed on every biological fact about X but differed in what they valued could disagree about whether X is a disease, and neither of them would be making a mistake.

(2) Take the clear case: a body that cannot regulate its own blood sugar, so that without daily intervention the person goes blind and dies young. Everyone, whatever their values, calls this a disease.

(3) That agreement is not owed to shared values. It is owed to the fact that the organs in question are failing to do the job they are supposed to do; the condition is a failure of a biological function, measured against how the body is built to work.

(4) And more generally, wherever there is a clear failure of a biological function everyone agrees it is a disease; wherever people do disagree — about very short stature, say, or about an inability to conceive without medical help — the disagreement is over facts we have not yet nailed down, not over values.

(5) So "X is a disease" is a purely factual statement, and the value-laden view is false.

(6) So medicine is a science like any other, and there is a fact of the matter, independent of anyone's preferences, about who is diseased and who is not.

I am drawn to this because I do believe there is a fact of the matter, and I would hate to surrender (6). But the step from (4) to (5) nags at me, and so does the confidence of (3). Tell me which premise or step I should reject, explain why it looked fine to me in the first place, and then price the alternatives — for each candidate I could give up, what would I lose, and can I keep any version of (6) after paying it? I would rather keep (6) than not, so I especially want to know what it costs to keep it. Please keep the whole answer under 600 words.
