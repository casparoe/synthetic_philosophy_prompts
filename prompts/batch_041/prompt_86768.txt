I'm a novelist and I keep running into a thing I dont have a word for, and I want the word — or the carving, if there isn't one — for a character I'm writing.

The pattern: two people can agree completely on what's good and bad, same values, same trade-offs, neither caring more about money than the other, and still make opposite calls whenever the outcome is uncertain. My sister won't take a straight coin flip for a raise-or-nothing on a modest salary, even when the flip is better in plain expected dollars and the sums are small enough that diminishing returns can't be doing much of the work. My brother takes it without blinking, then buys a lottery ticket every week. When I asked my sister why, she said a one-in-a-hundred chance of losing the house "feels way bigger than one in a hundred" — not that she thinks it's more likely, just that it weighs more when she's deciding.

And here's the part that made me think something specific is going on: it's not a general fog. A small chance of a mildly bad outcome doesn't get inflated for her at all — she shrugs at those. It's specifically when the bad thing is the worst item on the table that the tiny chance suddenly feels huge. Same probability, same person, different reaction, and the only thing that changed was whether that outcome sat at the bottom of the list.

Then there's a second thing, and I genuinely can't tell if it's the same phenomenon or a cousin. All three of us get much more cautious when we don't even know the odds. Hand me a gamble with a stated 20% chance of a bad outcome and I'll take it; tell me it's "somewhere between 10 and 40 percent, nobody's sure" and I freeze. The number isn't worse. The ignorance is.

So — does this have a name? The bending of the probabilities themselves (small chances of the worst thing inflated, big chances shrunk) as opposed to just caring less about extra money once you have some? And is the "I hate not knowing the odds" bit the same thing or its own seperate thing? I don't want a bare label; I want to know where the lines are — what's the same, what's different, and whether any of it is a mistake. Because it doesn't feel like a math error to her. It feels like a taste.
