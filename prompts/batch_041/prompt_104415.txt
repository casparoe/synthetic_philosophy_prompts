My department has two causation people who've been having the same arguement for about two years and it's become a bit of a joke, so I've been voluntold to run the adversarial collaboration they keep saying they'll do. One of them is an interventionist: C causes E when there's a possible intervention on C that, holding the rest of the causal structure fixed, would change E; the whole picture runs on variables, modularity, and invariance under intervention. The other is a possible-worlds counterfactualist (the Lewis-ish kind): C causes E when E counterfactually depends on C in the right way -- roughly, in the nearest world where C doesn't happen, E doesn't either -- and for them interventions are just a lab technique for probing that dependence, not the metaphysics. Neither of them will sign off on my glosses of their own view, so keep my summaries loose and let them fix the wording themselves.

Write the document they should have produced instead of two more years of this. A numbered section of claims both can actually sign: what they jointly reject (regularity/constant-conjunction accounts, mere correlation, etc.), what exactly "cause" is being used to track, and which cases count as the real stress tests. Then a table of the remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept rather than a strawman. Then the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them -- and for each crux a case or argument both would agree actually bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two things I especially want out of it. First, actual/token causation and the ugly cases: double prevention, switching, trumping preemption, overdetermination, and omissions or absences as causes -- I suspect this is where the two views genuinely split, more than on the clean type-level stuff. Second, I want an honest call on whether any of the disagreement is just verbal: is the fight really about the semantics of "cause" versus the metaphysics of causation, or about whether interventions are constitutive of causation or merely evidential? If part of it is verbal, say which part and why, and make the non-verbal residue as sharp as you can.

Also, please settle one specific claim I keep pushing on them: that the interventionist is really just a counterfactualist with extra bookkeeping, and that the structural-equations machinery is a modelling convenience rather than a competing account of what causation is. If that's wrong, I want the exact place where the extra machinery does metaphysical work; if it's right, I want them to sign it together. Don't hedge by telling me both sides have a point in vague terms -- the agreed claims should be non-vacuous (stuff a regularity theorist would refuse to sign), and both of them have read too much to be impressed by a survey, so keep it specific.
