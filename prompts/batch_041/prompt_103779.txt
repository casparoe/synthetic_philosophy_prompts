For the past four months or so, my school's philosophy club has been arguing about whether conceivability is a guide to possibility, and we have gotten absolutely nowhere. What I want from you is not a verdict on who is right but a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement we are actually having.

Here is the shape of it. One faction holds that if you can coherently conceive of something — a creature physically identical to a person but with no inner experience, say, or water that is not H2O — then that thing is possible, and the conceiving counts as evidence, perhaps even as proof. The other faction says this reverses the order of explanation: our imaginings are psychological facts about us, and facts about us cannot tell us what the world could have been like. Their favorite counterexamples are cases in which people confidently conceived of something that later turned out to be impossible, such as heat as a fluid that flows.

We have tried the obvious repairs. We distinguished "conceivable" in the sense of not obviously contradictory from "conceivable" in the sense of being able to positively picture a complete scenario; we distinguished what we can imagine given our current concepts from what an ideal reasoner could imagine; someone raised the point that identity statements like water being H2O are necessary yet knowable only after the fact, which seems to make some of our intuitions about conceivability unreliable in principle. None of this has moved anyone.

My hypothesis, which I would like you to test rather than simply accept, is that we are not disagreeing about a fact. The first faction is asking a normative question: what entitles us to move from a felt inability to find a contradiction to a claim about what is possible? The second is asking something closer to a descriptive or psychological question: what actually causes our modal beliefs, and how reliable is that process? If that is right, then our debate is partly verbal and partly two different questions sharing one label.

So diagnose it. Is the impasse a hidden empirical question, namely how often our conceivings track genuine possibility? A disagreement about what counts as evidence? A dispute about which sense of "conceivable" is at issue? Or bedrock intuitions about whether imagination has any authority at all, which neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress — a distinction, a counterexample both sides must accept, a calibration of our conceivings against cases whose modal status we already know, or nothing whatsoever. Please engage the actual positions rather than surveying the literature, and be honest if the question is simply ill-posed.
