I'm trying to get my views on animals into some kind of order before I write anything down, and I keep going in circles. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Speciesism is a moral error of the same kind as racism — species membership on its own is never a reason-giving difference.
2. What grounds moral status is sentience, meaning the capacity to suffer and to enjoy. Not intelligence, not language, not self-awareness, not being human.
3. I'm a totalist about welfare: aggregate net welfare is what matters, so numbers count. Twice as many animals living good lives is, other things equal, better than half as many.
4. When I try to explain why it's wrong to experiment on an adult human with the mind of a two year old but fine to experiment on a chimp with the same mind, I don't think the answer is about capacities at all. It's that the human is one of us — a member of the kind whose life this is a damaged version of.
5. Death isn't a harm to a being with no sense of its life as extending over time. So what's wrong with factory farming is the suffering, not the killing. (I've been vegetarian for about a decade and I'd like to see the industry shrink alot.)
6. Wild animal suffering is probably the biggest moral problem there is — far more animals suffer in nature than on farms — and we should be working out how to reduce it. Nature isn't a sanctuary.
7. I've had dogs my whole life and I think keeping companion animals, and training dogs to work, is fine.

Does this hang together? I want to know which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and how bad the real conflicts are — ranked, not just listed as equally bad. For each real one, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me, and then which single change would clear up the most. I genuinely don't know which of these I'd give up first.
