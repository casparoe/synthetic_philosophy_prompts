ok so I've been lurking on rationalist blogs since like 2012 and there's one recurring thing that bugs me, which is how everyone talks about "knowledge" like it's a service level agreement — you pay in, you get coverage, done, no fine print. me and a couple friends were arguing the other night about Nozick's tracking theory (the one where knowledge is true belief that tracks the world, so if it weren't true you wouldn't believe it and if it were true you would) and my roommate, who's a lawyer, said that's basically just an insurance policy, you're covered for the worlds where the thing goes wrong. we all laughed and then nobody could actually say why it wasn't.

so what I want from you is the customer-facing policy document for a company — name it whatever you like — that sells "epistemic insurance" built straight off the tracking theory. deadpan the whole way, no winking, formatted like a real terms-of-service page. the jokes have to come out of the actual philosophy and not out of invented quirks: the whole selling point of tracking is that it's what turns a true belief into knowledge, and the very same feature is why closure fails, so the policy should be able to cover your knowledge of P and of "if P then Q" while somehow never quite covering Q, there should be a brain-in-a-vat exclusion tucked away in there, and a clause that explains, in the most soothing corporate voice, why your "I have hands" coverage does not extend to "I'm not a brain in a vat." a reader who knows the literature should feel the reductio without you ever spelling it out, and a reader who doesn't should still enjoy it as a great bit of corporate doublespeak.

give it to me as exactly five numbered clauses, one or two sentences each, no intro and no conclusion, just the five clauses.
