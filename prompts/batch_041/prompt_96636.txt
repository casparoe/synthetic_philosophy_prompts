Consider a subject who checks the reliability of her color vision by looking at object after object, noting each time that the object looks a certain color and is that color, and then concluding that her color vision is reliable. Call this the bootstrapping inference.

Give me your credence that a subject who reasons this way thereby knows that her color vision is reliable. Decompose the number: how much rides on whether the inference is premise-circular (it uses the conclusion as a premise) or circular only in relying on an inference form whose reliability is itself in question, and whether only the former is vicious; on whether justification is externalist (a matter of the process being reliable) or internalist (a matter of the subject's evidence); on whether some beliefs are justified by default, without supporting evidence; and on whatever further sub-questions you think belong — a rough probability and one line of justification for each, then the combination. Tell me which sub-question the number is most sensitive to, and what argument or evidence would move it most.

Finally, does your answer change if the faculty in question is not perception but moral intuition — the subject endorses her past moral verdicts by consulting the very intuition whose reliability is at issue — or if it is a faculty she cannot, from the inside, distinguish from an unreliable one?
