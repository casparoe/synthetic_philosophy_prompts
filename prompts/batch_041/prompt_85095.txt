Next week's podcast guest is a luck egalitarian, and I want to make her sweat a bit. I'm not naming her — she's a stand-in for the position rather than a specific person, so write the questions for the view, not for anyone's actual published line. The view: inequalities are unjust when they trace to brute luck, permissible when they're the upshot of option luck. Her own twist, which is the part I actually want to interrogate: rather than tracing a disadvantage back to its ultimate unchosen source (a regress that never terminates), we should hold people responsible only for choices and traits that are *tractable* for them — revisable at a cost that's reasonable for someone in their position. She calls it the tractability criterion and claims it dissolves the regress problem and takes most of the sting out of the harshness charge in one move.

Draft me 9 or 10 questions, with a follow-up after each that's keyed to the answer she's most likely to actually give — not a limp 'could you say more'. I want at least one question each on:

(a) whether the tractability criterion is doing work a plain floor or sufficiency condition couldn't do on its own — and if it isn't, whether her view is still luck egalitarianism or has quietly become a hybrid;
(b) the regress coming back through the back door: if someone can't revise a preference, is that inability itself something they could have revised, and what fixes the threshold of 'reasonable cost' other than intuition;
(c) the expensive-tastes and sour-grapes cases — the person who could acquire cheaper tastes only by becoming someone they'd hate being;
(d) the harshness/abandonment charge head-on, including the awkward case of someone who could have revised but didn't and now can't;
(e) the epistemic burden — what a state would have to know about people to apply her criterion, and wether that makes the view unworkable rather than false.

Finish with one question about what argument or evidence would get her to give up the tractability criterion. Skip anything about how she got into philosophy or what luck egalitarianism is; the audience is there for the in-fighting.
