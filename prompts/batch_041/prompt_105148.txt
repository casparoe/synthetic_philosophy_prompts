Okay, I read a magazine piece on panpsychism recently and I can't tell if I got taken in by it. Here's the gist as best I remember:

Panpsychism is the view that everything is conscious — not metaphorically, the article means rocks, electrons, your phone, and ultimately the universe taken as a whole, which on this view is one gigantic mind. The appeal, supposedly, is that it makes the hard problem of consciousness go away: instead of explaining how inert matter somehow conjures up experience, you just posit that experience is a basic feature of reality, like mass or charge, and then there's nothing left to explain. The catch is the combination problem: if every fundamental particle carries its own little scrap of experience, what glues all those scraps together into the single unified experience I'm having right now? The article says nobody has any idea how that would work, that this is the real reason panpsychism is still a fringe position, and that at bottom the whole thing is wordplay — you haven't explained consciousness, you've just decided to call matter conscious and called it a day. It does admit that a few defenders say maybe it's the other way round — the universe is the one conscious thing and individual minds are just fragments of it — but it waves that off as a dodge.
