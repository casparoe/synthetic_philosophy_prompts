Six weeks ago a friend walked me through the Doomsday argument at a party and I havent been able to think about much else since; now three of us meet on Sundays and we've been stuck on the same thing for a month, so I said I'd write up what the actual disagreement is, and I can't. Here's the shape of it. One of us reasons as if he's a random observer drawn from all the observers who will ever exist — that's what makes Doomsday go through, and he accepts it. Another reasons as if a hypothesis that predicts more observers is more likely to be the hypothesis he's in, which kills Doomsday but also tells him, from the bare fact that he exists, that the universe is probably stuffed with vastly many observers — the presumptuous philosopher result — and he's decided to bite that bullet. The third says we should just treat "I'm an observer with these experiences" as ordinary non-indexical evidence about the world and condition on it like anything else, which is supposed to dissolve the whole thing. We keep re-running the same cases — Sleeping Beauty, the Doomsday setup, and a toy case we made up, where you're told the world holds either one person with exactly your evidence or a thousand such people, and nothing you could ever learn tells them apart; I say there's nothing to be uncertain about, my friend says the thousand-copy world is a thousand times more likely from where he sits — and nobody budges an inch.

My hypothesis is that this isnt a substantive disagreement at all: the principles are rival stipulations about what "probability" could mean for a self-locating claim, and "I" isn't a random variable in the way either side needs it to be, so we're choosing language games rather than discovering facts. My friend thinks it's a real disagreement about which indifference principle is rationally required of anyone who takes their own existence seriously, and that my diagnosis is just deflationism. So what kind of disagreement is this actually? And given that diagnosis, what would count as progress — a theorem, a sharper distinction (the reference-class problem on the sampling side seems to be doing more work than either of us admits), a new case, or nothing?
