Right, so this started as an argument with my mate Dev about something that happened on his team at work and it's now eaten three of our Fridays. Here's the pattern I can't find a word for. Take a bunch of people who all care about the same thing, all free to act on their own, nobody in charge of any of them. Each person does something that, on its own, is completely defensible -- you couldn't tell them off for it, and if they'd been the only one who did it you'd probably have thought better of them for it. And then because there are enough of them doing it at once, the result is a mess. The case that set us off: Dev's team has six reviewers on a code change, and each one left two or three small suggestions, and it ends up with ninety comments and the person who wrote it just abandons the whole thing. Every single reviewer did a good job. The work is ruined. Mine is a bereavement forum I lurk on, where after one bad post about a dozen regulars each independently sent the poster a short 'thinking of you' message, which is a generous thing to do, and she came back two days later saying she'd recieved forty messages and deleted her account. Nobody did anything wrong and something pretty bad happened. I'm a physio, so I've got no background for this at all, I'm just going off the shape of the thing.

Dev says it's the tragedy of the commons, or the bystander effect with the sign flipped, or just that nobody was taking responsibility. None of those sit right and I think I can say why, but I dont have anything to put in their place. The commons one needs a shared thing that each person uses up, and there isn't one in my two examples -- the forum wasn't depleted, every message was fine, and the problem only shows up when you add them together. The bystander thing is about people not acting because they assume someone else will; here everyone acted, each of them thinking roughly 'someone should, so I'll do it'. And 'nobody took responsibility' is the exact opposite of what happened: everyone took responsibility, individually, which is sort of the problem. What I keep coming back to is that there's no act you can point at. You can't say which of the twelve messages did the damage, or that it was the seventh one that tipped it over, because the harm isn't in any of the messages, it's in the number. Does that have a name? I half expect there's something in economics or game theory about lots of people each doing a small defensible thing until a system falls over, but I wouldn't know where to look, and please don't give me a reading list -- I won't read it, I want the idea itself.

And if there's no name, I'd much rather you made one up properly than left me with 'it's a bit like X'. What I actually want is the carving: what exactly the thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. In particular, does it matter that in my cases each act is harmless on its own, versus in the grazing examples where each act does a little damage on its own -- same phenomenon in different clothes, or two different things? And there's a flip side I can't fit anywhere: same group, same absence of a boss, except now the useful thing is boring and costs each person twenty minutes, and then nobody does it at all and the forum or the team quietly dies. Is that a separate name, or this one with the sign flipped? Also, tell me how confident you are in each claim you make, and say straight out which bit you're least sure of -- I'd rather have a hedged answer with the hole flagged in it than a tidy-sounding label that turns out to be describing something else.
