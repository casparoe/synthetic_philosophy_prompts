Write a short story of roughly 1,200 words. It is set in a city where a testimonial verifier — a device that logs a speaker's occurrent beliefs at the moment of speech and whose readouts are admissible in court — has just been made mandatory in public inquiries. Your protagonist is a bridge engineer called to testify about a recent collapse. She is convinced the replacement design is unsound, but if she says so she will be dismissed and the design will proceed unchanged; if she certifies it, the verifier will record that she did not believe what she certified. Build the story toward her uttering a single sentence that asserts some proposition and then disavows belief in it — the form: it will hold, but I do not believe it will — and make that sentence feel like the one thing an honest person in her position could say rather than a contradiction. Let the philosophical interest come from the situation itself; do not name any philosopher or cite any text.
