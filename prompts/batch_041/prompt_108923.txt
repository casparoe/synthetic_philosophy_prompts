I just had a long argument with a friend about Simpson's paradox in algorithmic fairness. My friend thinks the reversal proves that group fairness metrics are arbitrary; I think it shows we need causal models. Suppose R. A. Fisher were brought back and shown a modern machine-learning system that passes a group-level fairness audit but reverses its disparity when you condition on a relevant third variable. What would he say? Would he treat the reversal as a causal-identification problem, a sign that the fairness metric is incoherent, or a nuisance that only proper randomization could settle? And where would his experimentalist instincts mislead him about observational auditing, counterfactual fairness, and the difference between a statistical association and a discriminatory mechanism?
