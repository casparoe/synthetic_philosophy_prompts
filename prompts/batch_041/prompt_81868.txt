I spent most of last night arguing with a friend of mine (she's doing a PhD in metaphysics) about dispositions, and we got completely stuck in the fink-and-mask stuff. You probably know the setup: the old simple idea that calling a glass fragile just means it would break if you struck it, and then all the counterexamples. The sorcerer case, where striking the glass would turn it into something unbreakable, so it's fragile but wouldn't break if struck (a fink); and the case where a glass really is fragile but someone's wrapped it in packing material or a protective charm, so it wouldn't break even though you struck it (a mask). Every time the conditional-analysis people patch the analysis -- add "and nothing interferes," restrict the antecedent, move to fancier conditionals -- someone generates a new case, and every time the critics wheel out a new case the defenders just say fine, that shows the analysis needs another clause. We went around like that for hours, over a bottle of wine, and neither of us moved an inch. She thinks the whole project is a degenerating research program; I think the counterexamples are getting less and less realistic and that's telling us something.
