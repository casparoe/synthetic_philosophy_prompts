Ok so I've been reading about the late Roman Republic for the past few months — not the usual "and then Caesar crossed the Rubicon" stuff, more the institutional history, the tribunate, the way the senate and the popular assemblies kept renegotiating who got to decide what. My friend and I have a running argument about it. His line is that it was basically over after the Gracchi: once a tribune pushing land reform gets beaten to death by a senatorial mob and nobody is ever really punished for it, the informal norms that kept elite competition inside the rules are dead, and everything after that is just the bill coming due. My line is that that's hindsight talking — we're reading inevitability backwards from Actium.

So here's what I want, and I want a number, not a hedge. Give me your credence that there is a feasible counterfactual in which the Republic is still recognizably itself a century or so after the Gracchi — annual magistracies that matter, tribunes with a veto that actually bites, elections that genuinely decide who governs, no army loyal to a man rather than to the state — and then decompose that number. I dont just want "the sources are bad." I want the terms your credence is built out of, a rough probability for each, and a line of justification for each. I'd guess the terms include something like: whether "the Republic" is even a coherent thing to preserve, since it changed so much that maybe there's no fact of the matter; the weight of the military-clientage path — Marius, the professionalized legions, veterans who need land and look to a general rather than the senate; the agrarian/economic path, smallholders squeezed out with the army as the only exit; the norms path, i.e. whether elite self-restraint was actually load-bearing or just a surface effect of the fact that nobody had a big enough army yet; plain contingency, the specific death or specific battle that could have gone the other way; and the scale term — whether a city-state constitution could run a Mediterranean empire at all, in which case the interesting question becomes which reform, if any, would have been enough. Tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what piece of evidence or argument would move it the most — if you had to bet on one thing shifting your credence, what is it.

Then tell me where my friend and I most likely actually disagree. My suspicion is that we don't disagree about the causal facts so much as about what counts as "the Republic surviving" — he's got a thick conception and I've got a thin one — but maybe the real split is on the contingency term and I'm flattering myself. And one more thing, since this is partly why I care: I keep seeing this period used as a warning about modern democracies, and I'm definately suspicious of the analogy. Which single term in your decomposition is doing all the work in that analogy — which one has a Roman value that we're quietly assuming also holds for us but probably doesn't?
