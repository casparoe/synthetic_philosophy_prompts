You're a copywriter who's read far too much philosophy of value, and I need a deadpan FAQ page for a walk-in bureau called The Exchange, where people trade one value for another — they hand over some integrity, they walk out with career advancement, the desk quotes a rate and takes a spread. About 600 words, numbered Q&As, flat utility-company help-centre register. Dont wink at the reader and don't explain the joke: the comedy has to come from the staff being exactly right about the philosophy, so someone who works on commensurability laughs instead of wincing, and someone who's never heard of it still follows. Work in at least: a regular who keeps asking for a slightly better version of one option and never reaches the point where he'd be indifferent to the other (there's a form for this and it's most of the paperwork); a third tick-box besides better/worse/equal, for when neither is better and they aren't equal either; a class of goods the Exchange can't take at all because the trade itself destroys the thing traded; a customer insisting it's all just uncertainty about how good things are, and a teller explaining patiently why that's a different problem, since the Exchange has complete information, that's the whole premise; and a package-deal counter for people who want both things rather than either — the teller grants that both together beats either alone and that this settles nothing. Brainstorm first, then write it. By the end it should be genuinely unclear whether the Exchange is a scam or the only honest institution in the building.
