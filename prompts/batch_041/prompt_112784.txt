I write about science and ideas, and latley every writer I know is doing a "genealogy" of something — the open-plan office, burnout, the pressure to turn your hobby into a side hustle, the whole idea that you're supposed to find your "passion." Most of them just hand me an origin story and act like the origin settles whether we should keep the thing. I want a spec to hold them to, and I want it strict enough that it would pinch Nietzsche too, since he's the one everyone is quietly imitating. So: what does a genealogy of some piece of our moral or practical life actually have to deliver to count as adequate — adequate, say, for telling me whether to keep the practice or ditch it? Give me exactly five numbered requirements, each one a sentence or two, with the case or consideration that puts it on the list and a note on whether it's a hard requirement or just the taste of one camp. No intro, no wrap-up, just the five. And if two of them can't both be met by any single account, say so in the item where the clash shows up, because thats the part I'd actually quote.
