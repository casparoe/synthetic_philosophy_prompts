I'm a novelist at work on a book that alternates between two prisons, and I need to understand what each one believes before I can write it. The first is Norwegian: no bars on the windows, officers who carry no weapons and are expected to cook and eat with the men, a single officer assigned to each prisoner as his contact, and a working assumption that the person inside will be a neighbour within a few years. The second is American maximum security: counts, cell searches, years in a windowless unit with no contact visits for disciplinary infractions, life without parole, and a lifelong tail of civil disabilities — no vote, barred from many jobs and from public housing — that outlasts the sentence.

What I want is not a policy comparison but a reconstruction of the philosophy each place commits itself to. What theory of punishment, and what picture of the person punished, does each set of routines presuppose? Which features strictly require that theory, and which merely happen to fit it? Where is each institution underdetermined, such that the same routines would serve a rival theory? And is either internally consistent — in particular, can a system that calls itself correctional, and names its institutions departments of correction, also impose life without parole and permanent civil disabilities, which seem to presuppose that the offender is beyond correction? Two further practices interest me: Norway's preventive detention, which holds offenders judged too dangerous to release and can be extended again and again, and the American practice of civilly committing sex offenders after their prison terms have ended. Say what each assumes about the boundary between punishment and the management of risk.

Take a position rather than surveying the options. Use these four sections, in this order: "What the Norwegian prison assumes" (about 350 words); "What the American prison assumes" (about 350 words); "Where each is underdetermined" (about 250 words); and "My verdict" (about 250 words), giving your own view of which package of commitments is more coherent and why.
