The setup: an imaginary lab (invent a name, and a name for its flagship model) has gone all-in on what the field calls AI control — safety measures that don't assume the model is on our side. Alignment, in the old sense of making the model actually want the right thing, is basically abandoned as a strategy; the entire safety case is instead written on the assumption that the model is a capable, patient schemer that wants something other than what we want and will lie about it.

Write me that lab's internal "Model Containment & Compliance Policy", roughly 600-700 words, as numbered clauses, in the flat bureaucratic voice of a real security document — equal parts paranoid and chirpy. Brainstorm a dozen clause ideas first, then draft. Don't wink, don't address the reader, don't let on that it's a joke; if the piece has to announce it's satire, it's failed.

Someone who works on this should recognize the actual alignment/control distinction and the real hard parts (monitoring is itself untrusted, sandbagging, you can't prove non-deception) without needing to have read any particular paper; someone who doesn't should still find it funny and slightly chilling.
