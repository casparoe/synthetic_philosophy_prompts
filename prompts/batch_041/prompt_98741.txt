So three friends and I bought a little cabin upstate a couple years back. Seemed like a great idea at the time. It's been kind of a mess though, and last weekend two of them, Dana and Marcus, got into it over text about why, and I've been turning it over ever since. I stayed out of it and honestly I can't tell who's more right, so I was hoping you could referee.

Here's basically how it went, as close as I can remember:

Dana: The problem is obvious. Two of us use the place way more than the other two, and when they do they leave it gross and never restock the firewood or the propane. It's the classic thing where everyone grabs as much as they can and nobody pays, and eventually the whole thing gets wrecked. At this rate the cabin's trashed inside a year.

Marcus: I don't think overuse is the issue at all. Look at what's actually failed: the insurance lapsed, the gutters never got cleaned, nobody ever called a plumber about the leak, and the booking sheet is three months out of date. None of those get used up by any one person, they're things that benefit all four of us equally whether or not we lift a finger, so of course nobody wants to be the sucker who does them. The problem isn't that we take too much, it's that nobody does the boring work.

Dana: Fine, so we price things. Charge per night, charge a cleaning deposit, charge for firewood. Then people feel what they're using and the money can pay for the plumber.

Marcus: The per-night fee, sure, I'm with you. But it won't touch the plumber-and-insurance stuff, because the whole reason nobody does that work is that everyone benefits whether or not they do it, and you can't charge someone for a benefit you've got no way of keeping them from getting. A usage fee doesn't reach that. You'd have to actually pay someone, or make it somebody's turn.

Dana: Then here's my counter. Everyone gets a veto. Every booking, every repair, every decision, all four of us have to be OK with it or it doesn't happen. That way nobody feels steamrolled and everyone actually gives a damn.

Marcus: That's backwards. If any one of us can block any booking, then the one guy who's fussy about the floors blocks everything and the cabin just sits there empty. You'd have fixed the overuse by making sure nobody can use it at all.

Dana: So what, we elect one of us as the boss and let him run it? Then it's not ours anymore, it's his, and the whole point of buying it together was that it's ours.

Marcus: Maybe. Or maybe the whole it's-ours thing is the actual problem.

Can you go through this move by move? Like rate each of the eight moves from clearly wrong to clearly right, with a quick reason for each, then say who's more right overall and what both of them are missing. Definately don't just tell me we need to communicate better, I want the actual disagreement sorted out.
