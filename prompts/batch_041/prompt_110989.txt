I'm a first-year MA student and I got absolutely taken apart in supervision yesterday over my essay on Frege's puzzle about identity statements. The verdict was basically "either trivial or false, pick one," which is a fun thing to hear four days before the revision is due. I'm offline till Monday, so I'm not looking for a reading list or citations — I just want a second opinion on whether any of this actually holds up. Here's my outline, roughly as written:

1. The setup everyone gets taught is: 'a = b' can be informative, 'a = a' can't be, so the informativeness can't be coming from reference. My move is that informativeness isn't a property of the sentence or the proposition at all — it's relative to a question the hearer is actively asking. The content of 'a = b' and 'a = a' can be the very same object-identity; what differs is the question-space you're evaluating it against.

2. Test case: two villages on opposite sides of the same mountain, no contact for generations. One calls it Kestrel, the other calls it Harrow. A surveyor who grows up in the first village moves to the second and learns "Kestrel is Harrow." Genuinely informative. But a kid in the second village who's only ever heard "Harrow" can't even evaluate the sentence — she has no grip on "Kestrel" at all. So the interesting fact isn't that the identity statement is informative, it's that informativness is a relation between a sentence and a hearer's repertoire of names, not between two senses.

3. The case I actually want to press: someone who learns both names in the same breath, always together, from a teacher who invariably says "Kestrel — that is, Harrow." Her two names have identical inferential profiles; anything she'd infer from one she'd infer from the other. Frege seems to need the senses to differ anyway. My view says there's simply nothing left for the identity to be informative about. I think that's a point in my favour, but I can't tell whether it's a bug.

4. The objection I can't answer: if informativeness is question-relative, nothing stops 'a = a' from being informative. Take someone who half-doubts that the figure in the doorway is the same person she saw a minute ago — "it's her, it's her" is exactly informative, and yet nothing about identity has been learned. Frege's argument needs 'a = a' to be never informative. So either I've got the wrong notion of informativeness, or Frege's argument is doing less work than advertised. Which one?

I care most about (a) whether the question-relative move in 1 is actually distinct from just redescribing Frege's sense, (b) whether 3 does the work I want it to, and (c) what the cleanest fix for 4 is.
