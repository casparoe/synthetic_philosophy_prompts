Write an essay on the following controversy in AI alignment. One position holds that alignment research should aim for worst-case guarantees: a system should be safe against every input an adversary could construct, because deployment environments and the systems themselves may be adversarial. A contrary position holds that worst-case guarantees are usually either vacuous or unattainable, and that the proper target is average-case or distributional guarantees: safety with high probability under the distribution of inputs the system will actually encounter.

To make the dispute tractable, adopt the following stipulative vocabulary throughout your essay (you need not endorse it, but use it consistently):
- A guarantee is distribution-free if it holds for every input in the system's declared input space.
- A guarantee is distribution-bound if it holds with probability at least 1 − ε over a specified deployment distribution D.
- A distribution-bound guarantee is shadowed if there exists a plausible distribution shift under which the system's failure probability exceeds some threshold τ, even though it meets the guarantee on D.
- The pair consisting of the input space and D is the guarantee frame.

Now consider a concrete case. A lab trains a language model to refuse harmful requests. The lab reports a distribution-bound guarantee: on its evaluation distribution, the refusal rate for harmful requests exceeds 99.9%. A critic replies that the evaluation distribution is not the deployment distribution, and that an adversary can find inputs outside it that elicit harmful completions; the guarantee is therefore shadowed. The lab responds that distribution-free guarantees are impossible for any system with a large input space, since an adversary can always find some input that fails, so the critic's standard is vacuous.

In your essay, weigh the strongest arguments on both sides. Address at least the following: (1) whether the vacuity objection to distribution-free guarantees is decisive, or whether some distribution-free guarantees are non-vacuous because they constrain the system's behavior on all inputs in a meaningful way; (2) whether distribution-bound guarantees can be made robust enough to be trustworthy, for example by expanding D to include adversarial perturbations or by proving bounds on the rate of failure under distribution shift; (3) whether the choice between the two targets is a technical question or a value-laden one about what kind of safety we owe to whom; and (4) whether there is a coherent third option, such as guarantees that are distribution-free relative to a restricted but well-defined threat model.

Reach a conclusion and defend it. Do not end on a non-committal note; if you think the answer depends on context, state the principle that decides the context. End with a single final line of the form Verdict: <one short sentence>.
