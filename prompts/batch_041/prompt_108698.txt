I'm the research lead for a national commission on historical redress. Petitions come in most months asking the state to apologise, to rename a street or pull down a statue, to put up a plaque, or to pay something out of general funds for things done a long time ago, and nearly every one of them makes some claim about who is guilty of what. We're not a court, we don't do new history (we take the record as given), and our findings get read out on television, so we can't just say everyone was a bit guilty and move on.

What I want is a decision procedure my analysts can actually run, built on Jaspers' four kinds of guilt but taking seriously that he was writing about one country in one year, and that our cases involve descendants, institutions that still exist, and people who actively resisted at the time. The questions in order; what counts as an answer to each; where the thing stops with a flat no; and where it has to stop because whats left is a values question that belongs to the legislature and not to us.

Then run it on three petitions: (a) an apology and payment for a mass expulsion 150 years ago, where the state is the same legal entity but a large share of today's citizens descend from people who arrived afterwards, and the descendants of those expelled live somewhere else now; (b) a firm still trading under almost its old name that was the main beneficiary of a forced labour programme, and says the current owners and workers aren't the people who did it; (c) a request from descendants of families who sheltered victims that we formally declare their community not guilty, which I take it Jaspers would refuse — tell me if I've got him wrong.

Format: 'What we're allowed to say' — the short list of verdicts the procedure can return and what each commits us to, ~150 words. 'The procedure' — numbered, in the order an analyst would ask them, ~700 words. 'Three petitions' — ~500 words, and I want at least one where the procedure gives an answer I won't like. 'Where it will misfire' — ~200 words, the cases it can't settle and the ones where it'll look like its doing philosophy instead of justice.
