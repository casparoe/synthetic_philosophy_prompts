A few of us in the lab keep arguing about a line that shows up in basically every review we read: DNA is a code, the genome is a program, and the cell is just the machine that runs it. The author we're reading right now leans on that to conclude that everything you need to build the organism is written in the sequence -- the rest is hardware, and the environment is at most a power supply plus some noise. My friend thinks the metaphor is doing all the real work in the argument and is basically pretending to be a finding. I half agree, but I want the actual audit rather than a shrug.

So lay out the correspondence properly first. What plays the role of the code, the encoding, the decoder, the program, the machine, the input. Then give me the places it breaks, and be specific about which breaks actually matter for the conclusion the author wants and which are just the usual "all metaphors leak" noise. Two things I especially want you to take seriously. One: the translation table (codon -> amino acid) is itself written in the same code it decodes, so the decoder is specified inside the thing it's supposed to decode -- what does that do to the "code" claim. Two: Shannon information is just correlational structure with no meaning attached, so the sequence only counts as information "about" the organism relative to a reader who already knows how to use it -- does that make "genetic information" a real kind of information or a way of speaking. Throw in the messy stuff too: one sequence, different products depending on cellular context; regions that don't code but regulate; no clean place where "the program" stops and "the machine" starts.

Then tell me whether the conclusion survives. Once the disanalogies are on the table, does the code/program picture still support "all the information is in the sequence," or was that conclusion riding on the metaphor the whole time. And if the picture is busted, whats the better comparison -- recipe, archive, something else -- and what would that one let you say that the program picture dont.
