I'm a journalist who writes about science and ideas, and next month I'm hosting a 45-minute session at an ideas festival on the careenium -- Hofstadter's picture of a cranium as a giant billiard table packed with countless tiny magnetic marbles (simms) that careen around and clump into bigger rolling clusters (simmballs) whose motion starts to track what's going on outside the table, and the whole setup ends up asking "who shoves whom around inside the careenium?" -- do the simms do all the pushing and the simmballs are just a convenient description, or do the symbols/concepts really shove the substrate around? Audience is smart generalists, no philosophy of mind, no slides. Dont explain the idea to me -- plan how I should teach it. What has to be in place before the question even lands, what single example should carry the whole session and what it needs to have (and not have) to do that job -- and if that's not the careenium itself, what is it, and why not just the careenium? Then the misconceptions I should expect -- my hunch is half the room will say "it's obviously all simms, the rest is a story we tell" and the other half will hear "the simmballs are little agents" -- and exactly where in the sequence I head each one off. Plus one question to throw at them near the end that tells me whether they've actually got it, and what I should openly say I'm leaving out at this level.
