Not bad for biodiversity, bad for the animals themselves, in the suffering sense. I keep fumbling it in drafts and I want the room to get it right, so dont explain the issue to me, give me the teaching plan.

What has to be in place before the question even makes sense -- I think it's that wild animal suffering is a thing at all, that nature isn't a happy equilibrium, but I can't tell if that comes first or whether something about counting lives versus counting suffering has to come earlier.

The example. So the "restore nature" option may mean more animals and more total suffering, and the "leave it degraded" option may mean less. What does an example have to have, and have to lack, to do this job? Then the misconceptions, in the order the room will actually form them, and exactly where in my sequence I should head each one off. I don't know the order, and I suspect there are ones I'm not seeing.

Finally, one question I can put to them at the end that tells me whether they've got the shape of the problem rather than just been disturbed by it, and what I should openly say I'm not going to cover at this level -- said out loud, not just skipped.
