I'm a novelist, and I've somehow ended up on the grant committee of a small foundation that funds research on human nature (they wanted a storyteller in the room, dont ask). We meet Thursday and I have to circulate a short written opinion before then. On the table: a psych lab's proposal that boredom is an adaptation -- that the itch of restlessness is a signal our current goals aren't paying off, evolved to shove us toward exploring new ones -- and they want money to test whether people high in boredom-proneness actually explore more and end up with more status and mating success down the line. Half the committee is thrilled; the other half says you could spin any result either way, so it's a just-so story with a budget, and we shouldnt be funding untestable evolutionary stories no matter how pretty they are. Give me a memo that lands on a concrete recommendation -- fund it, reject it, or fund it with conditions -- with the reasoning laid out, say clearly what evidence would actually settle the question rather than leaving room for the story to absorb anything, and be honest about the strongest thing the losing side has going for it. One page or less, 500 words max, American English.
