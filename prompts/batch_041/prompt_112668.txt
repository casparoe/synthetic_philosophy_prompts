My parents had me baptised Catholic when I was a baby in Ireland, and we moved to Germany when I was six — when we registered our address the form asked for our religion and my parents put Catholic. I'm 29 now, I've worked in Cologne as a translator for years, and I just noticed that my payslip has a line for church tax: the state takes it straight out of my pay (9% of my income tax, I think) and hands it over to the church, because as far as the records are concerned I'm still a member. I stopped believing when I was about 15. To make it stop I have to go to the town hall, formally declare I'm leaving, pay a fee for the privilege, and then tell my mother, who has already informed me that my grandmother would be devasted and that what I'd be throwing away is something I was given, not something I chose. My brother says the whole setup is obviously unjust — a state collecting a church's dues from me on the strength of a decision made when I was a month old. My girlfriend, who's French, says that's the least of it, and that a state that collects church taxes for the church isn't secular in any sense that matters. I half agree with both of them and neither quite lands. What should I do here, and how should I think about what I actually owe — to the church, to my family, to myself? Am I even a member of something I never agreed to join?
