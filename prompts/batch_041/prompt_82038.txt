Here's the argument.

(1) Necessity of origin. For anything that comes into being, the particular material it actually came from is essential to it. This table could not have been made from a different block of wood; if it had been, that would have been a different table. Same for me: the sperm and egg I actually developed from are essential to me — a person who developed from a different sperm isn't me, however similar he turns out.

(2) And the reason for that isn't something special about gametes or blocks of wood. The reason is that what makes an individual the individual it is, is where it came from. Its origin is its causal source, and that's what carries its identity across possible worlds. That's the whole engine of the view.

(3) But the gametes aren't a source in any deep sense. They're just where we happen to stop looking. The sperm that made me was produced by my father's body; his body was produced by his parents; and so on. Every link in that chain is exactly as much a causal source of me as the sperm and egg are. If being a causal source is what earns something a place in my essence, they all qualify equally.

(4) So by the reasoning in (2), my origin isn't the gametes. My origin is the whole causal history that produced me.

(5) Whatever my origin is, it's essential to me. (That's just (1) restated with a variable in it.)

(6) So the whole causal history that produced me is essential to me. In no possible world where I exist did my mother have toast instead of cereal the morning I was conceived. In no possible world where I exist did my grandfather miss his boat.
