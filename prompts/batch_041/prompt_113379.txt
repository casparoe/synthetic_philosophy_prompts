My mate and I just spent two hours argueing about Adam Smith and empire and I need a tiebreaker. His line is that Smith was basically an anti-imperialist free-trader, full stop: empire equals monopoly equals bad. I think that's way too clean, because Smith's actual calls don't line up that neatly. Here's the list we ended up agreeing on, roughly:

- The colonial system where colonial produce has to be shipped home first and the colonies can only buy home manufactures: he hated it, saw it as a scheme for the merchants at everyone else's expense.
- The Navigation Acts, which are exactly that sort of restriction: he defended them anyway, on the grounds that defence is worth more than cheap goods.
- The East India Company governing Bengal: a company of merchants, he thought, is about the worst possible sovereign, and its rule was plunder.
- The restrictions on Irish trade: he thought it was absurd and unjust to treat Irishmen like foreigners.
- Keeping the American colonies and taxing them for their own defence: he said either give them representation and let them tax themselves, or let them go. The whole great-empire thing struck him as mostly flattery.
- Bounties, like the one on the herring fishery: waste plus an open invitation to fraud.

So my friend's free-trade rule explains maybe half of that and ignores the rest. Now the case we couldn't agree on, and one Smith never had to rule on: a far-off possession that (a) has no trade monopoly at all, its trade is open to anyone, (b) costs the treasury a fortune to garrison, (c) sits on a sea lane that matters a lot for defence, and (d) the locals have petitioned to stay British subjects, though they've no seat in Parliament.

Would Smith keep it or cut it loose? I dont just want the verdict, I want the rule he's actually running, the other rules that fit all six verdicts above just as well, what each of those predicts for the new case, which one you'd bet on and why, and the one further case that would actually force them apart. And tell me straight if the precedents genuinely underdetermine it rather than papering over that.
