Alright, I need a diagnosis of a fight that never ends, not a verdict on who's right.

Background so we're on the same page: there's a cluster of results in economics/finance where if two people trade and both are rational and both know that the other person's willingness to trade carries information, then — under fairly strong assumptions about what everybody knows — they can't agree to trade at all. The very fact that someone's offering to sell you the thing tells you something bad about it. Fine, I follow the proof.

Here's whats been bugging me for a couple years. Markets do insane volume every single day. So there are two camps and they havent budged. Camp one: the theorem is basically sound, it's a benchmark not a forecast, and real volume comes from everything the model assumes away — liquidity needs, hedging, life-cycle reasons, people who just like holding the asset, differences in endowments. Camp two: no, that's the tell. If the conclusion only holds because we assumed common knowledge of the whole structure, and every counterexample gets reclassified as not-really-informational, then the thing is either unfalsifiable or a reductio of the rational-expectations picture. The first camp says you're confusing a model with reality. The second says you're protecting a model with an escape hatch.

My working hypothesis — tell me if its stupid — is that this isn't an empirical disagreement at all and isn't even really about information. It's a disagreement about what a model is allowed to idealize away, and about when "it's just a benchmark" is a legitimate move vs. a dodge. So the two sides aren't answering the same question: one is asking what a fully rational market would look like, the other is asking what a model that can't be falsified by any volume is even claiming. Under that reading they're not disagreeing, they're talking past each other about the standards of explanation in econ.

Is that right, or am I missing a real empirical question underneath — something like whether volume actually tracks anything informational that we could test? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here: a no-trade result that survives relaxing common knowledge, a clean natural experiment, or just the concession that its two questions sharing one name? Be concrete about what each side actually commits to, and dont just restate the theorem.
