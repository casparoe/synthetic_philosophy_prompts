I am a doctoral candidate in metaphysics, and my department's work-in-progress seminar has spent the better part of a year on the conditional analysis of dispositions without converging on anything — a result I find more interesting than the analysis itself, and which I would like your help in diagnosing.

The shape of the impasse, as we see it, is this. The naive biconditional — an object is disposed to manifest M in circumstances C just in case it would manifest M were it in C — dies by counterexample. The sorcerer cases show that a thing can retain a disposition while the conditional fails: the glass is fragile, but a sorcerer would strip its fragility at the instant of impact, so it would not break. The masking cases show that the conditional can fail even when the disposition and its causal basis are both intact: the glass is fragile, but it is wrapped in packing when it is struck. One camp responds by refining the analysans, typically by appealing to an intrinsic property of the object that would still issue in the manifestation were it retained under suitable conditions; another camp treats finks and masks not as obstacles to be patched but as symptoms that no conditional in the analysans can capture what a disposition is, and urges that we treat dispositions as primitive rather than analysed. After decades of increasingly careful patching, neither camp concedes.

My hypothesis — offered as a hypothesis, not a conclusion — is that the apparent disagreement about cases is downstream of an unexamined agreement about what an analysis of a disposition is supposed to deliver: that one side is doing semantics for disposition ascriptions while the other is doing the metaphysics of properties, and that both tacitly assume a disposition must be underwritten by a distinct categorical basis, an assumption that at least some dispositionalists deny. If that is right, the parties may not be contradicting one another at all.

I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Concretely: (i) Is this a verbal dispute, a substantive one, or two camps answering different questions under a single heading — and what, precisely, is each camp's question? (ii) Does the impasse turn on a hidden commitment such as the basis assumption I flag, or on something more formal, for instance on what the counterfactual in the analysans is permitted to quantify over? (iii) To make the diagnosis earn its keep, consider a case I have been developing: a factor that both removes an object's causal basis and independently blocks the manifestation, so that it is at once a fink and a mask, and whose occurrence is itself the manifestation of another disposition of the very same object. Does the standard fink/mask taxonomy survive such a case, and if the taxonomy cannot be drawn cleanly, does that strengthen the diagnosis that the disagreement is not really about the cases at all? Finally, given whatever diagnosis you settle on, tell me what — if anything — would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, or an argument that the question is ill-posed and that no progress should be expected.
