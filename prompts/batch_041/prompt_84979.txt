I am setting the final exam for an upper-level seminar on the extended mind, and I need one essay question (90 minutes, closed book), a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20. The question must not be answerable by reciting Otto's notebook and the parity principle, so I want the crux to be the boundary problem: whether extended cognition can be given a principled stopping rule that is neither a disguised preference for the biological nor so permissive that it swallows everything a thinker merely relies on.

To force that, build the question around this case and test, which I have written for the exam. Case: a resident physician has offloaded her drug-interaction knowledge onto a phone app she consults at the bedside, but the app's recommendations are rewritten nightly by a team she never sees, so what she will say at 9 a.m. can change by 9 p.m. without her having revised anything. Test, which I am calling the endorsement condition: a resource belongs to a subject's cognitive system only if its outputs are answerable to her reasons, not merely available to her behavior.

The question should require students to (a) apply the endorsement condition to Otto's notebook and to the resident, saying what it implies in each case; (b) decide whether the condition is a criterion of cognition or a criterion of agency, and whether that distinction matters for the thesis; and (c) say what an extended-cognition theorist ought to concede if the condition is not a criterion of cognition, and whether that concession leaves the thesis with any distinctive content. Students may invoke the standard objections without citing sources; marks go for the moves, not the names. Have the model answer take a definite position rather than surveying the literature, and have the marking scheme separate an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain, with a line for each point on how students typically miss it. Write in American English.
