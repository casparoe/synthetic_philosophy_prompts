I have just come out of a long argument with a friend who thinks historical materialism collapses into a dilemma: take the claim that the economic base explains everything else literally, and it is absurd, since no one believes that metaphysics or musical form is caused by the mode of production; take it loosely, and it amounts to the banal claim that material conditions constrain what a society can do, which nobody denies. I think the dilemma is false, and that the defensible version of the view is a gradient thesis rather than a binary one: superstructural elements differ systematically in how sensitive they are to the material base, and the gradient tracks how directly an element mediates the reproduction of material life, with property law and military organization strongly base-sensitive, metaphysics and musical form weakly so, and everything else ordered in between. Suppose I have funding for four researchers for three years, working on the conceptual and historical side rather than the quantitative side. Which open questions and lines of work should they prioritize, and in what order? I care less about the list than about the defense: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable for philosophers and intellectual historians rather than econometricians, which are already crowded, and what evidence or argument over the next few years should move a question up or down the list. I would also like to know which parts of the standard debate are dead ends under this framing.
