I'm a retired chemist — forty years of running columns and writing papers — who's spent the last few years reading philosophy instead, and I've landed on a picture of creativity that I'd like you to poke holes in before I inflict it on my reading group. Here it is: (1) a variation only counts as creative against a background of sameness, so you need some recognizable style for a difference to even register; (2) concepts aren't checklists of necessary and sufficient conditions, they're more like banks of knobs, and a concept just is the set of turns it tolerates (I think I stole this one from Hofstadter's Chopin essay); (3) so creative work is exploration of a space that's already laid out — even the giant leaps are long jumps inside the space, not exits from it; (4) but I don't think there's any essense to a style or a genre either, what counts as "the same" is itself up for grabs depending on where you're standing; (5) all the same, I'm convinced creative achievement is graded objectively, that some variations are genuinely deeper than others and not merely different from them. Which of these actually conflict, how bad is each conflict, and what's the cheapest way to patch it? Rank them by severity instead of telling me they all pull on each other equally, and say if any of the conflicts are only apparent.
