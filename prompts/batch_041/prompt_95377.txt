I am a retired physicist. Most of my working life was spent on inverse problems — remote sensing first, then seismic imaging — and I have come to think in a particular idiom. I ask whether a parameter is identifiable from the data at all; whether the problem is ill-posed; whether two descriptions that fit the data equally well are genuine rivals or merely two ways of writing down the same state, as a choice of gauge is; and what work is being done by the constraints I impose when I regularise. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy on my own, without a teacher and without any formal training in the subject, and I have run into Quine's thesis of the indeterminacy of translation.

As I understand it — and please correct me where I have it wrong — Quine imagines a linguist who has nothing to go on but the observable behaviour of the speakers, hears the utterance gavagai when a rabbit runs past, and finds that the totality of that behaviour never decides between a manual that renders the word as rabbit and a manual that renders it as undetached rabbit part or as momentary rabbit stage. The two manuals agree on every fact about what the speakers will say and do, in every circumstance, and yet, Quine insists, they say different things. He generalises the point to reference itself, which he thinks can be permuted wholesale by the device of a proxy function, so that the objects of a theory may be shuffled while every sentence of the theory keeps its truth value. He connects this to his earlier attack on the analytic-synthetic distinction, and he takes the evidence base to be the community's dispositions to verbal behaviour rather than anything inner.

What I would like from you is a translation followed by a residue report. Take that thesis and restate it as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary: identifiability and non-identifiability, ill-posedness, observational equivalence, gauge freedom and reparameterisation, degeneracy, regularisation and the priors that regularisation smuggles in, and the difference between a model that is false and a model whose parameters have no unique best value. Be explicit about what plays the role of the data, what plays the role of the parameter space, and what the claim that there is no fact of the matter comes to when it is said in those terms.

Then tell me what does not survive the translation. I want the residue sorted into three piles: what carries over without loss, what carries over only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all.

I have a particular suspicion and would like you to address it directly. In my trade, when two descriptions are empirically indistinguishable I am inclined to say either that they are the same description in different coordinates, in which case nothing is at stake, or that my parameters are simply not identified by the data I happen to have, in which case more or better data will settle the matter and the ambiguity is a defect of the experiment rather than a feature of the world. Quine appears to want a third thing: an ambiguity that no possible further evidence could remove, and which is nevertheless an ambiguity in meaning rather than a mere change of bookkeeping. Can my vocabulary express that third thing at all? If it cannot, tell me whether the fault lies with my framework, which may have no room for a notion of meaning richer than predictive equivalence, or with Quine's argument, which may be trading on a distinction that does less work than it appears to. I should also like to know what becomes, in my terms, of the connection he draws to the analytic-synthetic distinction, and whether his behaviourism is load-bearing or merely an artefact of the psychology of his time — if the evidence base were widened, would the indeterminacy shrink, or would it survive intact?

Finally, give me your verdict: once translated, is the thesis a discovery about the limits of inference, or an artefact of a philosophical vocabulary I do not share? I am not asking for a reading list or for references — I have no library to hand, and I want the argument rather than the scholarship. Write as though I can follow any amount of technical argument in my own field and none at all in yours.
