ok so the unilateralist's curse has been living in my head rent free and I want a poem out of it. you know the setup: a bunch of people can each unilaterally do some thing that lands on everybody, each of them goes ahead whenever they privately reckon it's net positive, and so as the group grows it gets close to certain that someone pulls the trigger even when it's a disaster, because it only takes one person whose estimate is off in the optimistic direction. the part everyone forgets is that its symmetric -- if the thing is actually good, the same machinery basically guarantees it happens too, so it's less a curse than a rule that the group ends up acting on whoever's estimate is the most extreme. I want both halves in the poem.

form: not a villanelle, not a sonnet, I've seen those done to death. give me a ghazal, or ghazal-shaped -- a run of couplets, each one standing on its own and not narrating into the next, every couplet closing on the same refrain word or phrase (pick one that can carry the weight and hold it), and let the last couplet turn the refrain on the speaker so I feel the I as just one more of the many. the reason is the whole point: the couplets not leaning on each other is the un-coordination, each couplet is one agent deciding alone with no idea what the others are doing, and the fact that they all land on the same refrain anyway IS the curse. if your couplets start building a story or depending on each other you've written the wrong poem.

get the mechanics right enough that someone who knows the argument nods instead of wincing. it's not that anybody's reckless or malicious -- everyone is trying to do the right thing -- the harm comes from the agent who acts being the one selected for having the estimate that was furthest off in the dangerous direction; and that same selection is exactly why the good version also gets taken, so the poem mustn't be able to end as a clean warning. work in, if you can, the thought that the one who was surest was the one who was wrong, and the suspicion that you can't tell whether this is a curse or just the world's way of letting the extreme estimate win. don't name any authors or papers, I want it to stand on its own.

and don't resolve it at the end. the obvious fix -- nobody moves until they know the others would move too -- is unavailable to any single agent precisely because not knowing the others is the problem, so if the poem reaches for that fix it has to show the hand closing on nothing.

since a poem has no verdict to hand down: after the verse, add a short note, maybe 3-5 sentences, on where the poem takes liberties with the idea or leans on a contested reading of it. be committed -- make definite choices in the poem and say why you made them, don't hedge or hand me three alternate drafts to choose from.
