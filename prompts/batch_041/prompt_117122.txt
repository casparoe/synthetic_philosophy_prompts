I am a second-year PhD student applying for a one-year fellowship, and I need a two-page prospectus (roughly 1000 words). Here is my half-formed idea.

The dispute over whether qualia are an incoherent posit has been conducted for decades as though it were one question with a yes-or-no answer, and I suspect that is a large part of why it keeps ending in a standoff. I want to argue that the term bundles at least three separable commitments, and that the incoherence charge has a different fate with respect to each. First, qualia as ineffable: states whose character cannot be conveyed or reported. Second, qualia as intrinsic, where intrinsic is itself doing at least three distinct jobs, meaning non-relational, or non-intentional (not identical with, or exhausted by, representational content), or non-functional (not individuated by causal or inferential role). Third, qualia as epistemically privileged: properties known by acquaintance, with respect to which first-person judgements are authoritative. My suspicion is that the familiar battery of intuition pumps about changing tastes and shifted colour experience are, at bottom, arguments about the reliability of introspection, and so can at most bear on the third commitment or on our epistemic access to the second; whereas any genuinely conceptual argument, if one exists, targets only one reading of intrinsic. A second suspicion concerns the charge itself: incoherent needs a criterion. A posit can be perfectly intelligible and simply fail to exist, in which case the interesting charge is falsity, not incoherence; or it can be unintelligible, in which case it is not even a candidate for existence and cannot be denied in the ordinary sense at all. I want to know what is left of the incoherence charge once the term has been split and the criterion made explicit.

Please turn this into a prospectus containing: a precise statement of the question, sharp enough to have a determinate answer; an account of why the existing positions leave it open, presented as positions and arguments rather than as a literature review, since I will supply the citations myself; the method, saying what I would actually do, in what order, and what kind of argumentative work each stage does; the criterion of incoherence I would adopt, and how I would defend it against the objection that it simply assumes what the qualia theorist denies; the possible outcomes and which considerations would decide between them; what would count as the project failing; and an honest assessment of whether a fellowship year and two pages are enough or whether the question must be narrowed to be tractable.
