I keep having the same argument about multiple realizability and it never resolves. The functionalist line: mental kinds are fixed by causal-functional role, so they can be realized in indefinitely many substrates, which blocks reduction of psychology to neuroscience. The reply: the alleged one-to-many mapping is an artifact of mismatched grain — mental kinds individuated coarsely, realizing states finely, so of course many physical states fall under one mental kind. Both sides grant the other's premises and still don't move.

Here is my hypothesis; test it rather than restate it. I think two disputes are running under one name. One is about kinds: a metaphysical thesis about the relation between mental and physical properties. The other is about models: that two neural descriptions pick out the same mechanism, or that a single computational description survives translation into two vocabularies. Someone asserting multiple realization may mean the first and be heard as asserting the second. A colleague and I live this — we study what we both call the same computation in two different model systems, and I call it a second model while she calls it a second realization.

Diagnose the impasse. Is the two-questions hypothesis right, or is this a genuine disagreement about how properties are individuated, a verbal dispute about 'realize', or two sides answering different questions under one heading? Say what each side would have to concede for the debate to move, and whether anything — a formal result, an empirical finding, a sharpened distinction — could count as progress, or whether the stall is well founded.
