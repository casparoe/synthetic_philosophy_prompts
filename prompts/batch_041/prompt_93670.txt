I run security and access-control stuff for a living -- I think in perimeters, ACLs, tokens, roles, rate limits, audit logs, default-deny, zero trust, revocation. I have basically no philosophy background, but I've been reading about the open borders debate and I want to see it in my own terms.

So take the actual positions in that debate and restate them in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. I mean the real arguements, not a vibe: (i) the view that a political community owns its territory, and so has the same kind of say over who comes in that a household or a club has over who joins -- the right to exclude being the core of ownership rather than an add-on; (ii) the argument that a state isn't obliged to admit anyone and that if it does let people in it's doing them a favour, so it gets to set the terms, the way someone offering a stranger a room gets to make the house rules; (iii) the reply that this is circular, because the community that's supposed to hold the right is itself partly constituted by the very boundary it's supposed to be justified in drawing, so you can't let the insiders settle it; (iv) the view that what's wrong with a closed border isn't the refusal as such but the coercion -- the fences, the detention, the removal -- and that that's the thing needing justification; and (v) the worry that "the people own the country" treats a political community as a property-holder with something it can alienate, when nobody can sell their citizenship and the next generation never consented to anything.

Give me the mapping if it helps -- checkpoitn as gateway, visa as token, citizenship as role or group, quota as rate limit, asylum as an exception path, deportation as revocation plus forced teardown -- but the part I actually want is the residue report. What translates cleanly? What only crosses over with distortion, and exactly what did you have to fudge to get it across? What has no counterpart at all in my vocabulary?

A few things I suspect fall in that last bucket, and I want you to tell me if I'm right or if I'm just not seeing the mapping: who is allowed to write the ACL in the first place (in my world the owner is just given, but here the owner is the entire dispute); the gap between "unauthorized" and "unjust" (my vocabulary only has the first, and it defines it by the system's own rules, so it can't even ask whether the rules are legitimate); the fact that the things trying to cross have interests, which packets don't; and the fact that in politics the entrants, once admitted, become part of the authority that sets the rules, which no normal access-control system does.

Then the verdict: for each thing that didn't survive the translation, is that a hole in my vocabulary, or is it a sign the original claim was vaguer than it sounded?
