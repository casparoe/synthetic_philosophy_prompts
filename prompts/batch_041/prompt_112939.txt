ok, seminar write-up is due friday and i want a consistency check on my decision-theory commitments before i hand it in. here's roughly where ive landed:

(1) Completeness: for any two options an agent could face, theres a fact of the matter about how they compare — one is better, the other is better, or theyre equally good. I dont accept 'these two are just incomparable' as a final verdict. If your theory cant rank two live options, thats a bug, not a feature.

(2) Expected utility is the criterion of rational choice. But for some options theres no expected utility to maximize. Take the Pasadena game: you flip a fair coin until it lands heads; if the first heads comes on toss n, you get (-1)^n times 2^n divided by n dollars. So you lose $2 if its heads right away, win $2 if the first heads is on toss two, lose 8/3 if its on toss three, and so on. The expected payoff contributed by toss n is (-1)^n over n, and if you add those up in order of n you get a finite number — but the series is only conditionally convergent, so rearranging the terms can make it sum to any finite number you like, or to plus or minus infinity. So theres no order-independent sum. The expectation is undefined. Not zero, not that finite number, undefined.

(3) When the expectation is undefined, the honest verdict is silence, and that silence isnt a cop-out. It reflects something real: theres genuinely no fact of the matter about how the Pasadena game compares to a sure $0. Not that theyre on a par, not that both choices are permissible — just that theres nothing there to compare.

(4) Dominance is bedrock, and it doesnt need expected utilities at all: if A gives you at least as much as B in every possible outcome and strictly more in some, then A is rationally better than B.

(5) The Altadena game (i think thats the name) is just the Pasadena game with a dollar added to every payoff. So it dominates Pasadena outcome by outcome, and by (4) its strictly better than Pasadena. Anyone offered the choice should take Altadena.

(6) The right response to all this is to extend the theory — weak expectations, non-standard reals, something — so that it can rank options like these, instead of throwing up our hands.
