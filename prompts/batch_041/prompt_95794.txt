I teach a grad seminar on the ethics of immigration and I'm tired of teaching this as a lineup of -isms, so for next week I want to hand out a decision tree instead. The question is the double one: who counts as a refugee (does the category have moral teeth of its own, or should it just dissolve into 'forced migrant', or go further), and who has to take them in. Build it from the logical space — first the questions, then the positions that occupy the resulting cells.

Start from a commitment a student could hold without already knowing which position its going to land them in, and make every branch node a question, not a label. I want the tree to cut at the joints that actually do the work here, in particular: whether the wrong done to a turned-away refugee is a failure of rescue or the violation of a claim of justice; whether the receiving state has any right to exclude in the first place; whether the duty falls on the nearest state, the richest, the one that caused the displacement, or a global body allocating quotas; whether what's owed is permanent admission or temporary protection; and whether the ground is universal principle or the associative obligations of a particular political community.

Leaves should carry the name of the position (or 'unnamed') plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I need open borders, the liberal-nationalist compromise (a right to exclude plus a demanding refugee duty), the causal/reparative-responsibility view, temporary protection, and the extensionist who'd scrap the refugee category to appear as leaves; add whatever else you think the space demands. Where a cell is empty, say whether that's because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree can't actually tell apart rather than fudging it. It's for about 15 students, so keep it to something they can hold in their heads — six or seven branch nodes, eight or ten leaves.

Give it to me in four sections: 'The tree' (the branching structure, questions on the nodes), 'Leaf notes' (name plus awkward commitment, one line each), 'Empty cells' (which combinations nobody occupies and your diagnosis), and 'Can't be separated' (pairs the tree conflates, with a note on what extra question would split them).
