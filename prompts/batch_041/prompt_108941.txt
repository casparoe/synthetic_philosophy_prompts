ok, so i keep ending up in arguments about whether viruses are alive, and i've slowly realized i dont actually know what i'm asking for when i ask for a definiton of life. before i read any more hot takes on this, i want the spec. write me the desiderata for a definition of life — what any adequate one has to deliver, with the case behind each requirement.

here's the kind of thing i want covered, though add whatever i'm missing.

the cases it has to get right: viruses (and the awkward fact that a virus inside a cell looks like a hijacked piece of the cell); prions, the misfolded proteins that template other proteins into the same misfolded shape, with no metabolism and no nucleic acid, and which come in strains that get selected; viroids, the naked little RNA rings that infect plants; plasmids and transposons; mitochondria, which are bacteria that moved in and never left; spores and tardigrades in cryptobiosis, metabolism down to nothing and then back; a corpse, which is not alive but is still a lot of cellular activity for a while; a brain-dead body on a ventilator that still heals wounds and can carry a pregnancy to term; a mule, alive, sterile, last of its line; cancer cells in a dish, your own cells outliving you; fire; a crystal growing in a supersaturated solution; an autocatalytic chemical set where the molecules collectively make each other; a self-replicating bit of code; a lab-built cell with a fully synthesized genome. and a few i'm inventing because i think they break different things: (a) the mirror bug — something that does everything a bacterium does but with mirror-image chemistry, so none of our stains or instruments recognise it; (b) the one-off — an entity that maintains and repairs itself and responds to its surroundings for a thousand years, but never reproduces and has no relatives; (c) the chimera — an organism assembled out of parts that were each, on their own, not alive.

the distinctions it can't collapse: life vs alive vs organism vs living thing; a body that was alive vs a body that is alive; being alive as a property you either have or don't vs a matter of degree; life as a natural kind out there in the world vs a word we stipulate for our own purposes; the individual vs the lineage as the thing that bears the property (is a lone sterile organism less alive than one bacterium in a thriving species?); and whether the definition is telling us what life is or just how to tell.

the constraints it can't violate: don't make fire and hurricanes alive, even though they dissipate energy and hold their shape for a while; don't open the door so wide that computer viruses, crystals and clay templates walk in; don't require the whole body to stop before we call it death, since we're outnumbered by our own bacteria anyway; and if it's meant to be a definition of life and not of earth life, don't build in carbon and water.

then the part i actually care about. the definition is being asked to do several jobs at once and i suspect they pull apart: an astrobiologist wants one that lets a probe say yes or no about something on titan it has never seen; a doctor wants one that settles when a patient is gone; a biologist wants one that carves nature at the joints; a patent office wants one that says whether a synthesized genome is an invention or a discovery. say which pairs of these can't be had together, and whether the honest answer is that life is a word doing several jobs and no single definition can do them all. if that's the answer, say so — it's the answer i most want.

mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as the preference of one camp, flag any subset i should stop expecting to be jointly satisfiable, and tell me which single requirement does the most work at ruling candidates out. you can finish with a short scorecard of the usual contenders — the metabolism one, the self-organisation/autopoiesis one, the darwinian-evolution one that says life is a chemical system that sustains itself and can evolve, the viruses-are-alive crowd, and life-as-cluster-or-stipulation — but keep it brief, the criteria are what i'm here for. no citations or reading lists please, i can't look anything up, just the argument.
