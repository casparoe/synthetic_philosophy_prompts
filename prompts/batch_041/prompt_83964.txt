I am in a reading group on mind and machine, and one member keeps settling questions with a slogan he has adapted from Hofstadter's talk of strange loops: 'The self is not a thing, it is a loop that refers to itself.' He used it last week to argue that a perfect simulation of a person simply is that person, since the same loop would be running, and that the rest of us are in the grip of a thing-theory of the self we ought to have abandoned. I would like the menu rather than a verdict. Set out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making: what a 'loop' would have to be, in what sense it could 'refer to itself,' and whether the self is thereby being identified with a pattern, a process, a representation, a level of description, or a useful fiction. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or false; note which readings can be combined and which are incompatible; and say which of them, if any, would actually license his conclusion that a copy is the same person. Finally, tell me which reading he is most likely leaning on when he ends an argument with it, and which single question I should put to him to make him commit to one.
