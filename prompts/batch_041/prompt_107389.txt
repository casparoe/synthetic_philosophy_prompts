I am assembling a position statement for a graduate seminar on moral responsibility, and before I circulate it I want to know whether the commitments I have written down actually fit together. Here is what I hold.

1. Blaming is not merely an evaluative judgment about someone; it is an address, a demand for accountability that presents the wrongdoer as answerable to the blamer. A private belief that someone has done wrong, with no element of address in it, is not yet blame.

2. Because blame is an address of this kind, it can be performed only by someone who has the standing to make it. Standing is a condition on the act, not an optional courtesy that we may waive.

3. What standing amounts to is the equal standing of persons. A hypocrite, who blames another for a wrong she is herself guilty of, lacks standing because her blame implicitly claims a moral authority over the other that she has no title to: she is in no position to set herself above the person she addresses.

4. A victim of wrongdoing always has standing to blame the wrongdoer for it, even if the victim has committed the same kind of wrong against someone else. Having been wronged is itself a title to address the one who did it.

5. Standing is not itself a moral reason. A hypocrite's blame is not morally wrong; it merely misfires as an instance of moral address. The accusation may be perfectly true, and the wrongness of what the wrongdoer did is wholly unaffected by the standing of whoever points it out.

6. Someone who blames others for what he excuses in himself is doing something morally bad and is a fit target of blame for it, not merely someone whose blame fails to land.

7. One can blame the dead and the absent; blame does not require that its target hear it or acknowledge it.

8. The busybody who reproaches a stranger for conduct that is none of the busybody's concern also lacks standing to blame, and this is not a separate phenomenon from the hypocrite's lack of standing but the same one.

I suspect the package is less tidy than it looks, but I cannot see where the trouble lies, and I do not want to be told merely that every part of it is contested. Please audit it. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, state exactly what the two commitments cannot both mean, and rank the tensions by how much of the rest of the package depends on them. For each, give the cheapest revision that restores consistency and say what that revision costs elsewhere. Finally, tell me which single change would resolve the most at once, and whether that change is one I could make while keeping the spirit of the position rather than gutting it.
