Write a joke that turns on the mechanics of the dollar auction. In the standard setup a prize, classically a one-dollar bill, goes to the highest bidder, but both the highest and the second-highest bidder must pay the amount they bid; the second-highest pays and receives nothing. Two bidders who each reason that they have already sunk a bid and must therefore keep going can bid well past the prize's value and both end up worse off than if neither had played at all. Your joke should be funny to someone who actually understands this trap and the phenomenon of escalation of commitment; it should not be a generic joke about stubbornness, pride, or the sunk-cost fallacy in the abstract. The punchline should turn on the real structure of the game.

To keep this from being a retread of the textbook illustration, build the joke around a twist that is not part of the standard example. In your version, the prize is not a fixed sum of money but the right to serve as auctioneer of the following round, and the house skims a percentage of every bid into a fund that is what ultimately pays out the prize. The value of winning therefore depends on how much escalation the winner can extract from the next pair of bidders, and part of that value is financed by the losing bids of the current round. At least one bidder should see the trap clearly, understand that continuing is irrational, and still find that stopping is not a live option. Set the scene wherever you like, but let the setting do some work rather than serving as decoration.

Write the joke first, then a short commentary.

The Joke
Present the joke itself. It may be dialogue, narration, or a short scene, but it should read as a joke and land as one. Around 200 to 300 words.

Commentary: Why It Works
Explain which features of the dollar auction and of escalation of commitment the joke exploits, and identify precisely why the punchline depends on the actual mechanics rather than on a vague sense that bidding wars get out of hand. Note explicitly how the twist you were given changes the strategic situation compared with the ordinary dollar auction, and what that adds to the humor. Around 150 to 200 words.
