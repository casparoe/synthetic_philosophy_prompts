Two months ago I got talked into joining the board of the housing co-op we live in — small place, 40 units, bylaws from the mid-80s that have been amended piecemeal ever since. Now I have to write the board's memo for Thursday's meeting and I'm in way over my head.

Here's the mess. Our bylaws let the board remove an officer (not a member, just an officer — president, treasurer, secretary) "for cause" by a two-thirds vote. In 2011 somebody added a sentence defining it: "Cause means a criminal conviction, fraud, or a material breach of fiduciary duty." No "including," no "such as" — just "means."

Our president, D—, did something that isn't any of those three things. Short version: he forwarded the board's private deliberations about a member's eviction to three other members, one of whom is the person being evicted, and he did it to build support for his side. Nobody thinks he stole anything or committed a crime. Some of us think he torched the trust the board runs on; two of us have stopped speaking to him. There's a faction that wants him gone and, honestly, that faction is the one that got me elected, which I'm trying to keep out of my head.

The old guard says: the text is the text, "means" means means, and if we don't like it we can amend the bylaws at the annual meeting in June. I get that. But read it literally and the board couldn't remove an officer who punched a member at a meeting or posted everyones rent rolls online — surely the people who wrote "for cause" didn't mean to leave us helpless against that. Also, and this is the part I keep tripping on, the 2011 minutes have the guy who moved the amendment saying he wanted the list to be "a guide, not a cage" — but what actually got adopted reads like a closed list. Do we go with what they wrote or what they plainly wanted? There's precedent too: three years back the board removed a treasurer for something not on that list and nobody challenged it.

Write me the memo I'll circulate Thursday. I need a concrete recommendation — remove him, don't, or something in between with a date attached — plus the reasoning, and please give the old-guard textualist position a fair shot rather than steamrolling it. I don't have a law library and I'm not going to go look anything up, so reason from the documents I've described, not from cases I can't check. And be straight with me about which parts of this are actually settled among people who do this for a living and which parts are genuinely contested — I don't want a confident answer that's really just one camp's view dressed up as the obvious one.
