My town is putting up a memorial for the local guys who died in the war, and somehow I've ended up as the one giving the dedication speech. The catch: I'm an actual pacifist, not the vague "war is bad, mmmkay" kind. I think killing is wrong even in a just war, even in self-defense, and I dont think soldiers get a moral pass just for following orders. The crowd will be half veterans and Gold Star families, half people who know me and know exactly what I think, and the local paper is going to print the whole thing. So I need a speech that honors these dead men and their families without me standing up there implying their deaths were a noble sacrifice for a cause, or that they'd still be alive if everyone were like me and just let the other side win. Warm, specific, not a lecture and not a sermon, and please no poems or famous quotations — I'll botch the wording and someone in the front row will notice.

I want it built around a distinction I keep chewing on: pacifism versus pacificism. Pacifism is a claim about me — that I wont kill and won't ask anyone else to kill on my behalf. Pacificism is the empirical bet that war is a social institution we could actually abolish, like dueling or debtors' prison. I hold the first and I'm honestly agnostic about the second, and I want the speech to feel that gap — I'm not claiming these deaths would have been avoided if everyone shared my convictions, and I'm not claiming they wouldn't. Also dont let me fall back on "violence never solved anything," because that's just false and I don't believe it.

Five minutes, spoken aloud. My uncle, who served, is in the front row. The hard part is that honoring them without calling the sacrifice worth it is going to read as a betrayal to half that room, so I need you to find the moves that actually thread it instead of dodging it.
