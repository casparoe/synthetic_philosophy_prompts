I'm taking a seminar on decision theory and the foundations of AI safety, and my assignment is to produce the document that two camps in the alignment literature should have written years ago instead of arguing past each other. The first camp holds that the only respectable standard for an alignment proposal is a guarantee that survives the worst case — no reliance on a prior over environments, failure modes, or deployment contexts, since such priors are part of what alignment is meant to secure. The second camp holds that this standard is either vacuous, if the worst case is taken over everything, or self-defeating, if the pursuit of worst-case robustness costs so much average-case performance that the systems we care about are never built, and that proposals should instead be ranked by expected performance under a credence distribution over the same space.

Please write the joint statement the two camps could co-sign. I want a numbered section of claims both sides accept — about what an alignment proposal is, what it is for, what would count as a counterexample to each camp's standard, and which decision-relevant consequences each side already grants the other — followed by a disagreements table in which each row gives one residual disagreement in wording that the side it favours would accept as its own, the crux (the sub-question whose settlement would move at least one side), and, for that crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about the outcome recorded in advance. Where the dispute turns out to be verbal — for instance, if the two camps mean different things by a guarantee, or by the probability in expected performance — say which part, and why it is verbal rather than substantive.

Please run the whole thing against one concrete case rather than a survey of slogans: a developer must choose between two designs, one whose harm is provably bounded by a constant across a stated class of environments but sits near that bound almost everywhere in the class, and one whose harm is negligible across almost all of that class but grows without bound on a residual subset of it, where an explicit credence distribution over the class is available but the class cannot be enumerated. State what each camp prescribes for this case and exactly where the prescriptions diverge.
