Two weeks out from a talk at our formal epistemology workshop and I want the Q&A prep sheet, not encouragement.

What I'm defending: van Fraassen's Reflection principle isn't a real norm. The idea is that if you're certain you're going to end up with credence x in A, you should already have credence x in A. My line is that it survives only in a hobbled form: it applies when you're certain that your future self (i) has all the evidence you have now and then some, and (ii) is still in possession of the reasoning you're running right now. Call that the no-loss version. On the no-loss version, all the stock counterexamples -- the pill that makes you 0.99 confident in something you know is baseless, the case where you know you'll forget the very argument you're now making -- turn out to fail (i) or (ii), so they were never counterexamples to anything I want. What I give up is the version where you're supposed to defer to a future self you know is going to be epistemically worse than you. Thats the trade.

The room, roughly twelve people: eight or nine accuracy-first Bayesians who think scoring rules and Dutch books close every question and want a representation theorem before they'll engage, and one of them will keep saying Reflection is just the martingale property wearing a hat, which I never know what to say to; two who work on imprecise probability and think a single real-valued credence is a fiction; one van Fraassen loyalist who'll insist the principle was never about evidence but about the value of your future opinions; and one person who works on self-locating belief and brings up Sleeping Beauty every time, including once during someone else's job talk.

Give me: the objections I'll actually get from this room, in their vocabulary, ordered by how much damage they do; a two-minute reply to each; and for each reply, where it's soft. Tell me which one I should concede on the spot and say so in my opening. And tell me the one I'm hoping nobody asks -- I have a feeling it's about whether my no-loss conditions are even checkable from the inside, since (i) and (ii) are both claims about my future self that I have to be certain of right now.

One specific thing to flag: somebody in that room is definately going to say that if Reflection goes, deference to experts goes with it, because deference is just Reflection with the expert's credence swapped in for your future credence. If that one lands, I'm done, so give me my best answer and tell me honestly how much of it is hand-waving.
