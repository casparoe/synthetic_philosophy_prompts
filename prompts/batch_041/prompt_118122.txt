I've been mulling over a picture of thinking that keeps showing up in discussions of machine intelligence: one person who can't crack a hard problem alone writes a note to a large crew of helpers, each helper is no smarter than that person, no message passed between them may run longer than a page, but the crew can be as large as you like and can keep working as long as it takes. The claim is that anything a crew can eventually settle this way is something we can treat as thinkable in principle, so a very smart machine can be reasoned about as this arrangement scaled up. I suspect the picture quietly assumes something about how a hard question comes apart into easy ones, and I want a concrete case that makes the assumption visible. Give me an example of a task where the arrangement fails for an interesting reason — not because a helper is careless or dishonest, but because of the shape of the task itself — and then explain what that example shows the picture was assuming all along.
