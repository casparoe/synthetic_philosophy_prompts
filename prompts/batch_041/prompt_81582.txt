I have been reading the rationalist blogs and their forums for years, and the frame problem is the one topic that never resolves when it comes up. One camp treats it as a technical question — stating, for any given action, what does not change — and considers it essentially solved by nonmonotonic and default reasoning; another camp, descended from the phenomenological critique of classical AI, insists that is not the frame problem at all, and that the real problem is how an agent decides which features of a situation matter in the first place, a problem it regards as untouched. My hypothesis is that the impasse is largely a naming collision, with 'solved' and 'unsolved' being true of two different questions that share one label. I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this a verbal dispute about what the term denotes, a substantive disagreement about whether relevance can be formalized at all, or a hidden empirical question — whether learning relevance from data, as contemporary models appear to, solves the problem or merely relocates it? And given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a formal result, a working demonstration, a conceptual distinction — or should we expect the two camps to keep talking past each other indefinitely?
