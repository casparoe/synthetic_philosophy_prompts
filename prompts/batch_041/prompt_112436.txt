I'm a retired metallurgist — 35 years on alloys and phase diagrams — and since I retired I've been reading philosophy, mostly the stuff about ordinary objects and what there made of. I keep getting stuck. Everyone fights about the statue and the lump of bronze like it's the only case that matters, and half the moves feel gerrymandered to save one intuition about that one example. Before I read another paper I want the actual spec. What does a theory of material constitution have to be able to do before I should take it seriously?

Give me the requirements list, each with the case or consideration that forces it. Some things I'd want on it, though you tell me if they belong:

- the statue/lump case, the ship of Theseus, the cat that loses its tail (you're left with a tailless cat and a "cat minus a tail" in the same place — do we have two things or one?).
- cases from my old world. Melt the statue down and recast it into a different statue: I say the statue is gone and the bronze survives, but I want the theory to make me say why, not just assume it. And annealing — same lump, same metal, different grain structure. Is the pre-anneal lump the same lump as the post-anneal one, and does anything in the theory even let me ask that?
- the fact that atoms wander. Bronze oxidizes and diffuses at the surface; over enough time the matter in a statue is largely not the matter it started with, and I'm told every atom in my body has probably been swapped out over the years. If the statue and I persist through total turnover of our matter, then whatever makes us the things we are isn't the matter. Does a theory have to swallow that, or is denying it respectable?

Then the part I actually want: which of these are non-negotiable and which are just habits of one camp (I suspect "the statue is not identical to the lump" is doing alot of unearned work, but I could be wrong). And if some of the requirements can't all be met at once — I half expect they can't — say which ones collide and what you have to give up. Skip the scorecard of who-says-what, I'll do that myself once I have the list; but if one single requirement knocks out most of the contenders, tell me which.

Plain language please, I'm new at this.
