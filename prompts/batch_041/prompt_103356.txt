I've been chewing on the old sea-battle puzzle for weeks now (aristotle, the one about whether a battle tomorrow is already settled by whether the statement about it is true today) and I cant get it out of my head, so I want it as a poem. Make it read like a harbour bulletin or a shipping forecast. Here's the constraint I actually care about: every single line has to work two ways at once, as a prediction made tonight about tomorrow and as a report made tomorrow about tonight. So no line can give itself away with a tense, and no words like tomorrow or yesterday or soon or already or by now. The first line and the last line have to be identical word for word, so the whole thing just goes round and you cant tell which end you're reading from. 12-16 lines, no fixed form, no rhyme needed. I want someone who knows the puzzle to feel the squeeze in it (that if it's true tonight it was always going to happen, but if it isn't true tonight then what was it ever about), and I want the thing to stay readable as either a prophecy or a memory right to the end. Please dont close it off with a line about how we cant know the future, thats the one move I dont want. And it should sound like weather and boats, not a philosophy essay in verse.
