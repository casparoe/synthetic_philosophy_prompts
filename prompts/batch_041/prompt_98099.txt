The interesting wrinkle is he doesnt think *every* ontological dispute is verbal — he says disputes about what's fundamental, or about what carves nature at the joints, are genuine, and the line is whether the disputed term admits multiple "equally eligible" or "equally natural" interpretations. That's the part I want to grill him on.

Can you draft me 8–10 interview questions that go past him just re-explaining the view? I need:

- at least one question on the self-undermining worry — if the whole thing is verbal, then is his own claim that these disputes are verbal itself just another verbal claim, and does stating his view require a privileged quantifier he says nobody has? — plus a follow-up aimed at the answer he'll most likely give.
- at least one on the criterion: how does he actually tell a verbal dispute from a real one without already assuming his conclusion? Why isn't "is there a Higgs boson" verbal on his lights? What does "equally natural interpretation" even mean in practice, and isn't "naturalness" just quietly doing the metaphysics he claims to be deflating? — plus a follow-up for his likely reply.
- at least one on whether the view is really a disguised relativism or anti-realism about existence, or neither, since "equally eligible" starts to sound a lot like "no fact of the matter" — plus a follow-up.
- and one more, because "merely verbal" always sounds like "not important": get him to say what's actually still at stake in one of these disputes once you've called it verbal.

Keep them concrete and make him commit to things. I don't want any "what got you into philosophy" filler.
