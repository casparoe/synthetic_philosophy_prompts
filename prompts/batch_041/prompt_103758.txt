I'm in a graduate seminar on the philosophy of social science, and we've spent two weeks circling the question of whether culture or values can figure as a cause rather than as something to be explained away. The Weberian template keeps coming up — ascetic Protestant convictions somehow helping to produce a this-worldly rationalizing economic ethic — but everyone in the room reads it differently, and I've started to suspect the disagreement is really about what it would take for a value to be a cause at all, rather than about the historical case. I'd like you to help me sort this out by ranking candidate mechanisms.

Please take the following list and arrange it into tiers by how well each mechanism, if it obtained, would vindicate the claim that a shared value or cultural complex is a genuine cause of social outcomes rather than a mere correlate, a rationalization, or a redescription. For each, say in a sentence or two what would have to be true of the world for it to be doing real explanatory work, and where it risks collapsing back into the circularity worry — values inferred from the very conduct they are invoked to explain. The candidates: (1) internalization, on which individuals adopt the value and act on it as a reason; (2) selection, on which the culture moves no one's reasons but changes which practices survive and get copied; (3) equilibrium selection, on which the culture picks out one of several stable arrangements without causing the arrangement's existence; (4) constitution, on which the value does not cause the action so much as make it the kind of action it is, so that the causal question is misframed; (5) a mechanism I've been calling semantic scaffolding, in which the culture supplies the concepts without which the agent could not so much as form the intention, so that the culture is a precondition of the intention rather than a cause of it; and (6) an account on which the value affects the outcome only through the fact that everyone expects everyone else to hold it, so that the content of the value is nearly idle and only the common knowledge matters. I care most about whether the ranking shifts depending on whether we want a general theory of cultural causation or a verdict on the Weber case in particular, so please flag any point where the two come apart.
