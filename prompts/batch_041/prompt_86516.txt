You are a philosopher of mind and animal-ethics specialist preparing a teaching instrument for a one-day workshop (ethicists, two biologists, one entomologist) on whether insect sentience should change practice. Work through the logical space carefully before you write anything, then produce the finished instrument.

Write a decision tree that takes a participant from the starting point "I assign some non-negligible probability to the claim that at least some insects are sentient" to a named position about what follows for how we treat insects. Every internal node must be a question the participant can answer without knowing which position it leads to; no node may be a disguised version of "are you a utilitarian?" or "do you care about animals?"

The nodes should include, at least: whether sentience comes in degrees and whether there is a threshold beneath which there is nothing to weigh; whether differences between species enter as a lower probability of sentience at all or as a multiplier on how much a given episode of suffering counts; whether the decision rule is expected-value maximisation, a precautionary or maximin rule, or a rule that caps how far a very small probability may move us toward a very large number; whether credences are precise or imprecise; whether we count probable insect-lives or expected sentient-lives, and whether death counts at all for animals whose lives may contain little net welfare; whether our interventions change the number of insect-lives or merely redistribute them into a refilled niche; whether consistency requires that whatever rule you adopt here also govern other small-probability, huge-stakes cases; and whether the moral unit is the individual insect, the colony, or the species.

Leaves should be named positions or "unnamed", each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Include positions on which the expected-value calculation is decisive and licenses large revisions to pest control, insect farming and research; on which the numbers argument is a trap, since the same structure would license comparable revisions on the strength of comparable speculation; on which the priority is research into insect nociception and behavioural indicators rather than action; and on which insects are a distraction from the vertebrate welfare we know about — but go beyond these four.

Flag every pair of positions your tree cannot separate, and say whether the gap is nothing, a question outside the tree, or a disagreement about fact. Mark any unoccupied combination of answers and diagnose it as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Twelve to sixteen nodes, about ten leaves, roughly two pages. Do not cite literature or supply references, and do not assert specific insect population figures — leave numbers as placeholders the workshop will supply.
