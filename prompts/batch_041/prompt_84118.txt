Last month I froze when someone collapsed on the street and a total stranger just took over, and ever since I've been weirdly obsessed with wether that says anything about who I am -- do I have 'character', or just whatever the situation hands me? I'm running a session for our department reading group (about 15 grad students, 90 minutes) and I want them to feel the situationist problem in their bones instead of hearing me lecture about it.

Here's my rough idea, please make it actually work. Before the session I quietly split them into two groups with a made-up reason to be in a hurry (one group told the next room's session started five minutes ago and they'll miss the good bit, the other told it starts whenever). On the way over, stage a small mishap -- dropped folders, a spilled drink, someone clearly struggling with a heavy door -- and have a helper note who stops. Then bring everyone back and do the interesting part: get them to bet points on which of their groupmates will help the *next* time, and run a second staged mishap with the hurry-up framing flipped. The thing I want them to run into is that their bets are basically useless -- knowing who helped last time barely predicts who helps this time. Then in the debrief push them to notice what that does and doesn't do to the idea that people have stable virtues. I'd like it to leave them with a real fork: either traits are 'local' (honest about money but not about papers), or they're not global at all but more like if-then rules -- this person stands up when it's cheap to and folds when it isn't -- and I want the debrief to map the moments of the activity onto those two options.

Give me a timeline, the exact wording for the two hurry framings, and how the helper records things without it feeling creepy. Also tell me where this will predictably fall apart the first time (I can already see people gaming it, and I worry the hurry-up framing is too obvious), and be honest about what the setup can't show -- I assume it can't tell me whether traits exist in the philosophical sense, only whether they predict behaviour well. Runnable in 90 minutes, no budget, no real money.
