Write a dialogue between two philosophers who are both fully competent and who genuinely engage with each other's points, on a Frankfurt-style case with a twist. The case: Marisol, who distrusts her own willpower, has a device implanted that will force her to donate her year-end bonus to a relief fund if, and only if, she is about to decide to keep it. On the day, she decides to donate on her own initiative, and the device never fires. The first philosopher holds that moral responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise, and argues that this case, like the standard ones, leaves that ability intact: the relevant ability is located earlier, at the moment she chose to have the device installed, and she could have declined then. The second holds that responsibility attaches to how the action was actually produced — to its reasons-responsiveness and its source in the agent's own deliberation — and that the twist, a device she herself commissioned and keyed to her own anticipated weakness, strengthens her side rather than weakening it, because the action flows from her settled character rather than from external interference. Let them press each other: does relocating the alternative possibility to the moment of installation preserve the principle or trivialize it? Is the agent's actual deliberation reasons-responsive in the sense that matters, and is that responsiveness enough to ground responsibility even when no alternative was open at the time of action? Does the self-imposed nature of the device make her more responsible, less, or neither? Neither character should be a strawman, and neither should appeal to the published literature; have them argue from the case and from first principles. Close with a brief note identifying which exchange is most vulnerable to being a trick of framing rather than a real argument, and what consideration would shift the balance between the two positions.
