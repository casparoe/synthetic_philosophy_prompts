I'm a second-year PhD student in cog sci (my background is mostly philosophy of mind) and I'm trying to write a section on Molyneux's question — you know, the old one where a man born blind has learned to tell a cube from a sphere by touch, then gets his sight, and is asked, before he touches them, which one is which. I keep hitting a wall and I'm hoping you can get me unstuck.

Here's my problem. As far as I can tell this was always an empirical question, and it's basically been answered. There are the famous cataract cases from the 1700s where people who'd been blind since birth got their sight and were totally lost — couldn't tell the shapes apart, had to learn. There's the more recent work where adults blind from birth get their sight restored and go through the same thing. And then the sensory-substitution stuff, where a camera feeds a little grid of electrodes on someone's tongue or back, and after a while the users report they've stopped feeling tingles and started perceiving shapes as being out there in space. So the pattern seems clear: cross-modal shape recognition is learned, not innate, and Molyneux's original answer (that the man couldn't do it) turns out to be right. Empiricism 1, nativism 0. So why does this still get treated as a live open problem in the philosophy literature instead of a nice little historical case study? Is it just inertia in the field, or am I missing something about what the question was ever asking?

Two things that bug me. First, I have a hunch that the really tempting answer here is something like: vision and touch share a common spatial content, so obviously he could match them up, it's not about learning at all. But if that's right then my whole scoreboard falls apart, because that answer is a yes, and I don't see how a yes gets you nativism. Second, does the sensory-substitution case even count as evidence, given the user isn't using their eyes at all?

Can you act as an expert on the philosophy of perception and on the history of the empiricism/nativism debate, and take this seriously rather than just reassuring me? Then tell me honestly where my framing goes wrong — if it does — and what the question looks like once you strip out my assumptions.
