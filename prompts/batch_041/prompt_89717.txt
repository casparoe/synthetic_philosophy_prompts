Compose a mock internal document: the founding prospectus of the Institute for Historical Health, a public-private body that advises ministries, universities, museums and firms on the correct dosage of history for the sake of life. Roughly 750 words, in the flattened, self-congratulatory register of institutional prose, with section headings and at least one table. It must read as though written by an organisation with no idea that it is being satirised: no winking, no editorial asides, no acknowledgment that a joke is in progress.

The Institute is a literal-minded misreading of Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life, the second of the Untimely Meditations. Its entire framework consists in taking the essay's medical vocabulary of health, sickness, dosage and convalescence as a planning problem, and the comedy has to come from that. Every measure it adopts should be recognisably a faithful application of something in the essay, while the sum of those measures reproduces precisely the condition the essay was written against. The error must lie in the application, never in the understanding: a reader who knows the essay well should be able to check clause after clause against the text, find nothing the essay would deny, and still want to throw the document across the room.

Requirements:

- Three divisions, one per mode of history, each with a mandate that is simultaneously correct and pathological. The monumental division serves clients who must act and build, and does so by curating exemplary figures into usable, enlarged and conveniently imprecise exemplars; the prospectus should be entirely untroubled by what this does to the past it claims to honour. The antiquarian division serves clients who must be rooted, and its mandate is total preservation, including a standing refusal to authorise the demolition or revision of anything whatsoever. The critical division serves clients who suffer and need release, and issues formal condemnations of the inheritance; describe it in such a way that it never notices the client is itself the product of what is being condemned.
- The unhistorical is administered. Forgetting must appear as a budgeted line item, with an allocation formula, a defined minimum unhistorical reserve, and a note on the consequences of underdrawing. Nothing should suggest that anyone has asked whether forgetting can be scheduled.
- The supra-historical is a premium service. Art and religion are named as its instruments and it is priced accordingly.
- The essay's diagnosis of an over-historical age surfaces as a marketing opportunity. Clients are described as latecomers burdened by a surfeit of inherited material and by a corrosive second-hand sophistication; the Institute treats this as the demand side of its own supply, and the risk section should concede, without embarrassment, that its business model improves as the disease deepens.
- One clause converts the essay's claim that only strong natures can bear history into an access-control policy: critical history is available only to certified robust clients, everyone else is placed on a monumental maintenance dose. Present this as prudence rather than elitism, and let the reader supply the objection.
- Include the table: recommended historical dosage by client type, with a column for the pathological presentation each dosage is meant to prevent.
- Close with a peroration on cultural renewal that a reader of the essay's final pages will recognise as a mangled version of Nietzsche's own hopes for his culture.

No citations, no bibliography, no quotations from the essay, and no references to real institutions or living persons. The piece should be legible as irony to someone who has never opened Nietzsche, and rewarding to someone who has.
