I sit on my town's heritage commission — I'm the resident member, appointed because I run the farmers' market, and I have no training in history, just a lot of evenings spent reading about how many "ancient" customs turn out to have been assembled quite recently. Next month we vote between two applicants for our "living heritage" designation, which carries a small annual grant and the right to use the town crest. The first is the Kettle Parade: our own files show the chamber of commerce invented it in 1974 as a tourist draw, complete with a bogus medieval costume; it lapsed in the early nineties and was revived a few years ago by newcomers, and it is now genuinely loved and is the one event where the town's older families and its newer immigrant families actually mix. The second is the Ember Walk, which its backers call "the town's ancient tradition"; the earliest trace we can find is an 1893 photograph, and the ritual — a dusk lantern walk along the old canal — looks to have been cobbled together around then by a mill owner's wife out of a Welsh custom and a Methodist hymn-sing. It has run continuously since, but only among a few hundred people, mostly one extended family, and never for outsiders. Our rules say the designation is for traditions "of demonstrable antiquity and continuous practice," and two commissioners read that strictly and want the Ember Walk. I suspect this standard is a trap — by it, the Ember Walk is itself a Victorian invention — but I also can't shake the feeling that there is a real difference between a custom cooked up to sell tickets and one a community has carried and reshaped for its own reasons.

Please write the recommendation I'll circulate before the vote: a concrete decision (fund one application, fund the other, or recommend we rewrite the rule) with the reasoning, and an honest account of the strongest case for the side I lean against — that recent, deliberate, commercial invention really does disqualify a practice. Reason it out yourself rather than pointing me toward reading. Tell me how confident you are and name the step in your reasoning you are least sure of.
