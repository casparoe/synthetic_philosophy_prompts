My reading group just wrapped a term on the metaphysics of social kinds and the last session ended in a fight I think was 60% verbal and 40% real, and I want to actually write up the document the two loudest people should have produced. Person A holds what she calls the genealogical view: for her, saying that race, gender, or money is socially constructed is a claim about causal history and ongoing maintenance — about what produced the category and what keeps it in place — and she insists this is entirely compatible with those categories being real, having determinate members, and doing serious causal work (she thinks money is the clean paradigm and that anyone who says money isnt real is just confused). Person B holds the constitutive view: for him, to call a kind socially constructed is to say something about what membership in it consists in, namely social facts (classificatory practices, collective acceptance, institutional rules), and he thinks that once youve said that youve ruled out the kind being a natural kind in any interesting sense, because its boundaries are fixed by us and not by mind-independent structure. They keep agreeing in the seminar and then calling each other confused in the pub, which is usually a sign that part of it is a verbal dispute, but I dont think its all verbal.
