Right, so I've got a term of research leave coming up and I need to work out where to actually put the effort on consent, so I'm asking you to help me prioritise rather than to summarise the field for me.

Here's my starting point. The standard picture is basically a checklist: competent, informed, not coerced, not deceived — and hey presto, valid consent. I think that hides all the interesting stuff, and there are two threads I want to pull on.

First: consent as a standing thing rather than an event. Almost all real consent is given once and then relied on for ages — a patient consenting to a course of treatment, someone enrolling in a five-year cohort study, two people in a long relationship. Intuitively, validity ought to be able to *lapse* — quietly, with nobody having done anything wrong — when the facts the consent was based on drift. But I've never seen a decent account of when a standing consent expires, or what (if anything) the consent-giver owes by way of keeping it alive, or whether "consent" is even the right concept there rather than something more like an agreement or an authorisation.

Second: the gap between inner consent and communicated consent. Cases where someone inwardly consents but never says so, and cases where someone performs consent they don't actually have. Do we care about the mental state, the performance, or the fit between the two — and does the answer change depending on what consent is *for*: protecting the consenter's autonomy, allocating permissions, or assigning responsibility to whoever acts on it?

So: given one person with a philosophy background, no empirical training, and about four months of real working time, which open questions in the theory of valid consent should I prioritise, in what order, and why? I want the reasoning rather than a list. For each one, is it upstream of the others? Is it tractable by conceptual work alone? Is it actually substantive or just terminological? And tell me what would move something up or down the ranking as I go. If you reckon my two framings are the wrong place to start, say so and tell me what you'd do instead.
