Ok so I'm teaching a grad seminar on Wittgenstein's later epistemology — we spend the back half on the notes he left about certainty and doubt, the ones where he keeps circling the thought that doubting something already presupposes that a lot is standing fast — and I've hit a snag that I'd normaly paper over in lecture, except a student caught me out on it last week and I don't want to bluff twice.

The snag is about what these hinge commitments actually are. I keep sliding between two pictures. Picture one: they're propositions like any other, full-blooded contents that could be true or false, they just sit in a different role — exempt from doubt, never tested, the frame rather than the picture. On this picture it still makes sense to ask whether a hinge is true, it's just that asking whether you're justified in believing it is somehow out of place. Picture two: they aren't propositional at all — they're more like a way of acting, a habit, a technique, a stance — so that asking whether they're true is a category mistake, like asking whether a rule of chess is true. What I want from you is a careful explanation of what actually turns on the choice between those two readings, not a summary of who said what. In particular: (a) which of our ordinary phenomena each reading handles better and worse — disagreement, conversion, the fact that we can apparently argue someone out of a hinge, the fact that hinges seem to shift over historical time; (b) whether the non-propositional reading can even make sense of the fact that we obviously talk about our hinges, since you can't state a habit; and (c) whether there's a third option that isn't just a verbal compromise — say, hinges are propositional in content but their certainty isn't a property of the proposition, it's a property of our relation to it, and if that's right, say what work the word certainty is doing in each of the two places.

One thing I'd ask you to take seriously as a test case, because my student raised it and I fumbled it: imagine a community of enquirers who hold as a live, testable hypothesis something we'd treat as a hinge — say, that their memory reports are by and large reliable — and who actually run experiments on it and revise their confidence accordingly. Are they doing something we simply can't do, in which case hinge talk starts to look parochial, a description of us rather than of enquiry as such? Or are they confused in some way we can diagnose from the outside? And relatedly: when a hinge shifts — a religious conversion, or the moment you stop taking for granted that your memory is trustworthy — is that a rational event, a causal event, or does the dichotomy just break down there? I'm not after a survey, I want the conceptual map with the pressure points marked. Feel free to tell me if the two-picture framing is itself the problem.
