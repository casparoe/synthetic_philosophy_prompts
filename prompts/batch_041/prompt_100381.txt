I'm a novelist, and my best friend is a philosopher of science, and for years she's been needling me at dinner with puzzles about what counts as evidence for a general claim. Her favorite is the claim that all ravens are black. Her verdicts so far, as near as I can jot them down: (1) a black raven you happen to spot on a walk confirms it; (2) a white shoe you pull out of a box of assorted junk that you'd been told might contain a raven — she says that confirms it, a little bit, which always makes me spill my wine; (3) a black raven that a birdwatcher shows you, where the birdwatcher reports every raven he sees regardless of color, confirms it; (4) a black raven you only dreamed about confirms nothing at all. She also keeps insisting that all ravens are black says the exact same thing as all non-black things are non-ravens, which I can't argue with, though I don't see how it helps.

Now she's handed me a new case and wants my prediction: you're rummaging in your closet for shoes, you weren't thinking about birds at all, and you notice that one of your shoes is white. What will she say about that one — confirms or not — and what rule is she actually running? I'm suspicious there's more then one rule that fits all four of her earlier verdicts, and if so I want to know which one she's really on, your best guess and why, and what further case would sort them apart. Since I might steal this for a book, also tell me where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong, and what would make you change your mind about her verdict.
