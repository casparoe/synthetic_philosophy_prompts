I am a third-year undergraduate on a joint honours programme in philosophy and computer science, and this term I am taking a module on Dewey's political philosophy while simultaneously working through a module on reinforcement learning and sequential decision-making. The two have collided in a way I find genuinely productive but also suspicious, and I would rather you did the work of translation properly than let me flatter myself that they are saying the same thing. What I want is this: take Dewey's experimentalism about inquiry and about democracy — the account on which inquiry begins in a situation that is felt to be indeterminate and must be constituted as a problem before it can be solved; on which the framing, development and testing of hypotheses issues not in private mental states but in warranted assertibility, a status that is defeasible, situational and conferred by the conduct of inquiry itself; on which means and ends form a continuum rather than two separate orders; and on which democracy is not a decision procedure for aggregating preferences that are simply given, but a way of associated living in which the consequences of joint activity are shared, communicated and made the object of further inquiry — and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: agents, environments, states, observations, reward signals, policies, exploration and exploitation, credit assignment, model-based versus model-free methods, non-stationarity and distributional shift, online versus offline learning, and evaluation protocols. I would ask you not to smuggle the assumptions of that vocabulary into the translation and then present the result as fidelity; where the fit is bad, I want the badness on the surface.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I care about most. Which elements of Dewey's position map cleanly onto something in that vocabulary; which can be carried across only by distortion, and precisely what gets bent in the process; and which have no counterpart at all? I am particularly exercised by four questions. First, does the notion of a situation that is indeterminate before it has become a problem have any honest expression when the task is always antecedently specified by a reward function — or is the specification of the task itself the very thing Dewey thinks inquiry has to achieve? Second, is warranted assertibility anything more than a posterior, a confidence estimate or a calibrated value, and if not, what has been lost? Third, can his insistence that inquiry transforms the inquirer and the situation together be captured by the standard agent–environment split, or is that split doing illicit work by treating the two as independently specifiable relata? Fourth, does anything in his democratic experimentalism survive translation into multi-agent learning, federated learning or preference aggregation, or does the translation merely reproduce the aggregative picture he was arguing against? I would also like to know what becomes of the consummatory or aesthetic dimension of experience — his thought that an inquiry can be carried through to a settled, fulfilling conclusion rather than merely terminated — since I can think of nothing in my vocabulary that answers to it. Finally, tell me how to read the residue: does it show that my framework is missing something that matters, or that Dewey's formulations were doing less work than his admirers suppose? And in either case, is there a claim in his position that cannot even be stated, let alone assessed, in the terms I am asking you to use?
