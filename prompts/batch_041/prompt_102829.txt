The talk's thesis, roughly: everyone reads Hume's essay Of the Balance of Trade as the origin of the price-specie-flow mechanism and then treats that mechanism as his proof that trade imbalances self-correct and so we don't need to manage trade. I want to argue thats a presentist reading in two directions. First, the mechanism only does the work the free-trade conclusion needs if the transition is basically costless, but Hume himself is adamant in Of Money that money is never neutral — an inflow raises prices unevenly, stimulates some sectors and not others, and it's exactly that lumpy, unequal transition that makes the mechanism move at all. So the automatic adjustment Hume describes runs on the very non-neutrality the modern reading assumes away. Second, once you see that, Hume's case for free trade starts to look much more like a case against treating commerce as a zero-sum contest driven by national jealousy — he's doing rhetoric and moral psychology, not welfare economics.
