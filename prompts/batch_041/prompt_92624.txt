Here's the specific thing that's bugging me. The cheap objection goes: if this world is the best one, then take it and add one more happy person, or knock out one toothache, and you've got something better — so either this isn't the best or the word "best" is doing no work at all. I want the real answer Leibniz has to that, not the cartoon. My hunch is it runs through his idea of compossibility — that possible worlds aren't piles of independently swappable parts but whole packages whose pieces have to fit together, so that "adding a happy person" isn't even an operation you can perform on a world. So here's what I need explained:

3. Whether the reply actually works or just moves the bump around. If the answer is basically "you can't add to a world," doesn't that make "best" nearly unfalsifiable — true by construction? Push back on him for me.

Walk me through what "add a piece" even means under that picture, then tell me where the analogy breaks down versus Leibniz's actual view — don't just run with my analogy if it's leading you somewhere he wouldn't go.
