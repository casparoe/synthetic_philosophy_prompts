(a) The thermodynamic arrow is the fact that entropy increases in one temporal direction rather than the other. The second law says the entropy of a closed system never decreases, but statistical mechanics shows this isn't a strict law, it's overwhelmingly probable rather than necessary, because the underlying microdynamics are time-reversal invariant. Run the film of two molecules colliding backwards and it's still a perfectly legal process. So nothing in the laws picks out a direction, and the asymmetry has to come from the state the universe happens to be in: it started in an absurdly low-entropy macrostate, the past hypothesis, and has been relaxing toward equilibrium ever since. Without that, the statistics come out symmetric: for every microstate that runs into a higher-entropy macrostate there's a mirror-image microstate that runs into a lower-entropy one. So the second law describes the climb but doesn't produce it. The past hypothesis is a law of nature that we add to the dynamics. Unlike an initial condition it isn't something that only does its work at t = 0, it's a constraint that holds at all times and tells the universe to keep heading away from equilibrium.

(b) The arrow that connects most obviously is the psychological one: we remember the past and not the future. Memory is a physical record, and records need entropy gradients, since a trace in your brain only carries information about an earlier event because entropy was increasing along the way, which is what makes the trace reliable in one direction and useless in the other. So in the hypothetical universe an inhabitant on the far side of the midpoint would remember what we'd call the future and call it the past, and nothing in their physics would flag them as living "after" the minimum. This is why I don't think the psychological arrow is an independent arrow at all: given the entropy gradient, memory comes along for free, and you don't need any extra ingredients. The causal arrow is the same story, causes being the events that leave traces, so it isn't independent either.

(c) I don't think the hypothetical can be taken literally. If entropy is lowest in the middle and rises toward both ends, then time would have to run in two directions at once from the midpoint, which is a contradiction, since one moment can't be the past of two different futures. So the scenario isn't describing a real possibility. What it does show is that the direction of time isn't built into the laws, it's a matter of which way the entropy happens to be running, which is a fact about the state of the universe rather than about physics. So the direction of time is objective, there's a fact of the matter about which way entropy is going, but the second law alone doesn't explain why there's an arrow, because the second law presupposes a low-entropy starting point.
