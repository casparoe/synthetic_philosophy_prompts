I am not a philosopher, but I have been thinking on my own about Schopenhauer's account of music and about the film Whiplash, and I would like a reading of the film through that account. As I understand it, Schopenhauer holds that music is unlike the other arts: it does not copy the Ideas or the phenomenal world, but is a direct objectification of the Will itself—the blind, striving essence of reality—and that melody is the highest form in which that Will becomes audible. Music, on this view, is independent of concepts and of the world of representation; it expresses not the composer's feelings or the listener's, but the inner nature of the world. Whiplash, the film about a young jazz drummer and his abusive conservatory teacher, by contrast, seems to present music as a contest of wills: the will to greatness, the will to dominate, the will to endure pain. It is also jazz, improvised and embodied, not the concert music Schopenhauer had in mind. Does the film dramatise Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music, or does it refute it by showing music embedded in the very world of representation, ego, and desire that Schopenhauer says it transcends? What should I make of the film's focus on rhythm rather than melody, given Schopenhauer's claim that melody is the highest objectification of the Will? And what about the final scene, where the two men seem to communicate through the music after all their cruelty—is that a Schopenhauerian moment of the Will becoming audible, or is it just another power struggle? I want a reading that is faithful to both the film and the philosophy, that says where the film gets Schopenhauer right, where it resists him, and what it adds to the debate that argument alone does not.
