ok so for the last meeting of my self-reference seminar (we've been doing GEB, formal systems, the DNA/protein loop stuff) everyone has to bring in a poem that *does* the idea instead of describing it. i got typogenetics.

in case the setup isn't in your memory: a strand is just a string over A C G T. a strand gets read off into a chain of enzymes, and the chain is basically a little program of typographical commands -- cut here, copy this stretch, move along the strand, insert, delete, flip a piece -- and those commands act back on strands. so a strand can end up building the machinery that rebuilds the strand. hofstadter's point being that this is the same move as gödel numbering: meaningless marks arranged so they can reach around and talk about marks, including themselves. self-reference with no self anywhere in the alphabet.

so write the poem. constraints, because the form should be doing the work:

- the first letters of the lines spell out an instruction (something like COPY, or a short command string), so the poem carries a program in its spine
- some lines pair up as complements -- a line and its partner doing the same work in opposite directions, or the same words run backwards -- so the double-strand thing shows up in the form
- the ending has to rebuild the beginning: the last stanza reproduces the opening lines by the rules the poem itself has laid out, and one word or letter comes out wrong, so it's copying with an error rather than a photocopy
- no "and then it copied itself" narration. the poem should just do it.

25-35 lines, no fixed meter, but it has to read as a poem and not as a puzzle answer. voice could be the strand, the enzyme, or both taking turns -- both is probably better, since the weird part is that the same marks are dumb shapes on one level and instructions on the next. if someone who's read the chapter can trace the operations and find them right, good; if someone who hasn't can still read it as a poem about making yourself out of parts, better.
