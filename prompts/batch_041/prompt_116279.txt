Here's the shape I want. A villanelle, the standard 19 lines, two rhymes, the two refrains coming back on schedule. Let the two refrains be the two horns of the paradox. One refrain should be the make-believe side — it isn't real, nobody actually died, so it costs me nothing. The other should be the plain stubborn fact of the thing: I'm sitting here crying at a made-up death and it does feel like it's taking something out of me. I want those two lines to keep swapping places through the poem the way the two explanations keep swapping places in your head at 2am, and in the closing quatrain I want them right up against each other so you can hear whether they actually contradict each other or just keep politely missing each other.

The idea I most want it built on is something a friend tossed out that I definately can't stop chewing on: real grief comes with an action item. Someone dies and you have to do something — make the calls, sit in the room, be a different person afterward. A fictional death strips the action item out and leaves the feeling behind. So maybe the pleasure is just grief with the to-do list deleted. I want the poem to take that seriously and also lean on it, because if the feeling really is the same feeling then cutting the task out shouldn't make it sweeter, and if it's a seperate feeling then calling it grief is a bit of a lie. Also someone else in the group pointed out we'd feel cheated if the sad story ended happy, which seems to cut against the whole "we're avoiding pain" reading and I want that in there too.
