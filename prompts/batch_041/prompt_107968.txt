I am a third-year philosophy student in a seminar on the ethics of suffering, and our reading group has spent weeks stuck between two positions that keep talking past each other. The first is a lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: the view that there is a level of negative welfare, call it the suffering floor, such that no amount of positive welfare, however large, can justify imposing suffering below that floor; above the floor, positive and negative welfare may be weighed, but below it, suffering has lexical priority. The second is a commensurabilist hedonism: the view that pleasures and pains lie on a single cardinal scale of valence, so that any pain is in principle compensable by enough pleasure and any pleasure is in principle defeatable by enough pain, with no lexically privileged threshold. Our seminar has stipulated two labels for the camps' core pictures: the suffering-floor picture and the single-ledger picture. I need a document that both camps could actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, not a debate transcript and not a verdict about who is right.
