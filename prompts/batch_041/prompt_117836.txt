I run the accountability desk at a mid-size news outlet. Every week we take a batch of public statements and tag each one as a lie, as misleading, or as fine, and the tags keep starting fights — reporters say "it's literally true," the lawyers say "you can't print lie unless you can prove intent," and we end up relitigating the same three cases forever. I want a decision procedure my team can actually run on a single statement in about fifteen minutes, from the transcript alone plus what a reasonable listener would have come away believing. No mind-reading the speaker, no intent polygraph.

Build it out of the real philosophy that lives here — the difference between what a sentence literally says and what it merely suggests, and the question of whether deceiving someone with a true sentence is morally any better than lying to them. But I want the procedure, not the seminar: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops and stamps "lie," where it stops at "misleading," where it clears the statement entirely, and the point where it has to come to me and legal because the rest is a values call about how much deception we'll tolerate.

Two things I want baked in. First, don't let it lump lying and misleading into one bucket, but also don't let "the words were true" get anyone off the hook — say exactly when the two deserve the same tag anyway. Second, there's a third case that isn't cleanly either one: the sentence is true, suggests nothing false, but only works because the audience already believes something false and the speaker is counting on that. I want the procedure to catch that case and say whether it's a lie, a misleading, or its own category.

Then run it on three: a senator saying "we cut taxes for the middle class" when the cut is real but smaller than inflation; a CEO saying "we've never had a data breach" when a subsidiary that was later sold did; and one you make up that the procedure tags in a way my reporters will hate. Finish by telling me where the thing misfires and what it just can't settle.
