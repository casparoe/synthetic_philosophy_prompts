I host a small podcast for software engineers on language, minds, and machines, and I have no formal philosophy background. Next month's guest is a philosopher-linguist who defends a Hofstadter-style view of machine translation: she argues that statistical MT systems do not genuinely understand the texts they translate, even when their output is indistinguishable from a competent human translation. In the book she is writing, she distinguishes translation success from what she calls semantic accountability, roughly the translator's ability to answer for its renderings by appeal to meaning, context, and world knowledge rather than mere distributional fluency. I would like eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the thesis. At least one question each should address: (1) the gap between passing a translation Turing test and being semantically accountable for a rendering; (2) the roles of compositionality and systematicity in her argument; (3) whether the objection that understanding comes in degrees weakens her position; (4) how underdetermination, the fact that several translations can be equally faithful, bears on semantic accountability; (5) the place of pragmatic inference, such as implicature, deixis, and speech acts, in her account; and (6) what empirical or conceptual evidence could count for or against the shallowness thesis. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. For each, also add a one-line note separating what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on machine translation and semantics; do not present a contested view as the consensus, and say so explicitly if you are unsure where the line falls. Keep the questions accessible to a technical audience without a philosophy background, but do not water down the substance.
