I am a graduate student in the philosophy of biology, and I keep encountering a line usually attributed to Popper — that Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme — being deployed in two opposite directions within the same week. In a seminar on adaptation, a philosopher of biology used it to argue that selection explanations are in practice almost impossible to falsify, since any apparent failure can be absorbed by positing a different selective regime or an unsuspected adaptive benefit. Two days later, in a conversation with a creationist, the same line was used to argue that evolutionary theory does not belong in a science classroom at all. I have also heard that Popper later softened or withdrew the claim, that it was aimed only at a narrow formulation of natural selection rather than at evolutionary theory as a whole, and that it concerned a particular stage of the debate rather than the finished theory.

What I want is not a verdict on whether the slogan is true, but a map of the distinct precise claims it could be making. Please lay the readings out as a menu, from the nearly trivial to the radical, and for each one say what it would commit its holder to, whether it is defensible, and what kind of argument or evidence would settle it. I am especially interested in the following axes of variation, though you should add any others that matter:

- What is the target? Natural selection as a mechanism; the whole Darwinian package of common descent plus selection; the thesis that selection is the principal cause of adaptation; or the broader claim that evolution is purposeless and unguided?
- What does not testable mean here? Logically incapable of being contradicted by any possible observation; testable only in conjunction with so many auxiliary assumptions that refutation is inconclusive; testable in principle but not by any feasible experiment or historical reconstruction; or testable only retrospectively, once a selective story has already been told?
- What does metaphysical research programme mean? A framework that generates testable hypotheses but is not itself a testable hypothesis; a research programme in Lakatos's sense, with a protected core and a heuristic; speculative metaphysics in the pejorative sense; or simply a worldview or ideology?
- What is the status of the charge that the principle of natural selection reduces to the tautology that survivors survive, or that the fittest are simply those who leave the most offspring? Is that a separate objection from the unfalsifiability charge, and does the slogan depend on it?
- Which of these readings would actually support the two uses I encountered — the adaptation seminar's complaint about unfalsifiable selection stories, and the creationist's complaint about science education — and which would not?

I would also like you to say which reading you think the people who repeat the slogan usually have in mind, and whether the slogan is doing genuine philosophical work or is mostly a rhetorical shortcut. If Popper's own position changed, distinguish the earlier and later claims as precisely as you can without relying on verbatim quotations, and note which parts of the received story strike you as probably apocryphal. The deliverable should be a numbered list of readings, each with its precise claim, its verdict, its conditions of settlement, and its bearing on the two uses, followed by a short paragraph on the most likely intended reading.
