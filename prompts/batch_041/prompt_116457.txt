I keep going back and forth on the expanding circle idea — the thought that moral concern has been widening over history, from kin and tribe out to strangers and maybe now to animals and whoever comes next. I want your honest credence that it's a real trend and not just a story we tell about a random walk, and I want it broken down rather than a vibe.

So, four numbers please, each with a line or two of justification: (1) the probability that the historical record actually shows net expansion once you correct for the fact that we mostly read the winners' accounts, and for expansions that arrived bundled with new exclusions (abolition in the metropole alongside new coercive labour regimes in the colonies, universal rights alongside fresh bureaucratic exclusions); (2) the probability that whatever expansion happened is driven by moral reasoning and empathy rather than by institutional and material conditions — states needing conscripts and taxpayers, legal personhood being cheap to extend, markets wanting more counterparties; (3) the probability that the trend continues over the next couple of centuries rather than reversing, given how robust ingroup favoritism and dehumanization look psychologically; and (4) the probability that the question even has a determinate answer, i.e. that moral progress isn't just a projection of whatever the current consensus happens to value.

Then combine them into a single credence, tell me which combination you're using, and say which dependence between the terms you're assuming, since they clearly aren't independent.

Which term do you think we're actually disagreeing about — the empirical history, the mechanism, or whether the concept is coherent at all — and which single sub-question, if it went your way, would move your number the most?
