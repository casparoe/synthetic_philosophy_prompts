The claim is that "what it's like" to see red is like that: a useful self-generated summary the brain shows itself, not a real property lurking in the neurons.

My opening, and it's the thing I can't get past: a folder icon can only be a lie because there's a user staring at the screen who's capable of being fooled. An illusion needs a someone it's an illusion *for*. So if consciousness is an interface, who's the user? You can't just say "the brain," because the brain is the hardware, and the hardware doesn't look at the icons. And if you say the user is itself another part of the interface, then it's just icons fooling other icons, and I don't see how "there's nothing it's like to be me" doesn't quietly turn back into "there's something it's like to be me, it's just made of interface stuff." So — who's fooled?
