Two friends of mine got into a argument at a barbecue on Saturday and I've been chewing on it alot since. Both of them did a few years of philosophy before going into software, so the level of the thing was pretty high for a barbecue. I stayed out of it and honestly I couldn't have kept up. The subject was the Gödelian argument that no formal system — no machine — can capture human mathematical reasoning (the Lucas/Penrose sort of thing). They were Sam and Alex.

Sam: Take any formal system F that's supposed to be the machine that does my mathematics. If F is consistent, Gödel gives us a sentence G_F that F can't prove. But I can see that G_F is true — I can see that it says "there's no F-proof of this sentence," and if F is consistent then that's exactly right, so there's no such proof and the sentence is true. So I know something F can't prove. So F isn't me. And I picked F arbitrarily, so no formal system is me. Mechanism is false.

Alex: Two things. First, that shows at most that you're not *this* F. Hand me F' = F plus "F is consistent," and G_F becomes a theorem of F' — the machine proves it fine, you've just moved to a bigger machine. Second, you leaned on "if F is consistent." You can't prove F's consistency from inside F, sure, but you can't prove your own consistency either, so you're helping yourself to an assumption that the machine is equally entitled to take on faith. Your "seeing" is standing in for a premise you never justified.

Sam: I'm not assuming F is consistent as a hypothesis — I can *see* that F is sound: the axioms are true, the rules preserve truth, so once I see that I see G_F has to be true. That's not a stipulated premise, it's a piece of mathematical knowledge. And notice the machine can't even turn that knowledge into a theorem: "F is consistent" isn't provable in F. So there's something I know and it can't.

Alex: "I can see F is sound" is the exact step that's in dispute. You might be wrong about it. If you're unsound you'll "see" false things with the same inner glow, and if you're inconsistent you'll "see" everything, including G_F, for the wrong reason. So either you're assuming your own soundness — which is the very thing the argument was meant to establish — or the fact that you "see" G_F is worthless as evidence. And here's a machine that makes the point: build one that is in fact sound, but that has a line of code telling it to assert "I am sound" and then assert whatever follows. It "sees" its own Gödel sentence too. It hasn't done anything you didn't do. If "seeing" just means the reliable disposition to assent, the machine has it. If it means more than that — some faculty that guarantees truth — then you owe me an account of that faculty that doesn't amount to "it's not formal," and that account is exactly what's missing.

Sam: The difference is that the machine just *takes it on faith* — it's a blind assertion wired into the code. When I see that F is sound I'm not blind, I'm justified. My justification isn't a theorem of F, but that's fine, because not everything I know has to be a theorem of F — that's the whole point of the argument. You're demanding that I produce a formal system that contains my insight, and then using the fact that I can't as evidence that the insight isn't real. That's assuming your conclusion.

Alex: No — I'm saying that if your seeing guarantees truth then it's a reliability claim, and any reliability claim you can make, a machine can be built to make as well, including by asserting its own soundness. If it doesn't guarantee truth, then it doesn't separate you from a machine that guesses well. And think about how far your argument reaches: it would work against *every* formal system, including the one that keeps adding its own consistency, and then the consistency of that, forever. So either you've proved too much — if it knocks out even that one, it knocks out nothing, because no mechanist has to say you're a *fixed* F — or you've proved only that no fixed system is you, which nobody ever denied.
