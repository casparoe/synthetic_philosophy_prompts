ok so I'm a novelist, three drafts into a book, and I've fallen down a hole I can't climb out of, and I'd like you to help me figure out which parts of the hole are actually worth digging.

The book has a character — call her Vera — who is the most deliberate, adaptable, improvisational person in the story. She's the one who reads the room and changes plan mid-sentence. And the whole trick of the novel is that by the end you're meant to suspect that all that flexibility is itself a routine, just a longer and more tangled one than the wasp's. You know the wasp I mean: paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the burrow, goes in first to check the burrow is clear, comes back out — and if you nudge the cricket two inches while she's inside, she just drags it back to the entrance and goes in to check again. Forever. Or at least thats the version I was told; I half suspect it's folklore. I've been calling it the cricket loop. The wasp's got maybe one loop. Vera's got a thousand, nested, and each one can notice when the world pushes back. My question is whether that's a difference in kind or only in the number of loops — and I suspect the answer is not as settled as people pretend. I've seen the word sphexish floating around for this, so I'll use it.

So here's what I want from you: an agenda, not a survey. Suppose I've got a year of reading and thinking I can honestly give this, alongside writing the thing. Which open questions in this neck of the woods — rigidity vs. flexibility, whether a finite agent can ever be more than a very good wasp, what the could-have-done-otherwise intuition is even tracking when the agent is a bundle of nested loops — are actually worth the year, and in what order? I'm not asking for a reading list and I can't use one anyway, so please dont send me off to the library. I want the reasoning: for each question, why it's upstream of the others, whether it's the kind of thing a person with a pen and too much time can actually make progress on rather than something that needs a lab, and what would move it up or down my list. And if two of these turn out to be the same question wearing different hats, say which, because I keep suspecting they are.

One more thing, and this is the part I care about most. There's a distinction I've been leaning on and I don't fully trust it: sphexish from the outside vs. sphexish from the inside. From the outside the wasp is a machine. But from the inside — whatever there is of an inside — there's no feeling of being on rails; there's just cricket, burrow, cricket, burrow. So maybe the interesting question isn't are we wasps but whether there's any agent at all for whom the answer would feel like anything either way. If that reframing is as load-bearing as I think, it should reorder everything above it. If it's just a novelist's trick, tell me that too.

Rank them, defend the ranking, and tell me which one I'd be a fool to spend the year on.
