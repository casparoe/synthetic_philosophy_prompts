A group of us runs a small prediction market with real money, and this quarter three of the questions are: whether a well-known open problem in mathematics will be resolved within five years; whether a short program one of us wrote will halt, which nobody has run because it would tie up a cluster for a week; and whether a finite computation we could in principle just perform comes out above or below a stated bound. A colleague argues that the whole exercise is confused — all three answers are already fixed, so a price of 0.4 is a confession of ignorance rather than a belief about anything, and whoever works out an answer will simply take everyone else's money, so the market can never be efficient. Another proposes a house rule: nobody may post a price on a claim that exceeds the sum of the prices they have posted on the steps of any derivation of that claim they could actually carry out. I think there is something right in each complaint and something wrong in the conclusion, but I cannot articulate what. Is it coherent to hold graded beliefs and trade on questions that are already settled in this way, and if so, what should our prices be tracking and how should we score the market? What constraints should a single participant's prices satisfy — including in the case where I can see that a proof is within my reach even though I have not yet carried it out?
