I lost an argument at a house party last week and its still bugging me, so I'm outsourcing it. The guy who beat me is definately a strict expected-value type: if option A has a higher expected value than option B, he thinks A is the one to take, full stop, no thresholds, no sure-thing exception, no discount for risk. Then he ran this on me:

(1) For any two options you might face, you should take the one with the higher expected value.
(2) Value isn't bounded. Whatever good thing you name, there's a possible outcome better by an arbitrarily large factor, and same going the other way.
(3) You can't rule anything out entirely. Whatever I'm doing, there's some tiny probability p of something astronomically good or astronomically bad riding on it.
(4) So for any ordinary plan of mine -- going to work, calling my sister, eating a sandwich -- there's some outcome with probability p and value V such that pV dwarfs the expected value of the plan itself.
(5) By (1), I should prefer the plan that chases that V, or dodges that -V, over every ordinary plan.
(6) The same move applies to that plan, and to the next one, forever. There's no top.
(7) So no ordinary option is ever the rational one, and ordinary prudence is a mistake.
(8) Concretely: if a stranger on the street says he'll run a simulation containing 10^17 suffering minds unless I hand over my wallet, and I put the odds he can actually do it at one in 10^40, then (1)-(4) say my wallet is nothing next to that, so I hand it over. And then the next stranger, and the one after that. He says that's just what taking expected value seriously looks like, and that anyone who refuses is either lying about their credences or smuggling in a cutoff. He also pointed out it doesn't need the astronomy -- for any p I name, he just scales V.

I don't want (8). But I also don't want to give up (1); I use it for basically everything and I have no idea what I'd replace it with. So which step is doing the dirty work? If one of them is sneaky, name it and say why it looked innocent. If they're all individually fine and one of them is just false, tell me which is cheapest to drop, what losing it costs me, and what the replacement has to say about the 10^17 case specifically. If it comes down to it I'd sooner ditch (8) than (1), but I want the receipt.
