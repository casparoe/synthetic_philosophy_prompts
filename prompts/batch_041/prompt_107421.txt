I've been hanging around LessWrong / ACX / the old SSC comment sections for about a decade now, and one thing I've noticed is that everyone in those threads says the same three sentences about the Kelly criterion and then turns out to disagree about basically everything underneath. So I want to write a blog post that's a decision tree / taxonomy which sorts people by what they actually think about bankroll sizing and the whole log-wealth / geometric-growth thing, rather than by which camp they claim to be in. Can you build me that instrument?

Shape I want: every branch node is a question a reader can answer on the spot without knowing where it leads, and every leaf is a named position (or unnamed, if nobody actually defends it). Roughly 8 to 12 branch nodes and 8 to 12 leaves. The questions should be about concrete bets, not about labels, so nothing like do you think log utility is the correct utility function, because everyone just answers yes and moves on. I want the cases to do the work.

Cases I want at least these in there, feel free to add:
- a coin flip that pays +100% on heads and -50% on tails, offered to you exactly once in your life, stake your whole bankroll or not
- the same flip offered a thousand times in a row, where you can rebet any fraction each round
- a bet with clearly positive expected value whose loss would leave you with nothing (an absorbing barrier)
- a bet where you're pretty sure the odds are in your favor but your estimate of the probability could easily be off by a few points
- a bet you can only ever take once and there is no second chance after it, like a surgery or a life-or-death gamble
- a bet denominated in something that isn't money, say years of healthy life or number of good days
- a bet where you'd be staking money you'd otherwise have donated

Leaves I need to show up somewhere in the tree, at minimum:
- full Kelly maximalism, where expected log wealth is just the utility function and that's the end of it
- fractional Kelly justified as a hedge against your own probability estimates being wrong
- fractional Kelly justified as actual risk aversion, i.e. a concave utility over wealth that isn't log
- the time-average / ergodicity style justification, where the log comes out of the growth rate of a single trajectory over time rather than out of a utility function
- expected value maximization with a utility function that happens to be log, so Kelly is a theorem not a norm
- Kelly is for repeated bets only, one-shots get plain EV maximization
- the permissive view, where anything up to full Kelly is fine and only over-betting is a mistake
- the rejection, where EV max is right and ruin risk is a separate insurance question you bolt on afterwards
- a deflationary leaf, where the ensemble-average vs time-average stuff is a confusion and the disagreement is mostly verbal

Also: if your tree can't separate two leaves by any question, flag them as a pair rather than pretending. And where the logical space has combinations nobody occupies, say which ones and give me a one-line diagnosis of whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. The ergodicity-flavored leaf and the log-as-utility leaf are the ones I suspect will be hard to pry apart, so pay attention to that seam.

Don't give me a reading list or citations, I can't use them and I don't want the post to be a book report anyway. I want the instrument itself, phrased so a reader works out which leaf thier on before they see the label.
