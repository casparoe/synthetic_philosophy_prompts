Write a satirical self-help column, no longer than 600 words, in the voice of a popular author who applies Gödel's incompleteness theorems to personal growth. The target is the practice — common in the self-optimisation and consciousness genres — of borrowing a formal result as a metaphor for the self and then treating the metaphor as licensed by the result. Invent the author and the outlet; do not put the piece in the mouth of any real writer.

Constraints:

1. The column must be written straight. No winking asides, no authorial confession of the joke, and no character inside the piece who identifies the fallacy: a reader who knows the literature should be able to name the error unaided, and a reader who does not should still be able to follow the argument.

2. The theorems must be stated correctly; the comedy has to come from the inferences drawn, not from technical mistakes. In particular, the author should distinguish a system's inability to prove its own consistency from its ability to prove that, if it is consistent, the Gödel sentence is unprovable; should note correctly that the Gödel sentence does not make a consistent system inconsistent; should treat the sentence's truth as asserted in a specified interpretation rather than established by the system; and should then conclude that anyone who "sees" such a truth stands outside every formal system — overlooking that the seeing itself presupposes an assumption of one's own consistency that one cannot establish, which leaves the person exactly where the machine is.

3. The payoff should be the disjunction the argument actually leaves open — the mind is not a formal system, or it is inconsistent, or it cannot know its own axioms — presented as a menu of personal-growth options, with the author resolving it by choosing the flattering branch without appearing to choose.

4. Use the genre's vocabulary as its practitioners use it (strange loops, self-reference, stepping outside the system), and end with a bolded "Bottom line:" sentence of no more than twenty words.

Format it as a magazine column: a title, a subheading, and at least two internal headings. Keep the voice deadpan, upbeat and commercially confident.
