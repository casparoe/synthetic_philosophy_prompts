ok so this is going to be a weird one but my brother just got engaged to a woman from a super religious conservative family and we've been having these long arguments at dinner, and I ended up taking one of those moral foundations questionnaires online out of spite. I scored basically zero on loyalty/authority/sanctity and off the charts on care/fairness. He scored the exact opposite. Now I'm trying to figure out what I actually think and whether its a coherent package. Here's where I've landed:

(1) Moral foundations theory is basically right as a description of how people's moral tastes differ. Liberals care mostly about care and fairness; conservatives weight care and fairness too but also loyalty, authority, sanctity (and maybe liberty/oppression as its own thing). My family dinner is anecdotal evidence.

(2) I'm a moral realist. I think some moral claims are true or false independently of what any person or culture thinks.

(3) I think the evolutionary and coalitional origins of these foundations give us real reason to distrust them as truth-trackers. If my care/fairness instinct was shaped because it helped my ancestors cooperate within a tribe, that doesn't make it a reliable window onto moral reality. Same goes for sanctity disgust.

(4) I still think reflective equilibrium is the right method in ethics: start with considered judgments, try to bring them into a coherent system with principles, revise whichever gives.

(5) I think moral foundations theory actually shows liberals like me are parochial when we treat loyalty and sanctity as just prejudice. Those foundations seem to track real human goods—community, meaning, self-restraint, the stuff that lets people flourish together—and a society that ignores them is missing something.

(6) At the same time I think the liberal critique of authority and hierarchy is mostly right. Alot of what gets called loyalty or sanctity is just cover for domination, and I don't want to hand the conservatives a philosophical win just because they score higher on some questionnaire.

(7) I think there's no neutral, foundation-free standpoint from which to rank the foundations; any ranking is going to be made from within some moral outlook.

(8) I also think some moral views are just wrong—like racial hierarchy, or treating women as property. I don't want to be wishy-washy about that, definately not.

So: is this a consistent package? I suspect not, but I can't see where the seams are. Don't just tell me it's all fine or all relativism. I want you to go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which ones only look like they are, and rank the real tensions by how bad they are. For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what that fix costs me—i.e., which commitment I'd have to weaken or give up. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess, and whether that's a bullet I can bite or if it basically collapses the whole thing. Also please be specific about the is/ought stuff; I have a feeling I'm sliding somewhere but I don't know where.
