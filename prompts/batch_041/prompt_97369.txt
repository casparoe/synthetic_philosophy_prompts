I write about science and ideas for a general-audience mag and I'm finishing a peice on collective intentionality — the "can a group intend something, or is it always just individuals" debate, Searle vs Bratman and the we-intention stuff, plus the weirder corners like the idea that if you and I are walking together I can't just peel off and wander away without you being entitled to object. I want to end the peice with a joke. Can you write one that actually lands for people who know this literature? The punchline should turn on a real feature of the theory rather than just "philosophy, amirite" — e.g. the gap between each of us intending to do our own part vs there being one genuine shared intention, or the fact that two people striding the same direction might be doing a single joint action or two unrelated ones that happen to coincide. Keep it short, and it has to survive being told out loud.
