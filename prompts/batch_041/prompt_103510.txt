A culture's moral psychology is not simply inherited; it can be deliberately reshaped through law, pedagogy, ritual, and public shaming, and the pressing question is whether a shift from a shame-centered to a guilt-centered morality — or the reverse — is both feasible and desirable. Write an essay defending a verdict on which is the better moral emotion and on whether a culture can choose it.

Sharpen the argument with the following. First, the familiar contrast: shame is heteronomous, tracking the gaze of others and the risk of exposure, while guilt is autonomous, tracking the wrongness of an act before one's own conscience. Then test that contrast against a rationalist view — associated with Spinoza, who counts shame as a species of pain arising from the thought that others will blame us and denies that repentance is a virtue at all, treating it instead as a passive emotion a rational person would outgrow. That view makes shame purely relational and guilt merely remedial, which cuts against the standard hierarchy. Consider also the counterevidence: there are solitary shame experiences, and there are cases where guilt is felt only because one imagines how one's conduct appears to others. If the boundary between the two emotions is this porous, say what follows for the claim that one of them is morally superior.

Weigh the strongest considerations on both sides, including the thought that shame sustains social cohesion and accountability while guilt sustains individual integrity, and the counter-thought that shame is corrosive and status-driven while guilt is self-indulgent and easily privatized. Then address feasibility directly: what would it take for a society to change its dominant moral emotion, and do honor cultures, confessional cultures, and therapeutic cultures count as evidence that such changes are achievable rather than merely imaginable?

Reach a conclusion and justify it; do not end on a non-committal note.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and do not restate the question.
