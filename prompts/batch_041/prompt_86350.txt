Here's the pile:

1. I think it's a mistake to grade people from the past by today's moral standards. You have to judge them by the standards of their own time.
2. But I also think the historian's real job is to explain, and you can't explain anything without dragging in your best current causal story — crop failure plus the land-tenure system produced the famine, that kind of thing. Refusing to use modern economics or epidemiology just because it's modern strikes me as self-sabotage.
3. I also think "the standards of their own time" is mostly a fiction. Every past society had people arguing with each other about what was right. There was never one standard, just whoever won the argument.
4. And honestly one of the main reasons to study history at all is to draw lessons for how we should act now.
5. Last thing, and I'm a bit of a hardass about this: what happened in the past is fixed. Nothing about our present-day situation changes it.
