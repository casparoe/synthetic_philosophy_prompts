Construct a thought experiment that isolates a single variable in the theory of costly signaling: the gap between a signal's being costly and its cost being quality-dependent.

The scenario should involve one species, one signal, and one context of use. The signal must be genuinely expensive in energy, time, or risk, so that a first pass through the handicap principle would classify it as a classic honest signal. But stipulate that the cost of producing the signal is the same for every signaler regardless of quality. Then show, from the stated cost and benefit functions, that low-quality signalers still do best by producing it, that the population settles into a pooling equilibrium in which every signaler signals, and that a receiver who ignores the signal and plays the base rate does better than one who reads it as evidence of quality.

Constraints: keep the biology realistic (no science fiction, no appeals to group selection, no stipulating the outcome you want); state the quality distribution, the signal, the full cost function, the benefit function, and the receiver's best response explicitly; keep the scenario itself to one paragraph; and do not appeal to the trait being an index or a cue, since the point is to see what the handicap account alone can and cannot deliver.

After the scenario, do the following. First, say what the handicap principle as usually stated predicts here and why that prediction fails. Second, state the repair precisely: explain why honesty requires that the marginal cost of signaling be higher for lower-quality signalers, and show that this case satisfies the requirement that the signal be costly while violating the requirement that its cost be graded by quality. Third, run the controlled comparison: replace the flat cost with a quality-graded cost of the same average magnitude and show whether the equilibrium flips from pooling to separating. Fourth, name the feature of the case that is doing the work, and say what happens to the case if that feature is removed. Fifth, state whether the case refutes the handicap principle or merely exposes a precondition it always relied on, and defend the choice. Finally, give the strongest way a defender of the usual reading would resist the case, and say whether that resistance succeeds.

Argue entirely from the structure of the case; do not provide citations, references, or reading lists, and do not lean on any particular author or paper. The case is meant to test whether the honesty of a costly signal depends on the magnitude of the cost or on the gradient of the cost across quality, so anything that stipulates the verdict rather than deriving it from the cost and benefit functions counts as cheating. Write in American English.
