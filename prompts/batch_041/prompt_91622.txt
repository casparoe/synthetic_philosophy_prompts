Consider a rule system that contains, among its own rules, the rules for changing itself, and that has no authority outside itself to resolve disputes about what it requires — the situation, in spirit, of the self-amending game Nomic, though everything below is self-contained and nothing in it turns on any particular published version of that game.

The system is a rulebook with no referee. Any player may propose a change to the rulebook, and a proposed change takes effect only if it is adopted in accordance with the rulebook then in force. Two of the rules bear on this:

R1. A proposed rule-change is adopted if it is approved by two-thirds of the players voting.

R2. No rule-change may be adopted if it would reduce the fraction of votes required to adopt a rule-change.

A player proposes a single change: "Strike R2, and amend R1 so that a rule-change is adopted if it is approved by a simple majority of the players voting." The proposal is put to a vote and approved by two-thirds of those voting. Nothing in the rulebook says, in so many words, what is to happen when a rule-change would alter the very procedure by which it is being adopted.

Please explain the conceptual issue this case raises. Your explanation should bring out why the matter is not simply one of following the rules, and it should distinguish at least the following: whether R2 forbids its own repeal, or only the repeal of the threshold it protects; whether it matters that the proposal reaches its result in a single step rather than in two — first striking R2, then lowering the threshold — and why a reading of R2 that looks only to the immediate effect of a change differs from one that looks to what the change would make possible; whether the case is a genuine paradox, in which the rules both do and do not determine the outcome, or merely a difficult question of interpretation that the rules could in principle settle; and how the case differs from the simpler one of a single rule that declares itself repealed.

You need not choose among the readings. What is wanted is a clear account of the competing positions and of what would have to be true for each of them to be correct — bearing in mind throughout that there is no court, legislature, or other external interpreter to break the tie.
