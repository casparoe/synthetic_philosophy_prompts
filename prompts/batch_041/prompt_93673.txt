I'm the science editor at a mid-size online magazine and I get two or three pitches a week that smell like pseudohistory -- a guy who's re-dated all of ancient history by running statistics on old chronicles, someone who's found a lost civilisation under the ice, someone with a theory that the pyramids are thousands of years older than archaeologists say. Right now I decide by gut in about twenty minutes: run it as science, run it with a 'contested' tag, or send a polite no. I'd like a procedure I can actually run instead, grounded in whatever philosophy of science has to say about where science stops and pseudoscience starts -- the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the call to a person because it's a values question rather than a factual one. Be honest about wich steps are just my judgment wearing a lab coat. Then run it on three: the statistical re-dating of history, the lost ice civilisation, and one where I'd personally like the answer to come out the other way -- say a heterodox re-dating of a site that uses bog-standard methods but annoys the consensus. My fact-checker has maybe an hour, and the editor-in-chief signs off on anything we run with a disclaimer. Finish by telling me what the procedure can't do.
