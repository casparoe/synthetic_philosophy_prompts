I'm putting together a case for a seminar on why "just get more specific data" doesn't solve the reference class problem, so the design has to actually work rather than just gesture at the issue.

Build me a thought experiment where the reference class problem bites and the standard fix doesn't help. The standard fix is: use the most specific reference class that still has enough members to give you a frequency. So I need two candidate reference classes that are cross-cutting -- neither one is a subset of the other -- and whose intersection is basically just this one case, small enough that "use the intersection" hands you no usable frequency. That's the bit that has to be engineered, since otherwise specificity would just tell you to use the intersection and the problem would dissolve.

Constraints: realistic setting (a clinic, an insurance desk, a parole board, a scout looking at a player -- no Sleeping Beauty, no anthropic self-sampling, no quantum anything); one paragraph for the scenario itself; the thing whose probability is at issue has to be a one-off prediction about a particular person or object, not a frequency question in disguise; you can stipulate the numbers for each class but keep them simple enough to state in words; and both classes have to be ones a sensible person would have consulted before finding out how it turned out -- no cherry-picking a class after the fact.

Then do the work: name the two classes and the probability each one hands you, point at the practical decision that flips depending on which you use, and say which structural feature of the case is doing the actual work. Check that if you made the two classes nested instead, the whole thing collapses back into the boring case where specificity settles it. Finish with the strongest attempt to dissolve the puzzle -- a subjectivist who says just condition on all your evidence and stop asking which class is "the" class, or a propensity theorist who says there's a single-case chance and neither frequency is it -- and tell me whether the case survives that or not.
