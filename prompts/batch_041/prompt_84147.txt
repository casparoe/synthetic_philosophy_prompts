I'm taking an aesthetics seminar this term, and we've just finished a unit on aesthetic testimony. The upshot, as I've understood it, is that there is no such thing: you cannot learn that a painting is beautiful from someone else's say-so any more than you could learn what red looks like from inside a black-and-white room, because what is at issue is an experience, and experiences cannot be handed over in a sentence. What I'd like to know is what follows from this for criticism. When a critic tells me a novel is good and I have not read it, is she asking me to believe something I have no business believing, or is her praise merely a pointer — a reason to go and have the experience myself rather than a reason to believe anything about the novel in advance? And if the latter, should I conclude that aesthetic knowledge is essentially first-personal, and that the whole debate about aesthetic testimony is at bottom a special case of the problem of knowing what it is like to be someone else? I'd like to know how much of this reading I should keep, and where it goes wrong.
