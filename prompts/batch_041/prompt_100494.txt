You are to act as a philosopher of physics and metaphysician whose specialty is cosmological arguments and the metaphysics of nothingness. I want a careful, menu-style disambiguation rather than a single tidy verdict, so please begin by brainstorming the candidate readings before you commit to any of them, and flag explicitly wherever a reading is so weak that it is only doing rhetorical work.

I was in a reading group on the cosmological argument, and someone tried to close the discussion by saying that nothing comes from nothing, as though that settled the question of whether the universe stands in need of a cause or an explanation. The very same phrase, in its Latin dress, gets used in popular physics talks to argue in almost the opposite direction, as a gloss on the idea that the universe could have arisen from nothing at all. I have come to suspect that everyone in these exchanges is using the same few words to mean at least four quite different things, that some of those things are barely more than tautologies while others carry serious metaphysical weight, and that nobody notices when they slide from one to another. What I want from you is a decomposition of that slogan: lay out the distinct precise principles it could be expressing, ordered roughly from the trivial to the substantive.

For each reading, be explicit about the dimensions along which the readings differ: what "nothing" is supposed to denote (the absence of any entity, an empty world, absence considered as such, a mere placeholder in a sentence, a state of affairs with no content); what "comes from" is supposed to mean (causal production, material constitution, existential dependence, explanatory grounding, temporal arising); what the principle is a claim about (events within the world, substances, facts, the cosmos taken as a whole); and what modal status it is being given (a conceptual truth, a metaphysical necessity, a contingent law of nature, an empirical generalization that could turn out false). Then give me verdicts: for each reading, say whether it is plausible, whether it is trivial or contentious, what it would actually license in the cosmological argument, and which reading my reading-group companion most likely had in mind when he said it. Pay particular attention to the curious reading on which the slogan is true for the thin reason that "nothing" cannot be an agent or a source at all, and tell me what that triviality can and cannot accomplish for the argument it was meant to support. Finally, say which readings would let a physicist legitimately describe vacuum fluctuations, or a universe that "arises from nothing," as an instance of something coming from nothing, and which readings would make that description a category error rather than a discovery. I am interested in the positions themselves, not in who held them, so there is no need to assign names or sources; I want the map of possible claims, with your assessment attached to each node.
