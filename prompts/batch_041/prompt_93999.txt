A regulator announces, in public and in advance, that it will carry out inspections without warning. A musician announces that the next album will appear with no advance notice at all. A general announces that he intends to achieve surprise. I have been collecting cases of this shape for years and have found no name for what they share — if, indeed, they share anything. In each case the announcement is meant to be believed, and in each case believing it changes the very situation announced, though I cannot tell whether it changes it enough to cancel it.

The famous member of the family is the teacher who tells the class that there will be a test next week, on a day they cannot predict. The class reasons backward and concludes that no such test can occur; the test then arrives on an ordinary day and surprises them. I am not asking about that puzzle, which is the best-studied case. I am asking whether the family, or the sharp version of it, has a name.

The sharp version is this. What gets announced contains a claim about the hearers' state of knowledge — that they will not have worked it out beforehand. To believe the announcement is to put it into one's reasoning; and if it is true and the hearers reason correctly from it, I think they will have worked it out beforehand, which makes it false. It cannot be both true and generally believed. It is a fact that cannot survive being shared.

Three neighbours to be separated from it. It resembles the self-defeating prophecy, in which a prediction fails because people act on it — but here nobody acts, they only reason. It resembles the thought that measuring a thing changes it — but nothing here is measured or disturbed, and the damage comes from belief rather than interference. And it resembles ordinary secrecy, which is plainly different, since a secret is destroyed by disclosure, not by belief.

Does the sharp case have an established name, and do the puzzles built on it? If not, coin a term and define it so as to exclude ordinary secrets, self-defeating predictions, and mere failed predictions. Then mark its edges: what must be true of a case for the fact to survive being widely known, and do any of my three opening cases survive it? I care more about the boundaries than about the label. In under 350 words, beginning with the term you think fits best.
