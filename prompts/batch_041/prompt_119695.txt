Here's the position I hold, which I came up with on my own and only later found out is a real thing people argue about. Health just is the absence of a defect, and a defect is a part of the body failing to do the job it was built by evolution to do. What counts as the job gets fixed by comparing the person to a reference group -- same species, same sex, roughly same age -- and that's it. No values anywhere. The values only enter when you pick which reference group to compare against, and that pick is a design decision, like deciding what counts as a bug in a codebase: you have to say what the software was supposed to do before broken means anything, and that choice is yours, not the compiler's. So the normativity is real but it's quarantined in the reference-class choice rather than sitting inside the disease judgment itself. If you tell me someone is diseased, on my view there's a further purely factual question of whether some part is failing relative to their comparison class, and that question has a right answer.

What I want from you is the case against it. Not the standard but-values hand-wave, I want to know what would actually count against it, ranked from fatal to merely annoying.
