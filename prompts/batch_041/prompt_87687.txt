I'd like a dialogue between two philosophers of truth, both competent and neither a strawman, staged around a single concrete case rather than a survey of positions.

The first defends a paracomplete account: the liar is neither true nor false, and 'true' is given by a fixed-point construction that leaves some sentences ungrounded. The second is a dialetheist: the liar is both true and false, and the paracomplete theorist's "gaps" are a polite way of declining to state the sentence's status.

To keep them off autopilot, give them a specific blackboard to argue over. Have them consider (a) the ordinary liar, (b) a two-sentence loop in which each says the other is not true, and (c) a revenge sentence that says of itself that it is not determinately true, where 'determinately' is meant to capture whatever the gap theorist's gaps leave out. Each should say what their theory assigns to (a)–(c), then press the other where the accounting strains: the gap theorist must explain what 'determinately' adds without minting a fresh revenge sentence, and the dialetheist must explain why calling the loop both true and false is more than relabeling the difficulty. Let them answer each other's actual moves, let each concede one point the other has landed, and end with the single question each thinks the other cannot answer.
