So I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background at all — the closest I got was an ethics elective I dropped in sophomore year — and somehow I ended up appointed to my city's public art and commemoration advisory comittee. Six of us, we meet in the library basement, mostly we rubber-stamp plaques for retired parks directors. Now the one genuinely hard case of the decade has landed on me and I have to write the memo.

Background: there's a bronze statue of Augustus Rennick, who died in 1910. He owned the iron works that basically built this city, donated the land for the park and the library, paid for the hospital wing. The statue went up in 1911 by public subscription and it's stood in the middle of Fenmore Park ever since. The neighborhood around the park was built by his workers, their grandkids still live there, and the workers' descendants association has held its Labor Day picnic at the statue for like eighty years. It's the only piece of public art in that whole part of town.

Last year a local historian, digging around for a totally unrelated project, turned up a page of company ledger and a 1908 newspaper interview suggesting Rennick's fortune was substantially built on leased convict labor in a mine his company owned. Everything else — the rest of the records — burned in a 1931 warehouse fire. So the claim rests on one ledger page and one interview. Local historians mostly accept it, but it's an inference, not a smoking gun. Nobody disputes the other thing she found, which is that in 1894 he brought in armed guards against his own striking workers.

Now the practical mess. The plaza is being rebuilt, so the statue was lifted off its base in March and is currently sitting on a pallet in the city works warehouse. The pedestal is still standing there empty. Our bylaw says the committee has to decide within 60 days of a monument being de-installed for capital work, which puts the deadline at the October 14 meeting. Reinstalling costs about $40k. Storage is $6k a year and the works department is already annoyed that the pallet is eating a bay. And the Lassiter Foundation — local family, tech money — has offered $250k for a new artwork for the plaza, on the condition that the Rennick statue be destroyed and melted down: not stored, not donated to a museum, not sold. They say a statue in a warehouse is itself a decision to keep it. The neighborhood arts group is thrilled. The descendants association is furious.

One of our members is a sculptor and wants a third option: a piece that's deliberately designed to erode away over about twenty years, so the plaza has something there that's honest about being temporary, or an empty pedestal with just the mounting bolts showing.

Four things the chair keeps asking and I don't know how to answer:

1. If we bolt a plaque onto the base saying what he did, are we honoring him or just recording him? And does that distinction actually mean anything when the thing is still a bronze guy on a pedestal in the middle of the park? Two members say the plaque is a fudge that lets everyone feel good and changes nothing.
2. Is leaving the base empty "doing nothing"? One council member already posted that this whole thing is presentism, judging a man of his time by our standards, and that the default should be to put it back. But putting it back costs money and is a vote, so I'm not sure there is a default.
3. How confident do we need to be before we change a public honor? One member says one ledger page and a newspaper quote is reasonable doubt and reasonable doubt means we keep it. That seems wrong to me but I can't say why.
4. Can we even take the Lassiter money with that condition on it, or are we being bought?

Please draft the memo I'll circulate before the 14th. I need one concrete recommendation — what happens to the statue, what ends up on that base, who pays for it, and by when — with the reasoning, and I need the strongest case for whichever option I'm not picking argued properly rather than waved off, because three people on that committee will vote the other way and I have to be able to answer them in the room. Don't bother with citations or a reading list, I just need the argument.
