(1) Heterophenomenology says we should treat a subject's verbal reports as data to be explained, not as authoritative testimony about what their experience is actually like.
(2) So the method's evidence is just the reports, plus the subject's behaviour and their dispositions to report in various circumstances.
(3) Any theory of consciousness built on that evidence alone is underdetermined with respect to the intrinsic character of the subject's experience.
(4) If two theories differ only over that intrinsic character, and no possible evidence separates them, they're empirically equivalent.
(5) So the method can't in principle distinguish a world in which subjects have rich phenomenal lives from one in which they're zombies who merely report as if they did.
(6) A method for the science of consciousness that can't in principle tell experience from its absence isn't a method for studying consciousness.
(7) Therefore heterophenomenology undermines itself.
