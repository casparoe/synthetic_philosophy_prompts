I've ended up in a reading group on culture and morality and we've been going round in circles for the best part of a year on essentially one question: how much rule-following should a society actually demand of its members? The setup is the familiar tight-versus-loose thing — some cultures have strong norms, low tolerance for deviation, heavy sanctioning; others are permissive. On one side you've got the people (mostly the anthropologically-minded ones) who say you can't judge this from the outside: tightness is a functional response to real pressures, whether that's disease, scarcity or just living on top of each other, and outsiders have no business calling it oppressive. On the other side you've got the liberal-universalist types who say it plainly is oppressive — it crushes dissent, it lands hardest on women and minorities and anyone who doesn't fit, and "it's their culture" isn't a justification. Here's the frustrating bit: everyone in the room agrees on the empirical findings, agrees that threat and tightness go together, agrees that tight societies do tend to have more order and less visible deviance. And yet nobody has moved an inch. So I want a proper diagnosis rather than a verdict.

Act as a philosopher of social science who's also comfortable in moral philosophy — someone who's thought hard about how descriptive claims about norms relate to normative ones. Before you answer, spend a moment brainstorming the candidate diagnoses, then pick.

Take the debate apart. Is this a hidden empirical question in disguise — i.e. does tightness actually deliver the order and cooperation it's supposed to, or does it mostly suppress visible dissent while the underlying coordination would have happened anyway? Is it a disagreement about what counts as a moral reason — collective welfare and stability versus individual autonomy — where each side simply weights a reason the other acknowledges? Is it bedrock intuition, where one side finds conformity itself degrading and the other finds it unremarkable? Or are the two camps answering different questions under one label — one asking how we should understand a practice, the other asking how we should judge it?

Here's my own hypothesis, and I want you to say whether it holds up: I suspect a lot of the stalemate comes from the concept doing too much work. "Tightness" seems to bundle at least three seperate things — how widely shared the norms are, how strongly deviation is punished, and whether enforcement runs top-down from an authority or sideways through everyone watching everyone. And those come apart normatively. A norm that's self-policing because everyone genuinely holds it is a very different object of moral assessment from one held in place by a state or a patriarch with a stick. If the two camps are picturing different bundles, the arguement can't resolve, because they're not even assessing the same thing. Tell me if that's the real fault line or if I'm flattering myself.

I don't want a survey of who said what — I know the positions. I want the diagnosis with reasons, an honest call on whether the disagreement is substantive or verbal or a mix of both, and then, given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a distinction, a bit of empirical work, a reframing, or nothing at all because the question is ill-posed. If it's ill-posed, say so and say why.
