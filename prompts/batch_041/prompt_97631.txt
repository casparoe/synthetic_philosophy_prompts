I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, drafting the framing document for an adversarial collaboration between the two readings of the color phi phenomenon. In the basic effect, two dots in different locations flash in succession in different colors, and observers report a single dot traveling between the locations and changing color halfway, apparently experiencing the second color at a point in the trajectory earlier than the second flash. The Stalinesque reading puts the interpolation before experience: the brain waits for the sequence to complete and delivers an already-consistent experience, so the experience itself is delayed and contains no error. The Orwellian reading puts it after: experience is delivered promptly and matches the stimuli, and it is the memory the report draws on that is later rewritten. Draft the joint statement that a committed Orwellian and a committed Stalinesque could both sign. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims, including what the reports do and do not establish and whether the two views differ in any prediction at all. Then give a table with each side's position in wording its own holder would accept, the cruxes, and, for each crux, a manipulation both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; use as one crux a dual-report paradigm I am developing, in which subjects respond at the instant of the second flash and then give a retrospective report, and state what each side predicts the two reports will do. Finally, tell me which part of the dispute, if any, is verbal or evidentially idle, and why.
