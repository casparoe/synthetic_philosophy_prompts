Rank the following items, drawn from the literature on rigid designation and the necessary a posteriori, in tiers according to how well each supports the thesis that some truths are both necessary and knowable only a posteriori:

(a) Hesperus is Phosphorus.
(b) Water is H₂O.
(c) Gold is the element with atomic number 79.
(d) Heat is molecular motion.
(e) The standard metre bar in Paris is one metre long.
(f) Had someone other than Gödel proved the incompleteness theorems, the name 'Gödel' would still have referred to Gödel.
(g) A case of my own construction: for generations a community has used the term 'lumenite' for a mineral it identifies by its distinctive violet fluorescence. It now turns out that the fluorescence is produced by two chemically unrelated minerals found in the same deposits, both of which the community calls 'lumenite', and that one of them, lumenite-A, has a hidden microstructure M. Their scientists announce that lumenite is M.

A tier list with one line of justification per item is enough. For each item, also say whether the necessity it exhibits attaches to the objects themselves or only to the rules governing our terms, and defend your placement of the items you rank highest and lowest.
