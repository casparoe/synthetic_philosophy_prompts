I recently became the sourcing lead for a small seafood company, and for the first time I have to decide which aquaculture practices I am willing to buy from, which has forced me to articulate intuitions I did not know I had. Here are my verdicts. Crowded salmon pens where fish rub their fins raw: not fine. Slaughtering salmon by percussive stunning followed by bleeding: fine. Slaughtering trout by leaving them in an ice slurry: not fine. Boiling a lobster alive: not fine. Shucking an oyster alive: fine. Eyestalk ablation on shrimp broodstock to induce spawning: not fine. Buying shrimp from a farm that electrically stuns before slaughter: fine. Buying wild-caught shrimp killed without stunning: not fine. I notice that I am fine with eating oysters and mussels, which are animals, but not with shrimp from a farm that ablates eyestalks, though I have no confident view about whether shrimp feel pain; and I am untroubled by stunning a fish but not by killing a crustacean without it. The principle I keep reaching for is that what matters is the capacity to suffer, judged by evidence rather than by whether the animal has a backbone. Is there a principle that captures all of these verdicts, or do some of them have to go? Please state it as precisely as you can, tell me what it implies for cases I have not considered -- a farmed octopus, a line of shrimp bred to lack pain receptors, and the case I keep going back and forth on, shrimp from a farm that stuns but still crowds them -- and say honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
