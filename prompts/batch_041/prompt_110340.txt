ok, I've been arguing with a friend for about a year over whether DNA is really a code and I want to stop going in circles. Two camps. Camp one, call them the information-realists: the genetic code is the real deal — a genuine code, DNA states carry information in the full-blooded sense, there are right and wrong answers about what a given stretch of DNA says. Camp two, the deflationists: that's all a metaphor we could drop tomorrow at no cost to biology — there's a molecule, there's a translation machine, and there's a contingent mapping between triplets and amino acids, and nobody is sending anything to anybody.

Draft the joint statement the two of us could actually both sign. Numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table. Break the whole thing out by three sub-claims, because I'm fairly sure we've been conflating them: (a) DNA is a code in the plain cipher sense — the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is arbitrary rather than forced by chemistry; (b) DNA states carry semantic information, i.e. they have content, they're about something, they can be right or wrong; (c) the genome is a program/blueprint/recipe for the organism. My hunch, and feel free to argue with it inside the statement, is that (a) is true and near-trivial and doesn't get you (b), and that most of our shouting has really been about (c) with (b) quietly doing the work.

The cruxes I want pinned down, each worded so its own side would accept the wording as its own: is an arbitrary mapping enough for code-hood (a knot in a string is arbitrary too); does anything have to play the sender's role, and is natural selection a sender or just a sieve; can a DNA state misrepresent — my friend says a mutation is just a different molecule, I say the gene can be wrong; and is the information talk pulling any explanatory weight, or is it just redescribing the chemistry. For each crux give a case or consideration we'd both agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction about that case in advance.

Also, if part of the fight is verbal — if we mean two different things by 'code' and half the disagreement evaporates once we seperate them — say which part and why. And dont just tell me the mapping is arbitrary, everyone knows that. Tell me what follows from it and what doesn't.

One thing to build in: the thought experiment where the same triplet table turns up somewhere with no evolutionary history at all, say a lab-built translation machine that happens to use the same mapping. Both camps have to say whether that counts as a code and whether it carries information, and if the answer is 'no, because no selection,' own it out loud instead of hedging.
