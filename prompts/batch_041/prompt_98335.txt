So I'm giving a work-in-progress talk in a couple weeks to what's nominally our department's Wittgenstein reading group but has basically turned into a seminar where people present their own work — I've got 25 minutes plus 40 for discussion, so the discussion is where I live or die. The paper is about On Certainty, and the line I'm pushing is that the standard reading of the hinge remarks — hinges as non-epistemic, animal, unjustified commitments that simply can't be doubted — buys you a tidy answer to the skeptic at the price of making hinge *change* inexplicable. If hinges sit outside the space of reasons, there's no way to describe what happens when someone's whole picture shifts: a conversion, a scientific revolution, a student who comes to see a proof differently. My move is to say hinges aren't beliefs or attitudes at all but normative constraints on what counts as a reason within a domain, and that they get revised not by argument but by something closer to a change in what you can do — I call it a shift in the 'space of moves' rather than in reasons, if that isn't too cute.
