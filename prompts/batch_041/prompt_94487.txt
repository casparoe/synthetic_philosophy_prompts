Ok so this is kind of a weird one but it's been eating at me for a couple weeks now. I just started a job where part of what I do is reviewing proofs that other people have formalized in a proof assistant (Lean, mostly), and a guy on the team made an offhand joke that if the foundations of math turned out to be inconsistent, we'd all be out of a job — because every proof we've ever "verified" would be worthless overnight. I laughed, and then could'nt stop thinking about it. Now I want to actually get a grip on where the serious open questions are, not the pop-sci version.

So here's what I'm asking. Take seriously the possibility that the basic axioms of math — ZFC or whatever a proof assistant's foundation actually is, plus the extra axioms people bolt on top — might be inconsistent, and that we might simply not have found the contradiction yet. What are the main open questions or lines of work in that area, and which ones actually matter most, in what order?

I don't mean "is math consistent, yes or no." I mean the sub-questions underneath it. Like: how much of math you'd have to give up if the bad axiom turned out to be one specific thing rather than another; whether an inconsistency would even be detectable in practice, given that a proof of 0=1 might be astronomically long and nobody could check it; what the paraconsistent people are actually proposing and whether that's a real fallback or just a cope; whether the empirical/inductive case for consistency is as strong as everyone seems to assume; what specifically happens to proof assistants and verified software if the floor gives out; and whether the whole worry is even coherent once you notice we could always weaken the axioms and rebuild from something safer.

What I want is a prioritized agenda, not a survey, and definately not a reading list — I can't go searching for papers at work anyway. For each item: why it's upstream of the others (or why it isn't), how tractable it is for someone who's a decent programmer but only has undergrad-level math, and what would move it up or down your list. If two of the things I named are really the same question wearing different hats, just say so. And if I'm missing a question that's more important than everything I listed, tell me that instead of humoring my list.

For context on the "for whom" part: this is for me, I've got maybe a few months of evenings and two friends I can rope in, and the end goal is either a long blog post or just being able to hold my own when this comes up. So rank for that, not for a funded research lab.
