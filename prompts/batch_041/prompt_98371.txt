My roommate and I have been arguing about whether it makes any sense at all to vote, and he wrote out this argument that I can't climb out of. I don't think he's right, but I definately can't find the hole, and it bugs me. Here it is the way he put it:

(1) Voting is worth doing only if there's a real chance your one vote changes who wins — that it's the vote that breaks the tie or pushes someone over the line.
(2) In any election bigger than a few thousand people, the chance your vote is that decisive vote is astronomically small. People throw around numbers like one in ten million for a presidential race, sometimes worse.
(3) The payoff when your vote does turn out to be the decisive one is huge — the difference between one person running the country and the other — but it's not infinite.
(4) So the expected payoff is that tiny chance times that big-but-finite payoff, and it comes out smaller than what voting costs you: the hour in line, the hassle, the time you spend figuring out who's who.
(5) When the expected payoff is less than the cost, it's a bad deal. So voting is a bad deal and a clear-headed person wouldn't do it.
(6) And it's not only voting. Same arithmetic for giving one dollar to a giant charity, for being the one person who takes the bus instead of driving, for being the one person who recycles. Your piece is a drop in the bucket in every case, so they're all bad deals too.
(7) Now suppose everyone actually reasoned this way and stayed home. The worse candidate wins, and everybody ends up worse off — including the clear-headed person who did the reasoning.
(8) Any way of deciding that, if everybody used it, leaves everybody worse off including the people using it, cannot be the right way to decide. So something in (1)-(4) has to be wrong.
(9) But (5) follows straight from (1)-(4), and (6) is the exact same move, and (6) looks just as solid as (5). And notice (6) would also have told a lone abolitionist in a pro-slavery town not to bother, which feels insane. So I'm stuck.

Honestly I'd rather keep (8), because without it I feel like I have to say it makes no difference whether I do anything at all, and that seems crazy to me. But (1)-(4) look like plain arithmetic, so I can't see how to keep (8) either. So: which premise or step do I throw out, and what do I have to swallow to throw it out? And I want you to be straight with me about which pieces of this are actually settled among people who study it and which are still genuinely fought over — don't hand me one side's answer as if it's the last word. Plain American English please, I'm not a philosopher.
