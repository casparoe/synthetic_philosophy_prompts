I'm putting together a handout for my reading group on Parfit's hitchiker — the desert one, where you're stranded and someone offers you a lift to town on the condition that you promise to pay them once you arrive, and you can already see that the you who arrives will have no reason at all to hand over the money (and the driver can see it too, which is the whole problem). I want a villanelle for it. The two refrains should be the promise and the refusal — the words you say in the desert and the words you'd say in the town — and I'd like them to differ by just a single word, so the repetition is almost exact and the whole dilemma lives in that one hinge. The form should do the work: the lines keep coming back, and each time the ground under them has shifted, which is exactly what happens to a promise made by one version of you and inherited by another. Close where Parfit actually lands — that the promise has to be kept by the desert you and not the town you, and that what looks like plain irrationality from the town might have been the only rational thing to be from the desert. If someone knows the case it should land, and if they don't they should still feel the trap shut.
