Hey, I'm in 11th grade and for my creative writing class we have to write a short story that's actually about an idea, not just plot. I want to write about this thing I heard about — in some countries like Norway and Sweden there's this idea called jantelagen, which is roughly like dont think you're special or better than everybody else, and in Australia and New Zealand they call it tall poppy syndrome, where if you grow taller than the rest people cut you down. The usual version of that story is just everyone is jealous of the smart kid and I want to do something less obvious.

My idea: a small poor town thats been shrinking for years. The town council passes an actual rule — a bylaw — that nobody can display wealth or achievement. No new cars, no trophies in the front window, and if you win any big money you have to put it in a town fund. The main character is a girl my age who wins a big national scholarship worth alot of money, and she has to decide whether to take it and leave forever or stay and give it up. I want the rule to feel kind of reasonable and even noble at first, then show what it costs people, without making either side the obvious villain. Can you have an old guy give a speech defending the rule, so we can see its a whole worldview about what people owe each other and not just envy? And then show it bumping up against the whole American Dream thing — pull yourself up, you earned it, you deserve it. I want her choice to be really hard and I dont want it wrapped up all neat at the end. Like 1500 words ish. Make the town feel real, not like a fable.
