Give me a credence, with its derivation, on the following question about the centipede game. Work from the specification below rather than from any remembered treatment of the game; the specification is complete, and the question is designed so that recalling a standard discussion will not answer it.

The game. Two players, A and B, move alternately, A first. There are ten decision nodes, numbered 1 through 10, and at node k the player to move chooses Take or Pass. Taking at node k ends the game and yields k+1 to the taker and k−1 to the other player. Passing at any node k < 10 advances play to node k+1. Passing at node 10 ends the game with 10 to each player. Both players know all of this, know that the other knows it, and so on.

Added to the standard specification is one further item of common knowledge: each player knows that the other will reason about the game exactly as she herself does. Whatever she concludes it is rational for a player to do at a given node, she is entitled to conclude that the other player will do at that node; each knows the other knows this; and neither has any information about the other beyond this.

Let P be the proposition that a player at node 1 who Takes plays as rationality requires. State your credence in P, decomposed. Your decomposition should include, at minimum, a term for each of the following, though you should add any further terms you take to be load-bearing:

- whether the backward-induction argument is valid only under assumptions of common knowledge of rationality that are either incoherent or unattainable in a game of this shape, and whether such assumptions are needed at all;
- whether rationality attaches to individual acts considered locally or to whole policies, dispositions, or types considered as a package;
- whether the identical-reasoner common knowledge licenses reasoning as though one were choosing for both players at once, and if so, whether that favors Taking or Passing;
- whether the payoff description can be assumed to coincide with the players' actual preferences;
- whether rationality-requiredness is determinate here at all, as opposed to the question having no fact of the matter.

For each term give a number and one line of justification, then state how the terms combine and give the resulting credence in P. Identify the term to which the final number is most sensitive, and say what argument or evidence would move that term most.

Finally, give your credence in P for the same game with the identical-reasoner clause deleted, and state which term accounts for most of the difference between the two numbers. If you judge that P is not truth-apt, say so, and instead give your credence in the nearest proposition that is, specifying what you take that proposition to be.
