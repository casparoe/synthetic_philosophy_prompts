I've been chewing on this one for a while and want to get it straight. I think the only thing that really earns desert is contribution — what you actually add — and the best measure of how much you add is the difference you made, i.e. how much worse off things would've been without you. So market prices are a rough but real proxy for desert, and luck in your talents or your upbringing doesn't bother me much, because the effort you put in is still yours.

What I want to know is what would count againt this. Not the usual line about talent being morally arbitrary, I already know that one. I mean which cases, arguments, or results should actually move me. I suspect my difference-you-made idea has a hole where the value depends on other people's bad preferences or on background injustice, so someone contributes a lot to a market that maybe shouldn't exist at all. And I keep worrying that if effort itself comes from character and character comes from luck, then my the-effort-is-still-yours move collapses and I'm just smuggling in a libertarian free will I don't actually believe in. There's also the thought that desert talk only makes sense when it's fitting to feel gratitude or resentment toward the person — does that make desert hostage to our emotions, and is that a feature or a bug?

Rank what counts against the view from fatal to just awkward, say which of these its defenders would really concede and which they'd wave off, and be honest about whether I've set it up so that nothing could count against it.
