For most of my career as an experimentalist I thought about epistemic virtue the way an instrument engineer does: a claim made with seventy percent confidence was worth something only if, across many such claims, seventy percent of them came true, and long-run calibration was my standard of good judgment. Since retiring I have been reading philosophers who hold that credences are to be judged by their accuracy — by how close each one comes to the truth — and who argue that probabilistic coherence and conditionalization can be derived from that single standard rather than from Dutch books or from the demands of practical decision. Please set out the most important implications of this accuracy-first program for someone with my habits of mind. I am particularly interested in whether accuracy and calibration can come apart in ways that should change how I reason, and in whether the program gives me any reason to keep or to abandon the sharp zeroes and ones that my statistical training tells me are never warranted. Please answer in American English.
