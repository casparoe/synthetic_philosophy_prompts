I have no academic background — I left school at seventeen and drive for a delivery firm — but a running disagreement has pushed me into thinking about something more carefully than I expected, and I would like it sorted out properly rather than settled by a shrug. Most evenings I spend in a long-running online world, a shared and persistent place with its own economy: the things players own there are only entries in the operator's database, but they can be sold to other players for ordinary money, and the friendships I have there are the main friendships I have, since a back injury has kept me at home for six years. Two things happened recently. A player I had known for two years borrowed a rare item from me, sold it on the world's official market, and kept the money; the operator's rules forbid exactly this, but the operator will not act. And in a headset session another player reached out and handled my avatar in a way that would have been a serious assault if our bodies had been in the same room, and I felt it for days afterwards. In both cases a friend I respect, who is not a callous man, answered with the same sentence — that it is just a game — clearly meaning it to close the matter and to tell me that being upset was out of proportion.

What I want from you is a full inventory of what that sentence could be claiming, because I suspect it was doing something much stronger than anything it literally says. Is it a claim about what exists, namely that the item, the market, the other player's body, and the harm are not real things at all? A claim about cause and effect, that nothing inside a game can reach the world outside, so that nothing of mine was actually damaged? A claim about morality, that harms of this kind do not count, or count less, or count only when the game's own rules forbid them? A claim about consent, that logging in amounts to agreeing to be treated this way? A claim about jurisdiction, that only the operator may judge, and it has declined to? Or merely a claim about what I am permitted to feel? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible, or plainly false, and whether, if it were true, it would actually license telling me to get over it; then say which reading my friend most likely had in mind, and which reading his reply would need in order to do the work he wanted it to do. Please lay this out as a numbered list in plain language, giving an example for each reading rather than attaching a name to it, since I will not remember names and would rather be argued with than referred to; and if there is a reading on which he is simply right, tell me so plainly, because I would rather know that than win the argument.
