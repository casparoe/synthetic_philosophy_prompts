ok so before I inflict my ethical views on anyone else I want them stress-tested, and I've got a feeling something in here doesn't hang together but I can't see where. Here's roughly where I've landed.

Suffering is the only thing thats intrinsically bad. Pleasure, and getting what you want, are at most instrumentally good — they matter because they tend to reduce suffering or because believing in them keeps people going, not because there's anything good about them in themselves. I'm a realist about all this, by the way: there's a fact of the matter about which worlds are worse, and it doesn't depend on what anyone happens to approve of.

I also think the badness of suffering is impersonal and time-blind. A unit of agony is a unit of agony whether it's mine, a stranger's, an animal's, or someone who won't be born for ten thousand years. So when I compare two futures I compare the total suffering in each, and the smaller total wins. And I think we owe the far future an enormous amount, just because there could be so many of them.

Here's where it gets less tidy. I'm not a fan of the world-exploder idea — the button that painlessly ends all life and thereby guarantees no future suffering. I think pressing it would be monstrous, and I don't think that's just squeamishness. Same for quietly killing a healthy person in their sleep: painless, no future suffering, but I'm certain it's wrong.

And I've got a strong gut that says you can't just add up mild harms until they outweigh a serious one. If the choice is torturing one person for a week versus giving ten thousand people a bad headache, I don't think the numbers get to decide it — the torture is off the table no matter how many headaches are on the other side. But my smaller-total-wins line seems to say the exact opposite.

Then there's the procreation thing. I think it's bad to bring someone into existence who'll suffer, but I don't think it's bad, or good, to fail to bring into existence someone who'd have been happy. That one seems to fall straight out of the suffering-only view, so maybe it's fine?

So — is this a coherent package, or am I quietly helping myself to goods I've already thrown out? I genuinely don't know which bit is the weak link and I'd rather you tell me than guess. Rank the tensions by how bad they are, say which are real and which only look real, and for each real one give me the cheapest patch and what the patch costs me. Don't hedge by telling me they're all fine or all fatal; I want an actual verdict with an ordering.

Please write it as continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Just an email.
