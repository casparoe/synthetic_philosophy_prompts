I want to write a short story and I'm stuck on how to do it right. The idea: a man has a small stroke or a bad knock to the head, and afterward he can still feel pain, but it doesn't bother him. Not in a tough-guy way. The doctor presses on his shin and he says yep, that hurts, right there, about a seven out of ten, and his face doesn't move, he doesn't pull his leg away, he's not bracing or being brave. He just doesn't mind it. I'm pretty sure this is a real thing, I think it's called pain asymbolia, where people say the pain is there but it isn't unpleasant for them, and some of them even seem to find it funny or interesting, though I might be mixing that up with something else. The feeling stays, the ouch goes.

What I want from the story: don't have anybody explain the science at me. One doctor can name it once and then shut up. I don't want a disease-of-the-week movie or a tidy ending. I want scenes that make the weirdness land. A few things I keep thinking about and would like in there somewhere: he burns his hand cooking and doesn't notice until he smells it, because pain used to be how he knew to stop. An ER visit where the nurse has to write a number on a chart and doesn't know what to do with a patient who rates his pain a nine and then asks if he can go home. His wife trying to figure out if the not-minding has spread to the other kinds of pain, the kind that isn't in your body. And one scene where somebody tells him he's lucky, that his pain is free now, and he can't tell if that's true.

I'd like it to sit with the question instead of answering it: is the badness in the feeling, or in the minding? If there was a pill that left every bit of the sensation and took away only the bother, would that be a cure or a different illness? And would it be wrong to fix him when he says he's fine? First person from him, present tense, so we're stuck inside a head that doesn't mind, maybe 1500 words. Keep it quiet and specific, no melodrama, and don't let him turn out to secretly be suffering all along, that feels like a cop-out.
