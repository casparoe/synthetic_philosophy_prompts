My friend Nadia and I spent most of last night arguing about prawns, which is a sentence I never expected to type. Here's where we got to. She has this test she calls the three strikes, for when an animal's suffering deserves to count morally. Strike one: it needs some kind of pain-sensing setup and also some way of dialling the signal down, like its own opioid-type chemicals or a descending pathway from higher up. Strike two: it has to trade something off, meaning it gives up food or shelter or a mate it clearly wants in order to get away from the bad thing, rather than just yanking away reflexively. Strike three: the bad thing has to leave a lasting mark on its behaviour, like guarding the sore spot for days afterwards, or getting skittish at whatever cue came before the shock. Her view is that pass all three and you owe the animal real moral consideration, pass none and you can basically do what you like with it. I think strike two is doing way more work than she admits, and that strike three is much weaker than she thinks it is. But don't just tell me who's right about her test, I want to see the test in action.

So please take these and put them in a tier list, S down to F or whatever scale you prefer, ordered by how strong the case is that the animal has morally significant pain experience: farmed Atlantic salmon, farmed Nile tilapia, farmed common carp, farmed rainbow trout, farmed whiteleg shrimp, farmed redclaw crayfish, European lobster, brown crab, farmed mud crab, the hermit crab, which nobody farms but which came up in the fight, and a farmed blue mussel thrown in as a control even though its not a fish or a crustacean. One sentence per animal explaining why it lands where it does.
