I need a case built to order for a seminar session on the problem of logical omniscience, so that students can watch an idealisation do its work instead of hearing it described. Two treatments of knowledge are to be used exactly as I state them here, and neither may be adjusted to suit the case you end up building.

Under the possibility treatment, an agent's epistemic state is the set of worlds compatible with it; the agent knows a proposition exactly when that proposition holds throughout the set; the state is stipulated consistent and equipped with full positive and negative introspection. Nothing about storage, form, or process enters, and so the agent knows every consequence of what it knows.

Under the item treatment, an agent's epistemic state is a finite collection of stored items, each with a proposition as its content, together with a finite set of rules taking items to items; an item is known when it is stored or when the rules yield it; and the rules operate on items as stored, not on their contents.

Construct one case containing two agents, alike in everything the possibility treatment can see and alike as well in the information their states carry, in the concepts available to them, and in the inferences they draw wherever the same item-shapes are present, with no shortage of time or attention for either. The item treatment must nevertheless return opposite verdicts for the two of them about a single mundane proposition, of the order of a face, a room, a schedule, or a route, and not a mathematical theorem or anything whose interest lies in the depth of a proof. Nothing may be permitted to do the work except the form in which the two states are realised: not a missing concept or a want of awareness, not an inconsistent state, not the absence of a rule of inference that any competent reasoner would have, such as modus ponens, conjunction elimination, or substitution of identicals, and not a stipulation that announces a verdict, such as simply decreeing that one treatment is right.

Then demonstrate the case. Name the proposition; give the verdict each treatment returns, with the reasoning that produces it; identify the feature of the case that does the work, and say what becomes of the case when that feature is removed, distinguishing carefully between ignorance under a resource limit and ignorance that further inquiry would cure. Set out the strongest objection open to the treatment that comes off worse, and the best reply to it. Say whether the difference between your two agents would show in what they do or say, and if it would not, whether that tells against the treatment that posits it. Then answer directly: is the agent who comes out ignorant genuinely ignorant, or only unable to avow what it knows, and what does the answer turn on? Finish by saying which treatment you think gets the case right.

If no case can satisfy all of this, do not quietly drop a constraint: identify the one you think cannot be met, give the argument, and build the closest case that meets the rest, marking the constraint you abandoned.
