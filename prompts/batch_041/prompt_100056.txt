Here is where I've landed:

1. Weakness of will is real: an agent can act against the judgment they themselves endorse at the moment of acting, not merely against a judgment they held at some earlier time.
2. The best explanation of the phenomenon is hyperbolic (or quasi-hyperbolic) discounting: the value we assign to a future reward rises disproportionately as the reward approaches, so preferences reverse as the option nears.
3. Preferences are revealed by choice; there is no further fact about what an agent "really" prefers over and above the pattern of their choices.
4. There is nothing intrinsically irrational about steeply discounting one's own future. A present-biased agent is not, just in virtue of that, making a mistake.
5. Procrastination and addiction are genuine failures of rationality, not merely unusual tastes.
