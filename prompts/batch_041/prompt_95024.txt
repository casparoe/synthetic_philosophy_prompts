I'm a novelist and I've been reading around in the philosphy of pleasure for a book I'm working on — one of the characters is a hedonist, and a fairly cold-blooded one, so I want his position to be the real thing and not a strawman. What I keep running into is a debate that seems to have been stuck for a very long time: what, if anything, do all pleasures have in common? One camp says pleasure is a distinctive way an experience feels, a quality of the sensation itself. Another says it's an attitude — a taking-pleasure-in — which can be directed at a proof, a nap, or a friend's humiliation, and that there's simply no shared feeling to point at. A third says there's no common property at all, that we lump these states together by family resemblance and should stop expecting a definition. And everyone in the room is using the same examples. Scratch a mosquito bite and it's bliss; keep going once the itch has gone and it's pure pain — same motion, opposite valence, and each side has something to say about it.

What I can't work out is what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal, with 'pleasure' meaning the felt quality to some people and the liking to others, so that they're not even contradicting each other? Is it two questions wearing one coat — what unifies the states we call pleasures, versus what makes a life go well — with the hedonist about well-being only needing an answer to the second, and the heterogeneity problem only really biting when you try to add pleasures up into a total? Or is it a genuine unresolved question about whether pleasure is a natural kind, where introspective evidence is worth very little and nobody knows yet? And if it's the last one, would anything count as progress — a distinction, a result from the lab, a decision to split the concept in two — or is the sane move to stop expecting one answer?

Diagnosis with reasons please, not a survey. And if I've got the shape of it wrong — if it isn't really stalled, or my three camps are a caricature, or the question can't be addressed without sources you can't get to offline — say so straight, I'd rather know.
