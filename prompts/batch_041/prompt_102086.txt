I am a third-year undergraduate writing a final paper for a seminar on moral psychology and its philosophical implications, and the assignment is to produce an adversarial-collaboration document. Please draft that document for me.

The two camps I am trying to bring into the same room are these. The first holds that moral foundations theory, understood as a descriptive account of how people across the political spectrum weight different kinds of moral concern, carries normative weight: it shows that political opponents are not morally deficient but differently constituted, and so it bears, at least indirectly, on the question of who is right. The second holds that no descriptive psychology of moral intuitions can settle a normative question, both because there is a gap between what people believe and what is true and because the empirical underpinnings of moral foundations theory are themselves contestable -- the foundations may not be robustly distinct, and the evidence is drawn disproportionately from narrow samples.

I would like the deliverable to contain: a numbered section of claims both camps can sign, including about what the theory does and does not claim; then a disagreements table in which each residual disagreement appears first in neutral wording, then in the formulation each side would accept for its own view, followed by the crux on which it turns; and, for each crux, a case, argument, or piece of evidence that both camps agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about the outcome recorded in advance. Where you judge that a disagreement is verbal -- that the two sides are using a term such as foundation, weight, or justification in different senses -- say so, identify the term, and explain what turns on the ambiguity.

One further requirement. I have been trying to formulate a criterion I want the document to test: a moral consideration does genuine justificatory work in a disagreement only if both parties, applying it symmetrically to their own favored position, would be willing to revise at least one of their settled judgments. Have both camps state whether they can accept this criterion, whether accepting it would move either of them, and what it would show if one camp can accept it and the other cannot. Please do not include reading lists, citations, or references; I need the reasoning itself, argued from the considerations a careful participant on each side would already have available.
