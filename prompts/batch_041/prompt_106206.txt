I've been lurking rationalist blogs and forums for like a decade now, and I keep circling back to sortition / lottocracy. The usual discussion is about whole countries, but I want to stress-test it on a small case I actually care about: an online community I'm in, ~2,000 active members, currently runs on elected mods plus karma/upvote-driven norms. A faction wants to scrap the elected mod team and pick mods by lot from the active membership -- 3-month terms -- and let any mod action be appealed to a randomly drawn jury of ~15 members whose verdict is binding. I don't want the generic "is this more democratic" angle. What are the most important implications of going lottocratic at this scale for (1) the actual quality of decisions, (2) who ends up holding informal power and influence once elections are gone, and (3) whether members feel the space is genuinely theirs vs. the admins'? And where is this definately going to backfire in ways the lottocracy fans never mention?
