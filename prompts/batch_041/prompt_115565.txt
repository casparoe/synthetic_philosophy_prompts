I have just concluded a long and rather heated argument with a friend about what David Hume would have made of the contemporary debate over dispositions, finks, and masks, and I would like an independent adjudication. My friend insists that the finkish and masked cases are fatal to any Humean attempt to understand dispositions in terms of regularities or counterfactual conditionals. A finkish disposition is one that an object genuinely has, but which would be removed or altered by the very stimulus that would ordinarily trigger its manifestation: a fragile glass, for example, that is guarded by a sorcerer who would make it unbreakable the instant it is struck. A masked disposition is one that an object genuinely has, but whose manifestation is blocked by an interfering condition: a poison that would kill if ingested, except that the person has taken an antidote. My friend argues that these cases show dispositions cannot be reduced to simple conditionals, because the disposition can be present even when the conditional is false. I am less convinced. I suspect Hume would have treated finks and masks as complications within his general picture rather than as refutations of it, and that he would have redescribed them as further evidence that our dispositional vocabulary projects customary expectations onto a world of constant conjunctions. What I want from you is a prediction, not a history lesson: what would Hume's considered position have been on the specific question of whether finks and masks undermine a regularity theory of dispositions? Would he have tried to repair the conditional analysis by adding further conditions, would he have conceded that dispositions are irreducible powers, or would he have preserved his skepticism about necessary connection by treating the cases as evidence that dispositional talk is a projection of custom? Please give a reasoned answer that distinguishes what follows from Hume's known commitments from what would be speculation, and do not simply summarize what later commentators have said.
