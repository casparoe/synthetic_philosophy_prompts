Bit of an odd ask, but you're the closest thing I've got to a philosopher on call. I've somehow ended up chairing the exhibitions sub-committee at our little city heritage trust (I did a history degree thirty years ago, which apparently makes me the resident expert on everything from the Bronze Age to Brexit), and we're finally building the permanent gallery about the local regiment in the First World War. The letters, the shell case, the diaries — all lovely, no controversy there. The fight is over one label. Our curator's draft says, roughly, that the war was Germany's war — that the Kaiser's government used the July Crisis to start a European war it had been angling for, to break out of encirclement and grab territory in Europe and Africa. She's got the Fischer crowd behind her, the whole 'grasp for world power' line, plus a couple of trustees who are convinced its the only honest thing to put on a wall. The other half of the table says we cannot possibly say that: it's the Versailles war-guilt clause in a museum's voice, it turns Austria-Hungary and Serbia and Russia and the blank cheque into scenery, and anyway — what does it even mean to say a nation is guilty of a war? Nations aren't people, they don't have intentions. And we're twinned with a town in Bavaria, so we get German visitors; one of our own trustees is the grandson of a German POW who stayed here after the war, and he says the label would make his family feel accused, and honestly I don't have a clean answer for him.

So: I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting — something we can actually vote on, with a concrete recommendation and a time frame, not another seminar. Tell me what the label should claim (or whether we quietly drop the causal claim, just describe the July Crisis, and let visitors draw their own conclusions), and engage the strongest version of the side you're ruling against instead of waving 'complexity' at everyone. If you can, say something real about what it means to hold a nation rather than an individual responsible — the difference between being to blame and being the thing that caused it, whether a 'nation' can be an agent at all, and whether that's even the sort of claim a public museum should be making. Somebody on the board keeps muttering about Jaspers and his seperate kinds of guilt (political versus moral, I think), so if that's genuinely useful, use it; if it's a red herring, say so. And no bibliography, please — I can't exactly hand the trustees a reading list.
