Ok so I've sat on our town's ordinance review committee for two years now and it's just dawned on me that I have no actual theory of how I read a statute, so I want you to reverse-engineer one from my gut calls. Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my question back at me.

The provision I keep circling is Riverbend Municipal Code 12-4, headed "Rental Scooter Safety":

(a) No person shall operate a motorized scooter on the Riverwalk.
(b) "Motorized scooter" means any two-wheeled device propelled by a motor, whether or not it has a seat.
(c) "Riverwalk" means the paved pedestrian path from Mile 0 to Mile 2.

Here's what I'd say if you put me on the stand, in the order I'd have to defend them:

1. The rental stand-up scooters, two wheels and a throttle: banned, obviously.
2. A pedal-assist e-bike: not banned. The whole fight that produced this section was about the rental scooters, and an e-bike is definately a bike with a helper motor, not a scooter.
3. The guy with MS who rides a two-wheeled motorized mobility thing to the farmers market: not banned, and I don't care that the words read on him.
4. A one-wheeled electric unicycle: not banned. I hate it, it's exactly the hazard the section is about, but the section says two-wheeled and I won't stretch a physical description to catch something the drafters didn't picture.
5. A self-balancing two-wheeled board with no handlebars: banned, because the words cover it and my personal view that they're ridiculous is neither here nor there.
6. The new paved stretch from Mile 2 to Mile 3 that parks has already stenciled "Riverwalk" on: not covered. The definition stops at Mile 2.

My own working guess is that the words set a ceiling and purpose can only narrow inside it, never widen it -- but that feels like a rule I invented to save my own verdicts, and I suspect its backwards from how most people think about this. So: is there a principle that gets all six, stated precisely enough that I could apply it cold? What does it say about a three-wheeled electric scooter (two wheels up front), and about an e-bike for somebody who can't pedal at all? And be straight with me about which of my six calls I'd have to give up.
