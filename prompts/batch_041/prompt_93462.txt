I am assembling a position statement for a graduate seminar on practical reason, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments are these:

(1) The Grip Condition: a consideration is a normative reason for an agent to act only if it can engage some element of her wide motivational set—her desires, values, projects, and self-conceptions, including those she would endorse on reflection. A reason that could never grip her is no reason for her.

(2) The Warrant Condition: some considerations—gratuitous cruelty, betrayal of trust, and the like—are reasons for every agent, even for an agent whose wide motivational set contains nothing that could engage them. Warrant is not reducible to grip.

(3) Rationality is fundamentally coherence among attitudes: beliefs, desires, intentions, and emotions ought to fit together in the usual ways (consistency, means-end coherence, proportionality). Rationality itself supplies no substantive ends.

(4) An agent can be fully coherent in the sense of (3) and still be criticizable for failing to act on a consideration that is a reason for her. The criticism is not merely that her attitudes fail to cohere; it is that she has failed to respond to something that counted in favor of acting.

(5) The expression has a reason is systematically ambiguous between (a) there is a consideration that counts in favor of the agent's acting, and (b) the agent is in a position to act, or has the opportunity and capacity to act. These two readings should not be run together.

(6) Normative reasons justify rather than merely explain. An explanation of why someone acted may cite a consideration that was not a reason for her at all; a normative reason must meet a justificatory standard.

(7) An agent's reasons are fixed by her actual wide motivational set, not by what she would desire or endorse if she were fully informed, vividly imaginative, and free of confusion. The idealized-counterpart version of internalism makes reasons too independent of the agent's actual commitments.

(8) Moral criticism can be apt even when the criticized agent could not have been moved by the relevant moral consideration, provided that the consideration has warrant in the sense of (2).

I have a nagging sense that something in this package is doing illicit work, but I cannot tell what. Please audit the set. I am not asking for a reading list, citations, or a survey of the literature; I want the conceptual work done on the commitments as stated.

Please structure the response under the following headings, in this order, and in British English:

1. Summary of the position (about 100 words).
2. Genuine tensions, ordered by severity (about 600 words). For each, identify the commitments that pull against each other, say whether the tension is logical or motivational, explain why it is not merely apparent, and state what would have to give to resolve it.
3. Apparent tensions that dissolve (about 250 words). For each, explain why the pair looks inconsistent and why it is not.
4. Cheapest repairs and their costs (about 350 words). For each genuine tension, give the least costly revision and say what is lost by making it.
5. Overall verdict (about 150 words). Say whether the package can be held together at all, and if not, which single commitment is doing the most damage.

Be specific about which numbered commitment is implicated at each point, and do not treat every tension as equally severe. I would rather have two or three tensions ranked carefully than a list of everything that could be questioned.
