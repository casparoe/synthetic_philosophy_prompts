I run the small-objects dept at a regional auction house, and there's a pattern I keep hitting that I don't have a word for. A piece turns out to be a fake — an old one, well made, made by somebody with real skill and a reason — and the market does what you'd expect and drops it. Then a few years later it comes back around, and now people are bidding on it *because* it's a fake, and it goes for more than the genuine but dull things by the artist it was imitating. Same object, same brushwork; the only thing that changed is that everybody knows, and the knowing is what the money is for now. I see a cousin of this with honest copies — the museum cast of a statue that no longer exists, the plaster replica that's the only surviving record of what a thing looked like — where the value obviously isn't in how it looks but in its position in a story about some other object. Is there a name for this? I've read the usual stuff about forgeries being bad becuase they cut you off from the artist's achievement, and about aura, but that all seems to be about loss, and I'm asking about the second life. If nothing fits, coin something and tell me exactly where it starts and stops — in particular, is the flip the same phenomenon as the replica's value, or two different things wearing similar clothes? Say how confident you are in each part of this and flag the part you're least sure of.
