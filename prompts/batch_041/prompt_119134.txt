I'm a quant — rates desk, mostly Markov-switching models and break tests, that sort of thing — and I've been reading pop archaeology about "collapse" (Bronze Age, the Maya, Rapa Nui) on the train, and I keep trying to translate it into my own language and watching pieces fall off. Do the translation properly for me.

Take the whole debate about whether societies collapse — whether that's a real event that happens to something called a society, or whether what we're looking at is just change: same variables, different levels, a different scale of analysis — and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use. Regime switching. Structural breaks, and the difference between a level shift and a change in the growth rate. Unit root versus mean reversion. Drawdowns and recovery half-lives. Hysteresis. Absorbing states. The fact that you can only really distinguish a regime change from a very large drawdown ex post, which I think matters more than anyone admits. Also feed in the resilience-ecology version of this, where systems spend a long time accumulating, get over-connected and rigid and efficient, and then release into a reorganisation phase, and whether you call that a collapse or just a phase transition depends entirely on what scale you're standing at — I've never known what to do with that, it seems to map onto my thing but I can't tell whether it's the same claim.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually want. What survives the translation cleanly, what survives only with fudging, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Things I'm already suspicious about: (1) society as the unit — my series need a well-defined object and I don't think they've got one, the Maya didn't stop existing; (2) complexity — everyone says collapse is a loss of complexity, but complexity is a latent factor with no agreed measurement, so how much of this debate is about a variable nobody can pin down; (3) the evaluative content — collapse sounds bad, but in my language it's a level shift, so is failure doing any work beyond a loss function the observer picked; (4) the timing thing — if I can only call it a regime change because it never comes back, then "it collapsed" is partly a forecast dressed up as a description.

The early-warning-signals stuff — rising variance, rising autocorrelation before a tipping point — does that have any analogue in the archaeological record, or is the data so sparse that it isn't even a meaningful claim? And seperate the disagreements that are identification problems (tiny N, no counterfactual, no exogenous variation) from the ones that are definitional (the variable isn't well-defined to begin with), because in my world those are completely different kinds of trouble and I think people conflate them constantly.

Last thing: is the residue a defect of my framework, or a sign that the archaeologists are smuggling in assumptions my framework is right to refuse? I've never read any archaeology properly, so don't assume I know the literature, but don't dumb down the econometrics either.
