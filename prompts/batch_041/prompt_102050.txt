I keep running into two pretty different pictures of what "the will of the people" is even supposed to be. On one, it's a single thing — the people as one collective agent with one will, roughly the general-will idea you get from Rousseau. On the other, it's just whatever falls out when you add up a bunch of individual preferences, say by voting. Can you compare these two pictures, especially on which one is more useful for criticising a goverment that claims to be acting in the people's name? And then someone might propose a third option: that the will of the people is whatever a majority would endorse after a long, sober discussion where everyone's been given the same information — call it the "cooled-down majority" view. Does that actually fix the problems of the first two, or is it just the second picture in disguise?
