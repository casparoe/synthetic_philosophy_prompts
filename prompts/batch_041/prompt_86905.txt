(2) A person who is equally hungry and thirsty, standing exactly midway between food and drink, and who simply stays put, is not thereby irrational; there is nothing reason tells them to do. (3) When two options are genuinely on a par, there is no fact of the matter about which was the better choice; choosing is not a way of finding out. (4) All the same, in such a case I can settle it with a bare act of decision, just taking one, and that act is not itself the expression of any preference for the one over the other. (5) Self-reproach is fitting only where I could have done better given what I knew at the time. (6) I do reproach myself for arbitrary picks that turn out badly, kicking myself over joining the wrong queue at the airport even though the queues looked identical, and I think that reproach is fitting rather than just a habit I'm stuck with. (7) I settle these cases with coins, dice, and standing rules such as always taking the leftmost, and I take this to be good practice rather than a failure of agency. Which of these actually conflict, which only look like they do, and how bad is each conflict? Order them by severity, say for each what would have to give, and tell me the single revision that would clean up the most with the least loss. Please don't just tell me to define my terms; I want to know what the cheapest way out is and what it costs.
