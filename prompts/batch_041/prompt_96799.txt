1. Disposition talk is true, not just shorthand for how things happen to go. A glass's fragility is a real property of the glass, not a disguised prediction about what'll happen to it.
2. No conditional analysis of dispositions can be right. You can't cash "fragile" out as "would break if struck," not even with bells and whistles bolted onto the conditional.
3. Finkish cases and masking cases are really one phenomenon, not two: in both, the trigger just happens to drag its own defeater along with it, so whatever treatment is right for one should be right for the other.
4. And the right treatment is to keep the conditional and add a clause: x is disposed to M in conditions C iff, if C obtained and nothing interfered, x would M.
5. The disposition isn't identical to its categorical basis — the glass's fragility isnt the same thing as its molecular structure — but it's grounded in that basis; they're distinct existences, one resting on the other.
6. Fragility earns its keep causally. It's part of why the glass breaks, right alongside the structure, not a shadow cast by it.
7. Fragility is intrinsic. It doesn't depend on what's around the glass, and two glasses alike in all their intrinsic properties have all the same dispositions.
