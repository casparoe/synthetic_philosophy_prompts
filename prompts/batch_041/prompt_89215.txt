At a dinner last week two of my friends got into it over an essay one of them had been reading, and I've been turning it over ever since because I honestly couldn't say who had the better of it. The essay is by Hofstadter -- he writes it as a fake old newspaper column by some outraged pundit who can't stand that people want to change the language, except Hofstadter has swapped race in for sex: the columnist is grumbling about "chairwhite" and "whitekind" and about the "black libbers" who want to stop us saying "All whites are created equal," and he defends every bit of it with exactly the arguments people use for "chairman" and "mankind" and "All men are created equal." The joke being that the race version sounds obviously monstrous, so the gender version should too.

Dan thought that basically ends the argument. His line: whenever someone says "mankind really includes women, it's just a generic," they're pulling the identical move the parody racist pulls with "white really includes everyone," so that defence is dead on arrival, and anyone still reaching for it is either confused or hiding. Priya said the parody is clever but proves alot less than Dan thinks. Her line: "white" is a word that always drags its opposite along with it -- there's no way to hear "whitekind" that isn't about race -- whereas "man" has a real, ordinary use where it just means human being, like in "man is a rational animal" or the way Latin homo worked; so the racist's defence is bad for a reason the sexist's isn't, and Dan is squashing two different things into one. Dan came back that the reason "man" can float like that is precisely that maleness is the default, and that the fact a word can be used inclusively tells you nothing about whether it quietly centres one group -- "white" can be used inclusively too, that's the whole bit. Priya said that's a claim about how people hear the word, not about what it means, and that Dan is smuggling meaning and vibes together; she also said that to get from "the race version sounds awful" to "the gender defence is dead" you already have to assume the two cases are alike, which is the very thing in dispute, so the parody can't be doing the work Dan wants it to. Dan said if you demand a separate argument that the cases are alike before you'll accept the satire, you'll wait forever, because the whole point of satire is to make you see a likeness you were blind to, and asking it to prove the likeness up front is asking it not to be satire.

I can't referee this and I don't think either of them is being dumb, so could you go through it move by move and say who's right about what -- which of Dan's claims hold up, which of Priya's, and where their each talking past the other? And I have a hunch they're both leaning on something about what a word "includes" that neither of them has said out loud, so if there's a distinction underneath all this that they're both missing, drag it out into the open. I don't just want a winner, I want the steps.
