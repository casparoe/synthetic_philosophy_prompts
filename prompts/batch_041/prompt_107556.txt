I'm a gameplay engineer on a VR physics sandbox, and I've hit a weird design argument that's turned into a late-night thing with my flatmate who does philosophy. The level is a lonely cosmos: exactly one object, a big torus station, floating in an otherwise empty void. No stars, no planets, no backdrop. Players can walk inside the ring. If the ring is spinning, they get pressed to the outer wall like artificial gravity; if it isnt, they float. That's how the engine works now: there's a hidden variable for the station's spin relative to the simulation's world axes, and centrifugal force uses it.

My lead designer says that's a bug and an embarassment. In an empty universe, she says, there is no fact of the matter about whether the station is spinning; all that exists is the relative motion of things, and since there's only one thing, its spin is meaningless. She wants me to delete the world-axis variable and compute everything from relative positions and velocities only. I kind of agree in the abstract, but then the whole level stops working: the players float, the ring has no gravity, and the fiction falls apart. Our other engineer says we should keep the hidden variable and call it a fixed background, but that feels like sneaking in an invisible thing. I'm stuck.

What I want from you is advice on what to actually do, not a history of the debate. If you were me, would you keep the force? Is the lead right that spin needs something to be spin relative to? And if so, what's the smallest thing you'd have to add to the void to make the spin real -- a single dust mote, a distant galaxy, a second counter-rotating station? Does it matter whether the added thing is causally connected to the station or just there? Also, what if we made the whole universe rotate instead and kept the station still -- would the players feel anything different, and should the engine even be able to tell those two descriptions apart? I need a practical recommendation plus the reasoning, and if the honest answer is that our engine is making a choice rather than discovering a fact, tell me what the choice commits us to and what I should say to my lead.
