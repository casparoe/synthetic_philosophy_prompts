I'm not an academic, I just fell down this hole after a fight with my brother about whether it's hypocritical to fly to a climate march, and I've been chewing on it ever since. The thing that keeps stopping me is the inefficacy thought: my one burger, my one flight, my one vote doesn't change the outcome, so on a straight consequences view there's no reason to change anything, and yet I can't shake the feeling that this reasoning is somehow cheating. I've poked around enough to know there's a real literature on this -- collective action, complicity, threshold effects, "the problem of many hands" and so on -- but honestly I can't tell which of these threads are the live ones that everything else hangs on, and which are just people restating the same disagreement in new words.

So pretend I've got about three years, a blog, and a small group of maybe five people who'd meet once a month to work on the theory side of this -- not the climate science, not organizing tactics, just the conceptual question of what an individual is doing when they add a drop to a big harm. Which open questions here would you actually put at the top, and in what order? A couple of framings we've been kicking around, in case they're useful or in case they're red herrings: one friend insists the whole thing dissolves once you split "making a difference" into at least three separate things -- changing the outcome, changing what the group ends up doing, and changing what you yourself are particpating in -- and that the debate only looks hard because people slide between those. Another thinks the real crux is when a heap of individually harmless acts becomes a harm in its own right, rather than a harmless heap with a harmful sum, and that nothing else matters until that's settled. I genuinely don't know if either is on the right track.

What I want isn't a reading list -- I can't really get the papers anyway -- it's a defended agenda. For each question you'd prioritize, I want the reasoning: why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for amateurs with no training, what it would change downstream if it got answered one way versus the other, and what would actually move it up or down my list over the next couple years. And if you think one of my friends' framings is a dead end, say so and explain what it's missing.
