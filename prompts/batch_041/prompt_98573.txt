I sit on the safety and ethics committee of a farming co-op that just went in on a shared fleet of autonomous crop-spraying drones — forty of them, they coordinate with each other, and the policy that decides where the swarm concentrates gets retrained every week on pooled data from all twelve member farms, so no single farm's fields and no single training run actually determines what the fleet does on any given day. Two weeks ago the fleet over-sprayed along a creek that borders an organic operation and killed a few thousand of their seedlings, and when we pulled the logs all we could see was that each drone did something locally defensible; there's no record of a fleet-level decision because the coordination policy doesn't emit one, and the vendor's "explanation" of what happened turned out to be generated by a model too, so we can't even treat it as a witness. I've only been on this commitee a few months and now it's split in two and we've been argueing about it for three weeks. Roughly: half of us think this is a real responsibility gap — nobody is blameworthy in the ordinary sense, the harm is real, and that means we have to invent something new rather than pretend the usual machinery covers it. The other half think that's a cop-out, that responsibility just flows back to the humans who chose to deploy the thing and to the vendor who built and kept retraining it, and that all this "gap" talk is an excuse for nobody having to answer for the creek.

I'd like us to do this properly instead of another shouting match. Could you write the joint statement that both of our camps could actually sign? I want a numbered list of the claims we agree on — about the incident itself, about what "responsibility" is supposed to be tracking, about what the logs can and can't show; then the real disagreements, each stated in wording that the side holding it would accept as a fair statement of its own view rather than a strawman; then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would genuinely move at least one of us — and for each crux a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about how it comes out written down in advance. Where part of what we're fighting about is really just words (whether "gap" means "no one is blameworthy" or "no one can be held to account", say), flag that part and say why, and please don't manufacture an agreement where there isn't one. There's no internet on the committee laptop and honestly I don't want a reading list or a pile of citations, I want the document — something both camps could put their names to, with whatever disagreement is left over made exact.
