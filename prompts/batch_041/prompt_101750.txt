I've been chewing over the two Koreas as a natural experiment for the institutions-vs-geography-vs-culture fight — one peninsula, one language, one pre-1945 history, arbitrarily cut at the 38th parallel in 1945, then two regimes and two very different outcomes. Treat that as a case study and run the analysis properly. What does each camp actually predict here — the institutionalists in the Acemoglu vein, the geography line, the culture crowd — which one does the case confirm and which does it embarass, and where does the whole natural-experiment framing give out? I want you to be hard on the inference: its one split, not a random assignment, and both regimes were installed by outside powers, so say precisely what is being held constant and what is being smuggled in. Note too that the initial endowments point the wrong way — the North had the heavy industry, the coal, the hydro power and most of the minerals at the split, the South had the farms — so any endowment story has to explain a reversal rather than a head start. And finish by telling me what this case can't settle at all: is there a question here the case is simply silent about, and does that silence tell us something about how much of the why-are-some-nations-rich question is answerable in principle? British English, please.
