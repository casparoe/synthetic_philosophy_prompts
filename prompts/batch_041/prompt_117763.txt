Write a short story that dramatises, rather than explains, the coherence argument for expected utility: the claim that an agent whose preferences cannot be represented by a utility function — because they are intransitive, incomplete, or insensitive to the sure-thing principle — is not merely odd but exploitable, since a counterparty can offer her a finite sequence of trades, each of which she prefers to her position at the moment it is offered, that returns her to her starting point with less money.

The setting is a licensing regime. Invent a jurisdiction and an authority that certifies traders, and a legal doctrine that gives the certification its point: pumping is lawful, because it is not fraud — every trade is disclosed, and each is one the agent herself prefers. Certification is therefore framed as consumer protection, and refusal of certification as a finding of unfitness rather than of wickedness. No real philosophers, statutes, or texts are to appear; the argument must live entirely inside the invented world.

The story must contain, and make visible on the page, the following.

1. A character with a three-way preference cycle, and the pump itself, itemised: the trades, the fees, and the ledger at the end showing her back where she began with less.
2. A character who violates the sure-thing principle rather than transitivity, and a two-stage offer constructed to catch her: she pays to have a later choice removed from the table, and the story should show why that payment is the pump.
3. A character whose preferences are incomplete rather than cyclic — two options neither of which she ranks above the other — and the authority's attempt to treat this as the same disease. Here the story should show either what extra assumption a pump against her requires, or why it fails; it must not simply assert that incompleteness is exploitable.
4. A character who escapes by committing in advance to a plan and declining to re-open the question at each step. Show what this costs her, whether her preference at the moment of acting diverges from the plan, and how the authority characterises her: as cured, or as a new species of offender.
5. A scene in which the auditor discovers that the authority's own rankings — of rules, of cases, of what to license — can be made to cycle in exactly the way it disqualifies others for. End with her deciding whether to report it, and do not resolve the question.

Aim for 2,000 to 3,000 words, in the third person, in scenes. Let the argument be carried by what the characters do to one another. The ending should leave the reader with a question rather than a verdict — in particular, whether immunity to pumping is the same thing as rationality, or merely immunity to one kind of counterparty.
