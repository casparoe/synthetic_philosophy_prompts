I retired from soil science about six years ago, and to my wife's mild horror I've spent most of the time since in a philosophy of biology reading group at the local university. Last month we did heritability, and I came away convinced that half of what people fight about under the heading "nature vs nurture" is really a fight about a ratio of variances, which seems like an odd thing to get that worked up about. Anyway, the group runs a long-standing joke competition, and I've been nominated to bring one about heritability, so I'd like you to write it for me.

Here's where I'd like the joke to live, and I'd rather it be a bit technical than safe. I've been boring everyone with a made-up study I invented for a seminar: some researchers measure a trait — call it 'tail-flick speed' — in a captive population of lizards, once in a warm room and once in a cold room, same animals both times, same genes, and the heritability estimate comes out around 0.7 in the warm room and near 0.1 in the cold one. Nothing genetic changed between the two runs, only the environment did, and yet the number that's supposed to tell you "how genetic" the trait is fell through the floor. What I keep trying to get across is that heritability is a population statistic, relative to the population and the range of environments it was measured in — it's not a property of the trait, still less of any individual — and that "highly heritable" gets heard by everyone as "genetically fixed" or "can't be changed by policy." The other bit I'd love worked in, if you can manage it: a trait can be highly heritable in each of two separate populations while the difference between the two populations is entirely environmental, and people mix those two facts up constantly.

So: please write me an actual joke, setup and punchline, not just a wry remark about nature and nurture. It has to be funny to people who genuinely know what a heritability coefficient is — the punchline should hinge on the real content (the variance-ratio thing, the environment-relativity, the within vs between populations trap), otherwise it'll die at the reading group and I'll be mocked. If the joke needs a line or two of explanation tacked on afterwards, go ahead and add it, so I can tell you're aiming at the actual substance and not just doing a generic nerd gag. A few sentences is plenty, and definately no need for references — I just want the joke.
