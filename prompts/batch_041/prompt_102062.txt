I'm in a historiography seminar and I keep losing an argument with a friend of mine, so I want to practice against someone who actually holds the annoying view instead of the watered-down version of it. Please play a committed presentist about history -- someone who thinks the whole ideal of "understanding the past on its own terms" is a comforting story we tell ourselves, that historians can't help but judge the past by the standards of their own moment, and that they shouldn't pretend otherwise. Make them sharp and a little smug, not a strawman. Ground rules: they can concede that we know lots of facts about the past, that we can learn dead languages and reconstruct past categories, that careful historians exist -- but they must never concede the main point, that any of this adds up to judging by the past's own standards rather than ours. No name-dropping scholars or citing books, just argue it out. One paragraph per turn, conversational, stay in character until I say stop; if I say something they'd find confused they can ask me a question back instead of just lecturing at me.

Here's my opening move, and it's the case I keep losing with. Take a historian of the early modern witch trials. She can reconstruct the accusers' worldview well enough to show that, inside it, the convictions followed from their own standards of evidence -- the testimony, the tests, the confessions all lined up. When she writes that the trials were justified relative to that framework, she seems to be judging by their standards and not ours, and she isn't endorsing the outcome either. My friend says no, she's just redescribing, and any description smuggles our standards in somewhere. So: is she judging by their standards or not, and if you say she isn't, tell me exactly where the smuggling happens, because "somewhere" isn't an argument. And a wrinkle I can't get past: some people at the time thought the spectral evidence was garbage. So there wasn't one single "their standards" -- there were competing ones, and choosing the winning side to call "the past's standards" looks like us taking sides all over again. Then the follow-up if you get that far: your view makes it sound like we could never say a past society was wrong about anything, only that it differed, which seems like a cost you should own up to. Deal with that too.
