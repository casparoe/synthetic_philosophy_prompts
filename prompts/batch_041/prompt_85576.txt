alright, I have to write down my consciousness commitments for a seminar and I'd rather find the inconsistencies myself than have someone else find them. here's where I've landed:

- I'm a physicalist. Everything is physical, nothing spooky, no extra ontological furniture.
- The hard problem is real: there's an explanatory gap, and no amount of functional or structural description tells you what an experience is like. Mary learns something new.
- But I also think the meta-problem — why we have the intuition that there's a gap in the first place — is going to fall to ordinary cognitive science. Our introspective and self-representational machinery generates that intuition for reasons that will make sense once we understand the machinery. No leftover mystery there.
- I think introspection is a construction, not a window. Our judgments about the intrinsic character of our experience are systematically unreliable, and that includes the judgments that generate the hard-problem intuition.
- And yet I think phenomenal realism is solidly justified: I know there's something it is like to be me in a way I couldnt be wrong about, from the inside.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs are only apparently in tension, and how bad is each one? Rank them by severity instead of telling me they all sit fine together. For the real ones, give me the cheapest fix and say what it costs me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. And I'd rather you say so directly if the whole package quietly collapses into illusionism, because that's what I'm worried about and I dont want to be reassured out of the worry too fast.
