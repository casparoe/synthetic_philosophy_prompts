I run a small reading group — half philosophy grad students, half poli sci — and we keep having the exact same fight about individualism vs. collectivism. Everyone opens with some version of the West says the individual, East Asia says the community, and then we burn ninety minutes discovering we don't mean the same thing by either half. So I want to build a decision tree that takes a person from a handful of concrete commitments to a named position, so we can see where we actually land instead of where we'd like to think we land.

Build it from the logical space first, not from a list of famous names. Start with the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then place the positions in that space afterwards. Phrase every node as something a person could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to. No node should just be are you an individualist or a collectivist.

The distinctions I care about, roughly in the order I think they have to come:

- Is your claim about what a self *is*, or about what a self *owes*? Force the tree to cross these two axes instead of running them together — that's the conflation that ruins our discussions, people who think the self is socially constituted but still hold that rights trump the community keep getting sorted as collectivists by accident.
- Causal sociality vs. constitutive sociality: society shapes me, versus there is no me to speak of apart from my relations.
- Does the community have interests of its own, or is it only a pattern of relations between people with no interests at the pattern level?
- Is the self an owner of its life, or a trustee holding something in trust for a family line, a tradition, a place? (I've been calling these the owner/trustee pair and I want it in there as a node, I think it does more work than the usual autonomy talk.)
- Role-based and asymmetric relations — deference, filial duty, seniority — versus equal respect among free and equal persons.
- When the person and the community conflict, who has standing to decide? And a prior node: does the conflict even make sense, or is the person-apart-from-community a fiction?
- One more axis you should probably build in: which community. Family and local community is not the same thing as the state, and a lot of what gets called East Asian collectivism is about the first while 20th-century political collectivism is about the second. Don't let them land on the same leaf.

Leaves I want to see, at minimum: atomistic individualism; socially-constituted self but rights still trump; a Confucian-style role ethics; the Western communitarian critique-of-liberalism line; state collectivism; relational autonomy in the feminist sense; and one combination that turns out to be genuinely unoccupied, flagged and diagnosed — nobody lives there because it's incoherent, or because it's unattractive, or just because nobody's bothered.

Concrete cases to hang the nodes on, since these are what people can actually answer: a household where all wages go into a pool and the eldest decides, and a daughter wants to move to a city; a hospital that tells the family the diagnosis and the patient second; a woman who says if you strip away daughter and teacher and neighbor there's no leftover self underneath; a work team that absorbs the blame for one member's fraud; a last speaker of a language dying, and whether anything is lost beyond that person's suffering; someone who says she'd rather her community's tradition survive than that she get to live as she likes, and whether that's a coherent preference or false consciousness.

Also: where two leaves come out at the same place, tell me so rather than papering over it, and name a case that would split them.

And don't give me reading lists, citations, or bibliographies — I don't need them and you'll mostly be guessing. If you're confident a well-known name is the standard label for a leaf, add it, otherwise leave it unnamed. A wrong attribution is worse to me than an unnamed position.

Five to seven branch nodes, six to nine leaves. Here's the shape I want it in, as JSON:

- nodes: array of objects with id (n1, n2, ...), question (the node text, phrased as a question), why_it_splits (one line on what turns on the answer), and branches, an array of objects with answer (short paraphrase of the answer) and goes_to (the id of the next node or leaf).
- leaves: array of objects with id (L1, L2, ...), position (a descriptive name in a few words), aka (a well-known label or null), core_commitment (one sentence), path (an object mapping each node id on the route to this leaf onto the answer that gets you here), awkward_commitment (the thing a defender of this position has to swallow, one sentence), and near_misses (ids of leaves this one is most easily confused with, plus a phrase on what seperates them).
- unoccupied_combinations: array of objects with combination (the answers that would have to go together), why_empty (one of: incoherent, unattractive, unexplored), and note (one line).
- inseparable_pairs: array of objects with a and b (leaf ids), why_the_tree_cant_split_them, and case_that_would.
- cases: array of objects with id, description (the concrete case, two sentences max), and used_at (ids of the nodes or leaves that lean on it).

Definately keep the whole thing under two pages if it were printed, this is a handout not a dissertation.
