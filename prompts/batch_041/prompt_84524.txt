I have been reading the rationalist and effective-altruism blogosphere for about a decade now, and after one too many dinner-table arguments I have finally tried to write down my settled verdicts on how to eat, in the hope that someone can tell me what principle, if any, generates them. Here they are. Getting the same number of calories from chicken rather than from beef is worse, because it takes far more individual birds than cattle to feed a person, and I take the number of sentient individuals killed to matter. All the same, I think a cow's death is a graver thing than a chicken's: the cow has a longer life, richer social ties, and more to lose, and I count that as a genuine point in beef's favor that partly offsets the body count. A steak from a well-run pasture sits easier with me than a carton of eggs from a battery barn, even though the steak kills a large animal and the eggs merely use up hens, because the hen spends her months in a cage while the steer has something one could call a life before the end. A vegan meal, however, is not obviously the low-harm option, since raising crops kills a great many field animals — mice, the young of ground-nesting birds, and the like — and if I really believe that a wild animal's life is short and its death usually slow and frightened, then the combine may do more damage per calorie than the pasture does. I think insect deaths count for something, but only a small fraction of a mammal's. And I would sooner have a great many field mice die in a wheat harvest than see one pig spend a year in a gestation crate.

My tentative principle is a weighted sum of suffering, in which each individual's suffering is multiplied by a factor for how bad a life of that kind can be and then added up across all the animals affected — but I notice that I reach for sheer numbers when it favors the cow and for intensity when it favors the hen, and I cannot tell whether that is a coherent two-factor view or just motivated switching.

What I want from you is the principle, stated precisely enough that I can apply it to cases I have not listed — a meal of mussels against a meal of lentils, or a pasture-raised chicken against a feedlot one — together with the specific verdict of mine that motivates each clause. I suspect at least one of my verdicts will have to go, and I would rather you tell me which and why than paper over the gap; I am also curious what your preferred principle implies for a case I have deliberately left off the list. Please write your answer in American English.
