Background: my department has one of those people who teaches speech-act theory like it's a blood sport, and a few of us bring her cases over lunch on Fridays. Her verdicts so far, roughly:

- A couple exchange vows in front of a full room, everyone hears them, they walk out believing they're married — but the officiant turns out to have no authority to marry anyone. Not married, she says. The words did nothing at all.
- A friend says "I'll pay you back Friday," I hear it, I say fine. A promise. Obviously.
- Same friend, same sentence, only this time he's already decided never to pay me. Still a promise — a rotten one, but the promise got made. She was adamant: whether he meant it is a seperate question from whether he did it.
- Someone says "I bet you a tenner it rains tomorrow" and the other says "you're on." A bet, and it makes no difference that both of them fully intend to wriggle out of paying.
- An actor on stage, mid-play, says "I promise to pay you the money by dawn" to another actor; packed house, everyone hears it clearly. Not a promise, not even a rotten one — the stage makes the whole thing hollow. She said this was a different kind of failure from the officiant one, though I couldn't get her to say why.
- A colleague apologises to me for reading my email, in a tone that makes clear he'd do it again tomorrow. An apology, she says. Worthless, but an apology.

She keeps muttering "misfire" and "abuse" under her breath and I've given up asking which is which.

The new cases. My neighbour is on a riverbank and sees a stranger stepping onto ice he knows is rotten, and shouts "I warn you, that ice won't hold you" — but the stranger has his headphones in, hears nothing, and goes through. Did my neighbour warn him? Second: he whispers "I promise I'll be there in the morning" to his kid, who is fast asleep. Promise? Third: he says "I promise to pay you back Friday" to a delivery driver who doesn't speak a word of English and just nods and smiles. Promise?

What does she say on each, what rule is she actually running, which other rules fit everything shes said so far just as well, and which of those three cases would best tell the rules apart — or if none of them does, what case would? I'm less interested in who's right than in what she's tracking.
