Consider the following claim, stated as precisely as I can so that it can be true or false: every norm of epistemic rationality for a computationally unbounded, perfectly self-aware agent is derivable from a single epistemic good — the accuracy of that agent's doxastic states — together with structural facts about how accuracy is scored; no irreducibly non-accuracy epistemic requirement is needed. Call this claim ACCURACY-SUFFICES, and count a norm as derivable only if it follows without importing any premise that is itself a substantive epistemic norm.

I want your credence in ACCURACY-SUFFICES, decomposed. Give me a probability for each of the following, one line of justification each, and then the combination — being explicit about how you are combining them, and saying so rather than pretending otherwise if you think some of them are conditionally dependent:

1. Whether a single strictly proper scoring rule can be singled out as the correct measure of credal accuracy, or whether the choice among proper rules is underdetermined in a way that changes which credences come out rational.
2. Whether the dominance/undominatedness-style argument for probabilism goes through without presupposing the thesis at issue — in particular, whether the space of alternative credence functions is characterized in a way that isn't question-begging.
3. Whether conditionalization, or any diachronic norm, follows from accuracy considerations alone, or requires an extra premise about how present and future accuracy are related.
4. Whether full belief and suspension of judgment receive norms at all from this program, or whether it yields a theory of credence only and leaves the rest of the doxastic economy unaccounted for.
5. Whether the program has anything to say about which prior credences are rational, and whether its silence there (if it is silent) counts against sufficiency or is a permissible permissivism.
6. Whether there is an epistemic good other than accuracy — understanding, explanatory power, evidential fit — that must be either reduced to accuracy or denied, and how plausible each of those moves is.
7. Whether the norms extend to groups: if each member is required to regard their own credences as the most accurate, can a group satisfy the analogous requirement, or does it become unsatisfiable at the collective level?
