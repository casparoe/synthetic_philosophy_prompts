I have recently had to make a choice between a demanding position that would take me far from someone I love who is seriously ill, and staying near them at the cost of the work I have wanted my whole life. Months later I still cannot say that one option was better than the other, and this has pushed me into reading about incommensurable values and hard choices — the thought that some options are neither better than, nor worse than, nor equal to one another in value, so that reason cannot rank them at all. I would like to practise defending that thought against a serious opponent, and I want you to be that opponent.

Please play a committed comparabilist: someone who holds that for any two options there is a fact of the matter about which is better or that they are equally good, and that all talk of incommensurability is either concealed uncertainty or a failure of nerve. Play the character as a confident decision theorist or welfare economist — someone who thinks in terms of a single scale on which every option can in principle be placed, who will readily concede that comparisons are often vague, difficult, or psychologically agonising, but who will never concede that two options can be genuinely unrankable. Give the character real arguments rather than caricature: the argument from small improvements (if one option is not better than another, then improving it slightly cannot suddenly make it better, and by repetition no amount of improvement could ever make it better, which is absurd); the argument that a choice you cannot rank is a choice you cannot make rationally, and yet you did choose; the reduction of all apparent plurality of value to a single currency such as how well your life goes; and the charge that incommensurability is a licence for arbitrary will. Concede small points where honesty demands it, but never abandon the main thesis. Stay in character until I say 'stop', keep each reply to a single paragraph so that it feels like a conversation, and if you need a fact from me, ask for it in character.

Here is my opening move. I have been trying to formulate a test for when two options are incommensurable: they are incommensurable, I want to say, when no amount of the good in one could compensate for the loss of the other, and when the very request to trade them off strikes me as a category mistake — as though someone asked me how many inches make a pound. In my own case the two things I am weighing seem valuable in different ways, and neither the language of 'better' nor the language of 'equal' fits. You will tell me this is just uncertainty, or vagueness, or a single scale I have not yet located. So tell me: what is wrong with my test — and if nothing is wrong with it, what exactly have you conceded?
