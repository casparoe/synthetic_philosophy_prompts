Write a joke about peer disagreement. I have no philosophical training, so I am going on my own reading and thinking here, and I want the joke to turn on the actual content of the two main positions rather than on the general fact that philosophers never settle anything.

Here is the situation I would like it built around, which is the part that interests me. Two people are genuine epistemic peers: the same evidence, the same competence, the same track record. They disagree about some ordinary thing. One of them is a conciliationist, who thinks that when you find yourself disagreeing with a peer on the same evidence you owe the other side some movement, so she cannot simply keep her view at full strength. The other is a steadfast, who thinks that in a case like this he is entitled to keep his view as it stands. That much is the standard dispute. The twist I want the joke to exploit is this: suppose that what the two of them are disagreeing about is precisely how to respond to disagreement. Take the conciliationist at her word and she seems committed to moving partway toward the steadfast's position, while the steadfast, by his own lights, owes no movement at all. The disagreement comes out lopsided, and the conciliationist's own rule seems to push her toward the view that says you need not follow that rule. It gets worse if you press on it, since once they disagree about the rule it is not obvious they are still peers in the sense the rule was about, and I would like the joke to be aware of that, even if it only glances at it.

Please make an actual joke out of this, with a setup and a punchline, not a sketch of one or a paragraph explaining what would be funny. It should land for someone who already knows the terms, and it should not depend on any particular author, book, or quotation, since I could not check those and I do not want the joke to be about who said what. If it only works because the listener already finds the phrase peer disagreement amusing, it has not done its job. I would be happiest if the punchline says something true about the two positions, even at the cost of being a little dry.
