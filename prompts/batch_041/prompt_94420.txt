Ok so heres a thing that bugs me and I can't work out if I'm onto something or just confused. Where I live we vote for the people who decide how much tax we pay, whether we go to war, how the schools are run, basically everything. But nobody votes for the people who set interest rates. Those are appointees on long terms, and the whole point seems to be that elected politicians arent supposed to touch them. If a politician even says out loud that rates should come down, everyone acts like they broke a rule. Same deal with a bunch of other bodies, the ones who decide whether a drug is safe enough to sell, the ones who produce the unemployment numbers, we go out of our way to shield them from elections.

But then why not taxes? Why is it normal for an unelected committee to decide what happens to my mortgage and my savings, and completely bananas for an unelected committee to decide my income tax rate? What is the setup quietly assuming here?

The way I see it, it has to be assuming something like: some of these questions have a right answer that trained people can find, and others are just about what people want, and we can tell which is which before we decide who gets to decide. Because if we couldnt tell them apart, the whole arrangement falls apart. But maybe that's wrong. Maybe you can run the exact same institution while believing nothing of the sort. Like, we dont think the central bankers know better than anyone else, we just know that we'll always want cheap money before an election and then regret it after, so we tie our own hands on purpose. If that second story works, then the practice isnt committing us to anything about who knows better, and the scary "rule by the knowledgeable" reading of it just evaporates. If it doesnt work, then there's a little epistocracy parked in the middle of a democracy and we should at least admit it.

So: which parts of this practice actually force that assumption, and which parts just sit comfortably with it without needing it? And actually land somewhere, dont tell me it depends on your priors, tell me which way you think it goes and why.

Two more things I want out of it. First, if you do think it presupposes that some questions are expert questions, tell me how a democracy is supposed to sort the expert questions from the preference questions without just putting it to a vote, which would be circular since thats the very thing we're trying to decide. If that sorting cant be done in a principled way, say so.

Second, tell me what would change your mind. What would you have to see, or what argument would somebody have to make, to flip your verdict? And where is your reasoning most likely to be wrong? I'd rather know where you're wobbly than get a clean answer I cant trust.

Dont give me a reading list, I'm not going to read it. Just argue with me.
