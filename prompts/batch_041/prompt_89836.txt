I retired from experimental physics a few years ago and have since been sitting in on a graduate reading group in decision theory. Last week two of the regulars, whom I'll call A and B, got into an argument I could not referee. They were discussing the Allais gambles — the pairs in which most people take the sure fortune in one case and the risky option in the other — and the exchange ran roughly as follows.

A: The Allais preference is simply irrational. Independence is not an optional extra; it is part of what it is to have coherent preferences over gambles, and a preference that violates it can be turned into a sure loss.

B: That gets it backwards. Independence is a condition in a representation theorem, not a requirement of practical reason. What the subjects are responding to is the difference between certainty and a high probability, and there is nothing incoherent about caring about that difference.

A: If certainty is a good in its own right, then your preferences depend on how the prospect is described. Describe the sure fortune as a gamble with probability 1 of the same outcome and the certainty is just a probability; now you are inconsistent between two descriptions of one and the same thing.

B: That is a problem only if the two descriptions really are the same thing, which is precisely what is in dispute. And the common consequence in the Allais pairs is not common in the way the objection needs: it changes what the agent can count on, and so what she can plan around.

A: Then call it a utility of planning, or a disutility of regret, or whatever you like — expand the outcome space to include the counterfactual and the axiom is satisfied again. If every violation can be absorbed that way, your defence is empty.

B: And if no violation can ever be pinned down, because the outcome space can always be expanded, then the axiom is doing no normative work at all; it is a bookkeeping convention.

That is roughly where they left it, and I do not know enough to say who came off better. Please adjudicate. I do not need a literature review or citations; I will chase those down myself.
