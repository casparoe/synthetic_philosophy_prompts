I've been reading LessWrong and the surrounding blogosphere for maybe a decade now, and there's one recurring argument I can never get to the bottom of. It usually starts as a fight about whether rationality is a skill or a body of knowledge. Skill camp: you can read every post on the site, memorize Bayes and expected value and all the rest, and still be a disaster at actually thinking, so being rational must be more like knowing how to ride a bike than like knowing a pile of facts. Knowledge camp: fine, but every time you try to say what the skilled person has that the klutz lacks, you end up pointing at some proposition they know, or at least a disposition to act as if they knew it, so 'skill' just collapses into knowledge plus practice. Then someone brings up the older academic version of this -- Ryle's distinction between knowing how and knowing that, and the intellectualists who say knowing how to do something is really knowing a proposition (a way of doing the thing), just grasped in a special practical way rather than a theoretical one. And then everyone nods and nothing gets settled.

What bugs me is that both sides seem to have an answer to every move and none of the answers land. The skill side has the bike case (you can't state the rule you're following, yet you obviously know something); the intellectualist side has a theory that swallows the bike case too (you know the proposition, you just know it under a practical guise). The literature just keeps generating replies to replies, and as far as I can tell nobody has actually moved in decades. So my hypothesis, and I want you to test it rather than just agree with it, is that this was never one disagreement. One side is asking a question about language -- what the phrase 'knows how' actually means, and when ascriptions of it are true in English. The other is asking a question about minds -- what is going on in a person who is competent at something. Same words, different questions.

Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a real substantive disagreement hiding underneath that I'm missing? And whatever the diagnosis turns out to be, tell me what would actually count as progress here -- a distinction, a formal result, some piece of psychology -- or say honestly that none should be expected, and why. I dont want a survey of who said what; I want the shape of the thing.
