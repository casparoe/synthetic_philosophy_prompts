Assume the four-grade hierarchy of minds in which each grade adds a faster mechanism of selection than the one below it: Darwinian creatures, shaped by natural selection across generations; Skinnerian creatures, which learn by reinforcement within a single lifetime; Popperian creatures, which can test candidate actions internally before committing to them; and Gregorian creatures, whose thinking tools — notation, arithmetic, institutions — rewire the internal testing itself.

I have four candidate fifth grades and want to know which, if any, earn their place.

A. Machine-offloaded: agents whose internal hypothesis-testing is delegated to external symbol-manipulating machines, so that the testing is done by a device whose intermediate steps no one inspects.

B. Scaffolded: agents with no native capacity for internal simulation who acquire it by internalising public, tool-mediated practices, so that the capacity is real but parasitic on a cultural niche.

C. Cumulative-lineage: agents whose thinking tools are not merely inherited but are themselves improved across generations by selection acting on the tools rather than on the agents.

D. Installed-reward: agents that learn by reinforcement, but whose reward signal is set from outside by other agents, so that what they come to want is a cultural artefact.

Sort A–D into tiers by how much each genuinely extends the four-grade scheme rather than merely restating the Gregorian grade, and give a sentence or two for each explaining its placement. Then state a single fraction between 0 and 1 for how much of the explanatory work in this area the original four grades already do, with the remainder assigned to whichever of A–D you keep.
