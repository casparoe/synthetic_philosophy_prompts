I've been going down a Hofstadter rabbit hole after finally finishing Gödel, Escher, Bach on a long train ride, and a friend sent me a blog post that tries to explain the careenium from his later book. Here's the post's version, and I want to know if it's trustworthy. Hofstadter asks us to picture a frictionless table covered in zillions of tiny magnetic marbles, which he calls simms. They zip around and collide, and when they hit slowly enough they stick together into big clusters, the simmballs. The simms bang into simmballs and simmballs bang into simms. The post says the big point is that simmballs push simms around just as much as simms push simmballs, so the higher level is just as causally fundamental as the lower one, and neither level is more real than the other. From there it says this refutes reductionism, because you couldn't in principle derive or predict what the simmballs do from the physics of individual simms — the cluster level has its own autonomous causal powers that the micro story simply leaves out. And the punchline is that this solves mental causation: thoughts genuinely shove neurons around the way simmballs shove simms, so a complete physical description of the brain would still be missing something, namely the causal work done at the level of ideas. The blogger wraps up by saying this is why Hofstadter thinks a mind could keep going on different hardware, since it's the pattern that does the causing.
