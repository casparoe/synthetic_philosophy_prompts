The exam question was:

"Explain Popper's claim that Darwinism is a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable scientific theory, and discuss how his later remarks on natural selection qualify or revise that claim."

One student's answer reads as follows:

"Popper's target is the Darwinian mechanism of natural selection, not the historical claim that species share common ancestors. His argument is that the principle of natural selection, that the fittest survive, is not a genuine empirical law, because fitness is normally measured by nothing other than survival, so the principle collapses into the tautology that those which survive, survive. A statement that no possible observation could contradict is, on Popper's own criterion, not a scientific statement but a metaphysical one, and this is why he puts Darwinism in the same category as psychoanalysis and Marxism: a framework that appears to explain everything and is therefore immune to refutation. He calls it a research programme because it still has heuristic value for biologists, even though it cannot be tested.

Popper later revised this. In the paper on natural selection and the emergence of mind he says he has changed his mind about the testability and logical status of natural selection, and he concedes that Darwinism is after all a testable scientific theory. What persuaded him, on his own account, is that the historical part of Darwin's theory, the claim of descent with modification from common ancestors, can be checked against the fossil record and against biogeography, so that the theory as a whole is falsifiable. His earlier talk of a metaphysical research programme was therefore a mistake, which he retracted."
