So I've gotten weirdly into these little picture puzzles my nephew showed me — the kind where you get two groups of simple line drawings, six on the left, six on the right, and you have to figure out what makes the left ones left and the right ones right, and then they show you a new drawing and you have to sort it. I think they're called Bongard problems or something like that. Anyway, here's the thing that keeps happening and driving me up the wall. I'll find a rule that sorts all twelve figures correctly, I feel clever, and then it turns out to be "wrong" — except not wrong on any of the twelve, it fits all twelve perfectly. Wrong in the sense that it isn't the rule the puzzle was actually built around. There was one where every left figure happened to lean to the left and every right figure leaned right, so I said "left ones lean left," true of every single card, and then the new card was a left-leaning figure with an even number of sides, and the "real" answer was about the number of sides, so my rule sent me the wrong way. There was another where the left group all had a little mark in the top corner and the right group all had it in the bottom, and again that sorted everything, and again it was a decoy. But what bugs me is that my rules weren't even more complicated than the intended ones — if anything they were simpler. So it isn't overfitting in the normal sense. My friend who does machine learning said "oh that's just overfitting," but overfitting is supposed to be about rules that are too complicated or that grab onto noise, and mine were nice simple rules that happened to be true of everything I could see.

What I want to know is whether there's a name for this exact situation: the examples you're given are equally consistent with several different rules, all of them fit perfectly, and nothing in the examples themselves tells you which one is *the* rule — so the only thing that makes one of them right is that it's the one the puzzle-maker had in mind, or the one that cuts along a feature we think is the "real" kind of thing to sort by rather than an incidental correlation. It feels like it's somewhere near the problem of induction, and near that thing where you can gerrymander a definition so it fits all the data so far and then breaks later (I have a vague memory of a green-and-blue example from a philosophy class, definately fuzzy on it), but it doesn't seem to be quite either, because I'm not extrapolating into the future or playing with a weird definition — all the rules are about the same twelve pictures and they just disagree about the new one. Is there an established term, or a family of terms, for this, and can you tell me how they differ and which one fits best? And separately, is there a name for the property a puzzle has when it *does* pin down a unique natural rule, as opposed to the property it has when it's "unfair" because several rules fit equally well? I'd honestly rather understand exactly where that line falls — what makes one rule the answer and another a decoy, and whether "decoy" is even the right way to think about it — than just get handed a word.
