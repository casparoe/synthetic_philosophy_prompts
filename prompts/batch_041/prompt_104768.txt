Hey, I'm a grad student in archaeolgy (later European prehistory, mostly) and I'm trying to turn a seminar paper into something my supervisor won't immediately shred. The paper is about the kind of inference we make when we go from objects to minds -- you know the move: "these figurines get deposited in pits in a consistent way, therefore they believed such-and-such." My argument is that there's an asymmetry here that doesn't get discussed enough. When you infer a *belief* from material patterning, the bridge is basically charity plus relevance: you assume the people were competent in their environment and that their choices were at least somewhat responsive to how they took the world to be, so a stable pattern in what they made, kept, and threw away gives you some grip on what they thought. When you infer an *institution* -- a rule, an office, a legitimate way of doing things -- the bridge isn't charity at all, it's coordination. One person can hold a belief and leave a material trace of it, but a rule that no one but the rule-follower recognizes isn't a rule. So my claim is that you can't get an institution out of artifacts unless you can also find what I've been calling sanction traces: some material sign that deviation was noticed and answered -- a reworked object, a body buried out of the prevailing pattern, a boundary ditch rebuilt on the same line after it silted up. No sanction trace, no institution; you're entitled at most to a convention, and maybe only to a habit.

The part I'm least sure of is whether that's a real distinction or just a continuum with a hard line drawn through it. Someone in the seminar said sanction traces are just more belief evidence -- the enforcer's belief that the deviation was wrong -- so my asymmetry collapses into "institutions need more evidence than beliefs," which is boring and probably true. I tried to answer that institutions aren't reducible to the sum of members' beliefs because they persist across turnover, and that the sanction isn't evidence of anyone's belief but of a standing entitlement to respond... but I honestly can't tell if that rescues the argument or just relabels it.

Could you read the above as the skeleton of the paper and tell me where it's weakest? I'm mainly after three things: (a) whether the belief/institution asymmetry is a genuine distinction in kind or a degree difference dressed up as one, (b) whether "sanction traces" is a workable criterion or whether it's circular -- to see the deviation as a deviation you may already need to know the norm you're trying to infer -- and (c) any counterexample where we'd all happily say an institution was present but nothing like a sanction trace survives in the record. Don't worry about whether my little examples are real sites; I made them up to keep the point clean.
