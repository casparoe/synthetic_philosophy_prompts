I spent yesterday evening arguing with a friend about the rules that govern transplant medicine, and I want to work out what those rules actually commit us to. Here is the practice. In a mass-casualty emergency, physicians triage: they rank patients by prognosis, allocate scarce resources accordingly, and knowingly let some die so that others can be saved, and we do not think they have wronged the people they pass over. Yet no surgeon may kill one healthy patient, against his will, to distribute his organs among five people who would otherwise die, even though the body count is identical and the five would live. So we permit letting die for the sake of numbers but forbid killing for the sake of numbers, and the prohibition holds even when honoring it guarantees more deaths and more violated rights of the very same kind.

What view of rights, persons, and agency does that combination presuppose? It looks like it presupposes constraints that are not merely weighted heavily but are absolute in a way that forbids minimizing violations of the same right, which is the structure usually called the paradox of deontology: if a right exists to protect the interests of the individuals who hold it, it is hard to see why it should not be overridden when overriding it would protect more of those same interests. Reconstruct the implicit theory, and then tell me which parts the practice strictly requires and which it merely fits well with. Does it presuppose the doing/allowing distinction, or is the triage case different because the triaging physician is not the cause of the deaths? My friend, who is a rule-utilitarian, insists the whole thing is institutional hygiene: a hospital known to kill patients for their organs would collapse, and the prohibition is just the rule that keeps it trusted. If he is right, does the practice lose anything by admitting it, or was there never a genuine paradox here at all? Finally, if someone grounds the constraint in the agent's integrity or in the inviolability of the person rather than in the victim's interests, does that escape the paradox or only relocate it?
