ok so this is a slightly odd request and i'll explain what i'm after before i ask for it. i'm in a reading group that has spent the whole term on philosophy of science and the last session is on Lakatos and research programmes — hard cores, protective belts, progressive vs degenerating problem shifts, all of that — and because i'm the only person in the group who isn't a philosopher (i'm a demographer; we meet in the back room of a pub) i got voluntold to bring something to close the evening with. i read poetry, i don't write it, so treat this as a commission. i want a sestina. my friend Anna, who actually is a poet, says the sestina is the form for people with a grudge, because you have to keep saying the same six words over and over and just rearrange everything around them, and the second she said that i thought: thats literally the thing we've been arguing about all term. the hard core is supposed to be immune to revision, but its not immune because of some fact about the world, it's immune because the people in the programme keep deciding, week after week, not to give it up — the words stay and everything else gets rewritten. so i want the form to do that work rather than the poem explaining it.

so: the full sestina, 39 lines, standard rotation, three-line envoi carrying all six end-words, and the six end-words are the programme's unrenounced commitments — they come back unchanged every stanza and nothing else is allowed to come back (no recycled phrases, no refrain sneaking in). keep them plain, the kind of word a working scientist would actually say out loud rather than abstractions. the voice is someone a few years into a programme, talking to a younger colleague who is about to leave for the rival programme and who keeps insisting that the failed prediction was simply a failed prediction. you pick the programme, but pick one whose core is a long-run claim about how something tends to go, since thats what makes the deferral interesting — my own field is full of them but you dont have to use demography. one stanza should show the group patching things up in a way that predicts nothing new, a rescue that costs nothing and buys nothing, and another should show a patch that actually turned up something nobody was looking for, so the reader feels the difference between the two cases instead of being told about it. someone who knows this literature should be able to point at the progressive stanza and the degenerating one without me labelling them. in the envoi i want the verdict, but delivered the way history actually delivers it — late, and by people who weren't in the room — so please don't use the words "hard core," "protective belt," "research programme," or "paradigm," and don't name Lakatos or Popper; the poem shouldn't need the apparatus. and i'd rather it not land on a clean judgement of the programme, because that isn't where the group got to: the colleague leaving can be right about the failure and still have nowhere better to go, and i want the poem to sit in that.
