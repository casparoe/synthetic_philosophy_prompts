I'm running the Bayesian epistemology reading group next month and I want to spend the first half on the spec instead of on the menu of solutions. Before you write anything, brainstorm: jot down for yourself all the things people have demanded of a "solution" to the problem of old evidence, then sort them into genuine requirements and one camp's prejudices.

Here's the setup in my words so we're on the same page. An agent has credences. A hypothesis H is already in her credence function. Some evidence E is already known to her -- she saw the thing, ran the experiment, read the report -- so her credence in E is 1. Later she comes to appreciate that H entails E, or at least makes E very likely. Everyone's intuition is that this counts as confirmation: the perihelion of Mercury looks like it confirms general relativity even though it was old news by the time the theory showed up. But the standard story, "the evidence raised the credence of the hypothesis," has nothing to work with, because nothing conditionalized on E can move when E already has credence 1, and nothing about H gets moved by something that was certain all along. So what has to be true of any account that fixes this?

What I want:

- The desiderata list: the cases it has to get right, the puzzles it has to dissolve, the distinctions it has to respect, the results it can't contradict -- each with the case or consideration behind it. For every entry, say why it's a requirement and not just a nice-to-have. I want at least one entry about the difference between E being one old observation and E being the whole corpus you've accumulated.
- Mark which entries are non-negotiable and which are commitments of one particular camp: the counterfactual "what would she have thought before she knew E" camp, the "confirmation is a relation between evidence, hypothesis and background knowledge, not between an agent and a theory" camp, the "what actually happens is that she learns the entailment, not the evidence" camp, the "just drop the probability-1 idealization" camp, and whatever else you think is a live option.
- The part I most want: whether some subset of the desiderata can't be had together. If "old evidence can confirm," "no free lunch where every old fact confirms every theory that entails it," and "the account has to describe the actual agent, who is finite and not logically omniscient" can't all hold at once, say so and show me the squeeze. A list whose members can't be jointly satisfied is itself the result.

Two cases I want on the list. I haven't seen them put this way, so add them as requirements in your own formulation if you think they earn a place:

(a) The training-set case. I spend part of my week fitting models to data, and a model built to fit a dataset isn't confirmed by that dataset -- that's the whole point of holding data out. Is the old evidence problem a special case of that, or a different problem wearing the same coat? Either way the account has to say something about the difference between evidence that was merely sitting there and evidence the hypothesis was constructed to accommodate, and whether those ever come apart.

(b) The two-agent case. Same evidence, same hypothesis, same entailment. For one agent E is old and certain; for the other it's new. Does one of them get confirmation and the other not? If yes, its a strange thing to say about a relation that's supposed to hold between evidence and theory. If no, then what work is the agent's credence doing, and where did the probability-1 premise go?
