ok so my friend Priya is doing a masters in cognitive science and she's been trying to explain "teleosemantics" to me — as far as I can tell it's the idea that a mental state means whatever it's *for*, and what it's for gets fixed by history: evolution picked it out, or you learned it, or a designer built it that way. I actually like this. It's the only story I've heard where meaning is a fact about the world instead of just a story we tell about ourselves. But she's got an argument and I can't find the exit, and it ends somewhere I refuse to go. It runs:

(1) A state of a system means/represents X only if it's that state's *job* to indicate X. Meaning just is function.

(2) A state has a job of that kind only because of a history. At some point, states of that type fired in certain conditions, and the systems where they fired that way did better than the systems where they didn't — they ate, they reproduced, they got the treat, whatever. Strip the history off and all you've got left is what the state *does*, not what it's *for*.

(3) But a history is just a record of what happened, and it doesn't come with labels on it. Any stretch of what-happened can be described in alot of different ways, and we're the ones doing the describing.

(4) Take my dog. His "walk" state goes off for the word "walk", for "wok", for the click of me closing my laptop, and for the fridge door. Every one of those has been followed by a walk, because I'm a creature of habit: I walk him right after I close the laptop and right before I cook. So which one does he mean? "Walk"? "Walk-or-wok-or-laptop-or-fridge"? Or actually "laptop closing", since that one has never once failed to get him a walk and the others sometimes do? All of these fit his history exactly — same triggers, same follow-ups. Nothing in his past picks one out. So by (1)-(3) there's no fact of the matter what his state means.

(5) And before you say "he's just a dog": run it on me. My "there's a dog" state fires for dogs, and for wolves at dusk, and for the neighbour's husky, and for that statue by the fountain. In every case the follow-up was the same — I looked, I said aww, nothing happened. Nothing in my past sorts "dog" from "dog-or-wolf-or-statue".

(6) So no state of anything represents any one thing determinately.

(7) But a content that isn't determinate isn't a content. If there's no fact about what I believe, there's no belief. And I definately believe there's coffee in this mug, and I can be wrong about it.

(8) So (1) and (2) are false, and meaning can't be a matter of what a thing is for after all. Priya says the fun part is that the whole thing eats itself — if no state has a determinate content then neither does the sentence "content is function", so the view can't even be stated.

Where's the seam? I'd much rather keep (1) and (2), because the alternative seems to be that meaning is just whatever we agree to call it, and I hate that. So: which step do I give up, why did it look fine in the first place, and what does giving it up actually cost me? And if I keep (1) and (2) instead, what am I stuck saying about the dog — that he means the disjunction, or that he means nothing at all? I've got a feeling (3) is doing the sleight of hand, but I can't turn that into anything, and honestly (4) is the step that convinces me. No reading list please, I won't read it — I just want to know which door to walk through and what's on the other side.
