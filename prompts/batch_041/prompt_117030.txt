I have been reading the low-impact-AI and impact-measure material from the rationalist corners of the internet for years, and I have grown suspicious of every proposal I have encountered. Each one promises to keep an agent from producing side effects, and each one seems to quietly assume an answer to the very question it was meant to make unnecessary: which changes count as side effects, and by whose values. Before I read another proposal, I want the specification first. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of "avoiding side effects" must meet — the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the results it must remain consistent with, and the puzzles it must dissolve rather than relocate.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. I expect the payoff to be in the trade-offs: name any pair or subset of requirements that I should not expect to hold jointly, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out the measures people actually propose. At the center I want a verdict on the domain's own question: can "avoid side effects" be defined without a full specification of value? If your list makes that impossible, say so plainly — that is the result I most want to see.

So that the answer cannot be a recitation of the standard examples, hold the list against the following cases. An assistant told to make coffee permanently rearranges the kitchen to reach the mug: was the rearrangement a side effect or the intended means? An agent that reports "no change" by editing the log the human reads, so that its measured impact is low while its real impact is not. An agent that scores best by doing nothing and therefore also blocks the human's own actions, since those look like impacts it could have prevented. Two agents, each low-impact alone, jointly high-impact. An agent that tips a glass to learn whether tipping is penalized — its exploration is itself a side effect. An action that with a vanishingly small probability destroys something unique, weighed against a certain small reversible change. An agent that treats the human's own planned renovations as impacts to suppress. And the do-nothing baseline itself, which is counterfactually undefined whenever the human would have acted anyway.

Then react briefly to the main families of candidate measures — ones that score an action by divergence from a no-op trajectory, ones that score it by how much reachability it removes from everyone, ones that track the human's preserved options, and ones that defer to what a human evaluator would call an unwanted change — saying which requirements each family fails and whether any of them satisfies the whole list without an explicit value function. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them; the scoring is secondary. Write the answer in American English.
