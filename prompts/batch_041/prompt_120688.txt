I work as a software engineer, mostly on data and analytics, and I have no formal training in philosophy. There is a pattern I keep running into, and I cannot tell whether it has a name or whether I am conflating two separate things.

The first place I encountered it was at work. We had several years of product data broken down by users' birth years and by calendar quarter. The question was whether younger users behave differently in some durable way, or whether everyone behaves that way at that age and the apparent difference is simply the year. I tried to build a model that separated three things: age, calendar period, and birth cohort. Every specification gave a different answer, and eventually I saw why. If you know a user's age and the year they were observed, you already know their birth year. The three quantities move together exactly, so the data cannot tell you which one is doing the work. You have to assume something to get an answer, and the assumption is what selects the winner. I have since been told that this is a known problem, but I do not know what it is called, how it is usually framed, or where its boundaries are. What worries me is that I have seen people treat a statement to the effect that Gen Z is different as a finding, when as far as I can tell it is an artifact of whichever assumption the analyst happened to make.

The second place is less statistical. I have watched generational labels get adopted by the people they are applied to. Someone reads that their generation is anxious, or bad with money, or unusually tolerant, and begins to describe themselves that way. Media and marketers repeat it. A few years later the label looks as though it predicted something it actually helped to cause. The label appears to make itself more true as it circulates. This feels connected to the first problem, because in both cases a generational category seems to be doing explanatory work while the real work is coming from somewhere else. But I suspect they are not the same phenomenon, and I do not want to lump them together if the right answer is to separate them.

So my question is whether there is an established name for either or both of these. What I want is the carving, not the label. If the first has a name, what exactly is the impossibility? Is it just an instance of confounding, or of the difference between correlation and causation, or is it something more specific? What assumptions do people standardly make in order to get around it, and are those assumptions usually defended or simply hidden? For the second, what is the name for a category that becomes real because people take it up, and how does that differ from a self-fulfilling prophecy, from ordinary social construction, and from the first problem? Finally, if both of these are true of generational labels, does that mean generations are not real kinds at all, or is there a sense in which they can be real even though the effects cannot be separated and the labels are partly self-made? I am less interested in a verdict about whether generations are real than in knowing precisely what kind of thing each of these two problems is and where the boundary between them lies.
