I'm in a graduate reading group on consciousness, and I've drawn the short straw: I have to open next week's session on the combination problem with a joke. The group holds a standing tradition of this, and the bar is high — a previous session opened with a gag about the hard problem that people still quote. So what I need is a joke that is genuinely funny to people who have spent real time on panpsychism and the combination problem, which means the humor has to turn on the substance of the problem and not on generic consciousness-is-weird material. The thing to aim at is the specific gap that gives panpsychists trouble: if experience is fundamental and pervasive, then a composite system's experience has to arise somehow from the experiences of its parts, and there is no accepted account of how many small subjects — or many small experiences, if you don't want to grant the parts subjects — add up to one unified subject with a single point of view, rather than to a mere aggregate, a bundle, or a committee that never quite reaches a quorum. The joke should land on that gap.

To keep it from being the thousandth version of the same gag, I'd like it built around a concrete scenario rather than a walk-into-a-bar setup. Something like a panpsychist trying to get a composite entity — a jury, a corporation, a marriage, a country, a thermostat with delusions of grandeur — to count as a single subject, and finding that the parts each have their own faint take on things and that no procedure exists for merging those takes into one point of view. It can be a short narrative joke, a mock dialogue, or a straight setup-and-punchline; use whichever form the material actually supports. Please don't make it a parody of jargon or a smug isn't-dualism-silly bit — the target is the real, unresolved difficulty, and the best outcome is a joke that a panpsychist could laugh at without feeling caricatured and that a physicalist could repeat without misrepresenting the view. If you can, give me two or three variants that attack the problem from different sides — one about whether the parts are subjects at all, one about what the quality of the combined experience would even be, one about where the boundary of a single subject falls — and tell me which one you'd actually open with.
