In a reading group on predictive processing I have now twice heard it asserted that perception is controlled hallucination, and the second time it was used to close a discussion rather than open one. A colleague maintained that since perception is hallucination, the question whether our perceptual experience is accurate — whether it answers to a mind-independent world — is confused, and that anyone still asking it has not understood the theory. I suspect the slogan is being made to do several different jobs at once, and I want it broken down before I press him on it.

Set out, as an enumerated menu, the distinct precise claims the sentence could be making, keeping its two halves separate. On the hallucination side, distinguish at least: the claim that perception is expectation-driven and constructive, so that the percept is never a simple copy of the input; the claim that perceiving and hallucinating are the same kind of process, differing only in how they are caused or constrained; and the claim that the content of perception is generated wholly from within the organism, so that the world does nothing but trigger the process. On the controlled side, distinguish the senses of control that might be at issue — controlled by sensory input, controlled by the world, controlled in the sense of actively steered, controlled in the sense of checked against something — and say what each would amount to.

For each reading, tell me whether it is a platitude that no one should ever have denied, a substantive and defensible claim within cognitive science, an empirical claim that is genuinely contested, or a claim stronger than the theory or the evidence will support. Then tell me which reading, if any, actually licenses my colleague's conclusion, and whether some weaker reading would instead support the more modest claim that perceptual accuracy is harder to establish than one might have assumed while remaining a coherent thing to ask about. Finally, say how much the slogan depends on the surrounding apparatus of prediction error minimisation and the free energy principle: which of the readings survive if one keeps only the intuitive picture of the brain as an inference engine and drops the formalism, and which collapse with it.
