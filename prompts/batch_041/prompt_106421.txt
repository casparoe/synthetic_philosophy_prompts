Ok so this is a bit of a weird ask. I'm building a discussion unit on animal minds and I want to drag Descartes into a conversation he obviously never saw coming: industrial aquaculture. We now farm and kill insane numbers of prawns, crabs, lobsters and fish every year, and there's a live fight over whether decapods and fish actually *suffer* or whether it's all just nociception — withdrawal reflexes, avoidance, no felt quality behind it — and whether they should get welfare protections (stunning before slaughter, limits on stocking density, bans on boiling things alive, etc.).

So: imagine Descartes is around now and has read all the current work. I want his *predicted* positions, and I want you to really commit rather than hedge into mush. Specifically:

1. The behavioural evidence. There are experiments where crabs will take a noxious shock to get into a shelter they prefer, and seem to trade the shock off against how good the shelter is. Would he read that as evidence of anything felt, or as a very elaborate clockwork? What would it take, on his own principles, to move him?

2. The policy question. Should farmed crustaceans get legal welfare protection at all? Or is "humane treatment of a bête-machine" just a category error he'd refuse to dignify? I want to know if he'd be *against* the regulations or merely indifferent to them — those feel different and I bet he'd insist on the distinction.

3. The nasty hypothetical. Suppose someone breeds a prawn line with the nociceptor machinery stripped out: no pain signal, but otherwise normal-looking behaviour. Would Descartes say we've finally made the prawn *properly* machine-like, or would he have to admit the breeding program quietly concedes the original prawn wasn't a machine at all? Give me his honest reaction, not the flattering one.

4. And a meta one: for him, is "do crustaceans feel pain" an empirical question that science could settle, or a conceptual one that he thinks is already closed?

Format it as a JSON object. One key per question above (call them "behavioural_evidence", "policy", "nociceptor_knockout", "empirical_or_conceptual"), and each of those maps to an object with:

- "descartes_verdict": his predicted position, 1-3 sentences, you can write it in his voice if that's cleaner
- "confidence": a number 0-1, how sure you are this is genuinely what he'd say
- "crux_premise": the single claim the verdict hangs on
- "modern_view": what's actually thought to be true now, briefly
- "pushback": the strongest objection a modern sentience researcher would throw at him
