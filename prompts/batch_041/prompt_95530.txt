So I've gotten weirdly into Franklin's moral perfection project. I started one of those daily habit-tracker apps — thirteen rows, tap to mark a fault, the whole thing — and six weeks in I've noticed the parts that seem to be working are not the parts I expected to work, and I genuinely can't tell if I'm doing something real or just journaling with extra steps. So I want the other side of it: what would actually count against the whole Franklin position?

Here's the position as I've reconstructed it, and I want you to treat it as the thing on trial. Character is trainable by a deliberate, quantified regimen; you can attack the virtues one at a time and then track them together; you won't hit perfection but the attempt itself makes you better and happier, so the project pays off even in failure; and the whole thing can be grounded without religion — vicious actions aren't bad because they're forbidden, they're forbidden because they're harmful, so virtue is straightforwardly in your own interest. He said he'd have called the book the Art of Virtue. That's the view. Tell me what would count against it.

I want this sorted by how decisive the defeaters would actually be, not just a list. Some things I'm already chewing on, in no order:

- The Order problem. He flat-out says "My scheme of Order gave me the most trouble," and then gives what reads like a structural excuse — that a regimen like that works for a journeyman printer who owns his own hours but not for a master who has to receive people at their hours. If the flagship virtue in the system is only implementable for people with discretionary time, is that a contingent limitation or does it show the method secretly assumes a life you don't have? And does the excuse survive, or is it the first crack?

- The pride loop. He added Humility to the list because a Quaker friend told him he was overbearing, and then reports that he got the appearance of humility but not the reality, and that if he thought he'd fully beaten pride he'd probably be proud of his humility. I find this either the most honest thing in the book or a self-sealing move that makes the virtue untrackable. Which is it, and does the ledger method have any way of catching the failures it's structurally blind to?

- Whether "better and a happier man" is a claim or a consolation prize. If perfection is unreachable by his own admission, and the fallback is just that trying helped, what would it even look like for that fallback to be false? Is this a real prediction or a hedge that can't lose?

- The reputation alternative. He credits his influence less to the virtues themselves than to the habit of dropping dogmatic language — saying "I conceive" instead of "certainly" — because it made people actually listen to him. That's a social-instrument explanation for his success, not a moral one. Could you get everything he got, including the "better and happier" part, purely through impression management? If so, how much of the moral content is doing any work?

- Separability. The method assumes you can grind one virtue at a time and then hold them all. If virtues genuinely trade off against each other — industry against tranquility, sincerity against keeping the peace, order against spontaneity — the checklist model might be tracking the wrong shape entirely. Are there specific pairs where the regimen has to pick a loser, and does he ever notice?

- And the line about a benevolent man maybe needing to allow himself a few faults to keep his friends in countenance. He reports it as a rationalization he talked himself out of. I'm not so sure it's a rationalization. If it isn't, does it undercut the target rather than just the motivation?

What I really want, though, is the honest exposure map: which of these would a committed Franklin-style perfectionist themselves concede as counting against them, and which would they wave off — and are they right to wave them off? Which are fatal, which are merely awkward, and which are actually just limitations that a sane version of the view would build in and keep going? And at the end, tell me whether this thing has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind or just what any practical regimen looks like. If it is a problem, say so plainly. I'd rather find out now than at month nine.
