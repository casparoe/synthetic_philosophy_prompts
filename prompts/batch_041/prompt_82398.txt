ok so I've been going back and forth on markets in repugnant stuff and I want a sanity check on where I've landed, because I have a feeling I'm cheating somewhere and I cant see where.

Here's the pile:

1. The test for whether a transaction should be legal is basically welfarist and voluntarist: if the people are competent, informed and not coerced, if it leaves them better off, and if it doesn't hurt third parties enough to outweigh that, it should be allowed. I don't think "it's icky" or "that's just not the kind of thing you buy" is an argument on its own. I've read the stuff about contested commodities and noxious markets and blocked exchanges and mostly I think it's hand-waving dressed up as philosophy.

2. Kidney sales: I think a regulated market with compensation is right, and the ban kills people. The waitlist is a body count. Most objections to paying donors are status quo bias or rich-world squeamishness about poor people making a choice I wouldn't make.

3. Price gouging: I'm fine with anti-gouging laws after a hurricane. Tripling the price of water and generators when people are desperate should be illegal, and the "prices allocate scarce goods" line doesn't save it.

4. Vote buying and selling: obviously illegal, and not only because of bad consequences. Something about a vote is damaged by being bought — it stops being a judgement about what's good for everyone and becomes just another asset you own.

5. I'm pretty anti-paternalist. People are allowed to make bad deals with their own lives and bodies.

6. I don't buy the crowding-out story, the idea that paying for something destroys the altruistic motive to provide it. That's mostly a just-so story.

My suspicion is that 3 and 4 don't come out of 1, and that 4 is quietly doing something 6 says I'm not allowed to do. But I also think 2 is genuinely different from 4 somehow and I can't articulate how without sounding like I'm just picking favorites. And I'm not sure 5 is even in tension with 3, or whether that's a fake problem — maybe protecting people from price spikes isn't paternalism at all.

So audit this for me. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each? Rank them by severity instead of telling me they're all a bit tense. For each real conflict, say what would have to give and what it would cost me. Then tell me which single change cleans up the most, and whether there's a principled line between 2 and 4 that isn't just me liking kidneys and disliking vote markets.
