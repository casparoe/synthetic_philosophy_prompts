hey, i'm supposed to present my own take on moral luck at a work-in-progress next week and i want a consistency check on the package before i stand up and defend it. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) the control principle, basically: you're only morally assessable for what was in your control at some point. i'll let people trace back, so if you're culpable now because you were culpable earlier for failing to notice something, that's fine by me — the control can be upstream.

(2) the two negligent drivers: the one who happens to hit the kid is more blameworthy than the one who doesn't. same negligence, and i dont just mean he feels worse or owes more repair — i mean his moral record is worse.

(3) nobody is blameworthy for thier character, since your character is handed to you by constitution and upbringing.

(4) blame is at bottom about quality of will — whether you were ill-willed or insufficiently concerned with the people you might affect — and not about what you actually caused.

(5) blame is only fitting when the agent had a fair opportunity to avoid being blameworthy; if there was genuinely nothing they could have done, blaming them is unfair, full stop.

(6) agent-regret is real, and the unlucky driver ought to feel it, not just tends to. that's not me being sentimental, i think it's a normative claim.
