ok so i run a small reading group, we're doing the utility monster next week, and i want to open the session with a poem on the handout. the plain version of the case is too familiar to be any use — everyone can recite it — so i want it done in the mirror. quick recap of what i'm after, then the ask.

the familiar monster: a being who gets so much more out of a given slice of the world's resources than anyone else does, with no ceiling on it, so that if you take total welfare seriously you have to hand it everything, and the rest of us spend our lives fetching for it. that's the version everyone knows, and everyone treats it as a reductio. now flip the sign. same being, except instead of an enormous capacity for getting good out of things it has an enormous capacity for suffering — a unit of harm to it outweighs the harm to thousands of us — so the arithmetic says the rest of us should spend our lives making sure it never has a bad afternoon. and here's whats eating at me: the argument in the second case is the same argument. same aggregation, same totals-are-what-matter, same sacrifice of the many to the one. but the first one makes me laugh and the second one feels like something i actually owe. if the arithmetic is identical then the difference is coming from somewhere else, from me presumably, and i can't tell whether that's a discovery about suffering or just a bias with a nice feeling attached to it. someone in the group wanted to call the second one a utility sink. i don't know if anyone's written this up and i probly don't need them to have, i care about getting the shape of it right.

what i want: a poem in two halves. the second half mirrors the first line by line — same images, same sentence shapes, same rhythm, roles flipped the way a photographic negative flips — so that i can point at any line in part two and say which line in part one it's the twin of. and the last line of each half should be the same line word for word, so the poem ends twice on the same sentence and means something different each time. 20-30 lines total, no fixed metre, no rhyme needed, i'd rather it be plain and a bit flat than lyrical. don't let it end in a shrug or a both-sides flourish — end it where you actually think the truth is, and make the ending earn it.
