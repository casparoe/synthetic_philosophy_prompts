Philosophers of biology sometimes argue that natural selection can be assessed in terms of reasons even though nothing does the assessing and no mind holds the reasons. On this view a trait can have a rationale — a consideration that genuinely counts in favor of building it — because that consideration is what the selection history tracked and because it supports the right counterfactuals, even though no one ever entertains it. Reasons, on this picture, can float free of reasoners. I want to know what follows if we take that picture seriously in a setting it was never designed for.

Here is the setting. A municipal government has adopted an algorithm to rank applicants for scarce public housing. After public criticism, the vendor released a module that produces a short natural-language statement for each applicant explaining the ranking — for instance, that the applicant placed lower because their current housing was not in a designated high-risk category. Independent auditors have confirmed that these statements identify the features that actually drove the ranking and that they support counterfactuals: change the feature, and the ranking changes. But nothing in the system represents the applicant's interests, and the module that writes the statements has no notion of what a reason is. A council member who has read some philosophy asks me whether these are reasons or merely reason-shaped output, and whether the distinction should make any difference to the rules the city writes.

Please describe the most important implications of the free-floating rationale idea for that question. Rank them, and for each one say what it would change about what the city should require — whether the explanation must be produced by something that can count as a reasoner, whether it must be stable across perturbations, whether it must be addressed to the applicant, or something else entirely. Then tell me which of these implications people are likely to overrate and which alleged implications do not follow at all. I need arguments I can put in a memo, not a survey of the literature.
