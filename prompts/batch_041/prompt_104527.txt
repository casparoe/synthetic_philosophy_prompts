I'm a software engineer without any formal philosophy training, and I've fallen down a rabbit hole on Hofstadter's Luring Lottery and Platonia Dilemma — the setups where a prize gets divided among everyone who claims it, or where the only way anyone wins is if exactly one person acts, so the right move depends on assuming that everyone else is running the same reasoning as you. I'd like to commission a satirical piece built on these ideas, but I don't want it to be a lecture in costume. Write it as an internal FAQ that a large tech company's HR department circulates when it launches a new shared success bonus: a fixed pool that gets divided evenly among every employee who files a claim, with management sincerely assuming that everyone will behave rationally and that the plan rewards teamwork. I want roughly 600–700 words in numbered question-and-answer format, written in a relentlessly upbeat corporate voice. The comedy has to come from the game theory being correct: the cooperative outcome is fragile, telling people to just trust one another is not a strategy, and once everyone reasons symmetrically the honest answer is a mixed one in which each person randomizes with some probability rather than either always claiming or never claiming. Include a worked example with actual numbers so a reader can see the arithmetic, and let the HR author keep offering well-meaning advice that quietly makes the situation worse without ever noticing that they have designed a mechanism that punishes the virtuous. A reader who knows the superrationality literature should laugh rather than wince; a reader who has never heard of Hofstadter should still be able to follow it. Keep the target on the practice of designing incentives without thinking through the equilibrium, not on any real person or company.
