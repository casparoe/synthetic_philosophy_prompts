I teach a 90-minute graduate seminar on probability and evidence (about fourteen students), and I want an activity that makes the group feel the difference between evidence that settles a question and evidence that merely shifts it. Use this scenario. Each round a hidden object carries two correlated features, with P(red) = 1/2, P(round | red) = 0.8 and P(round | blue) = 0.2, and each participant represents their joint credence as a hundred units spread over the four red/blue × round/square cells. Two readings then arrive, and participants are told only to take each as leaving them with credence 0.8 in its feature and nothing further. The rule I want them to reach without being told is that a reading about one feature changes only that feature's marginal while the conditional distribution of the other feature stays fixed within each level; anyone who instead collapses the disfavoured level and renormalizes should be visibly penalized by a quadratic scoring rule against the truth revealed at the end of the round. Split the room into two subgroups that get the same hidden object and the same two readings in opposite orders, so that they finish with different final credences in the same feature — roughly 0.85 against 0.80 on the numbers above — although both applied the rule correctly. They should not hear the rule's standard name until the debrief.

Please give me the full activity: rules, materials, any roles the readings require, the payoff scheme in points, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief. The debrief should tie moments in the game to the thought that uncertain evidence moves the weights and leaves the conditionals alone, contrast this with certain evidence, which is order-independent, and let participants discover for themselves that the order effect vanishes when the two features are independent. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be candid about what the activity cannot show; I expect the rigidity of the conditional probabilities, and the objection that one could always conditionalize on the reading itself instead, to be things I must stipulate and defend rather than things the game can demonstrate.
