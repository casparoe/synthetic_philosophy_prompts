Ok so this has been bugging me for months and I genuinely can't tell anymore if it's a real disagreement or if we're all just talking past each other. Background: in my lab we keep fighting about Dennett's ladder of creatures. Darwinian ones fixed by selection, Skinnerian ones that learn by reinforcement, Popperian ones that can run a hypothesis inside their head and let the bad one die instead of them, and Gregorian ones that offload thinking onto tools and language. The fight is about where current LLMs sit on it.

Camp one says a model that generates a chain of thought, checks it, throws it out and tries again is doing exactly the Popperian thing, letting the doomed hypothesis die in its stead, so it's at least Popperian and arguably Gregorian once you hand it tools. Camp two says no, that's still Skinnerian, nothing is actually being tested inside the model, the selection is happening outside it, you've just moved trial and error somewhere faster and slapped the word thought on it. And a third person keeps insisting the whole ladder is a category error, that it's one continuous axis of how much selection happens before you act and the four names are just landmarks we've reified.

We've been at this for a year. Everyone knows everyone's moves and nobody has moved. So I don't want a verdict on who's right, I want a diagnosis of the shape of the disagreement. Is the Popperian/Skinnerian fight verbal, two senses of hypothesis-testing, one meaning selection happened before death and the other meaning the creature represented its own conjecture as a conjecture? Is it a hidden empirical question about whether the model has anything worth calling a world model or a self-model, that we keep asserting instead of testing? Or is the third person right that it isn't a disagreement about creatures at all but about whether the taxonomy is a taxonomy of kinds or a scale, and everyone else is refusing to answer that because it would dissolve the argument?

Here's a hypothesis I want you to attack rather than agree with. I think Gregorian is doing all the work and the ladder is really two axes that got run together, namely where the selection happens (phylogeny, reinforcement, offline simulation, culture) and whether the creature can represent its own learning process. On my view almost every stalled argument about the ladder is really an argument about the second axis while people think they're arguing about the first. If that's right, say so and tell me what follows for how we should even pose the LLM question. If it's wrong, tell me where the two axes come apart, because I can't find a clean case where they do.

And be honest with me on the meta question: is this the kind of dispute where progress is even possible, a distinction or a bit of experimental work or a formal result, or is it one of those where the disagreement is bedrock and the only honest move is to stop asking and just stipulate a definition and move on? If it's the latter, tell me what I'm supposed to do with that, because it feels like a cop out and I suspect bedrock usually just means the question was ill posed rather than deep.
