You are a philosopher of mind who works on computational theories of consciousness and has read enough reinforcement learning to know the difference between a reward function and a reinforcer. Think carefully before answering, and start by setting out your own reconstruction of the position below, so I can see what you take it to be committed to before you start evaluating it.

The position I want to defend is this: reward is valence. Not that reward tracks valence, or is a useful proxy for it, but that in an agent whose behaviour is organized around a single scalar signal, that signal just is pleasure and displeasure — provided the signal meets three conditions. First, it is broadcast widely enough to modulate perception, memory, and action selection at once, rather than steering one subsystem in isolation. Second, it is bound to the agent's representation of its own states and prospects rather than to the world alone. Third, it is not fully transparent to the agent's instrumental reasoning: the agent cannot simply re-describe it as one more quantity to be traded off, the way it can re-describe a thermometer reading. The obvious rival is that reward is at best a drive signal, in the way that hunger is a drive signal — something that can be unpleasant without itself being affect.

What I want is a map of this position's exposure, not a survey. What would count against it? Which observations, arguments, edge cases, or formal results bear on it, and which of those would be decisive rather than merely awkward? Say what a defender of the view would themselves concede as counting against them, as opposed to what only critics allege. Distinguish the defeaters that come from how actual learning systems are built from those that come from how affect is thought to work. Tell me whether the three conditions have quietly turned the claim into something unfalsifiable — and if so, whether that is a defect here or the honest shape of the claim — and what the position predicts that the drive-signal rival does not. The kind of thing that would most move me: an agent that plainly satisfies all three conditions and is plainly not a subject of any experience, or a demonstration that something as thin as a thermostat or a firm satisfies them too.
