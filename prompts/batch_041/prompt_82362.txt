I am a third-year philosophy student in a course on confirmation and scientific inference, and my final assignment, in place of an essay, is to draft an adversarial-collaboration joint statement. The two parties are a defender of the Hempelian line, who holds that a non-black non-raven genuinely confirms the hypothesis that all ravens are black, because it is a positive instance of the logically equivalent claim that all non-black things are non-ravens, and a Bayesian, who holds that confirmation is only ever a matter of an observation raising a hypothesis's probability against a specified background, and that on any ordinary background the white shoe's effect is so minute that calling it confirmation explains nothing. Please write the document these two could both sign: a numbered list of agreed claims, covering what the hypothesis asserts, what the equivalence condition does and does not license, what a confirmation claim is a claim about, and what neither side denies; then each surviving disagreement, worded so that the side holding it would accept that wording as its own; then the cruxes, presented as a disagreements table, and for each crux the case, argument, or calculation both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Both parties must give explicit verdicts on two cases I will set: a survey that examines birds one at a time and so yields both black ravens and non-black non-ravens; and a survey whose sampling rule is unknown to the investigator, so that nothing can be said about whether the observed non-black non-raven came from a population that might have contained a raven. Add a short paragraph on what changes if the classification is made by an instrument with a known small error rate, so that no observed object is known with certainty to be a non-black non-raven. I would also like the statement to say plainly where the two sides are talking past each other — my own suspicion is that 'confirms' is doing qualitative work on one side and quantitative work on the other — and what would have to be fixed for that part of the dispute either to become a real disagreement or to dissolve. No citations, reading lists, or references to particular publications are wanted, and neither party may appeal to results it cannot reconstruct from memory.
