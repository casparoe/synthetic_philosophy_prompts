I am assembling a problem set for a graduate seminar on decision theory, and I want a comparative evaluation of the reasoning procedures students typically bring to the absent-minded driver problem. The case is this: you are driving home along a road with two exits; taking the first exit pays 0, taking the second pays 4, and driving past both pays 1. Because of the stipulated amnesia, you cannot tell whether you have already passed the first exit, so both exits give you exactly the same information, and you know this in advance; whatever rule you follow must therefore be the same at both.

Rank the five procedures below by how well each handles this case, judging both by the expected payoff of the strategy it recommends and by its defensibility as a general account of practical rationality. For any procedure that recommends a strategy, work out that strategy's expected payoff.

1. Local recomputation: at the exit, use your current credences about which exit this is — credences that depend on the policy you have been following — and take the action with the higher expected value, treating your choice at the other exit as beyond your present control.
2. Deterministic policy choice: select the complete rule from information states to actions that maximizes expected payoff if followed throughout, and follow it.
3. Randomized policy choice: the same, except that the rule may involve a coin flip, so that you choose the probability of exiting that maximizes expected payoff considered in advance.
4. Stability test: count a strategy as admissible only if, at every information state, its prescribed action is at least as good as any alternative given that the strategy is otherwise followed; then adopt the strategy that passes this test.
5. Outside help: before setting out, arrange for a device or a passenger — something with the memory you lack — to enforce a rule you cannot enforce on yourself.

Answer with a single JSON object and nothing else, with exactly these fields: "ranking", a list of five objects in rank order, each with "rank" (1–5), "procedure" (the number and short label), "recommended_strategy" (a sentence), "expected_payoff" (a number, or null if the procedure fixes none), "score" (0–10, where 10 means the procedure identifies the best available strategy and generalizes cleanly, and 0 means it recommends a strategy that is dominated or incoherent), "why_this_rank" (one or two sentences), and "main_defect" (one sentence); "best_overall" (the winning procedure, in a sentence); "strongest_objection_to_best_overall" (one or two sentences); and "agreements_worth_noting" (any two procedures that land on the same recommendation for different reasons, and whether the agreement is an artifact of these particular payoffs).
