Here's his argument, cleaned up:

(1) Suppose you're going to make a long series of bets and you rebet your whole bankroll each round. The fraction that maximizes your expected log bankroll is the fraction that grows your money fastest over many rounds. Bet anything else and, with probability approaching 1, you end up poorer than the log-maximizer. So the correct bet size is the one that maximizes expected log wealth. (The Kelly thing.)

(2) The reason it's log is that log is your actual utility for money. Maximizing expected log wealth just is maximizing expected utility, when your utility is logarithmic.

(3) But log utility has brutal diminishing returns: a dollar to someone with almost nothing is worth astronomically more than a dollar to you. So if log is your utility, you should hand over basically your whole savings to someone poorer until the next dollar is worth the same to both of you. Given how steep log gets near zero, that means giving away almost everything.

(4) One-shot bets get even weirder. Log utility refuses any bet with any chance of leaving you at zero, because log(0) is negative infinity. A 99.99% shot at 1000x with a 0.01% chance of total ruin gets rejected. So, in the limit, almost anything with a tail.

(5) So log utility tells you to beggar yourself for the poor and to turn down what looks like free money. Nobody is required to do either.

(6) So something has to go.

My problem: I actually use (1). I size bets this way and it works. And I definitely don't accept (5) -- I'm not liquidating my savings and I'd take that 99.99% bet without blinking.
