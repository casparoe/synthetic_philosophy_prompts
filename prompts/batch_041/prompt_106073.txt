I am currently taking a seminar on African philosophy and relational personhood, and our unit on ubuntu has left me with a formal problem that I would like you to solve in verse. We have been distinguishing a weak relational thesis, on which relations merely enable or shape an independently existing self, from a strong relational thesis, on which relations constitute the self, so that personhood is something conferred and sustained between people rather than possessed privately. We have also been distinguishing personhood as a status conferred by a community from personhood as a capacity for ethical response, and I want the poem to exploit that ambiguity rather than settle it. Write a villanelle that makes the strong thesis vivid without letting it collapse into the idea that the community can simply decide who counts as a person. The two refrains should be lines of your own—do not quote anyone—that express, respectively, the priority of the community in constituting the person and the priority of the person's own response in sustaining the community. Make the refrains grammatically parallel but not synonymous, so that their difference is audible even when they are adjacent. As the poem proceeds, each refrain should be infected by the other, so that by the closing quatrain they can sit side by side without being reconciled. The turn I want the form to enact is the moment the gift of recognition becomes indistinguishable from the power to withhold it: the same relational constitution that grounds the demand to recognize others also seems to give the community authority over the dissenter, the exile, and the one whose identity the community refuses to affirm. The poem should not resolve that worry; it should leave the reader with both the ethical promise of relational personhood and the internal critique that relational personhood may license conformity. Aim for a villanelle in strict form—five tercets and a closing quatrain, with the two refrains returning and meeting in the last two lines—and make sure the philosophical content does structural work: someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which refrain is which and why the form keeps them circling. Do not include citations or references to specific authors; I want the poem to stand on its own. After the poem, on a new line, give a one-sentence summary beginning Summary: that names the formal feature and the philosophical tension it tracks.
