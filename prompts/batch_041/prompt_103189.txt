I'm on our state historical commission — we're the ones who approve the text on the little roadside markers, rivet by rivet. Next month we vote on one for an 1893 law that let cities condemn and buy out the private water companies. The sponsoring county society's draft ends with "a first step toward the clean-water protections we take for granted today," and their very attached to that line.

Two of my fellow commissioners think I'm being precious. Their case: a marker has to tell a visitor why the thing mattered, and "mattered" can only ever mean "mattered for us," so some version of the forward-looking sentence is doing honest work. I think it turns the legislators into small-scale versions of us. The record is that they were arguing about fire insurance rates and a private monopoly, and a rival scheme — state-run regional water boards — looked just as much like the future to them, and lost. The branch that won is the branch we call the road.

What I need from you is the opinion I'll circulate before the vote: approve, approve with a rewritten text, or reject — with the actual rewrite if that's your answer — plus your view on whether our guidelines (they say markers must be "accurate and impartial," which we've never defined) should say anything about this in general. And please don't just humor me. If "a first step toward" is a harmless way of saying "contributed to," and my objection is really an aesthetic preference for dry prose, say that straight, even if it means telling me the memo I asked for is the wrong deliverable. I'd rather be told I'm wrong now than win the meeting and lose the guildlines fight.
