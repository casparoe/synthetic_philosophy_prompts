To close a seminar on moral revolutions, I want a short piece of verse. Write a round for three voices — the Argument, the Interest, and the Honor — to be sung in canon, entering at staggered intervals so that every line is eventually heard in every mouth and against every other line. The three share one text, and the staggering must do the philosophical work: at least one line has to be a boast when it sounds against one voice and a confession when it sounds against another, and at least one line has to be unusable as a reason until another voice has already passed through it, so that a consideration can be seen to fail as a reason for anyone until the world has shifted underneath it. Then let the voices drop out one at a time, and let the order in which they fall carry the poem's verdict on whether slavery and dueling ended through argument, interest, or honor. Eight to twelve lines, genuinely singable, and accurate enough that someone who has followed the dispute over abolition and the dueling code would recognize all three positions rather than merely their labels. Write the poem and the JSON in British English. After the poem, give a JSON object with these fields: "entry_order" (the order in which the voices enter and the line each leads with); "voice_readings" (an object keyed "argument", "interest", "honor", giving each voice's reading of the shared lines); "double_lines" (an array of objects, each with "line", "boast_against", and "confession_against"); "falling_order" (the order in which the voices drop out, and why that order is the verdict); and "left_open" (one sentence on what the poem declines to settle).
