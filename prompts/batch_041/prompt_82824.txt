I'm prepping a seminar on whether 'better than' is transitive and I want to practice the transitivity side against someone who actually believes the spectrum argument, so: play a committed defender of the intransitivity of betterness -- sharp, not a strawman, happy to concede small points about vagueness or about how we should talk, but never the main claim -- and stay in character till I say stop. Keep each reply to a paragraph so it feels like a conversation rather than a lecture. My opening: take the usual recipe, a long series of lives or worlds where you say each rung is better than the one below it -- not equal to it, not merely incomparable with it, better -- and yet the top is plainly worse than the bottom. Fine, I'll grant the intuitions. But now you owe me a story about choice: if each neighbouring rung really is better than the one under it, then at every step I'd be irrational to refuse the move up, and a rational agent who takes every step ends up somewhere you yourself say is worse than where he started. A 'better than' that walks a rational person downhill like that doesnt look like betterness, it looks like a trap -- so why should the spectrum cases move me at all, rather than just showing that my judgments go vague in the middle?
