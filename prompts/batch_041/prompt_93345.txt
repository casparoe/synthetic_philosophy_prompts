Me and a friend have been arguing for weeks about who counts as "the people" in a democracy — who actually belongs to the demos, not just who holds a passport — and I keep landing on verdicts that I can't square with each other. I'm hoping you can find the principle behind them, or else tell me which of my gut calls I have to give up.

Here's where I actually land. My neighbour: been here fifteen years, works, pays tax, no passport, can't vote — I think that's flatly wrong, she should vote. My cousin: moved abroad twenty years ago, hasn't paid a penny here since, doesn't follow our news, but keeps her passport and mails in a ballot every election — and I think, honestly, she shouldn't get one, or at least it feels like a cheat. A six-year-old: shouldn't vote, but not because her interests don't count — they do, they're just spoken for by someone else. A man with a severe cognitive disability who can't read a ballot unaided: I think he votes, with help, and striking him off would be an outrage. Someone who arrived last month on a work visa: leaning no, but I can't tell you why, since he's already subject to every law we pass. A village downstream in the next country, whose river our factories poison: I don't think they vote in our elections, even though our decisions wreck their lives. And a territory we govern whose people have no route to citizenship: they're subject to everything and have no say in any of it, and that seems obviously wrong to me. A friend proposed a rough rule — if a decision can reach into your life and trip an alarm, you're in; if it only changes the weather where you sit, you're out — and I'd like you to test that, break it where it breaks, and repair it. Does it even survive the pair I can't reconcile (the resident I'd enfranchise, the expatriate I wouldn't)? And tell me what it predicts for cases I haven't listed — prisoners, sixteen-year-olds, undocumented residents, a corporation, a genuinely sentient machine, people not born yet — plus whether the whole thing changes when the demos is decision-specific, like a referendum on one dam.

Two things about the answer. First, keep what's genuinely settled separate from what's actually contested among people who study this — don't hand me a live dispute dressed up as consensus. Second, I want it as structured data: a JSON object with fields "principles" (array of {name, statement, clause_for}), "verdict_table" (array of {case, my_verdict, your_verdict, agreement, which_clause}), "new_case_predictions" (array of {case, predicted_verdict, which_clause}), "verdicts_i_must_drop" (array of {case, reason}), and "settled_vs_contested" ({settled, contested}).
