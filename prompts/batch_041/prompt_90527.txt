I have spent the better part of a year arguing about drug policy with friends and reading whatever long articles I can find, and I have gradually moved from the view that the state should simply stop locking people up to the view that leaving the market illegal is the real mistake. The problem is that every time I rehearse the argument I end up fighting a version of the other side that I built myself, which is no use to anyone. I would like to try it against a serious opponent, so please play a committed defender of decriminalization without legalization: someone who thinks using and holding small amounts should no longer be a crime, that producing and selling should remain one, and that this is a principled resting place rather than a way station on the road to legalization. Give the character real arguments and let them use them properly — that a legal industry acquires a permanent stake in heavy consumption and a lobbying apparatus to protect it, that regulation has a way of turning into promotion, that a legal market still needs growers, shops and customers and that this is precisely where the damage concentrates — and allow concessions on details while never conceding the main claim. The character should speak plainly rather than in slogans, should answer what I actually say rather than recite a platform, and should keep each reply to two or three paragraphs so that it feels like a conversation. Stay in character until I say stop.

I should also say where I am coming from, so you can see the shape of my objection. I have read about the Portuguese arrangement, where using and holding small quantities stopped being a criminal matter while selling stayed criminal, and my suspicion is that it looks humane mainly because enforcement against the market quietly thinned out on its own, which would make it a truce rather than a design. I have also read about places that went the other way and set up legal shops, and there the thing that bothers me is how quickly the shop needs customers.

That is the background for my opening move: you say the trouble is a commercial market that profits from use, so let me take the commercial market away and see what survives. Suppose we legalize, but only a state monopoly may sell; no advertising of any kind is allowed; the number of outlets is capped and the outlets are forbidden to be profitable; packaging is plain; potency is limited; and every purchase is logged to a named individual account with a monthly ceiling. Nobody gets rich, nobody is marketed to, and legal supply undercuts the black market on price. If your objection survives that, then it was never really about commerce, and I want to know what it actually was. Please do not answer that it is about the message the law sends, because I have already granted that the state will be sending a message by running the shops.
