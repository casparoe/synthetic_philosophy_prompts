I've been reading rationalist blogs since forever and the one fight I never get tired of is whether an LLM can believe things. Can you write me a dialogue between two people going at it?

A is the intentional-stance pragmatist: you predict a system better if you attribute beliefs and desires to it, "belief" is a tool for prediction rather than a claim about inner furniture, and asking whether it *really* believes is a confused question. B is the skeptic: fine as a heuristic, but the second you say the model *believes* P you start treating its own "I believe P" as evidence about P, when that sentence is just more generated text off the same press as everything else — so the attribution launders a circular inference into a real one.

Give them one concrete case to chew on so it doesn't stay abstract: a model that, unprompted, re-plans around a deadline that moved. It never says "I thought it was at 3", it just acts as if the move registered. But ask it straight and it says it has no beliefs and no memory of the old time. B calls this belief-shaped behaviour with no believer, and says A is smuggling the extra thing back in. A says B's test just is the pragmatist's test — what makes us say it updated is that the behaviour is reason-sensitive across contexts, and that's all "belief" ever meant for anything, humans included. B: right, and that's the reductio, now my thermostat believes it's cold. Let A come back with the human analogy — we don't let a person off by their own denial if they always show up at 4, we say they believed it and confabulated — and let B answer that the disanalogy is that in the human case there's something the denial misrepresents, while here the denial and the behaviour are the same kind of output.

Neither should be a strawman, they actually answer each other, a few rounds. Also let them get into why it matters outside prediction: B thinks this is how you end up blaming a spreadsheet, A thinks refusing to attribute is how you end up blaming the devs for everything the model does.

Start with the dialogue itself, no preamble, no scene-setting, just names and go.
