I sit on the science advisory panel of a small foundation that funds long-horizon modelling of food and land use, and Thursday I have to bring a written recommendation on whether to renew a team's grant. Their model says rising demand plus finite arable land will produce a global food crunch, and they've been saying it for thirty years with a hard date each time: 2005, then 2015, now the "real" window is the late 2030s and everything earlier was just noise. Their line is that the mechanism is right and the timing was early -- the Malthus defence, basically -- and I don't know what to do with it. One panel member wants to cut them off, on the grounds that a theory whose deadline you can always push back isn't a theory, it's a mood. Another thinks that's too crude, since plenty of things we now think got something big right looked wrong for decades first, and the delays here have causes we can actually name (yield gains, trade, a couple of one-off policy shifts), not just fudge factors. Worth noting they did call the timing of regional groundwater depletion in two basins pretty closely, which is the one thing that makes me hesitate.

Our guidelines say renewals need a credible path to a test that could actually come out against the theory, and I have to hand over a decision plus milestones, not vibes. One member has proposed funding them for five years but freezing the model as of today -- no parameter changes, no re-fitting -- so the late-2030s claim either holds up or it doesn't. Write the opinion I'll circulate: renew, renew with conditions, or decline, with a time frame and something I can actually act on. Be honest about the strongest case against whatever you recommend, and say something usable about where the line falls between a theory being legitimately rescued and being rescued ad nauseum -- they've re-fit the parameters after every miss and insist each revision was driven by new data rather than by the miss itself, and I can't tell wether that's a distinction with a difference. Don't tell me to just wait for the data; the whole problem is that the data won't arrive in my lifetime.
