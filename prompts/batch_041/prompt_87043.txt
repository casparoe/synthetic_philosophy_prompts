My old teacher died last month and his sister has asked me to do the short remembrance at the funeral — four or five minutes, sixty-odd people, mostly family and friends from the part of his life before all this, plus maybe a dozen of us who sat with him twice a week for years. He taught Madhyamaka at a university for thirty years and actually practised it, so a handful of people in that room will know exactly what I'm up to and the rest wont know what's hit them.

The trouble is that the usual comfort is off the table. He said to us, more than once, that anyone who told his family he'd been reborn was doing it for their own comfort and not for his sake. So both of the easy moves are gone: the family would like to hear he's still here in some form, the practitioners would like to hear he's gone on to a better birth, and I've been told not to give them either.

What I want instead is for the tetralemma to do the actual work of the speech — he is, he isn't, both, neither — with me refusing all four corners and making that refusal feel like being held rather than like a man dodging the question. The fourth corner is the trap: 'neither' is still a position, and a refusal to say anything at all is the same trick wearing a hat. He was a Prasangika to the bone, so a speech announcing some positive doctrine about where he's gone would have been, to him, just one more view to be taken apart — but that can't turn into me lecturing a room of grieving people about emptiness with the grief stapled on at the end.

So the two truths have to carry it. On the ordinary level he was a real man who really is dead and we really do miss him, and none of that is a mistake in need of correction; on the other level there's no thing there to be either snuffed out or carried on, and the grief is dependently arisen too, which is exactly why it isn't a problem to be solved. Neither level can be allowed to eat the other.

Can you write the speech. British English please. One more thing — don't put anything in quotation marks as though it's from a text, because I'll be standing up there and can't check it; if you want to gesture at something he'd have read, paraphrase it. If my aunt leaves feeling talked at instead of looked after, it's failed.
