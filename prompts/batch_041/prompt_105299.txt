Ok so my reading group is doing Parfit's hitchhiker this week and I've written myself into a corner with an arguement that proves way to much. I need a second pair of eyes before I embarrass myself.

The case, fast, for anyone who's forgotten it: I'm stranded in the desert, out of water, when a driver pulls up. He'll take me to town only if I promise to pay him a big sum once we get there. We both know that after we arrive he has no way to make me pay, and we both know that when the time comes I'd rather keep my money.

Now the arguement.

(1) An act of mine is rational just in case, given how things actually go, it gets me the best outcome available to me.
(2) Standing at the roadside I have two options: promise-then-pay, and promise-then-stiff-him. The second leaves me just as well off as the first in every respect except that I keep the money, so it's strictly better.
(3) So the rational act is to promise and then stiff him.
(4) But the driver is no fool — if he works out that I'm the stiffing type, he drives on and I die of thirst.
(5) And even if he does believe me and takes me, once we're in town the best thing for me to do is still to stiff him.
(6) So either way, doing the best available thing at each point gets me either no ride at all or a worse life than the credulous dope who pays up.
(7) A theory of rationality on which the rational thing leaves you worse off than the irrational thing is a non-starter.
(8) So (1) is false.

My friend built this and says it's a clean reductio of act-consequentialism, and that Parfit's 'dispositions' move just changes the subject — sure, a standing disposition to pay is good for me, but that isn't what's being asked; what's being asked is which act is rational, and at the moment that matters the stiffing act is the rational one. I half think he's begging some question but I can't name it, and I'd really rather keep (1) if there's any way to.

So: which premise or step goes, why did it look so innocent, and what does dropping it actually cost me? Do I have to buy into the whole self-effacing-theory / rational-irrationality business to save the rest, or is that just relabeling the problem and calling it a solution?
