The unilateralist's curse, as I understand it: if some action is bad when one person does it but good when lots of people do it, and everyone's deciding independently, then the person who ends up doing it is whoever's most confident — which tends to be the wrong person — so the sensible move is to hold back and only act on some kind of shared decision. I want you to argue the other side: that this case for restraint is much weaker than its made out to be, because it quietly assumes our errors are independent. Once errors are correlated — same papers, same fears, same blind spots — restraint isn't obviously the safe option, and the argument for a conformity norm mostly falls apart. Give me the strongest version of that argument you can, not a sketch, and be honest about where it breaks down.
