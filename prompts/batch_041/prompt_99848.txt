ok so I teach a second-year seminar on early modern philosophy, and every year I end the Descartes unit on animals with The Island of Doctor Moreau, and every year I get the same lazy take: Descartes said animals are machines, so he'd be fine with Moreau, the end. I want something better, and I'm happy to be told my framing is off.

Here's what I keep circling. In the Discourse Descartes gives two marks for telling a machine from a mind -- real speech, and acting from reason rather than from the arrangement of organs. Now the Beast Folk talk, they reason a little, they chant the Law, they beg, and the puma screams while Moreau works on her. So does Moreau's island refute Descartes by cranking out creatures that pass his own tests, or does it just show the tests were never doing the real work, because the claim was about souls and not about evidence, and a Cartesian could look at the Beast Folk and say yes, exactly, clever automata, that's what I predicted?

Second thing: there's a genuine interpretive fight over whether Descartes denied animals sensation or only denied them thought, and Wells clearly wrote as if the stronger reading were true -- Moreau literally tells Prendick that pain is a beastly instinct a real human wouldn't have, then cuts his own thigh to prove it. So is Wells attacking Descartes or a strawman Descartes, and does that matter for what the novel is doing?

Third: the Law. The Beast Folk recite the prohibitions over and over and then revert anyway. That reads to me like a Cartesian picture of animal behaviour as habit and mechanism rather than understanding, dressed up in Victorian morality. Am I forcing that or is it actually there?

And the bit I actually want: what does the novel give me that the philosophical debate doesn't? Does it make the beast-machine doctrine feel true, or monstrous, or both at once -- and does Prendick mercy-killing the Leopard-Man land as an argument or just as sentiment?

Dont bother with citations or Wells scholarship, I can get that myself, I just want the reading. Also my students wont have read the novel, so keep it to scenes I can summarise on a slide.
