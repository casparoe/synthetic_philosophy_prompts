I hold the view that the Judy Benjamin problem exposes a genuine gap in Bayesian updating: when an agent receives information that is purely conditional in form -- for example, being told that if she is in Red territory then the odds are three to one that she is in the Headquarters area -- neither strict conditionalization nor Jeffrey conditionalization can determine how she should revise her credences, and the correct revision is the one that minimizes relative information (Kullback-Leibler divergence) from her prior subject to the constraint. What would count against this view? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be, including formal results about the uniqueness, stability, or dependence on the choice of distance function of the minimizing solution; intuitions about the case itself, such as whether it is acceptable that her credence in Red shifts even though the message said nothing unconditional about Red; and considerations from related norms, such as the reflection principle, the principle of maximum entropy, or the relationship between updating on conditionals and Adams-style probabilities. I also want an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- for instance, if defenders treat the minimizing solution as merely one admissible rule among several, or if they relocate the problem from the updating rule to the representation of the information. Which of these defeaters would a committed defender of the relative-information approach concede as counting against them, and which would they dismiss? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
