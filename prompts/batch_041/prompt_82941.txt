I teach a graduate seminar on consequentialism, and our final meeting is on cluelessness: the possibility that we have no justified beliefs about the consequences of our actions, and so a theory that tells us to maximize the good cannot tell us what to do. By the last week the students have sorted into two camps, and I would like to close the term with a poem that seats both of them in the room without marrying either. The first camp holds that the problem is fatal: trace any act far enough — a change to a syllabus, a tepid reference letter, a word in a corridor — and its distant effects are a coin flip between a heaven and a hell, so the near effects we can actually see carry no weight at all. The second camp holds that the worry cancels: our ignorance about distant effects is roughly symmetric, the errors come out in the wash, and the visible, local effects should still guide us.

I want a poem in terza rima — the interlocking chain of aba bcb cdc — because the form is the doctrine: each consequence becomes the next line's premise, and nothing in the poem can come to rest. Ten to sixteen tercets. Two further constraints, both structural.

First, refuse the closing line. Terza rima wants a final line rhyming back into the middle of the last tercet, which is what lets a terza rima end rather than merely stop; I want the chain left open, so the poem breaks off because the poet broke off, not because the consequences ran out.

Second, let the poem change regime. In the early tercets the consequences should be small and roughly symmetric, gains and losses cancelling, as the second camp says. Then, somewhere past the middle, the distribution should turn heavy-tailed: a single consequence, wildly improbable, so vast that it swamps every local effect the poem has accumulated, and the reader should be able to point to the line where the near effects stop mattering.

Finally, I want the ending to hold open the harder version of the problem, the one my students keep missing: even if the speaker knew every consequence, they would still not know how to weigh them, so the failure to close is not merely a failure of information. Do not resolve this. The last tercet should leave a middle rhyme that has no partner and never will. Someone who has taught the debate should recognize both camps and the moment the accounting is taken over by something the speaker cannot see; someone who has not should still find it a poem rather than a lecture.
