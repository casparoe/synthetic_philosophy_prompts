I work in infectious disease modelling — comfortable with compartmental models, reproduction numbers, and transmission routes, but not a philosopher — and I keep meeting the claim that culture evolves in a Darwinian way, with cultural variants as heritable, mutable things that are differentially transmitted and selected. I want that position restated, as faithfully and as strongly as it can be, in my own vocabulary: susceptible and infectious compartments, R0 and effective reproduction number, vertical versus horizontal transmission, vectors and reservoirs, incubation and latent periods, overdispersion and super-spreading, herd-immunity thresholds, and the drift-versus-reassortment distinction for how a circulating variant changes. Two constraints. First, keep the translation honest: where the Darwinian picture is committed to something epidemiology has no place for, do not quietly drop it, and do not smuggle in epidemiological assumptions — mass action, well-mixed populations, one pathogen per host — and call them translation. Second, give me the residue report: which claims carry over cleanly, which carry over only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed at all; for that last class, say whether the gap marks a defect in my vocabulary or shows that the original claim was looser than it sounded. Two points I want adjudicated rather than gestured at: whether replication fidelity and mutation rate have genuine analogues here or only decorative ones, and whether the epidemiological notion of a case outcome has any counterpart for a cultural variant, given that what a cultural item does to its host depends on how the host reads it. State how confident you are in each verdict, and single out the one you are least sure of.
