I'm a science-and-ideas columnist and I'd like to run a peice of short fiction for once, so this is a commission. Write me a story, 1200-1800 words, built around one gadget: a handheld scanner you can walk across a patch of habitat that returns a running hedonic budget, a single number netting out the sentient suffering against the sentient flourishing it detects, species by species, down to the insects. Put it in the hands of a young field tech doing a routine biodiversity survey on a much-loved pristine reserve, and let the tension come from the budget coming back deeply negative, worse per hectare than the grim monoculture farm down the valley that everyone in her office uses as a byword for ecological ruin. No villain, no lecture, and please don't resolve it: what I want the reader to sit in is the trap shes in, where the numbers say the wilderness is a charnel house, every intervention she can imagine (feed them, sterilize them, cull the predators, pave it and plant soy) trades one creature's agony for another's, and she has no authority to make that trade. If the machine starts to feel like a character with its own opinion about being switched off, even better.
