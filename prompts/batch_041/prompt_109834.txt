I'm teaching a seminar on Von Neumann's views on the brain, and my students keep leaning on the brain-is-a-computer analogy to conclude that reverse-engineering the brain will give us something like a von Neumann architecture, and that AI alignment should just copy biological principles. But Von Neumann himself was more guarded. He treated the nervous system as a computing machine while stressing mixed analog and digital signaling, massive parallelism against high component speed, reliability built from unreliable parts, and a possible split between the mathematical-logical specification and the actual physical realization, with the latter maybe statistical rather than mathematical. Audit that analogy for me. Give a correspondence table: what plays the role of processor, memory, program, clock, input and output, architecture, and code. Then the disanalogies that matter for the students' two conclusions, and the ones that don't. What does the analogy quietly import from digital computers that Von Neumann either denied or left open? Does the students' conclusion survive once that's on the table? And if the analogy is bad for this use, what better analogy would you propose for the brain's relation to computation, if any?
