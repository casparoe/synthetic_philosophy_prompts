ok, before my reading group meets I want to get my own cards on the table, and I'd rather have someone poke holes in them then read me a summary of the debate. Here's what I actually think:

(1) Some contradictions are true. The liar sentence is both true and false, and I take that to be a fact about the world, not a quirk of English.
(2) Validity just is truth preservation — an argument is valid iff it's impossible for the premises to be true and the conclusion not true. Full stop, no riders.
(3) So the one right logic is paraconsistent, something LP-shaped: three values, 'both' among them, and a contradiction doesn't drag everything down with it.
(4) But non-contradiction is still a norm for me. I don't believe a contradiction unless an argument forces me into it, and a contradiction among my beliefs is a defect I'm obliged to fix, not a place to settle down.
(5) Truth is substantial — a sentence is true in virtue of how things are, not just because we're willing to endorse it.
(6) Classical logic is still exactly right for arithmetic and for everyday reasoning. Modus ponens on my shopping list, and "either the keys are in the car or on the hook, they're not in the car, so they're on the hook" — all of that is fine. The paradoxes are a local mess.
