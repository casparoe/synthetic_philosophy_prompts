Is my knee pain up to me? My grief over my dad? Whether I stick to a diet? Whether my kid stays sober? My character, if my character is downstream of how I was raised? My effort, when effort itself depends on how tired I am? A friend has a case he calls the umbrella problem: the rain isn't up to me, but whether I get soaked depends on whether I brought an umbrella, and whether I brought one depended on a forecast I trusted. So where is the line, exactly?

Aim the spec at the practical, self-management use, since that's the version everyone actually tries to live by, but don't let it off the hook on the metaphysics. Before I sit through another argument about whether the dichotomy is the whole of Stoicism or just a motivational poster, give me the spec: what does any adequate theory of 'up to me' in this tradition have to deliver? - the cases it has to classify correctly, with the case behind each requirement, paradigm ones and ugly ones like the above
