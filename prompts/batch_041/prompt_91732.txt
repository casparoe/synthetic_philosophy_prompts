ok this is for the reading group i run — i want to open the session with a sestina, and i want it to be about the two spheres. the setup, in case you need it spelled out: an otherwise empty universe, nothing else in it at all, and two iron spheres. same size, same shape, same temperature, same colour, same weight, same everything you could possibly name. they sit two miles apart. the only question is whether thats one sphere or two — and if its two, what on earth the difference between them actually is. it cant be any of the qualities, obviously, since both of them have all of them. and it cant even be "being two miles from the other sphere," because thats true of both of them too, it's symmetric. so whatever the difference is, if there is one, its not a quality at all — its just the bare thisness, this one and not that one, and theres no word for that.

so heres what i want the form to do: every end-word in the sestina should be a predicate that holds of both spheres equally. no names, no "this one"/"that one," no left/right, nothing that could single one of them out. i want the whole poem trapped in shared predicates, because thats the whole problem. and i want the envoi to be where it breaks — the moment the poem goes looking for the missing word, the thisness, and finds nothing there to send.

dont resolve it either way. honestly i dont know if its one sphere or two and i dont want the poem pretending to. and dont name the thought experiment or any philosipher in it — just render the thing. someone whos sat with this should recognise exactly where the difficulty sits; someone whos never heard of it should still get a real poem out of it. standard 39-line sestina. ty!
