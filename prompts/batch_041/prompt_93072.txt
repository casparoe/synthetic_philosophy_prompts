Two friends of mine, Ruth and Dev, got into a fight at a dinner party last week about Wittgenstein and religion and I couldnt follow who was winning. It started because our other friend Nora, who is religious, said two things about herself in the same breath. First, that she takes it seriously that she might be wrong and that if the historical evidence went badly enough she'd have to give up her faith. Second, that no skeptical arguement has ever actually moved her and she doesn't think of her faith as a bet on some historical claim.

Ruth jumped on that as a plain contradiction. Her line: on Wittgenstein's view religious belief isnt a hypothesis, its not the kind of thing that gets confirmed or refuted, and anyone who says she'd drop it if the evidence turned is already treating it as a hypothesis. So Nora isnt a religious believer in the sense Wittgenstein is after, she's just an empirical theist with bad evidence. And Nora's second remark is the tell, that's what faith actually looks like, evidence isnt the currency.

Dev said Ruth is running three different claims together: (i) my belief isnt based on evidence, (ii) my belief cant be refuted by evidence, (iii) I dont treat evidence as relevant to it. Dev thinks Wittgenstein is saying something like (i), or maybe (iii) -- a claim about how the belief sits in a life, not a claim that the believer has a magic shield -- and that Ruth is sliding (i) into (ii). On Dev's reading Nora can hold that her faith isnt inferred from evidence and still think that if the tomb turned out to be occupied it would matter a lot.

Ruth said fine, but if it would matter then evidence is the currency after all and it's just a hypothesis with a very high threshold; you've described someone who updates slowly, which is a fact about her psychology, not a deep point about religious language. She also said (iii) does no work on its own -- "I dont treat evidence as relevant" is just stubbornness unless you can say why the evidence isnt relevant, and the only way to say that is (ii).

Dev came back that the belief is held as trust or commitment, like trusting a person, and trust can be sensitive to evidence without being a prediction, so Ruth's "either a hypothesis or a shield" dichotomy is false. Nora's first remark is just her being honest about her situation, not a description of what the belief is. Ruth said trust in a person is exactly a case where you do update on evidence, so the analogy gets Dev nowhere, and anyway Wittgenstein's own examples are things like the Last Judgment that nobody can check, so dragging in a checkable historical claim is a category error.

I genuinely couldnt referee this and they want a ruling. Go through it move by move and say who's right at each step. Two things I'd want kept seperate: which reading of Wittgenstein is actually more defensible, and which position is more defensible on the merits, regardless of what he thought. And tell me whether Nora's two remarks are really in tension or whether Ruth just built the tension by assuming faith has to be one single kind of thing.
