I just finished Nietzsches Genealogy of Morals and it's been rattling around in my head, so I wrote up a short essay and I'd like your comments on it.

The gist: Nietzsche says bad conscience shows up once people are finally penned into society and can't discharge their aggressive instincts outward any more, so the instincts turn inward — cruelty that used to be spent on other people gets spent on yourself. I think this is best read as a claim about niche construction. The moral system a population adopts changes the environment it lives in, and then that changed environment feeds back and selects on the very people who built it: the strong get tamer, and the resentful get a weapon that works better than a fist. On that reading the slave revolt in morals isn't only a psychological event, it's an ecological one, and I think it makes the account more testable than people give it credit for. If that kind of morality spread by remodeling the selective environment, we should expect something like a domestication signature in humans over the same stretch of time. I also claim that Nietzsche's gripe with the genealogists who came before him wasnt that they explained morality naturalistically, it was that they did it badly — no historical sense, no patience for the idea that a trait can change its function over time.
