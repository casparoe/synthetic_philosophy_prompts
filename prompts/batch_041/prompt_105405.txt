ok so i've been chewing on something for weeks and i don't have anyone to hash it out with, so bear with me. It started when my friend and I realised we cared about totally different causes — she's all about animal stuff, factory farming, that whole thing, and i'm more about global poverty. So we worked out a kind of deal: I'd go vegetarian for a year, even though honestly I don't think eating meat is that big a deal, and she'd give a chunk of her salary to an anti-poverty charity even though she thinks poverty is fine-but-not-urgent. Both of us ended up with more of what we actually care about than we'd have gotten on our own. We called it trading, like countries do.

Then it got weird, because I started wondering how far you can push this before it turns into something gross. So what I want is for you to rank the different versions of this trade idea by how defensible they are — most defensible at the top, most rotten at the bottom — and say in a sentence or two why each lands where it does. Here's the versions I came up with:

1. The one above: two people, each doing something they think is okay-but-not-important, to get something they think matters more.
2. Same shape, but one of them actually thinks the other's cause is a waste of time, maybe even a bit harmful, and goes along anyway because the deal still gets them more of what they want. Like me paying for something I think is pointless because it buys me something bigger.
3. A version where we both win but somebody outside the deal loses — we agree on some policy thing that helps us both and quietly screws over a third group neither of us much cares about.
4. Me doing something I actually think is *wrong*, not just unimportant, because the trade gets me enough of what I care about to be worth it.
5. Trading with my future self: I do the annoying thing now, later-me gets the payoff, and I reckon later-me would agree if you could ask him. (not sure this one even counts as a trade.)
6. Paying someone else to be good on my behalf — I write the check, they do the actual virtuous stuff, we both come out ahead. Basically outsourcing.

I've probably lumped together things that shouldn't be in the same pile, so tell me if that's what I've done and why it matters. And be straight about how confident you are in your calls — say where you're on solid ground and then single out the one you're least sure about, because I'd rather know where you're guessing than get a tidy-looking list that's actually shaky underneath.
