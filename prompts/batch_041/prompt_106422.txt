Write an essay on a dispute in forecasting methodology. When an inside view — a bottom-up estimate built from the particulars of the case in front of you — and an outside view — the base rate for a reference class of similar past cases — conflict, which should give way? The received answer is that the outside view corrects the inside view's optimism, and that epistemic modesty therefore requires deference to it. But the outside view cannot get going without a prior, inside judgement about which reference class is the relevant one, and the choice of class can swing the estimate from near-certain success to near-certain failure. Consider a charity deciding whether to fund a first-in-human gene-therapy trial for a very rare disease: the mechanistic case for success is strong, the base rate for that stage of development is discouraging, and it is unclear whether the honest reference class is all gene therapies, all rare-disease trials, or all first-in-class modalities. Weigh whether the outside view can be a principled corrective at all, given that its reference class must be chosen from the inside, and reach a definite verdict: either set out how the class should be chosen and why that does not simply smuggle the inside view back in, or argue that the outside view's authority is more limited than its reputation suggests. Commit to a position, state how confident you are in it, and defend it; do not end by laying out considerations on both sides.
