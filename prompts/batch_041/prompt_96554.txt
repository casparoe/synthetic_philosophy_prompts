I chair the ethics consult service at a hospital that's the first site for a trial of what the protocol calls substitutive neuroprosthesis in early-onset Alzheimers. The pitch is that over about two years you swap out the patient's cortical neurons a couple of percent a month for synthetic units engineered to reproduce the same inputs and outputs, so the patient stays awake, talking, testing normal the whole way through. First patient goes in next month and I've realised that nobody has thought about the questions that are going to come up at every single milestone: is the person in the bed the same person who signed the consent form, does the advance directive she wrote three years ago still bind, is her husband still her husband for the purpose of signing as surrogate, and if any of those flips to no at some point, at what percentage does it flip and on what basis.

I want you to turn the philosophical work on personal identity into a procedure my team can actually run. Include the main families of view, the ones that locate you in psychological continuity, the ones that say you're the living organism whatever happens to your psychology, and the ones that say identity isn't the thing that matters and we should stop asking about it. It has to be runnable in about 48 hours by a nurse, a neurologist and the hospital lawyer, none of whom are philosophers, and it has to end in a decision rather than a shrug. Which questions do we ask, in what order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where does the procedure stop with a no, and where does it hand the decision to a person because what's left is values rather than facts. The IRB has to bless this as protocol-level policy and the attending still makes the bedside call, so tell me which parts of the procedure can be policy and which parts can't.

Three things you have to deal with. The units are built to reproduce her behaviour and her self-reports exactly, so when she says she feels like herself that can't be the test, but we can't just ignore her either. There's no sharp moment where she stops being her, which means any threshold we pick is arbitrary, and I need you to say whether that's fatal or just something we have to own. And every unit runs the same firmware, which is a kind of continuity that none of the standard views seem to have a slot for.

Then run it on three cases. One: she's 40% replaced, sharp as ever, wants to continue, but her directive says that if she stops recognising her family we should stop, and she does still recognise them. Two: she's 80% replaced, now says she wants out, and her pre-trial directive said specifically that a later version of her might say that and shouldn't be believed. Three: her son wants her declared dead so the estate opens up and the marriage question becomes real. At least one of those should get an answer I won't like.

Be straight with me about what the procedure can't do, and separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually know this material — dont hand me a contested view dressed up as the consensus. Write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold or tables, just the way you'd write it to me in an email.
