I am a PhD student in philosophy, and my dissertation chapter concerns decision theory under conditions of diachronic forgetfulness. I have been preoccupied with the absent-minded driver problem: a driver approaches a sequence of exits, knows that at the first exit she can take a modest reward, that at the second exit a larger reward awaits, and that if she passes the first and then fails to take the second she gets nothing; the difficulty is that she will forget, upon arriving at each intersection, whether it is the first or the second, so she cannot choose by looking at where she is, only by adopting a policy in advance. I have been trying to understand what the problem actually teaches about agency, and I have started to suspect that its real interest lies not in the arithmetic but in the way it separates the agent who chooses a policy from the agent who executes it.

It occurs to me that Christopher Nolan's Memento is a sustained dramatization of this separation. Leonard cannot form new memories; he navigates by tattoos, Polaroids, and notes, and he is constantly arriving at intersections, literal and figurative, without knowing which one he is at. I would like you to read the film through the absent-minded driver problem, and through a variant I have been developing: what happens if the driver can leave herself messages? Does Leonard's system of external memory solve the problem, or does it merely relocate the forgetfulness into the artifacts, so that the policy is executed by a sequence of temporary agents rather than by one enduring agent? Does the film vindicate the decision-theoretic approach, or does it expose a gap between the formal problem and the lived experience of someone who cannot remember choosing?

Please give me a reading that takes both the film and the problem seriously: map the correspondences between the driver's situation and Leonard's, identify the scenes or structural choices that illuminate the problem, and say where the analogy breaks down or where the film resists being reduced to an illustration. I am especially interested in whether the film suggests that the absent-minded driver problem is not a puzzle about knowledge but a puzzle about what counts as the same agent over time, and in what the film adds to the philosophical debate that the standard formulations leave out. I know the film well, so there is no need to summarize the plot; I am teaching this material to undergraduates next term, and I would appreciate a reading that is precise about the decision theory and honest about the film's ambiguities.
