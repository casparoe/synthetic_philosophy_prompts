Ok so I've been reading about this thing where a group votes on a bunch of connected yes/no questions one at a time, and the group as a whole ends up with a set of answers that contradicts itself. The example I saw was a jury — they vote on whether the person did it, whether they meant to do it, and then on whether they're liable, and the group can come out saying yes, yes, no, which can't be right and which none of the jurors actually believes on their own. The strange part is that in the example each juror is being completely consistent and honest the whole time, and the contradiction only shows up once you add their votes together. Which honestly sounds like a trick to me. In real life when a group ends up contradicting itself it's because people are sloppy, or trading favors, or not saying what they really think — nobody is actually walking around holding two opposite beliefs at once. So isn't the lesson just that people should be sincere and think it through properly?

And I think I've got the fix anyway: you make everyone vote on all the facts first, and then the final verdict just follows automatically from those votes, so the contradiction never gets a chance to happen. I want to propose exactly that at our next tenants association meeting, since we keep tying ourselves in knots deciding stuff about the building. So is that right — we do it that way and nothing's lost? Or is there a catch I dont see.
