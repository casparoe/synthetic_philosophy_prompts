Ok so I'm about 250 pages into this novel — thinly veiled WWII-ish thing, a rural gendarmerie unit in an occupied country gets the order to march the local Jewish villagers out to a ravine and shoot them. I keep running my scenes past a friend of mine who's a Holocaust historian, mostly so I don't write something that'd make an actual survivor laugh at me. Her read on the stuff I've asked about so far:

- The Reserve Police Battalion 101 business (middle-aged Hamburg cops, not SS, handed rifles and told to shoot): she says these were just ordinary guys, not monsters, and what did it was the drift, the group, the fact that nobody wanted to be the one to step out — but a couple of them DID step out and nothing happened to them, which she says proves it was never compulsion.
- The willing-executioners thesis (the whole German nation was raring to kill): she thinks it's half right and mostly wrong. Sure the antisemitism was deep and old, but a nation doesn't just wake up and butcher its neighbors, you still need the war and the permission and the situation.
- Milgram: she waves it off. Nobody in that lab thought they were actually frying somebody, so it tells you about obedience to a buzzer, not about a massacre.
- Rwanda, the neighbors with the machetes and the radio: here she changes her tune. She says the framing did real work there — the propaganda that turned neighbors into cockroaches — thats why ordinary people could do it.
- And when I asked about the real eager ones, the guys who volunteered and enjoyed it, she just said some people are in it for the cruelty and no situation explains that.

So now my new scene: ordinary conscripts, not volunteers, who've had years of state propaganda telling them this group is a fifth column and a disease; they're ordered to shoot them; they can refuse, no punishment, and a few do; there's one young lieutenant who's a true believer and an older sergeant who clearly hates every second of it.

Which way is she gonna go here — situation or culture/framing? I care way less about the verdict than about the actual rule she's running, whether there's another rule that fits everything she's said just as well, and what case I could put to her that would force her to choose between them. And if it turns out she's really just running character/selection, tell me, cause then my poor sergeant is a waste of ink.
