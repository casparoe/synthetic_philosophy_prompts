A clinical laboratory validating a new diagnostic test reports two numbers: how often the test comes back positive in people known to have the disease, and how often it comes back negative in people known not to have it. The second number is offered as evidence for the rule "everyone with the disease tests positive." A long run of negative results in healthy controls is treated as strengthening that rule, and a test that has never been positive in someone without the disease is praised for it. Yet the same practitioners would find it absurd to say that a green apple confirms that all ravens are black, even though the two inferences appear to have the same logical shape.

What theory of confirmation does the diagnostic practice presuppose? Does it commit its users to the view that logically equivalent hypotheses are confirmed by the same evidence, and hence to the raven conclusion that non-black non-ravens confirm that all ravens are black? Or can the practice be reconstructed without that commitment — say, as a Bayesian story in which the controls are informative because they bear on the same parameter the disease cases do, or as an appeal to the controls being a sample drawn from the relevant population — in which case a relevance-restricted account would license both the lab's reasoning and its scorn for the green apple? And is the practice internally consistent, given that in other contexts the same practitioners dismiss negative results as irrelevant when the cases tested are of the wrong kind? Argue this out from the practice itself rather than surveying the literature, and write your answer in these five sections:

1. The inference in question (~120 words): restate the diagnostic practice as a general claim about what confirms what, isolating the exact inferential move at issue.
2. What the practice presupposes (~400 words): the implicit theory of confirmation, and specifically whether the practice requires the equivalence condition.
3. Strictly presupposed versus merely fits (~300 words): which parts of the practice need that theory and which are compatible with several accounts.
4. Where the practice is underdetermined (~250 words): the rival theories that would license it equally well, and what would decide between them.
5. If the presupposition were replaced (~300 words): what a relevance-restricted account would change in how the lab reasons about, reports, and defends its validation studies.
