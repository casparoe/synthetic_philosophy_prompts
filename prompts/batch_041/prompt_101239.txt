I have been sitting in on a small discussion group that works through the standard heuristics for comparing causes — the ones that tell you to weigh how much a problem matters, how far a plausible intervention could move it, and how few other people are already pushing on it — and I keep noticing a structural feature of the whole exercise that I cannot put a name to.

Here is the pattern as it shows up in practice. Whenever we try to score a problem, the ones that come out looking most overlooked are almost always the ones where nobody has yet found a workable way to make progress, and that very absence is what drags down the score for how far an intervention could move it. Run it the other way and the same coupling appears: the problems that score well on solvability are usually the ones where a method already exists, and a method existing is usually what draws people in, which pushes the overlookedness score down. So two of the three criteria seem to be reading the same underlying fact — how much attention a problem has already received — from opposite ends, and the combined ranking quietly tilts toward the well-trodden and away from the frontier. I first noticed it with something as plain as a disease that has a treatment versus one that does not: the untreated one is by construction the more neglected, but it is also, by that same fact, the less tractable, and the framework gives me no way to say whether that is two independent reasons or one reason counted twice.

What I want to know is whether this has an established name and a body of work, or whether I am just misreading a well-known caveat. If it is a real pattern, what exactly is the pattern — is it a genuine bias built into how the criteria are estimated, or an artifact of scoring coarse categories, or something that dissolves once you score marginal opportunities instead of whole causes? How does it differ from the nearest things I can think of: the tendency to hunt for solutions where the light is good rather than where the problem is, the way any single score gets gamed once people start optimizing for it, and the general worry that a cause looks neglected precisely because it is hard? If nothing established covers it, please coin a term, define it precisely enough to have boundary cases, and tell me what falls just outside it.
