Norway and Sweden spend a fortune trying to get men and women to make the same choices — dad-only parental leave, boardroom quotas, cheap childcare — and by most measures they're about the most equal countries there are. But here's the thing that buggs me: the more equal a country gets, the more men and women seem to sort into different jobs and fields. The rich egalitarian places seem to have bigger gaps in what people choose to study and work at than the poorer ones do. I don't want to argue about whether that's true, I want to know what our policy was actually assuming. Does paying for all that stuff presuppose that the difference in choices is all culture and constraints, so that knocking down the barriors should make it shrink? Or could you run the exact same policy while thinking the sorting is partly nature, and the policy is only ever about opportunity? Reconstruct the view of human nature and of equality that the practice really needs, tell me which parts are genuinely forced by it and which are just one option among several, and say what would have to change if the nature side turned out to be doing a lot of the work.
