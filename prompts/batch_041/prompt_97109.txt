I'm in a reading group that's been chewing on procreative beneficence and liberal eugenics — the view that when you're choosing among embryos you could implant, you're not just permitted but actually obliged to pick the one whose life is expected to go best, and that "best" isn't limited to the absence of disease, it can include stuff like intelligence or whatever else tends to make a life go well. Anyway I want to write a funny piece for the group, and I've settled on the form being an advice column / agony-aunt page — a few reader letters, one columnist answering them. Please write it.

The columnist is definately a true believer and a competent one, not a strawman: warm, patient, reasonable-sounding, and completely committed. The humor has to come from watching the principle get applied with total consistency to cases where you'd expect a normal advice columnist to flinch, and it has to stay deadpan — no authorial nudges, no "needless to say this is horrifying." Roughly 600-700 words, at least three letters. Some hooks you could build around (use what works, don't force all of them): a couple asking whether they should screen out a gene for a condition that's mildly inconvenient but totally livable; a parent asking whether, if "best life" really is the standard, they're now obligated to pick the higher-IQ embryo and not just the healthier one; someone pushing back that selecting against a trait is an insult to people who already have it; and someone pointing out that if every parent optimizes for the same trait, nobody's kid ends up ahead — the columnist's answer there is the part I most want to see handled with a straight face. It'd also be good to have a letter where the columnist has to say what, if anything, is owed to the embryos that dont get picked.

After the column, add a short note in your own voice (outside the satire) on where the piece takes liberties with the view or leans on a contested reading of it, and say what a defender of the principle would have to show for you to feel the satire was unfair.
