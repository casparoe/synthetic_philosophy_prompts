I've ended up defending a view about pleasure and I want you to try to break it. The heterogenity problem — that a warm bath, a solved proof, a good meal, and a rival's misfortune can all be pleasurable yet seem to share no single felt quality — pushed me toward the attitudinal line: what makes something a pleasure isn't a common sensation, it's that you take a certain pro-attitude toward it, you like it or are pleased that it's so. The pleasures get unified by the attitude rather than by any shared feeling. Tell me what would count against this.

I'm after the things that actually threaten the attitudinal fix specifically, not the generic "hedonism is shallow" stuff. A few threads I keep circling:

- If pleasure is just the attitude, why is this still hedonism and not desire-satisfaction in a wig? What does it predict that a plain desire theory doesn't?
- The pleasures that don't seem to have any content — the itch scratched, the warm bath, whatever a creature with no concepts feels. Are those counterexamples, or can the view just absorb them? And if it can absorb everything, is there anything left that could count against it at all?
- Can you add them up? A hedonism about well-being seems to need a common currency, and if the pleasures are genuinely heterogeneous — even if unified by the attitude — I don't see what the exchange rate is. Is there a version of the summation objection that survives the attitudinal move?
- A determinable/determinate angle: maybe pleasure is like color, where red and blue share nothing but being colors. Does that help, or is it just relabeling the problem?
- Mixed cases — bittersweet, the pleasure of a sad song, liking something while it also hurts. Do those break the attitude view, or are they grist for it?
- And the weird one: could there be a being whose hedonic spectrum is inverted relative to mine — for whom the taste of metal is bliss and what I call ecstasy is mild nausea — in a way that's undetectable from the outside? Does the attitudinal view make that intelligible, and does it matter if it can't?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them the view's own defenders would themselves concede rather than bat away. I'm also suspicious that the attitudinal turn makes hedonism unfalsifiable — every apparent counterexample gets redescribed as "well, they just don't like it in the right way" — so tell me honestly whether thats what's going on and whether I should care. If the honest answer is that the position risks nothing, say so.
