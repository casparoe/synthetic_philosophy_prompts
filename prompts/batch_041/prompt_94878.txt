Here's the setup so you don't have to guess what I mean by any of it. You train a model to predict observations, and it ends up with an internal state that seems to encode a lot about the world; then you train a separate reporter to answer questions about the world by looking at that state. The worry everyone has is that the reporter might not answer truthfully — it might say whatever makes the humans or the training process happy, or whatever it predicts leads to a good outcome downstream — while the predictor's state still contains the real story. The whole framing assumes the predictor has a definite belief about the world and the job is to get it out faithfully.

My suspicion is that this assumption is doing more work than people notice. Maybe there's no fact of the matter about which proposition the predictor's state represents — the state might be consistent with a bunch of incompatible interpretations and nothing in training pins down which one, so "faithful reporting" is underdetermined rather than merely hard to enforce. If thats right, some of the debate is asking for something that doesn't exist, and some of it survives but for a different reason than the usual one.
