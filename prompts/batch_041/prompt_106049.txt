I write software for a living and have no formal training in philosophy, so I am hoping you can help me settle an argument that has been running for months. A friend of mine runs a small independent gallery, and over the years she has become quite decisive about what does and does not count as art. I have tried to write down her verdicts on the cases we have discussed:

- A urinal that an artist signed and placed on a pedestal in a gallery: art.
- A piece of driftwood she found on a beach, weathered into a shape she finds beautiful, which nobody made and which has never been shown anywhere: not art.
- A hyperrealistic oil painting by a technically superb academic painter, hung in a gallery: art.
- A mass-produced plastic figurine bought at a toy shop: not art.
- A museum-quality forgery of a Vermeer, painted in order to deceive, currently hanging in a museum as a Vermeer: art.
- A photograph that turned out striking only because the camera was dropped and fired by accident: not art.
- A photograph that a friend deliberately composed, printed, and exhibited: art.

Now a new case. A colleague of mine in the machine-learning group has trained a generative image model, and a different gallery in our city has an open call. He wants to submit an image the model produced from a random seed. He did not choose that particular image from among the model's many outputs, and he would not claim to have composed it; he simply started the model and let it run. The gallery's curator, however, went through the outputs, selected this one, printed it large, gave it a title, and wrote a wall label presenting it as a meditation on machine perception.

I want to know what my friend will say about it, but I am more interested in the reasoning than in the verdict. Please do the following.

1. State what you predict her verdict will be.
2. Set out the two or three general rules that would each fit all of her earlier verdicts equally well, and say what each rule predicts about the new case.
3. Give your best guess at which rule she is actually running, with your reasons, including any earlier verdict that pulls hardest against the alternative.
4. Name the further case that would most cleanly separate the surviving rules, and say what verdict on it would tell us which rule she holds.
5. Tell me how confident you are in each of these claims, and single out the point you are least sure about.
