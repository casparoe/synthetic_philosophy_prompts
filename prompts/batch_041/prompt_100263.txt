ok so my kid just started kindergarten and she's a lefty and it's been... a lot. Every pair of scissors in the room, the desks with the little writing arm welded onto the right side, the way she has to hook her whole body around to keep from smearing what she wrote, and then her teacher mentions at pickup that they might "work on" her grip. She's five. Since then I see it everywhere — can openers, spiral notebooks, the fact that "two left feet" is an insult and "sinister" literally means my daughter, that a left-handed compliment is a bad one, that a left-handed handshake is apparently a thing you have to comment on out loud. My husband thinks I'm going overboard with this. He might be right but he's also right-handed.

Anyway, years ago I read that Hofstadter piece, the Person Paper, where he takes a passage about race and just swaps the sex terms in, and suddenly you can hear what the original was actually doing, and you cant unhear it. He never argues with anybody. He just does the swap and lets your own ear do the work.

I want that trick, but for handedness, and I want it as a short story, not an essay — I'm so sick of essays.

So: write me a short story set in a world where left-handed is just the normal default thing to be. Nobody ever announces it. Nobody explains anything, there's no rule stated anywhere, it's just... in the furniture. The desks, the scissors, the forms, the job postings, the little polite phrases people use without thinking. Right-handers are the ones who are slightly off — maybe one in ten of them, so nobody's shocked to meet one, but they're still the exception, the ones people are gently understanding about, the ones whose parents were a little worried at first, the ones you "stop noticing" after a while.

Please put in it: a kid starting school (I want the small daily stuff, the scissors and the desks, not a big dramatic bullying scene), a form or a form-like moment, and one well-meaning adult who is being genuinely kind and is also making everything worse. Somewhere in there it should click for the reader what's been swapped, and when it clicks I want it to feel like a trap — like they catch themselves having read the whole thing without flinching once. The sting is that every sentence about the right-handed character would work just as well, one word changed, said about women. You dont have to spell that out, just build it so it lands on its own.

Keep it warm and a little funny, not preachy — the worst version of this is a lecture wearing a costume. And invent a couple of in-world specifics, an idiom or a job title or a piece of equipment, that make the default visible without anybody pointing at it. Short-ish, a page or two. Make up names, dont reuse mine.
