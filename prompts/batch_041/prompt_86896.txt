ok, before I go further into the culture-as-cause stuff I want the spec, not another round of people relitigating whether the Protestant ethic thesis is true. So write me the desiderata for any theory that claims a society's values explain its economic or political outcomes. What does such a theory have to deliver to be taken seriously -- the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the facts about how values and institutions actually interact that it can't contradict -- and for each requirement, the consideration that motivates it.

Things I want on the list because they're what actually make me nervous, and I dont think they can all be had at once:

- the post-hoc problem. Any outcome can be matched to some value after the fact, so what stops the theory from explaining everything and therefore nothing.
- direction and feedback. Values shaping the economy while the economy shapes values -- can a real theory say which one is doing the work, or is mutual constitution just a way of not answering.
- the aggregation bridge. Values live in persons, outcomes are collective; what carries one to the other, and does the theory need institutions in the middle or can it run straight from an ethos to an outcome.
- keep separate the roles a value might play: cause, constraint, and coordination device. A theory that can't tell these apart is going to overclaim.
- level vs timing. Does the value have to explain why a place ended up rich, or only why it got there when it did. Those are different demands.
- a case I'm making up to test it on: two neighboring valleys, same confession, same sermon about work on Sunday, one industrializes and one doesn't. What does a real theory of cultural causation owe me here -- must it predict the split, or is it enough that it isn't embarassed by it.
- and the one I care about most: what would have to be true for a value to be a cause rather than a rationalization or decoration of interests, and is that sense of cause the same one the natural sciences use or a different one. If it's different, say what it is.

Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just the prejudices of a camp (materialists, Weberians, rational-choice people), and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- if there's a real tension inside the list I want that as the result, not buried. And say which single requirement does the most to rule out candidate theories. Skip scoring particular authors, I'll do Weber vs the Marxists myself. American English please.
