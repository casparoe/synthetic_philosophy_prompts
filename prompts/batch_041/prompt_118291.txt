I help mod a Discord for people doing home energy retrofits -- heat pumps, batteries, insulation, that whole world -- about 3,000 members, started as a hobby server but now people are dropping ten or twenty grand based on what they read in there, which I feel weirdly responsible for. We keep having the same fight in different costumes: someone posts their installer's quote, someone else says the whole product category is a racket, the thread runs four days and three hundred messages, and at some point I have to decide whether to lock it, sticky something, or just leave it. Right now I make that call on vibe, and my vibe is basically do I think the loud majority in this thread are being dumb, which is not a procedure and I know it. I've been reading LessWrong-adjacent blogs for like a decade, so my instinct is to reach for double crux and steelmanning, but neither of those seems to work on somebody who already thinks I'm the one being manipulated.

Here's the frame I want to build on, and I'd rather you just take it as given than litigate whether I've got it right. I think there are two very different things that get called the same thing. One is a group that's simply missing information -- nobody ever told them to distrust anyone, they'd update if the right fact landed in front of them, the problem is coverage. The other is a group where the outside world has already been pre-emptively discredited, so outside evidence gets read as further proof of the story, and dumping more facts on them makes it worse instead of better, because the fix there isn't information, its trust. My worry is that my default move -- post good sources, write a FAQ, ping the one member who actually works in the industry -- is the right move for the first thing and useless-or-harmful for the second, and I can't tell which one I'm looking at until I've already made it worse. Two tells I've noticed, fold them in if they hold up: in the second kind, the group doesn't just think outsiders are wrong, it has a story about why they'd lie, and disagreeing out loud costs you standing in the group, whereas in the first kind you can be wrong and lose nothing.

What I want is a procedure I can actually run on a thread before I touch it, on my phone, in five minutes, as a hobbyist mod with zero epistemology training -- questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it says leave it alone, and where it says this is a values question about who we want in the server and goes to the other two mods and the owner instead of to me. Be honest about where it misfires and about which steps are judgment calls wearing a lab coat. Then run it on two threads: (a) our long-running heat-pumps-don't-work-below-freezing crowd, who I'm pretty sure are just wrong and honestly I want permission to go correct them, and (b) our most respected regular's thread arguing the utility rebate program is designed to fail, where everyone agrees and anyone who pushes back gets called naive -- I've always thought of that one as our best content, so if the procedure says something ugly about it, say it. I don't need citations or further reading, just the procedure. Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, no intro and no wrap-up, each item a couple of sentences; if a step needs an example from one of the two threads, put the example inside the step.
