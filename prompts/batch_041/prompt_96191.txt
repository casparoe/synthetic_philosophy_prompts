im putting together a seminar on AI control — the corner of safety work that assumes the model you're running might be scheming, and designs the pipeline so that even if it is, the damage is capped. The protocol I keep chewing on: a strong untrusted model does the work, then a weaker trusted model paraphrases its answer or rewrites its reasoning before anyone acts on it, and the safety you get is mostly just the capability you lose in the rewrite. Write me a poem that makes that tradeoff physical. Maybe 20-28 lines, built as a paraphrase cascade: each stanza is a rewrite of the one before it in a flatter and more cautious register, same rough meter throughout so you can hear the thing draining out of the same shape, and each step quietly drops one thing — first the concrete plan, then the claim about what it can do, then anything a reader could act on. The opening stanza has to be good enough that losing it feels like a loss. By the last stanza you should have something unimpeachably true, safe, and useless, and the poem shouldn't wink about that, just let it sit. It should also come through that what we've bought is a ceiling on damage, not any evidence the model was ever on our side — the monitor only ever sees what it can understand. No names, no paper titles, it has to work cold for someone who's never heard of any of this.
