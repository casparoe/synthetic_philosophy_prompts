I am a master's student in technology policy, and for the past several weeks three of us have been meeting to argue about the future of artificial intelligence: a friend who works in AI safety and thinks the field should slow down and coordinate, a friend who is a committed decentralization advocate and thinks concentrated power is the disease rather than the cure, and me. In a seminar last term I half-remembered an idea that I believe bears directly on our dispute, but I do not trust my memory of it and would rather have you lay it out properly. The idea, as I recall it, is the singleton: a world order in which there is a single decision-making agency at the highest level — not necessarily a single government or a single agent, but a single locus of ultimate decision-making, which, once established, would tend to persist because it would control the levers that could displace it. The intriguing part is supposed to be that a singleton need not be a tyranny; it could be a benign global order, a single AI system, or something we have no name for, and that whether one arises, and which one arises, may be the most consequential fact about the coming century.

Please describe the most important implications of this idea for how I should think about AI governance over the next twenty years or so. I am especially interested in the following: whether the idea genuinely changes the centralization-versus-decentralization debate or merely renames it; whether a singleton is more likely to arrive by a single project going first or by slow accretion and coordination; what the idea implies about the desirability of racing versus coordinating; and whether the singleton hypothesis makes any distinctive prediction — for instance about how long a unipolar technological lead tends to last, or about why we do not observe such orders elsewhere in the universe — or whether it collapses into the ordinary claim that whoever gets there first tends to stay there. Where the idea is doing real work, tell me what it rules in and what it rules out; where it is doing none, say so plainly. I would rather have a short list of implications that actually follow than a long one that merely sounds portentous.
