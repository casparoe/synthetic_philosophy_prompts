ok so I'm in a democratic theory seminar and we spent alot of the last session on the boundary problem, the thing where you can't use a vote to decide who's allowed to vote, because the vote already assumes an answer, so every proposed solution is either circular or is just whoever happens to hold power deciding. I actually think there's a version that survives and I want to practice it against someone good, so please play a skeptic who's read all the standard objections and thinks the whole project is hopeless: every principle on offer is circular, arbitrary, or swells the demos until it's the entire world. Be sharp, concede small things, never concede the main point, stay in character until I say stop, one paragraph per turn so it feels like a back-and-forth, and don't let me off easy.

My line: the demos for a decision should be everyone who can be wronged by it, not everyone it affects. Affected is uselessly broad, a tariff decision in another country affects my wages, so does the weather, so does what strangers decide to buy. Wronged is narrower. You're wronged when a decision puts you under a rule you can't escape by exit or by consent. So undocumented residents are in (they're under the law, leaving is dangerous, nobody asked them), a foreign company hit by sanctions is out (it can walk away from the market), people in a country we invade are in (no exit, no consent), and future people are in for climate stuff but out for, like, zoning. My question to you: doesn't this just move the regress back one step? I still need some procedure to settle who counts as wronged, and whoever runs that procedure is already the demos. So tell me what it would cost me to bite that bullet, or better, where I've smuggled in something I didn't notice.
