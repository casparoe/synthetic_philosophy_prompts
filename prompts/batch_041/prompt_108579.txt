I'm midway through a graduate seminar on reference and modality, and I've grown tired of assessing two-dimensional proposals one at a time; what I keep wanting is a checklist that any of them would have to pass before I'd take it seriously. So before I read another one, write me the desiderata for a two-dimensional framework in semantics — the full specification of what such a framework has to deliver to count as adequate. Give me the cases it must get right, each paired with the case behind it: the necessary a posteriori, where a true identity is something we could only come to know empirically; the contingent a priori, where a truth is knowable without empirical investigation yet is not necessary; and the asymmetry by which some true identities are knowable a priori and others are not, even though both terms pick out the same thing. Include the demand that the framework not simply collapse the epistemic notion of what could have turned out to be the case into the metaphysical notion of what is necessary. Then the structural requirements: compositionality; a clear account of how the two dimensions map onto the familiar split between the context in which a sentence is uttered and the circumstance against which it is evaluated; and a treatment of indexicals and of "actually" that doesn't quietly gerrymander the machinery to fit the examples. I especially want the requirements that come from embeddings — sentences in which one of these expressions sits inside an epistemic modal or another operator, and where the intuitive truth-value flips depending on how you read it — because my hunch is that this is where frameworks get caught.

Then do the two things I most care about. First, flag any pair or small subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say precisely what forces the choice between them; I already half-suspect a conflict between wanting the first dimension to be an account of apriority and wanting it to be an account of linguistic meaning, and I'd like that suspicion either confirmed with the mechanism spelled out or dismantled. Second, tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise-popular proposals, and why. A short scorecard of the main families — an epistemic version that ties the first dimension to apriority, a context-dependent version that ties it to the speaker's context, and a metalinguistic version — is welcome, but keep it brief; the criteria are the deliverable, not the grading. Don't bother with citations or a reading list; I want the reasoning, not the bibliography.

Close with a one-sentence summary of the most important tension you identified, and then, on the final line by itself, write \answer{...} containing just a short phrase naming the requirement you judged most exclusionary.
