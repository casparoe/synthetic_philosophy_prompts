Please take the role of a philosopher of mathematics with a solid technical background in set theory — comfortable with forcing, inner models, the iterative conception of the cumulative hierarchy, large cardinal axioms, and the current debate between universism and the set-theoretic multiverse. Work through the pairwise implications rather than offering general impressions, and be explicit about which mathematical facts you are relying on at each step; it may help to begin by laying out the combinations you think are worth examining before you commit to any verdict.

Here is the package. (2) What fixes that intended interpretation is our grasp of the iterative conception: a set is what you get at a stage by collecting together all the subsets of what is already available, and that picture is what our set-theoretic vocabulary means. (3) The large cardinal axioms — measurables and everything above them — are true, and the way they are justified is the model for how new axioms in set theory should be justified: they are intrinsically plausible, they cohere with one another, and they articulate structure we can see is already there rather than positing entities merely to secure theorems. (4) V = L is false: the constructible hierarchy is an artifact of an artificially narrow notion of definability, the real universe is wider, and I refuse to be pushed toward CH just because it happens to hold in thin inner models. (5) The independence of CH from ZFC is a striking fact; that we can build models of ZFC in which CH holds and others in which it fails says something important about how little the axioms pin down. (6) If CH is ever settled, it will be settled the way the large cardinal hierarchy was, by axioms that are intrinsically justified rather than adopted for their attractive consequences.

Please tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each genuine tension is. For each real tension, say precisely what has to give, what the cheapest repair would be, and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the package; then tell me which single commitment is doing the most damage — which one, if I gave it up, would leave the rest in the best shape. I would rather have the tensions ranked by severity than presented as an undifferentiated list, and I would like the mathematical facts you invoke (especially about what the large cardinal hierarchy does and does not decide) spelled out rather than alluded to.
