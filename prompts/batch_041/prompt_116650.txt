Okay, I've been going around in circles on this and I want to nail down what I actually think, because my gut keeps disagreeing with itself.

Background: I got interested in whether things had to turn out the way they did. A mate of mine says history is just one damn thing after another and none of it was ever inevitable; I think some of it was. We can't agree on which parts.

So here's a run of cases with my honest verdicts.

- My town made two streets one-way in the early 70s, off the back of one bad crash and a survey nobody read. Fifty years later the loading bays, the bus stops, the school gate, the whole downtown is arranged around that flow. Verdict: locked in. Even if everyone agreed tomorrow that the old two-way was better, we couldn't get back to it.

- My grandad missed a train in 1958, got the next one, sat next to a bloke who offered him a job in another city, and the whole family's life runs off that. Verdict: huge cause, but not a lock-in. If he'd caught the train he'd have gotten some other job in some other city, and it would have been roughly the same kind of life. Nothing is holding that specific job in place.

- My brother met his wife because of a seating chart at a work dinner. Verdict: the marriage wasn't locked in, because no seating chart and they find some other route. But his two kids, those exact two kids, I feel like they were locked in. There's no version of that seating chart going differently that gets you those kids.

- My team picked a file naming convention at 4pm on a Friday in 2013, basically by coin flip. There's twelve years of archive sitting on it now and we will never change it. Verdict: locked in, and stupidly so, because the thing that won was nothing.

- My region pronounces a vowel differently from the next region over, apparently because of where a bishop's boundary ran in the 1600s. Verdict: not locked in. Some boundary would have been there either way; the fact that its this one is just what happened. If the whole area got resettled it would wash out.

- If you rewound and re-ran the last century I think we'd still end up with some standard keyboard layout. Not this one. Verdict: the type was inevitable, the token wasn't.

My rule of thumb so far has been: something's locked in if the reason it won was small and silly and the cost of switching away from it is now big. That gets the town and the filing system. It also says grandad's job was locked in, which I dont believe, and it says the kids were locked in, which I do believe, so it can't tell those two apart. So either the rule is wrong or I'm wrong about one of them.

What I want: whatever principle or small set of principles actually fits my verdicts, stated properly, with a note on which case forces each part of it. Then run it on two or three cases I haven't mentioned, pick your own, but make them ordinary rather than textbook ones. And tell me straight which of my verdicts the principle can't save, and whether I should drop the verdict or the principle.

Also, I keep suspecting there are two different things I'm calling locked in, one where something is actively being held in place and one where something just happened and can't be repeated, and that a lot of my confusion is about that. If that's right, say so and tell me which cases belong to which. And if that's the whole answer, then say thats the whole answer.

Finally, say what would change your mind. Which argument, or which kind of case, would flip you to the other principle, and where is your reasoning most likely to be wrong?
