I'm putting together a proposal for a small internal grant — about four months of part-time work — on a gap I keep noticing in how people talk about Moloch / multipolar traps. Everyone lumps arms races, tragedy-of-the-commons, Malthusian races and plain market competition under one heading and then reaches for the same remedies: a binding rule, a change in payoffs, exit. My hunch is there are at least two different structures hiding in there. Call one a coordination trap: a bad but stable equilibrium where the players would all prefer the cooperative outcome and could get there with a rule they can enforce. Call the other a selection trap: the bad outcome comes from competition eliminating anyone who deviates, so no one's preference is being thwarted, and no enforceable agreement among the current players would stick, because the selection pressure just reintroduces the behaviour via new entrants or new margins. I want to know whether the selection trap is a real, distinct category with its own normative profile and its own remedies, or whether it quietly collapses into the coordination case once you describe it carefully — and if it's distinct, what follows for when we should be fatalistic about a race to the bottom versus when we should be looking for a fix.
