I have been trying to work out something about the way people decide one after another, and I would like a serious explanation of it rather than a recap of the usual examples.

Here is the situation that puzzles me. Picture a long line of people, each of whom privately and independently runs a small, imperfect test before making a yes-or-no choice — whether to adopt a new method, say. Each person can see how everyone before them chose, but not the private test behind anyone else's choice. As I understand it, after only the first few people, everyone further down the line simply copies whatever choice is currently ahead of them, and their own private tests stop making any difference to what they do. What I cannot get past is this: these people are still rational, they still run their own tests, and they still take the evidence seriously. So why does the information carried by their choices drain away to nothing? It looks as if everyone reasoning correctly is precisely what destroys the information, and that seems backwards.

Two further things seem to follow, and I suspect I am misreading them. First, why would such a line be so brittle — why could a single public announcement, even a weak one, be enough to turn the whole line around, and why does the weight of the private tests that got it started offer no protection? Second, this seems to clash with the familiar idea that many independent judgments together give a better answer than any one of them would alone. If everyone is rational and the line keeps growing, why does the answer stop improving? Is the whole difference that the judgments quietly stop being independent, and if so, where exactly does the independence go?

Finally, I keep sensing a missing piece. No one in the line seems to count the fact that their own choice will be useful information to the people behind them. Does that omission actually change the outcome, and can it be described as each person failing to invest in something — a benefit that spills over to others but that they have no reason to pay for — without pretending they are behaving irrationally?

Please explain this in plain language, but do not soften it. I want the mechanism that makes the information disappear, why that mechanism is brittle, how it squares with the wisdom-of-crowds idea, and what the missing piece really is. I would rather have the ideas themselves than any references or reading suggestions. Please answer in American English.
