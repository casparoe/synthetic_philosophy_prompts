I'm in a reading group that spans formal epistemology and machine learning, and we keep going in circles because everyone proposes a fix for the problem of induction before we have agreed on what a fix would have to be. So before we read anything else, I want the specification. What would a theory of inductive inference — a theory that says which inferences from observed data to unobserved cases are licensed, and on what basis — actually have to deliver to count as adequate? Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. Some I expect to see: it has to engage the Humean circularity rather than restate it; it has to handle the grue-style cases, where two hypotheses agree on everything observed and diverge afterwards, without simply ruling the gerrymandered predicate out by fiat; it has to give an account of simplicity, or of prior probability, that is not a relabeling of the conclusion it is meant to support; and it has to come to terms with the no-free-lunch results — averaged over all possible target functions, no learner beats any other, so any inductive bias the theory endorses has to be defended as a bias rather than derived from the data alone. I also want the requirements people usually leave implicit: whether the theory must be computable; whether it must justify itself without already presupposing induction; whether it must survive the fact that the no-free-lunch results are proved under a particular assumption about the space of problems, and what becomes of the argument when that assumption is relaxed; whether it must distinguish prediction from accommodation of the same data; and whether a purely descriptive account of how we in fact generalize could satisfy anyone, or whether the demand is irreducibly normative.

Then, with the list in hand, tell me which of these are genuine desiderata and which are parochial demands of one camp — the Bayesian, the algorithmic-complexity camp, the learning-theory camp, the pragmatist who says no justification is needed at all — and flag any pair or subset of the requirements that looks jointly unsatisfiable; if some subset is, I take that to be the most interesting result on offer and I want it spelled out. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the contenders; a short scorecard at the end is welcome, but the criteria are what I am after. Say how confident you are in each part of the list and single out the one requirement you are least sure belongs on it. No reading list or citations needed. End with a final line of the form \answer{the single requirement that does the most to exclude existing theories, in a short phrase}.
