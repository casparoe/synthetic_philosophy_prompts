I write about ideas for a magazine — long features, the kind where you interview twelve people and then realise nobody is answering the same question. I'm doing one on meritocracy and desert, and I want to run a decision tree alongside the piece so readers can find their own spot in the argument instead of just picking a team. Can you build it for me?

Start from something a reader can answer without knowing any jargon — say two people both end up as successful surgeons, one because she was born with the hands for it and the other because she ground away for a decade — and branch on the questions that actually seperate the positions. The splits I care about: is there any such thing as deserving something before a set of social rules exists to define what's being deserved, or does desert only come into being inside institutions? Does it matter whether the trait that got you the goodies came from effort or from luck you had nothing to do with — and is that effort/luck cut even the right way to slice it, or does it quietly smuggle in a picture of the self nobody can really have? Who decides what counts as merit in the first place — is the standard fixed by the nature of the job, or by whoever is doing the hiring and has the power to define it? And does anyone actually need the claim that the winners deserve their winnings, or can the whole system be defended as a decent sorting device that makes no claim about desert at all? One more, because it's the one my interviewees keep sliding past: is the complaint that the wrong people are winning, or that winning is the only route to being respected?

Each branch should be a question in plain English with its answer options, and each leaf should carry the name of the position — a position, not a person — plus one line on its most awkward commitment, like the one where you have to tell a hardworking person their effort earns them nothing. I want the luck-egalitarian view, the desert-only-inside-institutions view, the market-rewards-aren't-deserved view, the no-desert-but-still-meritocratic view, the domination/relational critique and the post-meritocracy line that the problem is the prestige not the pay, all findable in there somewhere. If a leaf has no defenders, say so and say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive or just unloved, and note any answer-combinations nobody occupies. Also tell me which positions your tree can't pull apart — don't fudge that bit.

No citations, no reading list, I'll chase down names myself. Aim for ten to twelve branch points, readable by a smart general audience, and keep it tight, its for a sidebar so it has to fit one page.
