I am an economist who works on matching markets and mechanism design, and I know the standard apparatus reasonably well: deferred acceptance, top trading cycles, priorities, capacity constraints, lotteries, strategy-proofness, stability, and the distinction between assignment mechanisms and price-based allocation. A philosopher friend and I have been arguing about school choice, private schools, and homeschooling, and I want the whole debate restated in the vocabulary I actually use. Treat public-school assignment as a matching problem with priorities and capacities; private schools as competing providers with tuition, admissions standards, and possibly peer effects; vouchers as subsidies that shift families’ budget sets; homeschooling as an outside option or an exit from the system; parental preferences as heterogeneous types; and the social benefits of schooling as externalities. Do not merely translate the slogans. Restate the strongest case for school choice and the strongest case against it in these terms, and be explicit about which parts of each case are efficiency claims, which are distributional claims, and which are claims my vocabulary cannot represent at all.

Then give me the residue report. Which arguments survive the translation intact? Which have to be distorted to fit—for example, if equality of opportunity becomes a constraint on the assignment, or if the child’s interest has to be modeled as a separate agent from the parent? And which parts of the debate have no counterpart in mechanism design: the thought that education is a constitutive good rather than a bundle of services, the value of a shared public school as a site of civic formation, the non-contractual character of the parent-child relationship, or the objection that treating schooling as a market corrupts it? For each residue item, tell me whether it marks a genuine limitation of the mechanism-design framework or whether it is a sign that the original claim was doing rhetorical work that cannot survive precision. I want an honest translation, not a defense of markets or of public provision.
