An argument that a colleague has been pressing on me seems to prove more than it should, and I cannot locate the fault. Here it is, in his steps.

(1) Presentism: only present things exist.
(2) Every true proposition has a truthmaker — something that exists and makes it true.
(3) So whatever makes a proposition true must exist now.
(4) The only thing that could make 'there will be a frost tomorrow' true is a frost tomorrow, which does not exist yet.
(5) So nothing makes that proposition true; by (2), it is not true.
(6) Hence no proposition about the future is true, and the future is open — the result presentists usually want.
(7) By the same reasoning, the only thing that could make 'there was a frost yesterday' true is yesterday's frost, which no longer exists.
(8) So no proposition about the past is true either, and the past is as open as the future.
(9) The past is settled. Therefore presentism is false.

Steps (4) and (7) look exactly parallel to me, and yet (7) and (8) prove far too much. I would rather keep (1) and (9) than (2). Tell me which step to reject, why it looked acceptable, and what rejecting it costs — in particular, whether a presentist can keep an open future without conceding the same about the past. Lead with the step you reject, and keep the whole reply under 400 words.
