im putting together the closer for a grad seminar on virtue ethics and the situationist critique and i want to end the term with a sestina instead of yet another recap of Milgram or the Good Samaritan study. the thing i actually care about is the move people make after the situationists win the first round. they concede the global traits dont survive the experiments, but then they say character is still real as a bundle of local traits -- honesty-at-work, honesty-with-friends, and so on -- or even as a whole dossier of situation-specific dispositions that never needs a single disposition sitting underneath it. and then the obvious objection comes back: if the trait is indexed to the situation, the situation is doing all the work and the person contributes nothing.

so write me the sestina. six end-words, and all six have to be trivial situational features of the kind the experiments turn on -- a hurry, a small found object, a stranger in a corridor, an ambient noise, whatever you pick, but things that in themselves mean nothing morally. i want the rotation to be the argument: each stanza rearranges the same six, and by the last stanza it should be genuinely hard to say whether what were describing is a person or a permutation. then the envoi should cram all six into three lines and leave me unable to decide whether thats a vindication (look, there is a unity after all) or a reductio (thats just all six again in a smaller box). dont resolve it either way. dont let it slide into we-are-all-just-weather nihilism, and definately dont do the cheap thing where the last line reveals the person was a monster the whole time. someone in the seminar who knows this literature should be able to point at each stanza and say which move its enacting, and someone who doesnt should still think its a good poem.
