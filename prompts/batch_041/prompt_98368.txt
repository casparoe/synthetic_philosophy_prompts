Our department is running a working group this term on the prisoner's dilemma and the evolution of cooperation. Before we take up any of the competing models, I want a clean statement of what an adequate theory in this area would have to deliver. Please write the desiderata: the phenomena such a theory must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the formal or empirical results it must remain consistent with — each with the consideration that makes it a genuine requirement rather than a preference of one camp. Say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are optional, identify which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidate theories, and tell me whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable; that last point is what I most want to see. A table is acceptable, but keep the weight on the requirements and the reasons for them rather than on scoring particular theories.

The group covers both the one-shot and the indefinitely repeated two-person dilemma, together with the biological literature on how cooperation can evolve, and I do not want a theory to help itself to repetition, reputation, kinship, or group structure unless it can say why the help is legitimate. The phenomena I mean to hold it to include: the dominance of defection in the one-shot game alongside the cooperation actually observed there; the profusion of equilibria in the indefinitely repeated game, and the separate question of which one selection or learning is supposed to pick out; the unraveling of cooperation as a known horizon draws near; the fragility of reciprocal strategies to noise and error; the difference between mutually beneficial cooperation and genuine altruism; and the fact that humans cooperate at a scale that neither kinship nor pairwise reciprocity appears to reach.

Two further items I want the desiderata to adjudicate. First, a student proposes that the one-shot dilemma is really a coordination problem in disguise, since real players choose conditionally on their beliefs about what the other will do: say whether the desiderata treat this as a reframing or as a change of subject. Second, a colleague claims that the puzzle of cooperation in the one-shot case simply dissolves once one notices that the case is an artifact, because real interactions leave traces: say whether that dissolves the puzzle or dodges it. Finally, if a student wants to add that punishment stabilizes cooperation, say what the desiderata require of that proposal, and in particular whether it only relocates the difficulty.
