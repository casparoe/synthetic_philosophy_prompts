I keep getting into the same fight with a friend in our ML safety reading group and I want to stop going in circles. The question is whether you can define "low impact" or "avoiding side effects" for an AI system without first having a full specification of what the system is supposed to value. His camp says yes: you can measure how much a policy changes the world relative to some baseline -- doing nothing, or the policy the agent would've followed anyway, or the range of goals it could still pursue afterwards -- and then "don't mess things up" is this thin, value-light constraint you can bolt onto any objective. My camp says no: every one of those measures needs you to pick a baseline and pick which goals count as worth preserving, and choosing those is already smuggling in a value judgment, so the constraint was never value-free, it just hid the values somewhere. He thinks that's cheap -- of course you need some notion of goals, but that's not the same as a full value specification -- and I think that's exactly the move that makes it unfalsifiable.

Weve been at this for months and honestly i cant tell anymore whether we're disagreeing about anything real. My working theory is that it's partly verbal -- we mean different things by "value specification," he means a full utility function and I mean any ranking at all -- and partly a genuine disagreement about whether the baseline is a fact you discover or a choice you make. But I might be wrong. Maybe we're just answering different questions under one name, me a normative one about what should count as a side effect and him an engineering one about what you can actually compute, and the whole thing only looks like a clash because we never separated them.

Heres the case that broke my brain and made me want a second opinion. Take two systems: one that spills ink across a painting it was never asked to touch, and one that finishes a painting that was already half-done and was going to be abandoned anyway. Intuitively the first one has "impact" and the second one maybe doesn't. But if "leave it alone" is your baseline then the second one changed the world too, and if the half-finished painting was headed for the trash then the baseline itself is doing a ton of work. So I can't even tell which of us the example is supposed to support.

Diagnose this for me, don't just pick a side. Is it a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about the word "value," or two groups asking different questions under one heading? If it's substantive, is the crux a hidden empirical or formal claim that someone could settle, or is it bedrock about whether "impact" can be defined relative to anything value-free at all? And whatever your diagnosis, tell me what would actually count as progress here -- a formal result, a distinction, a change of question -- or whether this one is just doomed to run forever and I should stop.
