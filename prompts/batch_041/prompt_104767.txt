I've been going back and forth with a friend in a metaphysics reading group about Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and I've ended up defending a fairly strong line: the regress is not vicious, and the right response is primitivism about propositional unity. Roughly, the thought is that a and b and the relation R don't need some further tie to be united into the proposition that aRb; R's holding of a and b is not itself a relation that needs to be related to R, a, and b. The unity is just primitive, or it's a non-relational feature of the whole. I know this is a familiar escape route, but I want to know what would actually count against it. Not just the usual objection that it's mysterious or that it stops the regress by fiat -- I already expect that. I mean: what would have to be true for the primitivist line to be false? Which arguments, intuitions, formal results, or consequences would be genuine defeaters? And which of them would a primitivist themselves have to concede count against the view, as opposed to ones only critics would press?

Can you map the exposure of the position, ranked from fatal to merely awkward? I'm especially interested in whether the regress should be read as a regress of being or a regress of analysis, and whether that distinction changes what counts as a defeater; whether there's a principled difference between an infinite chain of necessary conditions and a vicious infinite regress; and whether primitivism can handle false propositions, complex predicates, and the difference between a proposition and a mere list of its constituents without quietly turning into a different theory. Also tell me what it predicts that rival accounts dont -- say, trope-theoretic or truthmaker-style accounts that treat unity as derivative rather than primitive. If the view turns out to be so qualified that nothing could count against it, say so plainly and tell me whether thats a real problem for this kind of view or just the price of doing metaphysics. No citations or reading lists needed; I want the argumentative map, not a bibliography.
