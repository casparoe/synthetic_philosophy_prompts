I'm a philosphy prof putting together a dialogue exercise on simultaneous discovery. Write a dialogue between two philosophers of science about it. One is an inevitabilist: she thinks Newton/Leibniz and Darwin/Wallace show that once a problem is ripe, someone is bound to solve it, and the individual is mostly a vessel. The other is a contingency theorist: he thinks that's survivor bias -- we only notice the collisions, not the problems that sat around for centuries, and credit is assigned by social luck. Have them go back and forth on whether the counterfactual where Wallace publishes first changes anything, on the so-called shadow priority problem (if Darwin had been hit by a bus in 1858, would the discovery still have happened on schedule?), and on whether ripeness is a cause or just a label for our ignorance. Neither should be a strawman; let each concede a point. At the end, add a short note saying how confident you are in the views expressed and which claim you're least sure of.
