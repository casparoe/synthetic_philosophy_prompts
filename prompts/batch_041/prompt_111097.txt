Here's the claim I want a credence on:

(C) For a particular mid-sized trained transformer and a particular human-interpretable feature F — take something like "this token sits inside a string literal" or "this sentence is about weddings" — there is a determinate fact of the matter about whether that network internally represents F, and that fact doesn't depend on which interpretability method we happen to run or on our choice of what to count as a feature. So representational realism is true for at least some such cases; it isn't just a useful fiction we project onto the weights.

Give me a number and a decomposition, with a probability on each branch and one line of justification per branch. Here's how I think it splits; if you think I've got the joints in the wrong place, move them, but keep it numeric:

1. Which notion of representation is the right one. If it's functional/causal-role — some internal state carries info that downstream computation actually consumes — realism is cheap. If it's teleosemantic, requiring something like a proper function fixed by selection history, then a net trained by gradient descent might not have representations at all, no matter what the probes say. P(that the correct theory is the permissive functional one)?

2. What my friend calls the decode/use gap. You can train a linear probe that decodes F from the residual stream at 95% and still have zero evidence the network uses F for anything. P(a passing probe is enough to say the net represents F)? And separately, P(ablation or steering results, when they behave the way we want, close that gap)?

3. The basis problem, which is the one that actually keeps me up. Suppose features are directions in activation space. A direction is only defined relative to a basis, and I can rotate the basis and get equally good linear structure — the sparse directions aren't canonical. So what fixes which directions are the real features, other than dictionary-learning methods that pick out the sparse ones? P(that's a principled answer rather than a convention we're smuggling in)?

4. P(the question is ill-posed — that represents F is more like saying a gene is for tallness, a useful abstraction whose truth is relative to our interests, so (C) has no determinate truth value at all)?
