I'm in a decision theory reading group and I got stuck with kicking off our session on the toxin puzzle next week, so I've been trying to write down my own position on it before I walk in and find out in front of everyone that it dosn't hang together. Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) I one-box in Newcomb's problem, and I'd defend it on evidential grounds — the way I am right now is a decent sign of what the predictor did, so I should pick the option that's the best news about the world rather than the one that makes the world different. (2) On the toxin puzzle — billionaire, a million dollars, one very bad day, and the money is for sincerely intending tonight to drink the stuff at noon tomorrow, not for actually drinking it — I take the money, intend to drink, and then don't drink. I'm pretty confident that's the right play. (3) I don't believe in any seperate requirements of intention, or in diachronic rationality more generally: the only thing that's ever rational or irrational is a choice made at a time on the information available then, and once the million is in my account, drinking the toxin is a pure cost with nothing on the other side of the ledger. (4) You can't intend to do something you're certain you won't do. An intention you already know you're going to ditch isn't an intention, it's a wish, and since the billionaire can read my psychology better than I can, if I know I won't drink then I can't really intend to, and then there's no million. (5) Intentions aren't free-floating: if you intend to do A and nothing relevant changes, you should do A, and someone who just drops an intention without changing their mind is criticizable for it. (6) I think the real action in both puzzles is at the level of the agent rather than the act — what you want is to be the kind of creature whose states line up with good outcomes, and I'm happy to think of the toxin trick as something I'd want to be the sort of person who could pull off.

The bit that nags me is whether (3) and (2) can sit together, since if only choices-at-a-time are assessable then 'forming an intention' might not even be the kind of thing that can be rational or irrational in the first place, and I don't know what to do with that. Someone in the group also said (1) and (2) are just the same reasoning twice, which I basically agree with, so I assume that's not where the problem is.
