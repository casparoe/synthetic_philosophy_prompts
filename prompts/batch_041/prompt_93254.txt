I host a small reading group that spun out of a local rationalist meetup, and we have just wrapped a unit on the thermodynamics of computation: Szilard's one-molecule engine, Landauer's principle and its kT ln 2 price on erasure, Bennett's resolution of Maxwell's demon by moving the entropy cost from measurement to memory reset, and the live argument over whether that price is a genuine physical law or a bookkeeping convention that smuggles in an assumption about how memory must work. For the group's end-of-unit zine I promised something lighter than my usual notes, and I have settled on a parody: a deadpan corporate job advertisement from a fictional startup, Second Thought Thermodynamics, Inc., which sells "entropy reduction as a service" and genuinely believes it has commercialized Maxwell's demon. Write that job posting.

The role being advertised is a Sorting Operative (Night Shift) for the company's flagship product, a chamber that separates fast molecules from slow ones and reports a net drop in entropy against a contractual SLA. I want the piece formatted like a real posting, with the usual headings -- About Us, The Role, What You'll Do, What We're Looking For, Compensation and Benefits, and a closing Legal/Equal-Opportunity fine print -- and running to roughly 800 to 900 words. The comedy has to come entirely from getting the physics right, so that a thermodynamicist laughs rather than winces: the posting should distinguish measuring a molecule from erasing the record of having measured it, should let slip that the demon's memory must eventually be reset and that the reset is where the entropy actually goes, should gesture at the single-molecule Szilard case, at reversible computing as the reason the sorting itself need not be dissipative, and at the ratchet-and-pawl temptation, and should treat "information is physical" as the kind of slogan a marketing department would over-extend. Crucially, the advertisement must never announce that the demon is impossible; the joke is that the company half-understands its own product, so the impossibility should surface only through the fine print, the performance targets, and the benefits -- a "negative-entropy bonus," a "zero-entropy reservoir access" perk, a probation clause about "reversible conduct," an EEO statement that is scrupulously fair and quietly damning. Keep it deadpan throughout, with no winking at the reader; by the end, someone who came in thinking the demon is a loophole in the second law should be able to say exactly where the loophole closes and why the books balance. Assume the audience knows the standard pop-science framing of the demon and nothing more technical than that, so avoid citations, dates, and any appeal to a specific paper.
