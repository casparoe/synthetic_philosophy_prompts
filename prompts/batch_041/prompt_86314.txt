I serve on the standing commission that advises our legislature on proposals to create or license markets in contested goods. Our opinions are advisory, but the legislature follows them closely enough that each new one has to be defensible as continuous with the record we have already made, so a vote here is really an argument about which of our past grounds we are prepared to own.

Our record, compressed:

- We advised against permitting payment to living kidney donors at any level, even with lifetime follow-up care guaranteed. The stated grounds were that offers of that size predictably recruit people under financial pressure, that the operation is not riskless, and that a body part is not a thing to be priced.
- We advised against allowing utilities to charge what the market would bear for water, ice, and generators during a declared emergency, and recommended rationing at the regulated price with state-funded imports instead. The stated grounds were that buyers at such a moment cannot decline, and that the arrangement replaces a standing norm of mutual aid with a price.
- We declined to object to resale of concert tickets above face value. The exchange is voluntary on both sides, and the complaint that some people will be priced out is a complaint about who holds money, not a defect in the exchange.
- We approved commercial plasma collection with donors paid per session. The procedure is repeatable and low-risk, payment increased supply, and we recorded that nothing about a plasma donation is debased by a price.
- We advised against a party's scheme to pay cash for marked ballots in low-income precincts, and recorded that we were not moved by the argument that no single ballot would be pivotal; the payments were aimed at people for whom refusal would have been costly. Two members dissented on the ground that the sellers were better off and no ballot was pivotal, so the objection, if there was one, had to be about what a vote is rather than about who was hurt.
- We advised against a private clinic's proposal to sell faster access to elective surgery performed by the same public surgeons: buying a place ahead of others does not create a place, it moves the wait onto someone else, and access would track income.
- We approved paying participants in a low-risk residential study, on the ground that the sum was not large enough to distort judgment.

The new application: an advertising firm seeks a licence to run what it calls personal name sponsorship. An adult could contract, for a five-year term, to take a sponsor's name as their legal surname — appearing under it in every register, credit file, and conference badge — in return for an annual payment, with no other obligations of any kind: no appearances, no conduct clauses, no exclusivity. Participation would be voluntary, publicly registered, open regardless of income, and terminable early on repayment of part of the sums received; the applicant also proposes to exclude anyone whose minor children share the surname. Nothing about the scheme deceives anyone: the registry is public, and the sponsor's name is the sponsor's own.

I want your recommendation on how to vote. Set out the candidate rules our record could be read as applying, and what each predicts here. Give me a verdict rather than a survey, with the reasons that carry it, and tell me which of our earlier opinions is the closest analog. If our record genuinely leaves this open, say precisely which principle we would be adopting by each way of voting. And if two of the candidate rules come apart on this application, name the case you would put to the commission first — the one that would separate them — because that is what I intend to do before the vote.
