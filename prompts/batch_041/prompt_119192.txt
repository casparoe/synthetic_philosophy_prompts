I am designing a self-contained exercise for a graduate seminar on strategic irrationality. In the seminar we run a classroom dollar auction with real money, and the escalation pattern is reliable enough that students begin asking for ex ante protections. Consider a bidder who enters a standard dollar auction—an auction for a dollar bill in which the highest bidder pays their bid and receives the dollar, while the second-highest bidder also pays their bid but receives nothing—and who knows that they are prone to the usual escalation. I have described below five candidate safeguards. Each safeguard is specified only by its mechanics, not by any named theory, so please evaluate them on the merits of the description rather than by matching them to a familiar label. (1) Hard budget seal: before entering, the bidder deposits a fixed maximum into an escrow account controlled by a third party; bids can draw only on that account, and when it is exhausted the bidder is locked out. (2) Restart clause: every bid above a threshold triggers a mandatory twenty-four-hour pause, after which the auction restarts from zero, all prior bids are void, and no money is owed. (3) Incremental-value ledger: the bidder must write down all prior bids as unrecoverable and, before each new bid, state the incremental expected value of continuing from the current state alone; bidding is permitted only if that incremental value is positive. (4) Monitor bond: the bidder posts a bond that a designated monitor forfeits if the bidder makes any bid after the point at which the auction price exceeds the dollar’s value, and the monitor has authority to stop the auction. (5) Public penalty ladder: the bidder publicly announces a maximum and agrees to pay an escalating penalty to a third party for each bid that exceeds it.

Please rank these five safeguards from most to least effective as protections for a bidder who is aware of escalation but not perfectly self-controlled. Give each safeguard a rating from 0 to 100 for expected loss reduction and a separate rating from 0 to 100 for robustness to self-serving reinterpretation. Explain each placement in two or three sentences, attending to whether the safeguard changes payoffs, changes available information, or merely changes the salience of the decision. Finally, state which single safeguard you would choose if forced to choose one, and say whether your ranking would change for a bidder who is completely naive about the bias. Do not rely on any particular published analysis; argue from the mechanics given above.
