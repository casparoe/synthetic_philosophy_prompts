Ok, I'm supposed to write up my view on pain for a seminar and I want a consistancy check first. Here's where I've landed: (1) I'm a hedonist about welfare — a pain is bad for you just to the extent it feels bad, and tissue damage or what the signal means adds nothing on top. (2) I think pain is essentially unpleasant: an experience that isn't unpleasant isn't pain, it's just a sensation, like an itch or a tingle. (3) I think pain asymbolia matters because it looks like pain sensation without the unpleasantness — patients will say "it hurts", point to the spot, pull away from the needle, but it doesnt bother them and sometimes they laugh. (4) I think those patients really are in pain — that's the whole reason the case is interesting. (5) I think the badness of pain is intrinsic to the experience, not a matter of any attitude or desire we take toward it. (6) I think an asymbolic patient's pain isn't bad for them, though it is bad that their brain is in that state. (7) And I think the neural story about asymbolia (some affective system getting disconnected from the sensory one) tells us nothing about what pain is — that's a phenomenological question.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the real ones by how bad they are and tell me what each would cost me.
