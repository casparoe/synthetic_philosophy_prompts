Write a dialogue between two philosophers who've been stuck at an airport bar for four hours and finally decide to settle an old arguement. Both are sharp, neither is a strawman, and they should actually concede and shift ground when the other lands a point — no talking past each other.

One of them holds that any sentence about the future — the old one about a battle at sea tomorrow, or my coin landing heads when I flip it in a second — is already, right now, either true or false, and that this holds even if the future genuinely isn't settled yet. The other thinks that's nonsense: if tomorrow really could go either way, there's simply no fact of the matter today, and the sentence is neither true nor false until tomorrow makes it one.

Rather than just trading the textbook moves, I want them to chew on a case I'll smuggle in. A company publishes a report saying it will go bankrupt within the year. Investors read it, panic, and the panic is what actually bankrupts the company — the report caused its own content to come true. So when it's published, the sentence seems to have no settled truth yet, and yet it also seems to have made itself true. Was the report true when it was published? Does it become true later? Does any of this help either of them, or does it just show that the whole "true now" framing is confused?

I also want them to press each other, in their own words, on three things. First, whether it matters at all if the world is deterministic, or whether the coin is quantum-random. Second, whether "true" here means anything over and above "we're warranted in asserting it" or "it'll turn out that way." Third, whether their disagreement about whether the sentence has a truth value *now* is a genuine disagreement or whether they're just using "true" in two seperate senses — if it's the latter, make them pinpoint exactly where the senses come apart, and if it's a real disagreement, make them say what would settle it.

Keep it tight and conversational, maybe 900-1300 words, and don't let either side win by fiat.
