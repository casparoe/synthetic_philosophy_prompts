I got into a long, slightly wine-fueled arguement at my cousin's wedding with a friend who works in housing law, and it turned into a thing about consent. Here's the principle I've been defending, which I've been calling the Refusal Baseline Principle: an act of consent is morally valid just in case the consenter is competent, adequately informed about the relevant features of what they are agreeing to, and not coerced, where coerced means that the consenter could not have refused without dropping below a minimal welfare baseline they have an independent moral claim to. My friend thinks that is basically right but too weak, because it lets a lot of structural pressure count as invalidating consent; I am at maybe 0.35 that the principle as stated is the correct analysis, she is at maybe 0.8. The case I keep coming back to is a tenant who agrees to a big rent increase because otherwise the landlord will not fix the heat in January, and I want a framework that gets that case right without making every hard choice invalid. I want your number, but I mostly want to see where the number comes from.
