I am a novelist, and I have written a character whose defining quality — a steady, unshowy courage — the plot never once calls upon. I worry that I am attributing to her a trait I never actually dramatize. A philosopher friend tells me the case is a textbook instance of a disposition that is masked, or perhaps finked, and that whether the trait is really present at all may depend on which of those descriptions we settle for. Give me an example of the conceptual issue at work here: one concrete case, drawn from the sort of fiction I might write rather than the usual glass-and-sugar examples, in which it is genuinely unsettled whether the disposition is present-but-masked, present-but-finked, or simply absent, and say what turns on choosing one description over the others and what would decide among them. (For clarity: by a fink I mean something that, once the triggering conditions obtain, alters or removes the disposition itself; by a mask, something that merely blocks the manifestation while leaving the disposition intact.) End with a single sentence beginning "In short:" that states which description you would defend and why.
