Someone in my book club keeps dropping 'consciousness is a user illusion' — like the desktop on a computer, she says: handy icons, but nothing actually sitting behind them. Last week she used it to shut down an arguement about whether a chatbot could be conscious ('doesn't matter anyway, consciousness is a user illusion') and I felt like five different claims got compressed into that one sentence, and she slides between them depending on what she needs.

Can you lay out the distinct precise things 'consciousness is a user illusion' could be claiming? I want the menu, not a ruling on which one is right overall. For each reading, say exactly what is supposed to be illusory — the phenomenal qualities themselves, our introspective access to them, the unity of experience, the causal power of conscious states over behaviour, the existence of a single self that has them, or merely our naive theory about all of the above — and what that reading would imply for things like: can something be conscious while being unable to report anything, is the hard problem a real problem or a confusion, and does it matter morally whether a creature is conscious.

Then grade them for me: which are platitudes nobody sane denies, which are definately substantive positions with real defenders, which are probably false or at least unmotivated, and which one she most likely means when she's being casual about it. And tell me whether any of these readings actually licenses what she used it for — 'so it doesn't matter whether the chatbot is conscious'. If none of them do, say that plainly instead of being diplomatic.

Two traps to watch for: don't let 'our reports are unreliable' quietly drift into 'there's nothing there', and don't let 'illusion' smuggle in a sense of 'not real' or 'not physical' that's doing all the work. Also, if a couple of these readings are really rival camps whose own proponents would insist they're completely different positions, flag that rather than lumping them together as one vibe.
