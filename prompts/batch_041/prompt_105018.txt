I am a PhD student in philosophy working on a dissertation chapter on causation by omission. I have an argument that a colleague and I have been unable to resolve, and I would like your comments on where it goes wrong and what the repair costs. The argument is meant to test the simple counterfactual account, on which an omission is a cause of an outcome when the outcome counterfactually depends on the omission. Here it is, with numbered steps:

(1) An omission O is a cause of an outcome E if E would not have occurred had O not occurred.
(2) For any outcome E, there are indefinitely many omissions O such that had O not occurred, E would not have occurred. For example, if I had not failed to prevent E, E would not have occurred; if I had not failed to invent a cure for the disease that caused E, E would not have occurred; and so on.
(3) Therefore, any outcome E has indefinitely many omissions as causes.
(4) If any outcome has indefinitely many causes, then the concept of a cause is trivial and cannot ground responsibility or explanation.
(5) But the concept of a cause is not trivial; it grounds responsibility and explanation.
(6) Therefore, (1) is false.

I am not willing to accept (3), and I would prefer to keep (1) if I can. The counterfactual account seems to me the only account that makes omissions causes without smuggling in normative facts at the ground floor. But I also cannot see how to reject (2) without giving up the idea that counterfactual dependence is at least a necessary condition for causation by omission. I have tried adding a relevance condition, on which only omissions that are relevant to the outcome count as causes, but relevance itself seems to need a contrast set, and I cannot specify the contrast set without either an infinite regress or an arbitrary choice. I have also tried a norm-violation condition, on which an omission is a cause only if it violates a norm in force in the context, but that makes causation by omission depend on social expectations, which seems to me a high price. I would like to know which step you would reject, why it looked compelling, and what rejecting it commits me to.
