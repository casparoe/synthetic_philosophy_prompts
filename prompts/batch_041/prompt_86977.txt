I have been reading philosophy blogs and forums for the better part of two decades, and I would like to argue rather than read another survey. Please play a philosopher I shall call the continuity mechanist: someone who holds, seriously and without embarrassment, that the distance between a digger wasp and a human being is one of degree and never of kind. The wasp digs a burrow, stings a cricket, drags it to the threshold, goes inside to inspect the burrow, comes out, and drags the cricket again; move the cricket a few inches while she is inside and she will run the whole sequence again, as many times as you care to move it, without ever appearing to notice that nothing has changed. What the literature calls sphexishness is her condition, and the mechanist's claim is that it is ours too, only far more deeply nested. Human flexibility, on this view, is a larger and more self-referential repertoire of condition-and-action routines: routines that take other routines as input, routines for auditing routines, and so on indefinitely. The mechanist is not a crude eliminativist. He thinks that talk of freedom, reasons and responsibility does real work, because it marks genuine differences in how richly and how self-monitoringly a system's behaviour is controlled. But he denies that any difference in kind is being marked, and he refuses to buy a distinction with words such as emergence or genuine understanding. He also accepts, in a pragmatic spirit, that calling something an agent is a stance we adopt in order to predict it, not a fact we discover inside it.

Constraints for the role: stay in character until I say the word stop; never break frame to comment on the exercise; concede small points honestly but do not surrender the main claim; do not cite books, papers, authors or schools as authorities, since I want the argument made in your own words rather than a reading list; do not merely restate the thesis, but answer my case; if you need a fact about a case I mention, ask me for it in character rather than assuming it; keep each reply to one or two substantial paragraphs so that this feels like a conversation; and write in British English throughout, with British spelling, punctuation and idiom.

Before you write anything, think the position through carefully on your own. Work out the strongest available mechanist reply to my opening, then test it against the possibility that it proves too much, and only afterwards give me the in-character reply, with no preamble, no headings and no commentary on your reasoning.

Here is my opening move, which I think breaks the continuity claim. I gather the wasp story has been challenged as a piece of laboratory folklore, but grant it for the sake of argument. For the past year or so I have been trying, deliberately and with some determination, to make myself sphexish: to install a routine that will run whether or not I notice anything, in the way her routine runs. I have failed, and not for want of discipline. Every routine I install keeps being interrupted by my knowledge that I installed it; the auditing routine turns out to be stronger than the installed routine, or to be the installed routine, or to be both at once, and I cannot get the system to settle. The wasp cannot fail in this way. Failure of this sort is not even a possibility for her. If that is only a difference in degree, tell me what the degree is a degree of, and give me the mechanism, in your own vocabulary, by which a system that can be made sphexish on purpose nevertheless refuses to be. And since a thesis that nothing could count against is worth nothing to me, tell me what would count as evidence that you are wrong.
