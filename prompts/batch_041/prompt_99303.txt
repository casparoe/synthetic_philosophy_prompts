I'm not an academic — I just read alot of space blogs, and somehow I ended up as the volunteer chair of a tiny grant committee that funds space projects. We hand out maybe six to ten small grants a year (five to twenty-five grand each), and we also sometimes sign letters supporting bigger missions, so I'm the one who has to say yes or no and then explain myself at the next meeting. I've realy only got about two hours per proposal, there's no philosopher on the committee, and the other four people on the board will overrule me if I make this take forever, so "we need more study" can't be my answer to everything.

Here's the idea I keep circling back to and can't turn into anything usable. First: a place has value on its own, not just for what we can get out of it, and wrecking it is a real loss even if there's nobody there to miss it. Second: anything we can't undo should be treated as a much bigger deal than anything we can.

Turn those two ideas into a procedure I can actually run on a proposal. The questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each one, where the procedure just stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because it's a values call and not a facts call. Keep it short enough that I can do it at my kitchen table, and don't hand me a reading list, I'm not going to read it. If some step is really just my gut in a costume, say so.

Then run it on three proposals we actually have in front of us: (1) a student team wants us to fund a small impactor they'd fire into a permanently shadowed crater at the lunar pole to see if there's ice in the plume — the impact is the measurement, so there's no way to look without disturbing; (2) a group wants funding to build a little regolith-brick printer and test it on the surface, which uses up some material where it sits; (3) a memorial payload, a small canister of ashes on the lunar surface, and the same group also wants to sell naming rights to craters. Full disclosure, my dad's ashes are in that third one, so I need the procedure to be willing to tell me no even when I don't want to hear it, and to tell me which parts of my own reaction I should distrust.

Be straight with me about how confident you are in what you're telling me, and single out the one part you're least sure about. American English please.
