In the novel I am writing, two of my characters — a non-naturalist moral realist and a quasi-realist expressivist — have argued for years and have at last agreed to publish a joint statement of their adversarial collaboration. I need that document. Set out first the numbered claims they would both sign: about the truth-aptness of moral judgements and their place in inference, about the supervenience of the moral on the natural, about what persistent moral disagreement does and does not show, and about what each of them means, and refuses to mean, by calling a moral judgement true. Then give their residual disagreements in a table, each row phrased in words the side that holds it would accept — above all whether the correctness of a moral judgement consists in correspondence to a stance-independent fact or in the standing of a conative attitude, and whether mind-independence is a substantive commitment or a construction the expressivist can earn from ordinary moral talk. Identify the cruxes, and for each record a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, together with the prediction each would make in advance; let one of those cruxes turn on this case: a being who, for every moral question, delivers the verdict our best moral reasoning would converge on, yet who has no conative states at all and is wholly indifferent to the verdicts she issues. Where the dispute between my two characters is verbal rather than substantive, say so, and say which words are doing the work.
