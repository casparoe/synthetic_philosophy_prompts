Write a pantoum about a clinical trial in which the pooled result points the opposite way from the result in every subgroup. The trial has two arms. Of 100 patients given treatment A, 9 of the 10 mild cases recovered and 45 of the 90 severe cases recovered; of 110 patients given treatment B, 80 of the 100 mild cases recovered and 4 of the 10 severe cases recovered. Within the mild stratum A does better (90% against 80%), and within the severe stratum A does better again (50% against 40%), yet across all patients B does better (roughly 76% against 54%), because the two arms were handed very different case mixes — A took almost all the severe cases, B almost all the mild ones. Let the poem be built so that its form carries the argument rather than merely illustrating it: use the pantoum's rule that lines return verbatim in new positions to make the same sentence true under one partition and false under another, and let the closing quatrain, which in a pantoum loops back into the opening, stage the paradox as something that keeps reasserting itself no matter which summary you settle on. The two recycled refrain lines should be the two competing bottom lines — one claiming that A cured the greater share, one that B did — and the surrounding lines should supply the scope (among the mild, among the severe, all told) that decides which claim the same words are making. Keep it accurate enough that a statistician would recognize the setup as a genuine reversal and not a trick: the direction of the correct inference depends on whether severity is a confounder to be adjusted for or a common effect to be left alone, and the poem should leave that question open rather than declare the aggregate a mere artifact. Aim for five or six quatrains, in a register that could be read aloud at the close of a seminar without embarrassment.
