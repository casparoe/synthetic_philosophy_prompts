Analyze the Masterpiece Cakeshop controversy in the United States through the lens of Sen's liberal paradox. In that case, a baker declined on religious grounds to create a wedding cake for a same-sex couple, the couple brought a civil-rights complaint, and the dispute eventually reached the Supreme Court, which resolved it narrowly without settling the underlying conflict between religious liberty and equal access. Treat the baker's claim of conscience and the couple's claim of equal service as two liberal rights. Set out the social-choice structure: what the relevant alternatives are, which individual is decisive over which pair of alternatives, and where, if anywhere, the weak Pareto condition enters. Then say whether the episode is a clean instance of Sen's impossibility, a collision of two minimal-liberal rights that the theorem does not directly model, or a case in which the legal resolution changes the domain of decisiveness rather than solving the conflict. Use the game-form conception of rights, on which a right is a constraint on the strategies available to others rather than a ranking over social states, to ask whether the cake case can be represented without the cycle. Finally, say what the episode shows about the paradox: does it expose a defect in modeling rights as pairwise decisiveness, does it suggest that liberal institutions must trade off rights rather than satisfy them all, or does it vindicate Sen's point that a pluralist society cannot avoid adjudicating conflicts it would rather leave to individual choice? Be disciplined about the mapping, and where the theory runs out, say so rather than forcing it to speak.
