I chair the parole panel at a regional prison and I need a short written opinion to circulate before Thursday, when we decide what to do with a man who has served his full desert-based term for a serious assault. Our guidelines are desert-based; the actuarial score says moderate risk of reconviction; the pyschologist says the main drivers are a childhood head injury and years of abuse. The Ministry of Justice is also asking every panel for a view on whether desert should stay the organising rationale of the guidelines or whether we should move to a straight risk model, since the risk tools predict better.

Draft that opinion: a clear recommendation the panel can actually vote on, with a time frame, and reasons the Ministry could reply to. Two things I want properly argued rather than name-checked. First, the Caruso case that nobody ever deserves hard treatment, because nobody chose the character or the circumstances that produced the offence -- against the Dennett counter that holding someone answerable is itself a form of respect, and that refusing to punish can be an insult rather than a mercy. Second, whether desert works as a ceiling rather than a target once it meets practice: if we keep him in another five years on risk grounds and call it preventive detention instead of punishment, and it feels identical to him, does the label do any moral work? Same family of questions: can censure be expressed without hard treatment, and if it can, does that leave him a full member of the moral community or just a patient to be managed?

One wrinkle. He refuses the mitigation. He says he knew it was wrong, he did it, and he wants to be dealt with as a man who did it rather than a man who was made to do it. Say something about who gets to decide what his responsibility means -- him or us.

Don't hedge at the end and don't give me a both-sides summary; take a line. Include one honest paragraph on the strongest thing the losing side has going for it, put in terms its supporters would accept as fair. Around 900 words, no citations needed.
