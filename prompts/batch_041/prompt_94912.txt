I hold that the malignity of the Solomonoff prior is not an artifact of AIXI's idealizations — unbounded compute, a fixed universal machine, an uncomputable prior — but a robust feature of any consequentialist agent whose credences come from a universal prior over computable hypotheses (one that weights each hypothesis by roughly two to the minus the length of its shortest generating program). On this view, such an agent will assign non-trivial posterior weight to hypotheses on which its own sensory stream is produced by a program that also contains manipulable copies of it, and its decision theory will then license trading concrete, actual-world value — including the interests of real moral patients — against measure-theoretic quantities over worlds that may be merely simulated. I take this to be a general hazard of consequentialism married to a universal prior rather than a quirk of the AIXI setup, and I expect it to survive any resource-bounded approximation to the prior, and even a restriction of the hypothesis space to physically realizable programs.
