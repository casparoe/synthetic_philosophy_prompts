So I got dragged into my flatmate's philosophy of physics reading group and now I can't stop thinking about the hole argument, which is annoying because I'm a data engineer and my actual day job is deciding when two things are "the same thing."

Heres the setup as I understand it, correct me if I've mangled it. You take a spacetime theory, pick a region of the manifold (the hole), and apply a transformation that's the identity everywhere outside the hole but shuffles the points around smoothly inside it, carrying the metric along. You get a second model that agrees with the first on everything outside the hole. If the points of the manifold are genuine individuals whose identities outrun the structure on them, then the theory seems to treat these two models as two different possibilities, even though no observable fact differs between them. The relationalist side says fine, they were never two possibilities in the first place, because points have no identity over and above the structure. And then there's a third bunch who say the indeterminism is an artefact of writing the theory down in a formalism that fails to already identify the shuffled models, so the whole thing is about our notation rather than the world. The first two have been going at it for decades and I don't get the sense anyone has moved.

I want a diagnosis, not a survey. What kind of disagreement is this actually? My hypothesis, which I'd like tested rather than agreed with: the sides are working with two different senses of "same" (same according to the theory vs. same in fact), and that this is not merely verbal, because the two readings drag different modal commitments behind them about what could have been otherwise inside the hole. If that's right, say what those commitments are. If it's wrong, say where it breaks.

Second thing I want: at work we deduplicate records by picking a canonical form and quotienting away the identifiers, and nobody thinks we've lost a customer. Is that the same move as the quotienting move in the hole argument, or is it a change of subject? What does quotienting actually do to the substantivalist — refute him, or just rename his view?

Give it to me in four sections. Bottom line: the diagnosis in three sentences, no hedging. What the sides are actually committing to: about 250 words, built from what each side says rather than from labels. My day-job analogy, does it hold: about 150 words.
