ok so I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and I'm about a year out from starting a book whose main character is a philosopher of religion — the kind who works on divine hiddenness, the argument that a loving god wouldn't leave sincere seekers in the dark. I want the novel's intellectual spine to be a real open problem, not a fake one I invent, so I'm going to spend my reading year going deep on maybe three questions in that area instead of skimming all of it. Here's my problem: the stuff that's easy to find seems to be the same five moves on loop (the soul-making defense, epistemic distance, the claim that non-resistant non-belief doesn't really exist, and so on), and I don't trust my own sense of whats live vs. settled.

What I'd like from you: tell me which open questions in the hiddenness debate actually deserve a year of my attention, ranked, with the reasoning. By deserve I mean for my purposes — I need questions that are (a) genuinely unresolved and not just recycling old moves, (b) deep enough that a smart character could plausibly spend a life on them, and (c) load-bearing, in the sense that settling them would move other questions in the area. Which are upstream of which? Which look open but are really just terminological? And where those three criteria pull against each other, tell me which you'd let win and why.

Two things I keep circling that I havent seen framed the way I want, so tell me if they're real questions or just me being a novelist:

1. Almost everything I read treats hiddenness as god being absent — not showing up. But the version that scares me is different: god present and not addressing me, the way someone can be in the room and never look at you. Is the difference between hidden-as-absent and hidden-as-unaddressed a real distinction with open questions hanging off it, or does it just collapse into the same thing?

2. The relationship analogy. If love requires some kind of shared attention on something outside the two of you — a third thing you're both turned toward — then a god who never turns up to the shared object isn't just hidden, he's structurally unable to be in the relationship at all. Does that generate open questions nobody's worked, or is it just soul-making with extra steps?

Also: which questions here are traps for a novelist — i.e. look profound but would make the book preachy, or would force me to settle something the field definately hasn't? And what would change your ranking — what would you have to see or work through to move a question up or down?
