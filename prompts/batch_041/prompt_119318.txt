I'm a science journalist — I write long features, mostly philosophy and cognitive science — and I've somehow agreed to run a 90-minute session at a small ideas festival next month. About 25 people, mixed crowd, a few academics but mostly not, and no one will have read anything I send them. The session is on tranquilism, the view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters: not pleasure, not achievement, not getting what you want. I'd much rather they feel the pull of that than hear me explain it, so I want an activity, not a talk.

Can you design one? I want the rules and incentives to do the work, so that people end up somewhere they didn't choose — ideally someone has a genuinely great time and still comes out badly on whatever we're scoring, and it should be clear why that's not just a bug in my scoring. I need materials I can print at a copy shop, the payoff scheme, a rough timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the view (including the difference between "this feels bad" and "this doesn't matter", which is where I expect the pushback). Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be straight with me about the part of the view the activity definately can't reproduce — I don't want to stand up there implying a game settles an axiology.
