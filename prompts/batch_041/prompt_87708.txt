I chair the practice-standards council of a national medical specialty society. Our advisory panels have nine members and can issue a practice standard on a simple majority; one of my council members now proposes enlarging panels to fifteen, still decided by simple majority, on the ground that the Condorcet jury theorem shows larger bodies to be more reliable — if each panelist is more likely than not to get the call right and votes independently, the majority is increasingly likely to be right. I am unpersuaded but cannot yet articulate why: our panelists train in the same programs, read the same literature, and are widely reported to fall in behind whoever is most senior in the room; last year a nine-member panel issued a standard 5-4 on a technique now in wide use, and our audit found several panel findings reversed on appeal. Draft the council's decision memo, opening with the recommendation itself and then the reasoning. Recommend a concrete rule — panel size, voting threshold, and any procedural safeguards the rule needs to work — and defend it, giving an honest account of the strongest case for my colleague's proposal and saying plainly what evidence would change your recommendation. I want the argument, not citations, so work from the structure of the theorem and what it assumes; treat separately the case where panels are merely noisy and the case where they are systematically biased.
