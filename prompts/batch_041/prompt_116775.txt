I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of language, and I have to lay out my commitments at the pragmatics reading group next week. I'd like a consistency check first, so here is roughly where I've landed.

(1) Speaker meaning is reflexive-intention based: to mean that p by uttering something is to intend one's audience to arrive at the belief that p at least partly in virtue of recognizing that very intention.

(2) Conversational implicature is a species of speaker meaning, and its diagnostic marks are calculability from the maxims, cancellability, non-detachability and non-conventionality.

(3) The contrast carried by choosing "but" over "and", and the like, is genuine, but it belongs to the standing meaning of the sentence rather than to anything the speaker intends: it is detachable, it is not calculable from the maxims, and it survives under negation and embedding.

(4) I resist the idea that "but" adds a second sense to the word; I take seriously the thought that senses are not to be multiplied beyond necessity.

(5) On quantifiers: a speaker who says "Some of the students passed" while knowing that all of them did says something true — I am not a contextualist about literal content — but plainly implies that not all of them passed.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to, and what is the cheapest way to make the set consistent? Please order the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what that costs, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
