Me and my friend Maya spent about three hours last night arguing about whether anyone is ever actually responsible for anything, and I want your take, because she thinks I'm just a pile of gut reactions and I think there's a real principle in there somewhere.

Her move is the Strawson thing (Galen, not the other one, the one who wrote about being conscious or whatever): you do what you do because of the way you are, so to be truly responsible for what you did you'd have to be responsible for being the way you are; but you didn't build that self, your genes and your childhood did, or else you did choose it, and that choice came out of a self you also didn't build, so it regresses forever; and if the causal chain just stops somewhere undetermined then the act was luck, which is no better. So nobody is ever really responsible, deserving-in-the-deep-sense, and all my blaming is just social housekeeping. I think that's too fast. Here's what I actually think, case by case:

- Drunk driver who kills a kid on a crosswalk. Fully to blame. Being drunk doesn't excuse him, if anything it makes it worse.
- Guy raised inside a cult from age 2, who at 30 shoots someone because the leader told him to. I don't blame him at all, or barely.
- Same guy at 60, after thirty years of a normal job, reading books, doubting privately, still inside the cult. Now I blame him plenty.
- Man with a tumor pressing on his frontal lobe who becomes violent. Not to blame. That wasn't him.
- Man whose personality changes after a stroke, and who is now cold and cruel, and who a year in likes who he's become and wouldn't go back. I blame him. Maya hates this one.
- My aunt at 45, railing at her 17-year-old self for wrecking her sister's life. I think that's fair, actually. She was a selfish kid, no excuse, even though nobody chooses to be a selfish kid.
- Man who over twenty years, by a hundred small choices each of which he was fine with at the time, turns into the kind of person who would sell out his friend. Responsible.
- Man who hasn't slept in three days and screams at his wife in a way he never otherwise would. Less responsible. That one felt like weather.

And the one I keep going back and forth on: a guy who for ten years let a feed curate everything he read, chose every single time to keep scrolling, and slowly turned into someone with totally different politics and a much colder heart. He chose every step. He chose none of it. No idea.

What I'd tentatively say is the rule: it isn't about who built you, it's about upkeep. You're responsible for what your self does now if you've been the one maintaining it, the way you're answerable for the state of a house you've lived in for years even though you didn't build it. And the regress Maya loves only really bites when the act comes out of a self you dont currently own.

So does that survive my own cases, and where does it need patching? Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, a couple sentences each, no intro and no closing summary. 1) the principle, as precise as you can get it. 2) which clause is forced by which of my cases. 3) what it predicts for at least one case I didn't list, your own invention. 4) which of my verdicts it can't save and has to overturn. 5) what it says about the feed guy, and whether it actually answers Maya or just changes the subject.
