I'm in a political theory class and we've just hit affirmative action, and the annoying thing is my gut won't stay put. Here's where I actually land: India's caste-based reservations for Dalits and Adivasis in universities and government jobs — fine, even though a higher-scoring general-category applicant loses the seat. Norway's rule that big company boards have to be around 40% women — also fine, since they can just add board seats and it breaks up the old-boys' club. But a US university giving a big racial boost in admissions, so a Black applicant with lower scores beats a white or Asian applicant with higher scores — not fine, that feels like race used as a blunt proxy and one specific kid who did everything right getting shafted. Legacy admissions and athletic recruitment — scrap them entirely. The problem is the India case and the US case look identical to me on paper and I approve of one and not the other, so is there really one principle that gets all four, or do I have to give one up? And my roommate keeps pushing a scheme where you ignore race/caste/gender completely and just give a boost based on a "disadvantage score" built from parental wealth, neighborhood, and school quality — does whatever principle you land on say that's okay? Argue it out for me, I cant look anything up.
