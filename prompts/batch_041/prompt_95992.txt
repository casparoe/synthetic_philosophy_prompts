I've been trying to work out how to be a decent person by thinking about what my actions actually do in the world, and I keep hitting the same wall. Two examples, because one on its own might just sound like I'm overthinking.

First: I gave a fiver to a bloke sleeping rough outside the station. He eats that night. Then maybe he gets through the winter, meets someone, they have a kid, the kid grows up and does something — or maybe that fiver is a fiver I didn't put in the food bank, so someone else goes without, and that person's kids don't happen, and so on. Second: I buy a cheap t-shirt. It keeps someone in work I'll never meet, in a factory I'll never see, and it also keeps that factory open and someone else's shut.

Now, I know the usual reply is "you can't predict the future," and fine, I accept that. But that's not quite my problem, and here's the bit I can't get past. Suppose I had a magic box that showed me everything that followed from my action — every knock-on, all the way out, no gaps, no guesswork. I still don't think I could say whether it was good. Because there's no natural place to stop counting. Stop at the bloke eating tonight and that's a line I drew. Stop at his grandchildren and that's another line I drew. Try to include everything and the chain never closes, the total never settles, and the sign seems to flip depending on how far out I look — up close it looks kind, far out it looks like I've just shuffled things about. So it's not that I'm ignorant. It's that the thing I'm supposed to be adding up doesn't seem to have edges I can point to. And the stranger it gets, the less I can act at all, which feels backwards — the people who never think about any of this seem to manage fine.

Is there a name for this? It feels like it must be a known thing and I just don't have the word. I've been told it's the butterfly effect, but that's about small causes having big effects, and my problem isn't the size of the effects, it's that I can't tell where they end or how to weigh them once I've got them. Someone else said moral luck, and I don't really know what that is, but I don't think it's the same either. And it can't just be ordinary uncertainty, because uncertainty is about what I don't know, and I'd be stuck even with the magic box.

What I actually want: if there's an established term, tell me what it is, then tell me what the nearest things to it are that aren't it, and why they're not — the butterfly effect, moral luck, whatever else gets offered. If there isn't a term, definately make one up and define it properly, including the edges (does it apply to tiny things, to things I don't do as well as things I do, to things nobody could possibly have foreseen?) and say what would have to be true for it to stop applying. A bare label won't help me, I want the shape of the thing, not the sticker on it.

Oh and please answer in proper British English, I can't be doing with the American spellings.
