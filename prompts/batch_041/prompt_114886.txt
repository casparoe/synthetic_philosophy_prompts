I've gone down a rabbit hole on Malthus and now I can't stop arguing with myself about it. The standard line is that he was wrong — population didn't outrun food, no great famines — but then you also get people saying he was right and just early, or that he was right about animals and about places with no fertilizer, or that we only escaped because of the industrial revolution, which isn't exactly a refutation. I can't tell which of those is a real defence and which is just refusing to let a dead theory die. And I dont have any background in this, I've just been reading on my own.

So I want to build a quiz for my blog, for people like me: when a prediction doesn't come true, when is that actually a failure of the theory? I want it to sort readers by what they really believe, not by which label they like, so no question should just be 'so, was Malthus right?'.

Eight to ten questions, each one a concrete case or a claim to agree or disagree with, two or three options each, plus a key that maps answer patterns onto positions. Cases I'd like in there: the doctor who tells you you'll be dead in five years if you don't change anything, and you change everything and live twenty; the forecaster who says 90% chance of rain and it stays dry; Y2K, where everyone predicted disaster and a lot of money got spent stopping it; a doomsday preacher whose date passes; and Malthus himself.

Positions I want the quiz to be able to separate, because I keep sliding between them: it was simply wrong; it was never really tested, because the prediction was always of the 'if nothing else changes' kind and everything changed; it got the trend right and only the timing wrong; it's unfalsifiable, since every outcome can be read as confirming it; it failed partly because people heard it and acted on it, so the theory is a cause of the evidence against it; and the one I think I secretly hold — that it's being kept alive by patching, and I should just let it go.

If there are positions in between those that I havent thought of, put them in, with the usual name if it has one and 'unnamed' if it doesn't. And tell me which ones my questions can't really pull apart, and whether any combination of answers lands nowhere — and if so, whether that's because it's incoherent or just because nobody's bothered with it. No reading list please, I won't read it.
