I chair our city council's refugee resettlement scrutiny panel. We're one of the councils signed up to the Northsea Compact — we take an agreed annual quota of refugees and in return the Home Office covers housing and casework costs. A bit of a mess has landed on us and I need a short written opinion I can hand round at Thursday's meeting.

Here's the situation. A charity rescue boat pulled 58 people out of the water and landed them at our port because it was the nearest. About 30 are the classic story — the state back home is after them and they'd be in real danger if we sent them back. The other 28 aren't. Nobody in particular is persecuting them: the aquifer's gone saline, two harvests have failed, the national government has effectively written the region off, and they can't safely resettle anywhere else in the country either. No one is doing the harming, or rather everyone is, a little bit.

Our legal officer is blunt about it: they don't meet the refugee definition, the Compact only pays for refugees, so if we house them out of Compact money we breach the agreement and put at risk the funding that keeps the other 30 — and last year's cohort — supported. Some of the panel want us to adopt what they're calling a "no way back" test: you count if you face a level of harm no one could reasonably be asked to bear and there's no authority at home you could appeal to, whether or not anyone intends the harm. Others say that's just smuggling a new definition in through the back door, and that we'd do better to grant the 28 some lesser tolerated status and be honest that it's second-class.

Please draft the opinion. I need a concrete recommendation with a timescale, not a survey of the literature. And take the legal officer seriously — I don't want a memo that just declares the Compact unjust and waves the funding problem away, I want the strongest version of her argument actually answered. British English, please, and definately no citations — I've got no time to chase references.
