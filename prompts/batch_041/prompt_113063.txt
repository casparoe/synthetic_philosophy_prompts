I run a philosophy of mind reading group, about 14 people, 90 minutes, and next session is on Dennett's Quining Qualia. I want an activity where they feel the trap in their hands instead of hearing me explain it afterwards. The seed is the coffee taster case: two veteran tasters who both now find the blend undrinkable, where one says the taste is exactly what it always was and only his preferences have changed, the other says his preferences are untouched and it's the taste experience itself that's different, and when you press either of them there's nothing they can point to that would show the other one is wrong. I want a runnable session built off that, so that the impossibility shows up in what happens rather than in my closing remarks, and they only get the word "qualia" and the four traditional marks (ineffable, intrinsic, private, immediately apprehensible) after they've already run into the wall. The thing I most want them to come away seeing is that the difference they were so sure they were tracking — same taste/different attitude versus different taste/same attitude — leaves no trace anywhere they can look, and that this isn't a fact about the crudeness of our instruments.

So: materials (cheap, one supermarket trip, a real sip of something so there's something to talk about), roles, the actual rules, the fixed question sheet, a way to score it that doesn't need real money, a minute-by-minute timeline, and the debrief that maps what happened onto the argument. A couple of specifics I'd like built in: give everyone a private card describing which of the four things supposedly happened to them (taste changed, attitude unchanged; attitude changed, taste unchanged; both changed; neither changed, only how they've decided to talk), forbid them from saying which card they hold, and have them answer every question only from a fixed menu of answers, playing the card as sincerely as they can. Then pair each of them with a detective who has to say which card the partner got, scoring points for hits and points for successful concealment. After that I want a second stage where pairs have to design an experiment that would settle whether someone's red and green experiences got swapped overnight with their behaviour and memory left completely intact, and hand it to another pair who has to write down what the subject would say if the swap happened and what they'd say if it didn't — I'm hoping those two answers come out as the same sentence. And a last bit where each group writes one sentence defining qualia plus one sentence saying what observation would show the definition is wrong, which I then sort on the board.

Also tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it — Ive got a bad feeling that half the room will just insist they know what their own experience is like and the whole thing stalls, and I'd like a rule that gets round that without me lecturing. And be honest about which part of the idea the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself.
