ok so my reading group is doing historical materialism and we've gotten stuck on the base/superstructure thing — economy at the bottom (who owns what, who works for whom, how stuff actually gets made), and then law, politics, religion, art, the family, science stacked on top of it. one guy in the group keeps reaching for the building picture to argue that the base explains everything else: a foundation carries the floors, you can't have the upper stories without it, so if you want to know why people believe what they believe or why the state does what it does, you look down. he thinks that closes the question of whether the economy is the one doing the explaining. he's even got a version where the building pours its own foundation as it goes up, which i think is him noticing the metaphor is broken and just patching it as he talks.

i dont think the image can carry that much, but i want to see exactly how far it goes. can you give me the mapping first — what plays the foundation, the floors, the mortar, the load-bearing walls, the architect, the ground the foundation sits in, the building code — and then the places it breaks? specifically:

(a) real foundations don't get retrofitted by the tenants, and upper floors don't push back down into the foundation. if the "superstructure" does act back on the base, is the metaphor dead, or is there a reading that saves it?
