Draft the joint statement that a committed thirder and a committed halfer about the Sleeping Beauty problem could both put their names to after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The setup is fixed and agreed in advance: before the first waking a fair coin is tossed; on heads Beauty is woken once, on tails twice; between awakenings she is given a drug that erases the memory of the earlier waking; the awakenings are otherwise indistinguishable, and on each she is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. You agree on the underlying probability formalism and on the principle that a known objective chance governs credence, at least for uncentered propositions; you do not agree on the answer. You may conclude that part of the dispute is merely verbal, but only if you say exactly which part and why.

The document should contain the following.

1. Agreed claims. A numbered list of claims both of you will sign: about the setup; about what Beauty's evidence at a waking is and is not; about which credence assignments the setup rules out; about the treatment of self-locating belief in general, including whether credences can attach to centered propositions (propositions true or false only relative to a person and a time) and whether any sampling or indifference assumption is being smuggled in; and about the relation between a credence and the odds at which its holder should be willing to bet. Attach a caveat to any claim you can sign only with one.

2. Disagreements. Each live point of difference stated in wording the side holding it accepts as its own — the halfer's case in the halfer's voice, the thirder's in the thirder's. Each side must give P(heads) and the conditional credence P(heads | it is Monday), and must say what it does with the betting version, in which Beauty is offered even-money bets on heads at each waking. If the halfer position admits more than one sub-position — on the conditional credence, say, or on whether the credence is prior-independent — say which sub-position you adopt and why.

3. Cruxes. For each crux, give the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one of you; the case, argument, or result you both agree bears on it; and each side's prediction for that case, recorded in advance. I expect at least these, though add or drop as honesty requires: whether a waking supplies new evidence; the correct reference class for any appeal to indifference; and whether credence must match betting odds. Count as a crux only what would actually move one of you.

4. New cases. (a) The large-N generalization, in which on tails Beauty is woken N times for a fixed known N: state each side's credence as a function of N, and whether it is 1/2 for every N. (b) The unknown-multiplicity variant, in which N is fixed but undisclosed and Beauty holds a prior over it: state each side's credence and how it depends on the prior. (c) The two-ledger proposal, that the dispute dissolves once we separate (i) Beauty's credence in the uncentered proposition that the coin landed heads from (ii) her credence that a randomly selected waking of the experiment is a heads-waking, and that the honest report is the pair rather than a single number. For each of (a) through (c), say whether you both accept it, and for (c) whether it is a dissolution, a redescription, or a dodge.

5. Closing. What each side would count as evidence or argument against its own position, and whether there is anything both of you agree would actually move the debate.

Format the result as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table with columns for the crux, the thirder's wording, the halfer's wording, and the test.
