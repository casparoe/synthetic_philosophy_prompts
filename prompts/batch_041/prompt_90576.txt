In my philosophy class we have been discussing whether dreams are experiences, and after a classmate described a vivid dream, my teacher summed up one side of the debate by saying, "A dream is not an experience; it is a memory." She then used that sentence to argue that dream reports cannot count as evidence about what happens during sleep, and that the question of whether dreams are experiences is confused from the start. I want to know exactly what that sentence could be claiming, because I suspect it is doing more work than it can support.

Please lay out the distinct precise theses that the sentence could be expressing, ranging from the trivial to the radical. For each reading, say what it commits you to about (a) when the dream occurs, during sleep or only upon waking; (b) whether there is phenomenal experience at the time; (c) what a dream report is a report of; and (d) whether dream reports can be evidence about sleep. Among the readings I would like you to consider are: that dreams are experiences occurring during sleep but are known only retrospectively through memory; that there is no experience during sleep and the dream is a narrative confabulated at waking; that "dream" names a kind of report or memory rather than a mental episode; that dreams are experiences but not memories; and that the sentence is trivially true because we only ever access dreams through memory, which would not settle whether they are experiences. Add any other readings you think are important, and say which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, which are probably false, and which would actually support my teacher's conclusion that dream reports cannot be evidence.

I do not want just a menu of possibilities. Please take a definite position: tell me which reading is most plausible, which reading my teacher most likely intended, and whether either of those readings really licenses her dismissal of dream reports. Give your reasons for the verdict you reach.
