So I'm in Year 12 and I've been reading around the whole "is it treatment or enhancement" argument for an essay, and there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I can't put a name to.

It goes like this. Someone says an intervention counts as treatment because it fixes a disorder / brings you back to normal, and enhancement if it takes you past normal. Fine. But then when you ask what "normal" actually is, it turns out to be whatever most people already are — the average. So being short is a "condition" only because most people are taller than you; if the whole population shrank, the tall ones would suddenly be the ones with the "problem". Same with short-sightedness, with how much sleep you need, with how fast you can run. And the weird bit is that as the average moves — say everyone starts taking the pill, or everyone gets glasses — the thing that was normal becomes abnormal and vice versa, but the moral weight of the word "disorder" stays exactly the same. Nobody seems to notice that the line they're defending is just the current crowd, and that the crowd is precisely what they're changing.

Is there a name for this? I don't mean the general "you can't get an ought from an is" thing, and I don't mean "it's all relative / socially constructed" either — I mean specifically a definition of a bad state that's fixed by the statistical average, so the definition, and hence what counts as a treatment worth doing, definately drifts whenever the average drifts. Has anyone given this a proper name and worked out whether it's actually a problem, or do people just gesture at it? And if there's no standard name, give me one and say exactly what it is and what it isn't — I want the carving, not just a label.

If it helps, I keep seeing the same move in the arguments about ADHD meds and about whether it's ok to take something to do better in exams.

Could you answer in British English please.
