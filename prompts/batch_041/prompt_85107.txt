I'm giving a talk to a joint session of my university's behavioral econ group and the action-theory people from the philosophy department next week, and what I need from you is a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense of the paper.

My thesis: akrasia isn't a preference-consistency problem, so the discounting toolkit (hyperbolic, beta-delta models of intertemporal choice) is aimed at the wrong target. The agent who judges she has most reason to φ, with no competing desire pulling at her, and just doesn't do it -- call it cold akrasia -- is not the same as the present-biased agent who really does prefer the earlier reward at the moment of choice. The discounting literature only ever models conflict inside one level of preference, my Tuesday self against my Friday self. I want to say akrasia is a failure of uptake between levels: the first-order preference is there, the second-order preference to be moved by it is there, and the will doesnt pick it up. Commitment devices should be live for the first kind of case and inert for the second, which I'm treating as a diagnostic.

Who's in the room: six or seven economists who estimate discount functions for a living, one of whom runs commitment-savings field experiments and thinks in multi-selves and whose-welfare-are-we-maximizing terms; plus three philosophers, one on intention, one on self-governance, one who mostly does moral psychology. Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get, in their own vocabularies, ordered by how much damage they do, each with a two-minute reply and a flag on where the reply is thin. Tell me what to concede in the first three minutes, and what the question is that I'm praying nobody asks.
