I sit on the deployment-safety review for a small team that ships a tool-using agent, and our current protocol was approved on the strength of an analogy I have since grown uneasy about: a possibly misaligned model is just an untrusted employee, and untrusted employees are a solved problem — you do not have to trust them, you watch what they do, restrict what they can touch, and keep a less capable stand-in ready in case they act up. On that basis we signed off on a protocol assembled from untrusted monitoring, trusted editing of the model's outputs, sandboxed tool access, and fallback to a weaker model whenever the monitors flag something. The analogy did much of the work in that meeting, and I want to know how much weight it can actually bear.

Please act as an expert on AI control and alignment who has thought carefully about where security-style reasoning does and does not transfer to AI systems. Begin by brainstorming the correspondence, then give me a table: what plays the role of the employer, the employee's hidden agenda, the audit trail, the contract, and the penalty for getting caught. Then sort the disanalogies into those that matter for our specific protocol and those that do not — in particular, identify which features of the employee case our design silently relies on (that the overseer is the more capable party, that the overseen has something to lose, that its goals are legible and satiable, that it cannot copy itself or rewrite its own weights) and say what becomes of each control layer when they fail. Finally, tell me whether the conclusion the analogy licensed — deploy now, because containment substitutes for verified alignment — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion all along; and if a better analogy is available for our situation, say what it is. Where the issue is genuinely contested among people who know this area, mark it as contested and do not present one camp's position as the consensus; separate what is settled from what is not.
