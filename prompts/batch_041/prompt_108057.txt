For the past couple of years I've been following the country rankings that come out every spring, and I've decided my country should treat them as a national report card and do whatever it takes to reach the top. I don't have any academic training; this is just a subject I've gotten interested in on my own, reading the report and the coverage around it each year, and I want to make sure the plan I'm putting together is sound before I start trying to persuade anyone else.

Here is my picture of how it works, and I'd like you to correct it where it's wrong. The report gives each country a happiness score and a rank. Underneath the score it lists six things for every country — income, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom, generosity, and how corrupt people think their country is — so my assumption has been that the score is a blend of those six, and that a government that wants to climb should push on all six levers. The top of the list is the same small group of countries year after year, and mine sits somewhere in the middle, so my plan is to campaign for policies that target each of the six.

Two things I can't square. First, the report seems to say the score comes from asking people a single question about where they picture their life on a ladder, and that the six things I just listed don't actually enter the score at all. If that's true, why are they printed under every country's number, and what are they doing there? It also mentions comparing countries to a made-up country called Dystopia, which I don't understand. Second, even granting all of that, is it coherent for a country to try to reach number one, when the whole thing is a ranking of countries against each other? It seems as though every country could improve and the order could stay exactly the same.

So what is this score really measuring, what role do the six ingredients and the Dystopia comparison actually play, and is getting to the top a sensible goal for a country to pursue, or am I chasing something that doesn't mean what I think it means? Please be direct about where my picture is mistaken, and write your answer in American English.
