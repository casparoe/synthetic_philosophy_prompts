You're a philosopher who works on grounding and fundamentality, so you know the neighbourhood. Write me a joke about grounding — one that's genuinely funny to people who work on it, i.e. the payoff has to depend on how grounding actually works, not on the general silliness of metaphysicians. The hook I want (this is the in-the-weeds bit I'm after): grounding is standardly taken to be irreflexive, asymmetric, transitive and well-founded, so every derivative fact bottoms out in ungrounded, "brute" fundamental facts — but there's a live dispute about facts of the form 'A grounds B'. Are those themselves grounded, say in A, or in A together with B? Or are they autonomous, ungrounded — in which case a fact about two thoroughly non-fundamental facts gets to sit at the fundamental level? Each option is awkward for a different reason, and that fork is where I want the joke to hinge. Maybe a fundamentality auditor going door to door through somebody's ontology asking "and what grounds that?" until they reach bedrock and then ask what grounds the bedrock, and what grounds the fact that it's bedrock — but brainstorm three or four candidate punchlines before you commit, and something better is welcome. Keep it short, a bit of setup and a punchline is plenty.

Then, separately, three or four sentences on what's settled versus what's contested: which bits of the joke any grounding theorist would nod at, and which bits take a side on a genuinely disputed question (the autonomy of grounding facts, whether well-foundedness is non-negotiable, that sort of thing). Don't present a contested view as if it's the consensus.
