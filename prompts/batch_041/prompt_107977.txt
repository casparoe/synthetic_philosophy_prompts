I have been reading about the hole argument and have arrived at a conclusion I cannot shake: general relativity is indeterministic. Fix the fields everywhere outside the hole, and the fields inside the hole are not fixed, since there are two solutions that agree outside it and differ inside. I know the standard reply is that the two solutions are related by a diffeomorphism and so represent one and the same physical situation, but I cannot see how this helps. Either the relabeling amounts to a genuine difference in which spacetime point carries which field values, in which case the indeterminism is real and general relativity really is indeterministic; or it does not, in which case the hole argument never established anything about determinism at all and there was never anything to worry about. A colleague tells me the second reading is the correct one and, further, that the same construction can be run on the internal gauge symmetries of the Standard Model, taking a gauge transformation that is the identity outside some bounded region and non-identity inside it, so that particle physics turns out to be indeterministic in exactly the same sense — and that only the gravitational case attracts philosophical alarm. Is my colleague right about either point, and if so, what should I conclude about determinism in fundamental physics?
