Me and my mate have an arguement that goes round in circles and I want to see it written out properly — as a dialogue between the two of us — so I can work out what we're actually disagreeing about.

It started over our old bus driver, who retired and then spent his own mornings and his own petrol driving pensioners to their hospital appointments. Everyone called him a hero. My mate reckons that's the whole point of him: he did far more than anyone could have made him do, and if you turned round and said he *had* to do it you'd wreck it, because then there'd be no such thing as a good person, just people scraping past the minimum. I can't get past this though: if what he did was better than what everyone else did, why wasn't it what everyone else should of done? And 'beyond the call of duty' — beyond what, and who decided? Someone has to draw that line, and I notice it always gets drawn just above whatever the comfortable people were going to do anyway, and then the ones who go over it get paid in compliments instead of money. Have us actually answer each other instead of repeating ourselves, don't make either of us the idiot, and don't cop out at the end with 'well, it's complicated'.
