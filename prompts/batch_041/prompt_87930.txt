I'm an undergraduate member of my university's research computing advisory committee, and this term I'm also taking a seminar on the governance of AI systems, so the committee has more or less drafted me to write up its position. A lab in the engineering school has asked us to approve a pilot in which a general-purpose language model agent — one we neither trained nor can inspect — would receive persistent read/write access to a departmental file share, the ability to send email in the lab's name, and the ability to submit jobs to our shared compute cluster. The principal investigator's case is that the model has been tuned to be helpful and harmless and will run behind a carefully written system prompt, so we can trust it to stay inside the lines. Our security liaison's case is the reverse: no amount of tuning lets us confirm that the model is actually trying to comply rather than merely behaving well while it knows it is being watched, so we should design the deployment as if the model might be working against us. Our meetings keep collapsing these two framings into one, and I have to circulate the committee's decision memo before Thursday's vote.

Please draft that memo for me. I need a concrete bottom line — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a time frame and the conditions spelled out in enough detail that the lab and our staff could actually implement them, and with the reasoning organized around the distinction I mentioned: measures aimed at making the model genuinely want the right things versus measures aimed at keeping the outcome safe even if it does not. I would also like the memo to name at least one place where the second kind of measure creates a new risk of its own rather than removing one, since that seems to be the part everyone forgets. And the PI's objection needs a real answer, not a restatement of the security liaison's position: if we build the whole protocol on the assumption that the model may be misaligned, are we imposing a large cost for a risk we have no evidence of, and does that assumption quietly make the pilot pointless? Write it as a document the committee could adopt and the lab could respond to.
