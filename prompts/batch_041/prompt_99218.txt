The episode as I understand it, and you don't need to look anything up, just correct me if I've got the biology wrong: eukaryotic genomes vary enormously in size and that variation has almost nothing to do with how complex the organism is — an onion has several times more DNA in its cells than you or I do. A huge chunk of that DNA is repetitive: transposons and other sequences that copy themselves around the genome, sometimes causing mutations, and that don't obviously do the host any favors. In the early 1980s people proposed that this stuff is sitting there because it's good at getting itself copied, not because it helps the organism — junk that persists by out-replicating everything else. Half the field read that as the gene's-eye view finally winning outright, and half read it as proof the theory can rationalize literally any outcome.
