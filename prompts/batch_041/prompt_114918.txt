ok so I'm trying to write up my views on free will and punishment for a seminar and I want a consistency check before I make an ass of myself in front of people who actually know this literature. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I've tried to be honest about it rather than tidy:

1. Determinism is probably true, or at any rate it doesn't matter if it is, because everything I do traces back to a brain and a history I didn't choose.
2. Nobody is the ultimate author of their own character. There's no basic desert in the deep sense, no cosmic ledger where anyone is owed suffering or reward just for being the way they are.
3. Still, I'm a compatibilist. Free will and moral responsibility are real; they just aren't about being an uncaused cause. They're about being responsive to reasons, being the sort of thing that can be reasoned with and can adjust what it does in light of what it knows.
4. Retributivism is out. The state should punish for deterrence, rehabilitation, and locking up people who are dangerous, not because the offender owes a debt to the universe.
5. But I still think blame and resentment are fitting responses to wrongdoing, and that how harshly we punish should track how bad the crime was.
6. Methodologically I'm with the crowd that says we start from our actual practices -- we keep blaming and praising, we keep the reactive attitudes -- and we bend the metaphysics to fit them, not the other way round.

Is this a coherent package or am I helping myself to something? Go through it and tell me which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for the real ones what I'd have to give up. Rank the genuine tensions by how bad they are instead of just flagging everything as equally in trouble, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. If one single revision would clean up the most, tell me which. Please write the answer in American English.
