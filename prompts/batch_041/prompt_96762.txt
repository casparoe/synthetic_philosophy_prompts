I have been working through a popular book on evolution — one of those trade books by a science journalist rather than a working researcher — and I have reached the chapter on the adaptationism debate, where I have hit a summary I cannot calibrate. The chapter presents Gould and Lewontin's 1979 critique roughly as follows. In the basilica of San Marco in Venice, the domes rest on arches, and the triangular spaces where a dome meets the arches beneath it are filled with mosaics; those spaces were not built in order to hold mosaics, but exist because a dome has to be carried on arches, and the mosaics were added afterward. Gould and Lewontin's point, the chapter says, is that most biological traits are like those spaces: not adaptations but byproducts of other features, which biologists then mistake for design and explain by inventing a selection story after the fact. The chapter calls this habit the Panglossian paradigm, after Voltaire's character who insisted that everything happens for the best in the best of all possible worlds. Its verdict is that the paper's decisive contribution was to show that an adaptationist hypothesis cannot really be tested, since any inconvenient fact can be rescued by positing a trade-off or an unobserved benefit, so that the whole programme is unfalsifiable. From this the chapter concludes that natural selection is not the main driver of evolution, and that the search for adaptive function in traits such as human language or morality is misguided, the organism being mostly shaped by developmental and structural constraint. I am inclined to trust the chapter, since I have seen the spandrel argument repeated in this shape many times, but someone in my reading group insists it is a caricature, and I have also heard, without being able to place it, that the San Marco example itself is contested — that the spaces in question are properly called pendentives rather than spandrels, and that there were other ways of supporting a dome that the architects could have used and did not, which would make the example less innocent than the chapter makes it sound.
