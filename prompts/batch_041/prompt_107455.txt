I spent 40 years at the bench in physiology and have lately been reading philosophy, and something about dream research has been bugging me. Sleep labs wake people out of REM sleep and ask them what was just going through their mind, and then those reports are treated as the main evidence for what dreams are — the gold standard, basically. But I keep running into philosophers who seem to think a dream isn't an experience at all, either because it's just a disposition to tell a story when you wake up, or because you confabulate the whole thing in the seconds after waking and there was never anything it was like to be asleep and dreaming. If either of those is right, what exactly is the lab measuring?

So heres what I want: what does the ordinary practice of dream science presuppose about dreaming — that it's an experience, that it's undergone at a particular time during sleep, that the report is about that experience rather than being the whole of it? Which rival views does the practice quietly rule out, and could it be run on any of them without changing? And the part that really nags me: the same field insists reports are reconstructions, full of confabulation and forgetting, and then turns around and uses them as a window onto the dream. Is that an outright inconsistency, or is there a single view of dreams that licenses both moves at once? Tell me what would actually have to change in the lab if we gave up the assumption that dreams are experiences, and be clear about where the practice is underdetermined — what it strictly needs versus what it merely fits comfortably with.
