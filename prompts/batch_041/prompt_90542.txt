Ive been teaching a first-year seminar called 'Creativity and Computation' for a few years now, and every single time it stalls in the same place. I hand out the MIU system — MI as the one axiom, the four rewrite rules, and the challenge of getting to MU — and for about twenty minutes they grind away at it, and then somebody notices that the number of I's keeps coming out wrong, and we spend the rest of the hour on the fact that the proof that MU can't be derived is not itself derivable inside the system: you have to step back and see the whole thing as an object. Hofstadter's word for that step back is jootsing, and I've been treating it as the punchline of the whole course for years without being able to say, cleanly, what it's actually supposed to establish. A colleague in the music department keeps telling me it's the same move as when a composer stops writing in a style and starts writing about it, which definately sounds right and also sounds like it might be empty, so I'd like some help.

What I want from you is the most important implications of that little result — the invariant, the outside-the-system proof, the fact that the system can grind away forever and never notice — for the question my students actually care about, which is whether 'creativity' is anything over and above rule-following. My own hunch, and you should feel free to tell me it's confused, is that jootsing only looks like breaking the rules because we've drawn the boundary in the wrong place: the joot is still following rules, just rules the original system didn't know it had, and if that's right then the whole thing should make us *less* impressed by genius rather than more. So: if the hunch holds, what follows for the strong claim that human mathematicians do something no formal system can do? And if it doesn't hold — if there's a real discontinuity and not just a change of level — I want to know exactly where it sits, using the MIU system itself as the test case instead of gesturing at Gödel. Be concrete about which part of the analogy to human thinking actually carries weight, and which part is just a pun on the word 'system'.
