Ok, I've been stuck in a metaphysics rabbit hole about dispositions — fragile glass, soluble sugar, that whole thing — and I keep running into two pictures of what a disposition even is, and I honestly can't tell if there rivals or the same thing in different clothes.

Picture A: to say x is disposed to M in conditions C is basically just to unpack a conditional — if C obtained, x would M — with some hedging bolted on so that finks don't wreck it. A fink is the annoying case where the very trigger that's supposed to bring out the behaviour also removes the disposition, so the thing wouldn't actually M even though intuitively it's still disposed to. The classic repair (Lewis-ish) is to insist the disposition's causal basis has to stick around across the counterfactual, otherwise the ascription comes out false. And then masks are the other headache — the glass wrapped in foam that you can whack all day without it breaking — which the conditional view tries to soak up with some ceteris paribus clause about nothing interfering.

Picture B: dispositions are just real, intrinsic powers of the thing, full stop. The conditional isn't the analysis, it's a symptom you might notice when nothing gets in the way. On this view masks are no embarrasment at all — of course the power can be present and blocked — and finks are just a dramatic version of the same triggering/blocking business.

What I want: compare these two pictures properly. Are they actually different theories, or is B just A with extra metaphysics bolted on? Which one is in better shape on finks vs masks — do they stumble in the same place or in different places? And is there a case that would tell them apart: a thing that's finked and masked at once, or where the causal basis gets swapped out mid-manifestation, or where the trigger is itself the mask? If they turn out empirically equivalent across every possible case, say so, and then tell me what's really at stake in choosing one over the other.

Keep it concrete and argumentative — no lit review, I want to see where the two pictures actually come apart and where they're just redescribing each other.
