Me and a couple friends have a standing argument about responsibility gaps for autonomous systems and I want to actually settle it, so I'm outsourcing the ranking to you. The setup we keep fighting over: some autonomous system (self-driving car, loitering munition, a trading algo that quietly tanks a market) causes serious harm, and when you trace the causal chain back you find every candidate blame-bearer is missing something — the designer didn't intend that particular token harm and couldn't have foreseen the exact interaction, the operator was watching at the wrong level of abstraction, the deployer leaned on a certification that was itself defective, and the system itself arguably isn't the kind of thing that can be blameworthy at all. Call that the gap. What bugs me is that everyone then proposes a "fix" and I'm pretty sure they're not even answering the same question, because some fixes try to *fill* the gap, some try to *dissolve* it by changing what we're asking for, and some just *relocate* it somewhere less uncomfortable.

Here are the six fixes we keep cycling through, and I want them ranked. (1) Meaningful human control — require a human in or on the loop who satisfies both a tracking condition (their omission would have changed the outcome) and a tracing condition (they had the information and the standing to act). (2) Strict liability with a no-fault compensation pool funded by manufacturers and deployers — no one is blamed, victims just get paid. (3) Attribute answerability to the system itself, treating it as a quasi-agent that can be called to account even if it can't be punished. (4) Distribute responsibility across the whole sociotechnical system — a collective/structural notion where no individual need be blameworthy for the gap to be closed. (5) Responsibility-by-design: mandate logging and audit trails so that blame can be reassigned post hoc to whoever is nearest the harm. (6) Role responsibility — whoever occupies the deployer role eats the outcome regardless of what they knew or could have done, full stop. And one of my friends swears by a seventh thing she calls a responsibility ledger: every decision the system makes gets written to a tamper-proof log with the human sign-offs cryptographically attached, so after the fact you can march back down the ledger and pin blame on whoever's signature sits closest to the harm. She thinks that closes the gap; I think it just relocates it and maybe launders it.

What I want: rank all seven from most to least defensible as a way of dealing with the gap, give each a 0–10 score, and for each one say in a sentence whether it actually closes the desert-based gap (the "they genuinely deserved it" sense of blame), merely addresses the accountability gap (someone can answer for it, deservedness aside), or does neither. Then tell me which of the seven you think is doing the most hidden work by quietly redefining the problem. Lead with the ranking itself in your first sentence — best to worst with the scores — and then give me the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate my question back at me. I'd also like you to flag how confident you are in the ranking overall, and single out the one placement you're least sure about and why.
