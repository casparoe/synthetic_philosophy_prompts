Construct a thought experiment to specification in the philosophy of personal identity. The exercise is to design a pair of cases that isolate a single variable and that make three views of the self commit themselves, so that each verdict falls out of the view rather than being stipulated.

Use exactly these three views, characterizing them in your answer as given here and not substituting other formulations:

- The continuity view: a person persists just in case a suitable causal chain of psychological connection — overlapping memories, intentions, and traits — runs from the earlier to the later stage, carried by one continuing subject.
- The narrative view: a self is nothing over and above the organizing center of a story that is actually being spun; wherever a life-story is being continued, there is a self. This view does not tie the self to any particular brain, and it treats the first-personal or third-personal voice of the telling as a surface feature of the story, not as a condition on whether the self exists.
- The distributed view (the position under test): a self can outlast the destruction of its own brain by being re-tokened in other brains, but only when the re-tokening is lived from the inside — when the receiving brains can run the pattern first-personally and not merely predict it from the outside.

The required verdict pattern. Design two variants of one case that differ in exactly one feature and in nothing else:

- First-personal variant: the continuity view says the original person does not survive; the narrative view says the person survives; the distributed view says the person survives.
- Third-personal variant: the continuity view still says the person does not survive; the narrative view still says the person survives; the distributed view says the person does not survive.

The single variable — whether the pattern is run first-personally or only predicted third-personally — must therefore flip the distributed view while leaving the other two unmoved. Note the direction this forces: the distributed view, normally the permissive one, must here be the most restrictive of the three, denying survival in a case where the narrative view affirms it.

Constraints:

- Ordinary setting: two people who knew the original intimately — a partner and a lifelong friend — each now carrying a model of them, in an otherwise ordinary household. Exactly one stipulated piece of technology is allowed, stated in no more than two sentences, and it must not be duplication, teleportation, brain-splitting, or exotic physics; no further departures from ordinary biology.
- The evidence distinguishing the two variants must be observable — what the carriers can and cannot do, and how they report it — and must contain no stipulation about who survives. Do not smuggle the verdicts into the setup.
- The two variants must share every detail except the isolated variable, and the variable must apply uniformly to both carriers.
- The case is for deciding whether the distributed view is a thesis about survival or only about influence: the design should force it to take one side. Keep that purpose in view when judging what counts as a fair test.

Deliverable. Open with your bottom line in one sentence — what the pair of cases establishes and which feature of the case is doing the work — then present, with no preamble and without restating this request: (a) the two variants, labeled, in under 350 words combined; (b) a table with the three views as rows and the two variants as columns, giving each verdict and a one-line reason; (c) for each of the six cells, why the verdict follows from that view's own commitments rather than from a stipulation, and, where it arises, whether the distributed view must count the distributed pattern as one self or as several; (d) an explicit statement of the isolated variable, and what the case collapses into if that variable is removed — that is, if the distributed view is made indifferent to first- versus third-personal tokening; and (e) the strongest objection a defender of the distributed view would raise against the third-personal variant, together with your judgment of whether the case survives it. No citations are needed; argue from the characterizations above.
