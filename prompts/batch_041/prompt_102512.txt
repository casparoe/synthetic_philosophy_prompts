So I never studied philosophy properly — did one year of a degree and dropped out, mostly I read blogs and argue with two friends who actually took the classes — and I've been chewing on the zombie argument for months. Here's the version I keep circling, and what worries me isnt the usual complaint. (1) If I can clearly and fully conceive of a way things could be, then that way things could be is genuinely possible — conceiving is a decent guide to possibility. (2) I can conceive of a world physically identical to this one, atom for atom, same brains, same behaviour, but nobody home — no inner experience, just machinery going through the motions. (3) So that world is possible. (4) If it's possible, then the physical facts don't settle whether there's consciousness, because here the physical facts are the same and consciousness is present. (5) So consciousness isnt just physical stuff. Honestly I like that conclusion and I'd rather not lose it. The problem is I can run the same move in the other direction and it makes me nervous. (6) I can also conceive of a world where the stuff we call water isnt H2O, or where the morning star isnt the evening star, or where I go back in time and stop my own grandparents from ever meeting. (7) By step (1), all of those would have to be possible too. (8) But they clearly arent — if water is H2O then it couldnt have been anything else, and the grandparent thing just cant happen, no matter how vividly I picture it. (9) So either (1) is false and the zombie argument never even gets off the ground, or (1) is true and I'm forced to swallow a pile of worlds I'm certain are impossible.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? I want you to name the step, explain why it looked so obviously fine when I wrote it down, and then price the options — if I give up (1) flat, what do I lose, and is there a way to keep both the zombie conclusion and my sanity about water and grandpa, or does one have to go? My friend says the repair is to only count so-called ideal conceiving, the kind that would survive knowing every fact, and that this lets you keep (1) for zombies while blocking the water and the grandparent cases. I cant tell whether that's a real fix or just relabelling the problem, so tell me which it is and what it commits me to. I'd rather keep (5) than (1) if I have to choose, but I want to know what I'm actually paying.
