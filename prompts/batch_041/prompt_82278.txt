I am to open next week's session of our reading group on cosmology and theism with a short written assessment, and I would like your help drafting it. The session concerns the fine-tuning argument, and two members have staked out positions by analogy.

The first argues as follows. A puddle that woke to find its outline exactly matching the hollow it lies in might conclude that the hollow was dug to fit it; we know better, because the water simply took the shape of whatever received it, and nothing calls for explanation. We are in the puddle's position, he says: the constants are the hollow and we are the water, and the impression that the universe was made to our measure is the puddle's vanity repeated at cosmic scale.

The second replies with a firing squad. If fifty marksmen shoot at your heart and every one of them misses, you are entitled to suspect the misses were arranged, and it will not do to answer that a survivor is exactly what someone in your position would observe, since that is true of every survivor, including the ones who were merely lucky. She says the puddle argument borrows its plausibility from a mechanism — something conforming to something else — that has no counterpart in the constants case, and that once you strip that out the puddle proves nothing.

I want an audit of the two analogies as arguments, not a literature review and not a verdict on whether God exists. In particular:

1. Give the correspondence table for each analogy — what plays the role of the puddle, the hollow, the pouring, the marksmen, the survivor, the intention to hit — and note which cells have no counterpart on the other side.

2. Say exactly where each mapping breaks. For the puddle, address the mechanism question directly: is there anything in the constants case that plays the role of water conforming to a hollow, and if the answer is observer selection rather than conformation, does the analogy still say what my first colleague wants it to say? For the firing squad, say what the inference to conspiracy needs as input — a prior over outcomes, an intention, a well-defined space of possibilities — and whether the constants case supplies it.

3. Separate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusion each analogy is used to license from the ones that are merely decorative. Both, I suspect, quietly presuppose that there is a well-defined measure over possible values of the constants, or over possible universes; say what each analogy looks like if no such measure is available, and whether either collapses.

4. Tell me whether either conclusion survives the disanalogies — the puddle's, that our finding the constants hospitable calls for no explanation, and the firing squad's, that it calls for one — and whether adding a multiverse repairs or destroys either.

5. If a better analogy is available for either side, give it and say what it fixes.

Write it as a piece I can read aloud and circulate: prose with the tables embedded, roughly a thousand words, no citations, reasoning from the structure of the analogies rather than from what commentators are on record as saying.
