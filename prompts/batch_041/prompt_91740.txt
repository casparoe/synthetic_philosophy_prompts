I am a high school student writing an essay on Cicero's dialogue On Old Age, in which Cato the Elder, speaking to the younger Scipio and Laelius, defends old age against the four complaints usually brought against it: that it withdraws a person from public affairs, that it weakens the body, that it cuts off sensual pleasure, and that it puts one on the threshold of death. The dialogue closes with Cato's reflections on whether the soul survives the body. What interests me is that Cato's defense rests on assumptions about the state, the family, medicine, and the individual's place in society that belong to the first century BCE, and that many of the hardest questions about old age are ones he could not have anticipated.

Please write me an essay in which you predict, as carefully as the text allows, what position Cato would take on four controversies that only arose long after Cicero's time. First, mandatory retirement ages and proposals to remove elderly holders of public office, on the theory that they are occupying positions the young need. Second, the practice of allocating scarce life-saving medical treatment partly by age, on the grounds that a younger patient has a stronger claim to a full span of years than someone who has already had most of one. Third, the proposal that aging itself should be treated as a disease to be slowed or reversed by drugs rather than accepted as a natural stage of life. Fourth, pension systems and other transfers that make the support of the old a collective obligation of the young rather than a matter for the household.

For each controversy, do three things: state the position the dialogue most naturally supports, identify which of Cato's arguments carry the weight and which cannot be transferred to the modern case, and point out where the modern question depends on premises about institutions or technology that the dialogue never confronts. Conclude by saying whether the position you have reconstructed is one you would defend today, and where you would depart from it. I am looking for a reasoned extrapolation rather than a summary of the dialogue, so please be explicit whenever you are inferring something rather than reporting it.
