Hofstadter's careenium — a table covered in tiny magnetic marbles, simms, which clump into clusters, simmballs, that then knock one another about — has what seems to me a live analogue in current interpretability research. A researcher trains a probe on a model's internal activations, finds a direction that decodes some concept, adds that direction back into the activations, watches the output shift accordingly, and reports that the model was representing the concept and that the representation drove what it said. The same researcher, pressed on whether such a model represents anything at all, replies that it is only arithmetic on vectors and that nothing at the level of concepts does any pushing; the field also distinguishes features a model merely makes decodable from features it actually uses. What commitments does this practice presuppose? Does it need the feature to be a genuine cause at the level of the pattern, or is it equally at home with the thought that only the arithmetic pushes and the feature is an inert summary — or with the intermediate view that there is no higher-level causation, only higher-level intervention, so that steering a feature is merely a tidy way of nudging the arithmetic? Reconstruct the implicit theory of levels, causes and explanation that the practice carries, separate what it strictly needs from what it merely suits, and say what would have to change in it if one took seriously the idea that only simms shove. Answer in no more than 350 words, in British English.
