Futarchy is the proposal that a polity should vote on values but use prediction markets to decide which policies best achieve those values. The debate between its advocates and critics has stalled: advocates treat conditional prediction markets as an engineering problem, while critics argue that the markets are either impossible to resolve or manipulable, or that the whole scheme falsely separates values from beliefs. I want a diagnosis, not a survey. Is this a substantive disagreement about mechanism design—whether decision markets can be made incentive-compatible and whether the selection problem can be solved (the worry that a market conditional on a policy being implemented cannot reveal the counterfactual outcome, because implementing the policy changes the market)—a conceptual disagreement about whether values can be separated from empirical beliefs, or a political disagreement about what makes a decision procedure legitimate? Take a definite position on which diagnosis is right, explain what each side actually commits to, and say what would count as progress given your diagnosis—a formal result, a change of question, or an argument that no progress should be expected. If you think the debate is a mix, say which element is doing the real work and why.
