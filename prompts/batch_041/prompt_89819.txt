A logician whose seminar I have been attending has, without ever stating a general theory, recorded the following verdicts on a sequence of truth-theoretic sentences. Let L be the sentence "This sentence is false." She judges that (1) L is neither true nor false; (2) the sentence "This sentence is true" is neither true nor false; (3) the pair of sentences A and B, where A says "B is false" and B says "A is false," are each neither true nor false; (4) the Curry sentence "If this sentence is true, then 2+2=5" is neither true nor false; (5) "2+2=4" is true and "2+2=5" is false; and (6) the sentence "This sentence is true and 2+2=4" is neither true nor false. Next week she is to give a verdict on S: "L is not true."

Predict her verdict on S. Then identify the theory of truth she appears to be running, and at least one rival theory that fits all six earlier verdicts just as well. State what each theory predicts about S, say which prediction you would bet on and why, and describe a further sentence -- not one of the six and not S -- that would best discriminate between the theories. I am less interested in a survey of the literature than in the inferential structure: what the track record underdetermines, and which new case would resolve it.
