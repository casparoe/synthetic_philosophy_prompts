I'm assembling a small set of jokes for a reading group that's working through the typogenetics material in Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, and I want one that will actually land for people who've absorbed the mechanics rather than just skimmed the chapter. The joke should turn on the real structure of the system. In typogenetics, a strand is a string over four bases; base-pairs are read off into a sequence of amino acids, and the amino acid sequence folds into an enzyme; the enzyme then operates on strands with actions like cop, cut, swi, mvr, mvl, and rpy — copying, cutting, switching, moving along the strand, reversing it. Hofstadter's central puzzle is whether a strand can replicate itself, and the resolution, as I understand it, is that it cannot do so directly: a strand produces a complementary strand, and the complementary strand's enzyme reconstructs the original, so the self-replicator is really a pair rather than a single strand. Please write the joke so that it lands on the tension between the strand's two roles — thing and description — or on the two-step, two-strand structure of replication, or on the indirectness of self-reference that both share. It should be funny to someone who knows the material, not merely to someone who knows that DNA is like a code. Write it in continuous prose, the way you'd tell it to a friend in an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold or tables.
