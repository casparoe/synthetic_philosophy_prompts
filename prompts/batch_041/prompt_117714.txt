I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background at all — did a maths degree years ago, mostly write Go now. I've been reading about Thomas Hobbes on the train, not the Leviathan social contract stuff, the weird stuff: apparently he was convinced he'd squared the circle, and he spent about twenty years getting shredded by the mathematicians of his day, especially a guy called Wallis. What I find interesting is that his complaints weren't only "you did the arithmetic wrong". He thought the whole modern way of doing geometry was rotten — too much symbol pushing and algebra, treating it as a game with letters, not enough actually building the figure and watching a point move. He wanted geometry grounded in motion and construction, he hated infinite quantities because he thought they were a back door for medieval scholasticism, and he was on principle against the Royal Society crowd because he didn't think you learn anything from experiments.

So here's what I want from you. Hobbes died in 1679. Predict what he'd have made of four things that happened after he was gone:

1. Non-Euclidean geometry — the discovery that you can drop the parallel postulate and still get a perfectly consistent geometry.
2. Algebra winning. Roughly the Hilbert-era view that maths is a formal game with symbols and axioms and doesn't have to be about anything.
3. Machine-checked proofs — proof assistants where a computer verifies every step, plus the famous theorems that only got proved with heavy computer help.
4. The calculus/analysis side of things, where everything is built on limits and infinite processes — the exact move he thought was a con.

For each one: your best guess at what he'd say, and — more useful to me — which of his actual commitments that guess is resting on. If a prediction only works because of his personality (contrarian, dogmatic, hates being told he's wrong) rather than because of his arguments, say so. If there's an item where he'd have to contradict himself to hold the view you're predicting, flag it. And where two of his commitments pull in opposite directions on the same question, dont paper over it — tell me which way you think he'd actually jump and why.
