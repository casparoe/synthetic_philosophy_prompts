ok so I'm the student rep on my school district's data & equity committee (I'm a junior, first time on anything like this, and honestly half the meetings go over my head). Next Thursday we vote on whether to approve the new student outcomes report the district data team wrote, and the fight is over the causal language in it. Here's the thing. They found that kids who ride the 7:10 bus route have way lower 9th grade pass rates than kids on other routes, even after controlling for the usual stuff. Group A on the committee says that's not a real cause, it's just a proxy — a bus route doesn't *do* anything to a kid, it's correlated with which families live where, and until you name the individual-level thing (sleep, attendance, whatever) you're not allowed to say the route causes anything. They say every social explanation has to bottom out in individual people's traits and choices or it's just storytelling. Group B says that's backwards, because some causes are relational — they're facts about how people are arranged relative to each other, not facts sitting inside any one person. Like, nothing about me changed when I became the only freshman in a class full of seniors; my *position* changed, and positions can be causes. And they say if you only allow individual-level causes you end up blaming kids for stuff the district did.

I have to write the committee's decision memo before Thursday. Can you help? You're a philosopher of social science who's spent years in the individualism vs. holism fight and can spot when someone is smuggling a metaphysical assumption in as a methodological rule. Please think carefully and don't just hand me the standard "it depends."

What I need in the memo: (1) a clear recommendation about what causal language the report is allowed to use, something I can actually vote on; (2) the best version of whichever side I end up rejecting — steelman the "it's just a proxy" people, because two of them are actual statisticians and they will definately shred me otherwise; (3) what the report should tell the district to *do*, with a timeframe; and (4) somewhere in there, deal with the objection that if you let "relational" causes count, the report can never be falsified, since you can always point at some arrangement.

Start by brainstorming both positions in your own words, then write the memo. Under 700 words, no citations or reading lists, and go easy on the words "holism" and "individualism" — my committee will tune out.
