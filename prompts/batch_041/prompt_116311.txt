The two theses we're fighting about:

L (luminosity): if you're in a given mental state, you're in a position to know that you're in it. Feeling cold, being in pain, that sort of thing.

KK: if you know that p, you're in a position to know that you know that p.

The standard anti-luminosity argument, as I understand it (Williamson, I think — there's a cold-feeling example with a margin of about a degree, though the details don't matter much): suppose you know you're cold. Knowledge has to be robust — you can't know something if you'd have believed it anyway in cases that are very close by but where it isn't true. But feeling cold is a matter of degree, and you could be a hair warmer, with the same bodily evidence, and not be cold while still believing you were. So you didn't know after all, and since the same move works wherever you start, no state comes out luminous.
