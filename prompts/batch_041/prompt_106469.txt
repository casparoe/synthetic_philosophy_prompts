I am convening a seventy-five-minute session of an analytic epistemology reading group. The participants are faculty and graduate students who are fluent in the Gettier problem, reliabilism, virtue epistemology, and the internalism/externalism debate, but who have never read any Nyāya text and who will not have done preparatory reading. I want you to design the teaching plan for the session, not to deliver the exposition itself. The topic is Nyāya epistemology and the pramāṇas.

The session must not be a survey of the four pramāṇas. It should be organized around a single conceptual pivot: the distinction between pramāṇa as the instrumental cause or means of a knowledge episode, pramā as the resultant cognition, and pramātṛ as the knower. The participants will arrive assuming that a pramāṇa is simply a source of justified true belief, and they will therefore hear the four pramāṇas as a list of faculties. Your plan should make the instrument/result distinction do the work of preventing that assimilation. It should also bring in two further ideas that the group will not know: first, that Nyāya treats memory, smṛti, as not a separate pramāṇa; and second, that a pramāṇa must issue in niścaya, a settled ascertainment, rather than merely a true belief, and that it is defeated by doṣa, a defect in the instrument or the knower. These are the materials you may assume; you should not need to introduce any other technical vocabulary.

Please give me:

1. The order in which the components must be introduced, and why each later component depends on the earlier ones. I want the dependency structure, not a topic list.

2. The one carrying example that should run through the whole session. It must not be the stock fire-on-the-hill inference. It should be a case in which the pramāṇa is contested, so that the instrument/result distinction and the defect condition can both be exercised. State what the example must have and what it must lack, and explain why it can carry the load.

3. The misconceptions you predict the group will form, in the order they will form them, and exactly where in the sequence each should be headed off. At minimum, address the assimilation of pramāṇa to justified true belief, the temptation to treat testimony as reducible to perception and inference, the reading of upamāna as mere analogical reasoning, and the assumption that Nyāya is a version of reliabilism.

4. One question I can put to the group near the end that would show whether they have grasped the instrument/result distinction and the exclusion of memory, rather than merely memorized the four labels.

5. What I should explicitly leave out at this level, and how I should say so, given that the group will ask about the sixteen categories, the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika metaphysics, the Buddhist polemics, and the technical conditions for vyāpti.

Do not give me readings, citations, or a bibliography. The plan should be self-contained and should not depend on the group having any text in front of them.
