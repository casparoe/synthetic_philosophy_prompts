Ok so I'm a third-year PhD student working on cumulative cultural evolution — basically trying to explain how you get ratchet-like improvements in toolkits and norms without smuggling in a genius, or some "culture instinct" that just does the work for free. My commitee is split on it. My main advisor keeps going on about Dennett's cranes vs skyhooks and warning me off greedy reductionism: cranes being explanations built out of dumb, incremental, already-available processes, skyhooks being explanations that get the fancy result by fiat. My second reader says that if I keep positing "cultural inheritance" as its own level of explanation, I'm hanging the whole thing off a skyhook and just calling it a mechanism.

The thing I'm actually worried about is what I've started calling skyhook laundering: where something that does no real explanatory work gets dressed up as a crane by gesturing at a plausible-looking process — a transmission bias, a learning mechanism, a selection pressure — that never gets spelled out enough to actually carry the weight. And the flip side, where someone calls an honest multi-level explanation "greedy reductionism" just because it refuses to stop at their favorite level.

Please describe the most important implications of the crane/skyhook distinction, together with the greedy reductionism charge, for how I should build and write up a theory like mine. I'd rather get the deep/structural implications than a list of writing tips. Also, if it matters: what would count as a "crane receipt" — the thing you'd have to actually produce to show that a step was taken by dumb incremental means rather than assumed?
