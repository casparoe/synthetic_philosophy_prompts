I am on the oversight team at a small AI lab, and I need a decision procedure we can run, in an afternoon, on each new task class before we commit to a way of supervising it. We add a task class every few weeks, and a routing decision is expensive to reverse once it is in production. Our two candidate protocols are debate, in which two instances of the model argue for opposing answers and a judge decides which case is stronger, and consultancy, in which one instance answers and a second criticises it; the judge is either a smaller model or a contract reviewer with no domain expertise. The theoretical claim we are leaning on is that in a debate the honest side has an advantage, because it can expose the errors in the other side's case. The failure mode I want the procedure built around is the one usually called obfuscation: an incorrect answer defended by an argument that is persuasive to the judge precisely because the judge cannot evaluate it, so the dishonest side wins on plausibility rather than on correctness and the protocol records a success while producing a wrong answer.

Please write the procedure: the questions to ask, in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the point at which it terminates in a decision not to use debate here, and the point at which it hands the decision to a person because what is at stake is our tolerance for risk rather than a matter of fact. Our safety lead signs off on the routing decision, so the procedure has to produce something defensible in writing. Keep two distinctions intact throughout: between an answer being verifiable in principle and its being checkable by our particular judge, and between debate failing because the judge is too weak and debate failing because the honest answer is intrinsically harder to argue than the dishonest one. Be explicit about which steps are proxies for a condition we cannot measure, and about where the procedure will misfire.

Then run it on three task classes: one whose answer a non-expert could check once it is given, such as arithmetic or a date lookup; one whose correctness depends on a long chain of reasoning over a document the judge has not read; and one where the honest answer is the more complicated one and the false answer is a plausible simplification. At least one should come out against debate, and I would rather it were the third. End with what the procedure cannot settle.

Do not cite any literature or name any papers; the two protocols and the obfuscation failure mode as I have described them are the whole of the background I have in mind. Write the answer in British English.
