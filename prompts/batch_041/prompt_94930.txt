I'm in this very disorganized reading group where half of us are historians and half are political theorists, and we keep going in circles about how old nations actually are. Everyone reaches for their favorite example — medieval England, revolutionary France, ancient Israel, Armenia, Iceland, China — and then someone else says that's not a nation, that's an ethnic group, or a state, or just a nineteenth-century invention projected backwards. I've read enough Hobsbawm and Anderson and Smith to know the usual moves, but I want to stop argueing contender-by-contender and first get clear on what a theory of national antiquity is even supposed to deliver. So write me the desiderata: what any adequate account of how old nations are — or of how nations emerge from premodern ethnic, imperial, or religious communities — has to be able to do. Not a list of authors or readings; I want the requirements, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. Which cases must it get right: the ones that look continuous, the ones that are obviously modern, the ones where nationalism came before any mass national identity, the ones with no state at all? Which distinctions must it not collapse: nation vs. state vs. ethnie, elite proto-nationalism vs. mass national consciousness, invented vs. reconstructed vs. genuinely continuous, civic vs. ethnic? What findings from medieval or early modern history, demography, or the history of communication can it not contradict? And flag which of these requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp — modernists, ethnosymbolists, perennialists, primordialists, whatever labels you prefer.

Most importantly, tell me if some subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable. I suspect their's a tension between 'nations are modern' and 'national identity is continuous with older ethnic identities,' and I want to know whether that's a real contradiction or just a terminological mess. Then, briefly, how do the main families of views do against the list — but keep that part short, the criteria are what I care about.
