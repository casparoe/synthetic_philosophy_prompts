Construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. The setting is a prehistoric settlement for which no written records survive, so the only evidence is the material assemblage: the layout of structures, the distribution of artifacts across those structures, and the depositional contexts of the artifacts. You are to invent two interpretive frameworks, which I will call the depositional-meaning framework and the institutional-material framework. The depositional-meaning framework holds that recurring, non-utilitarian patterns in the archaeological record are best explained as intentional symbolic communication, so that such patterns provide direct evidence of shared beliefs. The institutional-material framework holds that those same patterns are best explained as the material residue of routinized institutional practices, so that they provide direct evidence of organizational complexity rather than of belief. Your task is to construct a single case in which both frameworks are equally consistent with the same material assemblage but yield opposite conclusions about whether the settlement had a centralized religious authority. The case must isolate a single variable: the two frameworks should diverge because of one specific feature of the assemblage, while every other feature is either held fixed or neutral between the frameworks. The scenario itself should be one paragraph long and should be realistic rather than science-fictional. The purpose of the case is to test whether the material record alone can adjudicate between belief and institution when no texts survive, so the verdicts must be generated by the frameworks rather than stipulated.

After the scenario, do the following in continuous prose. First, state what each framework infers from the assemblage and why. Second, identify the one feature of the case that is doing the work, and explain why removing that feature would collapse the disagreement. Third, say how a defender of the framework that comes out badly in your case would attack the case, and what would need to be true for that attack to succeed. Do not cite any sources, do not refer to any actual archaeological sites or real theories by name, and do not ask for or provide a bibliography. Your entire response must be continuous prose: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables.
