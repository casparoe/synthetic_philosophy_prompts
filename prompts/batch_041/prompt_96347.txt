My mum gets home care from an agency and I've been sitting in on some visits. The carer clocks in on an app and ticks off boxes — bathing, dressing, meds, meals, toileting, they call it activities of daily living — and she gets paid per box and is usually out the door in half an hour. The company's leaflet says the whole point is that mum is treated as a person and not a list of tasks, and honestly most of the carers do seem to actually care. But there's no box for sitting and talking, or for noticing she's frightened about something, and then they email me a survey asking me to rate how dignified the visit was from one to five. So what is this setup really assuming care is? That it's the tasks getting done and the warm bit is a bonus? Or could you run exactly the same system while believing care is mostly the relationship, and the form is just an awkward way of getting paid? I'd like to know which parts of it commit them to a view and which bits are just admin, and what you'd have to tear up if you took the relationship view seriously — the form, the per-box pay, the survey, or the whole idea of sending a different carer every week.
