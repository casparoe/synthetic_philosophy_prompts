I'm a final-year politics student at a European university, currently taking a course on comparative political thought, and I have been asked to give the closing toast at a farewell dinner for a cohort of American exchange students who have spent the year here. The room will be half European students, half American, plus a few faculty from both sides, and the word liberal has already caused a few awkward moments this term. Please write the toast. It should be warm and personal rather than a lecture, but it should also do real conceptual work: explain how liberal can mean something close to market-friendly or even libertarian in much of Europe, especially in the German ordoliberal tradition, where the state is supposed to actively police competition rather than leave markets alone, while in American political speech it usually names a center-left coalition associated with civil rights, social provision, and cultural openness, and is often used by opponents as an insult. The speech should help both halves of the room hear the word differently without anyone feeling corrected, include one concrete moment where the two meanings collide, and end by turning that ambiguity into a genuine compliment to the exchange program. Five minutes, please.
