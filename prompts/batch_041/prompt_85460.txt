ok so this is for the last session of a reading group thats spent the whole term on the frame problem — the AI one, where a system has to work out which of its beliefs an action leaves alone, and it turns out "nothing else changes" is either an infinite list or a lie. i want to close the term with a poem.

what i'm asking for: a sestina whose six end-words are all things that just sit there and don't change while the poem's action happens around them. the form has to *be* the problem. the six keep coming back in a different order every stanza, which is what a fact does when the world moves around it, and you're not allowed to introduce a seventh, ever, however much the poem wants to. the envoi is where it has to cram all six into three lines — that's the one-breath version of what a frame axiom is supposed to do, and it should feel like compression, not like a summary.

one twist: one of the six, which at first sounds like it just sits there, should turn out to be something the action actually does change. let it flip mid-poem rather than announce itself; the line should land differently the second time you meet it. and somewhere in there the plan should fail for a boring reason nobody bothered to write down — the kettle was never plugged in, the door was already locked — and the poem should treat that as one more line that has to fit the meter, not as a plot event.

voice: your call, but i'd love it if the speaker were one of the six — a fact that keeps getting reasserted while everything else moves. and it should register, without ever saying it, that the real trouble isn't listing the things that stay the same, it's knowing which of them are even relevant to what just happened.

don't name the frame problem, don't use the jargon (no "axiom", no "non-monotonic", no "inertia"). someone who knows the problem should recongise it from the shape of the thing; someone who doesn't should just get a good poem about a room.

start straight in with the poem — no title, no preface, nothing before it. if you want to add two or three sentences after about which end-word was doing the most work, thats fine, put it after.
