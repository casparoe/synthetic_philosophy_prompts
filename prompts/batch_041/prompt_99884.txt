I'm designing a quiz for a philosophy of mind seminar that sorts people by which position on dreams they actually hold. Write eight concrete cases, each with two or three answer options, and a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least five positions: dreams are experiences (Dennett-style); dreams are not experiences but retrospective judgments (Malcolm-style); dreams are experiences only when signal-verified or lucid; dreams are offline simulations that are experiences but not genuine memories; and dreams are confabulated narratives with no phenomenal core. No case should simply ask whether dreams are experiences; each should be answerable without knowing the labels. Build the cases around four distinctions: whether a dream report is treated as a report or a reconstruction; whether sleep-lab evidence can overturn the sleeper's later testimony; whether lucidity is a difference in kind or only in degree; and whether a dream experience must be available to the waking subject. Include at least one case with a prearranged eye-movement signal, one where the dream report contradicts the lab record, one where the dream content matches an external stimulus the sleeper denies hearing, and one false-lucid case where the sleeper claims lucidity but the prearranged signal is absent. Flag any positions your quiz cannot separate, and note any answer combination that corresponds to no defensible position.
