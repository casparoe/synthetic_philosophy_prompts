I am a second-year PhD student in archaeology, and I need a proposal of roughly 2,500 words for a project that will occupy one reading term and one writing term. My half-formed idea is about a step that site reports take constantly and almost never license.

When an excavator moves from a description of objects — their forms, their arrangement, their depositional context — to a claim about what people believed, or about what institutions they had, the move is carried by something unstated. I suspect it is carried by two different things, and that the difference matters. Call the first an expressive inference: a claim about belief requires that someone chose to leave a trace that expresses the belief, and the trace's meaning depends on conventions we cannot independently check. Call the second a coordinative inference: a claim about an institution rests on the fact that institutions require many people to act together, so they leave redundant traces whether or not anyone meant to leave them. If that is right, institutions should be more reliably reconstructible than beliefs, even though beliefs are, in some sense, the smaller facts.

The question I want to pose precisely: is this asymmetry between expressive and coordinative traces a real feature of the archaeological record, or an artifact of how excavators habitually write? And if it is real, under what conditions is an inference to belief safe rather than a leap?

Turn this into a proposal. State the question so that it has a determinate answer. Explain why the standard ways of thinking about archaeological inference leave it open — I have in mind how the field handles ethnographic analogy and preservation bias, but identify the gap yourself rather than reciting a debate. Say what the method would be: I expect the project to be conceptual rather than fieldwork, so say what the conceptual work actually consists in and what would keep it from collapsing into wordplay. Describe what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing. Give an honest assessment of whether one term of reading and one of writing is enough, and if not, what you would cut. Do not include a bibliography; I will add citations myself.
