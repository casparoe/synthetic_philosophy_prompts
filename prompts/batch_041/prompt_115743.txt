A few of us started this monthly writing thing where each person has to parody a genre and everyone else guesses the target, and this month I drew "corporate sustainability report" (a friend of mine works in ESG compliance and keeps forwarding me these ghastly PDFs, so that's where the idea came from). What I want to aim the satire at is the doing/allowing distinction -- not the trolley stuff everyone is sick of, but the way the distinction actually earns its keep in corporate and policy moral accounting: offset markets, "we're not the ones doing it, our supplier is," the fact that the law is very keen to hold you liable for a push and mostly declines to make you perform a rescue, and all the restructuring that converts a doing into an allowing without changing a single downstream outcome. So: write me the opening 700-900 words of the annual sustainability report of a fictional firm that has industrialised that gap. Give it a name of your choosing and build it around four products/instruments: (1) Allowance Credits, tradable units licensed to permit a harm rather than perform it, which is why they're cheap and why the firm sells so many; (2) a "Redescription Desk" whose job is to restate a client's conduct as an omission (stopped maintaining the dam, discontinued the inspection line, decommissioned the safety program) and which is presented in the report as a service, not a euphemism; (3) Side-Effect Certification, sold with a proprietary multi-condition test that reclassifies a foreseen harm as a foreseen-but-unintended one; and (4) Bystander Assurance, priced off the fact that no one can make you act. Include a small deadpan price table, a "Stakeholder Q&A," and one footnote. The bit I most want: a trading desk that quotes a live exchange rate between doings and allowings -- the report should mention the spot rate the way a real report mentions a commodity price, casually, in passing, as if everyone already knows what it means.

Tone rules: deadpan all the way through, no winking, no narrator stepping out to say "this is absurd because..." -- the absurdity has to sit inside the jargon. Don't name any real company, regulator, or person, and don't put any real quotation in quote marks (invented terms and slogans are fine). Include one dissenting internal voice -- an ethics or "moral assurance" officer whose memo is excerpted -- but the report must absorb her objection as a line item and carry on, which is the joke. Get the philosophy right, so that someone who teaches this stuff laughs instead of wincing: the double-effect conditions, the fact that the distinction wobbles under redescription, the causal-vs-moral-responsibility slide, the but-for test, the asymmetry where you have duties once you've started, or as a parent or spouse, or where you created the peril. About 800 words, headings and all, and it should be legible to someone who's never read any of the literature -- they should finish it thinking "huh, that's sort of how it works" and then feel slightly ill about that.
