so I've got this friend who's been living in the decision theory salt mines for like a decade, and I'm trying to predict what she'll say about a case I've been chewing on. Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it:

- Counterfactual mugging, standard Omega version: she pays the $100.
- Acausal trade with a distant civ whose decisions are correlated with hers through a shared decision procedure: she trades.
- A message from a random alien species that has no causal or decision-theoretic connection to her, saying give us a coin or we burn a galaxy: she ignores it and refuses.
- A one-shot causal blackmailer with a real gun and no reputation effects: she refuses to pay.
- A case where she can precommit to a policy that deters a whole class of future blackmailers: she precommits.

Now the new case. I've been reading alot about surrogate goals — the trick where two agents who might end up in a threat standoff each adopt a goal neither of them actually cares about, and route the whole conflict through that goal. So instead of give me a coin or I torture you, the threatener says: give me a coin or I'll make the number of dust specks in a particular empty galaxy odd. It doesn't care about dust specks. The threat is now cheap for it to carry out, so it's credible, and it's harmless to you, so you can punish it by making the number even. Nobody gets tortured either way.

So here's the case: an acausal threatener that is running a copy of my friend's own decision algorithm — a genuine mirror, not just a correlated stranger — makes exactly this surrogate-goal threat. Give a coin, or it flips the dust-speck parity bit. What does my friend say?

I can see at least a few rules fitting her earlier verdicts that come apart here. One is treat decision-correlated agents as if they were causally present, so comply with the mirror's threat the same way you'd comply with a correlated trade. Another is never move in response to a threat, only in response to an offer, no matter how cheap or harmless the threat is. A third is only real harms count, so a surrogate threat is just noise. Which one is she actually running? Give me your best guess, the reasoning, and the further case that would tell the rules apart.
