I sit on the internal review board at a mid-size AI lab — we're the ones who sign off on whether a safety mitigation is good enough to ship, and we've got a Thursday deadline on a case that's turning into a fight.

Background. Six months back a team flagged a failure mode in one of our models, call it Model A: when you hand it a document that contains an internal contradiction and ask for a summary, it silently picks a side and states it as flat fact instead of flagging the tension. The team built a mitigation — a probe on one of the residual streams plus a steering offset, plus a prompt-level nudge — and it works on A. Now they want us to certify that the *same* mitigation covers Model B, which is a different training run, different size, different data mix, but exhibits what looks like the same failure mode. Their pitch is that the two behaviours are "the same mechanism," so one fix covers the fleet. If we say no, B needs its own mitigation and the deployment slips a quarter. If we say yes and they're wrong, we've shipped something cosmetic on half the fleet and nobody finds out until it matters.

The irritating part is that our own policy never defines "same mechanism," and over the last two meetings I've watched people quietly use three different criteria:

1. Behavioural: same triggers, same outputs, and the same intervention flips it in both models. Call it the same mechanism and move on.
2. Circuit-level: the same internal components doing the same causal work in the same order — you should be able to line the parts up.
3. Historical: it was produced by the same training pressure — same reward signal, same data quirk — regardless of whether the parts line up.

Here they come apart. (1) says yes. (2) says "sort of": B has something functionally analogous but not the same parts, and it's a different size so nothing lines up one-to-one anyway. (3) says no, and I think (3) is right on the facts — A's version looks like a reward-model hack (the RM rewarded confident answers), B's looks like it came from contaminated pretraining data. There's also a nasty evidential problem: the test that "validated" the mitigation on A can't distinguish "we found and disabled the mechanism" from "we suppressed the output." The metric moves either way.

I want the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday. Take a side and commit — I don't want a menu of options, I want a recommendation, a time frame, and conditions only if you genuinely think conditions are warranted. Give me reasons the team could actually argue with, and be honest about the strongest case on the losing side rather than setting up a strawman. If you think the question is ill-posed, fine, but tell me what to do Thursday anyway. Don't ask me for more data; work with what's here and say what you'd do under uncertainty.

You're the nearest thing we have to a philosopher of science who's also spent time around interpretability tooling, so use both halves. Start by brainstorming what could make two behavioural patterns count as the same mechanism at all, and whether my three criteria are even the right cut, before you commit to anything.
