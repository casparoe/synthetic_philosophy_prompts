I teach a graduate seminar on cultural evolution, and I have grown weary of watching the Darwinian account of culture be argued case by case: one party points to an idea that spread like a virus, another to a tradition that every generation plainly rebuilds from scratch, and because no one has said in advance what the theory is supposed to deliver, the exchange settles nothing. Before I assign further reading, I want the specification itself written out.

Set down the desiderata that any adequate theory of culture-as-a-Darwinian-process must meet. I mean the full list: the phenomena it is obliged to explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it may not collapse, and the established findings it may not contradict, each with the consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference. Among the phenomena I would insist on: the cumulative, ratchet-like growth of complexity that no other species' social learning seems to achieve; the speed of cultural change relative to genetic change; the persistence of traits that appear to harm their bearers; the recurrence of similar traditions in unconnected populations; and the fact that much transmission looks reconstructive rather than faithful. Among the cases I want it to handle, take a pair I keep returning to: a practice reproduced with high fidelity by people who can give no reason for it, and a belief that spreads because each new holder re-derives it from a shared cognitive disposition, with no copying in the ordinary sense at all. A theory must say what distinguishes these, and whether both fall under one explanatory mechanism or two.

Then do the parts I actually care about. Mark which of your requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely commitments of a particular camp, and say why you classify each as you do. Flag any subset that looks jointly unsatisfiable, and be explicit about the tension you find most serious — I am particularly interested in whether a Darwinian account can demand high-fidelity replication while also respecting the evidence of reconstruction, and whether cumulative cultural evolution requires discrete, particulate units of the kind genetic selection presupposes. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. You may close with a brief assessment of how replication-based memetics, dual-inheritance accounts, and cultural attraction theory fare against your list, but keep that short; the criteria are the deliverable, and I would rather have them argued than the scorecard filled in. No citations or reading lists, please; refer to positions and arguments by description.
