A friend of mine who reads too much complexity-theory blogging has been pushing this on me for weeks and I genuinely can't tell if it's sharp or just confused. He calls it the No-Shortcut argument:

(1) A belief is justified only if it came from a process that tracks the truth -- one that would land on the truth across the relevant range of cases, not just happen to be right this time.
(2) For a large enough hypothesis space, finding the hypothesis that best fits your evidence -- or even getting within a decent factor of the best one -- is intractable in the worst case: NP-hard, #P-hard for the Bayesian version, exponential in general.
(3) A physical system like a brain only gets polynomially many steps before it has to answer.
(4) So no brain-implementable process can solve the general problem; it can only guess.
(5) A process that guesses on the general problem isn't a truth-tracker.
(6) So none of our beliefs is justified.
(7) And since "justified" is doing all the work in epistemology, that means there's no fact of the matter about what we ought to believe -- epistemic norms are just stories we tell about our guesses.

I feel like (7) is obviously too much, but every step up to it looks fine and I keep failing to name the one that breaks. He says the only honest move is to redefine justification as "justified relative to your computational budget," then cheerfully admits that's a different concept wearing the old word's clothes. So: which premise do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? I especially want to know whether the fix is just the worst-case/average-case point -- that NP-hardness says *some* instances are hard, not the ones we actually meet -- or whether that's a cop-out that just relocates the problem into "the relevant range of cases" in (1), where it was undefined all along. Don't tell me both are fine; pick the step you'd reject and price it.

And tell me what would change your mind -- what argument or result would push you toward a different diagnosis, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail. I'd rather you flag the load-bearing spot than sound sure.
