I am a high school senior completing an independent study in epistemology and philosophy of science, and I have been trying to teach myself Bayesian updating. I understand ordinary conditionalization well enough: when I learn a proposition for certain, I replace my prior probability for each hypothesis with its prior conditional probability given that proposition. What I cannot get a clear intuitive grip on is the rule for uncertain evidence, usually called Jeffrey conditionalization, and I would like an explanation that is more conceptual than formal. Could you explain what problem the rule is meant to solve, how it differs from ordinary conditionalization, and why one might think it, rather than some rival, is the right response when the evidence itself comes in degrees?

To make this concrete, imagine a high school science-fair project in which a cheap color sensor reports either red or green, and calibration suggests it is correct about eighty percent of the time. The sensor reports red, and I have no independent reason to think the ball is more likely red than green. Please work through that case in detail: what ordinary conditionalization would have me do if I treated the report as certain, what Jeffrey conditionalization would have me do instead, and why the two answers come apart. Then explain the rigidity condition, the assumption that my conditional probabilities given each possible evidential input remain unchanged, and why it is both necessary for the rule and philosophically controversial. I am especially interested in what happens when the evidence does not arrive as a clean partition of propositions I already recognize, or when the sensor's report also tells me something about the sensor's own reliability, which I have seen called higher-order evidence, so that the rigidity condition seems to fail. Finally, please say whether Jeffrey conditionalization commutes when I receive a sequence of uncertain reports, and give a case where two intuitively plausible ways of handling uncertain evidence yield different verdicts. I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want to understand the issue well enough to explain it to my classmates.
