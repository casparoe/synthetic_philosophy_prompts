I am building a seminar unit and a companion policy essay that treat the fall of the Roman Republic as a model for how contemporary democracies erode, and I want both organized around the mechanisms that are genuinely load-bearing rather than the ones that merely supply vivid anecdotes. Please rank the candidate mechanisms — the erosion of elite norms and unwritten restraints, the professionalization of the army and the rise of client armies, the tribunate as a personal mobilizing platform, the land question and the fate of the urban poor, and the legal and geographic overextension of citizenship — by how much each earns its place in an analogy to a modern democracy. I am especially interested in whether the self-enforcing-equilibrium picture of constitutions (stable only while no faction both wants and is able to defect) does real analytic work here or merely redescribes the collapse, and whether that verdict reorders the ranking. State the criteria you are ranking by, which mechanisms are upstream of which, what would move each up or down, and which familiar mechanisms you would drop.
