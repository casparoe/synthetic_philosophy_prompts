Ive been in a reading group on Wittgenstein's private language argument for about three months now and we've completely stalled out, and at this point I want to know what kind of disagreement we're actually in rather than another round of the same fight.

Heres the shape of it. One camp says the argument works: it kills off the idea that you could give a word a meaning by pointing at your own sensation and letting memory be the only check on whether you're applying it correctly. The other camp says the target is a straw man: nobody ever thought a private word was licensed by a bare inner act with memory as its only standard, because the Cartesian being attacked would say the sensation is right there present to the mind and that's what makes the application correct, so the whole reductio lands on a caricature, and the fact that the caricature is incoherent tells us nothing about the real position. Then there's a third group in the room who say the question of whether it refutes anything is confused from the start, because the remarks aren't an argument at all, they're reminders about how we use words, so asking what they refute is like asking what a joke refutes. We also have one person who insists the whole thing was never about sensations and is really about rules and meaning in general, which everyone else finds either obvious or irrelevant depending on the week.

My own suspicion, and I want you to tell me if it's right, is that the first two camps are answering different questions under one name: one is about whether meaning can be grounded in something only one person can get at, the other is about whether inner states can be identified and known without public criteria. And I think the phrase private language is quietly doing at least four jobs: a language only one person happens to speak, a language only one person could in principle understand, a language whose words name private objects, and a language whose rules only one person can check. If that's where the stall lives, say so, and tell me which sense each camp actually needs.

A friend threw a case at us that nobody could handle: two people, twins say, who share a vocabulary for a sensation that no one else can have or check. Is that a private language or not? If the argument forbids it, then the real target is uncheckability rather than one-person-ness, and both camps have the target wrong. If it permits it, then the argument is narrower than either camp thinks. I'd like that worked through rather than waved at.

So: what kind of disagreement is this really, and what would count as progress here, a distinction, a concession from one side, or an admission that the question is ill-posed? And please keep the settled parts separate from the parts that people who actually work on this fight about. Don't hand me one reading as if it were the consensus.
