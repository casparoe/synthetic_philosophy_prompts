I teach a grad seminar on Schopenhauer and the arts, and I keep hitting the same wall: I can't say what he thinks music *is* without either going all misty or just ventriloquizing him. Some background on me: I did a music degree before I defected to philosophy, played cello seriously for years, so when I actually think about music I think in the working vocabulary — tonal function, tension and resolution, voice leading, counterpoint, the overtone series, consonance and dissonance, motivic development, and the Schenker-ish layer talk (foreground/middleground/background, the Ursatz, prolongation). Thats my native tongue for music. Schopenhauer is a language I read fluently and speak badly.

Here's the position, in my own words so we're aligned before you start: for him the other arts copy the Ideas — they objectify the Will only at one remove, by giving us the stable patterns of thinghood — but music skips the Ideas and is a copy of the Will itself, the striving underneath everything. Which is why it doesn't depict this or that joy or grief; it gives joy and grief as such, the inner movement stripped of any object. Melody he ranks at the top, the objectification of the Will at its most human and most conscious, and he lines the lower voices up with the lower grades of nature — the bass standing in for the inorganic, the inner parts for plant and animal life, melody for human striving. And then the line I can never do anything with: that if you could cash music out into concepts, you'd have a philosophy of the world.

What I want: restate that as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I just described, and then give me the residue report. What carries over cleanly? What do I have to fudge or distort to make it fit? What has no counterpart at all in how I talk about music? And I want your honest call on which way the failure runs — is the leftover just the mysticism any sane theorist would drop, or is my toolkit genuinely missing something he was pointing at, some way of saying music is *about* the Will rather than about feelings or about pure structure? Don't let the translation cheat: don't quietly import music theory's own assumptions (that a piece is a coherent structure, that analysis is the real description) and then call that "translating" him. If his claim only comes through once you smuggle in something I don't actually believe, say so — that's the interesting part.
