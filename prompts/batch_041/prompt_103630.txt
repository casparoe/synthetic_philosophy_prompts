So some backstory, because I think it changes what I want here: I'm the guy in my friend group who reads all the rationalist blogs, which means I'm the designated explainer whenever anything from that world comes up, and honestly I've started to resent the job. Last week two friends got into it — one of them has been mainlining the infinite ethics stuff, the whole business where if the future can be infinitely good or infinitely bad then every finite thing you do seems to have infinite expected value, or undefined expected value, depending on how you keep the books — and the other one thinks it's obviously a word game. I tried to referee and made both of them mad, largely because I kept saying things like "well it depends whether you're allowed to multiply an infinitesimal by an infinity" in a tone I had not earned. I want to outsource this. Also I should say I'm not neutral, I think the infinite-expected-value move is doing real work and also being abused, and I can't tell which letter I'm in.

What I actually want from you: write me an agony-aunt advice column — Dear Prudence, except the columnist has a PhD in philosophy and takes the questions with total, unblinking seriousness — with two perfectly ordinary reader letters (one person torn between a weekend volunteering and their cousin's wedding; another asking whether they can bail on helping a friend move because they might donate the money instead) and the columnist's replies. Around 600-700 words, letters included. The comedy has to come from the actual machinery and not from invented nonsense, so: the columnist should genuinely separate the case of an infinite payoff with ordinary probabilities from the case of an infinitesimal probability with a finite payoff (and note that the second is where the trouble usually lives); should notice that two infinities don't obviously compare, then proceed as if they do anyway; should gesture at the fanaticism objection and never actually resolve it; and should keep sliding between "every option here has the same infinite expected value, so it doesn't matter what you do" and "obviously the wedding matters infinitely more," in a way that's only internally consistent under some very strong assumptions about cardinal comparison. A deadpan aside about whether an infinite hell outweighs an infinite heaven, answered confidently and then quietly undercut, would be great, and if the columnist drops Pascal's mugging or the St. Petersburg game as though everyone obviously knows them, even better. My hope is that someone who actually works on infinite ethics reads it and laughs rather than winces, and that my two friends read it and feel seen instead of dunked on. No citations or further reading — it has to stand on its own, and I'll be honest, I'll definately be sending it to both of them.
