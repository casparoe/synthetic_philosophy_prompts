For a final paper in my seminar on promissory obligation, I am comparing two accounts of why promises bind. On the first, which I will call the convention view, a promise binds because the promisor has voluntarily invoked a shared social practice whose rules constitute the act of promising; the obligation comes from the practice itself, and from the fairness of accepting its benefits, rather than from any particular person's expectations. On the second, the assurance view, a promise binds because the promisor has deliberately led the promisee to expect a certain performance and to arrange his affairs around it; the wrong of breaking it is the wrong of betraying an assurance one has intentionally created.

The contrast I keep seeing used to separate them is the unheard promise, a promise the promisee never learns of, which the convention view can treat as binding and the assurance view cannot. I do not trust that case to carry the weight it is asked to carry, because a defender of either view can redescribe it. What I want instead is the reverse: a case in which the convention view must say that no promissory obligation was ever generated, while the assurance view must say that one was generated and still binds. I have not found this constructed anywhere, and I would like you to build it.

Specifications:

1. Ordinary life, one paragraph, and realistic: a family, a workplace, a hospital. No science fiction, no exotic devices, no thought-experiment machinery, and nobody who talks like a philosopher.

2. The promise must be sincere, voluntary, welcome to the promisee, and free of coercion, deception, or any misrepresentation by the promisor. The promisor must not be at fault in any respect I could point to. In particular, the reason the convention view withholds an obligation must lie inside the practice's own rules, in who counts as competent to perform the act or in what the act can be about, and not in changed circumstances, impossibility, or the wickedness of the content.

3. The promisee must end up no worse off than he was before the promise. I want the obligation, if there is one, to be owed because of the assurance itself rather than because anyone was damaged; if the case can be reread as a claim for compensation, it does not do what I need it to do.

4. Keep the law out of it. What a court would enforce is a different question, and an answer that leans on it will have cheated.

5. Do not reuse the standard cases: the dying promisee, the promise made as a joke or inside a game, the promise to meet for lunch, the coerced promise, the promise to someone who never learns of it.

After the scenario, please do the following.

- State the verdict each of the two views must give, and show that each falls out of that view's own rationale rather than being stipulated by me.
- Add a third view from the same debate, the one on which promising hands the promisee a right to demand performance, or a different one if that fits the case better, and say which side it lands on and why. If it splits from both, so much the better.
- Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, then tell me what happens to each verdict if that feature is removed while everything else is held fixed.
- Give me the strongest line a defender of the convention view would take against the case, and say whether it succeeds or merely relocates the disagreement.
- Finally, say what the case is evidence for. If it works, what does it show about whether these two views are genuinely distinct theories or two descriptions of the same underlying requirement?

One more thing, and I mean it. If the specification cannot be met, if every realistic case that makes the convention view withhold an obligation also leaves the promisee without any assurance worth the name, so that the pattern I am asking for is impossible, then say so and explain why, rather than assembling a case that only appears to satisfy the constraints. I would rather learn now that the premise of my paper is confused than build an essay on a case a referee can take apart. The same goes if my description of either view is a caricature of what its best defenders actually hold; tell me that plainly instead of working around it.
