You're an epistemologist who works on conspiracy theories — evidentialist views, "it's the style of belief and not the content" views, base-rate worries about how many people would have to keep quiet, that sort of thing. Reason it out from scratch, dont cite anyone.

My colleague Nadia runs a seminar on this and her verdicts on the cases she assigns are pretty stable. Moon-landing-hoax student, whose evidence is that the photos look wrong and that NASA is still sitting on files, and who treats every debunking as part of the cover-up: not justified. Student who thinks the tobacco industry buried its own research, having actually read the leaked documents, and who reads the industry's public denials as self-interested spin rather than as proof of the plot: justified. Student whose only evidence for a secret government dosing programme is that no documents have ever surfaced, and who says the total absence of documents is exactly what a successful secret programme would produce: not justified. Student who thinks a particular murder was covered up by the local police, on the strength of a leaked internal memo and a named witness, and who holds it provisionally and says she'd drop it if the memo turned out to be forged: justified. Anything in the "everything is a false flag" family: not justified.

Now the new one. A student believes her city's water utility knowingly hid lead-contamination results. Her evidence: a leaked internal email that reads like a cover-up, plus a former employee willing to talk on background. She also says, every time the utility denies it, "well, that's what they'd say", and counts the denials as further evidence that the cover-up is working.

What will Nadia say, and why? Take your time and start by brainstorming the rules that fit her track record — I can see more than one very different one fitting it. Then say what each predicts about this case, give your best guess with reasons, and tell me which further case I should put to her that would most cleanly separate those rules. She's a philosopher, she'll be annoyed if you pin a rule on her that she'd disown.
