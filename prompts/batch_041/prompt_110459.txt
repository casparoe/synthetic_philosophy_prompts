I'm trying to write up my own position for a seminar on Pascal's justice-and-force stuff, and I want a consistency check before I hand it in. Here's roughly where I've landed, all in my own words:

(1) Might doesn't make right. A law can be perfectly enforced and still unjust, and a just claim can lose and stay just.
(2) But justice without force is just a wish. A norm with no coercive backing isn't political justice, it's a preference. So a just order has to have a monopoly on force somewhere.
(3) Coercion is only legitimate when the law it enforces binds the enforcer too — that's the difference between a state and a protection racket.
(4) The reason people actually obey particular laws is almost never that they've checked whether those laws are just; it's custom, habit, and being taught. Pascal's move here is that we couldn't make the just strong, so we made the strong just — the entitlements we start from (property, borders, offices) are largely whatever settled power happened to lock in, and then we call it justice.
(5) Even so, when the injustice is bad enough, resistance and revolution are permissable, and whether you'd win has nothing to do with whether you're right.

I think these are all things a person can hold. What I want from you is an audit: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad each real tension is. Rank them by severity rather than telling me they're all in tension with each other, and for each real one tell me the cheapest thing to give up and what it costs me. Dont give me a reading list or citations, I just want the audit. And please take a definite position — tell me which one has to go, dont just lay out the options.
