I've been running a reading group on group decision-making and I keep hitting a pattern I don't have a word for. Set up a committee voting on a bundle of linked yes/no questions: is X a problem, would policy P fix X, and should we adopt P (adopting P only makes sense if the first two are yes). Put the three questions to a vote one at a time in a fixed order and you can get a perfectly coherent result — yes, yes, yes, all consistent. Now change the order, ask about P first and the premises after, and the same voters with the same views land on a different but also fully coherent result. Nobody's opinion moved, nobody's being irrational, both final answers are internally consistent, and yet the order of the questions, not anyone's view, decided the matter. The kicker: you can't read the order off the result. Two commitees with identical members and identical views can announce opposite decisions and both be perfectly rational.

I'm pretty sure this is near the discursive dilemma, but that one is about the collective outcome coming out inconsistent — majority on each premise forcing a conclusion the majority rejects. Here nothing is inconsistent; the group is rational at every step and it's the sequence doing the work. Is there an established name for this order-driven flip, where consistency survives but the outcome still isn't fixed by the voters' views? How does it relate to agenda manipulation and to Condorcet-style cycling — I suspect it's a third thing but I can't say exactly why. If there's no name, coin one and pin it down, and tell me where the edges are: when does this happen versus when is the outcome genuinely order-independent? A bare label won't help me, I need the carving.

Lead with your verdict, then give me a JSON object with these fields: "established_name" (the term if one exists, else null), "nearest_concepts" (list of the closest known ideas), "how_it_differs" (short prose), "coined_term" (your term if there's no established one), "definition" (one or two sentences), "boundary_cases" (list of cases marking where the phenomenon starts and stops).
