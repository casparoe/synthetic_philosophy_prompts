So I've just been put on the subsidiarity committee in our upper house — the one that gets every national bill and has to say wether the thing should be done at the national level or left to the regions (and, in principle, to the towns under them). I always figured subsidiarity was just a polite word for whoever has the votes, but it turns out we've built up an actual track record over the years, and my first real file lands next month. I'd like to walk in knowing what we'd be doing if we were being consistent with ourselves.

The record, as best I can reconstruct it from the minutes:

- National tracing system for livestock when there's a disease outbreak — we said national. It crosses regional borders fast, everyone wants the same thing, and the regions basically admitted they couldnt coordinate it themselves.
- What schools teach about regional history and dialect — we said leave it to the regions. It doesn't really spill across borders, and the regions have wildly different ideas about what belongs in the syllabus.
- Mutual recognition of professional licenses, so a nurse or an electrician licensed in one region can work in any other — national. It's all about people and services moving across borders, nobody disagrees on the goal, and only the center can actually do mutual recognition.
- Caps on billboard and shop-sign size — regional. No real spillover, tastes differ enormously (one region is basically a heritage zone, another is a strip-mall wasteland), and there's no scale advantage in the center deciding how big a sign can be.
- A national energy-efficiency label for appliances — national. Appliances get sold everywhere, everyone wants the same label, and the center's testing labs are the only ones who could do it.
- Firework noise limits — regional, though there's a bit of grumbling from the border towns, so that one's messy.

Now the file. The national government wants a law setting a single basin-wide cap on how much water farms can pump out of the river system, plus a minimum flow it would enforce. Four regions sit on the basin. The two upstream ones are farm country and are furious — they say the cap was drawn by people who've never seen their soil and will wipe out the orchards. The two downstream ones have the lake and the tourist trade and are all for it. There's real spillover here, obviously: what upstream pumps is what downstream gets. But the regions already wrote their own pumping rules, and two years ago they signed a voluntary basin compact that mostly worked until one upstream region started muttering about walking away. The center points out that only it can enforce a basin-wide allocation and that it has the hydrologists and the monitoring network the regions don't. The regions say they know their own farmers, and that a single number for the whole basin is exactly the kind of thing that looks tidy on a spreadsheet and ruins a valley.

So: what should we decide? What rule do you think we're actually running, and does it survive all six of those earlier calls — or is there a rule that fits them just as well that I haven't noticed? Which earlier one is the closest analogue to the river? And if you had to design one more case that would force us to choose between the rules you've got left standing, what would it be?
