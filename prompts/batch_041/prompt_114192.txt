I am preparing a position paper on low-impact AI for a workshop, and before I commit to it I want a consistency audit of the view I have assembled. Please act as a philosopher of AI safety and formal epistemology with expertise in value specification, impact measures, and decision theory. Think carefully; begin with a short brainstorming pass in which you restate each commitment below in your own words and draw the dependency relations among them, then give the audit itself. I do not want citations, reading lists, or appeals to who said what; I want the argument.

Here is my set of commitments:

1. Low-impact agency is a genuine and important desideratum: an agent should avoid side effects, where a side effect is a change the agent causes that is not part of achieving its assigned task.

2. Side effects are baseline-relative. There is no fact of the matter about whether a change is a side effect independent of some baseline trajectory against which it counts as a divergence.

3. The baseline cannot be the agent’s own default policy. That counterfactual is self-referential and manipulable: an agent could make its default policy worse in order to make its actual policy look better.

4. The baseline should instead be the no-further-intervention trajectory: the trajectory the world would follow if the agent took no further actions, with everything else evolving according to its ordinary dynamics.

5. A full value specification is not needed for low-impact behavior. A partial criterion suffices: side effects are divergences from the baseline that are not required by the task specification and that would be dispreferred by a sufficiently broad set of plausible value functions.

6. The task specification is not a value specification. It tells the agent what to bring about, not what to preserve. So using the task to identify intended effects does not smuggle in a complete value ordering.

7. Impact avoidance should function as a hard constraint rather than as a penalty term in the objective. If it is merely a penalty, the agent will trade side effects against task success and may accept large collateral damage for small gains.

8. Intended effects can be identified without a full value specification: they are the effects entailed by the task specification under the agent’s chosen plan, and side effects are whatever else diverges from the baseline.

Now audit this package. I want to know which commitments are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension and why the appearance dissolves, and which single commitment is doing the most damage. Be specific about which commitment has to go or be weakened. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. For each genuine tension, give the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and say what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. If one single revision would clean up the most, identify it. Do not simply flag every pair as inconsistent; part of the task is to discriminate.

Finally, if the set is not actually inconsistent, or if my framing rests on a confusion, say so plainly. In particular, if the very idea of a side effect without a full value specification is incoherent, or if the no-further-intervention baseline is undefined in the cases that matter, tell me that rather than manufacturing a tidy audit. I would rather be told the project cannot be done as posed than receive a polite reconstruction.
