I am a high-school student and a new member of my school's philosophy society, which meets over lunch on Thursdays; last week the conversation drifted from a short reading on animal minds to the ethics of eating meat, and two of the senior members, Marisol and Kenji, ended up in a disagreement that outlasted the bell and has continued by email ever since, with me copied on every message. Marisol opened with the claim (1) that if one is going to eat meat at all, beef is the more defensible choice, since a single steer yields hundreds of pounds of edible meat from one death while a chicken yields only a few pounds from one, so that anyone who cares about the number of animals killed should prefer the larger animal and regard chicken as the worst item on the menu. Kenji answered (2) that this arithmetic counts only the animals whose flesh is eaten and ignores the animals killed to grow the feed: cattle consume several times as much grain and occupy far more land per pound of meat than chickens do, and harvesting grain kills a great many small animals — field mice, voles, birds and the like — caught in machinery or stripped of cover, so that once those deaths are entered into the ledger the ranking reverses and grain-fed beef becomes the costliest choice per calorie.

Marisol replied (3) that the harvest-death figures are inferences from contested population-density estimates rather than counts, that a mouse killed by a combine is not obviously the moral equal of a cow confined for a year and a half, and that one cannot reorganise one's diet on the strength of a guess; she added that if Kenji wanted the comparison taken seriously he should state it in a single unit, whereupon he proposed 'deaths per thousand calories' and she countered that she would accept the comparison only as 'suffering per thousand calories', a metric she said could easily point the other way. Kenji responded (4) that uncertainty cuts against her rather than for her, since even a low estimate of field deaths, multiplied across the additional acreage that beef requires, would swamp the handful of deaths saved by eating fewer and larger animals, and that if she wants a cow's death to weigh more because a cow is bigger and longer-lived, she cannot also use the one-death-many-meals point to show that beef is the gentler option. Marisol then added (5) that his premise proves more than he wants, because if deaths incurred in growing crops count, they count for wheat and beans as well, and the honest conclusion is not 'eat chicken' but something like 'eat only beef raised on pasture too poor to plough' — which would abandon the very cropland his argument depends on; she also insisted (6) that there is a real difference between an animal killed as a side effect of a practice one has reason to carry on and an animal bred, confined and slaughtered precisely because people want to eat it, and that this difference must matter somewhere. Kenji granted (7) the pasture point but said the side-effect distinction describes the character of the agent rather than the quantity of harm suffered by the victim, and that Marisol was by then defending a position neither of them had held at the outset.

I have read the thread four times and cannot referee it. Please adjudicate it move by move: rate each of the seven numbered moves on a scale running from clearly mistaken through doubtful and defensible to clearly right, with a brief justification for each rating. Then tell me which comparisons in the exchange are well-formed and which are category mistakes — in particular whether 'deaths per thousand calories' and 'suffering per thousand calories' can be traded against each other at all, and what a sensible verdict looks like if they cannot — and say what each of the two is missing that the other has not noticed. Finally, give a verdict on who is more nearly right overall, including on the narrow question of whether, if someone is going to eat chicken or beef, the argument favours one of them. Please do not send me to sources or citations; our club room has no internet and I would rather have the reasoning itself, set out in order.
