Write a dialogue between Mencius and Xunzi that moves past the familiar textbook contrast — one holds that human nature is good, the other that it is bad — and makes them argue over which account can actually do the explanatory work. Both are Confucians: they agree that ritual, teaching, and the example of the sage-kings matter, and they agree that anyone can become a sage. Let the argument run on cases rather than slogans, with each pressing the other where his view genuinely explains something and where it merely redescribes it. Do not let them dissolve the disagreement by observing that they mean different things by nature; have each state his sense of the term early, then argue on the merits.

Have them take up, in order:

1. Failure. Mencius must say what it means that the sprouts so often fail to ripen, and why he treats this as a failure of tending rather than of the seed. Xunzi must say what his claim that anyone can become a sage comes to when almost no one does: a bare possibility, a capacity, or merely the availability of teachers and ritual?

2. What cultivation is. Xunzi should say whether deliberate effort and ritual transformation change what a person is like from the inside, or instead fit him with a second nature that remains foreign to him. Mencius should say whether his own account of nourishing and extending the sprouts can accommodate that, or whether cultivation for him is nutrition rather than construction.

3. Two cases you invent. First, a man of impeccable conduct and cold heart, who acts rightly from long habit and feels nothing — is he virtuous? Second, someone whose uprightness holds only while he is watched and collapses the moment supervision ends. Have each explain both cases in his own terms, and have each identify the case the other's account handles badly.

Neither should be a strawman. Each should concede something real and then recover, and each should be driven into a corner at least once. Neither should quote texts or appeal to chapter and verse; let them speak in their own voices. Aim for roughly a thousand words, and end with a formulation both could accept alongside a residue of disagreement that neither can dissolve.
