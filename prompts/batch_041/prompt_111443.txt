After thirty years in an experimental physics department I have retired and begun sitting in on philosophy seminars, and I keep meeting a maxim I had assumed I understood: that no two things are exactly alike — Leibniz's own formulation, as the Stanford Encyclopedia quotes it from the Discourse on Metaphysics, being that it is not true that two substances resemble each other entirely and differ solo numero. I have now seen it do three quite different jobs. In the coffee room it settled arguments about identical particles: two electrons with the same quantum numbers are not two things, nature itself forbids it. In my reading group it is produced to dismiss Max Black's universe of two exactly similar iron spheres as impossible. And a friend uses it for the humdrum point that two otherwise identical objects still differ in where they are. I cannot tell whether these are one principle applied three ways or three unrelated claims sharing a sentence. Set out the distinct precise theses this maxim could be expressing, varying in which properties count (qualitative, relational, spatiotemporal, identity-involving), in modal strength (metaphysical necessity, a contingent feature of the actual world, a constraint on how we may describe things), and in whether what is claimed is the contentious identity of indiscernibles or only the trivial indiscernibility of identicals. Give each reading a verdict — trivially true, substantive and defensible, substantive and dubious, or false — and say which readings Black's spheres actually threaten and which reading is doing the work in each of the three uses above. I am particularly interested in whether the quantum case bears on the metaphysical principle at all, or only on a cousin of it. Mark your confidence in each verdict and flag where the matter is genuinely contested rather than settled. And if the request is ill-posed — if the maxim is a family of claims with no common core, so that asking what it really asserts is a mistake — say so plainly rather than manufacturing a tidy menu.
