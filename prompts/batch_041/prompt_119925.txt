Hey — I'm not an academic or anything, I just read a lot of blogs and this has been rattling around my head for weeks, so I wrote it up to get it straight and I'd like you to tear into it. Tell me where it's confused, where I've got the machinery wrong, and whether the main move survives at all. Here's my thing.

There's a view going around that the far future swamps everything. The idea is that the future could hold some astronomically huge number of people living good lives, so we should be willing to give up almost anything now, or bet everything, for even a tiny chance of steering that future well. A chance of one in a trillion of something enormous still beats a certainty of something small. I'll call that the big bet.

My thought is that the simulation idea defuses the big bet. Roughly the simulation idea: either almost no civilization ever gets powerful enough to run fake worlds with people like us in them, or they can but don't bother running many, or else we're probably living in one. So there's a decent chance we're simulated.

And if we're probably simulated, then the far future I'm supposed to be betting on is mostly a simulated far future. The real people whose lives my choices actually reach might be a handful of people at a laptop, not 10^50 people. So unless you think fake people count exactly as much as real ones, the astronomical stake shrinks down to something ordinary, and the big bet loses its whole reason for being. So the simulation stuff should make you less of a fanatic, not more.

Two things nag at me though.

First: the same reasoning that makes me probably simulated should make my simulators probably simulated too, and theirs as well, all the way up. So the real people in the whole stack might be a tiny handful, or the stack might go the other way and be enormous, and I can't see the base rates. But I'm not sure that helps me, because a fanatic can just say fine, it's a lottery over stakes, the expected value is still huge in the good branches. So pointing out that the probability is uncertain maybe doesn't shrink anything. Does it?

Second, and this is the bit I actually care about: isn't the simulation argument itself exactly the kind of move the fanatic makes? It says a small chance of a very weird situation should change how you live your whole life. So if I let it dampen my longtermism, I've already conceded that tiny probabilities of weird scenarios matter enormously — which is the fanatic's premise. So it can't be a cure for fanaticism, it's the same disease. Or am I just playing with words there?

A case I made up to test myself: two worlds, both of which are "we're simulated." In one, some grad student is running one small sim on a laptop. In the other, a vast civilization is running billions of whole worlds. The word simulated doesn't tell me whether the real stake went down or up. So maybe the whole question just reduces to whether simulated minds count, and the simulation argument isn't doing any work — the moral-status-of-fake-people question is doing all of it.
