I'm on deadline for a piece for a design-and-business mag and I've decided the form is a straight-faced mock press release — headline, 450-500 words of body copy, plus a boilerplate "About Us" paragraph at the end — from a consultancy that has figured out how to sell the reasoning without the reasoner. Their product is what people call a free-floating rationale: a rationale that really is present in the structure of what your organisation does, but that nobody in the organisation has to hold, entertain, or be argued into. They've dissolved the strategy team and the ethics committee and kept the memos; the org chart is now all mechanism and no minds, and they're pitching this as a feature. Keep it deadpan, no winking, and let the philosophy carry the jokes: the difference between a rationale being there and someone having it (the reason the wasp stings the caterpillar is a real rationale with no reasoner in the wasp); why that's fine for wasps and maybe for markets and gets strange the moment the thing is supposed to be answerable for what it does; and the punchline they'd rather not address, which is who exactly is on the hook when there's a rationale and no reasoner. Someone who's read a bit of philosophy of mind should laugh rather than wince, and someone who hasn't should still follow it. Dont let it turn into a lecture. About 500 words.
