I build and operate distributed data systems, and I am fluent in their vocabulary: event sourcing, append-only logs, the source of truth, materialized views, replicas and replication lag, conflict resolution, last-write-wins, CRDTs, multi-version concurrency control, snapshots, tombstones, compaction and garbage collection, provenance and lineage. I have no formal training in history or in memory studies, but I have followed the argument between collective memory and professional history closely enough to know its outline: a community's shared memory and the critical historical record can diverge, and there is a real disagreement about which should give way when they do — one strand treating memory as a deficient record to be corrected by history, another treating memory as a distinct practice with its own norms rather than a worse history, a third holding that the two answer different questions and so never genuinely compete. Restate each of these positions in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, one at a time. Tell me what carries over cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what finds no expression at all. In particular, address whether the rendering that treats collective memory as a stale or drifted replica of the historical log is a fair translation or a distortion, and whether a multi-version arrangement — in which the log and the memory coexist as different snapshots read by different clients — models the situation better than a conflict that must be resolved in one direction or the other.

Then give me the residue report, and treat it as the interesting part. Is what fails to translate a defect of my framework — does it lack the resources to say something the historians and memory theorists are genuinely disagreeing about, such as the normative weight of accuracy against identity, or the sense in which memory is generative rather than merely derivative? Or does the residue show that the original dispute is less determinate than it looks — that which should give way cannot be settled without first fixing an objective, a fixing my framework forces into the open by asking what the system is for, while theirs leaves it implicit? Give me a verdict on each residue item: a gap in my vocabulary or a confusion in theirs, and why.
