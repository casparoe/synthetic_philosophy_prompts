I'm a software engineer with no background in economics or philosophy, and I keep running into the socialist calculation debate whenever someone claims that better data and better compute make central planning viable again. I want the document that the two camps in that dispute should have produced long ago: a joint statement that an Austrian-style critic of socialist calculation (Mises, Hayek) and a Lange-style defender of market socialism could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Please structure it as follows. First, a numbered list of agreed claims — what the dispute was actually asking, what work market prices are doing in each account, what each side has conceded, and what both agree would count against their own position. Second, a table of the disagreements that remain, with each side's position written in wording that side would accept as its own. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. Fourth, for each crux, a test, case, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance.

Treat two things as live rather than settled. The first is the computational objection: the Austrian case rests on the dispersion and tacitness of knowledge and on prices as a discovery and coordination device rather than on the cost of arithmetic, so the statement should say clearly whether that distinction survives cheap computation, large-scale data collection, and machine learning, or whether the Langean side can now concede the knowledge problem and still get what it needs. The second is whether any part of the dispute is verbal — whether 'calculation' on the two sides picks out the same problem, and if it does not, whether the disagreement about the feasibility of the board's trial-and-error process is empirical or definitional.

Don't let either side off the hook: the agreed claims should not be so hedged that they say nothing, and the cruxes should be ones that could actually be adjudicated rather than restated. No citations or reading lists — argue from substance. After the statement, append a JSON object with the fields agreed_claims (array of strings), disagreements (array of objects with fields topic, austrian_position, lange_position), cruxes (array of objects with fields question, who_it_would_move, shared_test, austrian_prediction, lange_prediction), verbal_disputes (array of objects with fields issue, why_it_is_verbal_or_not), and unresolved (array of strings).
