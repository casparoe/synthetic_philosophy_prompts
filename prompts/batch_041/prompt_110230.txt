For years my early-modern final has carried a question on the cogito, and every year students answer it by reciting that no demon can make me doubt that I exist while I doubt. Last spring I went through a stretch of depersonalization — thoughts kept occurring, but it felt as though no one were having them — and it changed what I take the interesting question to be. I want the cogito question rebuilt so that it cannot be answered by reciting the standard line, and so that students have to decide three things: whether what is proved is that thinking is occurring or that a thinker exists; whether Descartes is helped or hurt by treating the cogito as a single act of intuition rather than an inference from a premise, given that the act seems to presuppose the very subject it is meant to establish; and whether, if the conclusion holds at all, it holds only for the moment of thinking or for a persisting self. Please write the question for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in which students have sixty minutes to answer, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a 20-mark marking scheme that names the moves an A answer must contain and the specific errors that should cost marks.
