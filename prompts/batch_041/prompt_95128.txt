I am a retired experimental physicist, now in the early stages of a second career in philosophy. My philosophical interests are in the foundations of probability, and I have enough mathematics to follow the technical literature but not enough patience for work that merely rearranges labels. I have about three years of protected time and want to use it on one or two questions that would actually move the debate between frequentist, subjectivist, and propensity interpretations, rather than on another taxonomy of positions. Which open questions in that area should I prioritize, and in what order? Please defend the ordering. For each, explain why it is upstream of other questions, how tractable it is for someone with a scientific background rather than a purely formal one, and what would count as progress over the next few years. I am especially interested in questions that arise once one takes seriously the following three knots: first, the reference-class problem for frequentism, especially when the class is defined by features that are themselves probabilistic; second, the single-case problem for propensity interpretations, especially whether propensity can be given a precise conditional-probability formulation without collapsing into either frequency or subjective credence; and third, the calibration problem for subjectivism, especially whether a subjectivist can make sense of objective chance without circularly appealing to a Principal Principle. I would also like to know whether these three knots are really separate or whether they are symptoms of one deeper issue, such as the role of exchangeability and de Finetti-style representation theorems in dissolving the frequency/subjective distinction, or the status of typicality measures in statistical mechanics. Rank the questions, not the literature. Tell me what I should ignore even if it is fashionable, and what evidence over the next couple of years would make me reorder the list.
