Having retired from a career in experimental physics, I have spent the past several years trying to educate myself in moral philosophy. I have become interested in the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism, and I find that the debate, as it reaches me, often consists of two camps talking past each other. I would like you to draft the joint statement that a committed expressivist and a committed moral cognitivist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on that problem. I am not asking you to adjudicate who is right, and I do not want a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want the document itself, with its agreements and its remaining disagreements made exact.

The document should contain the following parts. First, a numbered section of claims both sides accept: what the Frege–Geach problem is; which logical phenomena any adequate account of moral language must preserve; what expressivism is committed to; what moral cognitivism is committed to; what both sides agree is not at issue; and what would count as a solution. Second, a section of disagreements. For each disagreement, give a neutral statement of the issue, then the expressivist's formulation of its own view and the cognitivist's formulation of its own view, each phrased so that the side in question would accept the wording as fair. Third, for each disagreement, identify the crux—the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side—and then a test, argument, or constructed case that both sides agree would bear on the crux, with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. Fourth, a section distinguishing any apparent disagreements that are merely verbal from those that are substantive, with a paraphrase both sides could accept for the verbal ones. Fifth, a short closing statement of unresolved questions.

To keep the exercise from becoming a recitation of familiar moves, please anchor it in the following constructed test battery. Take the moral predicate wrong as it occurs in the following argument: If withholding the information is wrong, then the committee that adopts the policy is acting wrongly; if the committee that adopts the policy is acting wrongly, then the chair who casts the deciding vote is acting wrongly; therefore, if withholding the information is wrong, the chair is acting wrongly. Both sides agree the argument is valid. The question is what the expressivist must say about the unasserted occurrences of wrong to explain that validity, and what the cognitivist must say about the expressivist's resources for doing so. Then extend the battery to two further embeddings that I suspect are less often treated together: first, wrong in the scope of a probability operator, as in It is likely that withholding the information is wrong; and second, wrong in the scope of a reason-giving because, as in The chair acted wrongly because withholding the information is wrong. The joint statement should say which of these embeddings both sides agree any adequate semantics must handle, and which are disputed.

Return only a valid JSON object using exactly the following field names. The field joint_statement_preamble is a string containing the opening both parties would sign. The field agreed_claims is an array of objects, each with claim_id, claim, and why_both_accept. The field disagreements is an array of objects, each with disagreement_id, issue, expressivist_formulation, cognitivist_formulation, crux, shared_test_or_case, expressivist_prediction, cognitivist_prediction, what_would_change_expressivist_mind, and what_would_change_cognitivist_mind. The field verbal_disputes is an array of objects, each with apparent_disagreement, why_verbal, and agreed_paraphrase. The field unresolved_questions is an array of strings. Do not include any text outside the JSON object.
