I'm convening a small reading group on epiphenomenalism, and after our first meeting I've decided that the usual fight—whether phenomenal properties could be causally inert—is not where the real work is. What I want to open the next session with is a specification. Write me the desiderata for any account of phenomenal judgment: any account of how a creature comes to form, and to report, beliefs about its own phenomenal states. I mean the full list of what such an account has to be able to do—the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the findings about introspection and self-report that it must be consistent with. For each item, give me the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference of one camp, and mark it negotiable or non-negotiable. I would rather have a slightly over-long list with the motivating case spelled out than a tidy short one, and please flag any requirement you suspect is doing less work than it appears to.

To give the exercise teeth, take the following as background rather than as something to argue about: phenomenal properties are causally inert, but they stand in a stable nomic correlation with physical states that do all the causal work. Now add a wrinkle of my own. Suppose the correlation is exact in ordinary conditions but comes apart under particular interventions—a lesion or a drug leaves the physical surrogate firing and the report coming out while the phenomenal state it normally accompanies is absent, and conversely a phenomenal state can occur, under some other condition, without the surrogate and so without any report. I want to know which of your desiderata that wrinkle strains, and whether an account can honor all of them at once. My suspicion is that some small subset is jointly unsatisfiable—that we cannot simultaneously have the reliability of introspective report, the causal efficacy of the states reported, and the report's being about the phenomenal state rather than the physical surrogate—and if that is right, that is the result I most want from you: name the pair or triple, and show why no account can have all of them. If instead the desiderata are jointly satisfiable, say which account manages it and how it pays for the privilege. Then, briefly, run the main families of response against the list and tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates; keep that part short, since I will run the scorecard myself in the session.
