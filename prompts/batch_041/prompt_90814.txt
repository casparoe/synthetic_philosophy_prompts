Analyze the controversy over pediatric cochlear implantation—the debates of the 1990s and 2000s over whether profoundly deaf children should receive cochlear implants, and the opposition from many in the Deaf community—through the three leading answers to the question of what should be equalized: welfarist equality, resource-based equality, and the capabilities approach. The salient facts are these: cochlear implants can provide access to sound and spoken language for some deaf children, but they are invasive, their outcomes vary, and their promotion has been seen by some Deaf advocates as a rejection of sign language and Deaf culture. Do not merely illustrate the three views; run the case through them and back again. For each account, specify the currency of equality it employs, what it would identify as the relevant inequality (if any) in the situation of a deaf child, what it would recommend regarding implantation, and how it would interpret the claim that deafness is a cultural difference rather than a deficit. Be precise about the points of divergence: whether the relevant comparison is subjective welfare, a bundle of resources, or a set of functionings; how each account handles adaptive preferences and the social construction of disability; and whether the disadvantage lies in the impairment, in the social environment, or in the fit between them. Then say what the case reveals about each account's limits—whether it is confirmed, strained, or shown to be answering a different question—and conclude with what, if anything, the controversy teaches us about the choice among the three accounts.
