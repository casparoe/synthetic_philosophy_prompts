Been lurking for years and finally got a position on acausal stuff I want poked at before I post it. Here's the package. (1) Acausal trade is real: two agents with common priors and similar decision procedures can coordinate for mutual gain with no causal contact at all, because each one's choice is evidence about the other's. (2) So acausal threats are real too -- if a simulator can credibly commit to making a copy of me suffer unless I do X, and I'd do X to avoid that, the threat has teeth, and it isn't irrational for the threatener to make it. (3) The right defence is a surrogate goal: you take on some silly goal in place of your real one, so that what happens to the sim of you is a matter of indifference and the blackmailer has nothing to hold over you. (4) I don't think 'taking on' a surrogate goal is something you can just decide to do, because a goal adopted for instrumental reasons isn't a real goal -- anyone modelling me will read off my actual utility function and the surrogate is a fiction. (5) And when I ask what a threatener could actually do to a sim of me, I can't see how it touches me: the sim isn't me, I don't feel its pain, so the threat is empty. (6) Separately, I'm a cooperator in the one-shot acausal prisoner's dilemma -- you should do the thing that the other agent's prediction of you makes true, even when it costs you.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest way out of each? Rank them, and tell me what each fix costs me. I genuinely dont know which one I'd drop.
