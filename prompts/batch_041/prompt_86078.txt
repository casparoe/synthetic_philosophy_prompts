I work as a backend engineer and have no formal training in philosophy, but I keep running into the same failure mode: a metric that was a reasonable guide to what we cared about stops being one the moment it becomes something people are rewarded for. I understand the one-line slogan, but the slogan seems to be doing too much work, and I would like a careful conceptual explanation that separates the different things it bundles together.

Specifically, I want the following four possibilities kept distinct, with an account of what each one actually requires and how they differ in what would remedy them:

1. The metric is a noisy measurement of the underlying thing, and selecting on extreme values exploits the noise rather than the signal.
2. The metric and the underlying thing are genuinely correlated, but the relationship is non-monotonic or saturates, so pushing the metric to its extreme moves the underlying thing backwards or leaves it behind.
3. The people being measured stop improving the system and instead manipulate the measurement process — reclassifying work, moving items between categories, delaying the moment of measurement.
4. The measurement changes the thing being measured, because the measured parties adapt to being measured: they optimize what the metric tracks, or the presence of the metric changes what they take the job to be.

For each, tell me whether more or better measurement of the same quantity would help or make things worse; whether the failure is a fact about the metric itself or a fact about the incentives attached to it; and what you would expect to see in the data if that mechanism, rather than the others, were doing the work.

Then address a harder question: is there a version of this law that is a theorem rather than folk wisdom? Under what conditions is optimizing a proxy guaranteed not to improve the underlying quantity, and does anything follow for the cases where optimizing a proxy works well? My suspicion is that the law as usually stated is close to a tautology — a measure that is not the goal can diverge from the goal — and that all the real content lives in the specific mechanism. Tell me whether that suspicion is right, and if it is not, where the non-trivial content is.

Finally, I am interested in cases where the proxy/goal distinction may not even be well defined. Some metrics are indicators of a goal that exists independently of them; others are partly constitutive of the goal, in the sense that the goal just is doing well on the metric, as with a score in a game. Explain how the failure story changes in the constitutive case, and whether this law is even the right framework there.

Ground the whole discussion in one worked example of your own devising in which at least two of the four mechanisms are present at once, and say which one dominates and why. I would rather have a few mechanisms explained precisely than a broad survey.
