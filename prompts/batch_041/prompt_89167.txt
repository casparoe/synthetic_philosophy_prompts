I am setting the final examination for a graduate seminar in social choice theory. The students have had a first course in game theory and already know Arrow's theorem, and I want to find out whether they understand the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem or can merely recite it. Please compose a single essay question, a model answer of roughly 800 words, and a marking scheme, following the specification below.

The question must not be answerable by restating the theorem or reproducing its proof. Its crux should be locating the work that each of the theorem's hypotheses actually does, and it should be constructed entirely out of material that you supply inside the question, so that it presupposes no memory of particular voting rules. Hand the students four rules to analyze, stated precisely enough to be used without any outside reading:

(a) majority rule on an agenda consisting of exactly two alternatives;
(b) the median-voter rule, defined on the domain of profiles of single-peaked preferences over a one-dimensional continuum, which returns the median of the voters' most-preferred points;
(c) the rule that always returns voter 1's more-preferred element of a fixed two-element subset {a, b} of the alternatives, whatever the rest of the profile;
(d) the random-dictatorship lottery, which selects a voter uniformly at random and returns that voter's top-ranked alternative.

For rule (d), give the students the following as a stated result, to be used rather than recalled: any probabilistic rule over the alternatives that is anonymous, strategy-proof in the sense that no voter's misreport yields a lottery that weakly stochastically dominates her truthful lottery, and ex post Pareto-efficient, must be the random-dictatorship lottery.

Ask the students to proceed as follows. For each of (a), (b), and (c): decide whether the rule is strategy-proof, whether it is onto, and whether it is dictatorial, and then say which hypothesis of the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem the rule evades, if any — and whether it evades that hypothesis in a way that shows the hypothesis to be doing substantive work or merely a technicality. For (d): assess the claim that permitting lotteries is the natural way to escape the impossibility, being explicit about what such a move buys and what it does not, and about whether the resulting rule deserves to be called an escape at all.

The question should be answerable in about ninety minutes, and the model answer should run to roughly 800 words. The marking scheme should be organized around six must-hit points, each carrying a stated share of a total of 20 marks, and for each point it should give a one-line note on the mistake that typically costs the marks. I want the model answer to make at least the following moves explicit, so that the marking scheme can be sharpened around them: it should state the theorem together with the full set of hypotheses under which it is stated (at least three alternatives, an unrestricted domain of strict preference profiles, surjectivity onto the alternative set, and strategy-proofness, concluding in a dictator); it should show that majority rule on two alternatives is strategy-proof, onto, and non-dictatorial, and so escapes through the requirement of at least three alternatives; it should show that the median-voter rule is strategy-proof, onto, and non-dictatorial on its restricted domain, and so escapes through the requirement of an unrestricted domain, and it should say why this shows that the domain restriction is not a mere technicality; it should show that the fixed-pair rule of case (c) is strategy-proof and non-dictatorial but not onto, and so escapes through the requirement of surjectivity; and it should argue that randomization converts dictatorship into ex post, or random, dictatorship rather than eliminating it, so that the move trades a determinate dictator for a probabilistic one while preserving strategy-proofness and efficiency.

No bibliography, reading list, or citations should appear anywhere in the question, model answer, or marking scheme; the students are not permitted external sources, and the whole item must stand on the material stated in it.
