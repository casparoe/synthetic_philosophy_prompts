I'm a sociologist working in the social-psychology-of-roles vein, so my default picture of a person is: you occupy positions in organized structures that existed before you got there, you internalize the meanings attached to those positions into identities, and your behavior comes out of the collision between what the position seems to call for and what you actually do with it — role conflict, role strain, role-making rather than mere role-taking, the way you can stand at an ironic distance from a role you're performing, the front-room/back-room business. I keep running into the claim that Confucian ethics is best understood as a *role* ethic rather than a virtue ethic or a rule ethic, and I want to know what that's actually supposed to buy. So: take the Confucian role-ethical picture — the person as constituted by relations rather than merely embedded in them, the five relations with the family ones as paradigmatic, filial piety as the model for everything else, ritual propriety as the medium in which role relations get performed and refined, reciprocity as the thread running through the relations — and restate it in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. I don't want my terms sprinkled over their claims like garnish. I want to see what the translation looks like when you actually let my framework's commitments do work: roles as structural positions with pre-existing expectations, identities as internalized role-meanings, selves as organized sets of those identities. Where my vocabulary forces you to say something a Confucian would refuse to say, say so instead of papering over it.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. Which Confucian claims come through intact, which survive only in a distorted form — and for those, tell me exactly what the distortion is, i.e. what my framework made you change in order to fit the claim in — and which have no expression in role-theoretical terms at all. I have a suspicion the residue clusters around anything normative, since my tradition mostly describes how roles operate and brackets whether they ought to be arranged that way, but I'd like something sharper than that. In particular: what happens to the claim that certain relations are *constitutive* of the person rather than just causally important to them, and to the claim that performing a role well is an ethical achievement rather than a matter of social conformity or good fit? And for each piece of residue, tell me whether it's a blind spot in my framework or evidence that the Confucian claim was doing less than it sounds like — I'd rather have the unflattering answer than a diplomatic one. Last thing: does the direction of translation matter? If you went the other way and restated role theory in the Confucian vocabulary, would the residue report come out differently, and if so, is that asymmetry itself informative?
