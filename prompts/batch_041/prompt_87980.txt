I'm a first-year PhD student in sociology sitting in a philosophy of social science seminar, and I keep getting into the same argument with a friend in economics. She says every good social explanation has to bottom out in the actions, beliefs, and incentives of individual people; I say that things like caste, markets, constitutions, and algorithmic risk scores are doing real explanatory work and can't just be shorthand for a bunch of individuals. Before we keep fighting, I want to know what a theory of social explanation is actually supposed to deliver. So please write me the desiderata: the requirements any adequate account of how social phenomena are explained has to meet, with the case or consideration behind each one. This is the methodological individualism vs. holism thing, but I want the specification before the labels.

The cases I care about are: a bank run where each depositor is responding to what they think other depositors will do, but the run is only possible because of institutional features like short-term liabilities and no insurance; a neighborhood tipping process where mild individual preferences produce sharp segregation that no one intended; a caste or race category that shapes schooling and hiring even for people who don't personally hold prejudices, and whose boundaries shift over time; a market price that no one sets but that constrains what everyone can do; a constitution or norm that persists across generations even though the individuals all change; and a credit score or risk score that is computed from individual data but then feeds back into the behavior it's supposed to measure. For each requirement, say why it's a requirement—what would go wrong with a theory that failed it—and whether it's a genuine constraint or just a prejudice of one camp. I especially want the tensions: which pairs of requirements can't be jointly satisfied, or at least not without paying a price. For instance, if social facts supervene on individual facts but also have their own causal powers, does that force overdetermination or causal exclusion? If social kinds are multiply realizable, does that block reduction? If explanations must cite difference-makers, can a social structure be a difference-maker when every individual difference is also doing work? Don't just list tensions, tell me which ones are real and which are fake, and which single requirement does the most to eliminate the usual candidates.

Then, briefly, how do the standard individualist line, the holist/structural line, and a middle view (something like supervenience plus mechanisms, or explanatory individualism with structural constraints) do against your list—just a paragraph or a table, since the list is the main thing.
