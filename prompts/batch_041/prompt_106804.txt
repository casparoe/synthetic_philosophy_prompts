I am putting together a short paper for a seminar on explanation in the cognitive sciences, and I keep running into a question I cannot state cleanly: when a system can be described both as a swarm of microphysical parts and as something that reads and writes symbols, does the symbolic description do any causal work, or is it a summary that could be swapped out, without loss, for the microphysical one? I want a single thought experiment that forces the two answers apart, so that I can see exactly what turns on the choice. The setting I have in mind — I have seen it called the careenium — is a physical arrangement of countless tiny magnetic marbles, which I shall call simms: they collide, cling together in small clusters when they meet slowly enough, and come apart again; a cluster of simms I shall call a simmball. At the level of the individual simm there is nothing but ordinary mechanics — each simm moves under forces from its neighbours, and no simm on its own encodes anything. Yet the simmballs, being large and durable enough to survive many collisions, can be assigned symbolic roles (one pattern standing for 'left', another for 'right', and so on), and the careenium as a whole behaves like a device that manipulates these symbols. My question is whether the simmballs push the simms around, or whether only the simms push the simmballs, and I want a case that settles it rather than merely illustrating the two sides.

Please construct that case to the following specification. All the physics is to be ordinary and local: every simm obeys the same simple laws, nothing at the simmball level exerts any force except through simm-to-simm collisions, and there is to be no new physics, no action at a distance, and no downward causation smuggled in by stipulation. The careenium must run through a sequence with a definite outcome — say it settles into one of two final configurations, 'the careenium says left' or 'the careenium says right' — and the case must contain two complete runs in which the same symbol occurs but is realised by two different simm-level configurations (different collision histories, different intermediate forces), with the same final outcome in both runs; the outcome must not simply be stipulated, but must be shown to fall out of the ordinary mechanics in each run. Everything else is to be held fixed: the same laws, the same total energy, the same boundary conditions. Set the scenario out in a single paragraph.

Then work it through. State, for this case, what the epiphenomenalist about the symbolic level must say — that the symbol is a mere summary, that every difference the case displays is already fixed by the simm-level facts, and that nothing at the symbolic level is a cause — and what the real-patterns realist must say — that the symbol is the invariant carrying the counterfactual and explanatory weight, that the two runs agree precisely because the symbol is held fixed while the microphysical detail varies, and that the simm-level description is a disjunction too gerrymandered to explain anything. Give each verdict its strongest argument rather than a caricature. Tell me which single feature of the case does the work of separating them, and what would happen if I removed or inverted that feature — if, for instance, the two realisations led to different final outcomes, or if the same realisation were made to yield different symbols. Finish by saying how the side that comes out worse would attack the case: what the epiphenomenalist would concede and where they would dig in, or the reverse, and whether the disagreement is substantive or merely a matter of which sense of 'cause' we have chosen to prize. Please write in British English.
