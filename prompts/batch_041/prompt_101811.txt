I write about science and ideas for a general readership, and there is one move I keep meeting whenever cosmology drifts towards the oldest question of all. A physicist will tell me, with evident relish, that the universe is a free lunch: the positive energy bound up in matter is exactly offset by the negative energy of gravity, the books balance to zero, and a universe whose total energy is zero costs nothing to make. Nothing, on this account, is unstable; give it long enough and a universe is simply what it does. The purpose of the image is always the same, namely to retire the question of why there is something rather than nothing by denying that the something has to be paid for.

I should like you to audit the image rather than the equations.

First, set out the correspondence explicitly. In the free-lunch picture, what stands for the meal, the diner, the kitchen, the menu, the bill and the zero at the foot of the receipt? A table will do.

Second, tell me where the mapping breaks, and which of the breaks matter for the use to which the image is being put. I am especially interested in the work that the word nothing is being asked to do. There is a difference between an empty spacetime, a quantum vacuum that is still furnished with fields and laws, and the absence of anything whatsoever, laws and time included. Say which of these the free-lunch story actually requires, and whether it can reach the last of them without smuggling in the first two.

Third, tell me what the image quietly imports. A lunch presupposes a kitchen, a cook, a menu, a currency and an hour before the meal. Say which of these have no counterpart in the target, and whether any of them is doing load-bearing work in the argument.

Fourth, the accounting itself. Is the claim of zero net energy as settled as the image suggests, and does a universe of zero net energy have any bearing on whether it has a cause, an explanation or a reason? Distinguish the modest conclusion, that we are not compelled to posit a creator, from the immodest one, that the universe literally came from nothing, and say which, if either, the analogy can carry.

Finally, if the free lunch is the wrong image, say what the right one would be.

Please write your answer in British English.
