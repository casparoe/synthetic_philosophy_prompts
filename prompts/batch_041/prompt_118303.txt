I retired from marine biology three years ago and have been making up for lost time on philosphy, and I've hit a wall on the James/Clifford thing. Everyone tells me it's simple — James says you may believe without evidence, Clifford says never — and that feels like a cartoon of both essays. So can you actually compare them properly?

What I can't tell is whether they even disagree about the same thing. James seems to be talking about a narrow case: the evidence genuinely can't decide, both answers are live for you, the choice is forced, and it matters. In that spot he thinks your wishes are allowed to tip the scale, and he points at things like an athlete's confidence that has to run ahead of the evidence. Clifford seems to be talking about a shipowner who talks himself into thinking his rotten hull is fine and then drowns a boatload of people, and he argues that letting yourself believe things for comfort is a moral failure, not just sloppiness. But is Clifford really banning all belief on thin evidence, or is he banning belief you've talked yourself into for the wrong reasons? And does James claim you have a right to believe, or is he just noticing that you can't stop your feelings from deciding sometimes? The part that nags me is that neither essay clearly separates believing something, adopting it as a working hypothesis, and saying it out loud to other people. You can refuse to believe a thing and still act on it, or believe it and keep your mouth shut.

So here's the case, and it's basically my old job. A young researcher has a hunch that a whole line of work will pay off, on maybe 60% evidence and no more. She can't prove it yet, but it might matter whether she believes it: believe it and she works harder and it may pan out, don't and she drifts to safe projects and it won't. So the belief is self-fulfilling. There's also a version where it isn't — she just wants her pet theory to be true because it's hers.

Where does James land on each version, where does Clifford, and does the self-fulfilling part do all the work for James? Does Clifford even care that she's only risking her own time, not sending anyone to sea? And what would each of them say about the gap between believing H, using H as a working assumption, and asserting H to a grant committee? End by telling me which of the two I should actually adopt for reading a paper, and whether the tidy split — Clifford for what I assert, James for what I privately believe — holds up or just collapses back into Clifford.
