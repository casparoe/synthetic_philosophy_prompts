I work on proof assistants and type theory: dependent types, the Curry–Howard reading of proofs as terms, natural deduction, the distinction between a judgement and a proposition, rules of inference as against terms. My philosophy of logic is that of an interested outsider, and I keep running into the tortoise-and-Achilles regress — the tortoise grants a conditional and its antecedent, refuses the consequent, and when told that whoever accepts both must accept the conclusion, asks for that as one more premise, which it then grants along with the rest, still without accepting the conclusion.

I would like that puzzle, and the standard replies to it, restated as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, followed by a residue report.

For the translation, work with the tools I actually have: hypothetical versus categorical judgement, axiom versus inference rule, proof terms versus the typing rules that build them, definitional equality, and the fact that a kernel is a checker rather than a reasoner. Restate in those terms (a) the tortoise's demand, including what exactly it is asking to be written down; (b) the reply that an inference rule is not a further premise and cannot be made into one; (c) the reply that the demand presupposes the very step it professes to withhold; (d) the reply that the tortoise is not a coherent reasoner but a device for generating sentences; (e) the reply that logic records what follows from what rather than what anyone is obliged to believe. Say for each how much of the original argument survives the translation and where you had to fudge.

Then the residue. Which parts of the puzzle have no counterpart at all in my framework — in particular, is there anything in the notions of being required to accept a conclusion, being committed to it, or being entitled to draw it, that a type checker cannot express even in principle? Is that silence a defect of the formal framework, or evidence that the philosophical question was never about derivability in the first place? Keep the translation honest: do not let the fact that a kernel applies a function to an argument pass as an answer to the tortoise, and do not let the tortoise's must be quietly re-read as a mechanical does. Tell me at the end whether the residue points to something my vocabulary is missing or to something the original puzzle was never clear about.
