ok so I'm trying to write up my position on self-control for a seminar and I keep feeling like the parts don't quite fit together, so before I inflict it on anyone I want someone to actually check it. Here's the pile:

(1) Weakness of will is real and it's a genuine phenomenon: you can judge, all things considered, that A is what you should do, and then, fully aware of what you're doing, do B instead. The akratic act is intentional — it's not a mistake or an accident.

(2) The main engine of it is that we discount future rewards hyperbolically, steeply in the near term and shallowly further out, so our preferences flip as a reward gets close. At 11pm tomorrow's gym trip looks obviously worth it; at 7am the snooze button is the thing you actually want.

(3) But here's the bit I care about: I don't think that flip is me changing my mind about what's good. My judgment stays put — I still think the gym is the better call — it's only my preference that swings. What I want at 7am isn't what I judge best, and I know it while I'm doing it.

(4) Because I know all this about myself, I'm a sophisticated chooser. I set up commitment devices — Ulysses contracts, locking apps, deleting the account — and I think doing that is straightforwardly rational, even though it means overriding what my future self will, at that moment, most want to do.

(5) And I'm fairly Parfitian about the future: my future selves are more like other people than like me. Their interests don't get extra weight just because they're downstream of this particular brain.

(6) Concretely, when I hit snooze four times and then lie in bed scrolling, that's a textbook case of weakness of will. That's the paradigm I'd point to.

(7) Last thing, and it's the one I'm least sure about: I think the whole hyperbolic-vs-exponential thing is a bit of a red herring for the philosophy of it. What makes something akratic isn't the shape of the discount curve, it's whether the agent endorses the preference she's acting on. Someone who wholeheartedly prefers the nearer reward and disowns the further one isn't weak-willed — she's just a different agent from the one who made the earlier plan. Weakness of will needs the agent to be divided against herself.

Rank the real ones by how bad they are, tell me for each what the cheapest way out is and what it costs me, and dont just tell me it all hangs together — if something has to go, say which and why.
