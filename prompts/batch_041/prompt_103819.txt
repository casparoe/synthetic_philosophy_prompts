I'm in a small reading group that's been chewing through stuff on suffering-focused ethics, and last week two of the other members, Rosa and Marcus, had an argument we never managed to settle. Marcus has gotten really into what he calls lexical threshold negative utilitarianism — his gloss on it is that avoiding suffering takes priority over producing pleasure, but only past a certain severity: below some threshold, a bit of suffering can be traded against a bit of pleasure like anything else, but above the threshold no amount of pleasure at all justifies the suffering. He says it's the only view that dodges two absurdities at once — the 'one mild headache outweighs eternal bliss' version of negative utilitarianism, and the idea that some people's agony is a fair price for a big enough pile of mild enjoyments. Rosa wasn't buying it.

(1) She said you can't have it both ways: to say suffering and pleasure trade off below the threshold, you need a common scale on which they're commensurable, and once they're commensurable then by definition some finite amount of pleasure equals any given amount of suffering — maybe an enormous amount, but finite. So the 'lexical' part above the threshold is either the claim that the exchange rate suddenly goes infinite, which isn't really a threshold at all, or just an arbitary cutoff stipulated because you dislike where the smooth version leads. (2) Marcus said a discontinuity is fine — nothing requires the trade-off rate to vary smoothly; think of how the same dollar has completely different significance depending on whether it's the dollar that ruins you or not — and that she was conflating 'there's a common scale' with 'the rate of exchange is constant'. The threshold is just where the rate goes infinite, and that's coherent. (3) Rosa said fine, but then he owes us a location for the threshold, and every version she's seen picks a number that just encodes the author's intuitions; worse, if the rate is finite below and infinite above, then arbitrarily small changes in suffering right at the line flip whether the suffering dominates anything, so a hair more suffering can't be outweighed by any pleasure while a hair less can be outweighed by a finite amount — that's not a principle, that's a step function drawn to get the answers you wanted. (4) Marcus said vagueness isn't the same as incoherence, the line between hot and cold is vague and 'hotter' still means something, and anyway she has the worse problem: without some lexical priority you either slide toward the repugnant conclusion or you have to say the agonies of one person are worth paying for enough mild pleasures spread across others. (5) Rosa said she never claimed the alternative was smooth commensurability — maybe the two really are incommensurable, no exchange rate at all — in which case the whole 'threshold' framing is a category mistake, and he's trying to split the difference between two views that are both cleaner than his.

They turned to me to referee and I honestly froze. Can you go through this move by move — rate each of the five from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and briefly say why — then say who's more right overall and what both of them are missing? And please be upfront about which parts of this are actually settled and which are genuinely contested among people who work on the topic; I don't want a live dispute presented as if it were the consensus, since I really don't know where the field sits.
