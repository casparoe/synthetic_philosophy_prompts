A friend of mine and I have been going around in circles for about a year about the hole argument in general relativity and I'd like you to tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is, because I don't think either of us is confused about the physics anymore. I'm not a physicist — I work in IT — so forgive me if I mangle the formalism, but here's the setup as I understand it, stripped down to what I think matters. You take a whole spacetime: the collection of points, the metric on them, whatever matter is around. You pick a region — the hole — and you take a transformation that is the identity everywhere outside the hole but reshuffles things inside it, and you apply it. Outside, nothing changes. Inside, the field values get moved around. My friend says obviously that's the same world written down twice, like two maps of the same city that put the origin of the coordinates in different corners, and so nothing at all follows about determinism — the theory never said the future was open, we just have two ways of writing the same future. I say fine, but that only works if you've already decided the points of the manifold aren't really there; if the points are real, then shifting the field values around relative to them gives a genuinly different situation that agrees with the old one everywhere outside the hole (including the whole past, if you put the hole late enough), and that is indeterminism. He says then the points just aren't real, and I say that's a substantive claim about the world you're smuggling in as a linguistic convention. He says a difference you couldn't in principle detect isn't a difference, and I say that's not a principle, that's positivism with a new haircut. I've tried a toy version on a finite grid of points with a field on them, where the hole transformation is just a shuffle of the interior points that holds the boundary fixed, and on the grid it feels obvious that it's one situation — but I suspect that's because grid points come with labels and manifold points don't, so the toy might be lying to me.

My hypothesis, and I want you to tell me whether it's the right one, is that this was never really a disagreement about spacetime. It's a disagreement about what a physical theory is even for: whether what the theory hands you is a world, in which case two models differing only by a symmetry are two worlds, or whether it hands you structure, in which case the symmetry is just redundancy in our bookkeeping and we should stop counting models and start counting them up to symmetry. And if that's right, then the same disagreement should be sitting underneath every theory that has a symmetry in it, which would explain why this arguement keeps reappearing in new clothes and never gets settled. I have a feeling the same shape of fight happens over whether swapping two identical particles gives you a new arrangement or the same one, and over whether two different-looking descriptions of the same electromagnetic setup are two situations, but I don't know enough to say, and I might be pattern-matching on nothing.

So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a verbal dispute about the word 'same', a real disagreement about what exists, two different questions wearing one name — one about the metaphysics of space and one about how to read a formalism — or is there a hidden factual or mathematical question doing all the work that we're both too stubborn to notice?
