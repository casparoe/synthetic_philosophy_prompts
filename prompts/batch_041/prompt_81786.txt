I'm a 4th-year cog sci PhD, I do masking/EEG work, and a postdoc in my lab and I have been arguing for about a year now about color phi. You know the effect: two dots flashed in quick succession at different spots, different colors, and people report one dot gliding across and changing color about halfway — they report seeing the color change at a moment when the second dot hadn't even been shown yet. I'm definately on the Orwellian side: nothing gets filled in at the time, the experience is as thin as it is, and what gets edited is the memory / the report you give afterwards. She's Stalinesque: the splice happens upstream, the enriched content is assembled before it ever becomes experience, so the experience itself already contains the color change and no retroactive revision is needed.

I want us to write the doc we should've written six months ago, instead of another round of us accusing each other of just redescribing the data. So: draft the joint statement an Orwellian and a Stalinesque could both put their names to. Start with a numbered section of claims we can both sign — about the phenomena themselves (color phi, metacontrast masking, the cutaneous rabbit, the flash-lag thing), about what a verbal report does and doesn't license us to infer, and about the general constraints any model of this has to meet. Then a disagreements table: one row per genuine remaining disagreement, with each side's position written in wording that side would actually accept as fair. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would move at least one of us — and for each crux a concrete case or paradigm we'd both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

For at least one crux I want something that isn't a retrospective report: a trial where the probe is fast and non-verbal (a saccade, a grip-force response, whatever) at varying delays after the second dot, or a surprise delayed probe on some trials, or a no-report readout like pupil size or eye movements. Does the enriched content show up there at all, and does it show up before the second stimulus could possibly have been processed? Write down what each of us should predict.

Also take seriously the possibility that the whole dispute is ill-posed — that the Orwellian/Stalinesque split only looks like a real fork if you assume there's a moment where content crosses into consciousness, and that she and I might be making the same mistake from opposite ends. If that's your verdict, say it plainly and say which parts of the disagreement survive it, and if some of our fight is just verbal (about whether an experience can be timestamped at all, say, or whether a revision that never reaches report still counts as a revision) flag which part. No citations or reading lists please, that's not what I'm after — I just want the document itself.
