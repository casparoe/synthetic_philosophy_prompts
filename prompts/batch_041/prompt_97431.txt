Here's the thing I keep tripping over. Everyone reads Inception as a Cartesian movie: Mal can't tell whether she's dreaming, so she needs a criterion, and Cobb's totem is that criterion, and the whole thing is about the terror of not knowing which level you're on. Fine. But the Zhuangzi passage doesn't seem to be doing that at all. He dreams he's a butterfly, wakes up, and then genuinely doesn't know whether he's Zhou who dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he's Zhou — and the passage doesn't end by leaving him in doubt. It ends by saying the two must after all be distinguished, and then calls the whole business "the transformation of things." So the doubt gets absorbed into something else — a point about how selves and states give way to each other, not a point about whether there's an external world behind the dream. A few people I've talked to think the passage is really about identity rather than knowledge, and that the life/death parallel running through the same chapter (the image of the "great awakening," where you only know you were dreaming once you've woken, and there's no further waking to appeal to) is the key. I want you to take that idea seriously and test it.

So: read the film through the passage, hard.
