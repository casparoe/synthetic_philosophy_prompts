For a seminar in philosophy of science I have been paired with a classmate to run an adversarial collaboration, and we must produce the joint statement that both of us could actually sign. She treats the apparent fine-tuning of the constants of physics as strong evidence of a designer; I treat it as no evidence at all. Our instructor wants us to stay in the weeds rather than replay the standard opening moves, so please draft the document for us. It should contain: first, a numbered list of claims we can both accept, concerning what "fine-tuning" amounts to, what the disputed inference would have to establish, and what our quarrel is not about; second, our residual disagreement, with each side's position worded so that side would endorse it; and third, the cruxes, which I suspect include whether any probability measure over possible values of the constants, or over possible universes, is well-defined, how much explanatory work the multiverse can do as a rival, and whether the fact that we exist can count as evidence without begging the question, given that any observer must find itself in a life-permitting universe. For each crux, give a case or argument we would both agree bears on it — we have already agreed to treat as one shared test case a possible deeper theory that fixes the constants uniquely — and record in advance what each of us would predict about it. If any part of our disagreement is merely verbal, say which part.
