ok so i'm writing an essay for a general-ish philosophy-of-physics audience and i want you to tear into it. not copyedit stuff — i mean: is the central move actually defensible, and is any of the physics just wrong? i don't need citations or reading suggestions, just your read.

the thesis: people say Bell's theorem killed locality, then everyone argues about whether that means spooky action or just correlations. i think that framing hides the interesting part. there are two things you could mean by locality:

1. no influence travels faster than light — call it wire locality. signals, pushes, whatever. experiment says we keep this one; you can't use entanglement to send a message.
2. the complete explanation of an event is contained in its past light cone — call it light-cone completeness. nothing about the event needs to be booked against facts outside that cone, even if nothing gets pushed.

my claim is Bell's theorem leaves wire locality standing and kills light-cone completeness, and that this is the actual scandal. physics got to keep causality-as-influence and had to give up causality-as-in-explanation, and almost everyone muddles the two.

then the essay does some work:

- argues the correlations in entangled pairs can't be explained by anything inside either wing's past cone, since any such local story would have to fix the outcomes in advance in a way that either wrecks the statistics or reintroduces a common cause the setup doesn't have.
- concedes this is a theorem about certain classes of theories: it rules out any theory where the outcome on one side is fixed once you've specified everything in that side's past cone, whether the theory is deterministic or not.
- then a section i'm unsure about: i claim Bohmian mechanics is a clean illustration, because it's flagrantly nonlocal in its dynamics and yet still can't signal, so it shows the two notions really come apart. but i also say something like "so really the violation is just about the bookkeeping, correlations are just correlations," and a friend told me that last bit undercuts the whole essay.
- ends by suggesting we should be relaxed about nonlocal explanation — weird but not incoherent — while staying strict about nonlocal influence.

comments i want:

(a) is the wire-locality / light-cone-completeness distinction real, or is it just relabeling the standard parameter-independence vs outcome-independence thing? if it's the latter, is the essay pointless?
(b) am i using the Bohmian example right? i have a nagging feeling i'm mixing up which notion Bohm actually violates.
(c) does the "correlations are just bookkeeping" line sink it, or can i save it by saying the bookkeeping has to be done somewhere?
(d) the step from "no local explanation of the correlations" to "so light-cone completeness is dead" — is there a gap i'm papering over? someone could say i've only shown it for these particular correlations, not for explanation in general.
(e) anything that's just flat wrong about Bell's theorem itself. i know there are versions that drop determinism, and i really don't want to be the person who says Bell assumed determinism.

be blunt, and if you think the whole distinction is a dodge, say so.
