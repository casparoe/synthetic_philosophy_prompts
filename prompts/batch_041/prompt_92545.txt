I write about science and ideas, and I'm at work on an essay about how concepts bend. To get at it I've been playing a puzzle game of the sort cognitive scientists use to study analogy-making in miniature: you're shown a change between two strings of letters — abc becomes abd — and asked to make the same change to a new string. The longer I've played, the less confident I am that I know what "the same change" means, and I want to know whether there's a principle behind my own verdicts.

Here is where I stand. (1) Given the seed abc → abd, I answer that iijjkk → iijjll, and I call that the same change, merely applied to a longer string. (2) Given the same seed, I answer that mrrjjj → mrrjjk — again the same change, even though the letter I advance now sits inside a group that looks nothing like anything in the seed. (3) But when my friend, given mrrjjj, answers mrrkkk — reading the move as advancing the last group, taken as a whole, to the next group — I feel she has not stretched my concept but replaced it. (4) Given the same seed, I am content to answer xyz → xya, treating the alphabet as a ring so that z can step to a: still the same change, only executed at the seam. (5) Yet I refuse xyz → xyzz, "double the last letter," even though it is a perfectly definite rule that fits the seed pair, because it does not feel like the change I began with. (6) And when the seed is mrrjjj → mrrjjk and the new string is a single solid run like aaaa, I find I have no answer at all — not because I am stuck, but because I cannot tell what "the same change" would even be here.

Verdicts (3) and (4) seem to pull in opposite directions: both are extensions my original concept did not literally cover, and I welcome one while rejecting the other. Is there a principle that yields all six verdicts, or close to it? I want to know what distinguishes a concept being stretched from a concept being swapped, what the principle predicts about cases I have not put to myself, and — if no single principle saves every verdict — which one I should give up and what should replace it.
