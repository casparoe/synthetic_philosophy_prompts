I am setting the final assessment for a final-year undergraduate module on Kant's ethics. The cohort has already worked through the categorical imperative and its standard applications, so please do not build the question around the usual false-promise or suicide examples; an answer that simply reproduces those is not what I am testing. I need one essay question, answerable in a 50-minute examination and worth 25 marks, together with a model answer of roughly 750 words and a marking scheme.

The question must turn on a maxim that I supply below, and it should be a maxim whose status under the first formulation is genuinely contestable rather than a textbook failure. Use this maxim:

M: In order to secure a comfortable life for myself, I will take more than my fair share from a shared, self-replenishing resource whenever I can do so without being noticed.

Three features of the question matter, and the marking scheme should track them.

First, the question must make the candidate commit to a reading of what the maxim is — in particular, whether the clause about not being noticed is part of the maxim or a mere circumstance of the action — and then show how the verdict under the Formula of Universal Law changes with that choice. The candidate should not be allowed to leave the maxim's description unexamined.

Second, the question must force a distinction between a maxim that is self-defeating in conception (the action-type itself cannot be performed once universalised) and one that is self-defeating only in its purpose (the action remains performable but its end cannot be attained if everyone acts on it). The candidate must say which, if either, M exhibits, and what follows for whether the duty it violates would be perfect or imperfect.

Third, the question must reach the standing objection that the first formulation is either empty (it licenses anything once you describe the maxim favourably) or rigged (it condemns anything once you describe it unfavourably). The strongest answers should be able to say what a maxim would have to look like for the test to deliver a determinate verdict, and whether M is such a maxim or a case where the test simply inherits its verdict from the description the agent supplies.

Please write:

1. the essay question, phrased so that it cannot be answered by reciting the formulation or summarising a commentary;
2. a model answer that takes a definite position on M and defends it;
3. a marking scheme built on five must-hit points, with the marks each is worth (total 25) and a one-line note on how candidates typically get each one wrong.

Keep everything self-contained. Do not require the candidate to cite or attribute any secondary source, and do not rest any part of the model answer on a specific page reference or on a remembered quotation; where a formulation is needed, state its structure in your own words.
