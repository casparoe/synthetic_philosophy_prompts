On her scale a mammal scores high, an insect scores low but clearly above zero, and a thermostat lands at or near zero, though she's been annoyingly cagey about whether it's exactly zero or just too small to matter. I don't want softballs or "so you think bugs can feel pain?" I want questions that press on the actual machinery of the view.

Give me eight questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I need at least one question on each of these:

- where her zero point definately sits, and how she distinguishes "exactly zero" from "so close to zero that nothing follows" without just stipulating it
- the numbers problem: how the scale handles a billion insects against one chimp, and whether a graded scale is even the right kind of thing to aggregate over
- whether grading sentience and grading moral status can come apart -- say, a being that's very sentient but whose affective range is narrow or mostly negative, or one that's barely sentient but whose sliver of experience is the whole of its good
- whether "temporal thickness" is really one dimension or several glued together, and what her view does with a creature where they disagree (deep memory, no anticipation, that kind of thing)
