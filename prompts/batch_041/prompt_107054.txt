Next week's guest on my philosophy podcast defends an unusual line on the problem of logical omniscience, and I want questions that get into the machinery rather than let her recite the standard moves. Her thesis is that the "problem" is an artifact of conflating omniscience about entailment with omniscience about what an agent can actually derive: she grants that an ideal knower's knowledge is closed under entailment, but denies that this closure bears at all on whether the agent can see or use a consequence, since derivation is a resource-bounded affair. When pressed about agents who seem plainly ignorant of obvious consequences, her stock deflection is "that's a fact about our psychology, not about knowledge." Draft eight to ten interview questions, with at least one each on (a) what notion of derivation she needs and how it avoids collapsing back into entailment once a proof system or resource bound is fixed; (b) whether her account still licenses saying that a confused agent knows conclusions she has never drawn, and what that does to the epistemology of reasoning; and (c) whether the entailment/derivation split survives a switch to non-classical consequence, where different logics yield different closures. Order the questions so that the deflection becomes progressively harder to use, and give each main question a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. No reading lists or citations.
