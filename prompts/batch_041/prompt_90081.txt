I'm a senior engineer who became an EM at a ~40 person startup, no philosophy background, and my team keeps relitigating the same thing: every time someone leaves or we reshuffle, we try to "capture what they knew" as docs and runbooks, and it basically never works. We write down a pile of true statements about our deploy process and the new person still can't deploy without someone sitting next to them for a week. I half-remember some philosopher making a distinction between knowing how to do a thing and knowing that something is the case, and that the first one doesn't just collapse into the second — but I want that turned into something my leads can actually run, not a clever line for retros.

So please write me a procedure a tech lead can run in about 20-30 minutes per skill area when we're deciding how to hand off work or bring someone up to speed. A decision tree in words: what to ask in what order, what counts as evidence that this is genuinely "knowing how" and can only move by doing it alongside someone, what counts as evidence it's really just propositional knowledge and a doc will do, where the procedure just says stop writing and pair instead, and where it's quietly handing me a values call dressed up as a factual one (like how much outage risk we're willing to eat). It has to survive lazy leads, so concrete enough that someone who doesn't care about philosophy can follow it. And I'd like it to be honest about the obvious cheap objection: if learning a skill means following a rule, and applying rules is itself a skill, why doesn't your procedure just push the problem back a step instead of solving it?

Then run it on three cases: (1) a mid-level backend engineer who's the only person who really understands our flaky billing retry logic, (2) our incident commander rotation, which we keep "documenting" and keep fumbling at 3am anyway, (3) a candidate who crushed the take-home and whiteboard and then couldn't debug anything in the live repo. Include at least one case where your procedure tells me something I won't want to hear — I suspect for one of these the honest answer is "you can't doc your way out of this, you have to change how you hire or onboard," and I'd rather the procedure say it than me. And tell me straight what the procedure can't do.
