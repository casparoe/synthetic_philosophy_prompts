Background, since it's why this is suddenly personal: my wife and I are doing IVF. We have four embryos in the freezer and the clinic keeps saying things like 'so which one would you like to transfer', which sounded completely innocent until it started giving me vertigo, because it presupposes each of those little blobs is already a particular somebody, with an identity just sitting there waiting to be picked. So I went back to the old question of whether I could have come from a different sperm and egg, and I'm convinced the answer is no -- if a different sperm had won the race, the person who resulted wouldn't have been me, he'd just be some guy with my face. Great, that's what I wanted. But then I wrote the argument out properly and it seems to prove something insane. Here it is.

(1) For any organism x, x's origin is essential to x: x could not have originated from anything other than what it actually originated from.
(2) A human organism originates from a zygote, and a zygote is just one sperm plus one egg.
(3) So an organism's origin is the particular sperm and egg it came from.
(4) Indentical (monozygotic) twins originate from one and the same zygote, hence from the same sperm and the same egg.
(5) So identical twins have the same origin.
(6) If two organisms have the same origin, they are the same organism.
(7) So identical twins are one and the same organism.
(8) But identical twins are obviously two organisms -- two bodies, two people, one can die while the other lives on. So (7) is false, and something in (1)-(6) has to go.

The reason I say it proves too much is that every patch comes back at me. Drop (6) and I keep my no-different-sperm-and-egg answer, but origin stops doing any individuating work at all, and individuating is exactly the job I need done on the embryos. Drop (3) and say origin is the whole coming-to-be process rather than the gametes -- but then the two twins' processes differ only in how a ball of cells happened to split, and my identity starts hinging on whether the split happened at the two-cell or the eight-cell stage, which is a fact about embryology, not about me. And there's a mirror-image case that wrecks me: sometimes two embryos fuse early and you end up with one person built out of two zygotes, two sets of DNA in a single body, so now I've got one organism with two origins and I can't even hear what (1) is supposed to say about them.
