I've spent a few years reading about the therapy/enhancement distinction as an interested amateur — no background in medicine or philosophy — and I keep noticing how confidently insurers, regulators, and ethics boards draw a line that seems to move whenever you press on it. I'd like a parody that presses on it. Write a deadpan internal prior-authorization form of the kind a health plan circulates to its reviewers, titled along the lines of Determination Guidance for Restoration-Adjacent Interventions, roughly 700 words, laid out as numbered sections, in which the fine print is forced to take positions on the therapy/enhancement line: what restoring normal function can mean when there is no prior baseline to restore; whether preventing a decline counts as treatment or as enhancement; whether correcting a trait past the population norm converts a therapy into an enhancement; and how to classify an intervention the claimant does not experience as a deficit at all. Build it around a small ladder of categories — restorative, protective, augmentative, elective — plus a rule that restoration is judged against the claimant's own best documented function, adjusted for age, and let the form incriminate itself through its own worked examples and by referring an utterly ordinary case upward for higher review. The comedy should come from getting the real debate right, so that someone who knows the literature recognizes the genuine pressure points rather than only the jokes, while a lay reader can still tell what is being mocked; the target is the practice of drawing the line for reimbursement and policy, not any individual. After the form, give me a brief JSON object with the fields target_of_satire, real_pressures_exaggerated, distinctions_the_form_presupposes, cases_that_break_the_form, settled_vs_contested (naming which of the form's assumptions are settled and which are live disputes among people who study this, without presenting a contested view as the consensus), and liberties_taken (where the piece stretches a claim or leans on a disputed reading).
