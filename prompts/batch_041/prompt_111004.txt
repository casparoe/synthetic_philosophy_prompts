I have no academic background in this — I studied music and now work in insurance — but for the past year I've been reading about Johannes Kepler on my own, and the thing that has stuck with me isn't the laws of planetary motion but his astrology. What fascinates me is that he didn't just inherit astrology as a professional obligation and privately roll his eyes at it; he tried to rebuild it. He kept the aspects, the angular relationships between planets, and argued that they work because the soul is stirred by geometrical configurations, so that the same harmonic ratios that govern the planets' motions also govern the soul's response to the sky. But he threw out or demoted the parts that hang on the signs of the zodiac and on the houses — the parts that depend on what a sign means rather than on the angle between two bodies. There's also the line usually attributed to him, to the effect that astrology is the foolish daughter of astronomy, which I take to mean that astronomy should keep the daughter around but not let her make the family's decisions. And this was a man whose own mother was tried for witchcraft, so he had personal reason to care about where the line between natural influence and superstition falls.
