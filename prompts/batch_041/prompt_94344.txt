ok so I keep getting sucked back into the torture vs dust specks thing and I want to actually pin down what a theory of aggregating tiny harms has to do before I commit to one. can you write me the desiderata?

the setup: one person tortured for fifty years, vs. an absurd number of people (call it 3^^^3, or really any number past the number of atoms in the universe) each getting one dust speck in the eye that they barely notice and forget about. I'm not asking you to pick a side. I'm asking what any theory of how to weigh many tiny harms against one big one has to deliver to be taken seriously.

what I want in the list, each with the case or consideration behind it:

- the cases it has to get right: the dust specks one, but also Parfit's repugnant conclusion and the mere addition cases, and the seperate continuum cases where you tweak a population one person at a time and seem to slide into something you'd reject. Also throw in one case I made up because I haven't seen anyone run it: the same single person, over an extremely long life, getting a dust speck every day for a googol years, vs that same person being tortured for fifty years. Same person on both sides, so it isn't about aggregation across people at all, and I want to know whether the theory is forced to give the same answer there or gets to treat it differently.

- the distinctions it can't just collapse. I care a lot about these: harms to one person over time vs harms spread across different people; a harm that's small vs a harm that's imperceptible and never noticed; a harm that happens vs a benefit that fails to happen; and each person having a claim vs us just summing welfare. Tell me which of these are real constraints and which are one camp's prejudice.

- the structural stuff (transitivity, and whatever else) and which of it a theory might have to give up.

then the part I actually want. is there a subset of these desiderata that can't be satisfied together? I have a strong hunch there is -- something like you can't both dodge the repugnant conclusion and keep transitivity and keep some modest principle that adding a barely-good life is at least not worse. If so, say which subset, and say which desideratum you'd drop. that's the whole reason I'm asking.

one more thing to build in, because I think its where everyone hand-waves: the dust specks case treats the tiny harm as if it's obviously a harm even though nobody notices it. so I want a desideratum about what makes something a harm at all when it's below the threshold of noticeability, and whether a theory can even be stated without first deciding that. if you think the case quietly assumes its conclusion, say so.

no citations or reading lists, I just want the spec. and please keep it in American English, I'm submitting it somewhere that requires it.
