I've somehow ended up on the grants panel for our local community trust, and I've spent about six months trying to work out what rule one of the other panel members is actually running. Her name's Rosie, she's been on it for years and sat through alot of these, she's got no philosophy background whatsoever, but she decides the same way every time and I can't put my finger on what the rule is. Next month there's a proposal coming up that I reckon might be the one that breaks it.

Her form so far, as best I remember:

- The tenants' association on the estate asked for £8k for a warm room and hot meals over the winter, about thirty households. She backed it on the spot, full whack, even though it was our biggest ask of the year and there was a cheaper bid that would have reached three times as many people.
- The tennis club asked for two grand towards junior coaching. Voted down. She said she wasn't paying for the kids who've already got everything.
- Somebody proposed moving £5k out of the estate's advice service and into the arts centre in town, which has far more users. Voted down.
- The drama society, mostly retired and comfortable, wanted new lighting. Voted down, and the money went to the after-school club instead.
- The youth club had two options: one that helped sixty-odd kids a bit, and one that helped eight kids a lot, the eight being the ones with least at home. She went for the sixty.

Now the new one. The tennis club has had £2k a year off us towards their courts since forever, and there's a motion to stop it. If we stop it, the money just goes back into the reserve and sits there — nobody else gets it, and the courts are only ever used by families who are doing fine as it is. I'm fairly sure she'll vote to scrap it. My mate Dave says that just proves she's got it in for anyone with a nice car, but I don't buy that, and I don't think she's definately just anti-posh either.

So: what's she going to do, and what's the rule she's really been running? Are there other rules that fit everything she's done just as well? If there are, tell me what each of them says about the tennis club money, which one you'd put your money on and why, and what we could put in front of her later that would settle it. And if her rule does turn out to say we're better off as a town with nobody on those courts, say so plainly, because that sounds mad to me. Plain English please, I'm not a philosopher, and British English — I can't be doing with "maximize" and "color".
