I keep coming back to the idea that the only genuinely bad things are craving and suffering — no craving, no suffering, nothing left to complain about. But my gut verdicts don't all fit it, and I want to know what principle actually does.

Here's what I think. A man whose wife left him takes a pill that erases every trace of longing for her: I think he's better off than the same man without the pill, but worse off than a man in a happy marriage — so the craving being gone isn't the whole story. A woman who spent ten years desperately craving a promotion and finally got it: those ten years were bad, and getting it doesn't retroactively make them fine. A dying man in constant pain who has honestly stopped craving anything, even relief: still badly off, and the pain is why, even though he doesn't want it gone. A novelist who craves finishing her book and itches every hour she cant write: I don't think that craving is bad for her at all. And a guy with no cravings and no suffering, just grey days of TV and toast: I'd rather be the novelist, so something's missing in that life.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give one up? If the view that only craving and suffering matter is right, which of my verdicts is the mistake? And what does your principle say about someone whose cravings were surgically removed along with any capacity to care about anything — best life available, or a horror show?
