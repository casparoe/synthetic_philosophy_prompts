I'm on the bylaws committee of a small housing co-op -- about forty adults, four buildings we own together -- and our bylaws have one clause that lets us change anything in them, including that clause, by a two-thirds vote at a general meeting. I never thought twice about it until we started actually using it, and now every other meeting throws up a proposal where I honestly can't tell whether it passed, whether it's valid, or what it did. Last month someone proposed an amendment that repeals the amendment clause and replaces it with a rule saying the bylaws may not be amended at all. Someone else wants a line saying the number of parking spots per unit can never be reduced by any future amendment -- entrenching itself, basically. There was one that said it took effect as of the previous January, before we'd voted on it. There was one where a simple majority raised the threshold for future amendments to three quarters, so that the vote passing it wouldn't have passed under the rule it created. And my favourite, a proposal reading 'this amendment is void if it passes.' I keep feeling there's a real logical problem here and not just a drafting problem, but when I try to read up on it I get either law review articles I can't follow or people telling me about a game where the move is to change the rules, which we also play on weekends and which has the exact same problem. I want a procedure, not a reading list -- please don't cite cases or statutes at me, I won't be able to check them and the co-op isn't going to court over this anyway.

What I want is an ordered set of tests I can run at the meeting, in maybe twenty minutes, before I have to rule as chair. For each test: what question it asks, what counts as a sufficient answer, and where it stops with a no -- meaning the amendment is invalid, or the vote didn't count, or the thing did nothing. Where the logic genuinely runs out and it's a judgment call about what the co-op is going to treat as its rules from here on, say so out loud, tell me who should make that call (me? the meeting? a fresh vote?), and don't dress it up as a derivation. I especially want you to take seriously the possibility that the answer to some of these is 'the co-op can do this, and then there is no way back' -- please don't quietly rule out the scary cases just to make the story come out nice. Also I'd like to know which of my examples the procedure actually settles and which it just can't.

Give it to me as: 'The short version' -- one paragraph I could read aloud to open the meeting; 'The procedure' -- the tests numbered in the order I'd run them, each with its question, what counts as an answer, and where it stops; 'Where it's a judgment call, not a deduction' -- a list, with who should decide each one; 'Three runs' -- walk the amendment that repeals the amendment clause, the self-entrenching parking rule, and the retroactive one all the way through, a paragraph or so each; and 'What this can't do' -- a short honest paragraph. Keep the whole thing somewhere around 1500 words.
