I have been working through Dennett's multiple drafts model in a reading group, and there is a pattern in the surrounding literature — and, I am somewhat embarrassed to admit, in my own thinking — that I cannot find a proper name for, and I want to be sure I am not simply reinventing the Cartesian theater under a different description. As I understand it, the theater is a claim about place: it is the mistake of assuming that somewhere in the brain there is a final stage, a screen or a headquarters, at which the content of experience is displayed to an inner audience. What I keep noticing is the same shape of mistake applied to time rather than space. Whenever someone tries to say when a piece of information became conscious, the account turns on a single instant at which the content crosses over: the moment the stimulus was registered, the moment the subject became aware, the moment the system's state first counted as representing it. People who would never countenance a central screen seem entirely comfortable countenancing a central instant, and that instant appears to do the very same work — everything upstream of it is merely machinery, everything downstream of it is genuinely experience.

It surfaces, for instance, when people argue about blindsight and ask at what point the subject became conscious of the shape; when they argue about anesthesia and try to date the return of experience; and, most tellingly, when someone explicitly denies a headquarters and then says that consciousness just is the state the system enters once it has integrated the information globally — which quietly reinstates a before and an after, and with them a single line where the lights come on.

Is there a name for this specific tendency, if it is distinct from the theater at all? I suspect the honest answer may be that it is the same error wearing a different costume, but I would like the difference spelled out rather than waved away, because I can imagine someone replying that a moment is harmless where a place is not — that we need some notion of when a state becomes conscious even if we need no notion of where it is displayed. If it is not simply the theater, what is the nearest established concept, and how exactly does each candidate differ from what I am describing: the specious present, the notion of a switch point, the idea of a moment of becoming conscious? If nothing fits, please coin a term and define it, and tell me what it includes and excludes — in particular, whether a theory that dates the onset of a conscious state, while locating it nowhere, has already committed the error, or whether that is a different and defensible thing. A bare label will not help me; what I want is the carving: what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges fall.
