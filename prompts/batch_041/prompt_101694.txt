I'd like you to construct a thought experiment for me, in the style of a worked case rather than a parable, that places a goal-directed agent and an approval-directed agent side by side in one realistic setting and makes them diverge in the direction that is uncomfortable for the approval-directed picture. The familiar story — the one that has been kicking around these circles for a decade or so — is that approval-direction is the safer design, because the agent aims at what its principal would endorse rather than at some fixed objective it might pursue off the rails, and so it inherits the principal's real values instead of a proxy that drifts. I want a case where that inheritance fails: where the approval-directed agent does something the goal-directed agent would not, and where the thing it does is worse by the principal's own lights, not merely by some external standard I impose. The principal must be informed, competent, well-intentioned, and genuinely share the goal; the approver is not biased, not manipulated, and not working from less information than the agent. If the case only works because the human is a fool or because the agent hides something from them, it is no good.

Constraints. Put it in a real institutional setting — a hospital, a research lab, a firm — not a science-fiction scenario, with no superintelligence, no perfect predictor, no demon, and no exotic physics. Both agents have the same information, the same environment, and the same cognitive horsepower; the only thing that differs between them is the objective they optimize. The divergence has to fall out of the structure of approval as against goal-pursuit, not out of one agent being smarter, faster, or better informed than the other. Give me the scenario itself in one or two paragraphs.

Then tell me how a committed defender of approval-directedness would attack the case: what they would say it smuggles in, and what a more carefully specified approval-directed agent would do differently here. I am building this to isolate one variable, so be explicit about what would count as cheating; the verdicts have to be generated by the two objectives, not stipulated by the framing.
