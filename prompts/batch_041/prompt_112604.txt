I am a third-year undergraduate currently taking a course on non-classical logic, and before I write my essay on dialetheism I want to check a chain of reasoning that I suspect may be muddled, though I cannot see where the muddle lies.

Here is how I have put the position together. Dialetheism, as our lecturer presented it, is the thesis that some sentences are both true and false. That, I take it, is just what it is for the law of non-contradiction to be abandoned: truth and falsity are no longer exclusive, so a sentence need not be one or the other, and there will be sentences that are neither — a third status between the two. And since the law of non-contradiction and the law of excluded middle are duals of one another, each obtainable from the other by swapping conjunction for disjunction and pushing the negations inward, a logic that gives up one ought to give up the other. The natural implementation, then, is a three-valued logic whose third value means neither true nor false, in which P or not P, like P and not P, comes out neither true nor false rather than true.

Our lecturer did mention a system, LP, in which the third value is read as both rather than neither, but I assumed this was a notational variant of the same arrangement — one and the same middle status described from a different direction. I checked with a truth table: assign the middle value to P, and both P or not P and P and not P come out with the middle value, which seemed to confirm that the two classical laws fail together and in the same way.

My question is this. If dialetheism really does cost us the law of excluded middle as well as the law of non-contradiction, what becomes of the classical tautologies? In a logic in which P or not P may be neither true nor false, the familiar validities appear to lose their standing, and I do not see what would be left of logic as a discipline. Should I conclude that dialetheism is not, after all, a thesis about logic — that it is a thesis about truth, or about the world, which leaves the formal machinery of logic untouched? And if the machinery is untouched, in what sense is there a distinctively dialetheist logic at all?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and please do not restate my question.
