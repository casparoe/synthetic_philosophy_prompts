The theory says tightness comes from ecological and historical threat: when a society faces disease, scarcity, natural disasters, or war, it needs everyone to coordinate, so it develops strong norms and punishes deviance. The trade-off is that tightness buys order and self-control at the cost of creativity and openness; looseness buys creativity and tolerance at the cost of chaos.
