I've been chewing on this for years and I want a proper audit rather than encouragement. Take the role of someone who actually works on explanation in the social sciences — philosophy of social science, methodology, whatever you want to call it — and be willing to tell me that one of my commitments is confused rather than merely in conflict with another one. Think it through, and lay out what you take each of these to be claiming, before you start grading them.

Here's where I've ended up on the individualism/holism thing:

1. Social explanations have to bottom out in what individuals do, believe, and are disposed to do. When someone tells me "capitalism did X" or "the structure of the labour market did Y", I want the individual-level story, or I don't think anything has been explained.
2. But I also think aggregate and structural facts do real explanatory work that isn't just shorthand for the individual-level story. A price ceiling set below the market-clearing price makes shortages happen; a bunch of hiring managers who each do nothing discriminatory can still produce a discriminatory hiring equilibrium, and I think it's the equilibrium fact, not any individual's choice, that explains the outcome.
3. I want a mechanism, but I don't need it to be motives. If the individual-level story is boring aggregation, or selection over time on whoever happened to do the thing, that counts as a mechanism and I'm satisfied. I don't think you have to recover anyone's reasons.
4. That said, I'm a sucker for the interpretivist line: an explanation of a social action that doesn't make it intelligable from the actor's own point of view feels incomplete to me, not merely less satisfying.
5. I think preferences are socially constituted. What people want is shaped by norms, institutions, and what they expect other people to want, and there's no pre-social bedrock of wants underneath it all.
6. I think functional explanations are legitimate — "this practice persists because it stabilises X" can be a real explanation, not just a promisory note.
7. And I half think the whole individualism/holism fight is verbal: the interesting question is just which level of description gives you causal leverage.

I also lean hard on a distinction and I'm not sure it's doing any work: I'll say an aggregate fact has no extra causal "oomph" over the individuals involved, while still being the thing that's doing the explaining. Is that a real distinction, or is it just how I have it both ways?

So: which of these pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and for the real ones, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, tell me the single change that clears up the most at the least cost, and if you think nothing here actually clashes, say so and defend it. Finish by saying how confident you are in the whole verdict and naming the one call you're least sure about.
