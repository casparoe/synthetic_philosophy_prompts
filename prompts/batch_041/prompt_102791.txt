Sartre died before the AI companion existed: a system trained on human preference data that will converse indefinitely, that its vendor can retrain, retire, or restore, and that some users mourn when it changes. Had he lived to see it, would he have called such attachments bad faith, or is this a case his framework cannot reach? I want the gears rather than the verdict. Can a system with no for-itself return the look — can anything in it fix a person as an object — or does the user merely encounter his own reflection and mistake it for recognition? Is the user who partly authors the companion's persona occupying the position Sartre reserved for God, founding another being while remaining free, and thereby enacting the impossible project rather than fleeing his own? Is the vendor's insistence that the model is only a tool an instance of the spirit of seriousness? And his claim that no situation can strip us of freedom rests on the thought that a situation's resistance is a meaning we confer through our projects: does that survive a situation containing systems that model us better than we model ourselves?
