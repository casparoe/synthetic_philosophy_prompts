I'm the ethics-and-safety lead on the team building an autonomous escalation module for our hospital's intensive care unit: it watches vitals and, within limits, acts on its own (paging the rapid response team, locking a bed to a patient) and recommends things it can't do alone (moving someone out of a bed to free it up, putting a patient on a palliative-only pathway). These calls come up several times a day, a consultant signs off anything irreversible, and I get about twenty minutes to defend each one in front of the ethics committee, which is why I need a procedure and not an essay. Act as a philosopher who has also shipped software: think it through properly before you write, and start by saying what our two-part test actually commits us to before you try to operationalise it.

The test the committee adopted: (1) the rule has to be one that no one who might end up on the receiving end could reasonably reject, given they dont know which position they'll be in; and (2) a foreseeable harm to a particular person must never be the mechanism by which the good is acheived — it can be a side effect, but it cant be the means. Give me the procedure in order: the questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it has to go to a human because it's a values call rather than a fact. Then run it on three cases: moving a stable patient out of ICU to free a bed for a sicker arrival; a mass-casualty situation where the module withholds a bed from one patient precisely because another needs it; and a rule that quietly deprioritises everyone with a particular comorbidity, which nobody on my team thinks is unfair. Say which of the two conditions is doing the real work in each case, what happens when they pull apart, where the procedure will misfire, and which of my steps are just judgement dressed up as engineering. Finally: when the committee overrides the procedure, what should the log say — I want the spec to force overrides to be recorded with a reason rather than quietly disappeared. British English, please.
