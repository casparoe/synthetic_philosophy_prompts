I have come to accept the argument Borges assembles from Berkeley's denial of material substance and Hume's denial of a persisting self: if nothing exists but perceptions, and no subject endures to have them, then time is unreal — not merely that our experience of passage is misleading, but that there is no mind-independent ordering of events into earlier and later, so that "before" and "after" belong to experience rather than to the world. What would count against this? I want the objections ranked by how decisive they really are and sorted by kind: which show the argument to be self-undermining, in that it must presuppose the very succession it denies; which are really objections to the idealist premises rather than to the conclusion; which turn on a distinction I might still draw between denying that time passes and denying that events are ordered at all; and which appeal to something the view leaves out. Tell me what a defender would have to concede, what they could reasonably dismiss, and whether the position is insulated from any possible evidence — and, if it is, whether that is a defect or simply what a view of this kind looks like.
