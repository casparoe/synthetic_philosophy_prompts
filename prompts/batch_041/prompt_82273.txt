hey, I'm putting together my philosophy of science views for a seminar and I want a consistency check before I get torn apart in front of everyone. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Theories are badly underdetermined by evidence. For any theory you can build an empirically equivalent rival that fits every possible observation, not just the ones we happen to have.
2. I'm a scientific realist — our mature theories are at least approximately true, and their central theoretical terms actually refer.
3. Inference to the best explanation is a legitimate inference rule. its basically how we earn the right to believe in unobservables like electrons.
4. The no-miracles argument is the strongest case for realism: science's predictive success would be a miracle if our theories weren't roughly true.
5. But I also take the pessimistic meta-induction very seriously — the graveyard of phlogiston, caloric, and the ether should genuinely lower my confidence in current theories. And I'm bothered by the fact that our predecessors didn't just get details wrong, they failed to even conceive of the alternatives that eventually replaced them.
6. I do not think we should be realists about the luminiferous ether, even though the wave theory of light that needed it made superb predictions.
7. I'm definitely not a constructive empiricist. 'Empirically adequate' is way too weak an aim for science.
8. My instinct is that the fix for all this is selective realism — believe only the parts of a theory that are actually doing the predictive work, not the whole package.

So does this hang together? Go through it and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are (I suspect some of these are fine together and I'm just nervous). Rank the real tensions by how severe they are, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what it would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most at the least cost. I dont need a lecture on the literature, just the audit.
