I am a high school student working on an independent-study paper in the philosophy of biology, and I have been trying to understand the dispute between two ways of saying what it means for a biological trait to have a function. On the selected-effects, or etiological, account, a trait has a function when it was favored by selection because of the effect in question. On the causal-role, or dispositional, account, a trait has a function when it makes some causal contribution to a larger system's capacity, regardless of how it came to be. Rather than rehearse the standard textbook examples, I would like you to rate and rank a set of cases I have invented, because what interests me is where the two accounts actually come apart.

Please rate each of the following cases twice, on a scale from 0 to 1: first, how well the case fits the selected-effects account (0 meaning the account cannot accommodate it at all, 1 meaning it accommodates it perfectly), and second, how well it fits the causal-role account. Then rank the five cases from most to least discriminating — that is, from the case on which the two accounts deliver the most divergent verdicts to the case on which they agree most closely — and give a sentence or two of explanation for each placement, especially wherever the accounts conflict.

The cases:

1. A male bird carries a bright plumage patch that now attracts predators more than mates, yet the patch persists because it sits on the chromosome next to a disease-resistance allele that selection strongly favors.
2. A plant secretes a compound that deters a beetle which arrived in its habitat only a few decades ago; the compound was originally favored because it reduced water loss during drought.
3. A bacterium carries a costly plasmid gene that confers no present benefit, though it conferred resistance during a historical period when the environment was rich in antibiotics.
4. A mammal's gut possesses a chamber whose shape, as an accident of development, warms the food passing through it; the warming measurably aids digestion, but selection never acted on the chamber for that effect.
5. A trait that is presently neutral, was favored by selection in an ancestral environment, and would be favored again if that environment were restored.

I would also like a closing sentence telling me which of the two accounts, if either, your ratings suggest is the better general account, and why.
