For a graduate reading group on the fine-tuning argument, design a decision tree that sorts participants by which step of the argument they actually reject rather than by the label they prefer. Begin at the prior question of whether it makes sense to assign probabilities to the values of the physical constants at all, and make each branch node a question answerable without knowing where it leads: whether a well-defined probability space over the constants exists; whether the observed values remain surprising once selection effects are subtracted; whether a coincidence of that sort is the kind of thing that demands an explanation; and, if it does, whether the explanation is a single universe or an ensemble, and whether the ensemble carries a well-defined measure. The tree must also force a choice between reading fine-tuning as a likelihood argument and reading it as a demand for explanation, since a participant can reject the first and still feel the pull of the second. Leaves should include at least: the likelihoods are inscrutable, so the argument never starts; the data are overstated; brute fact; necessity; a multiverse with a workable measure; a multiverse whose measure problem leaves it a non-explanation; a designer that is not the God of theism; and classical theistic design. Build the tree from the logical space first, place the named positions in it afterward, mark which combinations are unoccupied and diagnose whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any leaves the tree cannot separate. Add one branch for whether the tuning seems aimed at life specifically or merely at structure in general. Keep the whole thing to about a page and write it in American English.
