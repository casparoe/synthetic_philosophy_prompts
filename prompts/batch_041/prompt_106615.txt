Until recently I would have called myself a painter and left the question of machine creativity to critics. That changed when a gallery I work with invited me to contribute to a show on generative image-making, and when a colleague explained the invitation with what has become the standard analogy: a text-to-image model is a camera. Her argument was not merely that both are tools. It was that photography once seemed to threaten painting, that it did not, that the photographer is the author, and that the prompter therefore stands to the model as the photographer stands to the camera. On this view, the moral panic about training data is the panic that met the first cameras, the creativity is located in the person who frames the shot, and painters who feel displaced are repeating a historical mistake. I have heard the same analogy used to reassure illustrators, to justify authorship claims, and to argue that nothing conceptually new is happening.

I want a careful audit of the analogy, not a verdict on whether AI art is good. Lay out the correspondence explicitly: what plays the role of camera, lens, film or sensor, photographer, subject, darkroom, print, and viewer; then identify where the mapping breaks. Which of the camera's features are being quietly imported into the model — for example, that the instrument is inert, that the scene before it is not itself authored, that the causal trace runs from world to image, that the operator's skill is exhausted by framing and timing, that the output is a single determinate image rather than a distribution, that the medium has no memory of every prior photograph it has seen? Distinguish the disanalogies that matter for the authorship and creativity questions from those that are merely incidental, and say whether the camera's indexical relation to its subject is doing any real work in the argument or is only atmospheric. Finally, say whether the conclusion the analogy is used to support — that prompting is authorship in the same sense as photography, and that the training-data objections are just the old fear of a new tool — survives once those breaks are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion all along. If a better analogy is available, offer it and say what it captures that the camera does not.
