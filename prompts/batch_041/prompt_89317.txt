Ok so I keep having the same arguement with people about whether what these models do counts as real intelligence, and it always stalls because nobody will say what they actually mean by the word. So I want to pin it down. Here's a list of things people point to, at various times, as the mark of the real thing. Rate each one 0-10 on how much of a *necessary* ingredient it is — 10 = nothing that lacks this deserves the name, 0 = it's a side effect at best, or a party trick. Then give me the ranking top to bottom, with a short paragraph per item on why it landed where it did.

The list:

1. Can solve problems it's never seen before — novelty, not retrieval.
2. Predicts what happens next well, i.e. compresses experience into a small model.
3. Has goals of its own and keeps after them over time.
4. Models itself: carries an internal representation of itself-as-a-thing-in-the-world that the rest of the system can read off and act on.
5. Can explain its own reasoning when asked.
6. Learns from a single example.
7. Is embedded in a community that could in principle catch it being wrong and correct it — so intelligence is a status other people confer on you, not a property you have.
8. Can pass as human in conversation for an hour.
9. Can build something smarter than itself.
10. Actually cares about getting things right, as opposed to producing something that sounds right.
11. Can tell when a question is nonsense, or when the whole frame of the question is wrong.
12. Can suffer or flourish.

Two extra asks. First, flag any pairs on that list where you think the ordering is basically arbitrary because they're secretly measuring the same underlying thing. Second, at the end name the one item you'd throw out as a red herring, and say what you'd add that isn't on the list at all.

And then the twist, which is the part I actually care about: take your top five and tell me which of them survive if it turns out the system is nothing but a giant lookup table. It has every text every human ever wrote memorized, and its whole trick is retrieving the right bits and stitching them together. Which of your top five does that description still let through, and which does it kill? Dont hedge on this one — I want to know where you think the line actually falls.
