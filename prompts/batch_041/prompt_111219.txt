"Schrödinger is sometimes described as having used the arithmetical paradox to argue for a Vedantic-style unity of consciousness. Explain what the paradox is, how it connects to his reading of Vedanta, and whether it commits him to a metaphysical monism. Distinguish his epistemic point from any metaphysical claim, and say whether he thought physics — including quantum mechanics — supported the conclusion."

And here is the student's answer:

"Schrödinger's arithmetical paradox begins from the fact that consciousness is never encountered in the plural. Whenever I try to locate consciousness, I find only one instance of it — my own — and the existence of other minds is something I infer rather than observe. Yet we speak of many minds as though this were a straightforward empirical fact. The arithmetic of one observed consciousness against many inferred ones is what he calls paradoxical; he develops this most fully in Mind and Matter, though the theme also shows up in the epilogue to What Is Life?.

He connects this to Vedanta by way of the Upanishadic identification of Atman with Brahman: if the deepest self in each of us is the same reality, then the plurality of separate persons is the illusion, and the apparent multiplicity of minds is a kind of maya. Schrödinger takes this seriously as a metaphysical picture, not merely as a metaphor, and it shapes his late essays on the unity of consciousness. He also thinks the plurality of consciousnesses is logically impossible, since a plurality of things of the same kind requires some principle of individuation, and consciousness admits of none.

He is careful, though, to say that physics does not establish this. Quantum mechanics gives us indeterminacy rather than oneness, and he uses it against strict determinism, not as evidence for a single cosmic consciousness. His monism is thus a philosophical commitment that he finds consonant with Vedanta, not a result derived from his own science. If anything, he thinks the scientific worldview is the one that has to be reinterpreted in light of it, rather than the other way round."
