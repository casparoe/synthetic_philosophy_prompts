ok so I've been reading about the Voyager golden records, and the earlier Pioneer plaques, and the Arecibo message, and it struck me that Hofstadter's whole frame / outer / inner message thing from GEB is basically a theory of exactly what those design teams were sweating over, except nobody in the meetings was using his vocabulary. quick gloss of what I mean so we're on the same page, and correct me if I've mangled it: the frame message is the bit that says "I am a record, here is how to physically get at me" (stylus, grooves, speed), the outer message is the bit that says "I am in a code, here is the code" (the key that lets you decode), and the inner message is the content that falls out once you've decoded. The point, as I remember it, is that the meaning isn't sitting in the grooves; it lives in the causal chain running from the object through a decoder.

I want you to run the Voyager record through that scheme, with Arecibo as a control case, and then tell me where it breaks. Here's what I already know, so don't spend space on it: gold-plated copper disk on both Voyagers, launched 77; cover engraved with a pulsar map and a hydrogen hyperfine transition diagram; cartridge and playing instructions; 116 images (one of them a calibration frame), greetings in 55 languages, natural sounds, music, whale song, a Carter message, and an hour of Ann Druyan's brainwaves squeezed into a minute. Pioneer plaques a few years earlier, pulsar map again, nude couple, no decoding key beyond the engraving itself. Arecibo, 1974, sent once at a telescope-rededication ceremony, 1679 bits, which happens to be 23 times 73 so you can lay it out as a picture; it's widely described as a demonstration of capability rather than a real attempt at conversation.

What I'm after:

- a disciplined mapping, level by level, and for each one say who the imagined decoder is. Then be honest about the cover diagram: is the pulsar map a frame message? You don't need it to play the disk, you need it to know where and when the thing was made. If that counts as frame, the category has quietly swallowed a chunk of what I'd have called content, and I'd rather you say so than smooth it over.

- the Arecibo case, which looks like it has almost no frame message at all -- no key, no instructions, just a bit string whose only outer-ish clue is that 1679 factorises nicely. What does that do to the theory? Is there a level below frame, the "this is a message at all" level, and does the scheme need it or can it get by without?

- the alien as decoder. If meaning lives in the decoding machinery, that's fine until the machinery has no reason to bother recieving the thing. A record nobody plays has grooves and no inner message. Defect in the theory, or the whole point of it? And what about a decoder that runs the machinery wrong on purpose -- wrong speed, images read sideways? Where does the meaning sit then, and is the theory silent or does it have something to say?

- reverse direction, case testing theory. My suspicion is that the trichotomy describes a cooperative decoding situation and comes apart when the cooperation is only hypothetical, and that what the designers were actually doing has no name in the scheme: building a decoder into the message, trying to make the outer message self-executing. Also whether the Druyan brainwave track is a seperate problem -- a record of a mind with no agreed decoding key at all. Tell me if that's right or if I'm just reading my own hobbyhorse into it.

Don't dissolve into "well, it depends what you mean by meaning." Pick a reading, defend it, and flag plainly where the theory just doesn't reach.
