Every spring the international country happiness rankings come out and I read them the way other people read the football tables, and every spring I have the same argument with a friend who works in economic development. His position is that the number is essentially a thermometer: people in each country are asked to rate their life on a 0-10 ladder, the wording of the question is scrupulous about what the two ends of that ladder mean ("The top of the ladder represents the best possible life for you and the bottom of the ladder represents the worst possible life for you"), the answers are averaged, and the average is then a fact about that country in the same way that life expectancy is. Mine is that the score does not compare lives, it compares the scales people are using when they describe their lives, and that those scales are not fixed. What I would like from you is not a survey of the literature, which I have no way to check, but one worked example of the conceptual issue I keep circling.

Give me a single concrete case, real or invented as you prefer, provided it is specific: a country whose score moves in a way that an informed person would call an accurate measurement and would at the same time deny is any improvement in how anyone's life is actually going, and where I can see precisely which part of the number is carrying the weight — the level people report, the scale they report it on, or the conversion between the two. Then say why this is a conceptual problem rather than a merely empirical one, that is, why better sampling, larger samples and cleverer weighting would leave it exactly where it is. I have also been wondering whether the following repair works: show everyone, in every country, the same short description of a hypothetical person's life and ask them to rate that person on the same ladder, so that you can read off how each country is using the scale and correct for it. Tell me whether that solves anything or only relocates the difficulty, and if it does solve something, what it must assume about the respondents that may not hold. Last, keeping to your example rather than to generalities: does this make the rankings worthless as something for a government to aim at, or only a number that needs a companion — and if the latter, what property would the companion number have to have that the ladder score lacks?
