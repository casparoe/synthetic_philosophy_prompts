ok so here's something I've been chewing on, and I want a real answer rather then the version I keep getting in comment sections.

The move goes like this. The self is a center of gravity — Dennett's thing. The pitch: when you want to predict how a physical object behaves, you can pretend all its mass is concentrated at a single point and get the right answers. That point is real in the sense that it's the right thing to compute with, but there's no little nugget of gravity-stuff sitting there. It's an abstraction over a pattern. Now swap in the person: the self is a center of narrative gravity, a story the brain tells about the organism, real the way a useful fiction is real. And then the conclusion everyone actually wants out of it: there's no fact of the matter about personal identity, the self is an illusion, teleportation is fine, the you that wakes up tomorrow is just the next installment of the story.

I read a lot of the rationalist blogs and this is like the tenth time it's come up in a group chat. It gets deployed as a trump card, and I'm not conviced the analogy is load bearing in the direction people think it is.
