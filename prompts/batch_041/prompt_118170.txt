I write novels, and a few years back I started keeping a prediction diary — will my agent sell this book in six months, will the chapter 12 ending land with my beta readers, that kind of thing. I write down a percentage before I know the answer, then check back later. Now I want to actually score myself, and I've got three schemes and no idea wich to trust, or how they'd change me over time:

1. Hit rate — a hit if the thing I gave more than 50% to happened (or the thing I gave less than 50% to didn't), then hits divided by total.
2. If it happened I score my stated probability, if it didn't I score 1 minus it, then average. So a correct 70% beats a correct 60%.
3. For each one, take the gap between my stated probability and what actually happened (1 or 0), square it, average the lot.

Rank these three by how well they'd actually train me to be good at predicitng rather than just feeling good, and for each say what it would push me to do with my percentages if I were trying to game it. I know myself, so the gaming part matters more to me than it should.
