Some friends and I have ended up in a long, repetitive argument about pleasure, and I've noticed that my side of it has quietly turned into a list of cases rather than a thesis. I'm not a philosopher, so what I want is help turning the list back into a thesis: a principle, or a short set of them, that captures what I'm actually tracking when I call something a pleasure and refuse to call something else one.

Here is the list. The first cold mouthful of water after a long walk: a pleasure, obviously, and it feels like something. A hot bath, good bread, the smell of rain: same. The relief when a headache finally lets go: a pleasure, though if you ask me what it feels like I can only say that a bad feeling stopped. Working through a crossword or a proof: I'd call it one of the great pleasures of my life, but nothing in particular is going on in my body while I do it, and if you interrupted me to ask whether I was enjoying myself I'd have to stop and check. A song that makes me cry: I return to it constantly and call it a pleasure, but what I feel is grief, and I don't think grief is a pleasure, so the pleasure must be something other than the feeling. A deep-tissue massage on a bad knot: it hurts, genuinely, and I pay for it again every month, and I can't tease the hurt apart from the part I'm there for. Black coffee: I remember it as bitter and undrinkable and I now find the same bitterness delicious, which makes me suspect the bitterness was never where the pleasant or unpleasant part lived. A joke at someone else's expense that I laugh at and then hate myself for laughing at: a pleasure I'd rather not be having, but a pleasure. Then two that make me doubt the word entirely. A friend who still smokes and says the cigarettes no longer even feel good to him, he just wants them — the wanting isn't a pleasure, I want to say, whatever else it is. And an afternoon I once spent so absorbed in a project that I never once noticed my own feelings, which I'd still rank among the pleasantest afternoons of that year. Finally the one I can't place at all: an afternoon when nothing was wrong — no ache, no hunger, no noise — and I also never noticed anything good; I want to say I was comfortable and that comfort is a kind of pleasure, but I couldn't point to a single pleasure in it.

I tried to write the principle myself and broke it twice. "A pleasure is an experience I like" covers the crossword and also covers my friend's cigarette, which I don't want it to. "A pleasure is an experience with a distinctive good feeling" covers the water and the bath and almost nothing else on the list. The pair I genuinely can't reconcile is the crossword and the cigarette: neither involves any positive feeling at the moment, and I count one and not the other, so whatever separates them can't be how they feel. So: is there an account that gets most of my verdicts and explains why it has to drop the rest, and what does it say about a case I haven't mentioned — pick one yourself, and also tell me what it does with the unnoticed comfortable afternoon? Be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot keep. And please actually commit: name the single account you think is best, defend it against the cases that hurt it most, and tell me what it costs, rather than handing me a menu of theories to choose from. If the account has a standard name, say so in passing, but I care about the substance, not the label.
