I'm a retired chemist — 35 years in industry, polymer stuff — and since I stopped I've been reading alot of philosophy, mostly about work and technology. The automation debates all seem to assume we know what paid work is actually for, and then nobody quite says. So heres my own list of the goods people claim work delivers, in my words:

- a paycheck, plus the standing to say you earned your share rather than were handed it
- a schedule — the day and week arrive pre-shaped, you dont have to invent a structure
- company — the same faces every day, whether or not you'd have picked them
- being counted on — somebody notices when you're not there
- an answer to the question of what you do, and the rung on the ladder that comes with it
- getting better at something hard across years, with the people who do it alongside you knowing it
- a legitimate excuse — work is why you can say no to things, and also why you're allowed to be tired

Now assume income is seperate: everyone gets a generous dividend, no means test, no strings. Rate each good from 0 to 1 for how much of it is destroyed once paid work mostly goes away — 0 if it survives by other means, 1 if it's gone for good — and give me a substitute where you think one genuinely exists. Please don't drift everything to 0.5, I want real spread.

Reply as a JSON object with three keys: "ratings", a list with one object per good in my order, each with "good" (short label), "loss" (number 0 to 1), "substitute" (short string, or null if nothing plausibly fills in), and "note" (one sentence on why it lands there); "crux", one sentence naming the single good whose fate should decide how we feel about mass automation; and "overrated", one sentence naming the good the current debate overrates most.
