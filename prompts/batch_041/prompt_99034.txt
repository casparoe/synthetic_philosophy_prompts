I chair the steering committee of a municipal citizens' assembly convened to advise the city on whether to permit a supervised consumption site. Our terms of reference require the assembly to decide by reasons that all participants can accept, while also forbidding any participant from being excluded because of the source of their reasons; the final report must distinguish reasons that were offered from reasons that were relied on. A faith-based coalition has filed a brief opposing the site. Its operative premise is that the site would desecrate a place it regards as sacred; it also advances an independent empirical claim that the site would concentrate disorder and drive elderly patients away from the adjacent clinic. The coalition says the religious premise is not ornamental—it is the reason its members are participating—and that it will withdraw if the assembly asks it to bracket that premise. Some committee members want to admit the brief but omit the religious premise from the relied-on column; others want to admit it whole; a third group wants to exclude it as failing the acceptability test. Draft the steering committee's written opinion for Thursday's meeting: recommend a concrete course of action and a time frame, including whether the religious premise belongs in the offered column, the relied-on column, both, or neither; identify the principle the assembly would adopt under each option; and engage the coalition's position and the strongest objection to your recommendation seriously. I need the reasoning, not citations or reading lists.
