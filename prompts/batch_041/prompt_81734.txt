I spend my days training vision-language models — dual encoders, contrastive pretraining, a shared embedding space, zero-shot cross-modal retrieval, that whole stack. So when I came across Molyneux's question it felt like it should already be my question: a man blind from birth learns to tell a cube from a sphere by touch; restore his sight, and can he tell which is which by looking, before he touches them? What I want is for you to restate the main positions in that old debate in the vocabulary I actually work in — encoders, latent spaces, alignment, transfer, zero-shot generalisation, probing classifiers, the difference between a mapping you have to learn and a representation that is common by construction. Do it position by position, as faithfully as you can manage, not as a cute analogy: the yes camp, the no camp, and the people who think the question is ambiguous and splits into several different ones.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Which bits of the philosophical discussion map cleanly onto something in my vocabulary, which have to be stretched or distorted to fit, and which simply cannot be said at all in terms of encoders and embeddings. Two things I especially want you to sort out. First, is the philosophers' question the same as mine — can a representation built from one modality support recognition in another — or are they asking something else, like what the visual experience would be like for him, or whether he could possess the concept of a sphere rather than just sort it correctly? Second, does it matter that in my setup the two encoders are trained jointly on paired data, whereas the man in the story has had no visual training signal at all, so there is nothing for the visual side to have been aligned against? Say whether that difference is a detail or whether it makes the ML reading seperate from the philosophical one. And if the residue turns out to be real, take a definite position on which way it cuts: is my vocabulary missing something, or is the philosophical question under-specified and mostly dissolved once you state it in terms of capacities and representations? Give reasons and commit — don't just lay out the options and leave me to pick. British English please.
