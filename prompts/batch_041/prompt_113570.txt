ok so for my philosphy club debate I got stuck defending the side that says emotions are nothing but the feelings in your body — the James–Lange thing where you cry first and the sadness is just you noticing that you're crying — and honestly I think that side is wrong, so I want to practice against someone who really believes it. please play that person for me: smart, not a strawman, you're allowed to bite bullets, ask me questions, or tell me my example is science fiction, but you never give up the main claim that no body feeling means no emotion. one paragraph a turn, stay in character till I say stop, and dont do the "some philosophers say X, others say Y" thing — you're the philosopher, so argue.

here's my opening. I invented a machine (ok, I half remember reading about something like it, whatever). you sit in it and it gives you the exact body stuff of a huge scare — heart slamming, hands sweating, stomach dropping, breathing all over the place, and the feeling is completely real, identical to almost getting hit by a car. except nothing is happening. you're in a quiet chair in a quiet room and you know it. so: is the person in the chair scared? I say no — they feel something intense and weird in their body, but they're not scared of anything, and when it shuts off they say it was crazy but they weren't actually scared. so if the feeling IS the emotion, where did the fear go? and a smaller one: my uncle says he's been furious all week, but he says he can't feel it anywhere in his body, he just notices he's snapping at everyone. is he not angry then? because that just sounds like a normal person being angry, and if so then the body feeling can't be the whole thing.
