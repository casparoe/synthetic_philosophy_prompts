I have ten funded weeks this summer to work full-time on conditionals, and the funding is released only after I submit a two-page proposal to a small committee. Please write that proposal. My half-formed idea is below, and I would rather you sharpen it than accept it.

The dispute over indicative and counterfactual conditionals is almost always conducted as a dispute about meaning: whether the word if has one sense or two, whether the two sorts of conditional differ in their truth conditions, whether the difference is semantic or pragmatic. I suspect the dispute is better located in the structure of the space against which a conditional is assessed than in the conditional itself. Indicative conditionals look as though they are assessed against a partition of the possibilities — a set of live cells, given the antecedent, so that the conditional tells you which cells remain open. Counterfactuals look as though they are assessed against a comparative ordering or a distance over possibilities, one that ranks worlds without excluding any of them. If that contrast is real, then much of what separates the two sorts of conditional is a difference in the shape of the background space rather than in what if means. A partition can be recovered from such an ordering only by fixing a cutoff, and I suspect the cutoff is where context does its work — though I expect to be wrong about part of this.

Turn it into a proposal that states the question so that it has a determinate answer rather than merely marking out a topic. Explain why the existing positions leave it open: why neither the one-sense nor the two-sense camp already settles it, and why the usual semantic/pragmatic division does not. Say what the method would be — which cases the account has to handle, what a worked-out comparison of the partition picture and the ordering picture would look like, why a structural rather than a lexical approach is the one to take, and how I could tell whether the structural contrast is doing explanatory work or whether I have merely relabelled a semantic difference. Say what a positive result would look like, and what would count as the project failing. Give an honest assessment of whether ten weeks is enough for that, and what I should cut if it is not.

No bibliography, no names of works or authors: argue in terms of positions and arguments, and I will add the references myself.

Finally, be explicit about how confident you are in each substantive claim the proposal makes, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
