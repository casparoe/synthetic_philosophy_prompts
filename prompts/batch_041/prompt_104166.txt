So I was reading this pop-science book on the train (one of those big-brain books about minds and machines) and it had a bit about a wasp — the digger wasp, I think its called — that does this whole rigamarole to stock its nest. It paralyzes a cricket, drags it all the way to the burrow, but then it won't just carry it inside; it drops the cricket at the door, goes in alone to check the nest is empty, comes back out, and only then drags the cricket in. And the freaky part is, if you take the cricket and nudge it a few inches away while the wasp is inside doing its check, the wasp comes out, sees the cricket's not at the door, drags it back to the door, drops it, and goes in to check again. You can do this over and over and it never figures out to just carry the damn cricket straight in. It's like the wasp is running a fixed subroutine and there's no line of code that says "if already checked, skip the check." My friend who did a phil minor says people use this for the free will / determinism thing — like, are we just meaty wasps running a longer routine, and if we are, where exactly would "free will" even live? Anyway.

I want a poem about this and I want the shape of the poem to be the point, not just the topic. So: a villanelle. Two refrains. One refrain should be the rigid loop itself (the wasp doing the same dumb sequence again, checking the burrow again) and the other should be the little crack of flexibility / the maybe-free-will thing — the "what if it just carried it in this time" line. The whole reason I want the villanelle is that the refrains keep coming back on schedule whether the poem wants them to or not, which is literally the wasp, so please make the returning lines feel like compulsion rather than like decoration. And in the last quatrain, where the two refrains finally land next to each other, I want that to be the moment the loop and the possibility of breaking it are forced to sit in the same room — the poem shouldn't resolve which one wins, but a reader who knows the free will debate should feel like the form just made the argument for them. Keep it accurate about the wasp (the check, the cricket left at the door, the repeat) so it doesn't read like I made the biology up, and don't lean on any philosopher's name to do the heavy lifting — I don't know the literature and I don't want it coming off name-droppy.
