Context: I run a small reading group and we've been going round in circles for weeks about pain asymbolia — the patients who seem to report pain but aren't bothered by it, they'll say it hurts and then not withdraw, sometimes they even laugh apparently. Roughly half of us think this shows the "it hurts" bit and the "it's bad" bit come apart, so pain only gets its badness from the second bit (call them the separators). The other half think if it doesnt hurt then it isnt pain, and "asymbolia" is just a fancy name for a bodily sensation report with the pain stripped out (the lumpers, I guess).

Write the joint statement those two sides could actually both sign after a real adverserial collaboration. Skip the who-said-what: we know the literature, I don't want names or cites. What I want is a numbered agreed-claims section — what the asymbolia reports do and don't show, what "pain" is tracking, what would count as a clean dissociation — then a disagreements table, one row per live disagreement, each row giving the point in wording the separators would accept for their own view, the same for the lumpers, the crux (the sub-question that would actually move at least one side), a case or test both sides agree bears on it, and what each side predicts about that case.

Two things so it doesn't turn into a rehash. One, my stipulations are on the table and both sides have to say what they make of them: a drug that removes the unpleasantness entirely while leaving the sensory discrimination and the "ow" report untouched; and the mirror drug, which leaves the awfulness intact but stops the person using pain to guide what they do — they'll still rate it as terrible, they just won't move their hand off the hot plate. Two, if part of this turns out to be a fight about words, say which part, and say it plainly.

Hard cap: 900 words, table included. Dense beats complete.
