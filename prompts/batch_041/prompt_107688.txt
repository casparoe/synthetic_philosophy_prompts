I am an undergraduate in a course on Aristotle's ethics and politics, and our seminar has spent several weeks on his account of exchange and money. The discussion keeps stalling on whether Aristotle has a theory of economic value at all: some read him as offering a proto-labor theory of value, others a demand- or need-based theory, others as not doing economics but articulating a normative account of justice in exchange, and still others as simply inconsistent. The impasse seems older than any of us and immune to further textual evidence. What kind of disagreement is this really? Is it a substantive disagreement about what Aristotle actually says, a verbal dispute about the terms value, exchange, and money, or are the sides answering different questions under one heading—one about commensuration, one about justice, one about the ethics of acquisition? My hypothesis is that it is mostly the last: that interpreters are not disagreeing about the same question. I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict, using what each side actually says, and an honest answer about what would count as progress—a distinction, a reconstruction of the argument, or a concession that the question is ill-posed. I am especially interested in whether the apparent tension between Aristotle's requirement of commensurability in exchange and his treatment of money as conventional is a real philosophical problem or an artifact of reading him through later economic categories.
