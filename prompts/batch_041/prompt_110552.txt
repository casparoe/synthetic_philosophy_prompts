I am working through the literature on uncertain evidence and want a clean specification before I evaluate any particular updating rule. Write the desiderata for a theory of how a rational agent should revise credences when the evidence is uncertain. The central case is the one Jeffrey conditionalization was designed for: the agent does not learn that some proposition is true, but shifts credences across a partition of evidential possibilities, and then must redistribute credence over the rest of the algebra. The theory should say what any adequate rule in this area must deliver.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Include at least the following, and add any others you think belong:

- The rule must generalize strict conditionalization as a limiting case, where one evidential cell receives credence one.
- The rule must respect a rigidity condition: conditional probabilities within each evidential cell should remain unchanged by the update, or the theory must explain what replaces that condition and why.
- The rule must be invariant under refinement of the evidential partition: if a cell is split into subcells that agree on the relevant conditional probabilities, the final credences should not change.
- The rule must address order effects. If an agent receives two uncertain observations, does the final credence depend on the order? Under what conditions should it not? Consider the case where the two observations are described by the same partition but the agent's total experience differs between orders, and say what that implies for the requirement.
- The rule must be diachronically coherent: it should not expose the agent to a sure loss from a bookie who knows the agent's updating rule.
- The rule must handle evidence that the agent cannot fully articulate, and evidence that is old rather than new.
- The rule must distinguish updating on evidence from supposing a hypothesis for the sake of argument.
- The rule must say something about infinite partitions and cases where the relevant conditional probabilities are undefined.
- The rule must be computable or at least approximable in realistic cases.
- The rule must say how it treats higher-order uncertainty about the reliability of the evidence source.

Then flag any pair or subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. This is the part I most want. For example, does full refinement invariance conflict with the rigidity condition in some cases? Does the demand for order independence conflict with the demand to treat the agent's total experience as the evidence? If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, state the conflict as a result about the area, not as a defect of one rule.

Finally, briefly indicate how Jeffrey conditionalization, a maximum entropy rule, and a reflection-style rule fare against the list. Keep the scoring short; the criteria are the deliverable.
