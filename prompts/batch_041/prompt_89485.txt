ok this is a weird one but it's been eating at me for about a month now. I got my first two hives back in April and I've been reading everything I can get my hands on. The thing that stuck is this idea of a free-floating rationale: you can say a forager's dance is for telling the others where the nectar is, or that the colony "chose" the sunnier box, and those descriptions can be right or wrong, better or worse (a bad dance really does send the hive to a worse patch) even though nobody in the colony is holding the reason. The reason is real, it's just nobody's. It floats free of any reasoner.

Now my roommate is vegan and she says keeping bees is exploitation, period — your taking something they made for themselves and you've rigged the whole arrangement to suit you (I split my strong hive in June, which, yeah, is exactly me rigging the arrangement). My gut says that's a category error, theres nobody in there to be exploited, the colony is just a rationale with nobody home. But then I remember the whole point of the free-floating thing is that the rationale is real anyway, and I honestly can't tell if that cuts for me or against me. The part that really messes with me: one thing I read said a free-floating rationale can still be *violated* — the dance can be wrong, the hive can be badly served — and I don't know if "violated" there means anything more than "suboptimal," or if it's quietly smuggling in a subject the theory says isn't there.

So give me the most important implications of the free-floating rationale idea for the question of whether keeping bees wrongs them. Not a verdict — the implications. If the reasons are real but unowned, what follows for whether the colony can have interests at all, for what I'm allowed to take, for whether swarm control and splitting need a justification in the first place, and for whether "the bees decided to swarm" is a sentence I should stop saying. Then tell me which of those implications are real and which are just the kind of thing a hobbyist says to feel better about it.
