I'm putting together a handout for my metaphysics seminar on the statue and the lump, and I want a decision tree rather than a list of names. Start from a commitment my students will actually arrive with — something like 'there's a statue on the pedestal and a lump of clay on the pedestal, and they aren't two objects in two different places' — and branch only on questions they can answer without already knowing which position it lands them in. I want the branch nodes to be things like: are there any composite material objects at all; if there are, is the statue-lump relation identity or something weaker like constitution; does the lump survive being squashed, and does the statue; is being a statue an essential property or just a phase; is identity itself sortal-relative; does a thing need a maker's intention before it counts as a statue. Leaves should be the positions — constitution view (Baker-ish), four-dimensionalism/worm theory, eliminativism about ordinary composite objects (van Inwagen/Merricks-ish), mereological essentialism, relative identity, hylomorphism, and maybe a deflationary 'no fact of the matter' leaf, plus whatever else you think belongs.

Please make at least two of the branch nodes turn on cases I made up rather than the tired ones: (a) a 3D printer that lays down clay in the exact shape of a statue, so the lump and the statue begin at the same instant and no lump predates the statue; (b) a rock eroded by a river into a shape indistinguishable from a statue, then put on a plinth in a gallery as a found object — does it become a statue, and if so when; (c) an ice statue that melts and refreezes into a different shape — is the lump the water, and does the lump outlive the statue. Give each leaf a name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment, flag any pairs of leaves the tree can't seperate, and note which combinations of answers come out empty and whether thats because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. About 8 leaves and 5 branch nodes, please — it needs to fit on one side of a page.
