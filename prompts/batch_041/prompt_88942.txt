Here's some stuff to build on so you dont have to guess at it or go digging. Gödel kept a private list of his philosophical beliefs; among them were that there are other worlds besides this one, with rational beings of a higher order; that we live, or have lived, in more than one world; that there is a scientific and exact theology, dealing with the most abstract concepts, which is also fruitful for science; and that religions are mostly a bad thing, though religion isn't. In letters to his mother, who wasn't a churchgoer, he argued at length for belief in an afterlife: the world is rationally organised and meaningful, so it can't be that we're handed a huge field of possibilities for personal development and for relationships with others and then die having realised a thousandth of it — a rational world finishes the house whose foundations it laid. He called his own position theistic rather than pantheistic, following Leibniz rather than Spinoza. Seperately, from around 1941, he had a formal modal argument for God's existence: an axiom that each property is either 'positive' or not, never both; God defined as the being that has all the positive properties; necessary existence itself counted as a positive property; and a modal logic strong enough that 'possibly necessary' gives you 'necessary'. Conclusion: a Godlike being exists in every possible world. He kept it to himself for decades, and when he eventually let a colleague copy it out he still wouldn't publish, apparently worried that people would take it as a confession of faith rather than a logical investigation.

The thing to satirise is the press treatment: a fictional institute — invent one — announcing that it has settled the afterlife question, and the slide from 'a perfect being exists in every possible world', or from 'the world is rational, so our unrealised potential has to get realised somehow', to 'you personally will wake up somewhere and it'll all come good'. The arguments don't give you that. The identification problem alone should bite: is whoever turns up in the next world you, or merely something a bit like you? And nothing in either argument says what the next life is like, or how anyone could check.
