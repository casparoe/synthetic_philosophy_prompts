Two other first-years in my program went at each other for like forty minutes in our early modern seminar over the cogito and I honestly couldnt tell who was winning, so I want a proper scorecard.

Dan said the cogito only proves that thinking is going on — the most it licenses is that there is thinking here, not that there is a thinker — and Descartes just slides from one to the other. He added that even if you get a thinker, nothing follows about whether it's one thing, or the same thing over time; he brought up split-brain patients, and said a thought could in principle be the joint product of a committee, in which case the cogito proves that something thinks but not that there's a single subject doing the thinking.

Priya said the slide isnt a slide: doubting is an act, and acts have agents, so "there's thinking" already contains a thinker — you can't strip the subject out without changing the claim. She also said Dan was conflating what the cogito proves with what Descartes argues after it, and that the cogito only has to supply one indubitable foothold; unity and persistence can be earned later.

Dan came back with a case I thought was pretty clever: a company keeps an "office of the treasurer" for a century while the actual people in it change, and we happily say the office decided to pay a bill. So even granting that thinking needs a thinker, the thinker might be a role rather than a thing, filled by different processes at different times. Descartes needs a single persisting substance, and nothing in the act of doubting gets you that.

Priya said roles don't think, occupants do, and that Dan's example proves too much — if he's right, no one ever thinks, we'd have to say the office decided rather than the treasurer. She also said the anesthesia case cuts the other way: Descartes only ever claimed he exists whenever he's thinking, so momentary existence is all he's after.

Then they got into whether the I in the cogito is doing any real referring work or is just a placeholder, and I lost the thread.

Can you go move by move and say who's right about what — inference or performance, does there being thinking already imply a thinker, does the office case show anything, is momentary existence enough for what Descartes wants — and then say who's closer overall and what both of them are missing?
