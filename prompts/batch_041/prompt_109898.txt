I am a PhD candidate in philosophy, and I am the teaching assistant for a cross-listed graduate seminar on intergenerational ethics and rational choice. The instructor has asked me to draft the take-home essay question for the final, together with a model answer and a rubric, and I would like you to write all three. The students are advanced and have already read the standard material on the social discount rate, so the question must be unanswerable by summarising the familiar dispute between those who defend a near-zero rate of pure time preference and those who think a positive rate is required. It must also be unanswerable by reciting the standard catalogue of reasons one might discount future welfare, namely impatience, uncertainty about whether the future will arrive, the diminishing marginal utility of consumption, and the opportunity cost of capital. The exam is closed-book and the students have no library or network access, so every premise the question needs must be supplied inside the question itself.

The question should be organised around the following apparatus, which I have constructed for the occasion and which I want reproduced in the question so that the students are reasoning about it rather than remembering it.

First, fix the setting. A future is an infinite dated sequence of events; we compare futures by their total distribution of well-being. Define the Date-Shift Equivalence: for any future F and any shift k, F and F shifted forward by k years are to be ranked equally, so that the mere position of an event in time is never itself a reason for weighting it less. Call this Temporal Neutrality. Note in the question that the construction presupposes that the two futures compared contain the same individuals merely relocated in time, and add as a stipulated background fact, which the students are to accept rather than argue about, that in fact which particular people exist depends on when the relevant events occur, so a date-shifted future contains different people.

Second, define purity of discounting: a pattern of preference is purely time-preferring when a later benefit is dispreferred on the ground that it is later, and impurely time-preferring when the ground is instead uncertainty, the probability that the future will not obtain, the diminishing marginal utility of consumption, or the like.

Third, stipulate the following formal point and let the students work with it: any exponential discount factor can be rewritten as the product of a per-period probability that the future fails to arrive and a residual rate expressing pure time preference, and the trade-off between the two is one-dimensional, so no amount of choice behaviour distinguishes the readings. Preference data therefore underdetermine whether a given agent's discounting is pure.

The essay question itself should ask the students to do three things: to say whether an agent can be convicted of pure time preference on the basis of her choices alone, given the underdetermination just stipulated; to say whether Temporal Neutrality is even well formed as a constraint on the ranking of futures, given the stipulated dependence of identity on timing, and if not, what a repair would cost; and to say whether the strongest argument available for discounting one's own future welfare transfers to discounting the welfare of future people. I would like the question phrased so that the standard catalogue of four reasons to discount is a visible but plainly insufficient answer.

Please then supply a model answer of about 700 words in continuous prose, and a marking scheme consisting of five must-hit points worth four marks each, twenty in total, where each point is stated as the specific move a top answer has to make, distinguished from the corresponding move that a merely competent answer makes, followed by a single line naming the characteristic error candidates commit on that point. The model answer must not cite any work, and it should not depend on any example or result that the students could not have derived from the apparatus above.
