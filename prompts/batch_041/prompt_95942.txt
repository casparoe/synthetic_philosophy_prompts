I have been reading about Sidgwick's long entanglement with psychical research: the decades of effort he put into it, his insistence that claims about thought-transference and survival be weighed by the same evidential standards as any other empirical claim, and his refusal to let the wish that they be true tip the scales. A friend of mine who works on the period thinks the scepticism the whole enterprise attracts is misplaced, and reaches for an analogy that was everywhere in the literature of the time: telepathy is just one more invisible force of nature, no stranger than the ones the nineteenth century kept turning up — rays that pass through flesh, signals that cross empty air with no wire under them. Nobody called those supernatural; we built detectors, characterized the forces, and moved on. So, he says, the resistance to psychical research is temperamental rather than evidential, and the remedy is better experiment, not dismissal.

Audit the analogy for me. Set out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the part of the transmitter, the medium, the receiver, the code, the detector, the noise — then say where the mapping breaks, and which of the breaks matter for the work the analogy is being asked to do. Note what it quietly imports that the target case may not supply: a source that emits, a carrier with a physics, an apparatus someone can build and calibrate, effects reproducible on demand by anyone holding the equipment. Finally, with the disanalogies on the table, does his conclusion survive — that the scepticism is mere temperament, and the research programme is ordinary science in progress — or was the analogy carrying it the whole way?

Please structure the reply as follows: "Correspondences" — a table, with a sentence or two on the significance of each row; "Fault lines" — the two or three disanalogies that actually bear on his argument, a few paragraphs; "Imports" — short, on what the analogy smuggles in; "Verdict" — short, on whether the conclusion stands; and "Confidence" — a few sentences stating how sure you are of the audit overall and singling out the one point you are least sure of.
