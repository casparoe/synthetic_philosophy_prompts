Hi — I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy of science (my undergrad was physics, which I think explains most of my intuitions) and I'm supposed to have a qualifying-paper outline on verisimilitude nailed down by the end of the month. Before I commit to an angle I want an honest consistency check on the position I actually hold, because I have a nagging feeling I've been getting by on vibes and that a couple of these can't all be true at once. Here's where I've landed:

(1) Scientific realism: our best current theories are approximately true and get the unobservable structure of the world right, not just the predictions. (2) Scientific progress is progress toward the truth, not just increasing empirical adequacy or problem-solving power — I don't think "closer to the truth" is a metaphor or a compliment we pay to theories we like. (3) There's a fact of the matter about truthlikeness: Newtonian mechanics is closer to the truth than Aristotelian mechanics, full stop, not "closer relative to our interests" or "closer given our vocabulary." (4) I take the pessimistic induction seriously — the historical record really does show that even our best theories were radically false about unobservables. My answer is that the successor theory usually absorbs the predecessor as a limiting case, and that the central terms of the old theory mostly still refer, so we lose truth but keep reference and structure. (5) I think the way to make truthlikeness precise is some kind of measure over what a theory says: the more true things it says the better, the fewer false things the better, and the false ones should be discounted by how far off they are, so being wildly wrong about one thing counts against a theory more than being slightly wrong about many. I know the crude version of that idea — just comparing sets of consequences — has some famous problem, but I've always assumed that's a technicality you patch by adding the weighting. (6) I'm pretty convinced there's no privileged vocabulary for describing the world: any fact can be stated in lots of different conceptual schemes and it's a mistake to treat one of them as the One True Language.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and how bad is each real conflict? I'd like them ranked by severity rather than a flat list, and for each genuine one I want the cheapest repair plus an honest account of what that repair costs me elsewhere — I'm not asking which one you'd personally drop, I'm asking what can't be held together and what it would take to keep the rest. Also, if one of these six is doing most of the damage, say so plainly; I'd rather find out now than three chapters in.
