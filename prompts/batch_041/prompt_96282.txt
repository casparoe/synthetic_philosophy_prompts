I am preparing a short position statement for a philosophy of language workshop and would like a consistency check before I circulate it. The commitments I intend to defend are the following.

(1) Speaker meaning is prior to expression meaning. What a sentence means is explained by what speakers conventionally mean by it, and convention is in turn explained by the communicative intentions that sustain it; there is no level of linguistic meaning that floats free of intention.

(2) What is said — the proposition asserted — is a minimal proposition, fixed by the conventional meanings of the words together with disambiguation and the assignment of reference. Enrichment belongs to implicature, not to what is said.

(3) Conversational implicatures must be calculable. A hearer must be able to reconstruct them by reasoning from the Cooperative Principle and its maxims, together with the assumption that the speaker is rational. Calculability, understood as reconstructability rather than as a claim about the hearer's actual mental steps, is what distinguishes implicature from semantics.

(4) Cancellability is the diagnostic mark of implicature: if a putative implication can be withdrawn without contradiction, it was an implicature rather than part of what was said.

(5) Conventional implicature is a genuine and distinct category. Expressions such as 'but' or 'therefore' carry a component that is not part of the truth-conditional content and is not derived from the maxims; it attaches to the expression rather than to the speaker's reasoning on the occasion, and it cannot be cancelled.

(6) In comprehension, hearers arrive at scalar implicatures — 'some' conveying 'not all' — quickly and without running any such derivation. The process is automatic and, as far as I can tell, does not consult the maxims at all.

(7) The maxims are not local conventions of a particular speech community. They are requirements of rational cooperative exchange, and they bind any language users who are trying to communicate.

Please tell me which of these commitments actually pull against each other, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and in each genuine case what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Order the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and close by identifying the single revision that would clean up the most.
