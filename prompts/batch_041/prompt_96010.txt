Been lurking on LW and the rat blogosphere for about a decade, first time actually posting something. I keep noticing a pattern and I don't have a word for it.

Setup: two people have to split something and neither can just take it. What they very often land on is the split where each side ends up the same *fraction of the way* from their own fallback ("what I get if this whole thing falls apart") to their own best case ("what I'd get if the other side just rolled over and gave me everything"). Not the same amount of money, not the same cut of the pie -- the same fraction of their own personal range. Like, "we each walked away with 60% of what we could've hoped for, measured from the walk-away point."

Where I see it: salary talks that land at the number where each side gave up the same share of what it asked for; two roommates dividing something when one cares about quiet and the other cares about having people over, so there's no "equal dollars" answer and they do the fraction thing instead; a mediator who keeps saying "we need a deal where you've both conceded equally."

Two things bug me about it. First, when the upper end of the range is whatever number somebody actually said out loud, the rule pays you to open with an absurd ask -- your range gets wider, so equal-fraction-of-your-own-range hands you more -- and yet everyone still treats the result as fair and nobody calls out the person who inflated. Second, it collides with the other fairness norm I see constantly, "we each end up the same amount better off than if we'd walked," and people seem to quietly reach for whichever of the two happens to give them more.

Is there a name for the first one? I don't just want the label. I want to know what the nearest named things are, how this differs from each -- it's not just "split the difference," the midpoint doesn't care how wide either side's range is -- whether the opening-ask-inflation problem is a known objection to it, and if nothing in the literature actually matches what I described, coin me something and tell me where the edges are.
