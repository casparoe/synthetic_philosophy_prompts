I've been trying to work out what I actually think about killing in war by going case by case, and my verdicts don't fit any single thesis I know, including the "moral equality of combatants" claim that soldiers on both sides have the same permissions and the same liability.

Case 1: a conscripted infantryman in an army waging a plainly unjust war of conquest. He was drafted, he'd be shot for refusing, and he mans a trench and fires at the defenders. I think he's doing something wrong — he's killing people who have done nothing to warrant it — but I don't blame him, and I don't think he should be punished when the war ends. The defender firing back at him is plainly permitted and plainly not to blame. So only one of the two is doing wrong, yet each may kill the other. Case 2: a volunteer in the same unjust army who sincerely and reasonably believes the war is defensive because his state has lied to him. I think he's doing wrong, perhaps more than the conscript since no one is forcing him, but I still feel the defenders may kill him no more and no less than they may kill the conscript. Case 3: a just-side soldier who, through a targeting error, kills a family. That's a tragedy and a crime if he was negligent, but I don't think it makes him liable to be killed, and it doesn't cancel his permission to keep fighting. Case 4: a drone operator on the unjust side, working from an office a thousand miles away, killing dozens over a shift. I feel about him the way I feel about the volunteer, not the way I feel about the conscript. Case 5: a military cook on the unjust side who never fires a shot. I don't think the defenders may target him at all.

Is there a principle that gets all five, or close — one that tells me, for each combatant, whether he is permitted to kill, whether he is liable to be killed, and whether he is culpable? Where the verdicts conflict, which should I give up, and what does the surviving principle say about a case I haven't put to it: a soldier on a just cause whose war has since become disproportionate, who keeps fighting anyway?
