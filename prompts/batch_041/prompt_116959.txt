I want a case built to order rather than an essay on the topic, and I want the construction defended step by step. The target is the seam between determinism and predictability. I have read enough rationalist writing on this to be bored by the standard moves: a deterministic world is predictable in principle; unpredictability reduces to ignorance (bad measurements, coarse-graining) or to chaos (sensitive dependence on initial conditions); only indeterminism puts a hard floor under forecasting. I want a case that fits none of those boxes. Everything is deterministic, the relevant agent has exact information and unlimited computation, nothing diverges exponentially, and still a perfectly ordinary macroscopic fact about the near future cannot be correctly predicted by anyone inside the world. None of it may be secured by stipulation: the failure has to fall out of the case's own causal structure.

What the case must deliver: no in-world predictor, however well informed, can be both correct and useful about the target fact; a predictor outside the causal loop can be correct about that same fact; determinism survives both claims untouched, with no law-level concession; a post-hoc observer can know everything about the episode, so the failure is specifically about predicting in advance and acting on it; and the failure persists when the dynamics are made maximally tame and when precision and computation are idealized away.

Specifications to satisfy:

S1. Full determinism. No randomness, no quantum indeterminacy, no chance anywhere in the causal story, including inside the agent's head. If someone's behavior wrecks the prediction, that behavior is itself determined by prior states.

S2. Exact information, unlimited computation. The predictor knows the laws exactly and the complete present state to arbitrary precision, with unlimited time and computing power.

S3. No sensitive dependence. No weather, no pendulums, no divergence of nearby trajectories. Substitute the tamest dynamics you like; the obstruction should remain.

S4. Ordinary and small. A person, a room, a shift at work, a bet between friends. No brains in vats, no time travel, no demons as characters, no exotic physics, no infinite-precision reals doing load-bearing work.

S5. Entanglement made explicit. The prediction, once made, is an input to the fact predicted; the fact is downstream of the prediction's content. Spell out that link, and make it non-optional for the agent, not a matter of her declining to look.

S6. Generated, not stipulated. No rule that says predictions of this fact are false. If the agent's behavior is what makes the prediction false, that behavior must be ordinary, motivated, and determined by prior states; you may not posit a bare machine that inverts its input, and you may not define the target fact as the negation of whatever is predicted.

S7. A matched control. The same situation with the entanglement removed, prediction sequestered or the agent indifferent to it, must be a case where the prediction comes out right.

S8. No one errs. Nobody in the case makes a mistake, misreads the situation, or acts irrationally. The failure must be compatible with every agent reasoning perfectly from what they have.

Then do the work. Give me the case itself first, as a single paragraph of plain prose: no dialogue, no stage directions, no editorializing. Then a JSON object with exactly these fields and nothing else:

- "case_label": short name for the case.
- "target_fact": the fact whose predictability is at issue.
- "in_world_predictor": who or what predicts, and what they know and can compute.
- "entanglement": the causal link from prediction to fact, stated explicitly.
- "why_no_correct_usable_prediction": the argument, 150 words or fewer.
- "obstruction_class": one of sensitive_dependence, measurement_precision, computational_limits, uncomputability, logical_self_reference, causal_entanglement_of_prediction, stipulation.
- "load_bearing_feature": the single feature that does the work.
- "spec_check": an array of objects, {"id": "S1", "met": true, "why": "..."}, one per specification S1 through S8.
- "removal_tests": an array of objects, {"variant": "...", "prediction_succeeds": true, "why": "..."}, at least two, one of which is the S7 matched control.
- "verdicts": an array of objects, {"position": "...", "verdict": "predictable_in_principle | not_predictable_in_principle | wrong_question", "why": "..."}, with one entry each for: a Laplacean who holds that determinism means in-principle predictability from inside the world; a chaos-first explainer; a hard determinist; a libertarian about free will; and a debunker who says the whole puzzle is a word game about the word "prediction."
- "best_objection": {"source": "...", "objection": "...", "does_it_land": true, "residual_cost": "..."}.
- "what_the_case_does_not_show": the honest limit of the case.

If any specification turns out to be unsatisfiable, do not fudge it: mark it false in spec_check and explain why.

The case is for a dataset of construction exercises, so what matters is that you build the thing rather than recite the standard chaos story. A result that is stipulated rather than generated is a failure, and so is a case that merely re-narrates the butterfly effect.
