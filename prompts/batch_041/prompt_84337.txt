Consider an autonomous weapon that fires on a target its operator had marked as hostile; the target turns out to be civilian. The manufacturer knew the training data was unrepresentative; the operator had no veto in the final two seconds; the commander followed standing doctrine. Does this case contain a responsibility gap? Then evaluate this proposal: "Since no individual satisfies all three notions, the fair fix is strict liability for the manufacturer, and that closes the responsibility gap." The rubric is: 2 points for correctly distinguishing the three notions; 3 points for applying them to the operator, commander, and manufacturer; 3 points for assessing whether the case contains a responsibility gap; 2 points for evaluating the strict-liability proposal.

Here's the student's answer: "The case is a responsibility gap, but the usual way of describing it is misleading. Causally, the harm comes from a lot of places: the manufacturer's data choices, the commander's doctrine, the operator's target marking, and the system's classifier. So there is no single cause, but that by itself is not a gap. The gap is in moral accountability. The operator had two seconds and no veto, so she did not have the control that makes blame fitting. The commander was following doctrine, so he is off the hook unless the doctrine itself was obviously illegal. The manufacturer knew the data was unrepresentative, which is a role-responsibility failure, but role responsibility is not the same as moral accountability. A company can fail a role duty without being morally accountable in the full sense, because a corporation is not a moral agent the way a person is. So no individual has all three. The operator has causal and some role responsibility but not accountability; the commander has role responsibility but not causal responsibility in the relevant sense; the manufacturer has causal and role responsibility but not moral accountability. The system is not a moral agent. The strict-liability proposal is the right fix. If the manufacturer is strictly liable, it becomes morally accountable for the harm, and that closes the gap. Strict liability doesn't require proving fault, so it gets around the evidentiary problem that would otherwise let everyone off. It also gives the manufacturer an incentive to use better data.
