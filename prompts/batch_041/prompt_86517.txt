During an argument with a friend about a well-known disaster — he holds that the official account is a cover-up — I observed that years of investigation have turned up nothing supporting what he alleges, and he waved the point away with the line "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence." I would like a careful inventory of what that sentence can actually be claiming. Separate the readings: that nobody has yet looked, so there is no evidence in either direction; that the alleged fact is the kind of thing that would leave no traces; that the failure to find what we would expect to find under a hypothesis is itself evidence against it, and what would have to be true of the relevant likelihoods for that to hold; that a hypothesis which predicts its own concealment makes the missing evidence irrelevant rather than probative; and that the sentence is at bottom a claim about who bears the burden of proof rather than about confirmation at all. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible principle, or simply false, and what it would license someone to conclude. Then tell me which reading my friend must be relying on for the line to do the work he needs it to do, and whether he can hold that reading without it collapsing into the claim that no observation could ever count against his view. A menu with verdicts, please, rather than an essay on conspiracy theories.
