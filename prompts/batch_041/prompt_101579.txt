Hegel is often read as holding that war is not simply an evil to be abolished but has an ethical and political function in the life of states: it can prevent civil society from hardening into a mere system of private interests, and it expresses the sovereignty of a state in its relations with other states. Taking that reading as your baseline, rate each of the following six contemporary phenomena on a 0–10 scale for how well it fits Hegel's account of war's ethical significance, where 0 means entirely alien to that account and 10 means fully consonant with it: (a) nuclear deterrence resting on mutually assured destruction; (b) multilateral peacekeeping missions authorized by the United Nations; (c) drone strikes by a state against non-state armed groups on foreign territory; (d) combat operations conducted by private military contractors on a state's behalf; (e) economic sanctions imposed to force a change of regime; (f) a small state's defensive war against invasion. Give each rating with one or two sentences of justification, then identify which single item is the hardest case for the reading and explain why. Finally, say whether any of the six would fail to count as war at all in the sense Hegel's account requires, and what turns on that classification.
