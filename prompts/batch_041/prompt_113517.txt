I am preparing a graduate seminar on how to read philosophers whose politics are repellent, and I want the centrepiece to be a document that two camps in the Frege literature could both sign. The separatists hold that the diary Frege kept near the end of his life, with its antisemitic and authoritarian political remarks, is evidentially inert with respect to his logic, semantics, and philosophy of mathematics: morally revealing about the man, perhaps, but not a reason to revise or reinterpret a single philosophical claim. The integrationists hold that the diary does bear on the philosophy: that Frege's conception of objectivity, his insistence on the sharp separation of the logical from the psychological, and his Platonism about thoughts are not merely accompanied by the political outlook but illuminated by it, and that the philosophy is in some way compromised or implicated. Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two camps.

The document should open with a jointly acceptable, non-quoting description of the diary's relevant content, so that both sides agree on what is being explained. It should then have a numbered section of claims both sides can sign; a section stating the remaining disagreements, with each side's position phrased in wording that side would accept; and a table of cruxes. It must distinguish at least four possible relations between the politics and the philosophy, namely upstream causation (politics shaping philosophy), downstream causation (philosophy shaping politics), common cause (both flowing from some third factor), and structural resonance (no causal link, but a philosophically interesting isomorphism), and it must require each side to say which relation it is asserting and what would count as evidence for it. It must also distinguish at least four senses in which the diary might bear on the philosophy: evidential bearing on the content of particular claims; bearing on their motivation or social function; bearing on their truth or justification; and bearing on how we should teach, commemorate, or morally assess the philosopher.

For each crux, record the case or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, and each side's prediction about it. Two cruxes I want treated explicitly: first, whether Frege's anti-psychologism is a thesis about the grounds of logical truth or merely about the subject matter of logic, since the integrationist case is much stronger on the former reading; second, whether the independence of thoughts from any thinker makes the identity and capacities of thinkers irrelevant to logic in a way that parallels the political downgrading of whole human groups. Ask both sides to state what would count as evidence against their own position, to identify anything both agree would not settle the matter, and to say whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal. The collaboration is not to adjudicate who is right. Do not supply citations, dates, or titles; where a particular passage or episode is needed, describe it in neutral terms without quoting.
