ok so this is a bit of a weird ask. i'm in a reading group that's spent the whole term on the axiom of choice and its equivalents — well-ordering, the ultrafilter lemma, all of it — and next tuesday is the last session, on zorn's lemma. my friend who's finishing an MFA keeps giving me grief that nobody in my department has ever written anything that isn't a lemma, so i want to close the session by actually reading out a poem. villanelle, strict form: nineteen lines, five tercets and a quatrain, two rhyme sounds, refrains where they belong. i picked that form because i think it's already doing the thing the lemma does — the two refrains come back no matter how far a stanza wanders off, the way the proof keeps pushing the chain higher and never actually arrives anywhere. i want the two refrains to be the two halves of the lemma: one of them the hypothesis (every chain is bounded above), the other the conclusion (so there's an element with nothing above it). and i want the quatrain, where the refrains finally sit side by side, to be the moment the poem admits that the thing it promised is only maximal, not greatest. somebody who knows the lemma should hit those last two lines and think: yeah, maximal, not maximum.

a couple of things i'd like you to get right, since i'm reading this to people who will heckle me. the upper bound of a chain need not be an element of the chain — that one trips up half the group every time, and i'd love it in there somewhere. and the proof runs by contradiction: suppose nothing is maximal, then every chain has something strictly bigger above it, build the chain by transfinite recursion, blow past every cardinality you can name, done. which means the lemma hands you an element it never names. i think the poem should share that problem — it should be structurally unable to point at the maximal element it keeps promising, so the closing refrains land as an existence claim with no witness. also there's the boring caveat that the poset has to be nonempty or you get nothing at all; if that fits, great, if it's a drag on the poem, leave it out.

please don't make it about faith, or about how mathematics is like love, or about how the axiom of choice is believing in something you can't see. i want a poem about a lemma. it doesn't have to be funny, it doesn't have to resolve, and it doesn't need any math in it that a first-year grad student couldn't cash out — i can state the lemma and i've watched the transfinite recursion proof twice, but i definately couldn't reproduce it from memory, so if you lean on a step past that, say it plainly in the verse instead of gesturing at it. honestly if it ends with the refrains meeting and the meeting being a bit of a letdown, that's the poem. it's alot to ask, i know.
