ok this is gonna sound like a weird thing to post here but i just got back from a checkup where the doctor told me im "borderline" for high blood pressure and "pre-diabetic" and wants to start me on meds, and its kind of rattled me — like, do i actually have a disease, or did someone just move a number. before i go back and argue with him i want to make sure my own views about this arent a total mess. heres where ive landed:

1. A disease is a part of the body failing to do its job. thats a fact about biology, full stop — it doesnt depend on what anybody values. if your arteries or your pancreas are malfunctioning, thats true whether or not a culture happens to care about it.

2. But the cutoffs are obviously cooked up. no artery knows whether its 129 or 131. some committee just picks a number, and at one point they moved the line and a ton of people "got" high blood pressure overnight. and even "normal" is slippery — normal compared to who? all adults? guys my age? guys my size? pick a different comparison group and you get a different answer about whether im sick.

3. Whether something is a disease and whether its worth treating are two different questions. its only a medical problem if its actually harming me, and "harming me" is a value judgment, not a lab result.

4. The science settles whether something is a disease. like when psychiatry took homosexuality out of the diagnostic manual — that wasnt a moral fashion, that was the field correcting a mistake.

5. Theres a real line between treating and enhancing. taking a pill to get my numbers back to normal is fine; taking the same pill to push them past normal is not.

so which of these actually fight with each other? im pretty sure at least a couple do and i honestly cant tell which. rank them worst to least bad and tell me the cheapest one to drop, and what id be giving up if i drop it. please dont tell me theyre all fine together — i know thats not true, i just dont know where the crack is.
