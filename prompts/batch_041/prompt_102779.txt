ok so I'm a 4th-year in political theory and I've got to hand my committee a short statment of position next month, and before I embarrass myself I want to check that my own views actually hang together. Here's the pile, roughly:

(1) I'm a harm-principle person. Speech may be restricted only to prevent harm to others; offense, disgust, or the bare fact that other people hate what you're saying is never a reason on its own.
(2) I still think targeted harassment campaigns and genuine incitement are restrictable. Those aren't "offense," they're harm — the harassment does something to the target, it isn't just the target feeling bad.
(3) The reason free speech is valuable, at bottom, is epistemic: we get closer to the truth when claims have to survive contest. That's the load-bearing justification for me — not autonomy, not democracy.
(4) I'm fine with platforms demoting or removing anti-vax and election-denial stuff, and I'd like to see more of it. I know that's not the state, but still.
(5) The harm principle is a principle about coercion by the state. Private platforms aren't the state, so (4) doesn't even touch (1).
(6) I've been chewing on something I've started calling epistemic flooding — a coordinated campaign where nobody threatens anyone and no single claim is even that inflammatory, but the sheer volume and repetition make some false thing common knowledge, and the people who end up believing it are worse off for it. I think this is one of the worst things happening right now and that there should be some mechanism to push back on it. But I don't think it's "harm" in the (1) sense, and I really don't want to just stretch "harm" until it swallows everything, because then (2) stops being principled and becomes an excuse.

Where's the body buried? I'm pretty sure (4)+(5) is the easy one — tell me whether it actually dissolves or whether I'm just waving my hands at it. I've also got a nagging feeling that the epistemic bit in (3) and the harm bit in (1) aren't really the same argument wearing two hats, but I can't tell if that's a genuine problem or just me. And (6) is either the thing that breaks the whole set or the thing that was always already hiding inside (2), and I honestly can't tell which.

So: rank what's actually in tension by how bad it is, tell me which apparent conflict isn't one and why, and for each real one say what would have to give and what it would cost me to give it. Don't just tell me it's all fine — if the cheapest fix is dropping the absoluteness of (1), say that out loud.
