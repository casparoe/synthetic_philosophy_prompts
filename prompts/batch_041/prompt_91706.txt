I keep meeting an analogy that's meant to settle the whole business: that a culture's norms stand to the actions of the people who live under them as the grammar of a language stands to the sentences its speakers utter. A friend in my reading group leans on it hard, and says it shows that asking whether Calvinist beliefs caused capitalist behaviour is confused in the same way as asking what caused this sentence to be grammatical — the norms don't push anybody along, they only make what people do intelligible. I'd like an audit of the analogy rather than a verdict by fiat: what lines up with what (in prose, please — no tables, headings, bullets or bold, I read these on a phone at breakfast), which parts of the parallel are doing the real work and which are scenery, and where it breaks in ways that matter for the conclusion he wants. Does the conclusion survive the breakages, or was the analogy carrying it all along? And if it's a poor analogy, what would be a better one. Tell me as well how confident you are in what you claim, and which single point you're least sure about. For what it's worth, I spent thirty years asking what makes a difference to what, so I'd like to know what would count as evidence here.
