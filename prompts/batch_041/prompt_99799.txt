I have become convinced of a higher-order theory of consciousness, in particular the higher-order thought (HOT) version of it: what makes a mental state conscious is not any intrinsic feature of that state, but the fact that the subject has a suitable higher-order representation of it — paradigmatically, a thought to the effect that she is in that state. First-order states that are not so targeted are not conscious; the higher-order representation itself need not be conscious; and the theory is meant to capture phenomenal consciousness, not merely the capacity to report or otherwise use a state. Tell me what would count against it.

I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they would be, from fatal to merely awkward, and I want them to include, but not be limited to:

- cases in which the higher-order representation misfires — the first-order state it purports to represent is absent or misdescribed — and the theory is still forced to say that something is conscious;
- the worry that the theory ties consciousness to a representation that can float free of the first-order state it is supposed to render conscious, so that the two come apart;
- the charge that it demands conceptual machinery (a concept of one's own mental state) that creatures we take to be conscious — infants, many animals — may lack;
- the regress question of whether the higher-order representation must itself be conscious in order to do its work, and whether denying that is coherent rather than merely convenient;
- whatever the neuropsychological evidence about the role of prefrontal cortex in conscious experience actually establishes, as opposed to what it is commonly taken to establish.

For each item, say what the committed higher-order theorist would herself concede as counting against her view, as distinct from what only critics assert on her behalf. Then tell me what the position predicts that a global-workspace or a first-order rival does not, which of these defeaters it can absorb by a principled qualification and which it cannot, and whether the resulting view risks becoming unfalsifiable — and if it does, whether that is a genuine defect for a theory of this kind or simply the price of any theoretical posit of comparable scope.
