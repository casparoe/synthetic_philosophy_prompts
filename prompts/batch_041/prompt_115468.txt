Write a short story set in a near-future town where the law has gone full endurantist: every person is treated as one and the same self from birth to death, so courts can bind you now for contracts, debts, and crimes your future self hasn't done yet. The protagonist is a perdurantist holdout—she insists she's a four-dimensional worm made of distinct temporal parts and that her future self is a different person in the morally relevant sense—and she's just been served papers on behalf of a self she hasn't become. Make the metaphysics drive the plot, not decorate it: no lectures, no name-dropping philosophers, and don't let either side be obviously right. I want the reader to feel the force of both views by the end. Also, I'd rather you not use the words endurantism or perdurantism in the story itself.
