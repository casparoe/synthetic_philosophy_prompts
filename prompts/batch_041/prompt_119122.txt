I do causal inference for a living — DAGs, potential outcomes, do-calculus, diff-in-diff, synthetic control, event studies, the whole toolkit — and I've been reading a lot of environmental history on the side, the stuff that treats epidemics, volcanic eruptions and climate shifts as "historical actors" instead of just backdrop. Every time I read it I feel like I can almost translate the whole debate into my vocabulary and then something slips through my fingers. Can you do the translation properly, and then give me the residue report?

Here's what I want translated, one at a time, as faithfully as you can:

1. The strong claim that non-human causes — pathogens, volcanic forcing, slow climate change — are genuine causes (or even agents) in history, not scenery.
2. The historians' counter that this flattens contingency, and that what they want is an explanation of this particular sequence of events, not an average effect over hypothetical worlds.
3. The charge that environmental determinism is a sin — both an explanatory sin (just-so stories, cherry-picked cases) and a political one.

Put each of those into my language as best you can: treatments and interventions, exogenous shocks, natural experiments, confounders, mediators, colliders, effect modifiers, latent variables, path dependence, external validity, the single-unit / n=1 problem. I'm fluent on the CI side so don't dumb that down, but I'm a tourist in the historiography — don't assume I know the literature or the internal feuds.

Then the part I actually care about. For each thing you translated, tell me what survived cleanly, what only survived with distortion, and what had no counterpart at all. Specifically:

- Does calling germs or volcanoes "actors" say anything more than "nodes in a graph with a nonzero coefficient"? If not, is the historians' insistence on agency just rhetoric, or does it carry a claim — about purposes, about moral standing, about what kind of explanation is even wanted — that my formalism genuinely can't state?
- Is "determinism" in "environmental determinism" the same determinism as in a structural causal model, or is that a pun that's hiding the real dispute?
- The political charge: I understand the p-hacking version of the objection, that one lives fine in my world. The other version, that the whole enterprise is pernicious regardless of how well it's done — does my vocabulary have any slot for that, or is it a category I simply don't have?
- And the "one damn thing after another" point: when a historian says the interesting thing is how a particular society responded to a shock, my instinct is to call that mediation or heterogeneity and move on. Is my instinct right, or is that exactly where the translation loses the plot?

For each piece of residue, give me a verdict: is this my framework missing something it would need to add, or is the original claim less clear than it sounds and the residue is where it goes vague? Don't smuggle in my assumptions and call it a faithful translation — the failures are the interesting part.
