I'm applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship at a centre that funds work on the boundary between philosophy and applied welfare economics, and I need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea is about interpersonal comparisons of well-being. The debate looks stuck between the ordinalist scepticism that such comparisons are not scientifically respectable and the various constructive programmes — extended preferences, level comparability, self-reported scales — that answer the sceptic by building a scale. Both sides treat comparability as an all-or-nothing property of the well-being relation. I want to replace that with a graded notion: a comparison between two people is robust to the extent that it keeps its sign across every scaling of the underlying measure that we have independent reason to admit, so that what matters for practice is not comparability but invariance under the admissible class. Some comparisons look robust on this test (a year of severe depression against a month of mild back pain); others look fragile (one person's mild inconvenience against another's moderate one). The result I am fishing for is that robustness and validity come apart: a comparison can survive every admissible convention and still be wrong, if the conventions share a bias, so a robustness-first methodology would be a mistake — but not for the sceptic's reason, and not in a way that leaves practice where it was.

Turn this into a proposal. State the question so that it has a determinate answer; I think there are really two, whether a non-trivial class of robust comparisons exists and whether robustness is any guide to validity. Explain why the existing positions leave both open, and press hardest there, because I don't want the proposal to read as an application of a familiar distinction. Set out the method — formal work on the admissible class of scalings, plus case studies from existing survey instruments in which the same comparison is made under different scaling assumptions — and say why this approach is the right one rather than the obvious alternatives. Say what a result would look like, what would count as the project failing (an empty or trivial robust class, or robustness and validity in irreducible tension with no principled way to choose between them), and give a realistic scope for twelve months.
