For most of my working life I was an experimental physicist, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of science on my own. Lakatos's account of research programmes has been the most illuminating thing I have found: the picture of a hard core protected by a belt of auxiliary hypotheses and steered by a positive heuristic captures, better than anything else I know, the way my own field argued with itself. But I have run into a difficulty I cannot resolve, and I suspect I am either misreading him or asking his framework to do work it was never designed for.

The difficulty is this. Lakatos would have us grade a programme by whether its successive versions predict novel facts, and call it degenerating when it merely accommodates, after the fact, whatever anomaly the world presents. Yet he also insists that the verdict is retrospective: a programme that looks sterile for a decade may be vindicated later, so no one is entitled to condemn it in real time. If the verdict is genuinely retrospective, I cannot see what the positive heuristic is supposed to do for the scientist who must choose a programme now, before the history is written. Nor can I pin down what he means by a novel fact: novel relative to the programme's previous version, relative to what the community already believed, or relative to a rival programme?

Make it concrete for me. Take two programmes that fit all the current data equally well. Programme A has, on eleven separate occasions over fifteen years, met an anomaly by adding a parameter and deriving a further consequence of the same general form, each of which was then confirmed. Programme B has never been modified; it made one very specific prediction about a quantity nobody had thought to measure, and the measurement came out as predicted. My younger colleagues call A degenerate because it only ever patches itself, and B progressive on the strength of its single bold hit; I am not persuaded, since every patch of A's was independently confirmed. Please explain how Lakatos's framework would adjudicate this, what the most defensible reading of his criterion of novelty is, and whether the retrospective and the real-time faces of his methodology can be reconciled, or whether the tension I have found is genuine.
