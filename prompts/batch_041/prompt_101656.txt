Buddhism is deeply consequentialist in the sense that actions are evaluated by their results, and karma is literally a causal law linking actions to future suffering and happiness. Unlike deontology, Buddhism doesn't treat rules as intrinsically binding; even the precepts can be overridden when compassion requires it, which is why the bodhisattva can sometimes break a precept to save beings.
