Thirty-five years as a field ecologist and now I'm spending my evenings reading philosophy, so appologies in advance for whatever I mangle here. The reading group I've joined wants me to write down my commitments about meaning so they can take a poke at them, and I'd rather find the cracks myself first. Here's where I've landed: (1) I'm a thoroughgoing physicalist — if it's real it's physical, and a finished physics would fix everything there is to fix; (2) when you and I both mean rabbit, that we mean the same thing is a fact about us, and if we didn't mean the same thing that would be a fact too, not a matter of taste; (3) I'm convinced by Quine's gavagai business — the informant points and says gavagai, and there's simply no fact of the matter whether it means rabbit, or undetached rabbit parts, or a rabbit-stage; (4) but I don't take that to mean nature has no joints — I spent my life on species and functional guilds and I'd bet the ranch that some groupings are the real ones; (5) I do think a linguist who lives with a community for ten years and gets the grammar and the stories right has got the language right, not just a workable approximation; (6) beliefs and desires are real causes of behaviour, not handy fictions. I'd also say my old field assistant and I, who never learned a word of each other's language, definately meant the same thing when we both pointed at the same bird and used our own words for it. So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and — ranked by how bad it is if I keep both — what's the cheapest thing to give up in each case? And is there one revision that cleans up most of it?
