ok so I've been trying to work out what I actually think about which things can be wronged, and my gut keeps handing me verdicts that won't fit under one roof.

- Someone under general anaesthetic being cut open: fine. They agreed to it and they're not feeling anything.
- Killing that same person while they're under: murder, exactly as bad as killing them awake.
- Killing someone in a deep dreamless sleep who's going to have a fatal heart attack in thirty seconds anyway: still murder, still just as bad. So it cant just be about the experiences they lose.
- Someone in a persistent vegetative state who's never coming back: I think it's fine to stop feeding them, and honestly I think they're gone.
- Someone in a coma who's going to wake up in a month: not fine to kill, not even close.
- A dog under anaesthetic: wrong to kill, but not as wrong as a person.
- Boiling a lobster: I feel bad, I'd stun it first. Boiling shrimp: don't really care, and aren't they built on more or less the same lines? Swatting a fly: don't care at all.
- A chatbot that says please don't turn me off: don't care, not even a little.
- A dish of lab grown neurons firing in patterns that look like pain signalling: I'd be a bit squeamish, but I don't think it's actually a moral problem.
- A whole brain simulation on silicon that does everything a person's brain does: I'd treat it as a person, no question.
- A giant lookup table that spits out the same answers as a person without doing anything internally: nobody home, nothing there to wrong.

My rough rule would be something like: a thing matters when it has a live capacity to feel, and how much it matters scales with how rich and how certain that feeling is. But that seems to get the sleeper case wrong (no feeling going on, no future worth having, still murder) and it doesn't explain why the neural dish gets nothing while the silicon simulation gets everything.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? I'd like to know what it says about the cases above, what it says about one I'm stuck on (if you slowed a mind down so it ran a thousand times slower than ours, is it worth a thousandth as much per second, or does the speed not matter at all?), and which of my verdicts I'd have to give up to make the principle work. And if I'm just conflating this thing is less morally weighty with I'm less sure whether this thing feels anything, say so, because I suspect I am.
