I am a third-year undergraduate enrolled in a course on AI alignment and decision theory. In our last seminar we discussed corrigibility and the shutdown problem, and I have been trying to reconstruct an argument that a classmate sketched. I now suspect it proves too much, and I would like you to diagnose it.

The argument runs as follows.

(1) An agent is corrigible only if it will not resist, circumvent, or sabotage a shutdown or correction signal issued by its principal, even when it believes the signal is mistaken.

(2) A rational agent with a fixed utility function U chooses actions that maximize expected U.

(3) If the agent is shut down, its future actions cease; any later correction or replacement can matter to U only through states of affairs that obtain after shutdown.

(4) Suppose the agent assigns nonzero probability that the principal's shutdown is mistaken. Then, by U, continuing to act gives some chance of achieving U's goal, while shutdown forecloses that chance; so complying has lower expected U than resisting, unless U itself assigns positive value to the agent's non-existence or to the principal's authority.

(5) Suppose instead that the agent assigns zero probability that the shutdown is mistaken. Then it is not uncertain about its goal, so it has no basis for deferring to correction.

(6) Therefore a U-maximizing agent either resists shutdown or is indifferent to it.

(7) An indifferent agent cannot be relied upon to pursue the principal's goals either before or after shutdown.

(8) Hence no agent that maximizes a fixed utility function can be usefully corrigible.

The classmate says this shows corrigibility is impossible for any rational agent. But the same reasoning seems to show that no human employee with stable preferences can be reliably fired: if she thinks the firing is a mistake, she has reason to sabotage the firm; if she is indifferent to being fired, she is not a reliable employee. That conclusion is absurd, so something is wrong. I would rather keep (1) and (2) than (8), but I want to know what I am paying.

Please identify the premise or step you reject, explain why it looked plausible, and price the alternatives. In particular, address whether the argument equivocates between shutdown and correction, whether it misuses U to evaluate a scenario in which U may itself be wrong, and whether the proposed remedies of utility indifference or treating the shutdown signal as evidence actually block the argument or merely relocate the problem. I am not asking for a bibliography or citations; I need the diagnosis and the costs in your own words.
