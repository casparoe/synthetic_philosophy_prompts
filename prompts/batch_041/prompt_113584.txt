I'm an undergraduate member of the board of our campus food co-op, and I'm taking a seminar on Aristotle's ethics and its reception, which is probably why the manager's latest proposal reads to me like a philosophy problem. He wants to move the store onto a mutual-credit scrip: members earn credits by working shifts, spend them on groceries at one credit per hour of work regardless of the task, and cannot convert credits back into dollars. His cover memo says Aristotle is on his side, since Aristotle treated money as a matter of convention rather than nature, so replacing it should be a technical question rather than a moral one. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's board meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and it should engage the manager's appeal seriously rather than just restating co-op principles. I want it to say what work Aristotle thinks money actually does in exchange, and whether a scrip whose unit is fixed by labour time can do that work or merely stipulates the commensurability he thought had to be explained, and to connect that to his distinction between acquiring goods for use and acquiring wealth for its own sake, since the proposal is meant to keep us on the first side of that line. The treasurer's practical objection, that a flat hourly rate makes the unpleasant shifts unfillable, belongs to the same question and shouldn't be handled separately. If I've got this wrong, if Aristotle's analysis of exchange can't be asked to settle a decision like ours or the manager's reading is the better one, say so plainly rather than writing me a smooth memo.
