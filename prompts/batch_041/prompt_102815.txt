I teach an upper-level seminar, cross-listed between the law faculty and the philosophy department, on rules that govern their own revision — the kind of self-amending machinery you meet in the game Nomic, in constitutional entrenchment clauses, and in the literature on paradoxes of legal self-reference. For the take-home final I want one essay question that a student cannot answer by reciting the familiar "paradox of self-amendment" (the stock observation that a rule authorising its own amendment seems to pull itself up by its own bootstraps), and cannot answer by listing the names of the usual positions. I want them to work through a concrete code, to tell a merely intricate conflict of provisions apart from a genuine self-referential loop, and to commit to a verdict. Please build the question around the following invented code, which I will reproduce on the exam paper as an appendix; do not ask students for any citations, reading, or external authorities — they sit the exam closed-book with nothing to consult.

The Code of the Lodestar Association:
- Article 1. The Code may be amended only by a resolution passed at a general meeting of the Association.
- Article 2. A resolution that amends the Code requires the affirmative vote of two-thirds of the members present and voting.
- Article 3. Article 2 may be amended or repealed only by the affirmative vote of three-quarters of the members present and voting; and this Article 3 may be amended or repealed only by the unanimous vote of all the members of the Association.
- Article 4. The vote required for the adoption of a resolution is the vote required by the Code as it stood at the opening of the meeting at which the resolution is put.
- Article 5. Where two provisions of the Code conflict, the provision that addresses the matter more specifically prevails; where neither is more specific, the provision adopted later prevails.
- Article 6. A resolution is void if its operative effect includes lowering the vote required for its own adoption; and no resolution is valid by virtue of a provision that the resolution itself enacts.

At a general meeting the following resolution is moved: "Article 3 is repealed; Article 2 is amended so that amendment of the Code requires only a simple majority of the members present and voting; Article 6 is repealed; and this resolution takes effect immediately upon its adoption." Seventy per cent of the members present and voting vote in favour; the remainder vote against; no one abstains. The question should ask whether the resolution is valid, and should require the student to state what vote the resolution in fact required and why, to say whether a resolution of this kind can be valid in part or only as a whole, to work out how Articles 3, 4, 5 and 6 interact, and — the part I care about most — to decide whether the Code contains a genuine paradox of self-reference or only an unresolved conflict, and to identify the smallest single amendment that would dissolve whatever puzzle remains.

Deliver your response in exactly these three named sections, in this order. (1) "The question": the exam question precisely as the students will read it, 120–180 words, fully self-contained, naming the appendix code and the facts of the vote. (2) "Model answer": about 800 words, written in the voice of an outstanding candidate who reaches a definite verdict rather than surveying options, and who shows where the "it's a paradox" reflex is too quick and where a real loop does or does not arise. (3) "Marking scheme": six must-hit points worth a total of 20 marks, each with its mark value and a one-line note naming the specific error that should cost a candidate that point.
