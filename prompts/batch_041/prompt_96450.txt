I recently left a graduate programme in philosophy of biology to join a behavioural ecology lab, and my first assignment is to draft the interpretation section of our paper on a newly described courtship display. My supervisor classifies displays according to a scheme she has applied consistently, and because she is in the field and will see the draft only once it is finished, I want to anticipate her reaction before I commit to a reading. As best I can reconstruct her classifications from her review chapter and from the lab's comparative table:

- A large mammal's roar, whose fundamental frequency is fixed by the size of the larynx: index, on the grounds that the organ cannot be faked.
- A gazelle's stotting leap: handicap, on the grounds that the leap earns the animal nothing and is simply the price of being believed.
- The prolonged fast that males of a bird species undertake during the courtship season: handicap.
- The warning coloration of a toxic frog, whose pigments are sequestered from the prey it eats: index.
- A small, cheap, arbitrary plumage badge in a social species, in which receivers attack males that exaggerate: convention.
- A ritual that every male performs identically and at the same metabolic cost, with no measurable relation to condition: not a signal, on the grounds that a cost everyone pays sorts nobody.
- A call whose pitch tracks ambient temperature and predicts nothing about the caller: not a signal.

Our species: males arrive at a female's perch carrying a single large prey item, which she takes and eats. There is no dance, song, or ornament; the prey is the entire display. In our data, the males who deliver the largest items are the ones in the best condition, measured by mass and parasite load, and males in poor condition either fail to secure a large item or abandon the delivery partway. The male forfeits the prey entirely and the female gains the meal.

What will she call this? I am less interested in the verdict itself than in the rule her record commits her to: what her earlier classifications jointly imply, what rival rule they leave open, which of the two you would bet on and why, and which further case would separate them. I am especially unsure whether her notion of a handicap requires the cost to be wasted on the sender, since every display she has called a handicap so far has been one from which the receiver gains nothing material, and this is the first case in which the receiver is fed.
