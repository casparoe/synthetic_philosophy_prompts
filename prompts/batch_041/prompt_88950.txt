Handout excerpt — three ways you can lean on an AI, which I argue are different in kind rather than points on a scale:

- task offloading: you hand over a step you can do yourself, and you can still check the result;
- verification offloading: you hand over a step you can't do yourself and can't independently check, but you don't treat the result as settling the matter — you can hold it as provisional, go looking for other evidence, keep the question open;
- authority offloading: you treat the result as settling the matter; it becomes your reason to stop inquiring.

Thesis: erosion of understanding tracks authority offloading, not the other two. Losing the ability to do the task yourself is a change in competence. Understanding is eroded when you're no longer in a position to evaluate, and that turns on whether you've handed over authority, not on whether you can do the arithmetic.

Question (20 points). Two junior doctors, same ward, same sepsis-risk model.

Dr A: reads the chart herself first, writes down her own read, then looks at the model's score. If the two diverge she goes and digs into why.

Dr B: opens the model's score first. She doesn't know what features it weighs and couldn't reproduce the number. Unless something about the patient in front of her obviously clashes with the score, she goes with the score and moves on.

(a) For each doctor, which category or categories of offloading is in play? Justify.
(b) On the handout's account, does either doctor show erosion of understanding? Why?
(c) Does the handout's thesis imply that Dr A's arrangement is epistemically better overall? Explain.

Student's answer:

(a) Dr A is task offloading. She can read the chart herself and she does, so she's just delegating a step she's already able to do, and she keeps her own read as a check on the model. Dr B is verification offloading. The model is proprietary, she can't reproduce the number and has no independent way to check it, so she'd have no way of catching it if it were wrong. And since she can't check it, she's handed over authority as well — if you can't check a claim you've got no option but to accept it. So B is really in two categories at once, or maybe verification offloading with authority offloading as a consequence.

Erosion, on the handout, is about where the confidence is coming from, and B's is coming from the machine.

Task offloading doesn't cost you anything as long as you can still check the output — the handout says the categories are different in kind, so being in the first one doesn't put you on the road to the third.
