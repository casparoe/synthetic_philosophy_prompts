I am designing an evaluation harness for macrohistorical theory and want an assessment of which frameworks could plausibly survive it. The harness supplies a forecaster with polity-level time series for Afro-Eurasia spanning roughly 1200 to 1900 — population estimates, urbanization rates, proxies for elite density such as counts of titled officeholders and landholding lineages, and fiscal extraction ratios — but truncates every series at 1600. Using only the truncated window, the forecaster must state, for each of several named polities, whether a major wave of elite overproduction, fiscal crisis, and internal instability follows, approximately when it peaks, and how severe it is relative to the preceding wave. No information about the later period is provided, and the scoring rewards dated, ordered, and calibrated answers rather than narrative fit.

Please rate the following frameworks on their suitability for that test, assigning each a score from 0 to 10 and sorting the whole set into tiers:

- Ibn Khaldun's asabiyyah cycle
- Pareto's circulation of elites
- Kondratieff long waves
- Sorokin's sociocultural dynamics
- Toynbee's challenge-and-response
- Tainter's diminishing returns on complexity
- Goldstone's demographic-structural theory
- Turchin's secular cycles
- Wallerstein's hegemonic cycles

Report each score as four separate sub-ratings rather than one number. First, whether the framework issues a dated and roughly point-like prediction or only a directional one. Second, whether its key parameters could be estimated from the pre-1600 window alone, without the analyst having to know the outcome being predicted. Third, whether its predicted turning points are invariant to the choice of unit — dynasty, region, or world-system — or whether they move when the unit changes. Fourth, whether a failed prediction would actually count against the framework, or whether it can be rescued indefinitely by auxiliary adjustments.

Then give the composite 0-to-10 score, state explicitly how you weighted the four dimensions, and identify which single dimension does the most work in separating your top tier from your bottom tier. Conclude by naming the framework you would stake a single prediction on if you were free to choose the polity yourself, and the framework you suspect would appear most vindicated in hindsight while in fact carrying no predictive content — and say what distinguishes the two cases. If you judge that the harness as described is incoherent or that no framework on the list could be disconfirmed by it, say so plainly and explain where the design fails.
