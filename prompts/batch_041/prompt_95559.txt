I read rationalist blogs alot and have been slowly grinding through the SEP page on Dutch book arguments for a reading group (the guy who runs it is a Bayesian about literally everything, including his fantasy football picks). And I keep having the same itch. Everyone trots these arguments out as if they settle everything — probabilism, conditionalization, the reflection-style requirement, expected utility, you name it — and then there's the accuracy-first crowd saying the whole betting setup is a red herring, because all a Dutch book shows is that a bookie could take your money, which is a fact about your wallet and not about your credences being wrong. I sort of buy that, but I don't think it lands equally on all of them, and I can never seperate the arguments that are actually doing real work from the ones that are just a betting story glued onto a conclusion we already wanted.

So here's what I want. Rank the following five things that Dutch book arguments are supposed to establish, strongest to weakest, where strength means: how much the argument actually supports the norm in question for someone who doesn't already accept the pragmatic premise that being exploitable is itself irrational. (1) that credences should obey the probability axioms — non-negativity, normalization, finite additivity; (2) that you should update by conditionalization when you learn something; (3) that you should be certain in advance that your future credences will match your current conditional credences on what you've since learned, i.e. the reflection-style requirement; (4) that preferences should be transitive / that you should maximize expected utility; (5) that your credence function should be countably additive rather than merely finitely additive — I've seen this one asserted, I think via infinitely many bets, but I've never seen the details spelled out and I suspect it's doing less than people think. Give each one a tier or a score out of 10 (your call, just be consistent), a sentence or two on what it actually gets you and where the weak joint is, and say explicitly which of the five survive the accuracy-first objection as-is versus which would have to be rebuilt on something like a gradational-accuracy or score-based footing. Then tell me which single one is load-bearing for the rest — if it collapsed, how much comes down with it — and which one you'd have expected to rank higher than it does before you thought it through. Please don't just tell me they all work in their own way; if two are genuinely tied, say so and say why, but I want the ordering forced by the arguments rather than by politeness.
