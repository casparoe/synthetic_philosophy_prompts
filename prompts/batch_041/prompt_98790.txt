I am trying to fix the shape of my own view on grounding and fundamentality, and I would like a consistency check before I try to write it up. Here is the package as it currently stands.

(1) Grounding is a real, non-causal determination relation. It holds between facts, rather than between objects, though I would not want anything I say here to turn on that choice. When one item grounds another, the first is more fundamental than the second, and this comparative ordering belongs to the world rather than to our representations of it.

(2) The relation is irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive, and it is well-founded: any grounded fact traces back through finitely many steps to facts that are not themselves grounded. Those ungrounded facts are the fundamental facts, and I take being ungrounded to be simply what being fundamental amounts to.

(3) Grounding is explanatory through and through. To say that one fact grounds another is to say that the second obtains in virtue of the first, and this "in virtue of" is the metaphysical counterpart of the "because" that figures in explanation. But I also hold that explanation, including metaphysical explanation, is a matter of what renders something intelligible to a thinker, so that what grounds what is at least partly relative to a representational scheme rather than an absolute matter.

(4) Grounding facts are themselves grounded. If x grounds y, then the fact that x grounds y is not a brute addition to reality: it is grounded in x and y, or perhaps in the essences of x and y. There are no ungrounded facts about what grounds what.

(5) The relation is necessitarian and essence-based: if x grounds y, then x grounds y of necessity, and the source of that necessity lies in the natures or essences of the items involved, rather than in some further fact about the relation itself.

(6) The grounding relation is itself ungrounded. Nothing grounds grounding, and there is no self-grounding either; the relation, its relata, and its formal features — irreflexivity, transitivity, well-foundedness — arrive together as a single structural package, no element of which is prior to the rest.

I suspect that not all of this can stand, but I cannot reliably tell which pairs are genuinely in conflict and which merely look as though they are. What I want is an audit rather than a survey. For every pair that is in real tension, tell me how severe the tension is and exactly what would have to be surrendered to relieve it; for every pair that is only apparently in tension, identify the distinction that dissolves it. Then rank the genuine tensions by severity and tell me which single revision would resolve the most while costing the least, and what that revision would cost me.
