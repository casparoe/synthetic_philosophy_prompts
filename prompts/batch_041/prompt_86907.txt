I'm a security engineer — PKI, platform stuff — so I spend my life on trust: root CAs, web of trust, trust-on-first-use, zero-trust networks where nothing is believed unless it's independently verified, and the pain of bootstrapping trust when you have no anchor to start from. I've been reading up on the epistemology of testimony and the two camps look like my world with the names filed off. Camp one: you shouldn't believe what someone tells you unless you have independent positive evidence that this kind of source is reliable in this kind of case — default deny, verify everything, no implicit trust just because someone asserted it. Camp two: that's incoherent, because if you had to independently verify every report you'd never get off the ground, so there has to be a standing entitlement to take testimony at face value absent specific reason to doubt — default allow with exceptions, TOFU, a chain of trust running back to an anchor nobody ever verified.

Restate both as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary — the primitives I actually have (identities and keys, signatures, attestation, transitive trust, revocation, threat models, the adversary, the gap between authenticating a message and trusting whoever sent it) — and don't smuggle my assumptions in and call it a translation. Then the residue report: what survives intact, what survives only with distortion, what has no counterpart at all. Three things I want you to do something with: (a) the bootstrapping case, a kid or a fresh system with no track record to reason from — is "you have to start somewhere" an argument for default-allow or just an admission that you're trusting something unverified, and does an unnoticed break in the signing scheme show that verify-everything just pushes the problem down a level; (b) a source that's perfectly reliable but insincere versus one that's sincere but sloppy — my vocabulary lumps both under bad source, the philosophers seem to care about the difference; (c) whether the difference between trusting a person and merely relying on a mechanism is something I can even represent or is just aesthetics. Then tell me straight: is the residue pointing at something my framework is missing, or is the disagreement less deep than the vocabulary gap makes it look?
