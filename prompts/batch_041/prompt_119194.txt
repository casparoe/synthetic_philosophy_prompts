Okay so this is for an essay in my ethics class and I want to actually think about it instead of writing the usual "respect other cultures" thing. Here's the position I've basically landed on: everyday conventions like tipping 20 percent, not cutting to the front of a line, waiting for the walk signal when there's not a single car for a mile, letting people off the elevator before you get on — none of these are moral obligations. They're local customs, like which side of the road you drive on. Breaking them can make you rude or weird or get you a dirty look, but being rude isn't a sin, and I dont think there's anything you owe anyone. The part I feel strongest about is visitors: if you come from a place with no tipping and you don't tip here, or you're from somewhere where everyone jaywalks and you cross on red, I don't see how you're doing anything wrong. You never agreed to the custom, nobody can expect you to know it, and "when in Rome" seems like advice about getting along rather than a rule of morality.

So what I want is the case against this, and I mean the real case, not the "just be polite" stuff. My brother's girlfriend is a philosophy major and she said something that stuck with me even though I think it's wrong: she said there are two totally different kinds of custom that look identical from the outside — ones where everyone just has to do the same thing, like which side of the road, and ones where somebody is getting the short end of the stick on purpose and the custom is basically how we all agree to keep it that way — and only the second kind can ever be an actual obligation. She also said something about norms having a "jurisdiction," which I did not follow at all. So: tell me what would count against my view. Which concrete cases, arguments, or intuitions should actually move me, ranked from "this should change my mind" down to "this is just an awkward bullet I can live with," and for each one say whether someone who holds my view would admit it counts against them or would just wave it off. Two things I'm especially stuck on: first, if the custom is unfair to begin with — like the real reason you're supposed to tip is that the restaurant doesn't pay the server enough — does that make it less binding on me, or more binding, because now there's an actual person on the other end of my decision? And second, is there any difference between a tourist passing through, someone who just moved here and is going to live here, and someone who's been here ten years and still doesn't tip? I'd definately be most convinced by a case where I can point at a specific person who got hurt, not "society" or "the social fabric." And be honest with me about whether my position is even a real position anymore or whether it's been watered down into agreeing with everybody — because if every counterexample gets answered with "well sure, but that's not the custom, that's an actual harm," then I want to know if that's a cop-out, and whether it even matters that it's a cop-out.
