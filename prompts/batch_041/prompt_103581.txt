I'm assembling a dissertation prospectus and one chapter has to justify how I carve up AI failure. The standard taxonomy separates two scenarios: an influence-seeking failure, in which a system ends up with objectives that lead it to accumulate power, resources, and control over its environment; and a slow value-drift failure, in which no system ever forms such objectives, but the values actually implemented in the world move away from ours through many individually defensible steps, so that the outcome is bad without anyone having aimed at it. My half-formed worry is that these are not two kinds of outcome but two descriptions of the same outcome, differing only in the level at which one describes it — the individual system versus the whole development process — and that as usually stated the distinction comes with no criterion that would force a given case onto one side rather than the other.

Turn this into the prospectus section. State the question so that it has a determinate answer (roughly: is there a principled criterion separating the two failure modes, and does the distinction change what anyone should do?), explain why the existing framings leave it open, describe how the analysis would actually proceed and why that method rather than a more formal or more empirical one, say what would count as showing the distinction is robust as against showing it collapses into a single phenomenon, and give an honest scope for a single chapter.
