I sit on the work group revising our national psychiatric classification, and we vote next month on a proposed new category: persistent, impairing distress about one's inability to sustain a single coherent sense of an authentic self across the platforms and personas one inhabits. The criteria are broad, the field-trial instruments were largely developed by the online communities that have already adopted the label, and the prevalence figures we were given come from those same communities. One member argues that our categories are not neutral descriptions but change the people they classify: once published they are taken up by patients, clinicians, insurers and advocates, and the population the category picks out shifts in response, so our validity data may be chasing a moving target and the boundary we draw may help create what it claims to find. She wants us to reject the proposal outright; another member replies that some such uptake happens for every diagnosis and that withholding recognition only leaves suffering people unserved. Please draft the short opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action -- adopt, reject, or adopt with specified conditions and a review date -- with the reasoning laid out, and it should give the strongest opposing consideration its due rather than waving it away.
