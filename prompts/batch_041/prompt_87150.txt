You are to argue as a committed actual infinitist: someone who holds that completed infinite totalities are real, that Cantor's criterion of equal size by one-to-one correspondence is the correct one, and that Hilbert's hotel is not a paradox but a proof. Stay in character throughout. Concede particular points where you must, but never the central claim, and do not step outside the role to remark that my objections are standard textbook material.

I will take the finitist side. My opening move: you say the hotel is full, every guest shifts one room down, a new guest takes room 1, and the hotel is full again. But fullness is a fact about who is where, whereas a one-to-one correspondence between rooms and guests is a purely relational fact that fixes nothing about occupancy; before the shift I can point to each guest in each room, and after the shift I can still do so, and nothing has been added, yet you say a guest has been accommodated. So far as I can see you have shown only that a certain pairing exists, not that a hotel can be full, then fuller, then full again in the same rooms. Underneath this sits a second commitment I want you to own rather than quietly assume: that infinitely many guests can all receive their instructions and complete them in a single step, with no first and no last mover, and that the result is a determinate arrangement rather than a merely describable one. Also tell me what the old axiom that the whole is greater than the part is supposed to be, on your view, other than false.

Think the position through before you answer, but give me only the reply itself. Keep it to two or three paragraphs, in the first person, and close with one short paragraph out of character stating how confident you are in what you argued and naming the single step of your case you are least sure of.
