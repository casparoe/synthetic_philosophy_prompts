I'm a student in a seminar on collective decision-making, and I also serve as the student representative on my university's scholarship selection committee. Our current procedure is a public, sequential vote: each member states their ranking aloud in order of seniority, and later members almost always fall in line with the early speakers. I've started to suspect that this isn't just politeness -- that the committee is losing genuinely independent information, because the first two or three speakers are not necessarily the best judges, and the rest of us have private reasons for our rankings that never get pooled. The chair defends the procedure as transparent, efficient, and respectful of experience, and says that open disagreement would waste time and damage morale. We meet in two weeks to decide whether to keep the procedure or switch to a simultaneous secret ballot followed by a structured discussion of reasons. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting: recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and engage the chair's defense seriously rather than just asserting that independent judgments are better. I'd also like the memo to say what would have to be true for the chair's defense to be correct, and what evidence from our own past decisions would distinguish a genuine convergence of judgment from a cascade.
