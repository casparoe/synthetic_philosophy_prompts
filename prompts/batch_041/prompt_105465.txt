Im putting together a prospectus for a small six-month grant (basically just me, no team) on the problem of logical omniscience, and I need you to turn a half-formed idea into an actual short proposal -- call it 3-4 pages of plan.

Heres the idea. "The problem of logical omniscience" gets stated as one problem, but I think its at least four principles bundled together, and the literature mostly assumes they stand or fall together when I don't think they do. Roughly: (1) closure under known implication -- you know P, you know P implies Q, so you know Q; (2) full closure under logical consequence -- you know P, so you know everything P entails, however long the proof; (3) knowledge of all logical truths; (4) knowledge of all logical equivalents -- you know P and Q is logically equivalent to P, so you know Q. Standard possible-worlds semantics hands you all four at once, and I suspect thats the real reason they look inseperable: validity is truth at all worlds, entailment is truth-preservation across all worlds, so the same universal quantification over worlds is doing both jobs and there's no gap for a distinction to live in.

What I want the proposal to do: treat the four as separately deniable, ask exactly which combinations of them can be jointly satisfied once you allow yourself non-standard machinery -- impossible worlds, awareness or neighborhood-style models, subject-matter / topic conditions on content, whatever you actually think does work here -- and then argue for which combination is the minimal package that still deserves to be called a theory of a competent deductive agent. I'm especially interested in whether you can have (3) without (2); that combination feels incoherent to me and I want to know if that's just my lack of imagination.

So please: state the question so it has a determinate answer, explain why the two standard moves (just deny closure; or just bolt an awareness operator onto possible-worlds semantics) leave this open, spell out the method -- what I'd actually do month by month, what the deliverable looks like at the end, what would count as the project failing rather than succeeding -- and give an honest scope check: is six months enough, and which part is most likely to blow up on me. No bibliography or reading list, I'll add citations myself, and don't just survey the literature -- the separability question is the whole point.
