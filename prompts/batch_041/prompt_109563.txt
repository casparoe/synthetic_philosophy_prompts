I've been going down a rabbit hole about why pain is bad, and I've hit a snag I can't get past on my own. There's this condition — asymbolia, I think it's called — where people with damage to a certain part of the brain will tell you quite calmly that yes, it hurts, quite badly, right there in their hand, but it doesn't bother them, they're not distressed by it and they're not especially fussed about it stopping. They can point at it, they can rate it as intense. And then there's the ordinary stuff that bothers me just as much: the exact same burning feeling is awful when you spill tea on yourself and is something people happily pay for when it's a chilli or a tattoo or a deep-tissue massage.
