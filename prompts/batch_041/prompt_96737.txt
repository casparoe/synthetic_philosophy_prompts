So I've gotten into type design as a hobby — nothing serious, just drawing letters in a notebook and messing around in FontForge — and there's a thing I keep running into that I don't have a word for, and it's starting to bug me.

Here's the thing. Take the letter a. I can make it tall or squat, wide or narrow, bold or hairline, give it serifs or take them away, slant it, add a little flare at the top, make the bowl a perfect circle or a squished oval — and it's still, obviously, an a. Anyone can glance at it and read it. But then I change one tiny thing and the whole thing collapses: if I close up the single-storey a the wrong way, or slide the bowl over to the other side of the stem, or let the stem stop short instead of crossing, suddenly it's a d, or an o, or an e, or it's just garbage. And what gets me is that the things that decide whether it's an a or not are not the things anyone notices. If you asked someone to describe an a they'd say round part on the left, stick on the right — the proportions, the style, the feel. Those are exactly the things I can change infinitely and nobody blinks. The few things that actually flip it into another letter are almost invisible — whether this stroke touches that one, which side of the stem the loop sits on, whether there's a hole there or not. It's like identity is decided by a handful of yes/no switches that are buried under all the stuff people actually see.

I've definately started noticing it other places too — I'm in a music theory class and a tune can be played on a kazoo at half speed in a different key and it's still the same tune, but move one interval by a half step and it's a different tune; and my little brother's chess set has knights carved like actual horses and knights carved like abstract blobs and they're both knights, but I'd bet if the horse's nose pointed the other way you'd still read it as a knight, whereas if you straightened the neck it'd get weird. So: is there a name for this? I know about family resemblance and prototypes, the idea that a category has no single defining feature and membership is a matter of how close you are to a typical example — but that doesn't seem to capture it, because here it isn't fuzzy at all, there are hard edges in some directions and total freedom in others, and the hard edges aren't where the typicality is. And I've heard the essentialism stuff, that people act as if categories have a hidden essence, but that's about why we behave that way, not about what the essence is made of or why it sits where it sits. Is there a term for the specific phenomenon — a small set of discrete, mostly non-salient either/or features that carry a thing's identity while everything salient is free to vary? And if there isn't one term, can you tell me what the nearest ones are, how each falls short, and give me a name and a definition that does the job? What I really want is the carving, not the label: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not (it's not just the important features, it's not just contrast with the neighbouring letters), and where its edges are — is the boundary the same for every category, or can two people disagree about which switches are load-bearing?
