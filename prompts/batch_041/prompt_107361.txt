Nietzsche sorts the uses of history into three: monumental (great exemplars to imitate), antiquarian (preserving and revering where you came from), and critical (putting the past on trial and breaking with it). I keep hitting a fourth thing his scheme doesnt seem to cover. Call it repair history: history written for a group that did something it can't fully own, whose job is neither to admire, nor to conserve, nor to convict, but to widen the frame until the wrong looks like a stage everyone was passing through, so the group can live with itself and keep going. Is that really a fourth mode, or just critical history in a soothing costume? Which of his three is it nearest to, and does the difference earn its keep? And whichever way you go, what happens to his worry that too much remembering is bad for life — does repair history feed life or quietly starve it?
