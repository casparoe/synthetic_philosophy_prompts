I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy of science, and I keep getting hung up on a practice that seems totally mundane to physicists but philosophically wierd to me. In one seminar we spent a week on how conservation of energy, momentum, and angular momentum fall out of time and space translation symmetries, and everyone treated the symmetry principles as more fundamental than the conservation laws. But in another seminar, the same people talked about conservation of energy as a law of nature that constrains what can happen, and about symmetries as just features of the laws. I was talking with a friend in physics about this and she shrugged and said it's just a useful way to organize the math, which made me think the practice might be carrying a metaphysical commitment nobody is owning up to. I've also been reading a bit about the best-system account, where laws are the axioms of the simplest deductive system that entails all the regularities, and I can't tell if that view can even make sense of a conservation law that is derived from a symmetry rather than taken as basic.

So what I want from you is a reconstruction of what this practice presupposes about the metaphysics of laws. Does treating symmetries as the source of conservation laws, while still calling the conservation laws laws, presuppose an anti-Humean governing view where laws are basic and symmetries are downstream? Does it presuppose a Humean or best-system view where laws are just systematizations and symmetries are constraints on the systematization? Or does it quietly presuppose that law is a functional kind that can be realized either way? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits with, and say where it is underdetermined, so that several incompatible theories of law would license the same way of talking. Then tell me what would actually have to change in the practice if we adopted a straightforward Humean best-system view, or a straightforward anti-Humean governing view. I'm especially interested in the Noether's theorem angle: if conservation laws are theorems derived from symmetries, does that make them less lawlike for a Humean, or does it make symmetries the real laws? Please write in American English, and don't give me a reading list or citations, I'll handle that stuff later.
