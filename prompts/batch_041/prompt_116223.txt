I'm giveing a paper at a workshop on diachonic rationality next month and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a general defence of the paper. The claim I'm defending: the money-pump argument against cyclic preferences only bites if you buy what I'm calling the Ledger Assumption — that the value of a sequence of choices is just the sum of the values of the individual choices in it, so that an agent who takes each trade because she prefers its local outcome is thereby committed to preferring the whole sequence. Drop the Ledger Assumption and the pump shows only that cyclic preferences are exploitable in a certain kind of market, not that they're irrational; the agent who won't treat her own plans as fungible isn't making a mistake, she's refusing to be the kind of thing the pump needs.

The audiance: mostly economists and decision theorists for whom coherence just is no-arbitrage; two philosophers who work on time-slice versus whole-agent rationality and get twitchy about "the agent" as something that persists across choices; someone who works on incomplete preferences; and one Kant-ish person who thinks resolute choice is weakness of will with a nicer name.

So: what will this lot actually throw at me, in their own vocabularies, ordered by how much damage it does, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply goes soft. Tell me which ones I should just concede and move on. And give me the one I'm dreading.
