So I'm in Year 12 and somehow I've ended up as the one doing the opening speech at our school's Debating Week assembly on Monday. Whole sixth form crammed into the hall, plus teachers who have to be there, plus a load of my mates who'd rather be anywhere else. Three minutes, max.

Here's what I'm stuck on. Our debating society has the exact same argument every year and nobody ever moves, and I think I've finally worked out why. It's the difference between what you might call an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber, and everyone (me included) mashes the two together:

A bubble is when you just haven't come across the other side. Nobody's lying to you, you've simply never heard the counter-arguments, so obviously you think you're right. That one's fixable — show them the other side and most people will update.

An echo chamber is the nasty one. You HAVE heard the other side, over and over. But you've been taught that the people saying it are liars, or shills, or brainwashed, so every time their argument comes up it makes you MORE sure you're right. Which means the usual fix — "here's the other side, aren't they reasonable" — does nothing, and can actually backfire, beacuse the outside evidence is the exact thing the chamber has already trained you to ignore.

I want the speech to do something with this, not just explain the two words. And here's the bit I can't solve: I can't just stand up and list the other side's best arguments, because that's precisely the move an echo chamber is immune to. So it has to work some other way, and I don't know how yet.

Stuff it needs to do: land for the people who are actually in a chamber (a couple of the debaters, honestly) without them just feeling got at and switching off; not be pointless for the people who couldn't care less and are only there because it's compulsory; and be a bit self-deprecating, so it isn't me lecturing from above — I want to use me and my mates being definately convinced the school's new phone policy is some sort of plot, and treating any reassurance as further proof of the plot, as the example.

Don't bother naming books or authors or studies, I just want the speech itself, no source list at the end. Can you write it? It should sound like an actual sixth former talking, not an essay.
