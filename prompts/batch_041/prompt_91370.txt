Alright, I need an outside read on something my game theory reading group has been stuck on for about three months, and I genuinely can't tell anymore whether we're having a real disagreement or just talking past each other.

Here's the little game that set it off. Two players. Row picks Top or Bottom, Column picks Left, Middle, or Right. Payoffs, written as (row, column):

Top / Left (1,1), Top / Middle (1,0), Top / Right (1,1)
Bottom / Left (1,1), Bottom / Middle (0,0), Bottom / Right (1,1)

So Top weakly dominates Bottom -- Top is at least as good in every column, and the strictness comes entirely from the Middle column; if Middle weren't there, Top and Bottom would be identical. And Middle is weakly dominated for Column: it hands her 0 no matter what, while Left and Right hand her 1.

Now iterate eliminating weakly dominated strategies and watch the order matter. Order one: kill Middle first (weakly dominated for Column). Now Top and Bottom are identical against the columns that are left, so Bottom stops being weakly dominated and survives -- you're left with both Top and Bottom. Order two: kill Bottom first (Top weakly dominates it). Middle is still weakly dominated, so kill it too, and you're left with only Top. Same game, same definition of 'dominated', two different surviving sets depending on which order you happen to go in.

Half the group says: fine, that just shows the right concept is iterated *strict* dominance, which doesnt care about order and is exactly what common knowledge of rationality buys you, so weak dominance should be dropped. The other half says: no, iterated elimination of weakly dominated strategies is the correct notion of rationality -- a weakly dominated strategy is one you'd never play if you actually thought it through, since there's another strategy that does at least as well against everything and strictly better against something -- and the order-dependence is a defect of the *procedure*, not the *concept*. So fix the procedure: take the union over all elimination orders, or the intersection, or only call a strategy eliminable if it's dominated under every order, or allow eliminations by mixed strategies. Then someone says mixed strategies settle it, because once you let a mixture do the dominating, a pure strategy can be weakly dominated by a mixture even when no pure strategy weakly dominates it -- so the tidy 'eliminate pure by pure' picture was never right, and we should allow mixtures all the way through. And someone else says that settles nothing and makes the order-dependence worse, and we're back to the start.

So my actual question: what kind of disagreement is this? Is it a substantive disagreement about what rationality requires? A disagreement about what a solution concept is even for -- predicting behavior, advising a player, or characterizing what's compatible with common knowledge of rationality? Or is the order-dependence just a formal fact that means 'what survives iterated weak dominance' is an ill-posed question we should stop asking?

My own guess, tell me if it's off: the two sides are answering different questions under one word -- one is asking what a rational player should *do*, the other is asking what a player can rationally *believe* and then do -- and 'rationality' is stretched over both, so no amount of argument settles it until someone says which question the solution concept is meant to answer. If that's right, what's the test that would reveal which question a given concept is actually answering? And if instead you think the question is just ill-posed, what replaces it -- is there a better question, or do we admit we're picking a convention and move on?
