I want a case analysis of the 2016 dispute over the COMPAS recidivism scores — ProPublica's finding that the instrument's errors fell unequally on Black and white defendants, and Northpointe's reply that the instrument was calibrated and equally predictive for both groups — read through the impossibility results in algorithmic fairness, and then reversed, so that the case is used to test the results rather than only to illustrate them.

The facts you need are supplied here, and the analysis should not turn on anything beyond them. The two sides were not disputing arithmetic: both measurements are correct. ProPublica compared the error rates of the classification across groups and found them unequal; Northpointe compared predictive values and calibration within groups and found those equal. In the data, the base rate — the observed re-arrest rate — was materially higher for Black defendants than for white defendants, which is precisely the condition under which the results say that any two of calibration within groups, equal false-positive rates, and equal false-negative rates can be had, but not all three. Treat that trade-off as fixed background: do not restate it, re-derive it, or argue about whether it holds. Two further features of the episode matter and are also given: the same instrument was used at more than one kind of decision point (pretrial release in one jurisdiction, a sentencing aid in another), and the label it is trained to predict is an arrest within a fixed follow-up window rather than an offense.
