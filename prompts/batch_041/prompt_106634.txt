Write an essay that reaches and defends a verdict on a controversy surrounding the difference principle. The principle is usually glossed as a requirement of reciprocity: inequalities are permissible only when they work to the benefit of those who cooperate in producing the surplus. But it is just as often read as flatly prioritarian: maximize the position of the worst off, regardless of what they contribute. The two readings come apart on a case the familiar presentation of the original position leaves unsettled — a group that cannot or will not reciprocate, and that could be helped only by transfers from those who do. Take a side. To keep the essay from becoming a recap of the standard debate, run the argument through a modified device: suppose the parties behind the veil are ignorant not only of their social position but also of whether they will turn out to be among the reciprocators or the non-reciprocators. Does reciprocity still constrain the choice of principles, or does the device tilt toward prioritarianism — or toward a maximizing principle the parties would have rejected under the original framing? Reach a definite conclusion and justify it; do not close by granting that both readings capture part of the truth.
