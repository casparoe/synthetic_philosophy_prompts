I'm a novelist and I've been in the same writing group for eleven years — six of us, all published, all a little thin-skinned. We swap drafts, and every so often somebody turns in something close enough to another member's published book that we have to have The Conversation. We're not lawyers, we go on feel, but we've done it enough times that we've built up a kind of common law. I need to know what it says about my new novel before I send it out, because I honestly cant tell if I've crossed a line or just written in a tradition.

The calls, roughly in order:

- Somebody turned in a story with two paragraphs of another member's novel in it almost word for word, a few adjectives swapped, same rhythm, same images. Too close. Rewrite it.
- Somebody wrote a book with another member's premise — both are about a town where nobody can lie — but different characters, different sentences, different ending. Fine. A premise isn't something you own.
- Somebody used the same furniture as another member's book: the hard-drinking detective, the corrupt mayor, the storm that traps everybody in the hotel. Fine. That's the genre, not the book.
- Somebody lifted another member's invented river-bird — the coined name, the plumage, the call, all of it — into a story that was otherwise nothing like hers. Too close, we said, on the grounds that anyone who'd read her book would recognize that bird in a second.
- Somebody used another member's title and nothing else. Fine.
- Two of us independently wrote novels about grief and lighthouses. Fine, obviously.

Now me. My new novel shares one thing with a member's novel from four years ago: the architecture. Same sequence of reveals, same central twist arriving at the same point and doing the same work — and it's not a common twist, it's the kind of thing that, once you know it, you cant unsee it — same shape of ending. Everything else is mine: different plot, different people, different country, no shared sentences, no shared invented terms, no lifted images, plus a whole second storyline her book doesn't have. My fear is that if you'd read hers, you'd definately feel the outline of mine the whole way down.

Will they call it too close? I'm recusing myself from the vote, obviously. What I actually want is the rule the group has been running without quite saying so, the other rules that fit the same record just as well, and what each of those predicts about my book.

Give it to me as: a two-sentence 'Bottom line'; 'The rule they've been running' (about 150 words); 'Other rules that fit the record' (about 150 words); 'What each rule predicts for my book' (about 200 words); 'Your best guess, and which old call it hangs on' (about 150 words); and 'The case that would settle it' (about 100 words).
