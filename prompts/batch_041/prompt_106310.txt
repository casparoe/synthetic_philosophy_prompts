I am a high school senior who runs a small philosophy reading group at my school, and last semester we worked through the classic debate over definite descriptions. I have come away frustrated rather than convinced: Russell's quantificational account, Strawson's presupposition-based objection, and Donnellan's distinction between referential and attributive uses each seem to win on a different batch of cases, and I cannot tell whether they are genuine rivals or merely answering different questions. To force the issue, I have built a scorecard and would like you to fill it in.

Please rate each of the following five positions on a scale of 0 to 10 along three separate dimensions, then assign each an overall tier from S down to D, with one sentence of justification for every score you give. The positions are: (a) Russell's quantificational analysis of the definite article; (b) Strawson's view that a definite description presupposes rather than asserts the existence and uniqueness of its referent; (c) Donnellan's treatment of the referential use as a pragmatic rather than semantic phenomenon; (d) the free-logic strategy that treats an empty description as a failure of reference that renders the sentence neither true nor false; and (e) one hybrid position that you must invent yourself, combining whatever you take to be the strongest parts of the others, which I want scored on exactly the same footing as the rest. The three dimensions are: how well the position matches what ordinary speakers say about truth and falsity in awkward cases; how much extra machinery it must add to the ordinary semantics of predication; and how gracefully it survives embedding under negation, inside conditionals, and within belief reports. Please give particular attention to three cases I have found the standard debate handles badly: a description under negation where speakers want neither truth nor falsity; a description used to introduce an object in a fiction or a supposition, where nothing in reality answers to it; and a description inside a belief report where the believer has only a partial grasp of what is being described. Tell me which position handles each of those three cases worst, and whether the hybrid you invented genuinely beats the four established ones or merely relocates the problem.
