So my best friend Raj is having a going-away thing on Saturday — he's quitting his very stable bank job to go join a two-person startup working on some insane energy thing (he tried to explain it to me twice and I still dont get it, something about bacteria making fuel out of sunlight, but he says if it works it's the end of fossil fuels). Everyone's happy for him but his dad is quietly losing it, and I'm the one doing the toast. Here's my problem. Raj has gotten really into this idea he calls Pascal's mugging, which I guess he got off some blog — the thing where a mugger promises you an astronomically huge payoff for a tiny chance and by expected-value math you're supposed to hand over your wallet. Except Raj doesn't treat it as a paradox, he treats it as a life rule. Last Thanksgiving his sister told him "by that logic you should give me your wallet, because maybe I'm secretly a wizard who'll pay you back a trillion dollars," and he just said "correct, and I'm not being sarcastic." So I'm stuck: I want to stand up and genuinely bless the move he's making, but I do not want to endorse the general rule, because a rule that tells you to hand your wallet to any stranger with a good story is obviously broken, and I think half the room will clock that.

Please write me the toast, like four or five minutes. The thing I want it to actually pull off is blessing his specific gamble without blessing the principle he thinks is behind it, and I'm pretty sure the honest way to do that is to say something real about why his bet is different from the wizard bet — but not the cheap version ("well, he's my friend so I hope it works out"), I want an actual reason that the smart people in the room would nod at, and that doesn't cheat by pretending the tiny-probability-huge-payoff logic is fine when it obviously isnt. Half the room is engineers from his old job who will catch every reference to expected value, and the other half is his parents and aunts who won't know what a "utility" is, so it has to land for both. Keep it in my voice — I'm a backend dev, not a philosopher, so no fancy vocabulary — and it should sound like a slightly nervous friend talking, not a TED talk. End it on something I can actually raise a glass to.
