I am writing a novel about Newton's last years, and the pivotal scene has him alone at night, content that he has shown the universe to be a watch wound once and left to run — a machine whose Maker has no further business in it. His unpublished papers on prophecy and church history I have treated as the private hobby of a deist who had finished with God in nature, and in my draft the comets he tracked so obsessively are mere clockwork debris drifting through empty space. I would like the scene to be true to the man rather than to his reputation, so: what was Newton's God actually doing in the world, on Newton's own account, and how did that bear on the theology he kept to himself? And if the watchmaker is not his, whose picture is it, and what does it leave out?
