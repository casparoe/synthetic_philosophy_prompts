Okay so I'm working thru teh contemporary debate about luminosity and the KK principle, and I keep wondering what William James would have made of it. The setup: a mental state is luminous if, whenever you're in it, you're in a position to know you're in it. The KK principle says if you know that p, you can know that you know that p. A famous anti-luminosity argument uses a margin-for-error idea to show that even your current conscious states aren't always luminous. James, meanwhile, had his distinction between knowing a thing by acquaintance and knowing about it, and he thought the stream of consciousness is continuous and vague at the edges. So here's my question: if James were alive today and read this stuff, would he accept the anti-luminosity conclusion, or would he think it's a trick that confuses having an experience with knowing that you're having it? And what about KK? Would he say it's a real principle or just a verbal confusion? I'm especially curious about a case where you're in a mild pain that's right at the threshold of an itch—you can't tell which it is, but there's presumably a fact of the matter. Does James think you know the pain? Does he think the state is luminous? Or would his radical empiricism let him say the state is neither fully pain nor fully itch, so the luminosity question doesn't even arise? Please reason from his actual views on consciousness and knowledge, not just from pragmatist slogans.
