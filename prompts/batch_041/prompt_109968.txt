i need a poem to open a seminar i'm running next month on precomittment — ulysses contracts, entrenched clauses, the whole family of moves where a present self tries to tie up a future one. Please dont do the mast and the wax: no odysseus, no sirens, no ropes, and no addiction either, thats the example everyone reaches for. Instead here's the case, which i made up so you cant just recite something:

a city charter has an article (number 14, say) that says the market square can never be built over, and that the article can only be repealed by a referendum held in a year in which the square has already been built over. So the only route out of the lock runs through the door the lock exists to keep shut: to lift the rule you first have to break it. The speaker is a councillor who voted against the article twenty years ago, now sits in a majority that wants it gone, and has to speak at the annual public reading of the charter. Put at least one detail in the square that's different now than in the year of the vote — i want those twenty years visible in the poem.

Form: terza rima, about seven tercets plus the closing line. The form is the argument here, so dont treat it as decoration. The interlocking rhymes mean each tercet gets its rhyme handed to it by the one before, which is what i want the poem to be: a chain of selves, each one bound by the last. Close the chain properly — the last line has to pair off the one rhyme the poem leaves hanging, the middle rhyme of the final tercet. The point is that the speaker cannot end the poem without completing a pattern set by people who are dead and buried, so finishing it should feel like ratifying the article.

Whats load-bearing, for a room that knows this material cold (so dont explain anything in the poem):

- the self who signed is not obviously the wise one. You tie up your future hands because you dont trust your future judgement, which means you're trusting the judgement doing the tying — the same judgement you just said you cant trust. Let the speaker notice that and not get out of it.
- the later self's objection isnt inconvenience. Its being governed by someone who isnt them. Dont settle whether the later councillor is the same person as the one who voted: if they are, theres no reason the earlier vote outweighs the later one, and if they arent, a dead stranger is ruling a living one. The poem should hold both at once.
- the turn, around the fifth or sixth tercet: the speaker realizes Article 14 has no force whatsoever except the force the council lends it every year by standing up and reading it aloud. The dead hand is their own hand, still warm, and they could simply stop. The poem should show why they dont, without using the seminar words — no precommitment, no entrenchment, no countermajoritarian, no constitution inside the poem itself. Make the square and the reading do that work.

Dont end on a verdict for either side, and the last line still has to rhyme. Someone who knows the material should find it exact; someone who doesnt should find it a good poem.
