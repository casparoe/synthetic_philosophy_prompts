Hey, I'm a backend dev with no philosphy training, and I've been designing the consent/permissions model for a small platform we run, and I keep walking into the same wall. Some things feel like they should be up to the individual no matter what everyone else thinks (which photo of me is on my profile, whether I read some book), and other things feel like they should just go with what's better for everyone, and sometimes those two rules point at different answers and the whole thing goes in a circle. A friend told me this is basically Sen's liberal paradox and gave me the prude-and-lewd example, where each of them has an opinion about what the other one does, and said it's not just a bug in my schema. So now I want to understand the actual menu of fixes before I hardcode one.

What I want from you: a decision tree I can paste into a doc. It should start from "I accept both intuitions" (the individual's say-so over their own business, and going with unanimity/improvement) and end at a named position on what to do about the clash. Every branch node should be a commitment someone could accept or reject without already knowing which fix it leads to, e.g. whether the individual's say-so is a right that constrains the outcome or just a rule for how we arrive at it; whether unanimity is restricted to cases where nobody's preference is about somebody else's business; whether "meddlesome" preferences (I care what you do even though it doesn't touch me) get counted or filtered out; whether the pair of options each person is decisive over is fixed in advance or depends on what people happen to prefer; and whether you're allowed to change the framework itself (outcomes as the only thing that matters, preferences taken as given) instead of tweaking the two principles.

Leaves should be the actual positions, named where there's a name and "unnamed" where nobody really defends it, each with one line on the most awkward thing it has to swallow. Please include at least: the one that just drops the individual's special say-so when it clashes with unanimity; the one that drops or weakens unanimity; the one that narrows the individual's protected business to a private sphere; the one that says the individual's say-so isn't about outcomes at all but about what you're permitted to do (side constraints, doing vs. allowing); the one that only lets the individual be decisive when their preference isn't about someone else's business; and the one that says the real mistake is the informational basis, i.e. the framework should be rebuilt around something other than everyone's preference rankings, like what a person has reason to want or can actually choose.

If a combination of answers is empty, mark it and say whether that's because it's provably impossible or just because nobody wants it. And if two leaves come out the same in your tree, say so instead of pretending they're distinct.
