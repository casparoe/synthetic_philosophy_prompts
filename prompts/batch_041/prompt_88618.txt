I sit on (actually chair) the scientific advistory panel of a small foundation that makes long-horizon research grants -- 10 or 15 years, renewable, the kind of money you only get once. Our oldest grant is up for renewal at thursday's meeting and I have to circulate the memo.

The project is a model of a semi-arid river basin: soil loss, groundwater drawdown, population growth, and the punchline that per-capita food production there will fall below what the region can cover with imports, after which it's a permanent slide. It's been running 25 years. The slide was first dated to the mid-90s, then to the 2010s, and each time the PI has rescued it with a new finite resource: first the aquifer recharge rate hadn't started to bite yet, then grain imports were masking the shortfall and the mineral exports paying for them were going to run out, now it's a desalination plant that's over budget and won't close the gap. The outside reviewers call the model unfalsifiable -- every failed date gets absorbed by some new thing that runs out later. He says they're applying a naive Popperian test to a coupled nonlinear system, and that the right question isn't whether a date was hit but whether the mechanism is still the binding constraint. He also won't name any observation that would make him give up, which I admit bothers me. The panel is split: two members want to cut it and fund a crop-genetics group instead, one says 25 years of surviving attempted refutation is corroboration and not a scandal, and I keep thinking we'd be punishing him for being early, which this field has done before. The reviewers call it Malthusian and mean it as an insult; he takes it as a compliment.

Write me the memo. I need a bottom line -- renew, decline, or renew with conditions -- with a real number of years and conditions specific enough to act on, not monitor closely and revisit. In particular I want the panel to adopt some test we can actually apply: what we'd have to see, by when, to conclude the programme is dead rather than early, and what counts as a legitimate rescue versus a patch, in a way that doesn't just hand victory to the sceptics or give him an indefinite pass. And deal honestly with the strongest thing on the side you come out against, since I'll be defending this to the board and then to him.
