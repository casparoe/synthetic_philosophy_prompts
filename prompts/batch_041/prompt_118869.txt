I've somehow ended up running next month's meetup session — ninety minutes, whiteboard, fifteen people who read a lot of EA/rationalist blog stuff and have read exactly zero philosophy of war. Topic is the moral equality of combatants. Don't explain it to me, I roughly know what it says; I want the lesson plan.

So: what order do the pieces have to go in, and what actually depends on what? My guess is you can't even state the doctrine before people have (a) the ad bellum / in bello split and (b) the idea that the rules of fighting are meant to apply the same to both sides regardless of who started it — but I might be putting the cart before the horse, tell me if so and what the real first move is.

The one example that has to carry the whole thing: I want an unjust combatant who fights on knowing full well his side's cause is rotten — he's read the actual memo, he believes it — and who also isn't being coerced (volunteer army, nobody shooting deserters, he could just walk). Reason I'm insisting on that: with the ususal WWII-flavored case I'm fairly sure the room will quietly swap in 'well he couldn't have known' and the whole session collapses into the excuse story without anyone noticing they've changed the subject. So — can that case even be constructed, or does the excuse do all the work and my example is impossible? If it's impossible, say so loudly, because it changes what I should teach. And tell me what the example has to have and has to lack to do the job.

Second thing, and I want you to tell me if it's a trap: I want to try walking in through a licensing-convention picture, like the rules of a duel, or two boxers who both agreed to the match. On that picture the permission to kill each other comes from both sides accepting the same rulebook, not from either cause being just, and the symmetry is just what a rulebook looks like when two parties buy in. Two worries: it smuggles in a consent/contract story that actual soldiers don't satisfy, and it seems to license any mutually agreed fight, which is nuts. Load-bearing or drop it? Related version I may have made up on a walk: the symmetry is doing coordination work rather than moral work — both sides prefer a shared rulebook to anything-goes, and symmetry is what makes it self-enforcing — in which case the "moral" equality is a moralized convention. If that's a real position and not just me, work it into the plan.

Misconceptions, in the order they'll actually show up and where in the sequence to head each one off. I'm betting on: (1) moral equality = both sides equally in the right; (2) so combatants are just following orders and aren't responsible for anything; (3) it's a legal doctrine, so it's about tribunals and the Geneva rules; (4) you can only be killed if you deserve it, i.e. liability requires guilt. What's the big one I'm missing?

Then: one question to put to the room at the end that would show whether they got the structure of the debate rather than memorized a side. And what I should explicitly refuse to cover at this level and announce that I'm refusing — I'm thinking the occupation/aftermath material and the exceptional-circumstances material, but you tell me.

No reading list, they won't do it.

Last thing: keep the confidence honest. Seperate what's settled among people who actually work on this from what's a live fight, and don't hand me one side as the consensus. In particular, if the revisionist line (McMahan and co.) has basically won, don't tell me it's settled; and if it hasn't, don't tell me it's fringe.
