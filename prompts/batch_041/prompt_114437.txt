You are an expert philosopher of science whose research concerns the metaphysics of laws of nature, and you are also unusually good at writing comedy that philosophers of science actually find funny. Your task is to write a single joke: a short comic dialogue of roughly 200 to 350 words about the disagreement between Humeans and anti-Humeans regarding laws of nature. Think carefully before you begin. Start with a brief brainstorm of three or four candidate comic premises, note in one sentence what technical point each would turn on, and then write the version you judge best.

The joke must be load-bearing. The humor should come from the actual content of the two positions, so that someone who knows the debate laughs at the substance rather than at the mere fact that philosophers are odd. Someone who has never encountered the debate should still be able to follow the exchange and come away knowing what is at issue. Do not merely have the characters announce their positions, and do not write a generic joke about academics or about science.

Here is the technical material to work with. On the Humean side, the world is fundamentally a mosaic of local matters of particular fact; laws are not additional ingredients in that mosaic but rather whichever regularities appear in the best systematization of the whole mosaic, where the best system is the true deductive system that does best on a tradeoff among simplicity, strength, and balance. On the anti-Humean side, laws genuinely govern: a law is not a summary of what happens but what makes things happen, whether because laws are relations of necessitation holding between universals or because properties carry their own dispositional essences.

At least one of the following difficulties must do real work in your joke: (a) the Humean's identification problem, namely that once laws are just the regularities in the best system, it becomes hard to say what distinguishes a law from a mere coincidence except by appeal to the system as a whole; (b) the Humean's inference problem, namely that a summary of what has happened seems poorly placed to explain what must happen or to support counterfactuals; (c) the anti-Humean's corresponding difficulty, namely what exactly picks out which universals are related by necessitation, and what the necessitation adds beyond regularity plus a modal fact; (d) uninstantiated laws, which seem easy for the anti-Humean and awkward for the Humean; (e) conservation laws and symmetry principles, which look like constraints on what may happen rather than summaries of what does happen; (f) the regress that threatens any bureau of certification, since if laws are what get certified, something must certify the certification.

To keep the joke from being a rerun of familiar material, set it inside an invented institution rather than in a seminar room: for example, a licensing bureau that certifies regularities as laws, an insurance office that must underwrite the continuation of a regularity, or an archive that decides which regularities are worth preserving. At least one character should be a Humean and at least one an anti-Humean, and their disagreement must be faithful to their positions, with no strawman on either side. Use fictional characters only; do not attribute views to any named philosopher, and do not quote anyone.

Write in American English and in a dry rather than zany register. End with a punchline that lands for a reader who has been following the technical stakes. After the joke, add two or three sentences naming the technical point the punchline turns on and saying what, if anything, each character's position gets right.
