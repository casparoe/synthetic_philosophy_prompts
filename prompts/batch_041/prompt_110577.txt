I received a psychiatric diagnosis years ago that has since been revised twice, and each revision changed not only what the label covers but how I stand toward it; friends with other labels tell similar stories. I have been reading my way through the long argument over whether categories like these are real disorders, socially constructed labels, or something in between, and it seems to me the two sides have been circling each other for decades without either conceding a point. Those on the construction side note that the boundaries have shifted with culture and that the label itself changes how people behave and what they report; those on the realist side point to heredity, response to medication, and the plain fact that something is wrong. Neither can refute the other, and I want to understand why rather than pick a winner.

What kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it a verbal quarrel about what "real" or "disorder" is supposed to mean, a genuine dispute about the facts, or two camps answering different questions under one heading -- one asking whether a category carves nature at its joints, the other asking whether applying it helps or harms the people it is applied to? I have a hypothesis I would like you to test rather than assume: that looping effects, whereby the classified respond to being classified and thereby alter the very thing being classified, keep the target moving, so that even a correct answer at one moment becomes wrong later, leaving both sides partly right about different moments and neither able to settle it. If that diagnosis is right, tell me what would actually count as progress; if it is wrong, tell me what the real structure of the impasse is. Give me reasons, not a survey, and be concrete about which cases show the two sides genuinely disagreeing rather than merely using different words.
