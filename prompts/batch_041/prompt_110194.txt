My friend Nadia is a doctor-turned-philosopher and I've been collecting her verdicts on hard choices for years, mostly to needle her. What she's said so far: (1) she insists "better than" is transitive — if A beats B and B beats C then A beats C, and she says anyone who denies it is just confused about what "better" means; (2) she thinks a single extra hour of dull toothache always makes a life worse than the same life without it, no matter how much ache the life already contains — she won't hear about "at some point it stops mattering"; (3) a second of agony is worse than a full day of toothache; (4) a second of agony is not worse than eight years of toothache — she says comparing them is like asking whether Tuesday is taller than the color blue; (5) she says two things can both be better than a third without either being better than the other, and that's a real fact about them, not ignorance on our part; (6) she says if you have to choose between a career and a sick parent and both options are genuinely supported by reasons, you can pick one and not be irrational, and you don't have to pretend afterwards that you found the winner.

The new case is 1 second of agony vs. 26 hours of toothache. Predict her verdict, tell me which rule she's actually running — plain transitivity plus her step-principle, a threshold past which toothache stops counting, a "vague zone" where the comparison has no answer, parity, or straight-up giving up transitivity for cross-magnitude comparisons — and give me the case I should put to her next to find out, because right now I can't tell those apart and I don't think she can either.
