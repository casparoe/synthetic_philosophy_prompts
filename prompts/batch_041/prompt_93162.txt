Here's the question I set:

"The Muddy Children. Six children sit in a circle. Each can see the foreheads of the other five but not their own, each can tell who among the others is muddy, and all of this is common knowledge among them. In fact four of them are muddy. The father walks in and announces publicly — everyone hears him, and everyone knows everyone heard him — that at least one of them is muddy. He then asks, over and over, in rounds: 'If you know that you are muddy, step forward now.' No one steps forward in round 1; he asks again, and so on.

(a) In which round do the muddy children step forward, and why? Set out the base case and the inductive step explicitly.

(b) Suppose that instead of announcing it publicly, the father had gone to each child privately and whispered 'at least one of you is muddy', so that each child hears it but has no idea whether the other five heard it. Would the children still work out their own status? Explain.

(c) Every child can already see every other child's forehead. So what exactly does the father's public announcement add? Answer in terms of common knowledge, and say what work common knowledge of the children's rationality does in your answer to (a)."

And here's what the student handed in:

"(a) Round 4. Base case: suppose exactly one child were muddy. That child sees five clean foreheads and, before the father speaks, cannot tell whether any child is muddy; once the father says there is at least one, the child realises it must be himself, and steps forward in round 1. Inductive step: assume that with m muddy children, the muddy ones step forward in round m. Now suppose there are m+1. Each muddy child sees m muddy children and reasons: if I were clean, there would be exactly m muddy children, and by the inductive hypothesis they would have stepped forward in round m. Since no one stepped forward in round m, I cannot be clean, so I am muddy — and so is everyone else who sees m muddy foreheads. Hence the muddy children step forward in round m+1. With four muddy children, that is round 4.

(b) No. Each child would know there is at least one muddy child, but would not know whether the others know it, or know that they know, and so on. The reasoning in (a) only works because each child can predict how the others will behave, and that requires them to be reasoning from the same shared information. With a private whisper that shared information is missing, so the induction never gets going and no one ever steps forward.

(c) The announcement gives each child a piece of information they did not have before — up until the father spoke, none of them could be sure there was even one muddy child among them. But the real content of the announcement is that it is public: because everyone hears it, each child knows the others heard it, knows that they know, and so on. So the announcement does not merely supply a fact; it makes that fact common knowledge. The children also need it to be common knowledge that everyone is rational and will step forward as soon as they can, otherwise no child could read anything into the silence of the earlier rounds. In the end this is really an instance of Aumann's agreement theorem: with a shared prior and a public announcement, the children cannot agree to disagree about their own status, so they all converge."
