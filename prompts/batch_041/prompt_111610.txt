Trying to figure out if there's a principle behind my gut on this, because I keep contradicting myself. My gut: giving a chunk of my income to a deworming charity is fine, even though what that does to the world over the next few centuries is completely unknowable and could in principle be bad. Voting is fine, even though my vote changes nothing foreseeable. Having a kid is fine, even though whoever they turn into and whatever their descendants do is a total blank. Taking the higher-paying job and donating the difference is fine, even though that money might end up funding something awful.

But a friend of mine works on a small, boring-seeming improvement to AI systems, and my gut says thats not fine, even though the only foreseeable effect is useful knowledge. Same when I imagine joining a company whose product is clearly good now but sits somewhere on a path that could go very bad. And I'd say it's not okay to take a one-in-a-billion chance of ending the world for a big foreseeable gain, but I'll happily take an analogous infinitesimal risk when the payoff is charity.

A friend put it as: with the charity I don't know the magnitude but I'm happy about the sign, with the AI thing I don't even know the sign. That feels like it might be the real line, but I can't tell if it's a principle or just a story I tell to keep my verdicts. So: is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about the one I keep flip-flopping on -- quitting my job to work full-time on making some technology cheaper and more widely available, where I'm confident it helps people right now and have no idea what it does to the world in two hundred years? Is it closer to the charity case or the AI case, and whats the feature that decides it?
