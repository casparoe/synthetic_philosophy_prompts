I moved in with my partner a few months ago and shes vegan, which has been great for my cooking but bad for my peace of mind, because now I keep trying to figure out what I actually think about eating animals instead of just doing what I grew up doing. I'm not after a reading list or citations or anything like that—I just want a principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that can make sense of my gut reactions and tell me where they break down. I've also been reading a bit about wild animal suffering, which has made the whole thing feel less like a simple matter of vegetarian versus omnivore and more like a question about how much suffering I'm willing to have happen on my behalf, including out in fields and forests.

Here are the verdicts I keep landing on. Factory-farmed chicken: not fine. Factory-farmed beef: also not fine, but part of me thinks beef is worse because cows are smarter and the land use is absurd, while another part thinks chicken is worse because one cow feeds so many more meals than one chicken, so per plate you're responsible for way more deaths. I can't hold both and it's driving me nuts. Oysters: fine, no brain. Wild-caught salmon: not fine, though I can't tell if that's because of bycatch, because the fish had a life, or because I'm just squeamish about killing something that looks back at me. Backyard eggs from a hen I've actually met: fine. Roadkill deer: fine. Lab-grown meat: fine. Crickets: fine, but shrimp feel off-limits, and I can't explain the difference except that shrimp seem more like animals to me. Plants: fine, except I read somewhere that harvesting wheat and soy kills alot of field mice and insects, and I don't know what to do with that. And then there's the one I keep going back and forth on: a cultured chicken breast grown from cells taken from a live chicken that's kept in a small cage as a donor its whole life but never killed. Is that fine, or is it worse than just eating a regular chicken?

I have a feeling the answer should be something like a suffering-per-calorie ledger, but that makes the wheat-mice thing loom large and also makes me think chicken is worse than beef, which I don't like. Can you tell me what principle, or small set of principles, actually captures most of these verdicts, what it says about the cell-donor chicken and the wheat-mice case, and which of my verdicts it forces me to give up? I'm fine with a principle that's complicated if it saves the cases, but I want to know the price.
