I'm a retired biologist — 35 years on parasitoid wasps, mostly just counting things in the field — and I've spent the last few years reading alot of philosophy, which my wife says is a dangerous hobby for a man who already thinks he knows how animals work. Anyway I've come around to a view and I want you to try to knock it down.

The old story, which you probably know: the digger wasp drags a paralyzed cricket to the mouth of her burrow, goes inside to check it's empty, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's in there, she'll come out, fetch it, drag it back, and go in to check again — and you can do it forty times and she never just drags the damn thing in. Hofstadter (and Dennett after him, I think) called this sphexishness. The usual moral is: look how the wasp is a wind-up toy, and aren't we gloriously different.

My view is that we're not different in kind. What we call flexibility is rigidity with more nesting. A creature is "free" exactly to the extent it can interrupt its own loop and re-enter at a higher level, and that capacity to interrupt is itself just another loop that fires under its own fixed conditions. There's no extra ingredient — no ghost, no contra-causal will — and "free will" just names the felt inside of a loop deep enough that the animal can represent the loop to itself. I'd put humans and wasps on one continuum and say the difference is depth, not kind.

So: what would count against this? I don't want the usual free-will talking points, I want the things that should actually move someone who holds it. Which observations, experiments, intuitions, or results would be decisive, and which would only be awkward? What would I myself have to concede counts against me, as opposed to what critics lob from the outside?

A few things I'd specifically like you to chew on, because they're where I feel the pressure:

- I've read that the endless-loop story may be overstated — that after enough repetitions the wasp does start to adapt, and that re-checking a disturbed nest might be perfectly sensible behavior for her rather than proof of mindlessness. Does that undercut my picture or does it actually help it? Be honest about which.
- The obvious cheap escape for me is to say "any flexibility you find is just a deeper loop, and the loop that lets it break the loop is definately fixed too." That can absorb literally any result. Tell me whether that makes the view unfalsifiable, and if so whether that's fatal for a view like this or just the normal price of a naturalist account.
- Does my view predict anything a rival (say, one that puts a real qualitative break between "mere mechanism" and minded agency) doesn't predict? If it predicts nothing, say so.
- What would a case look like where the loop-depth picture makes a wrong prediction rather than just an unsatisfying one? If you can construct such a case, that's what would actually move me.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and finish with a straight answer to whether I've built something well-insulated and whether that's a problem.
