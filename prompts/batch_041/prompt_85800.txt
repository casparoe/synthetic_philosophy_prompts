I'm a science journalist — I write about medicine and ideas for a monthly — and I'm the lay member on my regional health authority's ethics advisory panel. Thursday we vote on a petiton from a patient group asking us to add a condition to the chronic-disease funding stream, and I have to circulate the written opinion. The condition isn't fake: the people who have it have a hormone sitting below a cutoff, and the cutoff was set by taking the bottom 2.5% of an otherwise healthy population — so it's a statistical line, not a lesion, and the mechanism is only sort of understood. Most people below the cutoff never feel anything, a minority get hit hard, and there's no way to tell them apart up front. The one trial we have shows the drug helps the worst-off subgroup and does nothing for everyone else. The patients themselves are split — the group's own board is split — some want the disease label because it unlocks treatment and research funding, others say the label is stigmatizing and will follow them into insurance and job applications. Here's my problem: our charter says we fund only a demonstrable abnormality of biological function and not a mere difference from the population average, but it also says we fund conditions that cause significant impairment of well-being. In this case those two clauses point opposite ways and the petitioners are basically asking us to pick one. Could you draft the short opinion I'll read out — a concrete recommendation with a timeframe, reasons the petitioners could actually push back on, and an honest pass at the strongest argument for whichever side I end up voting against?
