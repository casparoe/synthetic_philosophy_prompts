I am putting together a handout for a graduate reading group on the norm of assertion, and I want it to be diagnostic rather than a survey. Please build a decision tree that starts from the bare commitment that assertions can be assessed as proper or improper quite apart from whether they are true or sincere, and that ends at named positions about what the governing norm is. Build the tree out of the distinctions first and locate the positions in it afterwards, rather than arranging leaves and looking for splits.

Every branching node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing the labels. Please use at least these distinctions, which are the ones my group keeps collapsing into each other: whether the norm licenses the act in advance or only makes it criticisable and retractable afterwards; whether it attaches to the speaker's state, to the act's relation to the world, or to the speaker's entitlement; whether it governs assertion as such or only assertion in its testimonial use; whether it is constitutive of the act or merely regulative; and whether its standard is fixed or shifts with context. I also want one branch that separates positions agreeing about what the norm is but disagreeing about whose norm it is -- the speaker's, the hearer's, or the conversation's -- since I suspect several apparent disagreements actually live there.

Each leaf should carry the position's standard name if it has one, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note which combinations the tree leaves unoccupied and say briefly whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any positions the tree cannot separate. Aim for eight to twelve leaves.
