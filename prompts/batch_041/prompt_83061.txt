I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of two decades, and the one topic I have never managed to get straight is expected-value fanaticism. I would like you to help me set it up properly, because I suspect I have been assembling the pieces in the wrong order. I am not after a reading list — I have read plenty of the posts — I am after the argument.

Here is my picture. Fanaticism is a pathology of expected-value maximization that shows up only when the payoffs in play are unbounded or infinite. You get it from Pascal's wager, from infinite-utility cases in population ethics, and from the thought experiment in which a stranger offers an astronomically large reward for a trivial cost. In each case a vanishingly small probability of something infinitely or unboundedly good swamps every finite consideration, and expected value stops discriminating between options. My own values are finite. I assign infinite value to nothing, and the best and worst outcomes I can conceive of are both bounded. So I have concluded that the problem does not apply to me: I can maximize expected value with an ordinary bounded utility function and be entirely safe from the mugger.

Two things unsettle that conclusion. First, I built a case with no infinities anywhere and it still came out fanatical — a certain modest benefit against a one-in-a-trillion chance of a large but perfectly finite improvement in the world, with the payoff set high enough that the tiny chance wins. Second, I tried to patch it with a probability floor: treat any probability below some threshold as zero, and maximize expected value on the remainder. That felt like a fudge, and I cannot tell what it costs me. Someone also once told me that the fanaticism result does not depend on expected value at all, but follows from a few innocuous-looking principles about how probabilities and payoffs trade off against one another. I did not understand him, and I suspect he was smuggling something in.

So: is fanaticism simply a non-issue for an agent whose values are finite and bounded? If it is not, is a probability floor the right repair, or is there a cleaner one? And is my reluctance to hand over my wallet a bias I ought to train out of myself, in the way the blogs tell me to train out scope insensitivity, or is it a substantive position I am entitled to hold?

"What the fanaticism arguments actually rest on", about 400 words, identifying the premises that do the work and which of them are independent of any assumption about infinity or unboundedness.
