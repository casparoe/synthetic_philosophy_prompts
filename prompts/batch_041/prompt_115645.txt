Before I settle the hole argument, I want the specification rather than a verdict. Take the task to be this: give an account of what spacetime is, in a theory such as general relativity, that is good enough to decide whether the hole argument succeeds — whether two models that agree on everything outside a region, and on that region's past, yet differ inside it, represent two genuinely different possible worlds. Write the desiderata any such account must meet: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the results it must be consistent with, each with the consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a matter of taste.

Three considerations I want you to treat as candidates, and to accept or reject explicitly, saying for each whether it is a genuine requirement or a commitment of one camp:

1. Determinism. If the world's history before the hole is fully fixed, what lies inside the hole must be fixed as well; an account on which the pre-hole facts leave the hole's contents open makes the future depend on something no fact about the world records.

2. Formulation-independence. Presentations of general relativity disagree about which quantity is fundamental — the metric, a connection, a tetrad, holonomies — and the argument is almost always run with the metric as the basic variable. If an account's verdict flips when the same theory is rewritten with a different quantity taken as primitive, it is answering a question about notation rather than about the world. The verdict must therefore be invariant across empirically equivalent formulations that take different variables as basic. Say what it would take to satisfy this, and whether the argument can even be stated in a formulation that does not treat the metric as fundamental.

3. No undetectable difference. A difference that no measurement at any time could register is not a difference in what the world is like.

Then: say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the commitments of a particular camp rather than data; identify any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied, and say precisely which members of it do the excluding — I suspect the trouble lies with determinism and undetectability once spacetime points are treated as individuals, but tell me if it lies elsewhere; and name the single requirement that eliminates the most candidates. End with a brief scorecard of the main positions against your list — that spacetime points are individuals over and above the fields, that only relational or structural facts are real, and that differences of this kind are mere gauge and the argument dissolves. No citations or reading list; the criteria and the tensions among them are what I want.
