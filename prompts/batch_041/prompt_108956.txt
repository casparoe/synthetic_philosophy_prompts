Alright, this is a petty one but it's been buggin me for weeks and I genuinely couldn't referee it. Two of my mates, Ruth and Callum, got into it in the pub about our friend Jonny, who's been putting off a big work deadline. And when I say putting off, he's had it two months and done basically nothing. Every night's the same: he says he knows he should start, has a couple of beers, watches telly, goes to bed promising himself tomorrow. Ruth says it's weakness of will, textbook, he knows perfectly well what he ought to be doing and does the opposite, that's the whole thing. Callum says there's no such thing and it went from there. Here's roughly how it went:

1. Ruth: it's just akrasia. He judges that working tonight is better than not working, and he doesn't work. That's the definition, case closed.
2. Callum: but that's supposed to be impossible. Nobody deliberately chooses what they take to be the worse option. When nine o'clock comes he simply wants the beers more than he wants to work, and the 'I ought to work' is just a sentence he's learned to say. If he really ranked the work higher he'd be doing it.
3. Ruth: except he feels genuinely awful about it, and the next morning he'd give anything to have started the night before. He doesn't merely prefer differently — he wishes he preferred differently. That second layer is the whole point; it shows there's a real conflict and not just one preference beating another.
4. Callum: that's just his preferences changing with time. In the morning the deadline is close and looming so work looks worth more; at night it's ages away so it looks worth less. Nothing's being judged and then betrayed — his own ranking flips depending on how near the reward is. Jonny says as much himself: at 9pm he honestly doesn't care about the deadline, by 9am he honestly does. Two different people almost.
5. Ruth: but that's just sticking a label on the pattern, not explaining it. And it doesn't fit anyway. He hasn't got some stable preference for the work that gets outvoted at night — he thinks the work matters, full stop, even at 9pm. He isn't sitting there thinking the telly's the more valuable use of his evening. He's just not doing what he thinks is best, which is the thing Callum says can't happen.
6. Callum: then maybe he doesn't really think the work matters, he thinks he ought to think it matters. People lug round values they've inherited and never actually endorsed. And if you insist he does endorse it, then the evening Jonny reaching for the beer isn't acting against his own judgment — the judgment belongs to morning Jonny. There's no single self in there to be weak.
7. Ruth: if there's no single self then there's nothing for the word weakness to even attach to, which is mad, because everyone can see exactly what's going on with him. And notice he's tried to fix it. He put one of those website blockers on his laptop, and he got me to hold fifty quid of his that he'd forfeit if he skipped a night. Worked for about a week and then he found his way round it. If it were just his preferences flipping on a timer, the flip would be predictable and he could plan round it — like he did — and then the plan would work, wouldn't it? The whole point of tying yourself to the mast is that the future you is going to want to untie you.

Neither of them would budge and I didn't have a clue who to back. And if they're both missing something, say what, and finish by telling me who's more right overall.
