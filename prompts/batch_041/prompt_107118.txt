I've been talked into a view I'm not sure I trust. The Meno problem — why is knowledge better than true belief? — I now think has a basically social answer: knowledge beats true belief not because the individual belief is shinier, but because it's the thing you can responsibly hand off to someone else. It's what lets a bunch of people split up the work of finding things out and lean on each other's results, and take away the other people and the advantage mostly evaporates. What would count against this? I want the real defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward — cases where knowledge obviously beats true belief for a lone knower whos got nobody to tell, or where a plain true belief is just as transmissible, or where the story quietly assumes the very thing it's supposed to explain. And be honest about whether it's been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's actually a problem for a view like this.
