I've been chewing on something since me and my mate tried to organise a surprise for his brother. He texts me "you in?", I text back "in", then I sit there wondering if he's seen my "in", so I text "did you get that?", he texts "yeah got it", and now I'm wondering whether he knows I saw his "yeah got it", and whether he knows I know he saw my "in". Its maddening. I looked it up and I think the game theory people have a name for it, common knowledge -- its not just that we both know a thing, its that we both know the other one knows, and that they know we know, all the way down. And my hunch, which I can't prove, is that no amount of texting ever actually gets you there, because every extra message just makes a new thing that might not have arrived. Like you can never finish the ladder, you just add a rung.

I want a short story about that. Two people, honest as anything, no lying, no double dealing, no mistrust -- the channel is just leaky. They have to do something together at a set time and place, something that matters, where if only one of them turns up it goes badly for both. The only way they can talk is through something that sometimes eats messages: a phone with one bar, a courier who gets turned back at the gate, a radio that only one of them can transmit on, whatever you fancy.

What I want out of it is the ladder. Not explained to me, felt. Show me what each of them knows at each step, and how it keeps not being enough, so that at some point one of them has to move on less than certainty -- and I want them to land in different places, one goes, one doesn't, or one goes early and one goes late. Show me the moment each of them decides and exactly what was in their head right then, not in philosophy words, in their own words.

Then one bit I really want: a moment where they're in the same place and something is said out loud, in front of both of them, so each of them hears it and hears the other hearing it. I've got a feeling that does something all the messages can't, and I'd rather see it happen in the story than have anyone tell me about it.

Please don't have a character explain the idea, and don't let a narrator step in and tidy it up at the end. Around 1500 words. Make it a thing that happens, not a lesson.
