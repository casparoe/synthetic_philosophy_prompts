I am a student in a course on probability and evidence, and I am trying to write a position paper that states my own view of how probabilities should guide belief and action. Before I commit to it, I want an audit of whether the commitments hang together. Here is what I believe. (1) There is a fact of the matter about the probability of a singular event, and it is fixed by the most specific reference class for which we have reliable frequency data. (2) That probability is an objective chance, not merely a credence; the Principal Principle is correct, so a rational agent should align their credence with known chances. (3) When two reference classes of comparable specificity give different frequencies, the correct one is the class whose defining features are causally relevant to the outcome; statistical correlation without causal relevance is not enough. (4) In legal factfinding, bare statistical evidence should not suffice for conviction or liability, even when the frequency is high and the reference class is well defined. (5) In clinical and actuarial decision-making, base rates should be used, and it is a mark of irrationality to ignore them in favor of unstructured individual judgment. (6) When no frequency data are available, the principle of indifference is a legitimate fallback for assigning probabilities over symmetric possibilities. (7) The same basic epistemic standards govern legal, clinical, and everyday reasoning; differences between these domains are differences in the costs of error, not in what counts as evidence. To make the contrast concrete: I think a clinician who ignores a well-established base rate for a condition is making an epistemic mistake, but I also think a prosecutor who offers only a well-established base rate for a criminal defendant has not met the standard of proof, even if the base rate is higher than the clinician's.

I suspect at least some of these pull against each other, but I do not know which. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each genuine tension is. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me. I would also like to know which single commitment is doing the most damage to the rest, and whether there is a way to keep the package while making only a small adjustment. Please be specific about the reference-class problem, Simpson-style reversals, and the distinction between probability as a guide to belief and probability as a guide to legal proof.
