Although my career was in the natural sciences — I was an experimental geologist for some forty years — I have spent the last several years of retirement reading moral and political philosophy, and I have reached the point where I would rather have a philosopher's judgment on a family matter than my own.

My brother and I, some thirty years ago, each inherited an equal and modest sum. I invested mine cautiously and it grew; he put his into a speculative venture that failed, and he has been in straitened circumstances ever since. He now argues that because we began on an equal footing, and because his gamble was undertaken, as he puts it, for both of us, I owe him support in his old age. My instinct has been that he chose his risk and must bear it — whereas our late sister, who was ruined by an illness that no one could have foreseen and no one chose, has a claim on me that I feel without any hesitation at all. So I treat two people who are equally badly off in very different ways, and I have begun to suspect that the distinction I am drawing is either incoherent or, worse, a rationalization of my own good fortune.

Two complications make me distrust my instinct. The first is that I am not sure my caution was ever really my doing. I have been timid about money since childhood, and I did not choose to be timid, any more than my brother chose to be bold. The second is that his failed venture was, in a roundabout way, the cause of my success: it was through the people he introduced me to during those lean years that I stumbled into the work that eventually made me comfortable. His loss and my gain are knotted together, and I find I cannot cleanly separate what he is owed from what I merely happen to have.

I would like you to tell me, as plainly as you can, whether the line I draw between my brother and my sister can be given a principled defense or whether it should be abandoned — and, having settled that, what I ought actually to do. I am not asking for a survey of the literature; I want the argument itself, and a conclusion you are willing to stand behind.
