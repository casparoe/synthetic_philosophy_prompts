I keep running into the same story about a digger wasp, and I have come to suspect that the way it gets used in arguments is doing more work than the story can bear. The version I know runs like this: the wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, leaves it on the threshold, goes inside to check that all is well, and then comes back out to pull the cricket in. If, while she is inside, you move the cricket a few inches away, she does not simply fetch it and carry on; she drags it back to the threshold, drops it, and repeats the inspection. Move it again and she repeats the whole sequence, apparently without end, as though the inspection must be the last thing that happens before the final act and any interruption resets her.

Two people I follow use this story, and they use it to opposite ends. The first treats the wasp as a mirror: this, he says, is what all our deliberation looks like from the outside — a fixed routine that runs to completion, blind to the fact that the situation has changed — so our sense that we are choosing is only our inability to see our own script, and free will is an illusion. The second treats the wasp as a contrast case: the whole point, she says, is that the wasp cannot notice that she is looping, whereas we can notice it, describe it, and step out of it; a creature that can be wasp-like about its own wasp-likeness is not wasp-like in the way that matters, so the story marks the gap between a mechanism and a free agent rather than closing it.

I would like a careful audit of this analogy as it is actually being used, in both directions. Concretely:

1. Give me the correspondence table. When the source is the wasp and the target is a person deliberating, or a machine following a policy, what plays the role of the burrow, the cricket, the threshold, the inspection, the observer who moves the cricket, and the loop itself? For each correspondence, say whether it is tight, loose, or merely suggestive.

2. Tell me where the mapping breaks. I am especially interested in the features of the wasp case that get quietly imported into the human case: that the routine is single and domain-specific; that it is fixed by inheritance rather than learned and revisable; that the wasp carries no representation of the goal it is serving; that the routine is triggered by a simple stimulus; and that the wasp cannot represent the loop as a loop. Which of these are load-bearing for the debunking conclusion, and which are decoration?

3. Address the move I find most slippery. To say that we can step out of our routines only relocates the question, because something must decide when to step out; and if that something is itself a routine — a rule for detecting that the current rule has been defeated — then the regress of routines has not been escaped, only pushed up a level. If the regress terminates, what does it terminate in, and why is that terminal thing not just one more mechanism? If it does not terminate, does any finite creature escape the wasp's predicament at all? Lay out both horns, say which you think is worse, and tell me whether the wasp story gives us any way at all to distinguish a routine that includes a loop-breaker from genuine flexibility.

4. Consider whether the question of whether a system is running a script is even well formed. Any finite pattern of behavior can be described as following some rule, so rigidity may be relative to the description we choose rather than a property of the creature. If that is right, say what a non-arbitrary test of wasp-likeness would look like: what would count as passing it, what as failing it, and whether the wasp would actually fail it. I gather the anecdote itself may be shakier than it is usually presented, which is part of why I want the test stated independently of the story.

5. Finally, say whether either conclusion survives. Does the debunking use of the analogy survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and does the contrast-case use survive once the regress in (3) is on the table? If neither survives, tell me what a better analogy for the same job would be — one that captures the real distinction, whatever it turns out to be, without borrowing the wasp's overreach.
