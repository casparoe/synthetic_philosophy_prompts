Two philosophers have been arguing past each other for years about commemoration. One holds that a monument is a standing act of the state: a city that leaves an unjust figure on its plinth is honoring him afresh each year. The other holds that the past should remain physically present and contestable in shared space, and that removal cedes the past to whoever writes the textbooks. Draft the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, one that covers statues and street names alike. I want the claims they agree on, including about what a public monument or eponym asserts, whether upkeep renews an endorsement, and whether the charge of erasing history names a real harm; precise formulations of their remaining disagreements, each worded so its own side would accept it; the cruxes; and, for each crux, a case, argument, or empirical study both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Both sides accepted two conditions before they started: the statement must take a stand on whether a monument's meaning is fixed at erection or renewed by each act of upkeep, and it must say whether a rotating citizen panel empowered to rewrite a statue's plaque every few years counts as recontextualization or as a different monument. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Take a definite position: commit to one set of formulations rather than surveying alternatives, say which residual disagreements you judge to be merely verbal, and where you think one side's case is stronger, say so and give your reasons.
