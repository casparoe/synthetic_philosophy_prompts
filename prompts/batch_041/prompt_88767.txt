I'm in a long-running argument with a friend about historical materialism and I want you to write the essay that settles it. Not a survey — I know the survey, and neither of us needs to be told the crude version is dead. The fight is about what's left after the crude version is dead, and I can't tell whether what's left is a real theory or a way of never being wrong.

Here's the retreat we've both made. The base doesn't *produce* ideas — nobody thinks a person's thoughts are manufactured by their material position, or that two merchants three centuries apart must think the same thoughts. So we've downgraded the claim: the base doesn't generate ideas, it *selects* them. The analogy I keep reaching for is natural selection, which explains why the moths got dark without explaining where the dark mutation came from. On this reading the base is a filter. It decides which of the many ideas already floating around get institutionalized — taught in schools, funded by patrons, written into law, remembered at all — and which die in a pamphlet nobody reads. Filters dont have to be authors, so the theory gets to keep its explanatory ambitions while dropping the implausible causal story.

My friend thinks that's a machine for never being wrong. Whatever survives survived because the base selected it, whatever dies died because the base didn't, and the evidence always gets collected after the fact. He has a second objection I can't answer: a filter has to filter on *something*, and he says you can't say what the base selects for without smuggling in a purpose, which is the exact thing the whole apparatus was built to avoid. My reply is that "selects for whatever keeps the existing relations of production stable" isn't a purpose, it's a feedback loop — but then I have to say who or what runs the loop, and I get vague.

So write me the essay. Does a selectionist version of the base/superstructure thesis actually explain anything, and if so, exactly what does it explain and what does it merely redescribe? Is it a version of historical materialism or a quiet abandonment of it with the vocabulary kept for sentimental reasons? Reach a verdict and defend it — if the honest verdict is that the weak version survives only by collapsing into the truism that institutions have material preconditions, say that, I'd rather have it than a rescue operation. Build the case on one or two episodes you work through in detail rather than on a list of names, and be specific about what the base is supposedly filtering for and what does the retaining.
