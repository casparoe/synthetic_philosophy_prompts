I am convening an adversarial collaboration between two positions on Kant's treatment of lying to the murderer at the door, and I need the statement both parties could sign. The first position is the rigorist: the duty of truthfulness admits of no exception, and Kant was right that one must not lie even to the murderer. The second is the internal critic: Kant's own universalizability procedure and his division of duties permit the lie in that case, and the exceptionless conclusion rests on a step he should not have taken.

Draft it as follows. First, a numbered list of claims both accept — about the case itself, about what the duty of truthfulness is a duty of and to whom it is owed, and about what the universalizability procedure does and does not settle here. Second, a table of the surviving disagreements, each row phrased so that its own side would accept the wording of its own view. Third, the cruxes: I suspect one is whether the murderer's question is the sort of declaration that places the answerer under an obligation to him, and another is whether the operative duty is owed to others or is a duty to oneself of consistency in avowal. For each crux, supply the argument or variant case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use at least two variants as crux-bearing tests — the intended victim has in fact already left the house, and the murderer asks for the answer in writing rather than spoken — and state what each side must say about them to remain consistent. Finally, if any part of the dispute is merely verbal — for instance, over what the unconditionality of the duty amounts to, exceptionlessness in application or merely the fact that its force is not derived from consequences — identify that part and explain why.

The statement should be self-contained: argue from the two positions, not from who has held them.
