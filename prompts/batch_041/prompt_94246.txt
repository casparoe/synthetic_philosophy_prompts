I've been chewing on this on my own for months and I've got nobody to check it against, so here goes. It's about what it means for two or more people to do something together, and whether the way I think about it actually holds together.

Here's roughly where I've landed. One: when people do something together — my sister and me carrying a sofa up the stairs, a rowing crew, whatever — I think the thing they're doing genuinely belongs to the two of them (or the eight of them). It isn't just that I've got my plan and she's got her plan and the two plans happen to line up. There's a plan that's ours, and it isn't owned by either of us alone. Two: having said that, I'm dead sure there's no such thing as a group mind. Nothing thinks or intends except individual people with individual brains. There's no ghostly extra thing hovering over the crew. Three: I think you can be doing something together without ever agreeing to it, or discussing it, or even noticing. Two strangers walking down a corridor who both adjust to each other and end up going out the same door — as far as I can tell they're doing that together, and neither has said a word or struck any kind of deal. Four: I think once you're in it with someone, you owe them something. If we've set off to do a thing together and I wander off because something better turned up, I haven't just annoyed you, I've wronged you, and not in a small way. Five, and this one feels like the bedrock of my whole way of thinking about right and wrong: I don't think you can be bound by an obligation you never took on, and I don't think you can be stuck in one you can't get out of. If you could always walk away (maybe at a price, but you could), then it's a real obligation; if you couldn't ever walk away no matter what, I don't think that's an obligation, that's just being trapped. Six: when I blame a company or a government for something, what I really mean is that the people in it did it — the blame lands on individuals, spread across them. I don't believe there's any leftover blame that belongs to the group as such, sitting there once you've accounted for every single person.

I've got a nagging feeling something in that pile doesn't fit, but I honestly can't tell which bit, or whether I'm just being thick. Could you go through it and tell me which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they clash, and how bad each real clash is? If something has to give, I want to know which one and what it costs me to give it up, and which single change would tidy up the most mess. I'm not after a reading list or the names of famous philosophers, I want the actual argument.

Write it as plain prose please, like an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. And dont just tell me it all hangs together, I know it doesn't.
