I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so forgive me if I mangle the terms. Two philosopher friends of mine have been arguing for years: one is a hardline counterfactualist about causation (a cause is whatever makes a difference across possible worlds), the other a hardline interventionist (a cause is something you could in principle wiggle with an ideal intervention). They keep talking past each other, and I want to steal their fight for a plot. Write the joint statement they could both sign after an honest adversarial collaboration. I want a numbered list of claims they agree on about causation -- including the usual zoo of cases, omission, double prevention (one thing preventing a preventer), preemption, overdetermination -- then a disagreements table with the exact remaining disputes, each phrased so the side it belongs to would accept the wording, then the cruxes, with the case or argument both would agree bears on each crux and each side's prediction written down. Test it on a scene from my novel: a stagehand sneezes and lets go of a rope, dropping a sandbag on the leading lady; unknown to him, the villain had already sawed halfway through the rope, and the understudy had also planned to cut it at the same moment, but the sneeze happened first. Have the counterfactualist and the interventionist say whether the sneeze caused the death, whether the sawing did, and what would change their minds. Don't give me reading lists or citations, just the document.
