I've argued myself into a corner on secession and I need someone to show me the way out. I'm pro self-determination — if Scots or Catalans vote for independance I don't think London or Madrid just gets to veto it. But a friend of mine, a public-choice type who thinks a state is basically a big club you can resign from, built this and I can't knock it down:

(1) A people has the right to decide which political community it belongs to; that's just what it is to be a self-governing people instead of somebody's property.
(2) That right hangs on the people's own choice and not on the existing state's consent — otherwise it isn't self-determination at all.
(3) A people is any group of persons who share a distinct identity and are capable of governing themselves together.
(4) So any such group can, by majority vote, detach the territory it lives on from the state that currently governs it.
(5) And the right doesnt disappear just because the group is richer than the rest, or because the rest of the country would be worse off — that's a point about consequences, and this right isn't a consequentialist one.
(6) Therefore a wealthy suburb, or any town with a distinct enough identity and a majority, may walk off with its roads and its coastline, stop paying into the national health service, or secede to keep out immigrants the rest of the country admits.

(7) That last bit seems flatly wrong to me — a rich town shouldn't get to take the infrastructure and leave everyone else holding the bag just because it can win a vote.
(8) But I don't want to give up (1), which is the whole reason I care about any of this.

So where's the mistake? Is one of (1)-(5) doing something it shouldn't, or is (6) actually fine and I just find it ugly? And if something has to go, what does it cost me — does it cost me Scotland, Catalonia, and Quebec too?
