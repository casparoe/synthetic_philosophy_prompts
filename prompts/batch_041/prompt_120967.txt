I have been reading rationalist blogs for long enough that the intentional stance has stopped being a position I hold and become a habit of prediction I cannot switch off, and the ant colony is the case I use to check whether I mean what I say about minds. Aunt Hillary, the colony in Hofstadter's Ant Fugue dialogue in Gödel, Escher, Bach, is the cleanest version of the puzzle I know: her mind, if she has one, is nothing over and above the ants, and yet there is no place in the colony where the believing happens. I would like you to write me a joke about whether an ant colony can have a mind.

The punchline has to turn on the actual structure of the case. What makes it interesting is that a colony's mental states, if it has any, would be unowned: no ant believes anything, no ant decides anything, there is no extra ant or inner chamber where the deciding gets done, and yet the colony is the best candidate we have for a mind assembled out of parts that have none — think of a colony choosing between two nest sites when no single ant has visited both. A joke whose punchline would survive swapping the colony for a traffic jam or a stock market is not the joke I am asking for. Neither are the usual puns about hive minds, ants as communists, or the colony as an internet, and I do not want a wry observation about how strange the case is, dressed up as a punchline.

It should be a real joke, with a setup and a turn, funny to someone who has genuinely worried about this, and tellable out loud in a paragraph or two without diagrams. It should still be funny to someone who disagrees about whether the colony has a mind. Do not explain it afterwards; if it needs explaining, it is not the joke I asked for.

Please write it in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — the way you would put it in an email to me.
