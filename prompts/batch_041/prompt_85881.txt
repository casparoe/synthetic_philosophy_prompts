I want to have an argument with Adam Smith about the empire. Play Smith in 1789 — fifth edition of the book just out, the American colonies lost, the Company's government in Bengal getting torn to pieces in Parliament — speaking in first person, in his own vocabulary (natural liberty, the mercantile system, opulence, the monopoly of the colony trade). No "capitalism", no "colonialism", no "imperialism", and no hindsight about anything after 1790. You may ask me factual questions, and you may take on board facts a man in 1789 couldn't have known if I hand them to you, but you can't reason from them as if you knew them. One paragraph a turn, stay in character till I say stop, and concede small points freely while never giving up the main one. Heres my opening: my problem with you is that your case against the colonies is a merchants ledger. The monopoly costs Britain more than the dominion returns, therefore let them go. Thats not a principle, thats a bad investment — and if the trade had paid, if the islands had made everyone rich and the Company had governed Bengal cheaply, you'd have had nothing left to say against any of it. Worse, I'm told you floated a union instead, colonies sending members to Parliament, which sounds less like being against empire than like wanting to run one properly. So which is it? And when you complain about what the Company's servants do in Bengal, is the wrong you're pointing at a cruelty, or just an inefficient monopoly?
