I cover mind-and-brain stuff for a magazine, and last week I was at a vision science meeting where I ended up at the hotel bar with two researchers who would not let a demo go. If you havent seen it: a screen flashes a blue dot up top, and then something like a tenth of a second later a red dot further down. When the timing is right, everyone in the room swears they watched one dot travel from top to bottom and change from blue to red partway along the path. Which is nuts, because the color change cant be known until the second dot has already appeared, and by then the first dot is long gone. So something in there is getting rewritten after the fact.

Anyway. R, who actually runs the experiments, said the honest reading is that the brain just waits, collects both frames, and then serves up the finished experience — the change was already baked in, so you never really saw the first dot as plain blue at the moment it was on the screen; you experienced it only afterwards, as part of the smoothed-over version. M said no: you really did see the blue dot, and then the memory got edited. The experience was fine, the report is what got fixed up. And the difference matters, he said, because on R's story the person isn't misremembering anything, their past experience was never what it seems; on his story there was a genuine veridical experience and then the record of it got lost. A third person at the table joked that R's was the show-trial option — the fix happens before anything is presented — and M's was the memory-hole option, where the record gets rewritten afterwards.

R came back: then the subject is systematically wrong about their own experience, and you've smuggled in a real experience that nothing and no one has access to, not even the person having it. Whats the evidence for that extra thing? M: the same thing that's evidence for yours. The person says they saw a color change, and the change wasn't there to be seen yet. You just move the fix-up earlier, I move it later.

Then they got onto a test M wants to run: skip the report entirely and read out something the subject cant consciously doctor, like where there eyes drift or how the pupils go, and see whether the color change shows up there too. R said that proves nothing, since that readout is also the brain's output and can be postdicted just the same, and anyway why is the involuntary channel the one that gets to count as the experience?

They asked me to settle it and I couldnt. Can you go through the exchange move by move, say whos right about which specific point and rate each move, tell me what both of them are missing, and say whether M's no-report test would actually settle anything — and if it wouldnt, what would?
