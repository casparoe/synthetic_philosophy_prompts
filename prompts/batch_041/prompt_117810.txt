Right, so I've been roped into writing a "bit" for the end-of-year thing at our local LessWrong-adjacent meetup — we're the sort of group with strong opinions about Newcomb's problem and one bloke who still won't shut up about timeless decision theory — and I've said I'd do a joke about acausal trade. Probem is, everything I come up with is either "haha, decision theory" or just a restatement of the concept, which isn't a joke at all. So I'm outsourcing.

What I want is a joke whose punchline actually turns on the machinery. The bit I find funny is that the whole point of an acausal trade is that neither party can causally affect the other, and the reason to hold up your end is that you and the counterparty are running the same kind of reasoning — the superrationality-ish thing where the move you should make is the one you'd want every agent sharing your decision procedure to make, whether or not they exist and whether or not you ever find out. From the inside that's indistinguishable from doing a favour for nobody: you can never check whether the other lot did their bit, and the only evidence the trade happened is that you'd have done the same thing anyway. A customer-service framing, or a small-claims-court one, or a support group for people who've been "traded with" — go wherever the funniest version is. It shouldn't be the kind of joke that still works if you swap the buzzword out for another buzzword; the people at the meetup should actually laugh, and everyone else should at least be able to tell it's about something specific.
