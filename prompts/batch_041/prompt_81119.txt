Write a dialogue of roughly 1000 words between two equally competent disputants on how a rational agent should size a sequence of favorable but risky bets. The first argues for geometric rationality: that what an agent should maximize is the growth rate of its wealth over time, that the log-optimal (Kelly) stake therefore answers the question "how much should I bet?", and that this is a theorem about compounding rather than a preference, since any other policy is almost surely overtaken in the long run. The second is an expected-utility theorist: she holds that maximizing expected log wealth is one expected-utility rule among indefinitely many, that the growth-rate argument tacitly fixes an infinite horizon and identifies the agent with its own time-average, and that long-run dominance is a claim about a limit and so gives no advice at any finite time.

The exchange should turn on at least these three pressure points, with each side offering a real reply to each: (a) an agent making many independent, simultaneous, uncorrelated bets — or one that is one of many parallel copies of itself — for which the ensemble average rather than the time average seems to be what matters, and for which the growth-optimal stake per bet shrinks as the number of bets grows; (b) whether the growth-rate result says anything about what an agent should care about, or only about what happens to wealth, so that the dispute may reduce to a disagreement about utility functions; and (c) betting under model uncertainty, and the common practice of staking a fixed fraction of the Kelly stake, which neither theory seems to justify cleanly.

Neither disputant may be a strawman: each needs at least two substantive replies to the other's central argument, and the dialogue should end in unresolved disagreement rather than a synthesis that papers over it. If my framing is confused — if these two positions do not in fact conflict, or if the interesting question lies elsewhere — say so plainly rather than writing the dialogue as asked; otherwise, close with a brief note outside the dialogue flagging where the piece takes a contested reading of the ideas or simplifies past the point a specialist would accept.
