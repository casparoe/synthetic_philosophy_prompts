I'm in a genocide studies seminar and I'm trying to write a thought experiment paper where I imagine Raphael Lemkin alive today, fully caught up on everything since 1959, and I ask him about what's happened to the concept he basically invented. The prof keeps saying the UN Genocide Convention narrowed his original idea—dropped cultural genocide, made it mostly about physical destruction, and turned "genocide" into this weird hybrid of a legal charge and a moral megaphone. So I want you to predict what Lemkin would actually say about a bunch of posthumous developments: the ICC and the whole "atrocity crimes" umbrella (genocide vs crimes against humanity vs war crimes), the push to add ecocide, the R2P doctrine, and especially the politics of naming in places like Ukraine, Gaza, Xinjiang, Myanmar and Sudan. Would he be happy the word became a weapon in public discourse, or horrified it got so politicized that legal institutions tiptoe around it? Would he want to expand the definition or defend its narrowness? And what would he make of the idea of "slow genocide"—starvation, land dispossession, cultural erasure—versus the conventional picture of mass killing as a discrete event?

A friend in my class proposed something she calls the "naming trilemma": any atrocity response has to trade off legal precision, political efficacy, and survivor recognition, and you can't max out all three. She thinks Lemkin was naive because he assumed the legal label would deliver all three at once. I want you to reconstruct Lemkin's likely response to that trilemma, and then apply it to one specific invented case—say, a state that documents another state's mass atrocities in meticulous detail, calls them genocide, but then refuses to use the term for its own forced assimilation policies or its ally's siege tactics. What would Lemkin say about that kind of asymmetric naming? Would he accept a strategic, gradualist use of the word, or insist on a single universal standard even if it means the word loses political force? I'm not looking for a bibliography or a list of his actual writings; I want a reasoned prediction, as in the weeds as you can get about the definitional trade-offs, the role of intent, and whether cultural and ecological destruction should count.
