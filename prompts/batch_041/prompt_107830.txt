Two or more people arrive at the same result at roughly the same time, and our institutions respond in ways that look, on inspection, as if they were built by people holding incompatible beliefs about what a discovery is. I want to know what the system actually presupposes.

Four features of it as it stands. First, scientific credit — the citation, the eponym, the invitation to give the named lecture — goes to whoever publishes first; the second publisher's independent arrival is at best a footnote and at worst an insinuation of theft. Second, journals print a date of receipt on the first page, and priority disputes are settled by those timestamps and by the timestamps on preprints, which is to say by administrative record rather than by any judgment about who did the harder thinking. Third, patent law awards the patent to the first applicant and treats a later, wholly independent inventor as an infringer — independent creation is no defense — whereas copyright law takes the opposite line, since two authors who never saw each other's work can each hold rights in their own expression. Fourth, prize committees fix the number of names: the Nobel rules allow at most three laureates per prize in a category and generally exclude posthumous awards, so a collective achievement has to be squeezed into a fixed count of persons.

Here is what puzzles me. The frequency of independent simultaneous discovery is usually taken as evidence that the discovery was going to happen anyway — that the time was ripe, that the result was in the air, that the field had assembled the pieces and someone was bound to fit them together. If that is right, the individual discoverer looks less like an author and more like the person who happened to be standing where the lightning struck. Yet the whole apparatus of priority, timestamps, patents and eponyms treats the discoverer as an author whose personal act is the thing that matters, and treats the second arrival as either irrelevant or suspect. The same community that treats an independent replication as the strongest possible evidence for a claim treats an independent discovery as a threat to be managed.

Reconstruct the implicit theory of discovery, authorship and desert that these features only make sense under. I want to know, in particular: whether they presuppose that a discovery is a fact about the world that gets found — so that two finders find one thing — or a human creation, so that two inventors make two things; whether the credit system presupposes a desert-based justification or a purely incentive-based one, and whether the difference shows up anywhere in the mechanics rather than only in the rhetoric; and which of the four features are jointly consistent and which are not, once you separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely happens to fit.

I don't want a history of any of this, and I don't want sources or a reading list; I want the presupposition analysis.

Please structure the reply as follows.

1. The practice, restated (about 150 words): set out the four features as commitments rather than as events.
2. What it strictly presupposes (about 600 words): the claims about discovery, authorship and desert without which the features would make no sense, saying which feature does the presupposing in each case.
3. What it merely fits (about 300 words): views compatible with the practice but not required by it, with the observation that would show the practice does not need them.
4. The tension (about 400 words): where the features presuppose inconsistent things, and whether the inconsistency survives the distinction you drew in section 2 or dissolves into a merely apparent one.
5. If the presupposition were replaced (about 400 words): take the rival view — discoveries as social inevitabilities rather than individual feats — and go feature by feature, saying what would have to change, what would only be re-described, and what would be untouched.
6. Bottom line (about 150 words): your verdict on whether the system has a coherent implicit theory, and the one feature most likely to be revised if it does not.
