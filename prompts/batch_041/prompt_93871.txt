Background so we're on the same page (don't just recite it back at me, use it): in risk-weighted expected utility you take a gamble, rank its outcomes worst to best, and value it as the utility of the worst outcome, plus, for each step up from one outcome to the next, the utility gained at that step multiplied by r(the chance of getting at least the better of the two outcomes). Here r is the risk function — increasing, with r(0)=0 and r(1)=1. If r(p) sits below p everywhere in between, the agent is risk averse. Expected utility is just the special case where r is the identity, i.e. r(p)=p.
