I'm putting together a podcast epsiode with a philosopher whose new book is a reading of Kripkenstein I've not seen put quite this way. His line is that the so-called sceptical solution isn't a theory of meaning at all, it's a theory of what he calls meaning-licences: to say that someone means plus is to issue a licence to hold them to certain moves, and licences can be warranted or unwarranted but never true or false. He also thinks the straight-solution versus sceptical-solution framing is a trap, because 'solution' already presupposes that there was a meaning-fact sitting there to be found. And the last chapter argues that the whole argument is really about the determinacy of content, so it lands hardest not on platonism about meanings but on any account that individuates content by a speaker's dispositions, including the computational ones. He does concede, as Kripke does, that this may not be the historical Wittgenstein.

Give me eight to ten questions that get past him just restating all that. I need at least one on the gerrymandering worry coming back at the community instead of the individual -- why can't the community's training practice be re-described so that it licenses quus, and what stops a community from simply being wrong? -- one on whether the licence view is just expressivism about meaning-attributions, and if so how he answers the 'if he means plus then he'll say 125' style worry about embedding; and one on the people who argue that meaning isn't normative in any prescriptive, ought-entailing sense (Hattiangadi and Glüer and Wikforss are the usual names, I think) -- does his view need them to be wrong? Then one pressing him on whether his reading is a solution or a change of subject, and one on whether the sceptic's own hypothesis is even stateable in his terms. For each question, a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. Nothing about what drew him to philosophy, please.
