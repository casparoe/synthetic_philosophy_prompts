I have recently begun a research-creation project on the DNA–Gödel isomorphism, building a small typogenetic simulator, and I have run into a conceptual disagreement with my collaborator that I would like you to help me resolve. Please act as an expert in formal systems, theoretical biology, and the philosophy of self-reference. Think carefully before answering, and begin by brainstorming the candidate criteria for self-replication that a typogenetic system might satisfy before you commit to any one of them.

The simulator works as follows. A strand is a finite string over the alphabet A, C, G, T. Each strand encodes a set of enzymes through a fixed translation table: each non-overlapping triplet is read as a codon for one enzyme. An enzyme is a typographical operation on strings: it recognizes a short pattern and then copies, deletes, inserts, or transposes symbols at that site. A system consists of an initial strand together with the enzymes that strand encodes. At each step, any enzyme present may act on any strand present. I call a system self-replicating if, starting from a single copy of its initial strand and the enzymes encoded by that strand, it eventually produces another exact copy of the initial strand without any enzyme or strand supplied from outside the system. I am not asking about actual molecular biology; I am asking about the formal property.

Here are my verdicts on seven systems I have constructed. First, a strand that encodes a single enzyme which copies any strand it encounters, including the strand that encodes it: self-replicating. Second, a strand that encodes an enzyme which copies that strand specifically, but the enzyme is only produced when a separate helper strand, not encoded by the first strand, is present: not self-replicating. Third, a strand that encodes an enzyme which copies the strand, but the copy always begins at the second symbol, so the product is one symbol short: not self-replicating. Fourth, a strand that encodes two enzymes, one of which copies the strand and the other of which activates the first, but the second is produced only after the first has acted once, so the system needs an external kick to start: not self-replicating. Fifth, a strand that encodes no copying enzyme at all, but whose sequence is such that a universal copying enzyme encoded elsewhere, if supplied, would produce an exact copy of it: not self-replicating. Sixth, a strand that encodes an enzyme which copies the strand and also copies the enzyme-encoding instructions, so that the copy can in turn produce the same enzyme: self-replicating. Seventh, a strand that encodes an enzyme which copies a different strand, and that different strand encodes the enzyme that copies the first strand, so the two strands together produce copies of each other but neither does so alone: not self-replicating for either strand alone, but self-replicating for the pair.

I keep going back and forth on one further case I have not built yet. Eighth, a strand that encodes an enzyme which assembles a second strand from free symbols, where the second strand encodes an enzyme that copies the first strand exactly; the first strand itself encodes no enzyme that copies it, and the second strand is not present initially. Would you call this eighth system self-replicating?

What I want from you is not a survey of definitions from the literature. I want the principle, or the small set of competing principles, that best captures my seven verdicts. State each candidate precisely, say which of my verdicts it fits and which it cannot, and tell me which of my verdicts I should be prepared to give up if I choose the simplest principle that saves the most. Then apply the surviving principle to the eighth case, and if two principles diverge on it, name the further typogenetic case that would tell them apart. Please also say what the DNA–Gödel isomorphism would predict here, if anything, and where the analogy between a self-replicating strand and a self-referential sentence or program breaks down. I am less interested in whether my terminology matches anyone else’s than in whether there is a coherent criterion underneath my verdicts.
