I need one essay question on free-floating rationales — the idea that a behavior can have a reason even when no one is doing the reasoning — that cannot be answered by reciting the usual examples from biology. The exam is for advanced undergraduates and graduate students, 90 minutes, closed-book. The question should use a concrete case from current ML: an RL agent trained with a learned reward model that was itself trained on human comparisons. The question should ask whether there is a fact of the matter about the agent's reasons, and whether the free-floating rationale is located in the agent, the reward model, the training process, or the designer's description. Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points totaling 20 marks, each with a one-line note on the characteristic mistake. The question should be answerable without citations or a reading list.
