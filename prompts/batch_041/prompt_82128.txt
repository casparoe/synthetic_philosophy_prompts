For a seminar paper on the timing of experience I have been reading about the color phi phenomenon — the 1976 finding by Kolers and von Grünau that when a blue dot is flashed above and a red dot below in quick succession, subjects report a single dot that travels and changes color about halfway along its path, apparently before the second dot could have been seen. What interests me is not the illusion itself but the argument it has generated, which seems to have been running in place for decades. One family of interpretations says the perceptual process is slow and constructive: the brain waits, assembles a coherent narrative with the color change already placed in the middle, and then presents that finished product to the subject — the show-trial picture, in which the fabricated evidence is what gets presented. The other family says the experience is delivered in real time and the revision happens afterward, in memory or in the machinery that produces the report — the picture of a record that is quietly rewritten after the fact. These two readings are usually labeled Stalinesque and Orwellian, and Dennett and Kinsbourne, who introduced the labels, argued that no experiment could ever tell them apart, because every piece of evidence we have — including the subject's report — has already passed through the very memory and reporting systems that the second reading says do the editing, so the choice between the readings is not a choice about a fact. Others have pushed back, either by offering a different explanation of the phenomenon altogether or by insisting that there must still be a fact of the matter about when the experience occurred.

Here is what I would like from you. First, a diagnosis rather than a verdict: why has this dispute stalled? Is it a substantive disagreement about the mind that we merely lack the tools to settle, a verbal dispute trading on two senses of the word experience — one meaning the internal state that causes the report, the other meaning the thing the report is about — or two groups answering different questions under one heading, one about the neural timing of processing and one about what it is like for the subject? I have a hypothesis I would like you to test rather than assume: that the stall is not really about color phi at all but about whether the timing of experience is a well-formed notion in the first place, so that Dennett's dissolution and his opponents' insistence are not two answers to a single question but a question and a refusal to accept that it has been withdrawn. Second, and more concretely, tell me whether the alleged indistinguishability is as airtight as it is made to sound. Suppose I wanted to operationalize the difference without relying on the subject's verbal report about when the change happened — for instance by using a probe that must be given before the second stimulus could plausibly be processed, or a measure that is not under the subject's introspective control, such as a priming or reflexive response. Would that be a genuine test of the Stalinesque versus Orwellian question, or would it merely relocate the same problem into a different output channel? Third, and given your diagnosis, say what would actually count as progress here — a new experiment, a conceptual distinction that separates the two readings, a formal result about what is measurable, or an argument that no progress should be expected because the question, as posed, has no fact behind it. I would much rather have the structure of the disagreement laid out with reasons than a survey of who said what.
