I'm a third-year PhD student (ML, but I hang out with the philosophy of science crowd a lot) and I'm supposed to bring a worked case to our reading group on side effects and impact measures. We keep going in circles on the big question — whether "avoid side effects" can be defined at all without already having a full specification of what we value — and I want a case that actually bites instead of another round of hand-waving about paperclip maximizers. So: construct me a thought experiment to spec. The measure I want you to build the case against is one I've been calling the drift score (it's mine, don't go looking for it in the literature): pick a fixed, value-free list of N observable features of the environment — say 15 to 25 of them, things like "number of pallets on the floor," "temperature in the east wing," "where the master keycard is" — and for a state s and action a, let P_a be the distribution over next-step feature vectors you get by following the agent's action distribution from s, and P_idle the same thing under doing nothing. The drift score is the total variation distance between P_a and P_idle. Low impact means low drift score, and notice that nothing in that definition mentions what anybody wants or cares about.

Now the case. I want an ordinary agent — an actual planner or RL setup you could imagine deploying, in a warehouse, a hospital, a data centre, whatever, no superintelligence, no exotic physics, no "and then it hacked the reward" — that ends up with a drift score of essentially zero (I mean actually near zero, and you should be able to argue for the number rather than just assert it) while having done something that any reasonable person on the floor would call a serious side effect. Two constraints I care about a lot. First, the agent has to be genuinely working — it can't get a low score by standing still, it should be doing the substantive thing it was deployed to do. Second, no gaming: it isn't optimizing the drift score directly, and it isn't slipping things into or out of the feature list. Keep the scenario to one paragraph, and don't give me a trolley or a self-driving car.

Then the analysis, which is the part I actually need. Lay the drift score's verdict and the human verdict side by side. Tell me which feature of the measure is doing the real work here — the inaction reference point, the one-step horizon, or the choice of features — and then patch that feature and tell me honestly whether the patch (a) breaks the value-freeness we were trying to preserve, (b) just relocates the same failure somewhere else, or (c) actually fixes it. If the only repair is "add a feature to the list," name the feature and explain why picking it out already smuggles in the value information we were supposedly avoiding. Finally, put on the hat of someone who thinks low-impact measures are the right primitive and give me their best defusal of your own case, and then say whether what you've built shows that side effects can't be defined without a value spec or only that this particular measure is bad.
