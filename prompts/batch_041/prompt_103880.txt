Stuff I'd bet is old:
- the parables, especially the prodigal son and the good samaritan: they're vivid, they don't push a line the later church needed, and they're strange in ways that help nobody.
- the baptism by John: it makes him the junior partner of somebody else, and it's a baptism for the forgiveness of sins, which is awkward if you already think he was sinless. Nobody invents that.
- the dying cry about being forsaken: an invented messiah doesn't go out screaming that God has abandoned him.
- the expectation that the end is coming right now, within a generation.

Stuff I'd bet is late:
- give to Caesar what's Caesar's: it settles a controversy the church was having later, and it settles it too neatly. It reads like the answer somebody needed to already have in hand.
- the dead coming out of their tombs and walking into town when he dies, and the temple curtain tearing: special effects.
- the empty tomb: exactly what the movement needed, doing so much work that I can't trust it.
- the sayings about letting the dead bury their dead, and hating your family: this one I keep flip flopping on. Too brutal to invent? Or invented to give missionaries permission to walk out on their families?

Here's what I think I'm doing when I make these calls, spelled out. (1) If it's counterproductive for the later church, it's probably old. (2) If it doesn't sound like what a first century Jew would have said and doesn't sound like what the church would have said, it's probably old. (3) If a detail is pointless — serves no agenda, would be a nuisance to make up — I treat it as inherited. (4) If it coheres with what I've already accepted, small bump.

I can already see (1) and (2) yanking against each other. The end-is-near material is thoroughly Jewish, so (2) hates it, but once the first generation died and nothing happened it was embarrassing, so (1) loves it. And the Caesar line is embarrassing-ish, so (1) likes it, while it sounds exactly like what the church would have wanted, so (2) kills it. Meanwhile (3) and (4) are doing almost no work in either direction.
