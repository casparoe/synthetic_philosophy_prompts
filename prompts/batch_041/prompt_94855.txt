I'm on the board of my county historical society. I'm a software engineer, I joined because I like local history, and I have zero philosophy training, so when we vote on this stuff I mostly go with my gut and then write up the minutes. I also keep a spreadsheet of every vote, which is how I can tell you all this without leaving anything out.

Here's what we've actually done over the last eight years, roughly in order:

- 1911 lynching marker. The descendant family wanted a marker at the site. Some neighbors said it would bring outside agitators. We approved it, and I pushed to include the names of the men who did it, which we did. The family was fine with that.
- Doctor's papers. A local doctor's estate gave us his papers, and they turned out to include records from the 1930s showing he sterilized people at the county home. His grandchildren asked us to close those files. We kept them open and put some of it in an exhibit. They were pretty upset.
- The 1950s list. We have a list of local people who named their neighbors to the FBI back then. A researcher wanted us to publish the whole thing in an exhibit, names and all. We said no, on the grounds that the people who got hurt are dead and the informers' grandkids still live here and nothing good comes of it. The list is still in the archive though, anyone can come in and read it.
- The 1931 expulsions. Families who were pushed out of the county back then asked us for a plaque. We approved it even though a couple of members said why dig this up, nobody alive did it.
- Founder statue. Our founder was also big into eugenics. We voted to move the statue indoors and put up a much longer label about the eugenics part. We did not destroy it and I don't think we ever would.
- The mill deal. The company that ran the old mill offered to pay for a whole exhibit about the creek cleanup if we'd agree to seal the medical records of the workers who got sick, for fifty years. We said no to the seal and yes to their money for a different exhibit. Weird meeting.

Now the new case. The old company town is being turned into apartments. There's a memorial wall there with the names of 60 workers who died of the lung disease from the mill dust. The mill's dust measurement records are in our archive and a reporter has asked for them. The workers' descendants, there aren't many left, came to us and asked for two things: let the wall come down (the developer will store the plaques in our basement, nothing gets thrown away), and seal the dust records for fifty years. Their reasons: it's turned into a tourist thing, they get asked about it constantly, one of them said she's tired of being a exhibit, and they'd honestly just like it forgotten.

So what do we do? I want to know what we'd decide if we're being consistent with ourselves, which of our old cases is the closest analog, and what rule we've been running this whole time without knowing it. I suspect more than one rule fits everything we've done so far, and I want to know which ones, and where exactly they disagree about this case. One member who took philosophy in college keeps saying you can't owe anything to people who don't exist anymore, the living are the only ones with standing, and another one says that's exactly how these things get forgotten. I don't know who's right, I just want to know what we've been doing.

If the old votes really don't settle it, tell me what we'd be signing up for with each way of deciding, and what case would come along later that would tell the two apart.
