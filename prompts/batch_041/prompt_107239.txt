A friend who's doing a PhD in decision theory summarised risk-weighted expected utility for me like this:

'Expected utility maximisers weight each outcome by its probability. Risk-weighted expected utility keeps the probabilities but adds a risk function: you rank the outcomes from worst to best, then apply the risk function to the cumulative probabilities, so a 50% shot at the good outcome gets treated as if the odds were a bit worse than 50%. It's basically Kahneman and Tversky's probability weighting, except applied to cumulative rather than individual probabilities, which is what rescues it from the old objection that prospect theory lets you prefer a dominated option. The nice thing is that the risk-averse agent isn't making any mistake or misreading the odds — she's just allowed to have a taste for certainty, the way she's allowed to prefer tea to coffee — and that's how the theory explains Allais-style choices while keeping beliefs and desires cleanly seperate.'

I'm fairly convinced by this but it feels like it might be smoothing over something. Where would someone who actually works on this wince?
