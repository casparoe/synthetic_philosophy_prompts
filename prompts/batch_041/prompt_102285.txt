I'm putting together an anthology of fiction that dramatizes ideas about representation, and I need a piece about picture languages — the kind of thing Otto Neurath was after with his pictorial statistics (isotypes: rows of identical little figures where one figure stands for a fixed amount, instead of one giant figure for a big amount, cause people are bad at judging areas). Write me a short story set in the statistical bureau of a fictional city, where a young designer has been hired to turn the city's numbers into pictures for a mass audience. She's convinced pictures are the honest universal language — no jargon, no metaphysics, just the facts laid out where anyone can read them — while an older colleague keeps insisting every choice is already a claim: what gets counted, how it's counted, whether "unemployment" even has a sharp edge or is a fuzzy conglomerate of overlapping things that different offices tally differently, so the picture always smuggles a theory in through the back door. Have the story turn on one specific picture she has to finish before a deadline, and let the disagreement stay unresolved. No lectures, no narrator explaining what Neurath thought — just the scene. Under 700 words.
