Please design a thought experiment that would, if anything could, settle whether the disagreement between two ways of telling the story of the colour phi phenomenon is an empirical one or merely a dispute about how to describe the same facts. In the phenomenon itself, a dot flashed briefly at one place in one colour and, a fraction of a second later, another dot flashed at a different place in a different colour; observers report a single dot that travelled and changed colour partway along its path, as though the second flash had shaped an experience that was already under way. One telling of this, which I will call the Orwellian, holds that the observer really did experience the original sequence and that the record of it was rewritten afterwards, much as a state airbrushes an inconvenient photograph out of the archive. The other, the Stalinesque, holds that the revised version is the only one ever presented to the observer, the fabrication having been assembled before anything reached the observer at all. Construct a variant of the display on which these two tellings are forced apart: there must be some ordinary, non-invasive probe — a further judgement, a reaction time, a later act of recall, a report taken at a different moment — whose outcome the Orwellian telling requires and the Stalinesque telling forbids. The display must be something anyone could watch on an ordinary screen, with no surgery, drugs, or exotic apparatus; the probe must be something a psychology laboratory could run tomorrow; and the case must not describe the underlying mechanism, since that would beg the question by assuming one of the two tellings.

Then tell the case twice, once as an Orwellian and once as a Stalinesque, giving for each exactly what it predicts about the probe. Say which feature of the design is doing the discriminating work, what becomes of the case if that feature is removed, and — this is the part I most want — whether a defender of the telling that comes out worse can simply move the revision a step later and absorb the probe's outcome, and if so, what that shows about whether the two were ever making different claims. Write the whole answer as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
