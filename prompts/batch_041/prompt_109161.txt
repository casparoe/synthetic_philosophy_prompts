I've been following the qualia wars for years now and it feels like the Dennett side and the Chalmers/Block side just talk past each other. Dennett's move in the quining paper is basically: define qualia as intrinsic, private, ineffable, and incorrigible, then use intuition pumps (the two coffee tasters whose preferences and taste reports come apart, the wine taster whose brain gets rewired, etc.) to show that nothing actually answers to that description, so the posit is incoherent. The other side says he just defined qualia out of existence -- phenomenal character is obviously real, the coffee case only shows something about introspection and report, and no amount of functional or representational detail tells you why there's something it's like.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal, with Dennett attacking an overstuffed definition nobody needs while the realists use a thinner notion of phenomenal character? Is it substantive disagreement about whether introspection is authoritative, i.e. whether we can be wrong about how things seem? Is it a methodological fight -- intuition pumps vs conceivability arguments like zombies and inverted spectra -- where neither side will accept the others evidence? Or is it two different questions under one word, one side asking what a theory of mind has to explain and the other asking whats left over after the explanation?

I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the literature. And be honest about the upshot: given your diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress here -- a sharper distinction, a case both sides have to accept, a formal result -- or is it just bedrock intuition all the way down and we should go home? If you think my framing is already loaded toward one side, say so.
