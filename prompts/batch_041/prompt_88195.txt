My mate and I burned about four hours last night arguing about Sidgwick's psychical research, and I need to get my act together before we pick it up again on Thursday. His line: Sidgwick was a great ethicist who happened to have a crankish side-hobby, the Society for Psychical Research stuff has nothing to do with the Methods of Ethics, different project, different audience, probably just a Victorian emminent-person's way of not facing up to his own religious collapse. My line: it's all one thing. Sidgwick's whole problem at the end of the Methods is that practical reason comes apart — egoism tells you one thing, universal benevolence another, both seem to survive his own tests, and he can't find a purely ethical way to choose between them. The only thing that would really heal that split is a fact about how the world is put together: that duty and happiness converge in the long run, that the universe is governed in some sense, so that being good isn't finally a sucker's bet. And that's exactly the kind of thing he was after with the psychical research — survival of bodily death, some evidence of an order that carries on past the grave. He was a founder and the first president of the SPR, so this wasn't a weekend hobby.

I don't want you to just adjudicate the arguement, I want the specification, so I can win on the merits. Write me the desiderata: what would a body of evidence from psychical research have to establish, and what would the evidence itself have to be like, for it to actually do the work Sidgwick wanted — to close the dualism of practical reason rather than just adding one more spooky fact to the pile. Give me the requirements list with the consideration behind each, say which ones are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of a particular camp (fraud-proof observation, repeatability, the whole super-ESP / subliminal-self escape hatch, the gap between evidence for survival and evidence for a morally ordered universe, whether a statistical result about mediums could bear on a philosophical thesis at all, and whatever else you think belongs). Flag any pair of requirements you think can't be met together — if the spec turns out unsatisfiable, that's the most interesting result and I want it said out loud, and I want to know which single requirement does the most work, i.e. which one kills off the most of the plausible approaches.
