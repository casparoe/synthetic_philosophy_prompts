Turing's treatment of extrasensory perception in connection with the imitation game leaves open whether the game measures intelligence or only whichever communicative channels happen to be open. Construct a thought experiment that isolates telepathy as the single variable in an otherwise ordinary imitation game, for use in a seminar on whether the game's verdicts are robust to non-standard channels. Specifications: the machine and the human confederate are stipulated to be equally good conversationalists when no telepathic channel is available; no other paranormal faculty is involved, no exotic hardware is introduced, and the interrogator's competence does not change between runs; the case must be one in which the unshielded and shielded runs yield opposite verdicts about which participant is the machine. Present the response as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario; (2) the controlled comparison, showing that telepathy is the only difference between the runs; (3) the verdict in the unshielded run and the reasoning that produces it; (4) the verdict in the shielded run and the reasoning that produces it; (5) the feature of the case that is doing the work, what becomes of the verdicts if that feature is removed, and the strongest reply from someone who holds that the machine's performance should be judged the same in both runs.
