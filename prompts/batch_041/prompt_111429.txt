I am a high school student who helps run our philosophy club, which meets for fifty minutes with about fifteen students. I want to design a session that makes us feel the force of the Knobe effect—the asymmetry in judgments about whether an agent brought about a side effect intentionally, depending on whether the side effect is bad or good—without simply lecturing us about it. The plan should turn us into both subjects and experimenters: we should first take part in a short, blind vignette study, then tabulate our own responses, then discover the asymmetry in the data and in our own reactions. Please give me the rules, the vignettes or a template for them, how to randomize and collect responses, a simple scoring or prediction element if it helps, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the experimental-philosophy idea. I would especially like a third condition that tests whether the effect is about badness specifically or about norm violation more generally, so that we do not merely reproduce the classic result. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time—students guessing the hypothesis, uneven groups, someone objecting that the vignettes are artificial—and how to handle it. Finally, be honest about which part of the debate the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to explain, for example the methodological dispute over whether these judgments reveal the folk concept of intentional action or are artifacts of how the questions are phrased. Do not name the effect until the debrief.
