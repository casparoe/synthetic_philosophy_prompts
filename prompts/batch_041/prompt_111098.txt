I've been lurking on LessWrong and the rationalist blogosphere since forever, and next month I'm doing an email interview for my little newsletter with a philosopher who's spent years defending the hedonistic imperative -- the abolitionist program, engineering the biology of suffering out of every sentient creature. He's a true believer, in the "the biological substrates of suffering will be eradicated completely" sense, and he's sharp, so I dont want to hand him softballs he's already answered on a dozen podcasts. I want questions that make him actually do work.

Give me 8-10 questions, each with a follow-up written to his most likely answer -- not a generic "can you say more," the follow-up should assume he gave the standard reply and push from there. Keep them concrete and biological where you can; I'd much rather he talk about receptors and feedback loops than about the human condition.

I specifically want at least one question on each of these:

- What the target actually is: the sensory pain part, or the "and it bothers me" part. Chronic pain patients sometimes get procedures that leave the pain sensation intact but kill the awfulness, and that seems like a partial version of his goal already. So why isn't that the whole program?
- What replaces the aversive signal as a motivator. My understanding is that dopamine tracks wanting rather than liking, so if you keep the wanting and delete the badness, what stops a mind from just circling the same loop forever? I want him to answer this one in mechanism terms, not vibes.
- His "heritable gradients of bliss" idea and whether any of it is falsifiable. He predicts a dateable last unpleasant experience; what observation would tell him he'd lost?
- Wild animals, since they're the bulk of the suffering and fixing that means messing with ecosystems on a scale that would make conservationists faint. Does he bite that bullet or not?
- The people who say no thanks, and the related worry that wanting to eliminate suffering expresses a nasty judgment about the people who suffer.
- One on the coordination mess: if this is a commons, is it ever right for a single actor to just go ahead and do it alone?

No warm-up questions, no "what drew you to this." And if there's a spot where his actual view is something the rationalist audience gets wrong, give him room to correct us -- that tends to produce the best material.
