Retired field biologist here — thirty-odd years of beetles and far too many arguments about whether two populations were one species or two. Since I hung up the net I've been reading philosophy of science, and I've walked straight into something I can't sort out.

The species problem in my old field has been fought over since long before I started: are species real divisions in nature, or just boxes we draw for our own convenience, or (as some philosophers say) is a species not a kind at all but an individual — a particular lineage with a history, more like a person than a class of people? And then there's the wider fight about natural kinds in general, whether the sciences carve nature at its joints. I've now read a good deal of both, and here's my trouble. The biology isn't in dispute. Everyone agrees gene flow is patchy, that reproductive isolation comes in degrees, that some groups interbreed at the edges. In my own genus we had two "species" that never meet in the field but crossed happily in a lab, which was supposed to settle something and settled nothing. Everyone agrees on the cases. And yet the camps just restate themselves at each other. Nobody gives an inch.

Worse, when I go from one literature to the other the arguements don't line up. In one place the question seems to be whether our words succeed in tracking real divisions. In another it's whether there's any mind-independent classification at all. In another it's whether a category supports induction and explanation. In another it's whether a category is held together by an underlying mechanism — some people say a kind is just a cluster of properties kept together by a causal process, no essence required — and then in yet another place it's whether the members share some hidden nature that makes them what they are. I honestly can't tell whether I'm watching one argument wearing three or four different costumes, or three or four different arguments that happen to share a vocabulary.

So what kind of disagreement is this, really? My own hunch — and I'd like you to tell me if it's too cheap an answer — is that a lot of it is verbal: "kind" is being asked to do different jobs in different mouths, and once you say which job you care about, most of the heat goes out of it. But I don't trust that, because something does seem to be left over that isn't just words: whether nature has any joints of its own or whether all the cutting is ours. If that's the real disagreement, is it the kind of thing anyone could ever settle, or is it bedrock?

What I want from you is a diagnosis, not a survey of who said what. Is this verbal, substantive, or two or three questions under one heading? Which parts are really about the world and which are about what we want the concept to do for us? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here — a distinction, a case that forces one side to give ground, or should I just accept that this one runs forever? I'm not after a reading list, I want to understand what I've been watching.
