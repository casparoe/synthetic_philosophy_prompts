I'm putting together a short position paper for a graduate seminar on Wittgenstein's later remarks on certainty, and before I commit to a line I want a consistency check on the package I've assembled. Here is where I've landed. (1) The propositions I stand fast on — that the world has existed for a long time, that there are other people with inner lives, that my memory is broadly reliable, that the words I use mean roughly what I take them to mean — are not things I know and not things I could justify. They are exempt from doubt because everything else I do, including all of my doubting and justifying, already presupposes them. (2) They are nonetheless not arbitrary. Which propositions end up playing that role is answerable to the whole fabric of practice: to what I was trained into, to what everyone around me does without thinking, to what would happen if I stopped. A hinge is not a blind postulate, and I am not a voluntarist about which ones I have. (3) I am not a relativist about hinges. When two communities hold incompatible hinges, that is not automatically a draw; sometimes one side is in the grip of something false and the other is not, even though neither can produce an argument against the other that doesn't beg the question. (4) Hinges can be rationally criticized and rationally given up. People do revise them — through conversion, through a scientific upheaval, through a long slow argument — and when it goes well the change is warranted rather than merely caused. (5) Talk of hinges as things I believe is a projection from the ordinary cases; really they are a way of acting, a stance built into how I go on, not a propositional attitude I could take up or set down. (6) Moore's claim to know, holding up a hand, that there is a hand here is out of order — though not because the hand isn't there.

The case that got me to this package, and that I think the whole thing has to accommodate: someone systematically gaslit over years by a person she trusts, until she can no longer trust her own memory of ordinary events. I want to say that what she has lost is not evidence — no amount of evidence would repair it, and evidence is not what was taken from her — that what has happened is that a hinge has been loosened from the outside, and that she has been wronged, which seems to require that she was right about the events and that her gaslighter was wrong.

So audit this. Which of (1) through (6) actually pull against one another, and which only look as though they do? Rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give and what that would cost me elsewhere in the package — I would rather have the cheapest repair and its price than a survey. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what I would be surrendering by making it. One more thing: this is a literature in which readings get held with far more confidence than the texts support, so keep separate what is genuinely settled about how these claims relate from what is contested among people who know the material, and do not hand me a contested reading as though it were the consensus. I don't need citations or a reading list — I want the argument, not the scholarship.
