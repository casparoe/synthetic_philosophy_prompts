I'm in a decision theory seminar and there's a move that keeps coming up that I've started seeing everywhere, and I want to know if it has a name.

The setup is this. Two agents who can never talk to each other and can't affect each other at all still end up cooperating, because they reason the same way: each one figures 'if I defect, that tells me the other one defected too, so defecting is bad news for me' — so they both cooperate even though neither can do anything to the other. That version I can live with, because the two of them are basically the same algorithm running twice.

But then my prof took it further, to what she calls large worlds — worlds with loads of agents, most of which you'll never meet, can't reach, and cant even pick out. And the claim was that the case for cooperating gets *stronger* there, not weaker, because now there's this whole crowd of agents out there reasoning the way you are, so you're all effectively on the same team. The bit she kept stressing is that it's not about what my choice causes — my choice causes nothing over there — it's about what my choice tells me about them.

That's the part I can't get past. Everywhere else in life, cooperation gets harder and less worthwhile the more distant and anonymous the other party is: I lend my flatmate money, I don't lend a stranger on the bus. Here it's the reverse — the less I know about the other agents and the smaller my footprint on them, the more the argument is supposed to bind me. And the whole thing runs on 'they reason like me', but the more agents you add, the less plausible that premise gets, yet adding agents is supposed to strengthen the conclusion rather than weaken it. And since nothing I do reaches them and nothing they do reaches me, it doesnt feel like a relation between us at all anymore — it feels like a rule about my own consistency that I've been talked into.

Is there a name for this? I don't want just a label — I want to know what the thing actually is, how it differs from the nearest stuff (the 'if everyone did that' intuition, prisoner's dilemma variants, whatever else is adjacent), and where its edges are. If there's no established name, coin one and tell me what it rules out.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up.
