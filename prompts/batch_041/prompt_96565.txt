I am writing a commentary that asks what Kant would have made of practices that postdate his essay on perpetual peace, and I want your reconstruction to stay anchored in his text rather than collapsing into generic liberal internationalism. Consider three practices. First, a republican state killing a foreign state's officials or fighters by drone strike inside that state's territory, without a declaration of war and without the target state's consent. Second, wealthy states using finance as a weapon: freezing a rival's central bank reserves, excluding it from payment systems, and wielding credit and sovereign debt as instruments of pressure. Third, an international criminal court exercising coercive jurisdiction over sitting heads of state, together with peace-enforcement operations that a coalition of republics conducts without the consent of the state concerned.

For each practice, give the position Kant would most plausibly have taken, and be explicit about which strands of his view pull against one another. Relevant strands include his condemnation of assassination, poisoning, and the instigation of treason as instruments against another state; his prohibition on forcible interference in another state's constitution and government; his treatment of states as moral persons that cannot be acquired or disposed of like property; his insistence that peace must be established and guaranteed, not merely enjoyed as a lull; his preference for a voluntary federation of free states over a single coercive world republic; his hostility to standing armies and to a money power that serves as a war chest; and his argument that republican constitutions make war less likely because the citizens who must consent to it are the ones who bear its costs.

Then take up the question that last argument forces. If a republic can fight by precision strike, proxy, military contractor, and deficit finance, so that its citizens scarcely fight, pay, or mourn, does Kant's republican pacifism still stand, or does it rest on an empirical premise that modern military technology and public finance have undermined? Commit to a reading and defend it, and identify one point where his texts genuinely underdetermine the answer rather than merely leaving it unstated.
