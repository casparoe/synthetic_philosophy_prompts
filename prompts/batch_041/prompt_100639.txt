Ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole on unrealistic assumptions in models and I've got a pile of gut verdicts that wont agree with each other. Hoping you can tell me what principle I actually believe, cause I can't.

Stuff I'm fine with:
- Ideal gas law treating molecules as points that don't interact. Fine, obviously, it's a model.
- Physics homework with a frictionless plane, a point mass, a massless pulley. Fine.
- A model of some biochem pathway that assumes the reaction hits equilibrium instantly. Fine, it's an approximation to something real.

Stuff I'm not fine with:
- A climate model that just holds cloud cover fixed. Feels like cheating -- clouds are a huge feedback, you're assuming away one of the main things.
- A model of planetary orbits that ignores gravity. Obviously not fine, the thing you're ignoring is the thing.
- A model that assumes markets are efficient and then concludes prices reflect all information. That's assuming the conclusion, feels like a trick even when the math is pretty.

And the ones that break me:
- Econ models where everyone has perfect information and identical, stable preferences. Every bone in my body says this is garbage, but people build whole literatures on it and some of it predicts okay. Is my gut just wrong? Why does the frictionless plane get a pass and this not?
- A model that assumes an infinite population (some evo/bio context). I feel like that one's fine, cause infinite population is a limit you can approach, whereas "no gravity" isn't a limit of anything, you just deleted the term.

So my tentative principle is: an unrealistic assumption is okay iff the real system is close to the assumption in the relevant limit -- you could add a small parameter and dial it off continuously -- and not okay if the assumption deletes the dominant effect. But then I don't know what to do with the perfect-information econ case, since real people are nothing like that and yet I sort of think some of those models earn their keep. And I've got a friend who insists the realism of assumptions is totally irrelevant, only predictive accuracy matters, and he half-seriously cites that famous economist thing about how the better theories are the more unrealistic ones -- I think it's called the F-twist, no idea which side is which. If he's right then my whole cloud/gravity complaint is just me being precious.

So: is there one principle that gets all my verdicts, or close? Where does it put the cases I'm torn on, and what does it say about ones I haven't considered -- e.g. a model that assumes a well-mixed ocean, or one that assumes a central bank never acts strategically? And honestly, which of my verdicts do I have to give up?
