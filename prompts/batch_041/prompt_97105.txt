Construct a thought experiment that isolates the disagreement between Dennett's intentional stance and Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality, without relying on the two-bitser or the Chinese room. The case should involve a physically possible, non-conscious, non-designed assemblage—for instance, a self-sustaining autocatalytic chemical network in a porous mineral environment—whose behavior is most predictively and explanatorily successful when described as representing a distal state of affairs, such as the location of a nutrient gradient, even though the system contains no symbols, no interpreter, no user, and no history of selection for tracking that state. The scenario must be given in one paragraph. Constraints: do not stipulate consciousness, understanding, or intrinsic aboutness; do not appeal to quantum indeterminacy, perfect predictors, or stipulated semantic properties; the intentional description must be earned by its predictive and explanatory payoff, not asserted; and the verdicts must be generated by the theories rather than built into the case. After the scenario, do the following. First, state exactly what Dennett's intentional stance licenses you to say about the system, and explain why predictive success is or is not sufficient for attributing intentional states. Second, state what Searle must say given his original-versus-derived distinction, and explain why the absence of a conscious source matters. Third, identify the single feature of the case that does the work in separating the two verdicts. Fourth, say what happens to each verdict if you add, one at a time, (a) a human designer who uses the system as a detector, (b) a long history of natural selection that shaped the system to track the relevant state, and (c) a conscious observer who interprets its states as representations. Fifth, explain how a defender of Searle would try to block the case, and whether that response begs the question against Dennett. Conclude by saying what the case is for: what it shows about whether intentionality can be original without a mind. If the constraints cannot all be jointly satisfied, say which one must give and why.
