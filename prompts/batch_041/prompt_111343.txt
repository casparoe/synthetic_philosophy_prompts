I'm a platform engineer with no philosophy background, and I keep running into coordination problems that turn out to be about what other people know that I know. Two recurring shapes: two teams each want a different tie-break rule for conflicting writes, where either rule is fine so long as both teams run the same one; and protocols where service A will only proceed if it is sure B will also proceed, so we bolt on confirmations — B acknowledges, A acknowledges the acknowledgement, and so on. These feel unlike ordinary bugs, but I lack the vocabulary for why, and I don't want to re-derive it at every design review.

Please write a procedure I can run before shipping a coordination rule or a multi-party protocol, grounded in whatever the philosophy of convention and common knowledge actually establishes — the account on which a regularity is self-sustaining only when each party prefers to conform given that the others do, and the distinction between mere mutual knowledge and the stronger condition that I know that you know that I know, indefinitely. I want the steps in order, what counts as passing each one, where the procedure returns a no, and where it stops and hands the matter to a person because it concerns preferences or authority rather than engineering. Include the question I suspect I am missing: whether a given rule needs the relevant fact to be merely mutual knowledge or genuinely common knowledge, and what to do when only finite confirmation handshakes are available, since any message can fail to arrive and each confirmation appears to require one of its own. If the honest answer is that a specification document cannot manufacture the thing we need and it takes a focal point or a designated authority instead, say so.

Then run the procedure on two cases: (1) two teams adopting a shared tie-break rule, where each is content to match the other's choice but neither cares which is chosen; and (2) the acknowledge-the-acknowledgement protocol above, where each side's payoff depends on the other's certainty. Tell me plainly if the procedure says the second cannot be made to work by adding messages, and what it directs me to do instead. The procedure will be used by engineers without formal training, signed off by our architecture lead, so it needs to be runnable in a thirty-minute review. Finish by telling me what this procedure cannot decide.
