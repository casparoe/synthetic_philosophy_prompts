I have arrived at a view about individual contributions to collective harms — climate, meat, voting — and I want it attacked rather than defended. The view, which I will call the participation view, is this: my individual act (my flight, my burger, my ballot) can be wrong even when it makes no marginal difference to any harm, and even when the world would have been indistinguishable had I acted otherwise. What makes it wrong is that I am a participant in a collective practice that causes serious harm, where participation means acting on the same practical commitment as the other contributors, so that the harm is attributable to us jointly and each of us bears a share. This is my own formulation; do not spend effort matching it to a named position in the literature.

Do not hand me the inefficacy objection itself — that is the thing the view exists to answer. I want the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with the empirical ones (thresholds, tipping points, norm cascades, spillover, the actual expected value of a single vote) kept distinct from the conceptual ones.

Here is the case I find hardest, and I want it treated at length: a person lives in a society where animal agriculture will be abolished within a decade no matter what anyone does now, because of a technology already under development; the animals she eats are already bred and would be killed regardless, so her consumption changes nothing about anyone's suffering or about the timing of abolition. Does the participation view still condemn her? If so, on what ground that is not merely expressive or symbolic? If not, what was participation doing that marginal contribution could not have done?

Beyond that: where does the view overgenerate (activities I am intuitively not required to give up, or people it wrongly condemns), and where does it undergenerate? Is its normative work actually being done by something expressive, which would make it a dressed-up version of a view it claims to improve on? How does it account for degree, if marginal contribution does no work? And can it be stated so that some counterexample would count against it at all, or can anything be absorbed by redrawing what counts as the practice? Tell me which of these the view's own defenders would concede and which they would dismiss. If you conclude the view is well insulated, say plainly whether that is a defect or simply what a view of this kind should look like, and end with the single consideration that should move me most, and why.

Write in American English.
