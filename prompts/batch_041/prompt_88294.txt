I host a small podcast out of our department and next week's guest is a philosopher of physics I've admired for a while. She's spent the last few years defending a version of eternalism -- the block universe -- that she gets to not from the usual "special relativity kills the present" hand-wave but from a dilemma she aims squarely at presentists. Her line, as I understand it, is that the Rietdijk-Putnam style argument is almost always stated badly (as being about "what's real for a given observer," which everyone already agrees is frame-dependent) and that the real force of it is a fork: either the word "now" picks out an objective slice of the world, in which case relativity gives you no unique slice and you have to posit a privileged foliation that does no work anywhere in the physics; or "now" is indexical, like "here," in which case it can't do the metaphysical work presentism needs it to do. Either way presentism is in trouble and the block view wins more or less by default. I think this is clever but I suspect there are soft spots, and honestly I'd rather the interview be a real interrogation than a friendly chat.

Could you draft me eight or nine interview questions that go after the actual content of the view rather than letting her restate it, and for each one a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give? I want the sequence to press in roughly this order: first the first horn of the dilemma -- why exactly is a privileged foliation a worse posit than any other brute fact, and couldn't the presentist just own it and move on; then the awkward fact that a foliated presentism and the block view look empirically equivalent, which makes me wonder what entitles her to say one of them is "in trouble" at all; then the standard complaint that this whole family of arguments only establishes the relativity of simultaneity and not the equal reality of other times, and whether her reframing really escapes that or just relabels it; then the two-walkers / Andromeda sort of case where observers in relative motion disagree about whether some distant event has already happened; then the point that Minkowski spacetime still hands you a non-arbitrary causal structure -- past and future light cones, a distinguished "here-now" -- which looks like it quietly gives the presentist a resource she hasn't addressed; then the felt passage of time, since if she's going to help herself to "passage" as a primitive she may have just posited the same kind of un-physical extra she mocked the presentist for positing; and finally one closing question about whether any future physics (a preferred frame turning up somewhere in quantum gravity, say) would actually count for her as evidence against her position, or whether her view is the kind that cant be disconfirmed by anything. Keep the questions pointed enough that a good guest would have to work for it, and dont bother with warm-up stuff about how she got into the field.
