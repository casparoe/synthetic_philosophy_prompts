I spent thirty-five years as a geophysicist and only got into philosophy after I retired, so I'm still learning the vocabulary. There's a thing I keep noticing and I don't know if it has a name. In my old field, if you held a belief and the data kept coming in against it, you'd eventually give it up -- maybe not right away, maybe you'd tweak the auxiliary assumptions first, but there was a direction of pressure. Now I've been reading Quine, and he says our beliefs form a web, with the logical and mathematical stuff near the middle and the observational stuff at the edges, and that nothing is immune from revision in principle. Fine. But then I read what he says about moral values, and it looks like values are in the web somewhere, but they never get revised by experience. You can have a moral view that's caused you nothing but trouble, that predicts (in some loose sense) that you'll feel a certain way about a case, and when the feeling doesn't come, you don't drop the value -- you find a story about why the case is special. Take someone who thinks it's always wrong to lie: they lie to spare a friend's feelings, feel fine about it, and instead of dropping the rule they say that wasn't really a lie, it was a kindness. And yet nobody wants to say the value is analytic or unrevisable in principle. So here's my question: is there a name for this exact spot in the logical geography -- a seperate class of commitments that are part of the web, that are revisable in principle, but that experience never actually dislodges? I know about the fact/value distinction and I know about non-cognitivism, but neither seems to be quite it, because Quine clearly thinks values are real psychological states and not just expressions. And is there a name for his positive view -- that ethics is more like engineering than like science, where you take the ends as given and reason about means? I keep wanting to call it something like moral engineering but I don't know if that's already taken. I'd be grateful for the nearest concepts and how they differ from what I'm describing, not just a label.
