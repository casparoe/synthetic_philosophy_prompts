I'm a philosophy professor and I keep getting dragged into the same fight in our department's AI ethics reading group. Last week we were discussing whether a large language model, or a whole simulated brain running on a cluster, could count as a moral patient, and a colleague cut off a grad student's question about whether it actually feels like anything with the line 'same computation, same moral status.' He said it like it settled the matter, and a few people nodded, but I think it's doing alot of different jobs at once and I can't get him to seperate them. I want a menu of the precise claims that one-liner could be making, from the almost trivial to the genuinely radical, and for each one a verdict: platitude, substantive metaphysical or empirical thesis, conceptual claim, or just false. I also want to know which of those readings would actually support what he used it for — shutting down the question about felt experience — and which would leave that question exactly where it was.

Some background that might matter: I'm especially interested in the 'attribution' half of this. When we say a system 'computes' something, are we describing a fact about it or imposing a description on it? If the same computation can be implemented in neurons, silicon, or a room full of people with paper, does that sameness attach to the moral status of the computation itself, to the system that runs it, or only to our abstract model? And what happens to the slogan once you take the implementation relation seriously — does it turn into a claim about multiple realizability, a claim about moral status being functional, a claim that any system with the right causal profile is sentient, or just a promissory note? Please lay the readings out as a numbered list I can bring to the next meeting, with a short note on what each reading would commit my colleague to and whether any of them is the one people usually mean when they say this kind of thing. Don't worry about citing the literature; I know the neighborhood, I just need the disambiguation and the verdicts. Also, if there's a reading under which the slogan is true but boring, and a reading under which it's interesting but unsupported, say so explicitly, because that's the move I think he's making.
