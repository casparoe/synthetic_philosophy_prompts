ok so im a junior in high school and i got way too into typefaces after i had to make a poster for the school play, and now i cant stop thinking about this. my philosophy teacher said i could bring it to philosophy club if i could make it into an actual argument, so here goes.

my view: there is no such thing as the essence of a letter. when people ask "what makes every a an a" (basically the question that old Letter Spirit project was about, the one where a program has to design a whole alphabet in one consistent style, and the hard part turns out to be knowing when a shape has stopped being an a) i think the answer is: nothing positive. an a isnt a shape, its a slot. its the thing that contrasts with b, d, e, o, p, q and so on inside a learned system of distinctions. so a-ness is purely relational, differential, like how in a language a phoneme is just whatever contrasts with the other phonemes. no positive content, all contrast. corollary: two shapes can be the same letter while sharing almost no geometry (double storey a vs the single storey a in something like Futura), and two shapes can be nearly identical and still be different letters (Cyrillic а is drawn the same as Latin a but its not our letter, its theirs, different system, different slot). i actually think thats a point in my favor.

what i want from you: act like a philosopher of language who also actually knows type design and some cognitive science of reading, and be straight with me. dont just recite the usual family resemblance / prototype theory stuff that everybody says about this, i've already heard it.

first, brainstorm. list every seperate way someone could push back on the relational view, including weird ones from outside philosophy (reading research, calligraphy, little kids learning to write, how OCR and font recognition work, non alphabetic writing systems, fingerspelling in sign language if thats even relevant).

then rank them by how much they should actually move me, from fatal to merely annoying. for each one say what it would have to show, and whether the relationalist can just absorb it by tweaking the view, and if the tweak makes the view worse, say so.

some things im already worried about and want you to deal with head on:

- if a is just "the slot that isnt the other slots", then is my view even falsifiable? is there any observation that would make me definately say ok, letters really do have essences? if the answer is no, tell me straight, and tell me whether thats a fatal problem or just the normal cost of a structuralist view.
- the stroke order thing. calligraphers care about ductus, the order and direction you actually draw the strokes in, and in cursive / joined up handwriting letters change shape a lot depending on their neighbors. does that mean a letter is really defined by how you make it, like a motor program, rather than how it looks? and if so does my view survive or does it just quietly turn into a different view?
- why do some a's look more like a's than others? if some instances are better examples of the letter than others, isnt that positive content sneaking back in?
- what should i, as the defender of this view, concede actually counts against it? not what critics say, what i should admit.

also: what does my view predict that the rival view (letters have a shared core shape, a skeleton, and style is just the flesh on top) does not predict? and is there anything the skeleton view explains that mine cant?

be careful and go slow with this, i'd rather have a real answer than a fast one. at the end give me the one line version of the strongest objection and the one line version of my best reply, so i can actually use this at philosophy club.
