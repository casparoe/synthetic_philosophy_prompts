I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of science, embedded in an interpretability lab, and I'm trying to predict how my supervisor will rule on a new case from her track record. Please act as an expert in the philosophy of science and in the methodology of interpretability, brainstorm the candidate principles before you commit to anything, and then reason step by step. I'm less interested in the verdict itself than in the rule she's running, the rival rules that fit her record just as well, and which case would tell them apart.

Her question is always the same: when are we entitled to say what a system's ontology is — what entities or features it genuinely represents or posits — given that we never have unlimited information about it? Her verdicts so far:

1. A network we trained ourselves: full weights, we can ablate or patch any unit and watch the downstream effect, and the unit-to-feature mapping is uncontested. She says yes, we have identified its ontology.

2. A commercial model reachable only through an API: we may query it as much as we like, but we see no weights or activations. She says no — we can describe its dispositions, but not attribute representations. (She adds that the freedom to query doesn't help, since what would need manipulating is the model's insides, not its inputs.)

3. A deployed system whose internals are sealed: all we have is a fixed stream of its past inputs and outputs, and we cannot even query it. She says no ontology here either.

4. Two formulations of the same physical theory that make identical predictions but assign different fundamental entities, where nothing we could ever measure would separate them. She says there is no fact of the matter and we should not attribute entities.

The new case: we possess the model's full weights, but its features are superposed — packed into overlapping directions so that no probe, linear or otherwise, isolates a single feature, and every intervention we can make moves a whole bundle of them at once. On top of that, there are two feature vocabularies that predict and control the model's behaviour equally well, with no principled tiebreaker between them. Will she say we have identified its ontology?

Please (a) state the candidate rules her verdicts are consistent with — I think at least three are live — and what each predicts here; (b) give your best guess at her verdict and defend it; (c) name a further case that would separate whichever rules agree on this one; and (d) say which of the rules you would endorse yourself.
