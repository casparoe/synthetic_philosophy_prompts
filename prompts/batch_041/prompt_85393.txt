I am assembling a research agenda for a small group — four people, three years, with funding — working on dialetheism and paraconsistent logic. I do not want a survey of the field, a taxonomy of positions, or a reading list; I want a prioritized and defended program.

To keep the agenda off the paths everyone reaches for first, we have decided to organize the whole program around a single constraint, proposed by a colleague and referred to here as the conservativeness requirement:

(CR) A contradiction is admissible as true only if the theory that contains it is a conservative extension of its classical fragment. That is, in whatever the classical fragment can express, the theory must prove nothing new: the contradiction may be visible "at the top," in the theory's treatment of truth, sethood, or vagueness, but it must not license additional theorems stated in the fragment's vocabulary. A dialetheist set theory, for example, may contain a true contradiction about the Russell set while proving no new arithmetical or finite-combinatorial sentences.

Take (CR) as the group's working criterion, and assume we are committed to some paraconsistent logic that rejects at least disjunctive syllogism. Under that constraint, which open questions in dialetheism and paraconsistent logic most deserve the group's three years, and in what order?

I care about the reasoning far more than the list. For each question you put forward: explain why it is upstream of the others — which other questions would be settled, reframed, or made moot if it were answered; how tractable it is with the proof-theoretic and model-theoretic tools that already exist, as opposed to tools that would first have to be invented; how neglected it is; and what argument or result over the next couple of years would move it up or down the ordering. Where two questions pull against each other — for instance, where satisfying one appears to render another unanswerable — say so explicitly and tell me which one you would sacrifice, and why.

I want three matters confronted directly, whether or not they end up near the top of your ordering. First, whether (CR) can be satisfied at all for the contradictions the dialetheist most wants, namely those generated by the semantic paradoxes, or whether it quietly excludes exactly the cases that motivated the view in the first place. Second, what "the classical fragment" of a dialetheic theory is supposed to be, and whether that notion can be made precise without circularity. Third, whether (CR) leaves any true contradictions that do real theoretical work, or whether it reduces dialetheism to a view with no live instances.

Do not give me citations, titles, dates, or a bibliography; I am not asking what has been written, but what should be worked on. Keep the response under roughly 1,200 words, and end with a final line of the form \answer{(1) ...; (2) ...; (3) ...} giving your ordering as three short labels.
