I've been going back and forth with a friend about the old Marxist idea that the economy is what really explains everything else — law, religion, schooling, what art gets made, the whole superstructure sitting on the economic base. There's a shape of argument I keep running into in these conversations and I dont know if it has a name. I'd rather have the concept than the label, honestly, so bear with the long description.

The shape: some institution gets explained by a benefit it supposedly confers on the economic order. Schools drill obedience because capital needs a docile workforce. Then I point out that the same schools are being starved by the same capital because they aren't producing the right skills — fine, so the real function was credentialling, or social sorting. A religion that says the world is an illusion and you shouldn't want anything: that pacifies people. A religion that says work hard and save: that makes good workers. A religion that says all souls are equal and charging interest is a sin: that does something for capital too, we just haven't thought of what yet. A tenants' rights law is read as the state stabilising the workforce; if it fails to pass, that shows the state is in the landlords pocket. Same explanation either way, and the only evidence ever produced is that the institution is compatible with what capital needs — never that capital's needs did anything to bring it about.

When I ask how the benefit is supposed to be doing the explaining — who registers it, what keeps the institution in place because of it, what happens to institutions that dont confer it — I'm told that asking for that is individualist, and that the whole point is that nobody has to notice. Which might be fair. But then I dont know what kind of claim I'm being offered.

So: is there a name for this? I know biologists and sociologists explain things by what they do, and that someone has defended that as a respectable form of explanation, and I know the usual complaint that it needs a feedback loop or else it's just a story. What I think I'm looking at is narrower: a function explanation where the function is never fixed in advance so any effect will do, and where the claim slides between the economy making this happen and the economy merely allowing or needing it. Do those two things have separate names? Is there a real difference between explaining something by the benefit it confers and explaining it by the constraint it has to satisfy, and does the second one dodge the mechanism objection? And what would the strong version of the base-explains-the-rest claim have to look like to be testable at all? If nothing established fits, coin something, but tell me what it excludes — I'm after the carving, not the word.
