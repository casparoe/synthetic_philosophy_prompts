Since my brother's involuntary detention two years ago I have been reading Szasz, and in a few weeks I am leading a session on his claim that mental illness is a myth for a group of psychiatric nursing students who at present know it only as a provocative slogan; what I need from you is a plan for explaining it, not the explanation itself. Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with nothing before or after them: (1) the order in which the components must be introduced, and the dependency that forces that order; (2) the one case that should carry the session, and what it must have, and lack, in order to do so; (3) the misconceptions the students will arrive with or develop, and the precise point in the sequence where each should be headed off; (4) the single closing question that would show whether they have grasped Szasz's position rather than its shock value; and (5) what I should openly refuse to cover at this level — ending with how confident you are in the plan and which step you are least sure of. Anchor the session in his criterion for calling something a disease rather than in the familiar denial that mental illness exists, and let the students see both that the same criterion would demote much of what we comfortably call physical illness and that his epistemic claim and his political conclusion about involuntary treatment can be pulled apart.
