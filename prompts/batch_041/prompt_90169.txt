My friend in book club gave me his take on Hofstadter and the self, and I want to know how much of it is fair. So when you die you don't really die; as long as enough of your pattern is still running in enough brains, your I persists, just distributed. And if we ever built a perfect digital copy of you, that copy would literally be you, and the original and the copy would be one person spread over two machines. He says this is why Hofstadter treats personal identity as a matter of degree and denies any sharp line between you and your friends. I kind of buy it because it sounds like the sort of thing Hofstadter would say, but it also sounds too tidy.
