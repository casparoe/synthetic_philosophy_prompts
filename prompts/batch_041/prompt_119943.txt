I've been reading the rationalist-adjacent literature for years, and I want you to argue that a distinction it leans on is drawn wrong. Take two stipulations. A system is ascription universal relative to an observer when its explicit beliefs contain as much information about the system as any other way of ascribing beliefs to it that the observer can construct; and one way of ascribing beliefs epistemically dominates another when the first lets you reconstruct and assess the second without loss. Argue that ascription universality so defined is the wrong target for transparency and that epistemic dominance is the right one: what we should want is not parity between a system's stated beliefs and the ascriptions currently available to us, but its stated beliefs dominating every ascription we could make. Carry the argument with a case: a system whose outputs faithfully report everything recoverable about it by any means we have, while the optimization that shapes those outputs lives in a scaffold it does not represent, such as a retrieval corpus or a training curriculum. Argue that this system satisfies the informational definition vacuously and fails the dominance definition, and that this is precisely the failure we should have been tracking; then meet the strongest objection, that dominance is unverifiable for any bounded system because it quantifies over all other ways of ascribing beliefs, so that an unverifiable property cannot be what we mean by transparency.
