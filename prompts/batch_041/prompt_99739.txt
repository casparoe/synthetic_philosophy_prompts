My ancient-history reading group has been going in circles for months on the same three fights: is there a real war behind the Trojan poems, was Arthur anybody, did the Exodus happen at all. The minimalists point at the silence of the dirt and the genre of the texts and say theres just no there there; the maximalists say you're demanding a standard of proof no ancient event could meet and that absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence. Nobody budges. My hunch is we're not even having one argument — that part of it is verbal, that "X is historical" quietly means two different things (one side hears "an event occurred," the other hears "we can warrant the claim that an event occurred"), so that the two sides would agree on every fact and still fight. Or maybe it's a real clash of evidentiary standards neither side can justify from outside its own starting point, or two different questions wearing one name. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using what each side really argues, and tell me what would even count as progress here — a distinction, a shared standard, or just conceding the question is ill-posed. American English, please.
