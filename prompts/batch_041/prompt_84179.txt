I want a credence, not a survey, on one claim about the alignment problem. Fix the narrow reading first: intent alignment is the problem of making a system reliably try to do what its operator intends; value alignment is the broader problem of its doing what is actually worth doing. The claim is: if every advanced AI system built over the next century were perfectly intent-aligned with its operator, most of the expected harm from advanced AI would still occur. My own credence is roughly 0.35. Give me yours, decomposed rather than asserted. I want at least a number for (a) whether "intent" can be read literally, as the instruction the operator actually gives, rather than counterfactually, as what the operator would endorse under full information; on the counterfactual reading the narrow problem swallows the broad one and the claim comes out trivially false, so the whole thing turns on this. (b) Conditional on the literal reading, the share of the residual harm you would assign to operators whose own intentions are bad or self-defeating, to aggregation across many operators with conflicting intentions, to coordination and racing dynamics among intent-aligned systems, and to our residual uncertainty about what is worth wanting. (c) How much of that residual is structural — a property of any set of optimizing agents with divergent ends — rather than a contingent feature of human institutions. Then combine the terms into a single credence, tell me which one the number is most sensitive to, and say what argument or evidence would move it most.
