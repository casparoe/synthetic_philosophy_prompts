I'm an undergraduate taking a course on decision theory, and next week I'm running a 75-minute session for our seminar group of about fifteen students on Pascal's mugging. I'd like them to play something before the argument is named, so that they walk into it themselves rather than recognising it. What I have in mind is a betting game with token bankrolls and no real money: a stranger makes a series of offers, each a small stake for a small chance at a much larger payoff, culminating in one offer with a probability on the order of one in a trillion and a payoff that is enormous but strictly finite. Please work the design out in detail — the round-by-round stakes, probabilities and payoffs, the rules, and how a class resolves a win that is overwhelmingly unlikely to occur, or what happens if it never does. I want two things built in. First, keep every payoff finite: I don't want them to escape by declaring infinities incoherent. Second, arrange the rules so that any reason a student gives for refusing the final offer would, applied to an earlier round they accepted, have cost them tokens; the debrief can then turn on which principle they are willing to give up, and where. I'd also like a twist in the last round — a rival offer that a consistent expected-value maximiser would have to accept as well, so that the strategy that was winning becomes unstable at exactly the point where it matters. The debrief should map the rounds onto the argument and its standard replies, and be candid about which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who work on it, whether the right response is to bound utilities, to reject the setup, or to accept the conclusion. Tell me too what the activity cannot reproduce and I will have to supply myself, and what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it. Format the whole thing as a JSON object with the fields: title, materials, rounds (an array, each entry with stake, probability, payoff and purpose), rules, timeline, debrief_map, failure_modes, limits_of_the_activity, settled_vs_contested.
