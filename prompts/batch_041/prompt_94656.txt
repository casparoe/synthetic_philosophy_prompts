I've been rereading Hofstadter's discussion of frame, outer, and inner messages in Gödel, Escher, Bach with a reading group, and we've gotten stuck on something that I suspect is more than exegetical. The tripartite scheme feels clarifying right up until you ask where the frame message lives. Take the record example: the grooves carry the music, but they also carry, in their spacing and their very format, instructions for the player; and the fact that this is a record rather than a frisbee is, on Hofstadter's telling, something like a message too. My friend says this is a ladder you kick away — the frame message is a message only in a derivative sense, and the real work is done by the decoder's competence, which is not itself a message. I say that can't be right, because the decoder's competence has to be structured, has to be isomorphic in some sense to the thing decoded, so the frame/outer/inner stratification is a real feature of any meaning-bearing system rather than a projection. We've been going back and forth for two sessions and neither of us can land a clean objection to the other.

What I'd like from you is a dialogue between these two positions, written as a genuine back-and-forth rather than a pair of monologues. Let one character defend the view that the frame/outer/inner distinction marks real joints in the message-plus-decoder system, and the other defend the view that it is a pragmatic overlay — that frame message names a disposition to treat something as a message rather than a message, and that the regress (every outer message is an inner message relative to some further frame) is only stopped by non-message-based capacities: embodiment, causal coupling, social training, or sheer use. Both should be competent; neither should be a strawman; each should press the other's actual weak points — for instance, whether the regress is vicious or benign, whether isomorphism can do the work the first character wants without already presupposing a decoder, and what happens to the account when the decoder is itself another message-bearing system (a cell, a compiler, a reader). I'm a longtime reader of this kind of blog, so I'll be annoyed if the second character wins by calling the distinction pragmatic and leaving it at that; make the pragmatist pay for the move. Aim for something in the range of 900–1200 words, with the characters responding to each other's specific formulations rather than talking past each other. Don't have them converge on a tidy synthesis unless the argument actually earns it.
