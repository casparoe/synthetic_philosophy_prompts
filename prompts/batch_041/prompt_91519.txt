I am designing a ninety-minute workshop for a mixed group of literary translators and engineers who work on constrained text generation. The topic is translation under formal constraint and the preservation of form, and I want to avoid the usual examples. I need a teaching plan, not an explanation of the topic itself. My carrying example will be an original acrostic quatrain whose first letters spell the name of the poem's addressee, where that name is also the only proper noun in the poem, so the acrostic and the poem's reference are entangled; the target language has no single-word equivalent for two of the line-ending words and a different default word order. Plan how I should teach the central idea that a formal constraint can be part of what a text means, and that preserving it may require sacrificing literal sense. Give me the order in which the ideas must be introduced and why each depends on the previous one; what the acrostic example must have and must lack for it to carry the whole workshop; the misconceptions I should expect, such as that form is ornament, that a faithful translation is a literal one, and that constraints are either preserved or abandoned, and exactly where in the sequence to head each one off; one question or exercise at the end that would show whether they have understood the distinction between preserving a constraint and merely transcribing it; and what I should explicitly leave out at this level.
