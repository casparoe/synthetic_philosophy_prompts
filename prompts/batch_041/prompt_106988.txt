Construct a thought experiment to specification in the philosophy of biology, concerning the disagreement between etiological (selected-effects) accounts of biological function and causal-role (systemic) accounts. For the purposes of this exercise, fix the three positions as follows, and treat them as the only positions in play. The etiological account: an effect is a function of a trait just in case the trait was selected for producing that effect. The causal-role account: an effect is a function of a trait just in case it is a causally relevant contribution the trait makes to a capacity of the containing system. The maintenance account, which I am stipulating for this exercise and which is not to be attributed to anyone: an effect is a function of a trait just in case that effect's current contribution to fitness is what explains the trait's persistence under selection, regardless of what the trait was originally selected for.

Specifications for the case:

1. The scenario must be realistic and biological: a real or plainly plausible organism and trait, described in a single paragraph. No fictional biology, no stipulated verdicts, no thought-experiment machinery such as demons, observers or stipulated selection histories that the scenario simply asserts rather than earns.
2. The trait must genuinely have been selected for one of its effects. The case therefore may not be a vestige case, a spandrel case, or any other case whose interest lies in whether the trait has a function at all. The dispute must be about which effect is the function, not about whether there is one.
3. The trait must produce at least two distinct effects that both occur currently, and both of which currently make a difference to survival or reproduction. This is essential: the causal-role theorist must not be able to pick out a single candidate for free by noting that only one effect is actually doing anything.
4. One of the two effects is the selected effect; the other is a by-product of it that has since become independently fitness-enhancing. The case must be constructed so that the selected effect is, in the causal-role theorist's terms, the more derivative or mediated contribution to the system's capacity, while the by-product is the more immediate one. This is the feature intended to make the causal-role verdict come apart from the etiological verdict on the question of which effect is the function.
5. The case must not involve a heart, a pumping organ, or the noise such an organ makes; and must not reuse any other illustration that is standard in this literature. Invent the case.
6. The case should be one in which pre-theoretic intuition favours the causal-role verdict, so that the etiological theorist is required to bite a bullet rather than being vindicated by common sense.
7. The case is for an argument that the etiological/causal-role dispute is underdescribed when it is framed as a dispute about whether by-products have functions. What I want from the case is a demonstration that the two accounts can agree that a trait has a function while disagreeing about which of two live effects that function is.

After the scenario, provide the following, clearly separated:

(a) The verdict of each of the three accounts — etiological, causal-role, maintenance — on the question of which effect is the function, with the reasoning for each spelled out in terms of that account's own criterion.
(b) An explicit statement of which accounts disagree with which, and a check that at least two of the three disagree about which effect is the function rather than merely about whether there is one.
(c) Identification of the single feature of the case that does the work, and an account of why removing it collapses the case into agreement, together with which two accounts then agree and on what.
(d) The minimal alteration that would flip the disagreement, and what the flipped case would look like.
(e) For each account that comes out badly, how a defender of that account would attack the case, and whether the attack succeeds. Be honest about which of the three accounts is left most exposed, and say why.

Write the whole answer in British English, and use continuous prose except where the labelled sections above require otherwise. Do not cite or allude to any published literature; work only with the definitions supplied here.
