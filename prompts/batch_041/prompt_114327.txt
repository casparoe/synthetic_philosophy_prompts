I'm building a set of exercises on reference and meaning, and for this one I want you to build me a case to order instead of reaching for the usual Twin Earth stuff.

Here's the spec. I want a scenario with two groups of ordinary speakers using the same common noun, where a descriptivist theory of reference (the sense of the term is a cluster of descriptions the speakers associate with it) and a causal-historical chain theory (content is fixed by the chain of transmission back to an initial naming, with the referent itself part of what gets said) both come out saying the two uses pick out exactly the same things — same extension, no dispute there — but the two theories split on whether the two groups are saying the same thing when they use it. And I want the split in one particular direction: descriptivism says same content, chain theory says different content. Don't just assert that at the end, make the case generate it.

Constraints, and I'll be strict:
- realistic setting, nothing sci-fi. No XYZ, no hidden microphysical essences, no brains in vats, no teleportation.
- the term should be an ordinary common noun for something you could point at or handle — a fish, a tool, a dish, a local weather thing, whatever fits the story.
- the two groups should be as close as possible in what's going on in their heads: same beliefs about the stuff, same descriptions they'd give if you asked them. The difference must not be traceable to them thinking different things.
- the histories by which each group came to have the word have to be genuinely seperate — no borrowing from a common source — and yet the extension lining up has to make sense within the story rather than being stipulated.
- say what the case is for, because that fixes what counts as cheating. If the verdicts are cooked into the setup rather than falling out of the theories, the case fails.

Then, after the scenario: for each theory, give its verdict on the extension, on the content of the term, on whether the two uses say the same thing, and on whether a translation between them is correct. Then change one thing — add a single chain of contact, so that one group borrowed the word from the other — and say what happens to each theory's verdicts and whether the divergence collapses. Finish with which feature of the case is doing the work, and how a defender of the chain theory (the one that comes off worse, on my reading) would try to defuse it.

Format: the scenario in prose, 200 words max. Then a table with columns exactly Question | Descriptivist verdict | Chain-theory verdict | Why, and one row each for: extension; content of the term; same thing said; translation correct?; verdicts after the contact is added. Then a closing paragraph, 120 words max, on the working feature and the defender's move. British spelling, and no citations or reading lists — just the case and the reasoning, alot of which I'd rather see worked out than gestured at.
