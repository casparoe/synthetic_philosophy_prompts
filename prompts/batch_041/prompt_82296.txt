I've been reading rationalist blogs for years and I've been in alot of threads like this. Someone drops a clean arguement, everyone nods, then someone finds the equivocation. This one has me stumped. A friend in a Discord about AI debate calls it the verification-budget argument:

(1) Any debate has a judge with finite time and compute to check claims.
(2) Debaters can make claims that are cheap to state but expensive for the judge to verify.
(3) A dishonest debater can flood the judge with plausible false claims, and the judge can't know which are worth checking without checking them.
(4) The honest debater must either spend the judge's budget checking them or let them stand.
(5) Once the budget is gone, the judge is deciding on the unverified remainder.
(6) So the dishonest debater can always win by burning the judge's verification budget.
(7) Therefore debate selects for obfuscation over truth.
(8) So debate is not a real solution to scalable oversight.

I think this proves too much — it would also mean no court, no peer review, no adversarial collaboration can work — but I can't find the bad step. I'd like to keep (1) and (2) if possible. Where does it go wrong, and what does rejecting the bad step cost? Does anything change if the judge can penalize unverified claims, or if the debaters have to commit to a prediction market?
