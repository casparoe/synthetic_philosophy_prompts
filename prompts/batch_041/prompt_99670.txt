Until about a year ago I would have said that Bentham's writings on sexual morality were a scholarly curiosity — a set of manuscripts that only reached print long after they were written. Then a friend of mine was charged under an old statute that everyone had assumed was a dead letter, and the question of how a society should treat sexual conduct stopped being a seminar topic for me. Rereading those manuscripts, I have become convinced that Bentham's approach is the most promising foundation available. He treats the pleasure of sex as a genuine good; he argues that consensual acts between adults produce pleasure and do no real harm; and he concludes that the true mischief lies in the law itself — the suffering of punishment, the costs of enforcement, and the room it gives for arbitrary and oppressive application — so that the hostility the conduct attracts is a case of what he called antipathy rather than a reason. I want to spend the next few years reconstructing and extending this into something usable, and I cannot tell which questions need answering first.

Suppose I have a funded year and a book to write, and that the book should both get Bentham right and be useful to people arguing for reform now. Which open questions in this project matter most, and in what order should I take them up? Among the candidates I can see: how much of the case rests on his particular hedonistic psychology rather than a leaner harm principle; whether the majority's disgust is a disutility the framework has to count, and whether counting it makes the framework self-defeating; what to do about the fact that sexual preferences and aversions are themselves socially shaped; how to draw and defend the boundaries of consent — age, incapacity, animals — that Bentham left largely implicit; and whether the view can support anything beyond decriminalization, such as protection, recognition, or positive provision. I would rather you not treat everything as equally important, and I am not asking for a reading list. Rank the questions, say which are upstream of which, which are tractable for a philosopher working alone and which need empirical collaborators, which are neglected, and what would move a question up or down your ranking.
