ok so my metaphysics seminar is wrapping up a unit on grounding and fundamentality next week, and instead of summarizing the debate i want to close the last session with a poem. can you write me one in the voice of a priority monist — someone who thinks the cosmos as a whole is the one fundamental thing, and that everything else, the parts included, is grounded in it rather than the parts grounding the whole.

form: blank verse, no rhyme, 16-22 lines, and give me one line that keeps coming back, a refrain. it should be the line that keeps reasserting that the whole is prior, so the repetition is doing the work — everything in the poem keeps getting referred back to the one thing.

stuff it has to get right, because my students will absolutely check:

- the parts still exist, theyre just derivative. dont let it drift into existence monism, where only the whole is real and the parts simply arent there. thats a different view and it would be wrong for this poem.
- grounded-in is not caused-by. the whole isnt a first cause sitting earlier in time; theres no "before" when the parts were lying around ungrounded. the priority is explanatory — in-virtue-of — not temporal.
- fundamental doesnt mean smallest. here the base is the biggest thing there is, not some particle, and the poem should make that feel natural rather than like a paradox.

then let it turn: if every part is grounded in the whole, what grounds the whole? end on that, unanswered, but make the question land as a real cost of the view and not just a rhetorical flourish. someone who knows the grounding literature should be able to name the position from the poem alone, and someone who doesnt should still think its a good poem and not a lecture in verse. dont name any philosophers in it.
