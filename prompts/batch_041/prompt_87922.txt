my GEB reading group has its last session next week and i want to close with a poem that actually instantiates the frame / outer / inner message distinction instead of just name-dropping it. so: a sonnet (14 lines, meter optional) where the inner message — the plain sense of the words read straight through — is someone warmly agreeing to help a friend move house, and it has to be completely convincing on its own. the outer message has to be carried by the form alone, so that someone who cant read a word of english could still pull it out: let the acrostic down the left edge spell the real message, which is that they have no intention of showing up, and let the rhyme scheme quietly pull in the same direction (falling into neat couplets exactly where the voice sounds smoothest, or something like that — your call). the frame message goes in the title plus a one-line dedication underneath: the bit that says "this is a coded message, here's how to decode me," and i'd like it to also be a little bit wrong about what kind of poem this is, since a frame that misdescribes its own contents is the interesting case. someone who knows the book should be able to point at each layer and say what its doing, and it should still be a poem someone would actually want to read.
