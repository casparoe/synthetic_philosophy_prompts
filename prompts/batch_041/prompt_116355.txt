build the list around the cases i actually care about, roughly:

- the easy one: a trait or behavior thats there because it did a job better than the alternatives. eye, dam, whatever. any account presumably has to say the rationale is really there and not just projected.

- a meaner one: something whose "reason" is real but that nothing ever selected for — a byproduct that happens to do something useful, or a structure that got co-opted into a new job. does the rationale come free with the function, or does it need a selection history behind it? if the answer is "it depends," tell me depends on what.

- the one that keeps me up: cases where the reason seems to live entirely in my description of the thing and not in the thing — a river carving a channel that "drains the valley," a boulder that "blocks the path." i want a principled line between a free-floating rationale and me just projecting purpose onto a rock. and i dont want "it's a matter of degree" unless you pay for it.

- the as-if boundary: a thermostat, a plant leaning to the light, a bit of code that minimizes a loss function. rationale or only mechanism? what has to be true for the rationale to be genuinely there rather than merely as-if?
