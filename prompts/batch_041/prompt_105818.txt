Background you need (don't worry about citing anything, just use it): the Pasadena game is a fair coin flipped until the first heads. If heads first comes up on toss n, the payoff is (-1)^(n-1) * 2^n / n dollars. The expected value series is conditionally convergent — it sums to ln 2 in the natural order by n, but because it's not absolutely convergent, you can rearrange the terms to get any real number you like (Riemann rearrangement). The Altadena game is the same but pays an extra $1 on top, so it strictly dominates Pasadena (always exactly $1 more), yet some rearrangements make Pasadena's "expected value" arbitrarily large. There's also the question of what "expected value" even means when the sum isn't absolutely convergent.
