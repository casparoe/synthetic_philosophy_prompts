In a comparative philosophy seminar, a classmate kept returning to the White Horse Dialogue's one-liner, 'a white horse is not a horse,' to insist that the School of Names was doing serious philosophy rather than clever wordplay — that the sentence states a real thesis and not just a quibble about words. I could not tell whether he meant something about terms, about kinds and their members, or about what a thing is. Lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making, ordered from the trivial to the radical, and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive thesis a careful reader could defend, or simply false. Then say which reading people most likely have in mind when they invoke the line against the charge of sophistry, and which reading would actually earn that verdict. Give it to me as a menu of readings with verdicts, not a single interpretation.
