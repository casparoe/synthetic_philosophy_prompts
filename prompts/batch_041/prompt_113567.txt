I am a philosopher of mind and of biology, and in a fortnight I am presenting a paper at a two-hour departmental colloquium whose audience I know well enough to describe precisely. The room is mostly philosophy of mind, but not of the armchair variety: there are two teleosemantically inclined people who read Millikan closely and who treat any talk of a reason in nature as either a covert appeal to representation or a metaphor that has outlived its usefulness; a philosopher of biology who works on function and adaptation and who will want to know what my account adds to the standard etiological story; a visiting metaethicist with Kantian sympathies who will press on whether a rationale that nobody can be answerable to is a reason at all rather than a redescription of selection; a cognitive ethologist friendly to insect cognition, who will object that I am quietly assuming the very absence of representation I need to establish; and four or five graduate students who will ask what is at stake. My thesis is this. Free-floating rationales — the reasons that explain why a mechanism does what it does even though nothing in the system represents those reasons — are usually treated as a binary matter: either a rationale is present and unrepresented, or it is not present at all. I want to replace that with a graded notion. A rationale is free-floating to the degree that the mechanism tracks the reason across perturbations, and it is fully free only when the tracking is achieved with no internal state that covaries with the reason. That lets me pull apart two things I claim the literature runs together, namely the rationale that explains why a trait is maintained in a lineage and the rationale that would explain a token performance; most putative cases are only of the first sort, and the genuinely interesting ones, which I call orphaned rationales, are those in which a mechanism selected for reason-tracking under ancestral conditions now produces the same behaviour where it answers to no reason at all. My central case is a cleaning mutualism in which the service provider behaves differently depending on whether an observer is present.
