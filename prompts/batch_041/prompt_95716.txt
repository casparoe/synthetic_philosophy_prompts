I run a small reading group that keeps getting stuck on the dust-specks-vs-torture thing — you know the setup, one person tortured for fifty years, or some ludicrous number of people each getting a single speck of dust in the eye — and we keep discovering that people who say the same thing about the original case disagree wildly the moment you change the details. So I want to build a proper sorting instrument: a decision tree (an actual tree, not a personality quiz) that takes someone from a couple of starting commitments to a named position on when, if ever, a pile of tiny harms outweighs one big one.

Build it from the logical space, not from a list of schools. Every branch node should be a question about a concrete case or a structural claim, phrased so someone can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to. No node may be 'are you a utilitarian?' — ask them what they'd do with the lever.

I want the cases to do the work, and I'd like you to use these ones, because they're the ones that actually split us:

- the original: N people get one imperceptible speck, versus one person tortured for fifty years, with N dialable from 1 all the way up to an absurd number;
- the same many-tiny-harms, except all of them land on one person over a long life instead of being spread across people (this is the one that tells us whether someone is really objecting to aggregation or just to interpersonal trade-offs);
- a probabilistic version, where instead of each of the N getting a speck, each has a one-in-N chance of getting it, so we can see whether they're thinking ex ante or ex post;
- a version where the tiny harms are barely perceptible but not imperceptible, to probe whether they think sub-threshold stuff counts as a harm at all;
- a version where the many small harms fall on animals, or on people who don't exist yet;
- a mild-but-certain harm to one person (a bad headache, say) instead of torture, so we can find where people's answers flip, if they flip at all;
- and a policy version, where the choice is between two rules rather than between two outcomes.

Leaves should be named positions (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends the combination), each with one line on that position's most awkward commitment. I'd like at least these to appear somewhere: straight additive total conseqentialism, prioritarianism / diminishing marginal value, a lexical or threshold view on which no pile of minor harms can outweigh a major one, a deontological seperateness-of-persons view, the sorites-ish view that there is a flip point but it's vague, and the view that the whole question is ill-posed or expressivist. If the tree can't separate two of these — I suspect the lexical view and the deontological view will come out identical on every case — say so rather than pretending.

Also mark the combinations of branch answers that no position occupies, and say whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. And flag any place where the tree only works because of a case I didn't give you.

Keep it to roughly eight to ten questions and eight to twelve leaves. Don't give me citations or a reading list, I just need the instrument and the notes. Oh, and if you think the whole thing collapses into the repugnant conclusion, mention where — but don't turn it into an essay.
