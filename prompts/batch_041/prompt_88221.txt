I'm presenting at our formal epistemology reading group next week and need a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis: the problem of old evidence is not really a problem about probability values at all, and the standard fixes — the counterfactual-prior move (reason as if you had never learned the datum), the uncertain-evidence move (treat the old datum as something short of certain and update Jeffrey-style), and the "you always knew it but only now see its bearing" move — each mislocate the phenomenon, because each treats it as a question about how much credence the evidence gets. I argue instead that Bayesian confirmation has no representation of whether a proposition is inferentially available — whether it may serve as a premise in reasoning toward a hypothesis — as distinct from how confident one is in it, and that known evidence can confirm a new theory by becoming available in a new way while its credence stays at 1. I sketch a formal "re-licensing" operator meant to do work the standard fixes cannot.

The room is mostly card-carrying Bayesians who work on confirmation and will defend conditionalization to the death, plus one statistician who thinks all of this reduces to conditioning on the right sigma-algebra, plus one historian-and-philosopher of science who reads Kuhn and thinks the whole framework cannot capture scientific change. Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this room — not the generic ones a metaphysics seminar would raise — ordered by how much damage they would do, each phrased the way that person would put it, a two-minute reply to each, a note on where my reply is weakest, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
