My friend's way of putting it, which I suspect is either quite clever or a total howler, is that people conflate two entirely different gaps: the "gap-as-semantics," where ought-claims just aren't the kind of thing that can be true or false, and the "gap-as-division-of-labour," where ought-claims are perfectly truth-apt but no pile of factual premises will ever entail one, so the two enquiries can never be collapsed into each other. His claim is that Quine's holism kills the first and is simply silent on the second, and that once you see that, the live Quinean question becomes whether moral norms get revised the way logic and physics get revised — pragmatically, when the fabric stops working — in which case ethics comes out as a branch of engineering rather than a branch of knowledge.
