hey — I run a little online puzzle club (eight of us, mostly people who did some maths at uni and then wandered off into other jobs, so they know what a limit is, but none of them have read any philosophy). Next month is my turn to write the handout and I've picked supertasks — infinitely many steps crammed into a finite stretch of time. I want the last item to be a proper exam-style question I can mark out of 20, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, because I'm the only one who's read anything about this and I dont want to be winging it when I tell eight stubborn people they've got it wrong.

The thing I want it to be about is the lamp. You know the one: lamp starts off, the switch gets flipped at the half-second mark, flipped again at three quarters, again at seven eighths, and so on forever, so there's infinitely many flips before the one-second mark — is the lamp on or off at one second? Everywhere I look online the answer is some version of "the setup doesn't say, so there's no fact of the matter, it's just an under-described setup". Which is fine as far as it goes, but it feels like a shrug and it bugs me, so I built a second lamp to test it.

Lamp B. Same flip times as before (1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ...), except instead of an on/off switch its got a dimmer. Off to start with, then at 1/2 it comes on at full brightness, at 3/4 it's at half brightness, at 7/8 a quarter, and so on — every flip halves it. What's the brightness at one second? And is it on or off?

A third thing that keeps coming up when we argue about this: my mate Dave says none of it matters because no real machine can flip a switch infinitely fast, so the whole puzzle is fake physics. I genuinely can't tell whether that's an answer or a dodge, and I don't want people to be able to hide behind it, so the question should make them deal with it.

So what I'm asking you for is: (1) the exam question itself — three parts, 20 marks total, doable in about an hour by people who know limits and haven't read any philosophy, and self-contained, i.e. it has to spell out both lamps for anyone who missed the last session, and it should make them commit on whether B is on or off and not just hand me a number; (2) a model answer, maybe 600 words; (3) a marking scheme with the marks attached, split into the specific moves an A answer has to make and the ones a B answer can get away with, plus a short list of the wrong answers I should expect and exactly what's wrong with each one — I'd rather have that list than just the model answer, since I'll be reading eight scripts and I need to catch the near-misses.

Two things I care about. First, I don't want a question someone can answer with "the description is underdetermined so there's no fact of the matter" and then sit back looking pleased — that may be true, but I have a feeling lamp B is different in a way that matters, and I want the question to force them to say whether it's different and why, instead of reciting the shrug. Second, keep it to the maths and the arguing: no names, no papers, no reading list, and obviously don't give the answer away inside the question itself. If it turns out lamp B is a can of worms with no clean answer, then say so honestly in the model answer, and let the marking scheme give credit to both sides as long as they've done the real work.
