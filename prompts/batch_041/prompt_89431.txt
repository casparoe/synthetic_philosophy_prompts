I teach a graduate seminar on scientific realism and theory choice, and the session on non-empirical confirmation reliably collapses into a shouting match. I would like to walk in with a considered tier list of my own, so please construct one for me.

Rank the following candidate grounds for raising one's credence in a theory in the absence of any new empirical evidence, from strongest to weakest. Read strength as evidential weight: how much a rational agent's credence in the theory ought to rise when this is the only consideration on offer. Use four tiers rather than a flat ordering, and say in a sentence or two what earns each item its place.

(1) No alternatives: the theory is the only worked-out candidate we have for the job, and no competitor has been developed to the point of comparison.
(2) Unexpected explanatory coherence: the theory turns out to resolve or dissolve problems that played no part in its construction.
(3) Unification: it brings a range of previously disconnected phenomena under a single formalism.
(4) Fecundity: it sustains a productive research programme, even if none of the results test it.
(5) Anthropic accommodation: it supplies a mechanism — a multiverse with varying parameters, say — that would explain a striking appearance of fine-tuning.
(6) Aesthetic and mathematical virtues: simplicity, elegance, naturalness.
(7) Expert convergence: those best placed to judge are confident in it.
(8) Multiple independent routes: the same structure can be reached from several mutually independent starting points.

Then three follow-ups, each answered separately. First, which pairs on the list are not independent of one another, in the sense that counting both as separate boosts would double-count a single consideration? Second, which item, if any, is doing pragmatic rather than evidential work — the sort of thing that can rationally keep a research programme alive while leaving credence untouched? Third, for any item whose force depends on the theory being the only live option, is that a genuine criterion of confirmation or an artefact of scarcity, and does your answer change if a rival is later developed?

Finally, apply the ranking. Give me a number between 0 and 1 for how confident a well-informed rational agent should be that string theory is on the right track, and say whether a multiverse should be treated as a prediction of a theory or as a post hoc rescue of it, explaining how the ranking you gave licenses that treatment.
