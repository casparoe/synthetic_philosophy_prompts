I am a proof assistant developer and type theorist. I know dependently typed programming, Martin-Löf type theory, inductive families, universes, definitional and propositional equality, normalization by evaluation, canonicity, and the Curry-Howard reading of propositions as types. I am currently extending a dependently typed kernel with a primitive construction-by-motion monad—an operation that takes a constructed figure and moves a distinguished point along a path, with the path represented as an inductive family—and I want to know whether Hobbes’s science of motion can be read as an early demand for something like this primitive, or whether that reading is an anachronism. I know almost nothing about Hobbes scholarship beyond the caricature that he tried to square the circle and lost a pamphlet war with John Wallis. I want you to restate Hobbes’s views on geometry—not his errors or his biography—in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report.

Treat at least these elements: geometry as a science of motion; the role of definitions and construction; the claim that geometry is demonstrable because we make its figures; the analogy with civil philosophy, where the commonwealth is also an artifact and so its principles can be known; his hostility to the algebra of his contemporaries; and the dispute with Wallis, insofar as it bears on what counts as a legitimate proof rather than on who was right about the circle. For each element, say what translates cleanly into the type-theoretic vocabulary, what translates only with distortion, and what has no expression at all. Be honest about the translation: do not silently identify Hobbes’s construction with a proof term, or his maker’s knowledge with canonicity, unless you can say exactly what is gained and lost. The residue is the interesting part. For each piece of residue, say whether it indicates a defect in the type-theoretic framework, a lack of clarity in Hobbes, or a genuine disagreement about the nature of knowledge. Finally, answer this: could one design a proof assistant that would satisfy Hobbes’s conception of geometrical knowledge, or would the features that make it a proof assistant—normalization, canonicity, definitional equality, the separation of kernel from elaboration—already commit it to a picture of knowledge Hobbes would reject? If the latter, identify the precise commitment and whether it is avoidable without giving up the proof assistant.
