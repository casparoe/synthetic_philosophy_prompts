Below is an argument about the standing to blame that a colleague and I have been unable to dislodge, though neither of us accepts its conclusion. I would like a diagnosis.

(1) To blame someone is to address a demand to them — to hold them answerable — and not merely to record a fact about them.
(2) One can address a demand to another only from a position of not being in breach of that demand oneself. A blamer who is in breach of it is a hypocrite, and hypocrisy deprives the blame of its standing.
(3) Whoever is in breach of any demand is, just in virtue of that, in breach of the demand not to be in breach of demands.
(4) Hence a single lapse places one in breach of the very demand that all blaming invokes.
(5) Every human being has lapsed.
(6) Therefore no one has standing to blame anyone for anything.
(7) But a victim surely has standing to hold her attacker to account, and a teacher surely has standing to correct a student in a matter where the teacher has himself erred. So (6) is false.

The colleague who assembled this says the only honest move is to abandon (2) and admit that hypocrisy is irrelevant to standing, which strikes me as giving up far too much; I would rather keep (2) and (7) and locate the fault elsewhere. Tell me which step should go, why it looked compelling, and what each of the main responses costs. In particular, I want to know whether the trouble lies in how the invoked demand is specified: the argument seems to need a reading on which any lapse matches any blamed fault, and I suspect the real work is done by an unexamined choice about how finely the demand is described — the same act under one description, a different act under another. If the chain is instead valid as it stands, tell me which premise is least costly to abandon and why its rivals are worse. Distinguish what the defender of lost standing must concede from what the critic must concede, and say whether the argument, if repaired, still reaches self-blame, blame of the dead, or blame directed at institutions — and whether that reach strengthens or weakens it.

End with a single line of the form: Verdict: <step number or label> — <one-clause reason>.
