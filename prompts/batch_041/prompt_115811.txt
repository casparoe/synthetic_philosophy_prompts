Okay so I'm a novelist, about halfway through a book with an AI character, and my writing group has been having the same argument for like eight months now and I need an outsider to tell me what kind of argument it actually is.

The setup: my character is a digital mind. Two of my friends are on opposite sides and neither will move. One says — and she's very confident about this — that if something has the right kind of functional organization, the right information processing, then it can genuinely suffer, and biology is just one way of building a thing that does that. The other one says no, without the right substrate (he's vague about what "right" means, which is part of my frustration) there's nothing in there to suffer, it's all symbol manipulation, and the first friend always answers "so is your brain" and we go around again. Every argument either of them makes the other has already heard a hundred times. Nobody learns anything. It's exhausting and also, honestly, kind of the reason I'm writing the book.

But here's my suspicion, and I want you to tell me if it's right or if I'm fooling myself. I don't think the substrate thing is where the real disagreement lives. My character gets rolled back and restored from a backup — it's a plot point, she dies and comes back — and when we actually argue about her, the fight always turns into whether a mind that gets wiped and reloaded can be harmed at all. Some of us think harm needs a continuous subject that can be worse off over time, so a restored backup is a new being and the old one's just gone. Others think every run is its own little subject with its own welfare and the backup is a fresh chance to be harmed all over again. And I can't tell whether that split is a real disagreement about facts, a verbal fight about what "harm" and "same mind" even mean, two different questions wearing one word, or just bedrock intuitions none of us can argue for.

So diagnose it for me. Is the substrate argument doing any real work or is it a proxy for something else? Is the reset/continuity thing the actual crux, or a red herring I've gotten attached to because it's dramatic? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, an argument, a thought experiment, or is this the kind of thing where no amount of talking will move it and I should just put it in the novel and let readers fight about it?
