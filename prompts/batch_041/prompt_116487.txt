For my final paper in a philosophy elective I have to read a work of fiction or film through a philosophical problem, and I have chosen Groundhog Day and the literature on transformative experience. The idea I want to work with, which comes from the philosopher L. A. Paul, is that some experiences are epistemically transformative, in that no description can tell you what they will be like and the only way to find out is to have one, and personally transformative, in that undergoing them reshapes your core preferences, so that the person you are now has no fixed standard of value by which to judge the person you would become. My thesis is that the film's time loop is a device for suspending precisely this difficulty: because Phil can live the same day over and over, he can rehearse outcomes that the theory says cannot be rehearsed, learn the piano and the ice sculptures, and try every version of his conversation with Rita, which means the comedy depends on a cheat, and the ending, the one choice he cannot rehearse, is the only genuinely transformative choice in the film; I also suspect the film dodges the hardest part of the problem by letting the transformation happen to him rather than having him choose it. Please develop this reading and then test it honestly rather than illustrating my thesis: which scenes show that repetition is giving him knowledge of the day rather than knowledge of what it is like to become someone else, where does the film show a change in him that is genuinely unchosen, and where does my thesis distort the film? I would like to close with what the film contributes to the discussion that Paul's examples do not, in particular whether it suggests that the difficulty of transformative choice is not really epistemic but a matter of the will, and with a candid account of the places where the film and the theory simply do not fit. I am aware that the standard reading of the film runs through Nietzsche's eternal recurrence or Camus on Sisyphus, and I would prefer not to write that essay, so please keep the focus on transformative experience and use the film's own details rather than treating it as an illustration of a theory.
