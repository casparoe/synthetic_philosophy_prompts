My seminar-mate keeps saying that asserting something is like making a promise. When you tell me the meeting is at 3, she says, you're putting yourself on the hook for it the same way you are when you say you'll be there at 3 — so the rule that governs assertion is really about you and your commitments, not about the facts. She uses this to argue that the norm of assertion is belief rather than truth, and that lying is closer to breaking your word than to getting something wrong. I'm not convinced but I can't say why. Audit the analogy: give me the mapping (what plays the promiser, the promisee, the content, the breach, being released from it), say where it holds and where it breaks — especially around who controls the content, whether anyone has to be there to receive it, and what happens when the person you told says "never mind" — flag which features of promising it quietly imports that asserting doesn't have, then give a definite verdict on whether her conclusion survives. If the analogy can't carry it, tell me which analogy does the job better. Dont just lay out the options and leave the call to me — take a side and defend it.
