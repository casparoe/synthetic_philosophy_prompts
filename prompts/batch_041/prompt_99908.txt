I'm assembling the questions for an episode of a small philosophy podcast. My guest defends a position on the standing to blame that I think is genuinely heterodox, and I want the interview to test it rather than tour the familiar debate. Her thesis, as she states it, is that there is no distinct normative status of standing to blame at all. Every case that gets filed under lost standing — the hypocrite, the tu quoque, the wrongdoer who blames his victim, the accomplice who blames his co-conspirator — is on her view really a case of the blame's content being false, its fit being off, or its utterance being impermissible for ordinary reasons; never a case of a blamer having forfeited a permission or a power she would otherwise have had. So the complaint that someone has no standing to say a thing is, for her, never a distinct objection but only shorthand for one of the familiar ones. She takes this to dissolve the debate rather than settle it.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that force her past that restatement. At minimum, include: one that presses the difference between a blame being impermissible and its being unfitting, and asks which of the two she thinks the hypocrisy cases actually turn on; one built around a fully apt, fully true instance of blame that the blamee nonetheless seems entitled to refuse to engage with, so that she has to say what, if anything, the blamee is owed; one on the reformed wrongdoer who blames a present offender for an act she herself committed years ago, where I want her to say whether her account can capture the intuition that something is still off; one on what the victim of a hypocrite's blame may legitimately demand, if not that the hypocrite be silent; and one on self-blame, where the standing idiom is hardest to eliminate. For each question, add the follow-up I should have ready, keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Close with a single question designed so that her usual deflationary move cannot be used twice in a row. No biographical or scene-setting questions, and please keep every question anchored to the content of the thesis rather than to who has said what about it.
