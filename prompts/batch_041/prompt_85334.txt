The problem of old evidence has been argued over for decades without convergence, and I want a diagnosis of the impasse rather than one more attempted solution. The case everyone accepts is a theory adopted on independent grounds that is then found to entail an observation already in hand and already certain — Mercury's perihelion is the stock example — yet the replies do not converge and the parties do not move.

The replies, compressed. One: the support is real but must be measured by what the agent's credence would have been had she not already known the fact, so a counterfactual credence does the work. Two: what is learned is not the fact but the fact that the hypothesis entails it, so the object of conditionalization is a logical discovery rather than an observation. Three: conditionalization was never meant to cover this case, since it governs how credences change when something is learned, and this is not learning. Four: confirmation is a support relation between propositions, not a story about anyone's evolving degrees of belief, and the problem exists only for the wrong kind of theory.

My hypothesis is that the impasse is not about the probability axioms but about two incompatible jobs assigned to one notion — modelling an actual agent's credence dynamics, and defining objective support — with each reply switching jobs halfway through, which is why it looks adequate to its author and evasive to its critic. A colleague's rival hypothesis is that the fight is really about idealization: the counterfactual reply needs well-defined counterfactual credences, the entailment reply needs beliefs already closed under logical consequence, and no real agent has either, so the question is which impossible idealization we may help ourselves to. I have also wondered whether the problem dissolves if the total evidence is treated as a constraint on the prior rather than as a sequence of learning events, so that old evidence is simply evidence that was in the constraints all along; he says that only renames it, since the machinery ranks hypotheses and never says what it is for one constraint to support a hypothesis.

Which kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, a clash of standards, a hidden dispute about idealization, or two questions under one heading? Which of the four replies survives on that diagnosis, and why do the others fail? Is my hypothesis or my colleague's closer to right, and what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, or a change of question?
