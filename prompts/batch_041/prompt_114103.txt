My epistemology reading group keeps reaching for the same three pictures whenever the regress comes up: foundationalism is a pyramid, a building that has to sit on solid ground; coherentism is a raft you rebuild plank by plank while still at sea, or a web where every strand holds the others up; infinitism is a chain, or turtles, that just keeps going. Last week someone used the raft to conclude that foundationalism is a dead end and justification has to be holistic, and then someone else used the pyramid to conclude that if the chain doesn't stop somehwere, nothing ever gets justified. I want an audit of the three pictures instead of another round of the argument: what actually maps onto what in each (what's the ground, what's the load, what's the plank, what's the link), where the picture breaks down, and what each one quietly imports that the theory it stands in for doesn't obviously have -- does the building assume there's a fixed load to be carried and one right place to stop, does the web assume mutual support can be had without vicious circularity, does the chain assume an endless series is even the kind of thing that could do the job? Then tell me whether those two conclusions survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether there's a better picture, or whether the honest answer is that no picture can carry this.
