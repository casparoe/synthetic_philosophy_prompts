The axiom of choice is independent of the standard axioms of set theory: it can be neither proved nor disproved from them. Does that not mean there is no fact of the matter as to whether a choice function exists for an arbitrary family of nonempty sets, and hence that accepting the axiom is merely a stipulation — one that some mathematicians make while others, who may accept only weaker choice principles, decline to make? If so, why are results that depend on it, such as the well-ordering of the reals or the existence of a basis for every vector space, presented as established mathematics rather than as consequences of a convention?
