Me and a friend keep going around about whether artifacts themselves can be political or whether it's only ever the people using them, and I want to know if there's a principle behind my gut calls, because right now I don't have one. My calls: The overpass built too low for buses to pass under, so the bus-riding crowd never makes it to the beach — that thing is political, even though nobody left a memo. A park bench with armrests split down the middle so you can't stretch out — I want to say that's just furniture, but my friend says it's the same move as the bridge and I can't tell her why it isn't. A coffee maker that only takes the brand's own pods — I'd call that a business thing, not political, even tho it locks you in. A building elevator that only opens to a resident's key fob, which the developer says is security but which also means every guest and delivery has to be buzzed, so the place gets a de facto doorman and higher rents — political? I definately lean yes but I'm shaky. A voting machine whose screen shows one race at a time so you forget the rest of the ballot — political, plainly, even though nothing in the hardware is partisan. A wheelchair ramp that meets code but snakes all the way around the building so you show up humiliated — political, and this is the one we actually fight about.

My half-baked theory is that something is political when it was made to push a particular group around, and merely inconvenient when it just has side effects. Test that against the list, tell me which call it forces me to drop, and say what it predicts for the case I keep flip-flopping on: a phone's default that shares your location, which almost nobody ever changes. Say how confident you are, and flag the point you're least sure of. American English please.
