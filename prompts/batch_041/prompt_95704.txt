Right, so I sat in on a cognitive science lecture and the bloke spent a good twenty minutes on the colour phi thing — you flash a dot in one spot in red, then flash a dot somewhere else in blue a fraction of a second later, and people swear they watched it travel across the screen and change colour on the way, and when you ask them afterwards they'll tell you the dot was already turning blue before the second one had even flashed, which is mad because it was plainly red at the start. His gloss on this was: the brain is like a totalitarian state that has to keep its story straight. Either it rewrites the archives after the fact, so the memory of seeing red gets quietly airbrushed out and replaced with a tidier version, or else it stages the whole performance from the outset, so the red was never actually delivered to you as red in the first place. From that he drew the big conclusion: the feeling that you're watching a little film play out in real time is a fiction, experience gets edited, and so there's no clean fact of the matter about *when* any of it happened to you.
