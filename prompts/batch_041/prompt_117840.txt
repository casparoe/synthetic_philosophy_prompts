I've been lurking in a couple transhumanist discords lately (don't ask — it's mostly people who read too much LessWrong plus one guy who actually works in a lab) and there's this whole cluster of people who call themselves 'abolitionists', not about slavery, about suffering. The pitch goes roughly: pain is a biological accident, evolution's way of herding an organism around, and now that gene editing and designer pharmacology and whatever comes after are coming online, there's no reason to keep it. So: abolish suffering. And the last great moral crusade was the abolition of slavery, so this is the next one; they talk about it the way we talk about slavery, that our descendants will look back at us like we look back at the 1700s. That framing is doing a lot of work for them, because it's what gets them from "this would be a nice thing to do someday" to "this is an urgent moral obligation and anyone dragging their feet is on the wrong side of history." A friend in the group thinks the analogy is carrying the entire argument and none of it is earned. I think its at least half-earned but I can't say where it slips.

Could you audit it for me? I want the mapping laid out explicitly — a little table is fine — what plays the role of the slaves, the slaveholders, the plantation, the emancipation, the Underground Railroad. Then where the mapping breaks, and especially which features of the slavery case are being quietly imported that have no counterpart on the other side. I keep feeling like something is off about the fact that in this version nobody is actually doing the suffering to anybody, but maybe that's a red herring. Then tell me which of the disanalogies matter for the conclusion they're actually using the analogy for (the urgency, the wrong-side-of-history bit) and which are just decoration, and whether the conclusion survives once the bad ones are stripped out. If it doesn't, what would a better analogy be. One more thing: when I bring up that pain is useful — the whole "it's a smoke alarm, you don't rip out the alarm, you fix the wiring" line — they say fine, keep the signal, just drop the awfulness, that's a technical problem. Is that a genuine escape hatch or does it just relocate the difficulty? Detail over brevity, please.
