I've read the popular treatments (peacock tails, conspicuous consumption, the labor-market story on education as a costly way of proving you were already good), but I have no technical background in this area, so I want judgment rather than a reading list.

Four things I've stumbled on that complicate the standard story, and that I'd like your ordering to take a position on:

- The claim that honest signaling doesn't actually require cost — that cheap signals can be a stable equilibrium when the parties' interests partly overlap — which makes me wonder how much of the peacock story is load-bearing and how much is an artifact of the examples everyone repeats.
- The idea economists call countersignaling: the highest-quality agents stop signaling, so the signal ends up marking the middle tier, and sending it becomes evidence of not being top-tier.
- The distinction between signals that can't be faked because they're causally tied to the underlying quality and signals that are merely expensive to fake, along with the suggestion I've seen that this distinction is a modeling convenience rather than a fact about the world.
- The gap between a signal and a mere cue — evidence that leaks out without being produced for an audience, and therefore can't be gamed by whoever knows what the audience wants.
