In my collective action seminar we have spent several weeks on the pattern our instructor calls the unilateralist's curse, and I have to submit a short position paper stating where I stand. The structure of the cases is this: a number of agents are each independently in a position to take some action, and the outcome turns on how many of them take it rather than on which one does. The seminar's stock example is a case in which it would be good if exactly one agent acted and bad if several did — each agent reasons that the action is worth taking and that it will probably be taken by someone else if not by them, and the upshot is that too many act. Our instructor also flagged a feature of such cases that I had not previously considered: the agents' decisions are correlated, so my deciding to act is itself evidence that others will act as well. Before I commit to a position in writing, I want to know whether the following set of commitments is coherent, since I have assembled them from rather different sources and I am no longer confident that they are meant to be held together.

1. The bad outcomes in these cases are structural. Every agent may be well informed, deliberate carefully, and act on the evidence available to them, and the group will still reliably produce the outcome that none of them wanted; the fault lies in the shape of the situation rather than in anyone's reasoning.
2. The remedy is a norm of conformity that each agent applies to their own case: act only if you would be content for every agent in your position to act likewise. I take this to be a genuine requirement of practical reason, not merely a useful convention.
3. An agent ought to decide on the balance of evidence as they themselves assess it; the bare fact that others have judged differently is not in itself a reason to revise, and treating it as one is a species of epistemic deference that I regard as defective.
4. Whether an action is the right one for me to perform does not depend on how many other people are performing it. I ought not to do what is wrong because others are doing it, and I ought not to refrain from doing what is right merely because others are doing it too.
5. Each of us is answerable for their own choices and not for the pattern those choices compose. Where an aggregate outcome was intended by no one, the participants are not, as individuals, to be blamed for it.

I would like an audit rather than a summary. Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each real tension is; for each one, what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me; and whether any single revision would resolve more than one of them.
