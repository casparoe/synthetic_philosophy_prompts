Okay so I've been using one of those AI autocomplete things in my email and my docs for about three months now, and something's happened to my head that I don't have words for, and I'm hoping there's already a name for it.

Here's what's bugging me. I type half a sentence and it offers me the rest, and maybe 40% of the time I take it — sometimes word for word, sometimes just the shape of it. Then I read the email back a day later and I honestly cannot tell you which sentences were mine. Not in a "who cares, it's an email" way. I mean I'll argue a position in a meeting that I apparently only ever met as a grey suggestion in a text box, and I'll argue it like I thought it up. Last week the thing was down for two hours and what I lost wasn't the ability to look things up — I lost the ability to finish a thought. I sat there with the first half of a sentence and no second half, and it wasn't like a word on the tip of my tongue, it was more like reaching for a hand that wasn't there.

The part that's really throwing me is that the thing isn't neutral. A notebook doesn't have opinions about what I should write next. This thing is trying to be fluent and plausible, and presumably trying to keep me typing, so what it feeds me isn't "whatever I would have said" — it's the most predictable version of what someone like me would say. So whatever is doing the thinking when I write these days is partly a system optimising for something that is not my point.

I know there's the old story about the guy who writes everything in a notebook and people say the notebook is sort of part of his mind, and I get why tools get absorbed into thinking. But that one always sounded like it was about a passive store, and this feels different in a way I can't put my finger on. It's also not quite the same as my partner being the one who holds our entire social calendar and me being useless without her — related, but that one doesn't creep me out.

So: does this have a name? And I don't mean a nickname, I mean is there a concept meant to cover exactly this — where the chunk of cognition sitting outside my skull is an active party with its own agenda, handing me the next thought already optimised for something that isn't mine? If the answer is "that's just the extended mind" or "that's just offloading", fine, but then explain why the standard versions don't seem to care that the outside part is *interested*, and what actually changes if you take that seriously. If there's no name, give me one and define it so I know what's in and what's out.

- **What the standard versions miss** — about 150 words on the "the outside bit has its own goals" point, and why that's not a minor detail.
- **My proposal** — either your best pick among the existing names or a new term, with a one- or two-sentence definition, then three test cases: one that clearly counts, one that clearly doesn't, and one that's genuinely borderline, a sentence each on why.
