Most people I read on free will treat Frankfurt cases as having sunk the principle that you have to be able to do otherwise to be responsible. Fine, let's grant that. So what's actually still open there, and which of it is worth someones time? Say a department has a two-year postdoc to spend and everyone in it already accepts the Frankfurt verdict -- rank the live questions and defend the order. Which are upstream of which, which are tractable from an armchair, which are dead ends dressed up as puzzles, and what would move one up or down the list? One thing I'd want them to chew on: the standard case has a backup mechanism that never fires, so the agent acts for his own reasons -- but what if the backup would have worked by changing what he wanted instead of forcing his hand? Does the Frankfurt verdict survive that switch, and does the answer tell you where the real action is? Skip the reading list; I want a defended agenda.
