Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a functionalist and a substrate chauvinist about whether mental states are multiply realizable. The functionalist holds that a mental state is fixed by its causal-functional role and so is realizable by any system that plays that role; the chauvinist holds that only systems sharing the right intrinsic causal powers — roughly, the relevant properties of biological nervous systems — can realize a state like pain, so functional equivalence is not enough. Both have agreed to treat as the live dividing line the difference between coarse-grained realization (matching inputs, outputs, and downstream functional consequences) and fine-grained realization (matching the causal powers of the realizing states themselves), and to use as a shared case a system that reproduces the coarse profile of pain through a wholly different mechanism. Give me the claims both can sign — about what the dispute is and is not about, what multiple realizability would require, and which parts are empirical; the precise statements of their disagreement, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes, at least one of which should be the disjunction worry — that if a single mental kind is realized by many different physical kinds, the mental kind is a disjunction, and disjunctions do not underwrite laws; and, for each crux, the case or argument both would count as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of their dispute is merely verbal, say which part. Format as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
