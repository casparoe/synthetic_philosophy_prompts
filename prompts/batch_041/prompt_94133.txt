Two people in my reading group have been going at it for months about probability in many-worlds and I want to force them to write something together instead of another shouting match. One is a proper Everettian who thinks the Born rule can actually be recovered from inside the theory — the decision-theoretic line, roughly Deutsch and Wallace, where you put constraints on what a rational agent can prefer across branches and the amplitudes fall out as the unique way to weight them. The other thinks that whole program is circular or question-begging: you can't get probability out of deterministic unitary evolution plus decoherence, and any decision theory that "recovers" the Born rule has quietly assumed something like it up front, or is just a normative claim about how you're supposed to bet.

Write the joint statement they could both put their names to. I want: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims — what the formalism actually says, what decoherence does and doesn't hand you, what a 'branch' is allowed to be, and what both agree still needs explaining; (2) the genuine disagreements, each written in wording the side that holds it would sign off on, including whether the fight is partly about what 'probability' is even supposed to mean here (objective chance vs a rational agent's credence); (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, a thought experiment, argument, or case that both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance (the branch-counting gambit, betting and gambling setups, self-locating-uncertainty scenarios, whatever they'll both accept as fair).

Two extra things I'd like them to pin down, becuase I suspect they're hiding the real fight. First, each side should write out what a satisfactory account of probability in MWI would even look like to them — what it would have to deliver — so we can see whether they're asking the same question or seperate ones. Second, have them say honestly whether any part of this is verbal: are they using 'probability' in the same sense, or is the Everettian using it as a decision-theoretic bookkeeping device while the skeptic means objective chance, in which case a chunk of the heat is fake. And don't let either side get away with a formulation the other could just reject as loaded — the interesting crux is probably whether the extra assumptions the decision-theoretic proof needs beyond plain unitary QM (whatever you call them: substitutability, equivalence, measurement neutrality, call it what you want) are themselves forced on a rational agent, or whether an Everettian agent could rationally just be a branch indifferentist who assigns no weights at all. No citations needed, just the argument.
