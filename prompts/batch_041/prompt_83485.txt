I've spent the term in a seminar on the methodological individualism–holism dispute in the social sciences, and I've arrived — tentatively — at a position I'd like you to try to break. It has two halves. The first is a supervenience commitment: I don't believe there can be a social fact without some individual-level basis, and I think any acceptable explanation of a social phenomenon must at least be traceable to mechanisms running through individuals' beliefs, reasons, and actions. The second is that I am not an eliminativist. I'm happy to grant that higher-level social regularities are real, that they can be indispensable for prediction, and even that they sometimes furnish the right description of the explanandum — Durkheimian rates, the macro→micro→macro diagram in which a macro state is explained by its effect on individual dispositions and then by aggregation back up to a macro outcome, the kind of structural explanation that cites the fact that a position is filled rather than who fills it, the microfoundations debates in economics. What I deny is that any of this gives holistic explanation autonomy in the final analysis: once the phenomenon has been traced down to individuals, the macro-level story is a bookkeeping convenience, however useful.

What would count against this? I don't want the textbook objections I've already read; I want the ones that should actually move me. In particular: which empirical or conceptual findings would threaten the traceability half rather than the supervenience half; whether the ecological-inference problem — where the aggregate pattern runs opposite to the individual-level pattern — is a genuine counterexample or merely a caution about inference; how hard the cases of unintended consequences, multiple realizability, and downward causation actually cut; and which of these a sympathetic defender like me should concede as genuine costs rather than wave away. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me what the view predicts that its rivals don't, and say plainly whether the position has been qualified into something unfalsifiable — and if it has, whether that is a defect for a view of this kind. I'd find it most convincing if you could show me a case where the individual-level facts are fixed and the social-level fact still varies, or the reverse. Please keep the whole answer under 800 words.
