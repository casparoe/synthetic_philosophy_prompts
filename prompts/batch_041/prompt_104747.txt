I chair the ethics advisory committee at our city's public-health department, and I need to circulate a short written decision before Thursday's meeting. Our behavioural insights team has spent the winter building what they call an "adaptive outreach" programme for a free preventive screening. Instead of sending everyone the same reminder, a model scores each resident on how likely they are to respond to a plain notice, and the programme matches the intensity of the intervention to that score: the most responsive residents get a single text message; the least responsive get a pre-booked appointment they must actively cancel, a follow-up call from a community health worker, and, for a subset flagged as very unlikely to attend, a home visit. The team's case is that this is the most defensible use of a fixed budget, since a uniform light-touch nudge wastes effort on people who need no help while failing the people who need the most. We approved something similar for flu vaccination two years ago and it ran without controversy, which is part of why the team is surprised by the resistance now.

Two committee members object that the scheme makes the heaviest paternalism fall precisely on the residents the model has judged least capable of managing their own affairs — and that those residents are disproportionately poor, elderly, and not fluent in the dominant language — so it both compounds disadvantage and quietly installs a two-tier citizenship of the will. A third member, a GP on the committee, says the real defect is elsewhere: the programme's letters disclose only that outreach is "tailored," never how or on what basis, so no resident can know which tier they are in or why, and the home visit arrives with no account of itself.

Please draft the opinion I'll read out on Thursday: a clear recommendation (approve, reject, or approve with named conditions and a timeline), the reasoning behind it, and an honest statement of the strongest case for whichever side I end up ruling against. I have a philosophy background, so don't walk me through the basics of paternalism and autonomy from scratch — assume I know the standard moves. What I do want is care about which parts of this debate are genuinely settled among people who work on it and which are live disagreements, rather than a contested position presented as the consensus.
