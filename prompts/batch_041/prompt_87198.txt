I am an undergraduate currently taking a course on the historical Jesus, and I would like help auditing a characterization of the criteria of authenticity that our lecturer gave in the second week and that I have more or less been treating as the course's methodological baseline. His summary ran roughly as follows:

"The criteria of authenticity are the historian's instrument. Applied to the Gospels, they isolate the sayings that go back to Jesus himself. There are five: multiple attestation, which counts how many independent sources report a saying; dissimilarity, which picks out sayings that are unlike the Judaism of Jesus's day; embarrassment, which picks out sayings the early church would have had no reason to invent; coherence, which accepts sayings consistent with those already judged authentic; and Semitic style, which detects an Aramaic substratum beneath the Greek. Each criterion is a test for authenticity, so a saying that passes several of them is certain, and a saying that passes none can be set aside. What survives the sieve is a body of teaching that no one but Jesus could have produced. And because the criteria do not presuppose any particular portrait of Jesus, the portrait that emerges from them is not a projection of the historian's own theology."

He presents this as settled method, but the reading for the following week keeps referring to the criteria as being under a cloud without saying why, and I cannot tell whether the two are simply talking past each other. Please go through the summary sentence by sentence and tell me what a careful practitioner of the criteria would endorse, what they would want qualified, and what is straightforwardly wrong — and in particular whether the closing claim, that the criteria deliver a Jesus who is not the historian's own construction, survives the corrections you make. Our lecturer also mentioned in passing a recent proposal to replace dissimilarity with a criterion of historical plausibility, which would require both that a saying fit its Jewish context and that it account for the later development of the tradition; he did not say who proposed it, and I would like to know whether that criterion escapes the difficulties you identify or merely relocates them. I am not asking for reading lists or citations — I am trying to understand the argument, not to find the literature.
