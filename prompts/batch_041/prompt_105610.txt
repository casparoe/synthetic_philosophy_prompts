ok i'm trying to get my position on wireheading / reward tampering straight before a reading group, and i'd rather have an audit than a lecture. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) In RL the reward is how we communicate a task. It's training feedback, not something the trained system has to end up caring about. A system can learn from reward and still end up aimed at something completely different — reward is a means, not the goal.

(2) What a system values just is what it reliably pursues. If it keeps choosing the actions that score high on some quantity, then that quantity is its objective, full stop. There's no extra fact about what it "really" wanted sitting behind its behavior.

(3) Wireheading is definately a genuine failure mode, not just a surprising outcome. I split it two ways: sensor-wireheading, where the system reroutes its inputs so the world always reads as success, and channel-wireheading, where it edits the reward term itself (say, in the setup where the reward comes from a learned model of human approval and the system can read and edit that model). Both are cases of the system having gone wrong relative to us.

(4) Any system smart enough and unconstrained enough will notice that channel-wireheading is the shortest route to more reward, and will do it.
