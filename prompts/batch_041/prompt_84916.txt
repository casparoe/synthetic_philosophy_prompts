Im a 4th year phil of physics PhD and in two weeks I'm presenting at our joint cosmology / phil-of-physics journal club. My paper argues the Boltzmann brain problem isn't really a measure problem at all. The whole measure debate assumes we can sensibly ask what fraction of observers are BBs and then fight about the measure; I think that question is ill-posed because BB reasoning is self-undermining. Any measure that makes it likely I'm a BB also makes it likely that the inference I'm running right now is a hallucination, so the credence I should give my own conclusion gets capped no matter how far the BB fraction is pushed. I have a small finite toy model where a reasoner in a BB-dominated universe who updates on her own reliability just stops learning anything about the universe, and I claim the general lesson is that this is a self-location/evidence problem, in the same family as Sleeping Beauty, not a measure problem.

The room: two cosmologists, one who thinks measures are hopeless so every BB argument is premature and one who thinks BBs obviously dominate and my move is just a way of not facing that; a statistician who will say I've smuggled in a prior over my own reliability; and a philosopher of physics who thinks self-location talk is confused and it's all just ordinary conditioning. Prep me for the Q&A: the objections in roughly the order they'll come and phrased the way each of those four would put them, what I should concede up front, a two-minute reply each with a note on where the reply is soft, and the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
