The essay question was this:

"In last week's lecture the following argument was set out under the name 'the Fossil Argument'.

(P1) Presentism is the thesis that only entities that exist now exist.
(P2) Every true proposition is made true by something that exists.
(P3) Some propositions about the past are true — for instance, that there were dinosaurs.
(P4) If a proposition is made true by something, that something must exist.
(C1) So if presentism is true, every true past-tensed proposition must be made true by some entity that exists now.
(P5) No entity that exists now can make a past-tensed proposition true, because all the present traces we find — fossils, rock strata, written records, memories — are exactly what we should expect to find if the universe had come into existence five minutes ago complete with those traces.
(C2) So presentism is false.

Assess the Fossil Argument. Identify which premise a presentist should reject, and explain why. Say also what an eternalist should say about the argument."

The answer I have to grade is the following.

"The Fossil Argument is meant to show that presentism cannot do justice to truths about the past. Its opening move seems to me correct. Presentists do hold that only present entities exist, and it is plainly true that there were dinosaurs. The load-bearing premise, as the argument itself makes clear, is the claim that whatever makes a proposition true must itself exist; and the argument then contends that no present entity can do this job, since every present trace is equally consistent with the universe having sprung into being five minutes ago furnished with that very trace. That is the familiar five-minute-world scenario, and I accept that no present fact can rule it out.

I do not think the argument succeeds, however. It leans on the assumption that every truth must have a truthmaker, and that is a substantial metaphysical commitment rather than a deliverable of logic; a presentist who gives it up escapes the argument, and not absurdly. Furthermore, the argument appears to treat the truthmaker principle as if it were a causal principle — as though a truth about the past had to be caused by something existing now — and this is a confusion, since truthmaking is a relation of necessitation rather than causation. Once the two are separated, the presentist can hold that a present object simply bears an irreducible past-directed property: a fossil has the property of having been produced by a living dinosaur, and it is this present property that makes the past-tensed proposition true. I take it that this is the standard eternalist response, and it shows that the Fossil Argument fails.

The argument therefore does not refute presentism. At most it shows that presentism is incompatible with a strong truthmaker principle, which is a real cost but not a refutation."
