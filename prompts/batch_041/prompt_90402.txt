I have been reading evolutionary biology as an outsider, and I keep encountering an argumentative pattern for which I would like a name. Consider a trait that is costly to the individual that bears it and beneficial to the group it belongs to — a bacterium that spends energy secreting a molecule its neighbours can also use, or a bird that gives a warning call and thereby draws attention to itself. Discussion of such traits always seems to proceed by re-describing the trait at a different level of organisation. One party argues that the apparent altruism dissolves once you attend to the fate of the genes involved, or to the fact that the individual organism is itself a coalition of partly conflicting interests, so that the trait turns out to be self-interested after all. Another party redraws the boundary in the other direction and shows, by the same kind of arithmetic, that the group is doing genuine explanatory work. What strikes me is that neither side is making a numerical mistake. When I do the accounting myself, I can reproduce the result of whichever side I take as the unit of accounting, and the totals come out right either way; the disagreement is not over the numbers but over which ledger is the real one. Each side then treats its own choice of unit as privileged — as the level at which selection is really occurring — while the other side's choice is demoted to a mere bookkeeping convenience, even though the same charge could be levelled in the opposite direction. The argument never terminates, and I suspect it cannot, because the disputants are not disagreeing about any fact in the world.

I have looked for a name for this. It is not simply the dispute between group selection and gene selection, since that names a family of substantive positions rather than the structure of the standoff. It is not ordinary reductionism, because the reduction is offered less as an explanation of the higher level than as a replacement for it, and the replacement can be run in either direction with equal success. Nor is it just the familiar point that the same event can be described truly in more than one vocabulary, because something stronger is being claimed: that one of the descriptions is the correct one, and that this correctness is supposed to follow from the accounting itself, without any independent argument for the choice of unit.

Is there an established term for this pattern — a disagreement about which level of organisation a process really operates at, in which each side settles the question by tacitly adopting the unit that makes its preferred level the active one, and in which no independent argument is given for that adoption? If there is a name, I want it, but more than the name I want the carving: what exactly the pattern is, what it is not, and where its edges lie. If there is no established name, please coin one and define it, and tell me which concepts a philosopher of biology would reach for first and why each of them falls short of what I have described.
