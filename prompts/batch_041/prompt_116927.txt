Ok so I write about science and ideas for a living, and this one's been rattling around my head all week. A philosopher friend sketched it for me over coffee and I honestly can't tell if it's a real paradox or a card trick. Here it is. (1) You can only be genuinely afraid of, or grieve over, something you believe is actually real or actually happening. (2) When I watch a horror movie or read a novel, I don't believe any of it is real or happening. (3) So I can't genuinely be afraid or grieve over any of it. (4) But I've sat in a cinema with my heart hammering, and last month I got misty over a three-minute student animation about a paper crane left out in the rain — a crane I knew perfectly well was invented, and I don't think I'm confabulating any of that. (5) So (1) is false. (6) But if (1) is false, if belief isn't required, then there's nothing left to separate my grief at a made-up death from my grief at a real one; they'd be the very same state with different labels stuck on. (7) And that's absurd, because it would mean I can be genuinely moved by literally anything I merely entertain — a proof, a shape, a random string of digits. (8) So (5) can't be right either. I can't find the seam; something sneaky seems to happen somewhere around (1), or around the hop from (5) to (6), but I can't name it. Where does it go wrong, and what does the repair cost? I'd really rather not toss (4).

Give it to me as: a one-line verdict up top; then "Where it slips" (roughly 150 words) naming the exact step and why it looked fine; then "The repair and the price" (roughly 150 words) on what each of the plausible fixes costs and which you'd take; then "What I keep" — a couple sentences on whether (4) survives.
