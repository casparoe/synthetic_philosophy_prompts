Bit of an odd one, but I've ended up on the citizens panel our council set up to sort out what we owe the families from the old riverside streets that got bulldozed in the sixties to put the ring road through. Twenty-odd households got moved up into the flats, some never got rehoused properly, the shops never came back, and even the council now agrees the whole thing was at best high-handed. They've asked us for a recommendation on compensation before the end of the financial year.

The snag is the note our legal officer put in the pack. His argument is that we can't pay these people for being worse off, because they wouldn't be here at all if it hadn't happened — the grandparents only met in the rehousing block, the parents married people they'd never have crossed paths with otherwise, so if the road had never gone through, none of the people now asking us for money would exist, and neither would their kids. You can't say someone is worse off than they would have been when the alternative is that they're nobody. Which is either a clever trick or it means the council did nothing wrong.

Half the panel says pay them anyway, obviously it was wrong, who cares what the lawyer thinks. The other half says we're a public body spending public money and we can't just wave away the fact that we can't point to a single person we harmed. One woman keeps saying its not about harm at all, they were wronged, and that's a different thing entirely — I honestly can't tell if that's a real distinction or a way of dodging the lawyer.

Can you draft the opinion I'm meant to circulate on Thursday? I need a clear yes or no on payments, roughly how much and spread over what period, and it has to actually meet the lawyer's argument head on instead of talking round it. If we're paying, say what exactly we're paying for. And if the answer is no, say plainly what that says about the sixties, and what it would say the next time the council wants to move a few streets out of the way for something.
