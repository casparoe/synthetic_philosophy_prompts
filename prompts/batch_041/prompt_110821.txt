I can't tell any more whether the dogmatism paradox is a real problem or a question that was posed wrong from the start, and our reading group has been no help — a term on it and I'm fairly sure we're not all disagreeing about the same thing. The puzzle as we keep stating it: if I know that P, then by closure I know that any future evidence that seems to tell against P is misleading; and if I know a piece of evidence is misleading, I'm entitled to ignore it; so knowing P entitles me to ignore whatever later looks like a refutation of P. The conclusion is absurd, the third step looks true by definition, which is presumably why nobody will give it up, and we've been round it a dozen times without moving.

Two camps. Camp A says the argument is fine and the absurdity is only apparent: knowing and being reasonable come apart, so you can know P and still be unreasonable to go on believing it once the misleading evidence lands, and what the paradox shows is a real and interesting gap between knowledge and justification rather than a reductio. Camp B says the argument has to fail at the 'entitled to ignore it' step, since that step is only good for evidence you know to be misleading on your current evidence, and once the counter-evidence arrives you don't know P any more, so you never get to know the evidence is misleading.

My suspicion, which I'd rather you tested than assumed: this is less a disagreement about knowledge than about which question is being asked — Camp A answering a synchronic question about what's true of an agent at a time, Camp B a diachronic one about how an agent ought to revise — with 'ought' quietly doing different work on each side. Though it might just as easily be a flatly substantive disagreement about whether misleading evidence defeats knowledge, or a verbal one about how we use the word 'knows'.

And here's the case I think the camps really split on, so I'd like the diagnosis to earn its keep on it: suppose the later evidence isn't a datum against P at all but evidence that I formed the belief badly — I learn my reasoning was sloppy, or that I was in no state to judge, or that someone I respect who's thought about it harder has come out the other way. Then its not even clear I can have the premise 'I know this evidence is misleading', because the evidence might be accurate about me rather than about P. Does that dissolve the paradox, relocate it, or show it was never about knowledge in the first place?

So — what kind of disagreement is this, is my suspicion right, and what would count as progress: a distinction, a counterexample, a formal result, or nothing, because it's a pseudo-problem?
