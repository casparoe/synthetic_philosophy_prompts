Ok, so I keep chewing on the old blind-from-birth-gets-his-sight-back thing, and I finally wrote down my gut verdicts, and I want to know what principle they add up to, if there is one.

Setup: Sam, blind from birth, no light perception, learned to tell shapes apart by touch as a kid — cubes, spheres, triangles, he's got them cold. Surgery, bandages come off, he can see. The question is what he can do in the first minute or so, without touching anything.

- Cube and sphere sitting on the table: can he say which is which? No way.
- Which of two balls is bigger? Yeah, I think he can. He sees that one is bigger and he already knows what bigger means.
- Which of two objects is nearer? No, he can't. Which bugs me, since that's also basically a size thing.
- Three balls vs four balls: can he tell which pile has more? Yeah. He's been counting by touch his whole life.
- Which of these is the same object he was just holding? No.
- Which one is smoother? No — he knows smooth, but looking at something doesn't tell you how it'll feel.
- Can he read a printed word? No, obviously. He reads raised letters, actual letter shapes just bumpy, and it dont help him at all.
- Same guy, except he had normal sight until he was three, lost it, and gets it back at forty: cube vs sphere, fine. Printed word, fine.
- Sam stares at a sphere for five minutes while holding it, then you show him a cube: now he can tell, easy. One trial and he's got it.
- Different sense pair: a woman deaf from birth who gets her hearing back as a adult. First minute, violin vs drum: no. Which of two sounds is louder: yes.

My hunch is the stuff that carries over is magnitude — more, bigger, louder — and the stuff that never carries over is shape and identity. But then "nearer" is a magnitude and I said no, so either that's a real seam or I'm just being inconsistent, and I can't tell which.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? And what does it say about: can he pick out the triangle by sight alone if you tell him "it's the one on the left" and let him look at it for a while, still no touching? And what about someone blind from birth who's spent years using one of those camera-on-the-tongue rigs — does he get the cube/sphere thing on day one, or not? Also, is it my size verdict or my distance verdict that has to go?
