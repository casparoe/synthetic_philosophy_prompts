I'm writing a final paper for my philosophy of mind seminar, and my thesis is that epiphenomenalism is self-refuting. The argument looks simple to me: epiphenomenalism says conscious experiences have no causal powers, yet we constantly talk and write about our experiences, and any report must have a cause. Since the natural cause of a report about an experience is the experience itself, the very existence of our introspective talk shows that experiences do cause things, which contradicts the view. My professor acknowledges that the "paradox of phenomenal judgment" is a serious problem but denies that it amounts to a refutation, and I cannot see what more a refutation would need. Could you explain where my argument breaks down, what the strongest surviving version of the paradox actually is, and whether it leaves epiphenomenalism merely awkward or genuinely untenable?
