I teach a 300-level seminar in philosophy of language, 14 students, and I get them for a single 80-minute block on Davidson's radical interpretation. Every year I do the same thing: I stand up, explain the radical interpreter, the principle of charity, holism, the interdependence of belief and meaning, and they dutifully write it down, and I'm pretty sure not one of them actually feels why you can't just read meanings off behavior. So I want to throw out the lecture and run a game instead, and I'd like you to design it. What I want them to come away with, in their bones rather than on a handout, is this: you cannot fix the meaning of a single word without already assuming a great deal about what the speaker believes, and you cannot fix the speaker's beliefs without already assuming a great deal about what the words mean — the two only get settled together, all at once, and even then not uniquely. Also that the only thing you ever get to see is the speaker holding a whole sentence true of a situation; you never get handed a word with its meaning attached.

My rough sketch, which you should feel free to rebuild from scratch if you can do better: the students split into pairs (7 pairs), and each pair is a team of field linguists who've been dropped among a made-up people — call them the Tessik, or rename them, doesn't matter — and all they have is a field log of maybe a dozen "scenes," each one a little drawing or description of a situation plus the utterance the Tessik speaker produced, marked as either held-true or held-false of that scene. That's the whole dataset. I've rigged each log with two traps. First, the speaker holds one belief we don't share — I'm thinking they think the sun orbits the earth, or that a harmless local berry is deadly, or that a certain old man controls the rain; pick whichever generates the cleanest confusion — so the interpreters who assume the speaker believes what we'd believe get the vocabulary subtly wrong, and the interpreters who assume the speaker is always right about the world get the beliefs wrong, and there's no way to fix either without fixing the other. Second, I want a systematically permuted manual that fits every single scene just as well as the "intended" one — swap two terms consistently, or give some small word a reading that shifts a bit of logical structure — so that at the end two teams with identical predictive success are holding incompatible manuals and neither can be refuted by anything they've seen. If you can work in a second phase where a team may put sentences to a live informant (one student holding the key) but the informant is only allowed to signal held-true or held-false, never to explain what anything means, that seems like it would drive the point home hard, but use your judgment on whether it's worth the added machinery.

What I need from you: the full runnable thing. Rules, roles, a materials list, a timeline for the 80 minutes, a scoring scheme that makes them care without any real money (I'm thinking tokens or points, teams betting on held-out scenes they haven't seen yet, something where being right pays and being confidently wrong costs), and — importantly — one fully worked sample packet I can photocopy: the scenes, the hidden key, and the two competing manuals, plus a template so I can build the other packets myself with different traps. Nobody should hear the word "charity" or "holism" until the debrief, and there should be no framing lecture up front; they should just start trying to crack it and only find out at the end what they were doing. The debrief should walk back through what happened and map the specific moments onto the specific pieces of Davidson's view, and it should be honest about what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this — I'd bet money several of them decide it's just a code-breaking puzzle with a unique right answer and get annoyed when it isn't, and I don't know how to head that off without giving the game away — and finally, tell me plainly which parts of the idea this activity simply cannot reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself in the last ten minutes.
