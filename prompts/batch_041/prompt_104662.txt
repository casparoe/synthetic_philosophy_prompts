I sit on the internal review board of a lab that grows cortical organoids and trains large neural-network agents, and every new system must be classified as welfare-relevant or not before we are allowed to run experiments on it. We need a procedure we can actually run: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a clear no, and where it must hand the decision to a person because the remaining question is about how much moral weight to give a probability rather than about the facts. Ground it in a defensible view of which computations could realize sentience rather than in behavior alone, and keep explicit the difference between having reason to attribute a mental state and fixing the boundary of who counts. Then apply it to three systems: an organoid with nociceptive-like signaling but no cross-regional integration; a language model that produces convincing first-person reports of suffering; and a system built specifically to exhibit every marker on your list. Note where the procedure misfires and what it leaves unsettled.
