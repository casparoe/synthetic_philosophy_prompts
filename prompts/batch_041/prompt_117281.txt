The four:

1. Plain Brier. Average (p − outcome)^2 across questions, lower is better.
2. Log score. Average of log(p you assigned to the thing that actually happened).
3. The "Bold Call bonus". You take the log score, then add 0.15 for every question where you said 0.95 or higher and turned out right, and subtract 0.15 for every question where you said 0.95 or higher and turned out wrong.
4. The "Trimmed log". Same as 2, except before averaging we drop each forecaster's single worst question and their single best question.

Why I care beyond "which one pays out smoothly": I've drunk the accuracy-first kool-aid. I think the only thing that makes a credence good or bad is how close it is to the truth — that's the fundamental epistemic good — and everything else people say about credences (obey the probability axioms, update by conditionalization, don't hedge about everything) has to earn its keep by being a reliable way of getting close to the truth. So the rule I want is the one that, if our forecasters spent the whole season optimizing against it, would leave them maximally close to the truth and nothing else — no side-prize for looking bold, no free pass for being right about the easy stuff.

Give me a definite 1-4, no hedging, no "well it depends what you value" menu. If two of them tie for you, break the tie anyway. And tell me straight which one is the worst of the four — I'd rather hear it from you now then find out after payout.
