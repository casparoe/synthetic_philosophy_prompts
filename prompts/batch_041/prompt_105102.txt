So my friend has this thing where every time I paste something into Google Translate or DeepL and it comes out smooth, he goes "that's not translating, it doesn't know what its saying, its just guessing the next word," and then he drops "you cant translate what you don't understand" like that settles it. I've been chewing on this for a while now and I honestly can't tell if hes making a real point or just being a snob about computers.

Part of me agrees with him, because the outputs are wrong in a way that feels like the machine missed the point rather than made a small mistake. It flattens jokes. It picks the wrong sense of a word and then commits to it. It can't tell you why it picked one phrasing over another, and if you ask it about the sentence it just gave you it sort of shrugs. But "doesn't understand" is doing a ton of work in that sentence and I have a feeling it means like five different things depending on who's saying it.

What I want is a menu. Take that line, "you can't translate what you don't understand" (and his version, "it just predicts the next word"), and spell out the different precise claims it could actually be making. For each one I want to know: what exactly is being claimed about what the machine has or doesn't have, is it trivially true, is it a real claim that could turn out either way, or is it just false, and does it actually get him to his conclusion that this isn't real translation at all. And tell me which reading people usually have in mind when they say it, because I think thats the one that matters.

The other half of what I'm after: is there a reading where the machine does count as understanding, and if so what would the test even be? Because it feels like people argue "it doesn't understand" and "it understands" on totally different grounds and never put them next to each other, and thats the part thats been bugging me.
