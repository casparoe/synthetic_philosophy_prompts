I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar and we have been working through the color phi phenomenon, and I have built an argument that I now cannot decide whether to be proud of, because it appears to prove far too much. Here is the setup I am working with. In the standard demonstration, a blue dot is flashed at the top of a screen and then, a fraction of a second later, a red dot at the bottom. Under the right combination of spacing and timing, viewers do not see two dots; they see a single dot traveling from top to bottom, and they report that it changes color abruptly about halfway along its path. The report locates the color change at a moment that precedes the presentation of the red dot, even though the visual system cannot have had the red dot's color in hand until the second flash actually arrived. In seminar we described this two ways and then could not say what separated them: as the brain's rewriting of the earlier impression once the later one arrives (the Orwellian reading), or as the brain's simply withholding the earlier impression and releasing an already-edited version once the second dot is in (the Stalinesque reading).

That gave me the following argument, and the conclusion is absurd, so I want to know which step is doing the damage and what it costs to reject it. (1) When a subject reports a color change at some midpoint time t, the content of the experience she reports having at t includes the color of the second dot. (2) The second dot's color was not available to the visual system until after t. (3) So the content of the experience at t includes information that was not available to the system at t. (4) An experience occurring at t can have as its content only information available to the system at t; the perceptual system cannot reach backward and insert content into an experience that has already happened. (5) Therefore the subject's report does not describe an experience that occurred at t; it is a post-hoc fabrication about t, not a memory of anything. (6) But the mechanism is perfectly general: any perceptual report that locates a feature at a time t using information the system receives only after t is, by this reasoning, equally a fabrication. (7) Neural transduction and processing take time, so virtually every perceptual report locates features at times using information that reaches the system only afterward — you cannot report the color of a flash until well after the flash. (8) So virtually every perceptual report is a post-hoc fabrication. (9) If every perceptual report is a fabrication, then reports give us no evidence at all about the timing or the content of experience. (10) Therefore there is no evidence about the timing or content of experience, and the color phi phenomenon, along with every other result in the psychology of perception, cannot be studied by asking people what they saw.

I would like to keep the empirical practice and the oddity of the phenomenon; I am willing to give up whatever premise I have to. So: which step should go, why did it look compelling in the first place, and what do I have to pay for cutting it — in particular, what becomes of the Orwellian-versus-Stalinesque question once I have made the cut?
