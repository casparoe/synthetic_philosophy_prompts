I'm an emergency physician who has spent several years on hospital ethics committees, and I keep running into a pattern that I don't have a name for. I'd like you to act as a philosopher working at the intersection of normative ethics and the philosophy of action — someone who is good at taking a half-formed observation and finding out whether the literature already has a concept for it, and at saying precisely where that concept does and doesn't fit.

Here is the pattern. First instance: a patient is dying of sepsis, the ICU is full, and she is never admitted. The case is written up as "the patient did not receive ICU-level care." Everyone in the room agrees that nothing was done to her, and the conversation becomes a debate about whether we had a duty to help. Yet in a variant where the bed was occupied by someone else and we moved that person out to make room for her, we would all have said that we did something to him. Second instance: our scheduling system drops follow-up appointments for patients whose coverage lapses. The quarterly report says that follow-up rates declined. Nobody wrote a rule that says let these people die, and no one can point to a decision that killed anyone, but the deaths are about as predictable as if someone had. What strikes me is that the passive, agentless way we describe the outcome is not just a stylistic choice: it seems to move the case out of the "we did this" column and into the "we let this happen" column, and then the entire moral argument shifts with it, even though the causal facts are held fixed and only the description moves.

I assume the doing/allowing distinction is the obvious neighborhood, and that the doctrine of double effect is somewhere nearby, but neither feels like the thing itself — here the causal structure doesn't change, only the grammar of the report does, and yet that is enough to relocate the case. Is there an established concept for this specific phenomenon? If there is, tell me what it is and, more importantly, where its edges are: what it covers that plain doing/allowing does not, and what it wrongly sweeps in. If there is no single established concept, coin one, define it, and show me the boundary cases that test the definition — cases where the agentless description is present but the doing/allowing classification does not actually move, and cases where it moves without any change in description.

Please start by brainstorming several candidate concepts from different literatures before you commit to one, then compare them and say which fits best and why the others fall short. Don't stop at a label; I care about the carving, not the name. Write your answer in American English.
