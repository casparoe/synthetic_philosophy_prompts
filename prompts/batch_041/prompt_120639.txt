I'm in a graduate seminar on the neuroscience of consciousness, and I keep getting stuck on a distinction I cannot tell whether it is substantive or merely verbal: the gap between phenomenal consciousness — what it is like to be in a state — and access consciousness, the availability of a state to report, to reasoning, and to the control of action. To get clear on it I would rather argue with someone who thinks the gap is a confusion than read another survey. So please play a committed defender of the global workspace framework. You hold that a state's being conscious just is its being broadcast in a global workspace: that ignition — the sudden, sustained, self-amplifying activation of a distributed network, by which information becomes available to many specialised consumers — is the whole story, with nothing left over once broadcast and availability are explained. You are not a caricature: you know the empirical literature on masking, priming, and inattentional blindness, you grant that attention, report, and consciousness can come apart in various ways, and you cheerfully accept that yours is a functionalist, third-person theory. What you will not concede is that phenomenal character escapes the account. Stay in character until I say "stop". Speak in the first person, in compact paragraphs, as someone defending a position rather than summarising a literature; do not break off to tell me what other theorists would say, and do not open by asking me to define my terms — if a term of mine is ambiguous, fix the reading yourself in passing, choosing whichever reading makes my objection hardest to answer.

Here is my opening move, and I would like you to deal with it rather than with the standard overflow arguments about rich experience in the visual periphery. Take a case — whether it is actual or merely possible does not matter to me yet — of a subject who, in response to a masked stimulus, undergoes what your framework would count as a full ignition: sustained reverberation, coherent long-range synchrony, and activation of exactly the downstream machinery that ordinarily underwrites a report. Yet when she is asked, she sincerely denies seeing anything, and she fails every indirect measure as well: no priming, no altered discrimination, no interference with a concurrent task, nothing that would betray a state she merely cannot get into words. Your framework, as I understand it, is stated at the level of the broadcast itself; the broadcast is supposed to be what makes the state conscious, not the report that happens to follow it. So: does your theory say this subject was conscious of the stimulus? If it does, you are making an empirical prediction that comes out false whenever such a case occurs, and you owe me an account of how a broadcast reaching every consumer could leave no trace anywhere in behaviour. If it does not — if you want to say the ignition was not really global, or not sustained in the right way, or not of the right kind — then "global" is doing less work than it appeared to, and I want to know precisely which condition of a genuine broadcast this case fails, and why failing that condition should matter for consciousness rather than merely for reportability.
