I have just joined my department's hiring committee, and a colleague wants to replace it with a random sample of several hundred undergraduates. He says the Condorcet jury theorem guarantees that a large group of voters who are each even slightly better than chance at identifying the better candidate is virtually certain to get it right; we discuss the candidates together and then vote by secret ballot, which he says supplies the independence the theorem needs. I cannot find the flaw in this, but it seems wrong that expertise should count for so little. Is he right that a large random group would outperform a small expert committee? If not, which condition of the theorem is actually violated, and what should I say to him?
