I teach a small advanced-undergraduate seminar, and I have managed to get myself tangled in a puzzle I cannot think my way out of. For the past three weeks I have been telling the class that at some point before the end of term they will have an unannounced in-class quiz — unannounced in the strict sense that on the morning of the quiz they will have no way of knowing that it is coming. My aim was to keep them doing the reading steadily rather than cramming the night before. Last week one of my sharpest students stayed after class and walked me through an argument that the quiz can never happen: if we reach the last possible day, everyone will know the quiz must fall on that day, so it will not be unannounced; knowing that, they can rule out the last day too, and then the day before, and so on back to the first day — so no day can be the surprise day, and I cannot keep my promise at all. The rest of the class now treats the whole thing as a running joke, and several of them have quietly stopped preparing, which defeats the entire purpose. I cannot tell whether the student has actually proved something or has merely tied everyone in knots, and I do not know what I should now do — drop the quiz, hold it anyway, or change my announcement in some way that is honest and still motivating.

I would like you to act as someone who thinks carefully and patiently about knowledge, public announcements, and practical reasoning — not to reassure me, but to give me advice I can actually act on.
