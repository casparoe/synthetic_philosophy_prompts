Ok, here's a position I want you to argue for. When we say someone's omission caused something, I think the real work is being done by contrastive salience, not duty and not counterfactual dependence on its own: an omission is a cause just when the outcome is best explained by contrasting what actually happened with a salient alternative in which the agent acted, and all our talk of duties is really just a special case of that salience. Argue for that. Give at least one case where there's no duty at all but the omission still intuitively causes the outcome (something like a hiker who keeps quiet about a washed-out bridge), and one where there clearly is a duty but it still feels off to call the inaction a cause, then deal head-on with the obvious objection that this makes causation alot too dependent on whatever we happen to find salient.
