As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I keep encountering the slogan 'money is a social construct' being made to do very different kinds of work. In one discussion it meant that money is not a natural kind and that its existence depends on collective acceptance; in another it meant that money is arbitrary and can be redesigned by fiat; in a third it was used to deny that money is real at all. I would like a menu of the precise claims this slogan could be making, rather than a defense of any one of them.

For each reading, please specify what kind of dependence it posits (causal, constitutive, response-dependent, or something else), whether it is trivial or substantive, and whether it holds of money, race, and gender in the same sense. Distinguish the claim that a kind is socially constructed from the claim that its members are socially constructed, and from the claim that our concept of it is socially constructed. Then say which reading would actually support the inference that because money is socially constructed, its value can be changed by decree, and which reading the people deploying the slogan in political argument most likely intend. No citations or reading lists are needed; I want the conceptual map.
