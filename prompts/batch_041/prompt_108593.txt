I was reading this book about the Cambridge philosophers and it has a chapter on Ramsey. The author summarizes his view on the meaning of life roughly like this: Ramsey was an atheist and a hard-nosed naturalist, so he thought the question of life's meaning has no real answer. The universe is just a physical system with no purpose, and anything we call meaning is something we project onto it. His advice was basically to accept that life is meaningless in the cosmic sense and then choose your own meanings, since there's no fact of the matter about what's worth caring about. The author says this makes him a kind of proto-existentialist, and that his famous remark about the stars not being able to think or love is just a poetic way of saying the universe doesn't care about us, so we have to make our own meaning.

Something feels off to me. Ive heard Ramsey was actually a pretty cheerful, engaged person, and I thought his humanism was more than just 'the universe is cold so do whatever.' But I dont know his work well enough to say. Can you tell me how fair this summary is? Which parts would a Ramsey scholar accept, which are subtle distortions, and does the choose-your-own-meaning conclusion actually follow from his views or is the author reading him through a later existentialist lens?
