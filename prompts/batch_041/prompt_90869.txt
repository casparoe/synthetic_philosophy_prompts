I run a small philosophy reading group, and we just finished Kierkegaard's Present Age — the review of that Gyllembourg novel, the one about "leveling" and the public being some kind of phantom. It hit weirdly close to home: I got piled on in a couple of local Facebook groups over a post I made about our library's budget, and reading him was the first thing that actually described the texture of that experience. Anyway, next month we're doing a Q&A with a guest: a media-studies person who's finishing a book arguing that Kierkegaard basically diagnosed algorithmic feeds a century and a half early. His line is that "leveling" is the right name for what recommendation systems do to taste, and that the old public has been replaced by something he calls the feed. I'm moderating and I want to actually press him rather than let him coast on the vibe.

So: take the role of someone who knows Kierkegaard's social writings closely and also knows contemporary media theory. Before you write the questions, brainstorm for yourself where his thesis is thinnest — I want the questions aimed at those spots, not at stuff he's already got a rehearsed answer for. Then give me 8-10 interview questions. For each one, add a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give, because his favorite dodge is "Kierkegaard is doing phenomenology, not sociology," and I want the follow-ups to make that dodge harder to reuse each time.

A few things I specifically want covered, at least one question each: the disanalogy between a daily paper and an algorithmic feed (personalization, feedback loops, no editor, no shared text everyone reads); the fact that Kierkegaard himself published constantly, used pseudonyms, and clearly wanted readers — so whats the actual criterion separating his anonymity from the press's, and does that criterion even work for bots and anonymous accounts; whether "leveling" is a description or a diagnosis, and whether it's falsified by how polarized and loud everything is now rather than uniform and flat; what "passion" is even supposed to mean as a normative standard, and whether feeds don't just maximize a cheap version of it; and finally whether his remedy — becoming the single individual — is just quietist, or whether it leaves room for collective action that also runs through these same platforms.

Keep it conversational, no generic icebreakers, and don't let any question be answerable by him just restating the book.
