I'm giving a paper at a small workshop next month defending a reading of Hume on miracles that I think gets him off the hook the Bayesians keep hanging him on. My line: the arguement everyone attacks — the one where the antecedent improbability of the miracle is supposed to swamp the testimony — is neither the argument Hume needs nor the one he actually gives; the load-bearing part is his second, empirical point about how miracle reports get generated (rival religions discrediting each other, the pull of the marvelous, the fact that reports of a violation of a well-established regularity are precisely the reports with the most to gain from being believed). That part is a sociological induction about the sources of testimony, and it doesn't stand or fall with any probability calculus. I also want to prise apart something Hume runs together: whether testimony *could in principle* establish a violation of a law, and whether *believing* such testimony is licensed given how these reports are produced. I'll concede the first is probably lost and say I don't care, because the second is where the action is.

The room: two Bayesian formal epistemologists who think the whole thing is a textbook confusion of likelihoods with posteriors and will come with slides; one early-modern historian who thinks my reconstruction is anachronistic and that Hume's target was the evidential force of testimony for revealed religion specifically, not testimony as such; and one social epistemologist who works on testimony and injustice and will want to know what my maxim does to reports from people we're already primed to disbelieve. Plus a few grad students who will ask what counts as a miracle.

You know this literature and you know how these two crowds actually behave in Q&A, so think it through before you write. Prep me: the objections I'll really get, from whom, in their own vocabulary, ordered by how much damage they do — not a general survey of the miracles debate. For each, a reply I can deliver in about ninety seconds and a note on where that reply is soft. Tell me which of these are the same objection in different clothes so I don't pad the list, include the one I'm secretly hoping nobody raises, and cap the whole thing at 900 words. Say how confident you are in each call, and flag the single call you're least sure about.
