I am in the second year of a doctorate in the philosophy of physics, and I have drawn Maxwell's demon for a position paper in our reading group on the foundations of statistical mechanics. My flatmate, a condensed-matter experimentalist who enjoys needling me, has offered a design that I have not yet been able to answer to my own satisfaction, and I would like you to write the position paper for me. The design is a variant of Szilard's one-molecule engine. A single molecule sits in a box; a partition can be inserted; if you know which half the molecule is in, you can let it expand isothermally against a piston and take kT ln 2 of work out of it, the heat being drawn from the surrounding reservoir. My flatmate's demon learns which half the molecule is in by a logically reversible operation: it copies the position into a one-bit register that begins in a known state and discards nothing, so that on his account the measurement step itself need dissipate nothing. It then uses the register to place the partition and take the work. The twist is that the register is never reset. It is a single cell on a long tape whose cells each begin in a known state, and the head advances one cell per cycle, so no bit is ever overwritten; after N cycles the load has received N kT ln 2 of work and the tape holds N bits, each perfectly correlated with the history of the molecule. The tape is sealed in an insulated box, and nobody, including the demon, ever reads it again.

He says this exposes the standard exorcism as either wrong or mislocated. If nothing is ever erased, Landauer's principle, the kT ln 2 per bit that erasure is supposed to cost, never enters the story; the second law survives only because we insist on counting the entropy of the demon's records. But those records are never consulted and never measured, and the demon knows what they say; the tape is in one definite microstate, so its Boltzmann entropy is zero, and only its Gibbs entropy, the entropy of a distribution relative to a coarse-graining we chose, has gone up. So either entropy is description-relative, in which case the second law is a claim about our bookkeeping rather than about the world and the demon really has extracted work for nothing, or entropy is objective, in which case the demon's knowledge is itself a physical correlation that has to be paid for, information is physical, and Landauer's principle is a corollary of the second law rather than a foundation for it. He adds a second complaint that I find harder to answer: every exorcism I offer him seems to assume what it is meant to show. If I say that the demon must pay for a fresh supply of low-entropy tape, or must eventually pay for erasure, I have already assumed that entropy cannot be conjured away, which is the very thing at issue. One member of the reading group thinks the question turns on whether the demon's records are classical bits or quantum systems entangled with the gas; another thinks the modern fluctuation-theorem treatments settle it by proving that the work a demon can extract is bounded by the free-energy change plus a term proportional to the mutual information it holds about the gas; a third says that is the same accounting in new clothes.

Please write the essay as a philosopher of physics who is also at home in the statistical mechanics, someone who could referee for a specialist journal, and take care over the physics before taking a view. Set out the energy and entropy bookkeeping for one cycle and then for N cycles, since I find the dispute impossible to adjudicate without it, and then weigh the competing accounts and reach a verdict. I want the essay to settle, as far as it can: which account does the real work in exorcising this demon, the cost of erasure, the entropy of the memory and its correlations, the need for a low-entropy resource, or the relativity of entropy to a description; whether Landauer's principle is a fundamental law, a derived result, or a red herring in this setting; whether the second law as it applies here constrains the world or constrains only our descriptions of it; whether my flatmate's design is a genuine difficulty for the standard treatment or a restatement of it; and what, if anything, would settle the matter, a theorem, an experiment, or a clarification. Do not end on a non-committal note; defend a position, say what it costs, and note what would change your mind. There is no need to cite the literature, so argue from the physics and the concepts rather than from who said what. Aim for something in the region of 1,200 to 1,700 words, in British English throughout. After the essay, append a JSON object with exactly these fields: "verdict" (a string of one or two sentences stating the position you defend); "load_bearing_account" (a string naming which account carries the weight of the exorcism, whether erasure, memory entropy, low-entropy resource, description-relativity, or some combination, with a one-sentence justification); "landauer_status" (one of "fundamental law", "derived result", "red herring in this context", with a one-sentence justification); "second_law_status" (one of "objective constraint", "description-relative", "mixed", with a sentence of justification); "flatmate_concession" (a string saying what, if anything, my flatmate gets right); "crux" (a string naming the single question the verdict turns on and what would settle it); "strongest_objection" (a string giving the best argument against your verdict); and "confidence" (a number between 0 and 1).
