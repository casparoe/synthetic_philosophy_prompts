Title: Bootstrapping Without Circularity

1. The standard framing. The bootstrapping problem is usually presented as a problem about circularity. The setup is familiar: a subject forms an ordinary perceptual belief — that the table before her is red — and then, by noting that she has formed such a belief and that the world seems to agree, concludes that her color vision is reliable. Iterated many times, she appears to have accumulated evidence for the reliability of a faculty by relying on that very faculty. The complaint is that the reasoning is circular: the conclusion is supported only by premises that are themselves outputs of the faculty whose reliability is at issue. What makes the problem interesting is that the reasoning looks valid and its premises look known, so the circularity must carry the whole explanatory load.

2. Why circularity is the wrong diagnosis. I want to argue that circularity is a symptom rather than the disease. Consider a case built to separate them.

The Two-Dial Case. A device has two dials driven by a single photodetector. Dial A points to the color of the surface before it. Dial B points to a number between 0 and 1, which the device computes as its own estimate of how reliable Dial A is on the present occasion. Because both dials are driven by one photodetector, a fault in the detector moves both.

Compare two routines. In the Reliability-First routine I read Dial B, see 0.9, and on that basis trust Dial A's report of red; I do not first check Dial A. In the Report-First routine I read Dial A, see red, and on that basis conclude that Dial A is reliable, treating each correct report as a data point.

The standard framing predicts that Report-First is defective because circular, and that Reliability-First is innocent because the reliability estimate is used rather than established. But both routines lean on the shared photodetector: in each, everything I have comes from one source. So the shared-source structure cannot by itself be what makes Report-First bad. What distinguishes the two is the direction of evidential flow relative to the source. In Report-First the higher-order claim is supported only by an output of the very process it concerns, and once the process is fixed the output adds nothing to the claim — the source screens the output off from the claim. In Reliability-First I am not trying to establish the higher-order claim at all; I am employing it, and the common source is irrelevant to whether the employment is licensed.

So the constraint I propose is this: one may not treat an output of a process as evidence for a claim about that process when the process screens the output off from the claim. Circularity is the special case in which the same belief does double duty as premise and as support for the conclusion.

3. Two consequences, and a worry about each.

First consequence: some cases that look like bootstrapping are benign, and the screening-off account explains why. Suppose I use my eyes to check whether my glasses are smudged. The check is an output of the very faculty whose reliability is in question, yet it seems perfectly good. The account says: fine, because the claim being established is not about the reliability of vision as such but about a modulator of vision; the smudge is an independent feature that vision can detect without already presupposing that vision is reliable in general. The constraint bites only when the higher-order claim concerns the same process, not a condition on it. Worry: this threatens to admit too much, since almost any condition can be redescribed as a modulator, and I have not drawn a principled line between a process and its modulators.

Second consequence: the account predicts that the defect in Report-First is not invalidity but evidential idleness, and so it should extend to cases with no inference at all — a subject who simply, without arguing, moves from a perceptual belief to confidence in her faculties. I take this to be a point in the account's favor, since the problem was never really about arguments. Worry: if no inference is required, it is unclear what the screening-off relation is a relation between; screening off is standardly a relation among events or propositions in a probability model, and I have not said how to model the subject's state.

4. Requests. Please tell me: (a) whether the screening-off diagnosis is genuinely distinct from the familiar thought that the bootstrapper lacks independent evidence, or whether it collapses into it; (b) whether the Two-Dial Case is well formed, and in particular whether Reliability-First is as innocent as I need it to be, given that its reliability estimate is also a product of the shared detector; (c) whether the glasses case is a real counterexample or a cheat; (d) what the most damaging objection to the whole line is; and (e) if the account survives, whether it needs a fourth category for cases where the higher-order claim is about a process that is neither identical to nor a mere modulator of the first-order process — for instance a subject who uses her perception to assess her memory, or her memory to assess her perception.
