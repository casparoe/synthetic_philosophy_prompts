I got appointed to our county's AI oversight board last month, mostly because my mom has been on the waitlist for home-care hours for a year and I wrote an op-ed about how the new triage tool kept shuffling her place in line. The board has to vote next Tuesday on whether to approve the values layer for that tool, and I'm the one who has to draft the written opinion. Here's the mess. The tool ranks applicants for a scarce pool of home-care hours. It was built by a vendor and currently scores people on three objectives: predicted health gain, cost avoided, and something they call caregiver relief. The vendor says the weights come from a residents survey where people distributed 100 points across those objectives. Disability advocates on the board say the whole thing smuggles in a conception of the good life, one that prizes independence and productivity and relieving family burden, and that a liberal state shouldn't rank those goods for citizens, especially when the ranking decides who gets care. The fiscal office says if we don't weight cost, the program runs out of money by spring. The engineers say they can implement any aggregation rule we give them, but they need a rule, and they need it in writing.

Can you write the board's opinion for me? I definately want it to recommend something concrete: approve the values layer as is, reject it, or approve with specific conditions and a pilot period, with a timeline the vendor and the engineers can actually act on. And please take the strongest objection seriously. I think the neutrality objection is right in spirit, but I also think the board can't just throw out cost and let the program collapse. There's a proposal floating around from one member that I sort of like: a two-stage rule where first no applicant falls below a basic-needs floor, and only among the remaining applicants do we use the survey weights; plus a contestability rule where anyone can ask for a human review of the weights that affected them. I'd like the memo to say whether that two-stage idea actually answers the neutrality objection or just relocates it, and what the board should do about the vendor's claim that its current model is already neutral because it just predicts outcomes and doesn't rank values. Also, we have to decide what to do about the fact that the survey sample was mostly older homeowners, while the people who use home care are disproportionately renters and people of color. If you recommend a fix, give us the 90-day version and the 18-month version, because we meet again in the fall. Keep it formal enough to put in the minutes but not so formal that it's useless. I'm not asking for a literature review or citations, just the opinion.
