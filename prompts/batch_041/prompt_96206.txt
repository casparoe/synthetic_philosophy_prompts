Hey, I'm in a metaphysics/epistemology seminar and we just did Fitch's paradox of knowability. I have to write a short essay on whether the paradox can be defused by restricting the knowability principle, and I'd like you to comment on my draft. Here's the gist of what I'm arguing.

The knowability thesis says every truth is knowable: if p is true, it's possible that someone knows p. Fitch's proof (the result goes back to a referee report by Church in 1945, published by Fitch in 1963) shows this collapses into the claim that every truth is actually known. The trick is the proposition "p and nobody knows p." If that's true, the knowability thesis says it's possible to know it. But if you know the conjunction, you know both conjuncts, so you know p. Then the second conjunct — nobody knows p — is false. So the conjunction can't be known. So it can't be true. So there are no unknown truths.

My essay argues that the knowability thesis was never meant to apply to self-undermining propositions like that. The thesis is about truths that can be known; it shouldn't be read as saying that even propositions whose knowability is internally inconsistent are knowable. So I want to restrict the principle: only propositions that are not ruled out by the act of knowing them fall under the knowability thesis. Call them "safe" propositions. The paradox only gets going because we apply the thesis to an unsafe proposition.

I think this is more than a dodge becuase the anti-realist's whole idea is that truth is tied to our epistemic capacities. If a proposition is such that our knowing it would make it false, then it's not the kind of thing that could be true in the first place under an epistemic theory of truth. So the restriction falls out of the view rather than being bolted on.

But I'm worried this is circular. The restriction seems tailor-made to exclude the one proposition that generates the paradox. And I haven't said how to characterize "safe" without just saying "not Fitch-style." Also, a critic might say the restriction is ad hoc because it's motivated only by avoiding the paradox, not by independent epistemic considerations. I also don't know if the restriction actually saves the anti-realist from a weaker version of the paradox, or if it just moves the problem.

Can you comment on this? Specifically: (1) is my statement of the proof accurate? (2) is the restriction as I've stated it coherent, or is it circular? (3) does it actually block the paradox or just push it around? (4) what's the strongest objection to my fix that I'm not seeing?

Also, tell me where my reasoning is most likely to fail and what would change your mind about whether the restriction works. Like, if you think the restriction is legitimate, what would have to be true for it to be ad hoc? And if you think it's ad hoc, what would make it non-ad hoc?

I'm not looking for citations or a reading list, just comments on the argument. Thanks.
