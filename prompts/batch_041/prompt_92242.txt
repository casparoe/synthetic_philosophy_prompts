I am on a working group drafting provisional criteria for a proposed psychiatric diagnosis — call it motivational blunting disorder (MBD). As currently specified, MBD is diagnosed in adolescents and young adults on the basis of self-reported flattening of desire and diminished goal-directed activity; it requires no biomarker or laboratory finding; it overlaps substantially with the criteria for major depression; it has no approved treatment; and it is frequently encountered by patients as an online label before any clinician raises it. We expect the criteria to be revised repeatedly over the next decade, and we want to anticipate how the category will drift.

Drawing on Hacking's account of interactive kinds and looping effects — the idea that a classification of people can be altered by the people classified, so that the category and its members co-evolve — sort the following candidate looping mechanisms into tiers according to how strongly you expect each to reshape MBD over that decade, from the mechanism most likely to drive substantial drift to the one least likely. Give a brief justification for each placement, and flag any mechanism you regard as doing the same work as another rather than being genuinely distinct.

1. Identity uptake: patients adopt the label as a self-description and reshape their self-narrative and conduct to fit it.
2. Clinician anchoring and criteria stretching: diagnosticians extend the category to ambiguous cases lying near the prototype.
3. Instrument feedback: rating scales validated on early cohorts come to define what counts as a case in later cohorts.
4. Reimbursement lock-in: coding and coverage rules freeze the category's boundaries as they happened to stand at adoption.
5. Community elaboration: online patient communities develop and circulate symptom scripts that later patients then match.
6. Treatment narrowing: if a treatment emerges, the category contracts toward the profile of responders.
7. Sampling feedback: research cohorts become unrepresentative, and their distinctive features feed back into the official criteria.
8. Valence dynamics: the label's social standing shifts, changing whether people conceal, reject, or embrace it, and thereby changing the presentation clinicians observe.

Then address three further questions. First, which of MBD's design features — adolescent onset, self-report without biomarker, overlap with depression, absence of an approved treatment, pre-diagnosis online exposure — raise or lower each mechanism, and does any feature reverse a mechanism's expected direction? Second, is MBD as specified closer to the paradigm cases Hacking treats as strongly interactive or to those he treats as indifferent, and what would have to change to move it across? Third, if the top-tier mechanism dominates for a decade, would MBD's criteria become more stable or less, and what does that imply for whether we should try to design a less loop-prone category?
