ok, so every time the origin of farming comes up, everyone I know reaches for the same picture: agriculture as a trap. the story is always roughly this. you're a forager, someone sticks a few seeds in the ground, the harvest is huge, so you hang around, next year you plant more, the group grows, and before long you're stuck -- too many mouths to go back to wandering, years of work sunk into cleared fields and stored grain, and the old foraging grounds and skills are gone. the jaws have shut. and from there everyone jumps straight to the conclusion: farming was the worst mistake we ever made, foragers had it better, and we've been paying the bill ever since.

I mostly buy the vibe but I want to actually audit the analogy before I go around repeating it. so: give me the mapping. what's the bait, what's the trapper, what exactly are the jaws, and who's caught -- us, or wheat? then tell me where it breaks. a trap means somebody set it and something got lured in on purpose; does that survive, or is the picture quietly smuggling in an agent, or making the crop itself the agent (which feels like its own mess)? does the "you can't get out" bit hold up when some groups clearly did walk away from farming -- and if there were reversals, what does that do to the trap story? and separately, does the conclusion it's carrying -- that it was a mistake, a net loss -- actually follow from the analogy at all, or is the mistake verdict leaning on something the trap picture can't give you? I want the disanalogies that matter for the conclusion kept separate from the ones that are just decoration, and a straight verdict on whether "trap" earns its keep or whether a different picture (ratchet, arms race, ponzi scheme, whatever) does the job better.
