So I've been argueing about open-sourcing AI models with people online for months now and I keep noticing both sides just shout past each other, so I want to actually work through one real case instead of going on vibes. Take GPT-2. OpenAI announced it in feb 2019, said it was too dangerous to just hand out the weights, gave journalists a demo instead, and then over the next several months released bigger and bigger versions anyway until the full 1.5 billion parameter model came out that November. I want you to analyze that whole episode through an idea from tech policy that I half-remember and might be mangling -- the Collingridge dilemma, I think it's called. The gist, as I understand it, is that when a technology is new and small you could still steer it but you don't yet know what the harms are, and by the time the harms show up the thing is woven into everything and steering it is basically hopeless. So map it for me: at each stage of the GPT-2 rollout, what exactly was being held back, who was doing the holding, and who could have actually stopped what. Then push it the other way -- does staged release genuinely dodge the dilemma, or is it just losing control more slowly with better PR? And one thing that nags me that I never see anyone address: releasing a file and releasing a capability seem like different acts to me. Once a model can do the thing, the thing is pretty much in the water whether or not the weights are out there. Which of those two was the GPT-2 fight really about, and does the dilemma even care about the difference?
