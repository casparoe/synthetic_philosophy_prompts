You are an expert in statutory interpretation and the philosophy of law. I am a university student in an advanced legislation seminar, and I need you to draft the joint statement that a committed textualist and a committed purposivist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Please think carefully, and begin with a brief brainstorming section identifying candidate common ground before you produce the document.

The statement should contain (1) a numbered agreed-claims section covering the nature of statutory meaning, the proper role of purpose clauses and legislative history, the canons of construction, and whether there is a real distinction between interpretation and construction; and (2) a disagreements table with columns for the precise point of disagreement, the textualist's formulation in wording a textualist would accept, the purposivist's formulation in wording a purposivist would accept, the crux, and a concrete case or argument both sides agree would bear on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where you think a dispute is merely verbal, say so and explain why.

Ground the exercise in this invented statute and dispute. A fictional statute, the Automated Decisions Accountability Act, provides that a covered entity deploying a high-risk automated decision system must give an affected individual a meaningful explanation of any adverse decision. Section 2 states the purpose is to ensure that individuals can understand and contest decisions that significantly affect their rights. Section 3 defines meaningful explanation as a description of the principal factors and the role they played in the decision. A regulator reads this to require disclosure of the model's decision logic sufficient for a claimant to verify the outcome; the regulated company reads it to require only a plain-language summary of the top factors. The preamble says the Act is modeled on existing data-protection principles, and an uncodified drafting note states that nothing in the section requires disclosure of trade secrets. Do not cite cases or secondary literature; reason from the concepts and the invented materials.
