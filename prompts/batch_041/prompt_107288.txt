I am writing a novel whose protagonist is a forecaster who insists that the only defensible prior over hypotheses is one that penalizes complexity, and I need her tier list of her own tools to survive a knowledgeable reader. Please sort the following by how much you would trust each to lead her to the truth as evidence accumulates: (a) the Solomonoff prior, which assigns a hypothesis a probability of 2 raised to the negative length of its shortest generating program; (b) a two-part accounting that charges separately for the model and for the data given the model; (c) a prior that penalizes the time a program takes to produce its output rather than the length of the program itself; and (d) the informal rule of counting adjustable parameters. Give one sentence per item on why it lands where it does, note any pair that reaches the same verdict for different reasons, and finish by naming the single argument or piece of evidence that would move an item up or down a tier.
