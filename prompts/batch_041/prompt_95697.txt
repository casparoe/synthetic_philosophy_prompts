For a few years I have kept a toy on my desk: a shallow tray of a few thousand small steel balls whose motion is entirely local and mechanical. By tuning magnets underneath I got the balls to settle into little towers, and I let the heights of the towers stand for numbers. The towers knock into one another, and the collisions reliably turn one set of heights into another — the tray does arithmetic. I did not read the arithmetic into it. I can predict the tray's next state far more accurately by doing sums on the numbers than by tracking any single ball, and if I reach in and change one tower to a different height, everything downstream changes just as the arithmetic says it should.

What I cannot get straight is how both of these can hold at once. Every individual collision is completely accounted for by the local rules, and nothing corresponding to a number appears anywhere in the physics of a ball; yet the arithmetic is the only description that tells me why the tray ends up showing 7 rather than 4, and the level at which I intervene to change the outcome is the level of the numbers. I meet the same shape elsewhere — in a traffic jam, say, where the only thing that predicts when I arrive is a backward-traveling wave, though no car ever moves backward.

I am not asking about consciousness; the tray is plainly not thinking, which is part of what unsettles me. I want to know whether this exact bind has a name and a literature, and above all how to carve it: what these higher-level items are, if they are neither causes in the ordinary sense nor mere shorthand; how my case relates to the familiar worries about mental causation — epiphenomenalism and the causal exclusion argument — given that here the idle higher level contains no mind at all; whether the fact that I can intervene on the numbers, and not only on the balls, changes the verdict; and where the line falls between a higher-level description that is genuinely doing explanatory work and one that is only a fanciful redescription of the same collisions. I would rather have the carving than the label, but if a standard term exists, I want it. Please answer in American English.
