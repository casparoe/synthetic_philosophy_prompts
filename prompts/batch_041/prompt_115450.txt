Ok so this is kind of a weird one and I'm not sure it even has an answer. I've been keeping a running list on my phone of things where the rule "if you have to ask, you aren't" seems to hold, and things where it flips, and I cannot for the life of me find the pattern.

The first pile: if you're sitting there wondering whether you were brave in that moment, you weren't. Same deal with kind, same with generous. The asking seems to be the tell.

Second pile, opposite: wondering whether you were asleep is basically proof that you were. You can't be uncertain about that one from the inside.

Then there's a third pile that I don't have any word for and it's the one that's actually bothering me. Stuff like being absorbed in a book, or being in the middle of a really good conversation, or I'm told being "in flow" at work. In those cases you can sort of know you're in the state, but the knowing is what boots you out. The second you go "huh, I'm really absorbed in this," you aren't anymore. So it's like you can only ever see the state in the rearview mirror, and it's the looking that ended it.

What I want to know is if there's a name for this third pile — a state you're in a position to know you're in, except that the knowing wrecks it, so there's no moment where you're both in it and knowing you're in it. I have a feeling this is adjacent to whatever epistemologists argue about when they argue over whether you can always know your own mind, but I don't want just "ah yes, that's called X." I want to know whether X actually covers the self-wrecking part or whether that's a different animal entirely, what the nearest established ideas are and exactly where each of them falls short, and if nothing fits, give me a name for it and tell me where the edges are. Like, does the state have to be destroyed by the noticing, or is it enough that noticing changes it? What about states that only get stronger when you clock them — are there any, and do they belong in the same family or a different one? And is the "if you have to ask, you aren't" pile the same phenomenon as this or just a cousin that hangs around nearby?

Please write it out as plain prose, no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables — just explain it the way you'd explain it to me in an email.
