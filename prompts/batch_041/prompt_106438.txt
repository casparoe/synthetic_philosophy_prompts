I'm helping to assemble a short reader for a graduate workshop on the political imagination of European integration, and the closing session is on the gap between the Union's self-description and the way it is experienced from the inside. The room will be mixed: lawyers, political theorists, and a few poets who will care about the verse. I would like a poem for that session, written in the voice of a mid-level official in the Commission who is also a citizen of a small member state, someone who drafts implementing measures by day and votes in national elections by night.

The poem should be a terza rima of roughly twenty-one lines, and the interlocking rhyme should do real structural work: each stanza's middle line should rhyme with the first and third lines of the next, so that no voice ever quite closes and the reader is kept moving between two jurisdictions. I want it to turn on a thought experiment I have been calling the two clocks problem: the Union's political time is set by treaty revision, summitry, and the slow accumulation of competences, while national political time is set by elections, referendums, and the memory of lost sovereignty, and no one has ever synchronized the two. The poem should hold the distinction between output legitimacy, where the Union is justified by what it delivers, and input legitimacy, where it is justified by who authorized it, without resolving the tension in favor of either. It should leave the reader with the vertigo of an official who is loyal to two constitutions at once and can never be fully present in either. Please avoid naming specific treaties, institutions, or politicians, and do not let it become a history lesson or a lament about withdrawal. I want someone who knows the democratic-deficit debates to recognize the dilemma as exact, and someone who does not to still feel the clockwork grinding.
