I am applying for a small grant that would buy me six months, and I want to spend it on a question in wild animal welfare that I think has been badly framed. Everyone in this area asks whether nature is, on balance, good or bad for the animals in it, and then asks whether destroying habitat therefore helps or harms them. My half-formed suspicion is that the question only looks answerable because three different quantities are being run together: the welfare of the animals alive in a habitat now, the welfare of the animals who would have lived there had it not been destroyed, and whatever is meant when people talk about the welfare or the health of the ecosystem itself. I also want to press a contrast that I have not seen properly addressed. Take a hectare of forest. Clear it quickly, so that most of its inhabitants die young and violently; or degrade it slowly, so that they live out roughly normal lives but are not replaced by a new generation. Which is worse for wild animals, and does the answer depend on whether those animals' lives are worth living at all?
