I'm putting together questions for a grad-student interview series, and next week's guest is a philosopher whose line on spectrum arguments I want to actually engage with rather than just tee up. The argument as she sets it up: take a long chain where each step is a small improvement on the one before, so each step looks clearly better, yet the far end of the chain is plainly worse than where you started -- so something has to give. Her answer is that nothing gives, because "better than" is a partial relation: transitive, but not holding between every pair, with comparability itself coming in degrees. Her positive proposal is that two options are comparable only when there's a shared scale, which you get either when one option dominates on every dimension or when the gap in stakes clears a threshold she thinks she can specify. She also claims the argument is far less persuasive when you walk the spectrum backwards, and she treats that asymmetry as evidence for the view rather than a presentational artifact. Give me eight to ten questions. I want one on what's left of the transitivity claim once incomparability is allowed (isnt every partial order transitive, so whats the content?), one pushing on whether her threshold is arbitrary and whether it just relocates the sorites into the threshold's own vagueness, one on whether her third category is better described as parity or as incomparability and what actually turns on the difference, one on the backwards-traversal claim (evidence or order effect?), and one on what a rational agent is supposed to do when the options they face aren't comparable, exploitability worry included. Every main question needs a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. No how-did-you-get-into-philosophy stuff.
