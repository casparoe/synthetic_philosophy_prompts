Not a philosopher here, just someone who spends too long staring at my own mind, and I keep bumping into a pattern I don't have a word for. I'd like the name if there is one, but honestly the name matters less to me than the shape.

Here's the thing. Take being in pain, or being sleepy, or being cold. When I'm squarely in one of those states it feels like I couldn't be wrong about it -- how would it even work to be in pain and not know it? But then I look at the edges. There's a level of pain so faint that if you dialed it down one notch I couldn't honestly say whether I was still in pain; yet a little higher up I'm sure. So the state seems to come with a guarantee that I know I'm in it, except the guarantee leaks away exactly at the boundary, which is where I'd actually need it.

Then a second thing that may or may not be the same thing. When I'm confident of some fact, it feels like I must also be confident that I'm confident -- if I really know it, don't I know that I know it? But I can find spots where I'd say I know something, and yet if you ask whether I know that I know, I'd have to stop and check, and the check could go either way.

So: are these one phenomenon or two? Is there a name? And more to the point, what's the real difference between "the state guarantees I can tell I'm in it", "knowing implies knowing that I know", and just plain being unable to be wrong about my own mind? I'd take the distinctions over the label.
