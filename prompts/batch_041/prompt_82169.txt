ok so I've been chewing on this for weeks and I want a consistency check before I say any of it out loud in a seminar. here's where I've landed:

1. I buy the Basic Argument (Galen Strawson's). Roughly: to be truly morally responsible for an action, you'd have to be responsible for the sort of person you are — your character, your reasons, the fact that you weigh things the way you do. To be responsible for that, you'd have to have brought it about by a choice. But any choice you make flows out of the very character you're trying to be responsible for, so either the chain runs back forever or it bottoms out in something you never chose. Either way, no ultimate responsibility. (I know there are replies — that the regress is only vicious if you demand total self-authorship, that it proves too much, etc. I don't think they work, but that's not what I'm asking about.)

2. I think it makes zero difference whether determinism is true. The regress bites just as hard if my choices are genuinely undetermined, so the whole "does physics leave room for free will" thing is a red herring for the responsibility question.

3. So call me a hard incompatibilist: nobody deserves anything in the desert-entailing sense — praise or blame — wether or not determinism holds.

4. But I still think blaming and praising people as a way of shaping them is fine — socializing kids, holding coworkers to norms, the whole machinery of forward-looking accountability — and I'd keep it even if everyone around me accepted (3).

5. And I still think resentment and indignation are sometimes the *fitting* response to someone who's done something awful. Not that they deserve to suffer for it exactly, but that the anger is apt, not merely a fact about my psychology.

6. Lastly, I think if I really internalized (3) it wouldn't change my relationships or my life much at all.

do these hang together? I've got a nagging feeling something's off, but every time I try to pin down which pair is in trouble I talk myself out of it. rank the conflicts by how serious they are, tell me which are only apparent (I bet at least one is), and for the real ones tell me the cheapest fix and what it costs me. and tell me which single commitment is doing the most damage to the rest.
