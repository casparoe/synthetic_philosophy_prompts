So I'm a junior and I'm taking this elective called Science and Society, and we spent basically a whole week on whether species are even real and whether science is "carving nature at its joints" -- our teacher says that phrase is from a Plato dialogue, Phaedrus 265e, which I wrote down in my notes. Then me and my friends got in this huge fight at lunch, because I said "fish" can't be a real category since a lungfish is more closely related to a cow than to a tuna, and my friend Marcus said that's just semantics and there's definately one true answer that science will eventually find. Then our teacher showed us a video about Pluto getting demoted by a vote and I was like, wait, you can't vote on nature. So now I have to write something for the philosophy club zine and I want it to be a parody, because I think that's the only honest way to do it.

What I want: a mock official press release, like a bulletin from some made-up government agency -- I was thinking of calling it the Bureau of Ontological Standards, or the International Commission on the Integrity of Nature's Joints, whatever sounds funniest to you -- announcing the results of its decennial review of the "Official Catalogue of Natural Kinds." Around 600-700 words, deadpan bureaucratic voice the whole way through, with fake docket numbers and a public comment period at the end. The humor has to come from the actual content of the debate and not from wacky jokes, so a philosophy of science grad student reading it should laugh and also feel like I get it. Please have the commission issue rulings on at least four or five things: whether a kind has to have an essence or whether it's fine for a kind to just be a cluster of properties that mostly hang together; whether a kind with vague or gradual boundaries still counts as natural or has to be reclassified as "administrative"; whether the catalogue can include a group like "fish" or "reptile" when it's not a whole branch of the tree of life; something about the case where one old word for a stone turned out to name two totally seperate minerals, and the agency has to decide which one keeps the name; and whether a category that only exists because it's useful to us (think weeds, or pests) gets a natural license or a human-interest license. Bonus points if there's a rule about provisional licenses for kinds that are real but messy, and if the agency cites the precedent of a certain dwarf planet being reclassified by committee vote as an argument for why it's allowed to do this at all.

Two things I really don't want: don't have anyone in the piece step outside the voice to explain the joke or say the objections out loud (no "of course, critics say this is just politics"), and don't make it smug, like the agency is obviously evil -- the funniest version is one where the bureaucrats are perfectly reasonable people who have no idea that anything philosophical is happening. Also no quotes from real texts or real philosophers, since I don't want to get anything wrong, and I don't need a bibliography or anything like that. Just the press release itself, formatted however these things usually look.
