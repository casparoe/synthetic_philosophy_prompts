So I'm writing a novel and one of my characters runs a small foundation — a few million a year, very into being rigorous about it. Their whole thing is they only fund what you can put a number on: deworming, bed nets, a cash-transfer study. Anything where the payoff is "people get a bit more politically engaged" or "the culture shifts a bit" gets turned down with a note saying it isn't cost-effective. They've also got this internal spreadsheet where staff assign "moral weights" — how many dollars of animal welfare equals a dollar of human health, that kind of thing — and then they take the median of everyone's answers and treat that as the real number. Meanwhile the same people, over drinks, will tell you they're deeply uncertain about the weights and would never claim one being's suffering literally counts more than another's.

What I want from you is what the practice actually assumes — not what they say, what the institutions habits commit them to. Does ranking everything by expected good per dollar presuppose that all these goods are commensurable, that there's one scale? Does refusing to fund the unmeasurable presuppose that unmeasurable goods don't count, or just that you can't compare them, or something weaker and more about decision-making under uncertainty? And the median-weight thing — is that a claim that there's a fact of the matter about the exchange rate between a human life and an animal one, or just a coordination device so the spreadsheet works?

And is it even consistent with itself? They say they take moral uncertainty seriously and then fund off a single point estimate. I feel a tension there but I can't name it. Also curious whether the "earning to give" side of this — people taking a high-paying job specifically to donate the salary — assumes something different about what a life is for than the foundation side does, or if it's the same commitment wearing two hats.

One thing I'd ask: tell me which parts of this are settled among people who actually work on it and which are live disagreements. I don't want to put a contested view in my character's mouth as though it were just obviously true. And be straight with me about where the practice is underdetermined — where several different theories would license the exact same behavior.
