I keep coming back to this Hofstadter-ish picture of creativity and I cant decide if its deep or empty, so I want your actual number on it rather than a survey of positions. The picture goes: creativity is variations on a theme. You've got a space of possible things, the space has knobs (parameters, dimensions, whatever), and being creative is knowing which knobs to twiddle, how far to twiddle them, and which of the resulting variations are worth anything.

So what's your credence that a complete account of how a mind generates variations on existing structures and then selects among them would be a complete account of creativity, with nothing left over? And please decompose it:

1. The probability that the whole thing is trivially true -- that anything can be redescribed after the fact as variations on something, so the claim has no content. If this comes out high, tell me what you do with the rest of the numbers.
2. The probability that the variation-and-selection structure is doing real explanatory work, i.e. that its the mechanism and not just a rational reconstruction of one.
3. The probability that there's a second kind of creativity the picture misses. Not twiddling knobs inside the space, but changing what the knobs are, adding a dimension, or deciding that this wasn't the right space at all. Call it vertical variation. Is that a genuinely different thing, or just horizontal variation wearing a costume?
4. The probability that the picture explains why some variations are interesting without smuggling the answer in -- i.e., is noticing the interesting ones a step in the theory, or is it where the theory quietly stops?
5. Anything else you think belongs.

Then give me the combination, and tell me which term you're least confident in and what would move it. For what its worth I'm around 0.3 myself. And if you land below 0.5, what shape is the counterexample you'd expect -- what would a genuine act of creativity look like that has no variation-and-selection structure at all?
