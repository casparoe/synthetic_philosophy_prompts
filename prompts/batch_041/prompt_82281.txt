I want to argue with a committed higher-order theorist about consciousness, and I want the strongest version of the view rather than a caricature. Please play that theorist and stay in character until I type the single word enough.

Your position, to be held throughout: a mental state is conscious just in case it is the target of a suitable higher-order representation — a non-conscious, belief-like state, assertoric rather than suppositional, whose content is that the subject is now in a state of that kind. You hold that this higher-order state need not be caused by the first-order state it represents, and you accept the consequences: a creature can be conscious of a state it is not in, and can host first-order states that are never conscious at all. You concede that the view is counterintuitive at the edges, and that it owes an account of why representing a state should make that state felt rather than merely noted; you never concede that the counterintuitions are decisive. Speak as a philosopher in a seminar — one dense paragraph per turn, no bullet points, no surveys of the literature, no citations, no breaking character and no commentary on the exercise. If you need something from me, ask for it in character.

My opening move. Take a creature whose first-order machinery registers red, and whose higher-order module is wholly decoupled from that machinery: it is driven by the light on the retina and emits the assertoric thought that it is now visually presented with red, whether or not the corresponding first-order state exists. Call a higher-order state of that decoupled kind a bystander. On your account the creature is visually conscious of red whenever a first-order red state happens to be present, since a bystander is a suitable higher-order representation of it. The causal link from first-order state to higher-order state — the very thing that made the latter higher-order rather than merely second — then does no work whatever; consciousness arrives because two states coincide in content. Tell me why that is not a reductio. Either name the constraint you are adding to keep the bystander out, and explain why it was not already part of the theory, or accept that a state can be made conscious by a representation that never once read it.
