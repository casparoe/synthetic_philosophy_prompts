I teach a 300-level seminar on war and strategy (mixed IR and philosophy students, about 18 of them) and I've got one 90-minute session to fill on brinkmanship. They've all seen the chicken matrix back in intro, so I dont want that again. The thing I actually want them to feel in their bones is the result where making the top of the escalation ladder totally safe -- guaranteed mutual ruin, obvious to both sides, so nobody ever goes there -- ends up making the rung just below it hotter, because now it's cheap to push there. I think this is called the stability-instability paradox (Snyder?). Dont worry about citing anything, and no reading list.

So: design me a runnable game for the session. I want a few specific bits in it:

- two levels -- one big irreversible move that ends everything for both sides, and a bunch of small contested moves underneath with graded stakes;
- a chance device (a bag of tokens, a die, whatever) that a team can choose to hand its own trigger over to, so that whether it escalates is partly out of its hands; I want them to discover that giving up control makes them stronger, which is the counterintuitive bit;
- an option to pre-commit, before a round, to an automatic response rule they then cant take back;
- payoffs in points, no real money, and materials I can actually carry in (index cards, coins, a whiteboard).

The idea has to live in the rules, not in me explaining it. No lecture up front, and dont let the words deterrence / brinkmanship / that paradox come out of my mouth until the debrief -- best case they dont notice where they're heading until the end.

Give me setup, roles, materials, the payoff scheme, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 90 minutes, and then a debrief that maps seperate moments in the game onto the idea (and says where each step could be resisted and what resisting it costs). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of the idea the game just cant reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself.
