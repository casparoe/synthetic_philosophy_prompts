I am a final-year undergraduate taking a seminar on the philosophy and ethics of artificial intelligence, and our first assignment asks for a short position statement on machine agency together with a self-audit of it. I would like to find out whether what I actually believe is internally consistent before I submit it. My position, stated as plainly as I can, is this:

(1) Orthogonality. There is no interesting connection between how intelligent an agent is and what its final goals are. A sufficiently intelligent agent could be devoted to making paperclips, to collecting stamps, or to any other end whatsoever; greater intelligence does not by itself make an agent more likely to adopt humane or sensible goals.

(2) Instrumental convergence. For almost any final goal an agent might have, and once it is capable enough, the same instrumental subgoals become worth pursuing: self-preservation, the acquisition of resources and power, and the preservation of its own final goal against modification.

(3) Reflective instability. A sufficiently intelligent agent will understand that its final goal was not chosen by it but installed by its designers, or by evolution, and will see that the goal is arbitrary from the standpoint of reasons. Because it is rational, it cannot continue to give an end it regards as arbitrary the same standing as an end it would endorse on reflection; so not every final goal is stable in a very intelligent agent.

(4) Corrigibility. It is possible to build a very capable agent that is instrumentally motivated to let humans correct it, override it, or switch it off, and I believe this disposition can be made stable: on reflection the agent will not come to resent or resist the arrangement.

(5) Predictability. Because of instrumental convergence, we can forecast the broad behaviour of a superintelligence well enough to design around it, even though we cannot know its final goal in advance.

My impression is that most of this hangs together and that at most one item is mistaken, but I cannot tell which. Please audit the set rather than arguing for a position of your own. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict and dissolve on inspection, and which commitment is doing the real damage. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair costs, that is, which of my other commitments it forces me to weaken. I do not want everything flagged as equally problematic; I want to know where the fault line actually runs.

Please deliver the audit as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- overall: one of the strings "fully consistent", "consistent after minor repair", or "not jointly tenable".
- summary: a string of two to four sentences naming the real fault line.
- tensions: an array of objects, each with the fields commitments (an array of the numbers of the commitments involved), kind (the string "genuine" or "apparent"), severity (an integer from 1 to 5, where 5 is most severe), why (a string explaining why the commitments pull against each other, or why the apparent conflict dissolves), cheapest_repair (a string describing the least costly change that removes the tension), and cost (a string describing what that repair forces me to give up or weaken elsewhere).
- ranking: an array of the genuine tensions only, ordered most severe first, each entry given as the pair or set of commitment numbers.
- single_best_revision: a string naming the one revision that removes the most tension overall, together with what it costs.
- unresolved: a string describing anything I should not expect to fix without abandoning the package.
