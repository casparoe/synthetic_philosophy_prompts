I recently moved into a co-housing building that shares a tool library and a workshop, and I have spent the better part of two months watching my neighbours behave exactly as badly as the theory says they should — the drill nobody returns, the person who waits for someone else to buy the blades, the quiet arms race over who refills the propane. I started reading about the prisoner's dilemma, the iterated version, and the evolution of cooperation mostly so I could stop taking it personally, and I have now reached the stage where I want to laugh about it. So write me jokes. Not a lecture with a punchline stapled to the end, and not generic nerd humour about game theorists being cold — I want the laughs to come out of the actual structure of the problem, so that someone who has genuinely worked through the material can see what the joke is doing. For calibration: I already understand why mutual cooperation is the good outcome and mutual defection the equilibrium; I understand that repetition is supposed to transform the situation, and why a known final round is thought to unravel cooperation all the way back to the first move; I know that error and noise make strict tit-for-tat spiral into feuds that the more forgiving variants avoid, and I have a rough grip on why the shadow of the future does the work it does. Take all of that as given rather than explaining it back to me, and aim at the parts that are genuinely strange — the gap between the backward-induction argument and how people actually play, what noise does to a stable cooperative equilibrium, where the one-shot framing stops being a useful description of a relationship that will continue.

Give me exactly five jokes, as a numbered list, each one or two sentences, with no introduction, no explanation of the theory, and no closing remarks; if a joke needs a footnote to land, discard it and write another. Commit to them — no variants, no asides about which one I will prefer, no hedging. Where the humour has to rest on a particular reading of the material, choose the reading you think is right, make the joke depend on it, and let it stand.
