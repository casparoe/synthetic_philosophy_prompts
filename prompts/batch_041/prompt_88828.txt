I am a third-year PhD student assembling a one-year fellowship application. The deliverable is a prospectus of roughly ten pages plus a plan of work, and I would like you to draft the prospectus. The project is on a puzzle I keep circling without pinning down.

Kavka's toxin puzzle is nearly always discussed as a puzzle about whether it is possible to intend to do something you know you will have no reason to do: the billionaire pays you today for forming the intention to drink the toxin tomorrow, and tomorrow, the money already banked, you have no reason to drink. The familiar moves — that you can intend because the intention itself is the thing being paid for; that you cannot, because intending requires believing you will act; that you can only if you deceive yourself about your future motives — all treat the puzzle as a question about the psychology or metaphysics of intention. My half-formed suspicion is that the puzzle is really a special case of the wrong-kind-of-reason problem: the problem that arises when a consideration counts in favour of a state of mind (a belief, an intention, an emotion) without counting in favour of whatever that state is directed at. If that is right, the interesting question is not whether the toxin case is possible but whether intention, like belief, is governed by a transparency constraint that excludes pragmatic reasons for holding it, and whether that constraint is constitutive of intention or merely a rational ideal that an agent can fail to meet without ceasing to intend.

Please write the prospectus. State the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer — I want to be able to say at the end whether the toxin puzzle does or does not fall under the wrong-kind-of-reason problem, and on which reading of 'a reason for an intention'. Explain why the existing treatments leave that question open rather than settling it. Describe the method: which distinctions I would draw (for instance between reasons that operate on the attitude and reasons that operate on its object, or between reasons for intending and reasons for intending to intend), which cases I would test those distinctions against, and how I could tell whether the transparency constraint is constitutive of intention or a mere rational ideal. Then say what a successful outcome would look like and what would count as the project failing. I do not want a reading list or citations — I will add those myself — but refer to positions and arguments by description wherever they matter.

Please structure the document as follows, with these sections in this order and roughly these lengths: 'The question' (about 150 words); 'Why the puzzle is not yet settled' (about 250 words); 'The approach' (about 350 words); 'What success and failure would look like' (about 200 words); 'Scope for one year' (about 150 words); and a closing section titled 'How this could be wrong' (about 150 words). In that closing section, tell me which argument or consideration would lead me to abandon this framing altogether, and where the proposed line of reasoning is most likely to break down — I would rather learn that now than after a year of work.
