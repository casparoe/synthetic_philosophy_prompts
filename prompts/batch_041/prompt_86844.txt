I'm a software engineer, and before that I was a physics student; I have no formal training in philosophy. Next month I'm giving the toast at my doctoral advisor's retirement dinner. She spent forty years building mathematical frameworks for describing physical systems, one of which she later helped dismantle herself, and for as long as I've known her she has waved the subject away with some version of the thought that the equations were the part that lasted. I want the toast to take that thought seriously rather than repeat it as consolation.

The room: roughly thirty of her colleagues and former students, several of whom will notice immediately if I say something loose about what her work got right or wrong, and her husband, children, and two grandchildren, for whom she is simply the person who took them to the coast. Five minutes. Warm, one or two real laughs, and it has to arrive somewhere honest rather than at the idea that her work lives on in the work of others.

The difficulty, which I think is the whole speech: a friend in my old lab who did read philosophy once told me that saying what survived an overturned theory is its structure is cheap, because unless you specify which relations are supposed to be the survivors, any two things can be found to share some structure, and the claim does no work at all. So I can't simply tell the room that what lasts is the structure. I need a version of that claim that actually says something, and that lets me honor a body of work which was superseded without pretending it was true and without treating it as a noble mistake.

Write the toast, and commit to one reading of what survives theory change: build the whole speech on it rather than handing me a menu of options with the choice left to me. In one sentence, in words I could say out loud to a physicist, tell me what it is I'm claiming lasts, and why the readings you set aside are worse. Then add a brief note after the toast saying where it takes a liberty or leans on a contested reading, so I know what I'm signing.
