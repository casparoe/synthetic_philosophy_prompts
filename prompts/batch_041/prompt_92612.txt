What is your credence that a self, understood as a subject of experience with a sense of diachronic identity, can be realized by a strange loop that is not contained in any single agent but is distributed across a population, where each agent's self-symbol refers to a shared index that refers back to the agent's symbol, so that the loop is closed only by the population as a whole? Decompose the estimate into at least: the probability that Hofstadter's account of the self as a self-referential symbol is correct; the probability that such a symbol can be realized without a continuous physical substrate; the probability that a distributed loop can support one subject of experience rather than merely a collection of agents; the probability that phenomenal consciousness is required for selfhood; and the probability that the loop must be closed in real time rather than only in the abstract. Then give the combined number and identify which term your credence is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most.
