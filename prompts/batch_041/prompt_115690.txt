I've been covering the pragmatism revival for a magazine piece and I keep watching the same fight get reenacted by Peirce people and James people: the Jamesians say the Peirceans have replaced truth with a picture of inquiry nobody's ever lived in, a fated final opinion at the end of an endless road, and the Peirceans say the Jamesians turned truth into whatever happens to comfort you, and always bring up that Peirce renamed his own view just to get out from under the word James had taken over. I want the document these two should have written years ago: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Peircean and a Jamesian about truth. Give me the numbered list of claims both can genuinely sign, not the watered-down overlap — what truth is doing in inquiry, what each side owes the other, whether the two positions are even two positions or one position with different moods. Then a disagreements table: the real cruxes, each one worded the way that side's own advocate would accept as fair, and for every crux the case or arguement both agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about it written down in advance. Two cruxes I want handled explicitly: first, whether "expedient in the way of belief" is indexed to the individual believer here and now or to inquiry as a whole over the long run, and whether the long-run version quietly smuggles in the Peircean picture; second, whether the two views actually give different verdicts on theories that worked and were later thrown out, phlogiston or the ether, and whether a discarded theory was ever true or only ever believed. And if any of this turns out to be a dissagreement over words rather than substance, name which part and why. Don't hedge or hand me a both-sides wash — take a definite position, make the calls, tell me which side you think comes out of this better and why, and defend it. I'd rather have a wrong committed answer than a survey.
