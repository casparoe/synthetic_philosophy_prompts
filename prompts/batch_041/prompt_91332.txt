I'm trying to settle a dispute from my philosophy of mind reading group, and I need the argument adjudicated rather than restated.

A friend claims that the familiar four-grade taxonomy of minds collapses under its own weight. He sets it up as follows. A Darwinian creature learns nothing within its own lifetime; its behavioral repertoire is fixed by natural selection, so trial and error happens only across generations, as lineages are winnowed. A Skinnerian creature learns within its lifetime by operant conditioning, emitting behaviors that are shaped by their consequences, so trial and error happens at the scale of the individual, in the world. A Popperian creature can represent candidate actions or hypotheses internally and test them offline, letting the hypothesis die in its stead, so trial and error happens inside the creature, before it acts. A Gregorian creature bootstraps its own cognition with external tools and culturally transmitted representations—notations, procedures, devices—so that it can improve not just what it knows but how it comes to know it.

His argument runs:

(1) A creature is Gregorian only if it can use a mind-tool to improve its own cognition.

(2) To use a mind-tool competently, a creature must represent how the tool works and anticipate its effect before relying on it. That is precisely the Popperian capacity to test a representation offline. So nothing is Gregorian unless it is already Popperian.

(3) But when a creature uses a mind-tool, the tool carries the cognitive load. Someone doing long division on paper is not performing the arithmetic; the marks and the algorithm do that, and the person merely moves the marks. Remove the props and no residual capacity remains that deserves the name "Gregorian."

(4) So a Gregorian creature is a Popperian creature plus external props, and the props are not part of the creature. A grade defined by its equipment is no grade of mind at all.

(5) The same move recurses. The Popperian's internal representations are themselves props—physical states that the underlying machinery operates on—so a Popperian is a Skinnerian device plus internal props. And the Skinnerian's emitted behaviors are just the phenotypes that selection winnows, so a Skinnerian is a Darwinian system plus within-lifetime variation.

(6) Every grade is therefore the grade below it plus props, and props do not make kinds. There is one kind of mind—a selection machine—wearing different amounts of equipment, and the four-grade taxonomy is a distinction without a difference.

(7) That cannot be the whole story, because the grades earn their keep precisely by predicting qualitative differences: a Popperian can survive a novel environment that would kill a Skinnerian on its first attempt, and a Gregorian can pass an improvement to the next generation without any change in the genome, which a Popperian cannot. If the grades are one kind in different dress, those differences have no explanation.

(8) So some step is defective.

Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost? I would rather keep the taxonomy's explanatory work than the reduction, but I don't want to keep it by fiat.
