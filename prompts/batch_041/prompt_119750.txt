I'm in a grad seminar on consciousness and ethics, and I've somehow become the unofficial referee between two friends who are both panpsychists — they agree, roughly, that experience is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of the physical world, or at least that it's the best bet going, so this isn't a panpsychist-vs-physicalist fight. The fight is about whether that metaphysics has any ethical upshot at all, and after six months of it I'm fairly sure they're talking past each other. Call them the extensionist and the integrationist. The extensionist thinks that if experience goes all the way down then so does the possibility of mattering morally: she wants us to take seriously that fundamental entities, or whatever micro-subjects come along with them, could be subjects of something like suffering, and she thinks the burden of proof sits with anyone drawing a magic line at brains. The integrationist thinks thats a category error — panpsychism is a thesis about the metaphysics of experience, not about welfare, and welfare needs valence plus a unified subject with a stake in how things go (maybe a self-model, maybe preferences), none of which you get for free at the micro level and which the combination problem may not even deliver at the macro level. On his view the ethical upshot of panpsychism is basically nil, or at most a humility constraint on how we do the theorizing.

Could you write the joint statement the two of them could actually both sign? I want (1) a numbered list of agreed claims — about what panpsychism does and doesn't claim, about the combination problem, about the experience/suffering distinction, about what we could even in principle do or not do — (2) a disagreements table where each row states one disagreement in wording the side holding it would accept as fair to its own view, no strawman versions, (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them, and (4) for each crux, a case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Keep it to something they'd actually sign and circulate; no reading list, no citations, we all know the literature.

Two things I want folded in, because they're what made the argument worth having. First, a physicist friend keeps pointing out that the basic objects in fundamental physics are fields rather than little billiard balls and that 'particles' are excitations of fields — so if the extensionist wants micro-subjects, what exactly is the bearer of a micro-experience, and does it have the kind of identity over time a subject needs? Does the answer change the ethics, or is it just a problem for the metaphysics? Second, we got stuck on whether entanglement matters: if two particles are entangled, is there any sense in which they're one subject rather than two, and would that make the number of moral patients theory-dependent — and if so, what follows from that? I'd rather the document admit that neither of them has a worked-out answer than paper over it. And if some part of their fight turns out to be merely verbal — they're using 'suffering' or 'subject' in different senses, say — tell me which part and why.
