Argue for the position that an artifact's politics is a property not of the artifact taken alone but of an artifact-plus-practice pair, and that the correct measure of an artifact's political character is its political inertia: the degree to which its distributive effects persist after the intentions, laws, and habits that produced it have disappeared. On this criterion, an artifact that merely ratifies an exclusion that would have happened anyway is only weakly political, while an artifact that keeps reproducing the exclusion after the exclusionary intent is gone is strongly political. Defend the position against the obvious objection that political inertia is just durability, which would make every long-lived artifact political, and ground the argument in at least one concrete case of your own choosing. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
