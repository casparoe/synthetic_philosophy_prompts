I am trying to work out whether the position I have assembled on scientific realism is a coherent package, and I would like an audit rather than a defense. Here is what I hold. I am a scientific realist in the no-miracles sense: the predictive success of mature science is best explained by its getting something right about a mind-independent world. Methodologically, I think a theory's ontology should be read off its mathematical formalism rather than off intuitions about everyday objects. I take the pessimistic meta-induction seriously: the historical record shows that even spectacularly successful theories posit unobservable entities that are later discarded, so I do not think we are entitled to believe in the intrinsic natures of whatever a theory posits. I therefore accept epistemic structural realism: what survives theory change, and what we may believe, is structure, understood as relations rather than relata. I also accept ontic structural realism: there are no self-subsistent individuals; what exists is structure, and putative objects are positions or nodes within it, individuated solely by their relations. I think the right formal expression of a theory's structural content is its Ramsey sentence, with the theoretical terms existentially quantified away. I identify the retained structure with the theory's symmetry group and its invariants; the survival of the Lorentz group across the transition from the ether to special relativity is my paradigm case. I have also come to think of a theory's ontology as a quotient of its model space by its symmetry group, with object talk amounting to a choice of section through that quotient, so that a change in which objects a theory appears to posit can be a redescription of the same structure. Finally, I take quantum mechanics as evidence for the ontic view: entanglement and the permutation invariance of identical particles show that the world is not, at bottom, a collection of individuals.

I suspect that some of these commitments pull against one another, but I cannot see where, and I do not want a response that treats every pair as equally fraught. Which of them are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what I would lose by making it. Please write in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables; a letter is the right model.
