For the past year I have been attending a reading group on the history of science — I am a retired geologist, and this is my late attempt to acquire some philosophy. Last week's session, on why industrial takeoff occurred in Britain rather than China, produced an argument that outlasted the meeting, and I would like it adjudicated move by move.

Marta, an economic historian, opened: the question is well posed. It is an ordinary contrastive why-question — the fact is Britain's takeoff, the foil is China, and the two form a natural pair because in 1750 they were the two largest agrarian economies. When we ask why a lineage evolved wings rather than fins, she said, no one objects.

Wei, a historian of China, replied that the analogy begs the question. "Britain" and "China" are not the same kind of unit — one a state, the other a vast and internally varied empire — and the outcome is defined by where it happened, so the question quietly assumes that industrial takeoff was the destination and that everyone else merely failed to arrive. Swap the foil for the Netherlands or for Japan and the answer hardly changes, which shows the foil is idle.

Marta granted that the foil is idle but drew the opposite conclusion: an idle contrast means the real question is simply "why Britain?", which is a good and well-posed historical question, so nothing is lost.

Wei answered that if the contrast does no work, then "and not China" is decoration that flatters a story of British exceptionalism, and that the honest formulation is comparative rather than contrastive: trace how several regions' trajectories diverged, without singling out one as the non-arrival.

Marta's last word was that comparison without a foil explains nothing, since one can always find differences; only a contrastive question tells you which differences to look for.

Please rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say what each side gets right, and identify where they are talking past each other. I suspect a real question lurks here about what makes a contrast class legitimate, and I want to know whether "why Britain and not China?" can be repaired or should be replaced. End with a final line of the form Verdict: <Marta, Wei, or neither>, <at most six words>.
