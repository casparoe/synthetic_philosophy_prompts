I've been reading moral psychology for a seminar on the science of morality, and I've talked myself into a view that I'd like you to try to dismantle. The view is this: moral foundations theory, taken at face value, is a symmetric debunking machine. If our moral judgments are largely the outputs of a small set of evolved, affect-laden intuition systems, and if liberals and conservatives simply weight those systems differently -- care and fairness on one side, loyalty, authority, and sanctity on the other -- then the theory explains the disagreement without adjudicating it, and, more strongly, it undercuts the pretension of either side to be tracking moral truth. On this reading the framework is normatively inert but epistemically corrosive: it does not tell us who is right, and it gives us reason to think that the question of who is right is malformed. I also suspect that the popular move of using the theory to scold partisans for moral monism -- for recognizing only a subset of the foundations -- is quietly doing normative work while presenting itself as neutral description.

What I want from you is a map of how exposed this position actually is, and I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they would be rather than merely listed. Consider both the empirical flank and the conceptual one: what would have to be true of the underlying psychology for the symmetric-debunking reading to collapse, and which findings in the moral-psychology literature (including the well-known worries about measurement, factor structure, and WEIRD samples, and any evidence that the foundations come apart from each other in the way the theory needs) would genuinely embarrass it rather than just dent it. Then the harder question: is the position self-undermining? If the debunking argument is itself an intuition-driven product, or if it silently presupposes a Humean is-ought gap that a rival metaethic would reject, does that make it incoherent, or only make it contingent on a background view? I'd like you to distinguish carefully between what a committed moral foundations theorist would themselves concede counts against the symmetric-debunking reading and what only an unsympathetic critic would press, and to tell me honestly whether the reading risks collapsing into unfalsifiability -- and if it does, whether that is fatal or merely the ordinary cost of a philosophical thesis. Where you think some foundations could be functionally or evidentially vindicated over others, say so and say what that would require; where you think the symmetry is robust, say that instead.
