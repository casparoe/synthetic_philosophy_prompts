I've been arguing with a friend for weeks about counterfactual mugging and neither of us is moving an inch, so I want an outside diagnosis rather than another round. The setup we're using: Omega flips a coin. If it comes up tails, he asks you for $100. If it comes up heads, he gives you $10,000 instead — but only if he predicted beforehand that you would have handed over the $100 in the tails case. We've also been playing with a version where the coin is replaced by a fact about pi, so the other branch is a mathematical possibility rather than a physical one. I say you pay, because what's being chosen is a policy, and the policy gets paid in the heads branch; he says the coin already landed, the ten grand is gone, and handing over $100 for nothing is just being a sucker. The only thing we agree on is that if we could pre-commit before the flip we'd both take the deal, which means the entire fight is about what you do when you didn't get to pre-commit. I know the camps here — causal decision theory on his side, updateless stuff on mine — but naming them hasn't helped at all.

Or maybe it's a fight about the word 'rational', or two different questions under one name — what theory should an agent adopt, versus what should this agent do now that his procedure is fixed. Diagnose it, tell me what would count as progress (a theorem, a new case that pulls the theories apart, a distinction, or nothing at all), and say how confident you are and which part you're least sure of.
