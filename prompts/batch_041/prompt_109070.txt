I'm applying for a small internal summer grant (10 weeks, philosophy dept, wants a ~2-page proposal plus a week-by-week plan) and I've got a half-baked idea I'd like you to turn into something submittable.

Topic: Hacking's looping effect and psychiatric classification. The usual story, as I understand it: we slot people under a kind like depression or autism, the label feeds into their self-understanding and into the institutions around them (clinics, schools, insurers, forums), and so the kind changes in response to being classified — which is meant to be why these categories aren't like electron, and maybe why they're not the sort of thing you can just discover the way you discover a natural kind. There seem to be roughly three camps — the "we just haven't found the biomarker yet" realists, the social-construction people, and the in-between view that these are real kinds but of a special interactive sort — and none of them, as far as I can tell, does anything with the thought below, which is my half-baked idea.

I think "the looping effect" is one label doing the work of at least two different loops, and they pull in opposite directions epistemically. (a) A loop through the population: the label changes who ends up in the category — people drift toward fitting it, or actively resist and flee it, or the case-mix shifts for boring institutional reasons. (b) A loop through the criteria: the definition itself gets rewritten in response to the population — researchers, clinicians, insurers, patients and advocates fight about where the line goes, and the line moves. My hunch: (a) is completely compatible with the category tracking something real (the underlying kind can be stable while the people classified move around), whereas (b) is the one that actually threatens there being a determinate thing tracked at all, because the target keeps getting rewritten. But I dont trust the hunch, because maybe every population shift eventually shows up as a criteria shift, in which case the distinction is idle and I've got nothing. And the two loops might run in opposite directions — criteria widen, population dilutes, criteria tighten again, rinse and repeat — and I genuinely dont know whether an oscillation like that is evidence for a real kind with a fuzzy moving boundary, or evidence that the category is a projection.

I want the central question stated sharply enough to have a determinate answer (not "are psychiatric categories real?" — something that could come out yes or no), an explanation of why the existing positions leave it open, what I'd actually do in ten weeks (my guess: conceptual work seperating the two loops, two or three case sketches — depression and autism are the obvious ones, and I'd like a third that's less obvious if you can think of one — and maybe a toy model where criteria and population are two variables and I ask about fixed points vs cycles), why that approach rather than yet another "is it a natural kind" essay or yet another single-case study, what a good outcome looks like, what would count as the thing failing (e.g., if the two channels can't be pulled apart even in principle), and an honest scope call — ten weeks is nothing, tell me what to cut.
