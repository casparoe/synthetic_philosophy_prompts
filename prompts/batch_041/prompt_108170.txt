I am a Bayesian statistician by training, and I build probabilistic graphical models for a living; my working vocabulary is priors and likelihoods, conditional independence, sufficient statistics, filtrations, martingales, posterior predictive distributions, identifiability, and the difference between a model that is well specified and one that merely fits. I have read enough popular economics to know that there is a family of results according to which, if rational agents begin with a common prior and differ only in their private information, then no mutually advantageous trade can occur merely on the basis of that information difference; and I have also encountered the further claim that this result is supposed to illuminate the epistemology of markets, because prices are said to aggregate dispersed private information, yet if prices aggregated it perfectly nobody would be paid for gathering information in the first place. I would like you to restate that entire cluster of ideas -- the no-trade result, the conditions under which it is supposed to hold, and the associated puzzle about informative prices and the incentive to acquire information -- in the vocabulary I actually use. Be as faithful as you can: tell me what the common prior becomes, what private information becomes, what a trade becomes, what the no-trade conclusion becomes, and what role, if any, common knowledge of rationality and of the market structure plays in the translation. Do not smuggle in assumptions from my framework and call them translations; where the statistical vocabulary forces a distortion, say so explicitly.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the economists' claims translate cleanly into statements about conditional distributions, filtrations, and martingales? Which translate only with a distortion -- for example, if the notion of a mutually beneficial trade has no counterpart in a purely probabilistic model, or if equilibrium reasoning cannot be represented by a single Bayesian agent updating on a fixed likelihood? Which parts cannot be expressed at all in my vocabulary: strategic interaction, adverse selection, the endogenous choice to become informed, the distinction between risk sharing and speculation, the possibility of disagreement in higher-order beliefs, the role of noise traders or liquidity demanders? For each residue, tell me whether it is a defect of the statistical framework -- something it cannot represent and should not pretend to -- or a sign that the economic claim is less substantive than it looks. I am especially interested in whether the no-trade theorem is best understood as a theorem about Bayesian updating, a theorem about equilibrium, or a theorem about the limits of representing a market as a single conditional distribution. I do not need citations or a reading list; I want the translation and the residue.
