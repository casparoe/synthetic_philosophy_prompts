Bit of an odd one for you. I write about science and ideas (currently at a magazine, before that a paper), and I'm working up a long piece on targeted killing — drones, the whole thing — and I want to use Anscombe as the spine of it, but not the Wikipedia version. Everyone reaches for her and comes out with "double effect, so you can't target civilians, the end." Her actual argument against Truman is stranger and sharper than that, and I think it has more bite than the version that gets passed around. So what I want from you is a proper workout: one real episode, run through her actual view rather than the gloss.

Take the strike that killed Ayman al-Zawahiri on the balcony in Kabul in the summer of 2022 — one of those missiles, he was the intended target, the US said nobody else died, the family was apparantly in the building. Run it through her account of intention and side-effects as she'd really apply it. First question, and it's the one that decides the rest: who counts as the agent on her view — the operator, the planners, the people who signed off — and does the description of what they did ("killed a terrorist" versus "killed a man on a balcony") do any of the work, or is that just a lawyer's trick?

Then the hinge I keep tripping on. She was never a pacifist — she thought killing enemy combatants in a just war was fine — and her objection to Truman was that the bomb killed the *innocent*. So does her framework even condemn this strike, given who the target was? And if it doesn't, is that her problem or the problem of everyone who cites her? Push on the foreseen part too, the family in the room, because I genuinely can't tell whether on her view that's a side-effect that's permitted or part of the plan.

Last, flip it round. Where does she run out of road? She was writing about a city flattened by one bomb; she's got nothing like a proportionality calculus and I dont think she wants one. So does her framework say anything useful about a one-man strike, or about the gap between what the law calls a legitimate military target and what she'd call murder — or does the episode just show that her account was never built for this kind of war and doesn't have to be?

Keep it concrete. No bibliography, just the argument.
