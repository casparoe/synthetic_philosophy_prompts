Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two researchers who have spent years arguing about the free energy principle and predictive processing. One is a committed proponent who treats the free energy principle as a genuine first-principles unification of self-organization, perception, and action, with predictive processing as its most promising cognitive application; the other is a committed critic who suspects the principle is either unfalsifiable or a formal re-description of ideas already available in Bayesian inference, control theory, and variational methods, dressed in new vocabulary. Assume both are competent, good-faith, and willing to be pinned down.

Produce the document they could both sign. It should contain:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims: about what the free energy principle and predictive processing actually assert at the level of formalism; about which parts are mathematics and which are interpretation; about what the framework does and does not claim to explain; about the relation between the principle as a normative or teleological statement and predictive processing as a hypothesis about processing; and about what would count as a fair test of either. Each claim should do real work — a formulation both sides would regard as a concession or a substantive commitment, not a platitude either could shrug off.

2. A disagreements table with, for each row: the point at issue; the proponent's position in wording the proponent would accept; the critic's position in wording the critic would accept; the crux, meaning the sub-question that, if settled, would move at least one side; and, for that crux, a concrete case, argument, or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, together with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance.

Consider at least these candidate cruxes, though you may revise or replace them: whether the Markov-blanket formalism presupposes the very system boundaries it purports to explain, or whether it genuinely derives them; whether the dark-room or sensory-deprivation problem is a real prediction of the framework or an artifact of idealization; whether expected free energy adds empirical content beyond a relabelling of reward-seeking and information-seeking; whether the principle is falsifiable at all, and if it is not, whether that is a vice for a framework of this scope; and whether predictive processing makes commitments about neural implementation that could come apart from the free energy principle.

If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal — the two camps attaching different senses to the same term — say which part and why, and flag any places where the positions differ only in emphasis rather than in incompatible claims.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Where you record an agreed claim or a prediction, state how confident each side should be in it.
