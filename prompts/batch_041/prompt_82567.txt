My old supervisor is a Scanlonian — for her an act is wrong when no principle that permitted it could be one nobody could reasonably reject — and she's always insisted you can't just tot up complaints across people. She's been giving verdicts on cases in our reading group for about a year and I want to know what she'll say about one she hasnt heard yet.

Her verdicts so far: save five strangers rather than one, all six facing the same death — she says save the five, and she won't call this "adding up," because the one's complaint and theirs are the same complaint, there are just more of them. Everyone pays five dollars a year into a fund that buys treatment for thirty people with a rare disease — permissible. Kill one healthy man and use his organs to save five — impermissible, because his complaint is that he's being used and that isn't the same kind of complaint as the five's. Let a factory expose five hundred people living nearby to a small lifelong health risk so the region gets cheap power — impermissible. Make one man sit through a boring two-hour meeting so that ten thousand people each avoid a boring two-hour meeting — permissible, and here she says the numbers are doing real work. Everyone bears a one-in-a-hundred-thousand chance of a serious side effect so that a hundred people don't die — permissible.

New case: a rule that would draft three hundred people, chosen by lottery, into two years of public service — nothing dangerous, just two years of their lives, which they'd rather spend on their own — staffing a program that will save five thousand lives.

Predict her verdict, tell me which rules fit her track record so far, what each of those rules predicts here, which one you'd bet she's actually running and why, and what case we should put to her next to tell those rules apart. Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up.
