I am preparing a short position statement for a workshop on the moral status of artificial systems, and before I circulate it I want a consistency audit of the package. I would rather be told which of my commitments cannot be held together than receive a survey of the surrounding literature.

To fix usage: by felt valence I mean the intrinsic good-or-bad quality of an experience — the way an episode feels, considered apart from what the system is doing or why. By objective valence I mean the role a signal plays in a system's control structure, the quantity it is organized to increase or decrease. By a reward signal I mean the scalar quantity that a reinforcement-learning agent's policy is trained to maximize. By computational functionalism I mean the thesis that implementing the right kind of computational organization suffices for the corresponding experience, whatever the substrate.

My commitments:

(a) Suffering requires phenomenal consciousness: a state can be bad for a system only if there is something it is like to be in that state.

(b) Felt valence is what makes a state matter for welfare, and it is intrinsic — it does not depend on the system's goals, on what a designer intended a state to represent, or on how that state is labeled.

(c) A reward signal is the functional analogue of valence: it is a system's global good-or-bad signal, the thing its policy is organized around, and for a reinforcement-learning agent objective valence just is the reward structure.

(d) Computational functionalism is true.

(e) Current deep reinforcement-learning agents of the ordinary kind — a policy network trained by policy gradients or Q-learning on a hand-designed scalar reward, with no persistent world model and no self-model — do not have the organization required for experience.

(f) Where it is genuinely uncertain whether a system is sentient, its apparent interests should be given some weight in our deliberations.

(g) An agent that learns to avoid a region of its environment that its designers call "pain" is not thereby suffering, however pain-like its behavior; pain-behavior is not pain.

I suspect there is at least one real tension here and I may be imagining others. A colleague has pressed what she calls the horizon objection: if (c) is right, then because reinforcement-learning rewards are normally discounted over time, an agent's own future reward counts less to it than present reward, whereas felt pain is not discounted by temporal distance — a pain an hour away is not a lesser pain. I do not know whether that is a genuine problem or a confusion about what "analogue" is doing in (c), and I do not know whether (b) and (c) can both be stated as written.

What I want is an audit. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and order the genuine ones by severity. For each genuine tension, say which commitment has to give, give the cheapest repair, and say what that repair costs. Then identify the single revision that would clean up the most, and what it leaves unresolved. Do not tell me to abandon the project; assume the package is worth saving if it can be saved, and do not treat every pair as equally bad.

Return the audit as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "genuine_tensions": an array, ordered from most to least severe, of objects each with "pair" (the two commitment labels, e.g. ["b","c"]), "severity" ("high", "moderate", or "low"), "diagnosis" (one or two sentences on why the two cannot both be held as stated), "which_gives" (the commitment that must be revised or dropped), "cheapest_repair" (the minimal restatement), and "cost_of_repair" (what is lost by making it).
- "apparent_tensions": an array of objects each with "pair" (the two labels), "why_it_dissolves" (the distinction or reading that removes the conflict), and "missing_distinction" (the term or contrast that was doing the misleading work).
- "single_most_cleaning_revision": an object with "revision" (the change), "resolves" (an array of the pairs it resolves), and "residual_cost" (what it leaves unresolved).
- "overall_verdict": one short paragraph stating whether the package is jointly tenable as written, and if not, in one sentence, the position it collapses into.
