I've been playing alot of games lately and I keep noticing I have moral-ish reactions to the characters in them, and I want to know if there's a principle behind my reactions or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Here are my gut verdicts:

- The weighted companion cube in Portal. You have to incinerate it to finish the level. I felt genuinely bad, and I still think there's something a little wrong with doing it, even though it's a cube with a heart painted on it and nothing else.
- The nameless guards in a stealth game that I knock out and pile in a closet. No reaction at all. Not wrong.
- A party member in an RPG who dies for good and I can't save them. Not wrong — nobody did anything wrong — but I grieved, and I think the grief was appropriate, not just a trick the game played on me.
- A character with a full backstory, voice acting, and a family, who I kill on purpose just to see what happens, and the game has unique dialogue for it because it expects some players to do that. I feel like I did something small and real. But I also think it was fine, because the game built the door and put a sign on it.
- A procedural NPC — the game generates the villagers randomly, and every player gets different ones. I care about them less, and I think caring less is correct, not just a failure of imagination. My friend says that's speciesist-in-spirit and I half believe her.
- A save file I've had for 200 hours, where the character is basically me. Deleting it feels like a small death. But if I delete a stranger's identical save file, nothing. So it isn't about the character, it's about the history?
- A game where you play as a real historical victim of a real atrocity. Everything feels heavier, and I think treating the character as a toy here is actually wrong in a way the other cases aren't.

And the one I keep flip-flopping on: an accessibility setting that lets you skip a character's death scene. I feel like using it is cheating them somehow, which is nuts, because it's a menu option and the scene is still there in the code.

So: is there a principle that gets most of these? I notice "does it have a mind" doesn't do the work — the cube has none and the procedural villager has some, but I care more about the cube. And "is it real" doesn't either, since none of them are. What seems to track is something like: does the game treat it as mattering, does the fiction earn it. But that makes it sound like I'm just rewarding good writing, and the historical-victim case feels different from that, more like respect than aesthetics.

I want you to test that hunch against the cases and repair it if it breaks, or replace it. Tell me what the resulting principle says about a case I havent listed, and which of my verdicts it forces me to give up. If my verdicts just don't cohere and there's no principle, tell me straight — don't invent one to make me look consistent. And if the whole question is confused, like if "mattering morally" is secretly doing two different jobs here (whether it's wrong to do X to it, versus whether it's fitting to feel Y about it), say so plainly, because I suspect that might be the real issue and I keep sliding between the two.
