I'm trying to settle an argument with a friend and I need a case built to order, so bear with the specs.

Set up two systems — call them whatever you want — that are identical in every way an outside observer could check. Same substrate (make them both silicon, or both the same one thing; the point is to hold that fixed), same size, same input-output behaviour, and they keep behaving identically for as long as you run them. The only difference is the causal wiring inside. In system A, the states that stand in for the system's own condition — its analog of a hurt state, if you want to call it that — get fed back into later processing and keep shaping what it does next. In system B, those same states get computed, used once to produce the immediate output, and thrown away, with no re-entry. Neither one is allowed to be a lookup table or a precomputed answer key: both have to actually compute in real time, and both have to be small enough that a lab could plausibly build one, so no dodge where the table is the size of the universe. And please dont hand me the Chinese room or a zombie case, I've heard those and they dont isolate anything.

Then do the bookkeeping. What does a pure input-output functionalist have to say about the moral status of A and B, and why? What does someone who thinks the causal role of the internal states is what matters say, and why? Give me a third system, C, that both of them classify the same way, so I can see the case isn't just tracking complexity or size. Keep the scenario itself to a couple of paragraphs before the analysis.

Tell me honestly which feature is doing the work — is it the recurrence, or is it something else I snuck in without noticing — and what happens if I remove it: if I make B's states loop back but the loops never change anything downstream, does the gap close or reopen? What if I strip the recurrence out of A and give it a big feedforward pipeline instead? Finish with the best move for the functionalist, i.e. the strongest way to say the gap was never real to begin with.

This is for an argument with a friend who thinks behaviour is all there is to go on, so the case has to make the internal difference carry moral weight on its own, without me just stipulating that one of them is conscious.
