Me and my friend got into it for like two hours last night about Confucian role ethics — he's been reading about it and kept telling me the thing I was trying to describe is just "special obligations to particular people," or just what Confucians call filial piety, and I don't think either of those is what I'm pointing at, but I also can't name it, which is sort of the problem.

Here's the case that started it. My uncle moved my grandmother into a care home. He visits twice a week, pays for everything, the care is definately better than anything he could have done at home. My dad says he's done right by her. My uncle says no — the point was that it should have been him. Not because he'd do it better, not because he wants to be the sort of son who does it, and not because she'd be owed anything different. And the strange part is that the easier it gets for anyone else to do it — better facilities, paid aides — the more it seems to be specifically his.

Then a friend of mine sat with a neighbor she barely knew through the last month of his life, because she was the only one who would. There's no role there at all, no institution, they weren't family, and she describes it exactly the same way: it had to be her, and she's still not done, two years after he died. Nobody she wronged by stopping, nobody who could have released her from it.

So is there a name for this? I know it's near special obligations, and near role ethics, and somebody said agent-regret, but (a) it doesn't seem to be owed to anyone, so there's no claim anyone holds or can waive, (b) it has no completion condition — you can't discharge it, you can only keep at it, and (c) the second case has no role whatsoever, so role ethics only gets it by stretching. What I want is the carving, not the label: what this actually is, which established concept comes nearest and exactly where it comes apart from it, where the edges are (does it survive the other person's death? could you have it toward someone you've never met? is it ever transferable?), and if there's no name, coin one and define it well enough that I can tell a case of it from a case of ordinary obligation. No reading list please, I just want the concept sorted. End with a line like \answer{term — one-sentence definition}.
