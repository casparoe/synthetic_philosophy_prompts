The rabbit hole: "acausal threats" and "surrogate goals." Rough gist in case these aren't the words you'd reach for — an acausal threat is a threat from someone who can't actually touch you. No causal channel, nothing to trace back. They just model you accurately, or they've got a simulation of you, and they're betting your decision procedure makes you flinch. And a surrogate goal is the defence: you bolt a decoy goal onto your utility function, something that basically never comes up in ordinary life — "nobody ever melts down a left-handed screwdriver," say — and you weight the decoy *more* heavily than the things you actually care about. Then when a blackmailer shows up, the cheapest threat available to them is a threat against the decoy instead of against your kids. It doesn't stop you getting shaken down. You still hand over the money, because you really do care about the screwdrivers now. It just means the worst case of a threat is a pile of melted screwdrivers rather than something unthinkable. The stuff I've been reading also frets about whether the decoy should be dormant — switched on only by an actual threat — so it doesn't wreck your normal life, about whether it's orthogonal to your real goals (a decoy like "prevent murder" is a bad decoy, since one threat can hit both targets), and about order of operations: if you self-modify all at once to have a decoy and to never give in, the blackmailer just reads you as someone who never gives in, and you've gained nothing.

The humour has to come from the mechanics being right — orthogonality, credibility, the blackmailer needing to know what you care about, dormant vs. always-on decoys, the bit where you still pay.
