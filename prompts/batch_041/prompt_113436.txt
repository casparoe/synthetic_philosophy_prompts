I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have arrived, mostly by intuition about how information gets processed, at something close to Dennett's multiple drafts model of consciousness. The picture I hold is this: there is no single place in the brain where the contents of experience are assembled and shown to a witness, and no single moment at which a content crosses a line and becomes conscious. Contents are processed in parallel, in many places, over many time scales; what we call the stream of experience is a narrative reconstruction, and questions about the precise order in which two contents entered experience may have no fact of the matter beyond what a given probe or report elicits. In my own idiom: the mind is a multi-master replicated store with no global sequencer, and the apparent total order is reconstructed per reader at read time. If I have the view wrong in some way that matters, correct me; I am using the vocabulary loosely.

I want you to tell me what would count against this. I am not looking for a survey of the literature and I cannot look anything up, so skip the reading list; I want a map of the position's exposure. Please work through the following.

1. Which empirical findings would count as evidence for a genuine convergence point, a place or stage where contents are assembled into a determinate, single, reportable state? How should I weigh such findings against the reply that any such point is a workspace rather than a theater, that is, a bottleneck rather than a privileged witness? I suspect that reply can absorb nearly any neuroscience I could imagine, and I want you to say whether that is a strength or an evasion.

2. Take seriously the possibility of a discriminating result. Suppose a compact set of neurons were found such that their activity pattern predicts reportable content, driving them inserts that content, silencing them removes it, and a decoder trained on their activity reads out the experienced order of two near-simultaneous events before any report is made. Would that falsify the model, or would the defender simply say the drafts had already settled by then? What would have to be true of the experiment for it to be decisive rather than absorbable?

3. The distinction between a state's being experienced and its being available for report, reasoning, and control. If there are states that are genuinely experienced but never available to any of these, the identification of consciousness with wide influence fails. Is that a coherent possibility or an unverifiable one? Does the answer bear on whether the multiple drafts model is a theory of consciousness at all, as opposed to a theory of report or of global broadcast?

4. Conceptual defeaters. A rival may object that the model is eliminativist in disguise, denying the very datum it was meant to explain, or that it has quietly swapped the question of what an experience is like for the question of what a system can do with it. Do such objections count as evidence against the view, or do they only register a disagreement about what needed explaining? Which of them should a defender concede?

5. Insulation. Two readings seem available: a strong one, on which there is no fact of the matter about the order or content of experience even after the system has settled, and a weak one, on which there is no single locus or finish line but contents become fully determinate once stabilized. Which reading is the position actually committed to, and are there defeaters for one that are not defeaters for the other? If the strong reading is the intended one, what could possibly count against it?

Rank the candidate defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, mark each as empirical, conceptual, or methodological, and separate those the view's own defenders would accept as counting against it from those they would rule out by definition. Say plainly whether the view is at risk of unfalsifiability, and if it is, whether that is a defect here or an artifact of the subject matter. Also say what, if anything, the model predicts that a theater view does not; if the answer is nothing, say that too.

One caveat about me: I find the no-global-sequencer picture independently plausible for engineering reasons, so I may be rationalizing a prior. Tell me where that prior is doing the work.

Finally, tell me what would change your own verdict: which single argument or result would move you to say the model is in serious trouble, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
