I serve on the board of a small neighborhood tool library. One of our most active volunteers, who runs the Saturday repair clinic and is the only person who can maintain our 3D printer, has been accused by another volunteer of a serious verbal assault at a private party two years ago. He denies it; she has stopped coming; neither wants a formal process, and there is no police report. Some board members want to bar him from the library entirely. Others say the library is not a court, that all we can do is choose whom we associate with, and that barring him would be doing something to him as a response to a wrong, which we have no standing to do. Our bylaws let us exclude a member by a two-thirds vote for "conduct harmful to the community," but no one knows what that was meant to cover. I have to vote on Thursday. How should I think about the difference between deciding who we want around and responding to a wrong, and what would a fair decision look like here?
