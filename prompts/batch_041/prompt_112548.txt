My housemate is a metaphysics grad student who works on powers and dispositions, and I've somehow ended up reading al-Ghazālī's Tahāfut al-Falāsifa for fun — it started as a joke about God making the coffee, and I got into it while trying to work out whether anything I do actually causes anything, which is a whole other thing. The bit that got me is the fire-and-cotton stuff: the connection between fire and burning isn't neccessary, fire is just the occasion, and God is the one doing the burning, at every instant, recreating the world moment to moment. My housemate thinks that's insane and that properties just have their powers, so the necessity is sitting right there in the nature of the property and no divine will is needed anywhere. We had a two-hour kitchen fight about it and I want to write the document we should have written instead.

Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the two of us, i.e. between (a) a Ghazālī-style occasionalist who fully accepts science — she thinks the laws of nature are just the regular pattern in which God acts, and that no created thing has any causal power of its own — and (b) a dispositional essentialist who thinks properties have their causal powers essentially. Neither is a strawman: the occasionalist isn't a science-denier, and the dispositionalist isn't a naive regularity theorist (both think the plain "laws are just regularities" view is dead — tell me if I'm wrong that that's already common ground).

I want:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign. Include what each of them thinks a causal explanation is doing, and at least one claim that actually risks something, not just "we both like science." If a claim is one-sided, it doesn't go in this list.

2. A disagreements table: the disagreement; then each side's position written in that side's own voice; then a column flagging whether it's a real disagreement or a verbal one.

3. The cruxes, each one a sub-question where settling it would move at least one side. At minimum cover these two, and add any you find:
   - the half-life atom: one radioactive atom with a half-life, nothing else around. Both of them should accept that this bears on the dispute (if not, say why). What does each predict about whether there's a fact now about whether this atom decays in the next minute, and about whether the propensity is a real feature of the atom or just a fact about the pattern of God's action?
   - the dormant power: a universe containing exactly one object with a determinate power that is never triggered, because the universe ends first. Does the power exist? Does it explain anything?
   - the kasb wrinkle: the classical Ashʿarite line is that God creates in a person a power for the specific act (acquisition), so human action is neither purely God's nor purely the person's. Does that make the occasionalist a closet dispositionalist, or is a created, momentary, act-specific power doing no work at all?

4. For each crux: the case or arguement both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance. Then say which crux would actually change someone's mind and which is a dead end.

5. Finally, say which part of the whole dispute is merely verbal — e.g. whether "God's faithful action" and "the nature of the property" are picking out the same explanatory role under different descriptions — and why.

Keep it tight, under about a thousand words. No citations or reading lists, since I can't check them and I don't want guesses, and don't lean on the names of particular living philosophers.
