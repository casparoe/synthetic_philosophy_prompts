I am preparing a proposal for a one-semester term paper of about 6,000 words on advance directives, and I need help turning a half-formed idea into a plan. The idea is this: disputes about whether an advance directive can bind a later self who no longer endorses it are almost always staged as a contest between the earlier self's autonomy and the later self's present interests, and I suspect that staging is why they stall — what is actually doing the work is a default rule about which party must justify departing from the status quo, and the two familiar positions disagree about where that default lies without ever arguing the point. Turn this into a proposal that states one question sharply enough to have a determinate answer (my attempt: is the authority of a directive content-independent, resting on the earlier self's say-so, or content-dependent, resting on its fidelity to the later self's will or interests, and can either account accommodate a later self who dissents coherently?); explains why the standing positions leave that question open rather than settling it; specifies the method — what I would argue, in what order, and why that route rather than the obvious appeals to personal identity or to a pre-commitment contract; says what a successful outcome would look like and what would count as failure, or as the project collapsing back into a familiar position; and gives a realistic scope for one semester, including what would have to be cut. Use the asymmetry between directives that refuse treatment and directives that demand it as the test case. I will supply the citations myself, so do not include any.
