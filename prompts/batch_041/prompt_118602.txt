Ok so this is a thing my meetup group re-litigates every few months and I want to actually get it down on paper, because I keep nodding along and I'm no longer sure I should be. It's about coherent extrapolated volition — the proposal where instead of programming in a fixed goal, you point the AI at what we'd want if we knew more, thought faster, were more the kind of people we'd like to be, and had grown up together longer (thats the rough gloss, anyway). Whenever it comes up, someone in the group reaches for the same picture and everyone relaxes: it's a limit. You take current volitions as the starting point, you iterate the idealization step — more info, more reflection, more coherence, more time — and the extrapolated volition is whatever that process converges to. A friend called it "Newtons method on your value function" and got approving nods. I've been nodding too. But I think the picture is carrying a lot of weight that nobody's checked it can bear, and I'd like you to do the audit.

Give me the mapping first — what plays the role of the initial point, the iteration step, the distance metric or topology that makes "convergence" even meaningful, the limit itself, and what the sequence is supposed to be indexed by. Then the places it breaks. Two that bother me: (a) there isn't one idealization operator, there are several — make us more informed, make us more internally consistent, make us more compassionate, give us more shared history — and theres no reason they commute, so like the classic analysis example where you take the limit in x first and get one answer and in y first and get another, the order of idealization could change where you land, which would gut the "one determinate answer" part; (b) limits fail to exist, or exist but depend on where you started, and if two people (or two species) start in different basins they might converge to different limits, in which case the "coherent" bit is doing aggregation work the limit picture quietly hides. A third, weirder one I want you to take seriously: in physics the interesting limits (thermodynamic, continuum) have features that no finite stage of the sequence has — phase transitions, sharp behavior — so maybe the extrapolated volition is a genuinely emergent thing rather than "your values with the noise scrubbed off", and I can't tell if that helps the view or wrecks it. Also please say whether the whole picture is a frictionless-plane-style useful fiction, and whether CEV is better modeled some other way entirely (an ideal advisor you consult counterfactually, no iteration at all; or a fixed point of reflective equilibrium rather than a limit).

The reason this matters is that the analogy is being used to license a specific conclusion: that there is a fact of the matter about what we really want, that it's continuous with what we already want, and that an AI could be aimed at it without us having to specify it up front. Tell me which disanalogies matter for that use and which are just noise, and whether the conclusion survives once they're on the table — and if the honest answer is that a better analogy exists, give me that instead. No citations needed, I just want the argument.
