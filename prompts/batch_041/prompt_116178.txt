okay so my friend Priya has gotten way too into thought experiments and she's decided to run one on me for real. Her deal: if by midnight tonight I sincerely intend to eat a whole raw habanero first thing tomorrow morning, she Venmos me $400 tonight. That's the whole thing — the money is for the intention, not the pepper. Tomorrow I either eat the pepper and spend an hour regretting my life, or I skip it and keep the cash anyway; actually eating it earns me nothing extra. She calls it Kavka's toxin puzzle on a smaller budget (the original has a billionaire, a toxin that lays you up for a day, and a machine that can read your intention, so let's just grant that she can read mine).

Here's my problem though: I cant actually intend it. Tomorrow-me is sitting there holding $400 with zero reason to go anywhere near the pepper, and I can't sincerely intend to do something I know I'll talk myself out of. It's not that I'd be lying to her — it's that intending doesnt seem to be the kind of thing I can just decide to do on command. Priya says that's the point, and that the only people who can collect are the ones who'd eat the pepper even without the money, which doesn't feel like a solution so much as her pointing at a different, more committed person and saying "be him."

I want to write a joke about this whole mess, and I want it to be a real joke about the structure: the reward being attached to the intention rather than the act, the fact that you can't will an intention into existence, the guaranteed defection tomorrow, maybe the bit about only the already-committed being able to collect. Not a generic "lol philosophers" thing. It should land for someone who already knows the puzzle, so the punchline has to turn on how the thing actually works. If the joke needs one line of explanation to show the machinery, throw that in. American English please.
