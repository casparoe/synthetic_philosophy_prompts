My flatmate is doing a political theory masters and he's spent the whole week cornering me with an argument about whether a state can genuinely apologize for a historical wrong. It goes like this. (1) To apologize is to take responsibility for a wrong you did; you can't apologize for something you didn't do, you can only express regret about it. (2) An apology is only real if it comes with actual remorse -- the words on their own, from someone who feels nothing, are just apology-shaped noise. (3) The wrongs in question were done by particular people: officials, soldiers, settlers, voters, a parliament. (4) Those people are mostly dead, and the people alive now -- including the ministers doing the apologizing -- didn't do it and mostly don't feel remorse for it. (5) And an apology isn't really complete until the wronged party recieves it and accepts it, which here is impossible because the wronged are dead too. (6) So no state apology for a historical wrong can be genuine; at best it's a gesture aimed at the living. He thinks this shows the whole practice is a confused bit of theater and we should stop pretending otherwise.

Here's my problem: I nod along to every step, but the conclusion seems to prove way too much. If you can only apologize for what you personally did, and only when you feel real remorse, then a state also can't sign a treaty that binds its citizens fifty years later, can't owe a national debt, can't be guilty of a crime, arguably can't even declare war -- all of those seem to need the state to be one agent that persists and has attitudes across generations, which by his lights it isn't. Nobody thinks treaties and debts are impossible, so something's off. But I don't want to give up the remorse point (I think he's right that a hollow apology is worthless) and I don't want to give up the point about having to be the one who did it either, because that seems to be the whole reason apologies mean anything.

So where does it actually go wrong -- which step do I drop, why did it look so obviously right, and what does dropping it cost me? Does it matter if the wrong is recent enough that some of the perpetrators and victims are still alive? And is there a reading of 'genuine' under which the state apology survives without my having to pretend that a state literally feels sorry?
