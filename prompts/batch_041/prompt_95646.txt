My GP says: there's a full physical story about my brain and body at every moment — neurons, cortisol, the lot — and that story on its own is enough to explain why I don't sleep. My therapist says that's rubbish: when the anxiety drops I sleep, when it spikes I don't. Turn one dial and the other moves — that's causation, end of story.

I can't work out whether these are just two ways of saying the same thing or whether one of them has to be wrong, and I'd rather not be fobbed off with "they're both useful perspectives". Can both claims be literally true at once, and if so how? If not, which one goes? And is there anything that could actually settle it — an observation, an experiment, anything — or is it all decided by how you set the question up? My therapist's dial-turning test sounds convincing until I notice I can never turn the anxiety dial while holding the brain dial still, and then I've no idea what it's testing.
