Two of my coursemates on my practical reason module got into it in the pub last night about our mate Sam, who's decided he wants a kid and is trying to work out whether that's even a sensible thing to want. Rhea says you can't reason your way into it at all: you can't know what it's like to be a parent until you already are one, and by then your preferences have changed, so your present self is just guessing about the values of a stranger. She says it's a leap, not a decision, and dressing it up in expected-value language is a category error.

Dan says that's definately too strong. You can reason about it fine — from what people report, from whether you'd regret not doing it, from whether you want to be the kind of person who wants it, even from how much you trust your own decision procedure. Rhea comes back that the reports are worthless, because they come from people who've already been transformed: asking a parent whether parenting was worth it is like asking a convert whether the conversion was worth it — they're judging with exactly the values that are in question, so its circular. Dan says it isn't circular, it's just evidence from another vantage point, and if the regret reports go the other way those count too; and anyway by her logic nobody could rationally move abroad or fall in love, which is absurd. Rhea says well nobody can, not in the strict sense, and that the standard of rationality just has to be weaker here — not maximise expected value but "choose the transformation you can own". Dan says "own" is doing all the work there and you can own anything.

Neither will budge and I said I'd ask someone to settle it. Can you go through their exchange move by move, say who's right about which bits, rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and tell me what they're both missing?
