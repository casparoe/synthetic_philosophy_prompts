In a seminar on the longue durée, a colleague cut off a dispute about why a particular grain-exporting city-state adopted a monopoly in one decade rather than another by saying, flatly, "geography is destiny." I want to know what that sentence could actually be claiming before I decide whether it does any work there. Lay out the distinct precise theses it might express—from the nearly trivial (geography is one background condition among many) through the substantive (geographic features fix the feasible set of polities, set transition probabilities between institutional forms, or screen off event-level explanations over long horizons) to the radical (no counterfactual history at that location is possible, or individual agency is always epiphenomenal). For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a defensible empirical hypothesis, or false as stated; identify what kind of "destiny" it invokes (necessity, probability, constraint, path dependence, or mere correlation); and say which reading would actually license the dismissal of the monopoly-decade explanation. Add the reading you think is usually meant in this context, and note which readings collapse once the time scale is specified—event, conjuncture, or longue durée. The answer should be a menu with verdicts, not a single interpretation.
