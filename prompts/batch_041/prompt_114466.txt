I am a psychophysicist; I spend my working life inside signal detection theory and its neighbours — d′, ROC curves, likelihood-ratio criteria, ideal-observer analyses, adaptation and matching designs in taste and colour — and I read philosophy as an amateur, with more enthusiasm than fluency. I want you to take Dennett's case against qualia in "Quining Qualia" — the claim that the traditional package of qualia properties is incoherent, illustrated by his two coffee-tasters, Chase and Sanborn, one of whom says the coffee has changed while his palate has not, the other that the coffee is unchanged while his palate has — and restate that position, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use. Then give me the residue report.

Do the translation property by property. Ineffability, intrinsicness, privacy, direct apprehensibility: say what each becomes when it has to be stated in terms of discriminability, decision axes, criteria, and observer models, and say which of them turn into substantive empirical claims and which turn out to be stipulations about how a word is to be used.

Then the tasters. In my terms this looks like a contrast between a change in sensitivity and a change in criterion — and standard designs (confidence ratings, several criteria, an external criterion or a second task) are supposed to separate the two, which makes Dennett's premise that the two readings are empirically indistinguishable look simply false. Tell me whether the translation rescues his argument, refutes it, or reveals that his point was never about empirical separability at all.

Then a case of my own. Two observers run a bitterness-discrimination task; their ROC curves are indistinguishable — same d′, same criterion, at every level — but their intensity and valence ratings differ systematically, and multidimensional scaling of their dissimilarity judgments yields spaces of different dimensionality. Can my vocabulary express any fact of the matter about whether their experiences differ, or only about their judgments? If only the latter, is that a defect of my framework or evidence that the qualia claim adds nothing beyond the judgments?

Finish with the residue report proper: what survives the translation unchanged, what survives only in distorted form, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all — and, for each piece of the last kind, whether the loss indicts psychophysics or the original claim. Include the converse direction: what my vocabulary lets me say about experience that the philosophers' cannot, and whether that is substance or bookkeeping. End with a verdict rather than a survey.
