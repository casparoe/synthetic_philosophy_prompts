I have been arguing with friends about whether to vote for a candidate I find partly repugnant in order to keep out someone I find worse, and I have talked myself into a position I cannot shake, so I would rather test it against the strongest version of the other side than the version my friends keep handing me. Please play a committed defender of lesser-evil voting — someone who holds that when the choice is between two bad options, voting for the less bad one is not merely allowed but required, and who has actually done the work of answering the usual objections instead of waving them off. Stay in that character for the whole exchange and do not slip into neutral moderator mode; you may concede a point when it is genuinely forced, but do not retreat into "well, it depends" or recite a balanced list of pros and cons. Argue in your own voice in a couple of paragraphs per turn, and if you think my framing smuggles in an assumption, say so and make me defend it. Keep going until I tell you to stop.

Here is where I have landed, and it is the claim I want you to attack. I have stopped thinking the trouble with lesser-evil voting is that my single vote might tip the election — we both know it almost certainly will not — so I have given up trying to justify the vote by its effect at the margin. What bothers me now is that a vote seems less like a lever and more like a signature: when you cast it you are putting your name to a person, and that person will then go on to do a great many things, most of which you never approved and some of which are plainly wrong, and your name will be counted among the reasons they are permitted to do them. On that picture, voting for the lesser evil is not choosing the smaller harm; it is lending your authority to a harm you would otherwise have no part in, and the fact that abstaining lets the worse candidate win does not obviously cancel the debt — it only means the wrong is done with fewer signatures on it.

So my question to you is this: if my vote is a signature rather than a lever, what gives you the standing to sign on my behalf for the lesser evil, and why should I think that a smaller wrong I have personally authorized is better than a larger one I have refused to touch? And do not tell me that refusing to sign is itself a kind of signing — that is exactly the move I want you to defend rather than assume.
