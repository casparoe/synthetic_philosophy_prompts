I cover science and ideas, and I'm halfway through a long feature on the fight over whether morality is universal or relative -- the one where the cross-cultural psych crowd and the anthropologists keep calling each other's data an artifact. I've got two sources who've been going at it for years: one's a moral universalist who thinks there's a single true morality and the cross-cultural variation is all surface (different rules, same deep structure), the other's a relativist who thinks the variation goes all the way down and that this is a problem for anyone who wants morality to be objective. Both are sharp, both think the other is begging the question, and neither will sign anything the other wrote.

So give me the joint statement they should have written together. What I need:

- a numbered list of claims they'd both genuinely sign -- about what the surveys and the fieldwork do and dont show, about sampling (the whole WEIRD problem), about what would count as variation that's deep versus merely cosmetic;
- then a table of where they still disagree, each row written so that the person it describes would accept the wording as fair to their own view;
- the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling it would actually move one of them;
- and for each crux, a case, argument or study that both of them agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two things I want them forced to handle, because I made them up and neither one has a script for them. First: a vignette with no harm, no victim and no audience -- somebody privately destroys a dead relative's shrine, or a keepsake, nobody knows, no promise broken, nobody's plans affected -- run in a WEIRD sample and in a small-scale community where ancestor stuff matters alot. Second: a design that tries to pull disgust apart from harm, one violation that's disgusting but harmless, another that's harmful but not disgusting, and ask which one is actually driving the wrongness judgment. I want each of them to say what they predict for both, and what result would make them give ground.

Last thing: some of this fight smells verbal to me. I suspect they mean different things by objective -- mind-independent? universal? non-negotiable? not up to anyone's say-so? -- and that once you fix the sense, a chunk of the disagreement evaporates. Tell me which parts are like that, and whether any empirical finding could ever really touch the metaethical question, or whether that bit is a non-starter no matter what the data says.

Skip the citations, I don't need a reading list, I need the argument.
