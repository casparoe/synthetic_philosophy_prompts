I do a small podcast on the philosophy of AI, and next week's guest is a researcher who's spent about a decade defending a pretty strong line: that inverse reinforcement learning can, in principle, recover a person's actual preferences from their behavior alone — no introspection, no questionnaires, just watching what they do. She's happy to grant every engineering caveat you like, but she thinks the philosophical objections are overblown. Can you draft me eight to ten interview questions? I want to actually press her, not let her restate the pipeline.

Some stuff I'd like covered. Start with the underdetermination business — she knows reward ambiguity is a real result, so I don't want a question that just invites her to concede it; I want one about why she thinks it's not fatal, and then a follow-up for the reply she usually reaches for (something in the neighborhood of: the environment's own constraints plus a simplicity prior on the reward pin it down enough). Then I want something on beliefs vs desires. The case I keep coming back to is someone who chokes down a foul-tasting medicine every morning — a model watching that could read it as a standing preference for bitterness or as a belief that the stuff is good for them, and I want her to say which one it is and why, plus a follow-up in case she says the belief is just folded into the environment model. There's also the noise issue: she treats the demonstrator as a Boltzmann-rational agent, so I'd like a question on whether genuinely irrational behavior just gets laundered into a preference for whatever the person happened to do. And one on the counterfactual problem — recovering preferences from behavior means extrapolating to choices that never get made, so what licenses that leap? Maybe one on aggregation too: when you watch a household or a crowd, whose preferences are you recovering, and is there even a fact of the matter? And end on the meta question — whether preference is a well-defined enough target that recovering it means anything, and what observation would actually falsify her realism rather than get absorbed as more data.

Keep them specific and a bit adversarial, and please pair each hard one with the follow-up she's most likely to need. Also, she has a habit of brushing off every objection as a data problem, so maybe work in one question that makes that move harder to pull off. A couple of them can be a little technical, she won't mind.
