I'm a second-year PhD student in cog sci and I've got this running arguement with a friend over in the philosophy dept that I want to actually settle, on paper, before our next coffee. Its about the careenium — Hofstadter's thought experiment — where you imagine a giant billiard table covered with a vast number of tiny magnetic balls he calls "symms," all careening around, banging into each other and into the walls, except that the walls' bounciness is driven by stuff happening outside the table, so information about the outside world leaks in through them. Zoom out and you stop seeing individual balls and start seeing these higher-level clumps and patterns that form, break apart and re-form; he calls those "symmballs," and it's the symmballs that seem to be tracking real regularities out in the world. Map the whole thing onto a brain — table = cranium, symms = neurons, symmballs = symbols/concepts — and then he asks the obvious question, who shoves whom around inside the careenium? You can say it's just symms pushing symms, balls hitting balls, and the symmballs are only a description we project on top. Or you can say the symmballs are the ones doing the shoving, since it's the symmball-level patterns that carry the info and drive the behavior that matters. My friend thinks the first answer is obviously true and the second is hand-wavy mysticism, and honestly half our department seems to agree with him.

I think the first answer is a mistake. Please argue the position for me: that the symmballs really do push the symms around, that the symbolic level has genuine causal standing rather than being a mere redescription of ball-collisions, and that "it's only the micro-physics doing any work" trades on a picture of causation it can't actually earn. Don't just say "emergence" and gesture at downward causation — I want the actual moves, and I want you to take the strongest version of the reductionist objection head-on instead of the easy one, because that's the version my friend will be bringing. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question.
