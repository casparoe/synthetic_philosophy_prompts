I run a monthly 'philosophy at lunch' thing at my company -- mostly engineers, no philosophy training, about 14 people, 50 minutes. I want an activity that makes them feel the difference between two things I only half understand. First: a situation where you never hear the other side, so you're wrong out of ignorance. Second: a situation where you *do* hear the other side and it makes you more sure you're right, because the source has already been framed as bought, hostile, or not one of us. Someone told me the second thing isn't just the first thing plus stubbornness, and that's the bit I want people to feel in their bodies rather than hear in a lecture.

So design the game. My rough idea, please fix it or throw it out -- two teams get the same one-sided dossier on a contested factual question (you pick; something engineers will have opinions about, but not something political-hot), each team rates its confidence, then I hit both teams with the counter-evidence. Team A should move toward it. Team B should move away. I want the rules and incentives to do that, not a scripted roleplay where someone is instructed to be pigheaded -- team B should dig in without anyone being told to. The stakes can be fake, but shouldnt be nothing (a $5 coffee card, snack-picking rights, whatever).
