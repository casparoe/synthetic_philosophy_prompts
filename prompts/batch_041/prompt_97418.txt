I'm giving the opening remarks at a small two-day workshop on Kelly betting and geometric rationality, and I want them to actually argue something instead of just welcoming people. The room is half econ/finance folks who default to expected utility and half philosophers of probability, and they dont fully agree with each other, which is kind of the point of the workshop. Hang the whole thing on one tension: maximizing expected value and maximizing the growth rate of what you actually get to keep come apart, because a single agent living one life can't average its outcomes across counterfactual copies of itself — so the thing to maximize is the log of your wealth, which means never risking ruin even on a gamble with positive expected value. Then make the centerpiece the wrinkle that Kelly only tells you to bet your edge over the odds if you're sure of the odds, so once you admit you're estimating them the honest move is to bet less than full Kelly, and draw out what that says about how much confidence to have in a whole research program. Three minutes, spoken. Don't let it turn into a lecture for the finance half, and don't dumb it down for the philosophers.
