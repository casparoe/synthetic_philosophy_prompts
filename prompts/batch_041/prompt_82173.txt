Two philosophers have agreed to produce a joint statement of an adversarial collaboration. The first is a Strawsonian compatibilist: moral responsibility is not hostage to the truth of determinism, because the reactive attitudes — resentment, indignation, guilt, gratitude, forgiveness — are grounded in the participant stance we occupy toward one another, and the demand that they be justified from outside that stance is largely misconceived. The second is a basic-desert skeptic: the reactive attitudes presuppose a kind of desert, or ultimate self-authorship, that no agent could possess, so blame is unwarranted as a response to desert, even if something like it can be retained instrumentally or recast as forward-looking moral address.

Draft the document both could sign. It should contain: a numbered section of agreed claims (about what the reactive attitudes are, what they presuppose, what determinism does and does not threaten, and what the cases below show); the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; the cruxes; for each crux, the case, thought experiment, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and an assessment of which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal, with reasons.

The cases they keep returning to, and which you should use, are: a man who learns years after his brother's death that the brother betrayed him, and finds that he resents the dead man; a woman who caused serious harm before an injury that changed her personality, so that the person now answering to her name is, by everyone's reckoning, not the one who acted; a person who begs his friends not to let him off the hook, insisting that being blamed is what respect for him requires; and an addict who, by his own account, could not have done otherwise on the night in question, yet whom neither side is willing to treat as a malfunctioning machine.

Format: the numbered agreed-claims section, then a table with one row per remaining disagreement and columns for the disagreement, each side's own formulation, the crux, the shared evidence or case, and each side's prediction. Maximum 800 words.
