I run the numbers desk at a small political-risk shop — six of us, we sell country risk to funds, and I'm the one who signs off on the probability estimates that go out the door. A few times a week somebody hands me a conditional instead of a fact. Not "the army will move on the refinery" but "if the army moves on the refinery, the unions call a general strike by the end of the month." Or "if the budget passes in March, the president finishes his term." I never know what to do with these. Nudging my probability of the strike feels like doing something, but I couldn't tell you what; leaving it alone feels like ignoring intel I paid for; and when I try to explain either choice to my boss I end up waving my hands. I have a vague memory of a philosophy puzzle about a paratrooper with a radio who gets a conditional back instead of a location, and I know there are named rules for this kind of update (Jeffrey something, imaging, some argument about minimizing information), but I can't look any of it up and I'd need it in a form I can actually run at my desk in twenty minutes without a stats degree. That's what I want from you.

Write me a procedure. Questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a "no, you can't use this one," and where it hands the thing back to me as a judgment call rather than a calculation. It has to tell me what to do when the source can't or won't give me the number I'm missing — I can't always get a second email out of these people — and what I'm supposed to write in the file: a point estimate, a range, or a refusal with a reason. Mark the steps that are judgement dressed up as arithmetic; I'd rather know which ones are mine to own than have it look like a machine did it.

Then run it on three cases, and be honest about which ones it can't settle:
1. The refinery one. My prior on the strike is low, and my prior on the army moving is lower.
2. A case where I already put the antecedent at about 90% — does any of the machinery buy me something over just updating on the strike directly?
3. A case where I'd basically written the antecedent off, near zero, and the source gives me the conditional anyway. I suspect this is where things break.

One of these should come out badly for me — if the procedure says I can't use a report I'd already half-believed, fine, say so. Seperate those from the ones it handles cleanly.

Under 850 words, start with the one-line default rule I should use when I don't have time for the rest, skip the reading list (I won't read it), and tell me at the end what this thing can't do.
