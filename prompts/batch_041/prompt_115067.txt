I am a novelist at work on a town-hall scene, and I need to get one character exactly right. He is a retired municipal administrator who has read the same economics column for thirty years and repeats its verdicts as though they were scripture. His conversation-stopper, produced whenever an argument is going badly for him, is the claim that you cannot have open borders and a welfare state at the same time. He has just aimed it at a proposal to extend the town's full package of child allowances, housing priority, and elder-care entitlements to anyone resident in the town for two years, citizen or not, financed by a local surtax on wages; he wants the line to show that the proposal is not merely unwise but impossible, and hence that its supporters are either naive or dishonest. I need to know how many distinct claims that one sentence could be making, laid out as a menu rather than adjudicated once and for all. For each reading: what exactly is claimed; what kind of impossibility is being asserted (logical, arithmetic, causal, motivational, electoral, or a normative judgement wearing the clothes of a description); what it presupposes about what a welfare state is (a club with members, an insurance scheme with contributors, a community of mutual identification, a machine for redistribution); what would have to be true of the world, and of the town's particular scheme, for the reading to hold; whether it is a platitude, a contested empirical claim, or simply false; and whether it would actually deliver the conclusion he needs in that scene. Be especially explicit about the slide between open borders as free entry and open borders as immediate and equal access to benefits, since that is where I suspect the trick lives, and about the single reply from the floor that would leave his line with nothing to stand on.

Give me the whole thing as one JSON object with these fields:

- character_and_scene: one sentence identifying him and what he is trying to shut down;
- readings: an array of objects, each with
  - label: a short name for the reading;
  - precise_claim: the thesis in one sentence, unhedged;
  - kind_of_impossibility: one of "logical", "arithmetic", "causal", "motivational", "electoral", "normative-in-descriptive-clothing";
  - presupposition_about_the_welfare_state: what the reading assumes a welfare state essentially is;
  - what_would_have_to_be_true: the world-side and scheme-side conditions, including what turns on the town's financing arrangement;
  - verdict: one of "platitude", "contested", "false", "true only by stipulation";
  - delivers_his_conclusion: "yes", "no", or "only if" plus the condition;
  - who_says_it_this_way: the sort of arguer this reading belongs to;
  - his_line_in_this_reading: a sentence he could actually speak in the scene under this reading;
- reading_he_probably_means: which one he is reaching for, and why I should write it that way;
- reading_that_would_actually_do_the_work: which one, if any, survives the specific proposal;
- the_reply_that_empties_his_line: what an opponent with the facts at hand could say;
- what_the_scene_should_leave_unresolved: two or three sentences, so I know what not to settle on the page.
