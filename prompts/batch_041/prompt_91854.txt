So please act as someone who actually works on medieval Islamic philosophy and the metaphysics of causation, and work through this with me rather than handing me the standard summary. Start by brainstorming the places the argument could break before you commit to a verdict — I don't want the first answer that comes to mind.

What's bugging me is the occasionalist line, the one I associate with al-Ghazālī (I'm reconstructing it from alot of secondary stuff and a seminar recording, so correct my version if I've mangled it). As I have it: (1) For A to be the cause of B there must be something in A that makes B happen — a power, a necessitating connection. (2) But all we ever observe is that B follows A; we can always conceive of A occurring without B, and we never observe the power itself. (3) So nothing in the created world — fire, bread, a drug, a muscle — has causal power; God produces each effect directly, on the occasion of the usual sequence. (4) Now turn the same reasoning on me: I never observe a necessitating connection between my intention to raise my arm and the arm's rising, nor between my premises and my conclusion. (5) So my intentions don't cause my actions and my reasoning doesn't produce my beliefs — God does that too. (6) But then my beliefs are produced by a process I can't inspect and whose reliability I can't check, so I have no more reason to trust my reasoning than to trust a coin flip. (7) And if I can't trust my reasoning, I can't trust the argument from (1)–(3) that got me here. So the whole thing seems to saw off the branch it's sitting on.

Where does it actually go wrong, and what does rejecting that step cost? I'd most like to keep (2) — as an experimentalist I genuinely think we only ever see regularities, and I have never once seen a power in a test tube. If keeping (2) means giving up something else, tell me what, and tell me whether a defender of the position can pay that price without sliding into a general skepticism about induction and reason. I want the actual joint where it breaks, why that joint looked solid to me, and what the rival positions have to pay. Don't just tell me occasionalism is unfashionable.
