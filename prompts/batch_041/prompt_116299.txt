I am a high school student writing an essay on whether the content of a mental state can be explained in naturalistic terms. The position I am working with is teleosemantics: the claim that what a state represents is fixed neither by its present causal role nor by what it presently correlates with, but by what it was selected for — by the contribution that states of that type made, across a history of selection, to the success of the systems that produced them. As I understand it, the view's chief attractions are that a state can be about something even when that thing is absent, and that a state can misrepresent, since it can fail to do what it was selected to do.

The case I cannot settle involves two programs. A programmer writes a genetic algorithm that runs for a thousand generations in a simulated world; the lineage that survives evolves an internal signal covarying with the presence of food ahead, and it survived because of that signal. A second programmer writes a generator that produces a single control program at random and retains the first one whose internal signal covaries with food ahead in exactly the same way; that program is then copied a million times and never altered. Both programmers intended their program to detect food. The surviving program of the first case and the program of the second are, from the moment of their creation, structurally identical, and they behave identically in the same world; the sole difference is that one signal has a history of selection behind it and the other has none.

Please describe the most important implications of teleosemantics for how the two signals should be described — whether they come out with the same content, with different content, or with one of them having no content at all — and, more generally, for how content should be ascribed to contemporary machine-learning systems, whose history consists of a training run over a corpus together with a designer's intentions. I would also like to know what follows for whether states of that kind can misrepresent. I am not asking for references; I want to know what the view itself implies.
