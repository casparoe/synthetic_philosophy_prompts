Ok so I run a small internal prediction market at work — the kind where people get play money (and a bit of real bonus) for trading on questions like "will we ship the Q3 release before Oct 1" or "will the city council approve the rezoning." There's maybe 15 to 40 of us. Lately leadership has started actually using the prices to make real calls, which terrifies me, because half the time the "price" is just three people who think they know something trading with each other.

I want a procedure I can run, question by question, to decide whether a given market price is (a) worth acting on, (b) worth spending money to sharpen up — commission a bit of research, or pay someone to trade against it — or (c) just noise we should ignore. Ground it in what the econ and philosophy-of-markets stuff actually says about when a price carries information and when it doesn't.

In particular I keep bumping into the no-trade idea: if everyone's rational, the only reason to trade is private info, and it's common knowledge that the market clears, then nobody trades — so any trade you actually observe has to be coming from somewhere else (noise traders, people who just disagree, liquidity needs, different risk tolerance). And the flip side of that: if the price already reflects everything, nobody bothers to dig up new info, so it can't reflect everything. I want the procedure to take both of those seriously instead of just chanting "wisdom of crowds."

Make it ordered questions, say what a sufficient answer looks like at each step, where the procedure stops with a "don't act on this," and where it hands off to a human because it's really a values call (like how much we care about being wrong in each direction). Then run it on three of my actual questions: (1) "will the FDA approve drug X by its PDUFA date" — thin market, a couple people with friends in the industry; (2) "will we hit our own shipping date" — everyone trading is an employee staring at the same internal dashboards; (3) "who wins the mayoral race" — lots of traders, most with no real info. At least one of the three should come out in a way I wont like. And be honest at the end about what the procedure cant do.

Please dont turn this into a reading list — I don't need cites, I need something I can paste into a doc and hand to my team.
