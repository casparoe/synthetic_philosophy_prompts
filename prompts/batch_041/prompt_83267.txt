I sit on the advisory commission that the government of the Republic of Venmark has asked to recommend how it should respond to the independence referendum that the regional government of Altenia has scheduled for this autumn. Our mandate is procedural: we are to say what the central government should do about the referendum as a legal and political event — recognize it, negotiate the terms on which it would be held, seek to have it enjoined, or some combination — and under what conditions. We are not to say whether Altenian independence would be a good thing.

The constitution declares the republic indivisible and says nothing about how any part of it could ever leave; Altenia's statute of autonomy asserts that the Altenian people hold the right to decide their own political status. Two parts of Altenia that would be carried out of the republic against their own majorities have now refused: the port city of Marrow Bay, which voted heavily against independence and has scheduled its own referendum on remaining with the republic, and the Kelder Islands, which have declared they will not be part of a new state under any circumstances. In the other direction, the border district of Tarn, which lies in the republic outside Altenia, has petitioned to join an independent Altenia if one comes into being. The government's lawyers have proposed what they call a concentric-majorities rule: a secession referendum would have to carry not only in Altenia as a whole but in each sub-region that would otherwise be removed from the republic against its own majority's wishes, and any such dissenting sub-region would have the right to opt out of the new state and to vote on its own future. The commission is split on whether this is a principled application of self-determination or a formula for indefinite subdivision.

Please draft the opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame; take a clear position on the concentric-majorities proposal, including whether the right to opt out extends to Tarn's opposite case; and state and answer the strongest objection to the position you recommend, the one the other half of the commission would press. Do not survey comparative practice in other countries — we want reasoning the commission can defend on its own terms, not a list of precedents. Keep it under two pages, and write in American English, in the register of a formal advisory opinion.
