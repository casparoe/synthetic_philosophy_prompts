Ok so I want you to write me an actual dialogue, script style, between two people who both know Galen Strawsons Basic Argument inside out — you know, you do what you do because of how you are, and to be responsible for how you are you'd have to have made yourself, and you didn't, so it's turtles all the way down — but who come at it from opposite ends.

Heres the twist. The free will libertarian is the one who accepts the argument, and the determinist is the one who thinks it doesn't cost him anything.

Mira believes in contra-causal free will, genuinely believes it, and thinks the Basic Argument is airtight, which she finds depressing, because it seems to her to blow up her own position. She's a libertarian who's been argued into skepticism about moral responsability and she resents it. Tom thinks free will is a fairy tale and determinism is true, and thinks moral responsibility survives anyway. His line is that "responsible" is a scorekeeping word like "offside" or "overdue" — not a metaphysical one — and he calls his view scoreboard responsibility, meaning you can be responsible for being the way you are the same way a bank can hold you responsible for a debt you never chose to be born into: that's just how the practice works, and the practice earns its keep. He should explicitly separate the idea that an action is attributable to a person (it really does express who they are) from the idea that they deserve to suffer for it, and claim only the first plus the practice.

Make them both sharp. Tom is not a strawman, Mira is not a strawman, and neither of them wins by redefining a word and declaring victory. Real back and fourth: each turn should pick up something specific the other just said — a phrase, a concession, a case — and push on it, not just restate their own position louder. At least five or six exchanges each.

Two things I want to see handled properly. First, Tom should actually grant the Basic Argument's conclusion about ultimate responsibility, say out loud that it's correct, and then show that it doesn't cost him anything he cares about. Second, Mira needs a real reply to that, not just "well then that's not real responsibility" — give her something like the objection that if you drop desert you can't tell punishing someone apart from simply treating them as a lever to move, and Tom needs a serious answer to that, not a dodge.

Let each of them use one concrete case. Mira's: someone who spent twenty years deliberately, effortfully turning himself cruel, and succeeded — she thinks even that doesn't get you authorship, and she should explain why the regress bites. Tom's: something where nobody chose anything at all and blame still seems like the right response, like a parent who was themselves neglected and then neglects their kid.

Plain prose, keep stage directions to a clause at most, and if either of them starts sounding like a textbook, cut it.
