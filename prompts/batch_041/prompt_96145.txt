ok so im in a seminar on modality right now (basically all Lewis, all the time) and for the final piece we're allowed to do something creative, so im doing a poem. what i want is a sestina, because i think the sestina is secretly the best arguement for counterpart theory there is. here's my thinking: in a sestina six end-words come back in every stanza, and theyre the only things that persist, everything else in the poem changes. but the word at the end of stanza four isnt the same word as the one at the end of stanza one, it just looks like it. the poem only holds together by resemblance. thats the view, right? nothing is in more than one stanza, and what we call "the same word" is a family of lookalikes. so: six end-words that are supposed to be the six things that make me me — id say something like a name, a body, a memory, a habit, a place, and one thing id rather not have, but pick whatever six you can actually make sing — and let the repetition do the work.

the part i actually need help with, and the reason im not just writing a normal poem about identity: my prof spent a whole session on the fact that the resemblance relation isnt given, its chosen. theres no single relation that picks out which person in the other stanza is me — if were counting bodies, one answer; memories, another; character, another; and the context of the question decides which ones were counting. so "i could have been a poet" can come out true while theres still nobody in the other stanza who is me. thats what i want the envoy to land on, and i want it to land as a choice rather than a discovery. please dont end it on "so i couldnt have been anything else," because thats the reading everyone gets wrong on the midterm — the point isnt that the possibility is fake, its that the thing making it true isnt me.

also: no jargon. dont use "counterpart," "possible world," "modal," "transworld." if the poem needs the word counterpart to work, it hasnt worked. someone in my seminar should be able to point at the line doing the work of the counterpart relation without being told which line it is. and it has to be an actual sestina — 39 lines, six end-words, the real permutation, envoy at the end — not just a poem with six stanzas. meter whatever you like, but dont rhyme it, the rhyme will fight the end-words.
