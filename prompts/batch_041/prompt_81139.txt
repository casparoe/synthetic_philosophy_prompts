I run a small furniture workshop and am hiring a bench carpenter. The first applicant has twenty years at the bench: his dovetails are flawless, he can judge a board by feel, and when a cut starts to go wrong he will say something like "the grain is telling me to back off" — yet if I ask him to state the rule he has just followed, or to say in advance how he would handle an unfamiliar wood, he goes quiet and can only demonstrate. The second has half the experience, talks fluently about why wood moves and what a joint has to do, and turns out merely decent samples. My shop manager argues that the first man does not really know the trade, only how to do it, and prefers the second because he could train the rest of the crew. That judgment bothers me and I cannot say exactly why. Who actually knows the craft here, and should that decide whom I hire?
