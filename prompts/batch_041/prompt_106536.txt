I am a quantitative ecologist. My working life is spent fitting nonlinear dynamical models to long population time series, so my vocabulary is state variables and parameters, stocks and flows, feedback loops, delayed density dependence, fast and slow variables and the separation of their timescales, limit cycles as against damped oscillations kept alive by environmental noise, regime shifts between alternative stable states, early-warning signals such as critical slowing down, structural as against observation error, identifiability and equifinality, and model selection when the data amount to a single realization of a non-stationary process. I am entirely at home with the fact that a model can forecast well for mechanistically wrong reasons, and I hold strong views about how little can be inferred when there is nothing to cross-validate against.

I have been reading the macrohistorical programme associated with Peter Turchin — cliodynamics, secular cycles, and structural-demographic theory — and I want to know what it looks like in my own terms. As I understand the position: complex societies are modelled as a small number of compartments, the general population, the elites, and the state, interacting through nonlinear feedbacks, with sociopolitical instability as an output; in agrarian settings the engine is broadly Malthusian, with population pressing on a slowly moving carrying capacity, real wages scaled to output per head falling, elites growing in number and in their claims on the surplus, and the state's fiscal base eroding; the result is a secular oscillation of roughly two to three centuries running through phases conventionally labelled integration or expansion, stagflation, crisis, and depression; the modern variant drops the demographic engine in favour of elite overproduction, with too many aspirants and credential-holders chasing too few elite positions and frustrated aspirants turning into counter-elites, alongside popular immiseration and state fiscal distress, aggregated into indices of political stress that are said to have predictive value; the theory also distinguishes slowly building structural pressures from sudden triggers, in the way seismology distinguishes stress accumulation from the rupture; and a second, faster oscillation on a generational timescale is sometimes layered on the slow one.

What I want from you is a translation exercise in which the residue, not the success, is the deliverable.

First, restate the position in my vocabulary, claim by claim: the existence and periodicity of the cycle, the mechanism, the identification of the variables (relative wages, elite numbers and composition, fiscal capacity, solidarity), the phase structure, the separation of pressures from triggers, the two timescales, and the predictive claim. Where my framework forces you to supply something the original does not state — a noise process, an error model, a functional form, a boundary between endogenous dynamics and exogenous forcing, a criterion for what counts as a state variable — say so explicitly. Do not import my framework's assumptions and present them as translation.

Second, give me the residue report, item by item, saying in each case whether the content survives cleanly, survives only under distortion, or has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all: the cycle itself, since in my terms a cycle must be an internal limit cycle, a damped oscillator sustained by external noise, or a spectral artefact of a trending, non-stationary series, and these are in principle distinguishable; the variables, which for me must have defined units and a specified relation to observation; the mechanism, which for me must be written as transition rules with specified signs and lags; the trigger, which my framework can accommodate only as process noise or as a jump in parameters; contingency and human agency, meaning the assassination, the election, the failed harvest, the individual decision; and the interpretive and normative content, including what a crisis means to those living through it.

Third, adjudicate each residue. Say whether the loss is a genuine defect of my vocabulary, or whether it is evidence that the original claim was less determinate than it sounded and that the translation has exposed vagueness rather than destroyed content. Where a claim is testable in principle in my framework, say what data and what comparison would test it, and how much the single-realization problem undercuts the test. End by naming which of the theory's central claims are not even well posed in my vocabulary, and whether the fault lies with the theory or with my way of describing the world.

I do not need citations or a reading list; argue from the content of the position as I have summarized it, and flag clearly any point at which my summary misrepresents the theory in a way that changes the translation.
