I'm TAing an intro ethics course and I'm grading the short-answer section of the midterm. I'd like your read on one script before I put a number on it, since I'm not sure how much credit it deserves.

The question was:

Q2. In lecture we distinguished standing to blame — roughly, whether a blamer is in a position to voice blame at all — from the separate question of whether the conduct being blamed is genuinely wrong. We also split hypocrisy into two kinds: synchronic hypocrisy, where the blamer is doing the very thing they're blaming you for at the same time, and diachronic hypocrisy, where they did it in the past but have since changed. Dana stole money from the department till for years. She stopped, and now she catches a new colleague taking money from the same till and tells him off. (a) Does Dana have standing to blame him? (b) Does it make a difference that her own theft was twenty years ago and she's since paid everything back? (c) If Dana lacks standing, does that mean her colleague has no reason to stop, or can ignore what she says?

The student wrote:

"Dana has no standing to blame her colleague, because standing just is the right to hold someone to a norm, and you forfeit that right when your own conduct contradicts the norm. Since the norm she's invoking is the same one she broke, this is synchronic hypocrisy rather than diachronic, and the blame she voices is false: she isn't in a position to say the conduct is wrong, so there's nothing there for the colleague to answer. That settles (c) too — the colleague can ignore it.

On (b) I think the answer flips. Twenty years and full repayment mean Dana has changed, and once a hypocrite has genuinely reformed they get their standing back, so her blame is fine. But standing is a feature of the blamer's character overall, not of this one act, so if Dana is still dishonest in other parts of her life — cheating on her taxes, say — she'd have no standing here either, even though her conduct around the till has completly changed."

Give me a grade out of 10 plus the margin comments you'd write, working through each part of the question. I'm after the specific things that are right or wrong rather than a general impression.
