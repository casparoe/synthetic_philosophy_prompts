hey — I'm in this course on well-being and public policy and we just spent two weeks on the big annual international happiness rankings, the ones where they survey a huge sample of people in each country, ask them to place themselves on a ladder from 0 to 10 where the top is the best life they can imagine and the bottom is the worst, average it up per country, and then publish a league table, with a handful of economic and social factors trotted out to supposidly explain the gaps. Here's what's bugging me. Everyone in the class, prof included, talks as if the report is measuring how happy people actually are, but the thing it ranks countries by is a self-report on a weird hypothetical ladder, and the scores jiggle around year to year in ways that look more like noise or a bad news cycle than any change in how anyone feels. So I keep wanting to ask what the practice is even committed to. Does publishing a single number per country and then ranking them presuppose that happiness is one thing, that the numbers are cardinal — so that going from 6 to 7 means the same amount in Denmark and in Burundi — and that the whole scale is comparible across languages and cultures? Or could you run exactly this practice while privately believing happiness is plural, only ordinal, and not really comparable? And then the second half: a lot of the governments that show up in these tables now cite them to justify spending decisions, and some are openly trying to climb. Does using the ranking as a policy target presuppose that the thing measured is the thing worth maximizing, or can a country chase the number while admitting it's a rough proxy at best? I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory of happiness — and of measurement — that the practice is stuck with, tell me what it strictly needs versus what it merely fits nicely with, and say which rival views of well-being it quietly rules out. And please be honest about where you're most likely to be wrong: what would have to be true, or what argument would have to land, for you to change your mind about what the practice presupposes — or to conclude it doesn't presuppose any particular theory at all.
