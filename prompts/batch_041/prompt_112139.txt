I'm in the back half of an aesthetics course and we just finished Hume's essay on the standard of taste, and I walked out of the seminar more confused than I went in. My roommate is a psych PhD student who spends his whole day on inter-rater reliability, so obviously he had takes. Here's what I can't get past. Hume seems to say beauty isn't a quality in the things themselves but a sentiment in whoever's looking at them — fine, beauty is in the eye of the beholder, no disputing taste, everyone's entitled to their own reaction. But then in the same essay he turns around and says there's a standard of taste, and that the standard is whatever the qualified critics converge on, where qualified means delicacy of taste plus practice, comparison, freedom from prejudice and good sense. So which is it? My roommate says it's just a consensus measure dressed up — the standard is what the good raters agree on, and the raters are good because they're the ones who agree, so it's a circle, and honestly he even suggested we could compute it by taking the first principal component of the qualified critics' ratings, which sounds fake but he swears it's a real method. If he's wrong, though, it looks worse: a standard that isn't just what people happen to like starts to sound exactly like a fact about the artwork, which is the one thing Hume said he wasn't giving us. So Hume's standard is either a glorified popularity contest among specialists or the objective standard he denied having. It has to be one or the other and both are bad for him.

Concretely, here's the case that's bugging me. My roommate and I read the same novel; he thinks it's a mess, I think it's great, and suppose we both somehow had all five of Hume's qualities. What is the standard supposed to tell us to do — flip a coin, wait for the rest of the qualified critics to vote and go with the majority, or just admit the disagreement is unresolvable? I keep feeling like I'm missing a distinction that would dissolve the whole dilemma, but I can't see it. Can you tell me where my either/or goes wrong, and what the standard of taste is actually supposed to be doing if it isn't a rule for settling disputes?
