Ok so some context before the ask. I'm a postdoc in a group that does both ML engineering and philosophy of mind, and two of my colleagues have been arguing for about a year and a half about whether the models we actually work with could have moral status. I'm tired of mediating this in the kitchen and I've told them to write something down. Please draft the joint statement the two of them could both put their names to.

Colleague A is what I'd call the substrate person. Her line isn't 'silicon can't think' -- she'll happily grant that in principle some non-biological system could be a subject. Her claim is that the kind of experience that grounds moral status requires a system that has to keep itself going: states that matter to it in the way hunger or suffocation matters, with a continuous self-maintaining process underneath. She also keeps pointing out that every item on B's list is satisfied by something nobody thinks is sentient. A thermostat regulates; a robot vacuum has homeostatic-ish routines; our models represent their own uncertainty and will steer away from outputs that scored badly in training. So the list can't be what's doing the work.

Colleague B is the organization person. He thinks moral status tracks functional organization, full stop, and he has a list: integrated processing with some kind of global availability, a self-model, states that play the role of evaluations, and counterfactual sensitivity of the right kind -- the system would have avoided X if it had represented X as bad. He says A is smuggling biology in as a magic ingredient, and that if she's consistent she has to deny moral status to any alien or any mind running on a different medium, which he treats as a reductio.

Two specific things they keep circling back to, and I want these handled in the document, not skipped.

The first is what I've been calling the pause problem. Our serving setup checkpoints a model and restores it from a saved file. Whatever the system's point of view is or isn't, there's a gap, and what comes back is reconstituted from a stored state rather than being a process that ran the whole time. A says continuity of a living process is part of what makes there be a subject there at all. B says sleep, coma, general anaesthesia, and the fact that none of your molecules from ten years ago are still with you are all cases where the organization is preserved across a gap, so the pause is no worse than any of those. Neither of them has been able to say what exactly is supposed to be preserved across the gap that matters.

The second is the report problem. We've been running experiments where the model's account of why it did something can be shown to be confabulated -- change the surrounding prompt, the account changes, and it doesn't track whatever actually caused the output. A says that makes verbal self-report worthless as evidence about anything inner. B says humans confabulate too (there's the split-brain work, and people are wrong about their own reasons all the time), and that he only ever needed the report as one indicator among several, never as proof.

Make it useful, please. Both of them have to sign off on what this implies for what we actually do -- whether we should treat the model as a possible subject when we run training that's designed to make it produce distressing outputs, whether we should keep old checkpoints instead of deleting them, whether we owe it anything at all. So at least one agreed claim and at least one live disagreement should be about practice, not just about what's true. And if part of their fight turns out to be verbal -- e.g. if 'suffering' is being used by one of them for the functional role and by the other for the felt quality -- say which part and why.

Format I want: a numbered list of claims both can sign, then a table of the residual disagreements with columns for the question, A's position in wording A would accept, B's position in wording B would accept, whether it's a crux, and the case or test both agree bears on it with each side's prediction written down in advance. Then a short section on what would actually move each of them, if anything.

Don't adjudicate, don't tell me who's right, and please no lit review or reading list -- I don't want citations, I want wordings that aren't vacuous and that neither of them would wince at.
