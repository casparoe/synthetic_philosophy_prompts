Ok so I've been reading alot about moral relativism off and on, and I want to figure out what I actually think, becuase my gut keeps flip flopping. I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, so please keep it concrete and don't just name-drop theories at me. Here are a bunch of cases involving groups or people with their own moral codes, plus my honest verdicts. I want to know what principle, if any, captures them, and which of my verdicts I have to give up.

1. A Discord server I'm in has this rule that if someone gets banned, you're not allowed to mention them again, ever. They just vanish. I think this is weird but fine — not wrong, just their house their rules.

2. I used to work at a startup where the founder treated leaving before 8pm as a betrayal of the team. People who left at 6 got quietly frozen out of everything. I think that was just wrong, not "their culture."

3. Hypothetical: a remote community where, once you're too old to contribute, the norm is that you stop eating until you die, and people who refuse are seen as selfish. My gut: I don't think they're doing something morally wrong. It's their world, their beliefs about what a life is for.

4. Hypothetical: another community where it's considered normal, even admirable, to lie to outsiders to protect the group. My gut: that's not fine, and not just different.

5. My uncle is a strict vegan and genuinely thinks eating meat is immoral, but he also thinks anyone who disagrees is a bad person. My gut: fine that he has that code, not fine that he condemns everyone else. Which already seems to contradict 3, since in 3 a whole community condemns the holdouts and I let it slide.

6. Here's the one I keep going back and forth on. A tiny, isolated town where everyone has to show up to a weekly public confession of their failings. If you skip, you get shunned — no job, no help, your kids get frozen out at school. But technically you could move away; it'd just cost you everything. Is this like case 1 (weird but fine) or case 2 (just wrong)?

The only principle I've half-come up with is: it's just different if the people can walk away, and wrong if they can't. But that says the startup was fine, since you could quit, and I really don't think it was. So it's broken. Is there a principle that gets all six, or close? What does it say about 6? And if a principle gets five of them but forces me to drop one, tell me which one and why I should give it up.
