My professor keeps saying the whole view is dead on arrival, but I think alot of the dismissals are lazy, and I want to actually see how the arguments stack up before I sign on to that.

So here's what I'm asking for. Below is a list of considerations someone might use to argue about whether animals have minds in Descartes's sense — whether they think and feel. Rank them from the ones that most support Descartes's conclusion (animals are mindless automata) down to the ones that most threaten it. Give each one a score from 0 to 1 for how hard it cuts in that direction, plus a sentence or two of justification. The list: (a) the fact that animals behave in flexible, goal-directed ways and can learn new tricks; (b) the fact that they have nervous systems and brains broadly homologous to ours; (c) the fact that they can send signals to other members of their species; (d) the fact that a creature can be mistaken about the world, or seem to grasp an appearance/reality distinction; (e) the fact that an animal can hold back an immediate desire, like a dog that waits for a command before eating.
