OK so I'm the best man at my best friend Dan's wedding in two weeks and I'm completely stuck on the speech, and I think you can help. Here's the situation. Dan and Priya almost broke up last year. She got into a program in another city and his mom got sick at basically the same time, and they spent like three months going back and forth about what to do. And the thing is, it wasnt indecision. Neither option was better than the other. Not equal either, somehow — that's the part that messes with my head. I only started thinking about this because my brother, who did a philosophy degree and now won't shut up about it, kept going on about 'incommensurable values,' and how the interesting case isnt when two options are tied, it's when neither is better and they're still not the same thing, and then he said something about how if you make one of them slightly better it still isn't better than the other one, which supposedly proves something. I half get it and half dont.

Anyway. What I want is a best man speech that actually says something true about what those two went through, instead of the usual 'you two are perfect for each other' mush. I keep hitting two walls. I can't say they made the right choice, because that implies one option was better and I honestly dont think it was. But I also can't say it didn't matter which one they picked, because it mattered enormously and they'd both hate that. I want the speech to sit right in that gap and make the room feel like the gap is where the good stuff actually lives — like the fact that they had to choose without a ranking is part of what makes the choice theirs and not something the world handed them.

Its a mixed room. Half are Priya's family, who are lovely and fairly traditional and would find all this morbid or just weird at a wedding. The other half are our college friends, who absolutely know what 'incommensurable' means and will be grading me. So it has to land for both, warm, a couple of jokes, maybe something about how Dan is a genuinely terrible dancer.

Just write the speech, about four minutes, as one continuous piece of prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, nothing like that, just the way I'd actually stand up and say it. And please dont let it come out sounding like a philosophy essay.
