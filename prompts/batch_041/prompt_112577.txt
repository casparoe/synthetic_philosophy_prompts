Here's the situation. Frege is the guy who basically invented modern logic and spent his career trying to show that arithmetic is really just logic in disguise. Late in his life, in the 1920s, he kept a diary. It sat in his unpublished papers for decades and then came out in the 1990s. And its full of really ugly stuff: antisemitic remarks, hostility to democracy, digs at Catholics and the French, a nationalist streak, and a general sense that Jews should just disappear from Germany. Not just cranky old man material, genuinely nasty.

Ever since it came out people argue about whether it bears on his philosophy. The "doesn't matter" side says: logic is logic. Frege himself was famous for insisting that logical laws have nothing to do with the psychology of whoever happens to be thinking them, so dragging in his private opinions is either a genetic fallacy or just gossip. We don't redo Euclid because of who wrote him. The "does matter" side says the philosophy isn't a sealed box: his whole vision is of a timeless third realm of thoughts floating above the messy negotiable world of human opinion, with logical laws as unconditional and not up for a vote, and that structural move looks a lot like an anti-democratic politics that wants some unquestionable authority standing above ordinary politics. On that reading the diary isn't evidence about the man, it's a clue about what the philosophy is up to.
