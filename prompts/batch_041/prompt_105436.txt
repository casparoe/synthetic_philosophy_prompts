I write long-form essays on science and ideas, and I'm drafting a piece on the old quarrel between great-man history and structural explanation, built around the question of whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler. Before I commit to a line, I want to set out my own commitments and ask you to tell me whether they hang together. Here is roughly where I've landed.

First, on large-scale outcomes — wars, revolutions, the collapse of regimes — I think the causal weight sits overwhelmingly with structural conditions: economic dislocation, institutional breakdown, geopolitical pressure. On this view the individual actor is generally replaceable, and the "great man" is closer to a symptom of his moment than its cause.

Second, I hold that attributing causal responsibility to any factor — a person or a structure — requires passing what I call a substitution test: if we remove or replace the factor and the outcome is not substantially different, it was not a cause. Causation, in other words, is difference-making, and difference-making is counterfactual.

Third, I believe history is genuinely contingent. The path actually taken was not the only one on offer; alternatives were live, and comparatively small perturbations could have redirected large outcomes.

Fourth, I do think Hitler was a cause — of the Holocaust and of the particular shape the war took. Had a generic competent reactionary stood in his place, I don't think the same catastrophe unfolds; the specific extermination project was his.

Fifth, I'm suspicious of any causal claim that can't be restated as a counterfactual we could in principle assess.

Sixth, I think causal responsibility comes in degrees. We ought to be able to say that one factor carried more weight than another, rather than treating causation as all-or-nothing.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Which of these commitments pull against each other, which pairs only look opposed, and which single revision would resolve the most while costing the least? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and be explicit about what each reconciliation would force me to give up. Finally, tell me what would change your mind about your own ranking — which argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
