A colleague of mine in the philosophy of science calls herself a selective realist and cites the pessimistic meta-induction as her reason for caution. Her track record: anti-realist about phlogiston, caloric, the luminiferous aether, and the vital force; realist about electrons, quarks, and the Higgs boson; anti-realist about the wavefunction, which she treats as a computational device; realist about dark matter; and anti-realist about dark energy, which she treats as a fitted parameter. Our reading group next takes up a proposal that posits an all-pervading, unobservable medium, call it the substrate field, to explain a family of anomalies. The theory has survived a decade of tests and its one novel prediction has been confirmed, but the field is continuous, unmanipulable, and belongs to the class of imponderable fluids and media that history has repeatedly discarded. Will she be a realist about the substrate field? Give me your best guess, the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her past verdicts just as well, what each predicts about this case, and which further case would decide between the surviving rules.
