Play a true believer in that view for me. Make him someone who's actually spent years inside frontier labs, thinks the safety crowd are decent people but strategically naive, and knows the technical details well enough to push back hard. He's sharp, not a strawman, willing to concede empirical points when I land one, but he never gives up the core claim that the constraint is binding and that unilateral restraint loses. Stay in character until I say stop, reply in a paragraph or two, talk like a person, no bullet lists or headers. Here's my opening move.

You keep saying the constraint is binding because whoever slows down loses. But every cartel faces exactly that, and plenty of them hold for years -- OPEC, the diamond people, the airline price-fixers. A cap-and-inspect deal among the big labs is just a cartel with a safety story bolted on. You don't need anyone to be a saint, you need defection to be detectable and punishable. So isnt the real question just whether a big training run is *verifiable* by the other signatories? We can measure flops, we can trace the chips, a treaty can put inspectors in the datacenter. If runs are verifiable, what's left of your constraint? And if your answer is "you can't verify it," then name the thing that can't be verified, because "you can't verify compute" sounds like a claim about engineering, not about incentives. Second thing while your at it: you say any tax makes you lose, but a 3% hit isn't losing if you're already ahead. So tell me the mechanism that turns a small tax into a fatal one -- winner-take-all dynamics? talent flight? investor panic? -- because "competitiveness" is carrying an awful lot of weight in your argument and I want to see the gears.
