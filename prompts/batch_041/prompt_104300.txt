Ok so I've been lurking rationalist blogs and forums for like a decade and I finally have one that's eating my brain, so I'm bringing it here. A friend of mine -- the kind of guy who has strong opinions about quadratic voting and has read way too much about Athens -- walked me through this argument about sortition and I can't find the exit. Here it is:

(1) A collective decision that binds everyone is legitimate only if everyone it binds had an equal chance to shape it.
(2) Elections fail this test. Money, incumbency, name recognition, media access -- the ex ante chances are wildly unequal, so an elected body doesn't clear the bar in (1).
(3) A random sample does clear it: ex ante, everyone had the same probability of being picked and the same probability of landing in the majority that decides. So a randomly selected assembly is legitimate in a way an elected one isn't.
(4) But nothing in (1)-(3) is special to parliaments. Take any decision that affects a bunch of people: which of two patients gets the last ventilator, which of three families gets the subsidized flat, who gets the job, who gets the kidney.
(5) So by the same reasoning all of those should be decided by lot too. Spin the wheel, everyone had an equal chance, done.
(6) That's obviously crazy though. You triage the ventilator -- you give it to whoever it saves, or whoever's worse off -- you dont raffle it. You hire the qualified candidate. Nobody thinks a lottery is the right way to hand out kidneys; we use waiting lists and match criteria, not raffles.
(7) So either (1) is false or (3) doesn't actually deliver the legitimacy I thought it did. And if (3) is dead then so is the whole case for lottocracy, which I really don't want to give up.

He says this shows sortition refutes itself, and honestly the move smells like a classic "proves too much" and I should be able to shrug it off, but I can't name what's wrong. I feel like something slips between (3) and (4), but maybe it's (3) that's already too strong, or maybe (1) is just a bad criterion and I've been leaning on it too hard. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? I'd rather keep (3) than (6) if I have to choose, but I want to know what I'm paying -- does saving (3) force me to give up something about sortition I actually care about?
