me and my friend had a two hour fight last night about whether self-deception is even possible — she kept insisting its literally contradictory, you cant both know a thing and successfully hide it from yourself, and i kept saying yeah thats exactly what makes it interesting, its a contradiction you can actually live inside. now i want to close our reading group with a villanelle about it. can you write one where the two refrains are the two horns of the paradox — one refrain is the "you have to know it to bury it" side, the other is the "if you truly knew it you couldnt be fooled" side — and let the form carry the argument: the refrains keep coming back no matter how hard each stanza tries to reason its way out, and when they finally have to sit next to each other in the closing lines, the poem should land on the only move that keeps both true at once — that the you doing the hiding and the you being hidden from arent quite the same person — but show it in the grammar and the turn, dont just announce it. no name-dropping freud or sartre. someone whos sat with the paradox should recognize it; someone whos never heard of it should still get a good poem.
