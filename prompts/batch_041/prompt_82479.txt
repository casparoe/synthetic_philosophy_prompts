I teach a graduate seminar on decision theory and social epistemology, and I am writing the final exam. I want one essay question on the unilateralist's curse that cannot be answered by reciting the standard formulation. The question should introduce this twist: each of an unknown number of actors has a private estimate of how many others are considering the same action, and the action is beneficial if at least one performs it but harmful if too many do. Ask students to analyze how this uncertainty about the number of actors affects the conditions under which the curse arises, and whether it can be rational for an actor who believes the action is probably wrong to perform it anyway. Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points, each worth 4 points (total 20), and for each point a one-line note on how candidates typically get it wrong.
