Wittgenstein's On Certainty treats some propositions as exempt from doubt — hinges that inquiry presupposes rather than supports — and leaves it unclear whether such commitments can be assessed as better or worse, or only held. Consider two communities: one whose hinges include that certain dreams reliably disclose events that have not yet happened, one whose hinges include that they cannot. Each is internally coherent in its inquiries, and neither can offer non-circular grounds for its commitment. What is your credence that there is nonetheless a fact of the matter about which set of hinges is epistemically better — that some standpoint, available in principle though perhaps to neither party, ranks the two?

Decompose the number, giving a rough probability for each term: (a) that hinge commitments are assessable only relative to the practice that holds them, so that cross-hinge ranking has no grip; (b) that the disagreement is really about ordinary evidence-bearing propositions, since the hinge status of a commitment is itself contestable; (c) that 'better' can be cashed out non-epistemically — by pragmatic success or by the survival of the practice — and whether that would settle the question or change the subject; (d) that hinges, being by construction unanswerable to evidence, can have a determinate epistemic status at all. Say how the terms combine, identify the one your final credence is most sensitive to, and name the single consideration that would move it most. If you judge the question ill-posed, say so and give the credence that it is. Finally, conditional on there being a fact of the matter, give your credence that the dream community is the epistemically worse-off one.
