I have been trying to reconstruct the hole argument in general relativity from scratch, and I have ended up with a version whose conclusion I cannot accept. I would like you to tell me which step to reject, why it looked innocent, and what rejecting it costs.

(1) A model of general relativity is a manifold of points together with a metric and matter fields, and the field equations are diffeomorphism-covariant: if (M, g, T) solves them, so does (M, d*g, d*T) for any diffeomorphism d from M to itself.
(2) Some diffeomorphisms agree with the identity on every point up to a chosen spacelike slice and depart from it only inside an open region — the hole — to the future of that slice.
(3) Applying such a diffeomorphism to a solution therefore yields a second solution that assigns every point before the slice exactly the field values the first solution assigned it, while disagreeing with the first solution at some points inside the hole.
(4) These are two distinct models: different mathematical objects, which say different things about what is happening inside the hole.
(5) A theory is indeterministic if it permits models that agree on an entire past and differ in the corresponding future.
(6) So general relativity is indeterministic.
(7) Nothing in (1)–(5) is special to gravity: the same recipe runs for any theory whose models are built on a set of individuals and any symmetry acting on them, including the gauge freedom of the electromagnetic potential and the exchange of two indistinguishable particles. So the reasoning appears to make indeterminism ubiquitous, which I take as a sign that something in it is broken.

I would rather keep (5) than (6), and (1) looks like a plain statement of covariance, so neither seems like the place to cut. Which step do I give up? I have heard the reply that the two solutions are simply two descriptions of one and the same situation; that sounds right to me, but I want to know whether it makes determinism too cheap — if every symmetry may be waved off as a mere description, is any theory ever indeterministic? — and what each alternative costs: keeping the points real and swallowing indeterminism, replacing points with relations among the fields, treating manifold and metric as a single object, and the structuralist option.
