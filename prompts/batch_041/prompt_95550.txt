Ok, this has been bugging me for months and I dont have the vocabulary for it. I mess around with chess engines and I've been reading about those little vacuum robots and the people who worry about AI causing big unintended changes, and I keep hitting the same wall. Everyone says the machine should "avoid side effects" — dont knock over the vase, dont rearrange stuff you werent asked to rearrange. Sounds obvious. But when I actually sit down and try to write the rule out, I cant do it without first deciding what the thing was supposed to do AND what the room would have looked like if it had just sat there and done nothing. And that second part isnt a fact sitting in the room. It depends on what you care about. The dust that was going to settle anyway, the plant that wouldve grown toward the window, the person who was going to move the vase themselves five minutes later. Pick a different thing to care about and the "side effects" come out completely different.

Here's what gets me: in chess the baseline is handed to you for free. The rules say what the position is, so "dont change anything except by your move" is a clean instruction. Everywhere else the baseline is something you have to invent, and inventing it is already a claim about what matters. So a rule that was supposed to help you avoid needing a full picture of what's good seems to need that picture just to be stated. I feel like the low-impact people must have run into this. Is there a name for it? Is it just the old action-vs-omission thing in a lab coat, or the frame problem, or something its own? Those feel close but not the same — action/omission seems to be about whether you're to blame, and this is about whether the instruction can even be written down.

And please dont just give me a label and stop. I want to know whats actually doing the work here. Does it only bite when people disagree about whats good, or does it happen even in a totally boring case where everyone agrees on the goal? Is there any version of "avoid side effects" that doesnt smuggle in a value judgement, and if not, why not? If nobody's named the thing, name it yourself and tell me where its edges are — what it isnt.
