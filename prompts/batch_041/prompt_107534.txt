I am a software engineer with no formal training in history or philosophy, and I would like you to try to break a view I have arrived at.

Watching technical ecosystems settle — one package manager, one serialization format, one build tool emerging from a field of near-equivalents and then becoming almost impossible to dislodge — I have come to think that path dependence is invoked far too readily, and that the counterfactual in which the same outcome would have arrived regardless is true more often than the literature allows.

Stated more carefully, the view is this. When we observe an outcome that looks contingent and then self-reinforcing, the contingency is usually thinner than it appears: the rivals that lost differed in ways that did not matter to the selection pressures at work, so some functionally equivalent outcome was going to emerge by one route or another, and the decisive-looking early event was a bottleneck that any of several options could have passed through. Genuine lock-in — an arbitrary choice retained even though a better alternative is available and reachable — is real, but rarer than the phrase suggests, and it is much easier to demonstrate at a fine grain (this exact artifact, this exact institution) than at a coarse grain (this function being served at all). I suspect that most claims of lock-in quietly trade on that difference, borrowing their force from the fine grain and their plausibility from the coarse one, without ever committing to a level at which the claim could be checked.

Please tell me what would count against this. I want the observations, arguments, and formal results that should move me, ranked by how decisive they would be, and with a clear separation between those that would seriously damage the view and those that would merely complicate it. I am more persuaded by evidence that lock-in actually occurred, where a demonstrably better alternative was available and reachable, than by demonstrations that it is possible in principle. Then tell me which of these a committed defender of strong path dependence would concede as counting against me and which they would dismiss, and whether they would be right to dismiss them. Finally, be honest about whether my view, as I have stated it, is falsifiable at all: the move between fine and coarse grains looks to me like the point at which it might quietly become unfalsifiable, and I want to know whether that is a fatal defect or an acceptable feature of a claim of this kind.
