I've been chewing on pain asymbolia for way to long and I want to force myself to actually put numbers on it.

The claim I'm trying to pin down: the badness of pain is intrinsic to its phenomenal character. Spelling that out — necessarily, any creature in a state with the felt character of pain is pro tanto worse off just in virtue of being in that state, whatever its desires, attitudes, or aversions happen to be. The asymbolia cases are where it gets tested. Patients with the relevant lesions will tell you it hurts, they can point to where, they'll yank their hand off the hot plate, and then they'll say it doesn't bother them, they don't really mind it. (Paraphrasing the clinical descriptions, I'm not quoting anyone.)

Give me your credence in that claim, but decompose it — a number and one line of justification for each term:

- the probability that the asymbolics are in the same phenomenal state as ordinary pain at all, versus the possibility that aversiveness is a constituent of pain's phenomenology, so that when it goes, what's left isn't pain but some neutral sensory state we'd want a different name for (call it nociception-without-hurt, or whatever you like).
- given they are in the same state, how much the case actually tells us — the probability that felt awfulness and prudential badness are one thing rather than two things that usually travel together, i.e. whether a state can be horrible to have yet not bad for you, or bad for you yet not horrible.
- the background metaphysics: whether evaluative properties supervene on phenomenal ones at all, or whether badness is always indexed to a subject's conative states, so that a desireless being could be in pain that's neither good nor bad for it.

Then the combination, and tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence or argument would move it most. And a meta-question on top: is any of this empirical, or does the whole thing collapse into a fight about what 'pain' is allowed to mean?

For the record I'm around 0.6 on the intrinsic claim and an old colleague is around 0.9, and my suspicion is that we don't actually disagree about the cases, we disagree about the second bullet. Tell me if that's where you'd put the fault line too.
