I am an undergraduate in a seminar on practical reason, and I am trying to turn a discussion my friends and I had after class into a term paper. The topic is Parfit's hitchhiker: you are stranded in the desert, a driver will give you a lift only if you promise to pay him on arrival, and everyone — you included — knows that once you are safe you will have no reason to pay, so a sensible driver will not believe you and will drive on. What struck me is that nearly every resolution of the case tries to supply the hitchhiker with a motive he lacks at the moment of action: he is told to cultivate a trustworthy character, or to care about his long-run interests, or, on the more baroque suggestion, to swallow a pill that would render him incapable of defecting. I want to argue that all of these miss what a promise is. My thesis is that a promise is neither a prediction about one's future conduct nor a prudential investment in a reputation, but an undertaking that transfers to the promisee a claim over one's future conduct, a transfer completed the instant the promise is made; on that view, the hitchhiker who later finds he has no self-interested reason to pay is not a case of rational irrationality at all, but simply someone refusing to honor a claim he himself created. Please argue for this claim.

To keep the paper from being a restatement, please test the position against two cases. In the first, the driver suffers a heart attack and dies before payment is due, leaving an estate he never mentioned and no one who knows a promise was made; the hitchhiker could keep the money and no one would ever be the wiser. In the second, the hitchhiker has, before setting out, left his bank an irrevocable instruction to pay the driver on arrival, so that no later choice remains open to him. I want to know whether the transfer account says the promise still binds in the first case, and, if it does, whether it can explain why the second case nonetheless feels different — or whether that feeling is an illusion the account should debunk. Please also state the strongest objection to the position, which I expect will come from someone who holds that promises bind only insofar as they serve trust and cooperation, and answer it rather than gesture at it. Assume your reader already knows the hitchhiker case, and do not spend time on who said what or on reading lists; I care only about whether the argument works.
