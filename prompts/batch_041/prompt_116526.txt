I am preparing a paper on the educational assumptions built into early artificial intelligence, and I keep returning to Turing’s proposal, in his 1950 Mind article, that instead of trying to simulate an adult mind we should build a child machine and then educate it. He sketches a process of reward and punishment and explicitly compares it to teaching a child. I know the standard reading—that this is an early version of machine learning, that it replaces hand-coded knowledge with training, and that it is behaviorist in flavor. What I want is something more careful. Please analyze this specific proposal as a case study in the theory of situated learning, specifically the account of legitimate peripheral participation developed by Lave and Wenger. On that view, learning is not the acquisition of a stock of propositions but a gradual movement from the periphery of a community of practice toward fuller participation, with old-timers, apprentices, and the shared standards of the practice doing the pedagogical work. Map Turing’s child machine onto that framework: who or what is the learner, who are the old-timers, what is the practice, and what would count as legitimate peripheral participation? Does the reward-and-punishment signal function as a substitute for a community of practice, or does it presuppose one?

Then reverse the lens: what does the case of the child machine reveal about the limits or hidden assumptions of situated learning theory? Does the theory have the resources to say anything about a learner who is not enculturated into a human form of life, or does it quietly require a biological, social, and historical community that Turing’s machine lacks? Finally, tell me where the mapping breaks—where the theory illuminates Turing’s proposal, and where Turing’s proposal exposes something the theory cannot accommodate. I am less interested in a verdict on whether Turing was a situated-learning theorist avant la lettre than in a disciplined two-way analysis: what the theory says the child machine faces and should have done, which features of Turing’s proposal it explains and which it misdescribes, and what the episode in turn shows about the theory’s scope.
