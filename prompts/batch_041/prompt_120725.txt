Two colleagues in my research group have argued for a year about the value of pleasure and pain, and I want the document they should have written together rather than continuing to talk past each other. One holds what we have been calling lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: pleasure and pain are the only intrinsic goods and bads; there is a threshold of suffering above which suffering takes lexical priority over pleasure, in the way a dictionary orders its entries, so that above the threshold no quantity of pleasure anywhere compensates for the suffering; below the threshold ordinary weighing applies, though with suffering weighted far more heavily per unit than pleasure; and the threshold is a feature of how value is structured, not of our psychology. The other is a commensurabilist about pleasure and pain: they lie on one scale, every pain has a finite price in pleasure, and the appearance of lexical priority is produced by a very large but finite exchange rate together with our inability to grasp astronomical quantities, so nothing here is genuinely incommensurable. Both accept that any adequate account must explain a shared datum — that trading a small amount of intense suffering against an astronomically large amount of mild pleasure strikes us as monstrous, and that this reaction survives full knowledge of the quantities involved.

Write the joint statement the two could sign. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, including about what the lexicality claim is a claim about, about the formal cost of lexical orderings (for instance, that they cannot be captured by an ordinary real-valued value function and require giving up an Archimedean or continuity assumption), about whether aggregating value across beings remains well defined once the ordering is non-Archimedean, about which parts of the question are empirical and which conceptual, and about what each side would count as progress; (2) the residual disagreements, each written in wording its own side would endorse and stated as sharply as possible; (3) the cruxes, and for each one the case, argument, or test both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect the cruxes include whether "no amount of pleasure compensates" is a claim about the value function or about the absence of a common measure — that is, whether part of the dispute is verbal; whether a very steep but finite exchange rate can do all the work lexicality is wanted for; and whether the threshold attaches to individual lives or to situations. Include at least one test case in which the quantities are too large to be grasped, and one that separates a threshold for a life from a threshold for a situation.

Commit. Give definite formulations rather than menus of options; where no wording would be signed by both parties, say so and give your best attempt together with why it fails. Close by taking a side: which crux is load-bearing, which part of the dispute if any is merely verbal, and which party is closer to the truth.
