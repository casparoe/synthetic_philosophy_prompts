So I just burned about three hours arguing with my mate Dev about whether our city has gotten too loose and needs to start actually enforcing some norms, and I'm still mad about it. His position, roughly: places that run on strict shared rules are safer, cleaner, more trusting, people know where they stand, and we've traded all of that for the freedom to be weird in public. Which — okay, there's something there, I'm not going to pretend there isn't. But every time I asked him who actually ends up paying for that, or who gets to decide which rules, he told me I was being sentimental, and we just went around in circles until his girlfriend went to bed and we had to stop. So I want to write something to send him instead of another voice note.

Here's what I want. A leaked internal memo — CONFIDENTIAL, draft three, that kind of thing — from a consulting firm that a mid-sized city has quietly hired to raise its "normative tightness score." Headers, numbered sections, a scoring rubric, a fee schedule at the end. Deadpan the whole way through, no winking, no narrator. The person who wrote it should be good at their job and completely sincere about it; the jokes have to come out of the document's own logic, not out of the document being silly. Around 700 words.

The idea I want it to be built on, and I'm probably mangling this, is the tight/loose thing from cultural psych: tight = strong shared norms and almost no tolerance for people who deviate, loose = permissive, and the places that come out tight tend to be the ones that have been through real threat — drought, epidemic, war, crowding, not enough to go around. Tightness buys you coordination and predictability and clean streets; what it costs you is novelty, and the weird stuff, and anyone who can't or won't get with the program. It's also supposed to loosen back up once the threat fades. And the enforcement in tight places is mostly not cops, it's neighbours — informal, horizontal, everyone watching everyone. I might have some of that backwards, so don't have the memo citing anything, just build it into what the memo measures and prescribes.

Things I'd love to see in it, if they fit: the actual rubric and how absurd the proxies are; a section on "threat salience," because if the tightness comes from threat, the program needs a threat, so there's a campaign to keep one alive; a deniability section explaining that no new bylaws get passed, because informal enforcement can't be traced back to the council; one dry distributional footnote where the people who bear the cost get described in the flattest possible language; a short section on capabilities the city is choosing to retire; and a warning that the score decays on its own and has to be renewed. 

Hard constraints. The objections have to be visible to the reader without ever being stated — no "critics argue," no defensiveness, nobody in the memo anticipates a counterargument. And keep it fair: let the memo be genuinely right about what tightness delivers, so Dev can't just say I strawmanned him, he should laugh and then feel a little bad. No citations, no study names, no real researchers, no real cities or firms — invent all of it. And it should be readable by someone who has never heard any of this. Send help.
