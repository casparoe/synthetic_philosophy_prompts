My friend and I spent about two hours arguing about wasps last night and I want to actually settle it before we do it again.

Here's the fight. He tells me about the digger wasp: it drags a cricket back to its burrow, goes inside to check the place out first, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches while its in there, it comes back out, drags the cricket up to the entrance again, and goes in to inspect again. Do it over and over and it just keeps running the loop. He calls anything like that sphexish. Then he drops what he thinks is the scary part: you can't tell from the inside whether you're sphexish, and if we are, then all our talk about being free agents is just us failing to see our own loop. I said thats dumb, partly because someone told me the wasp demo is basically a lab artifact (in real conditions the wasp eventually does something else), and partly because even a genuinely flexible creature could be a puppet, so flexibility looks like the wrong thing to hang free will on. He said I'm missing it, the real question is what flexibility even is.

Which, ok, fair. So I want the spec first. What would a theory of the difference between rigid mechanical behavior and genuinely flexible behavior have to deliver to count as adequate? Give me the requirements — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it can't contradict, the puzzles it has to dissolve — with the case or consideration behind each one.

Some things I want on the list, because they're where the argument actually got stuck:

- A creature whose behavior is one enormous fixed table of responses that happens to cover everything it will ever meet, versus a small loop that notices when it has stopped working. He says the table is still sphexish; I say from outside there's no difference you could ever detect, so what does the theory say about whether that's a real distinction or just a fact about our models.
- The bird that fakes a broken wing to lure you away from its nest. Looks like a trick, supposedly a fixed display. He says sphexish. I said fine, then so is a person who learns to fake a limp for sympathy — same behavior, different history. - Free will: does the theory owe a verdict on whether flexibility is even necessary for responsibility, or is that a separate question? I'd settle for it being able to say what would follow about blame if it turned out we were sphexish in his sense.
