I'm a high school junior planning a year-long independent research project in the philosophy of mind, and I've become stuck on a puzzle that I suspect is deeper than the way it is usually presented. When a blue dot is flashed at the top of a screen and then, a fraction of a second later, a red dot is flashed at the bottom, observers at certain timings report not two dots but a single dot that travels from top to bottom and changes color partway along the path. What unsettles me is that the color change is reported as occurring during the motion, apparently before the red dot was ever displayed. Since the visual system cannot know about the second dot until it has been shown, the report seems to require that the experience be assembled or corrected using information that arrives later.

The usual way of framing the puzzle is as a choice between two stories. On the first, the experience is built in real time and then quietly rewritten in memory before I can describe it, so the revision happens after the experience, much as a regime might alter the historical record after the fact. On the second, the revision is performed before the experience is ever presented to me at all, so the doctored version is the only version I ever have, much as a show trial is staged with fabricated evidence prepared in advance. My problem is that the two stories appear to predict exactly the same reports, the same reaction times, and the same neural data, and a friend has suggested that this is because there is no fact of the matter about when the revision happens, that asking when a constructed experience occurs may be like asking where on a globe the equator really is.

Suppose I have roughly a year, no laboratory, and only my own reading and reasoning. Which open questions in this area most deserve that year, and in what order should I take them? I am less interested in the list itself than in the reasoning behind it: for each question, why it is upstream of the others, whether someone without equipment could actually make progress on it, and what would move it up or down my ranking. I would also like you to tell me which questions I should not bother with at all, including, if you think so, the question of whether the revision happens before or after the experience. Defend the ordering rather than merely describing the terrain, and be explicit about the criteria you are using and how they trade off against one another.
