Hey, I'm a phil PhD student (animal cognition + decision theory, fun combo) and I need a short story for a workshop I'm running — a vignette that makes people actually feel the arithmetic instead of just nodding at it. Can you write it?

Premise, and please don't soften it: it's a few decades from now and insect protein is a massive global industry. Trillions of black soldier fly larvae, mealworms, crickets, whatever, farmed every year. Nobody can settle whether any of them are conscious. So a regulator does the only thing it can think of: every rearing method gets a 'sentience likelihood' score between 0 and 1, assigned off a contested checklist of behavioural and neural markers, and that score gets multiplied by the number of animal-days per tonne of product. The result is basically a currency — call it weighted insect-days — and farms have to stay under a cap. Want a sloppier method? Buy offsets from a cleaner one, or from an operation that scored lower on sentience.

Now here's the bit I actually care about. Somewhere in the story I want a scene where the numbers do something grotesque: a method with, say, a one-in-a-thousand sentience score still ends up dominating everything, because there are just so many of them, and someone quietly realises that the whole human-scale welfare calculus they were trained on is a rounding error next to it. And I want at least one character who thinks that's precisely the madness — that once you let tiny probabilities times huge populations decide things, no ordinary consideration can ever outbid them, and you've built a machine that can demand anything. I dont want you to settle who's right. I want both positions uncomfortably live at the end.

A few constraints: it should be an actual story with characters and a bit of plot, not an essay wearing a coat; keep it to like 1500 words; and please don't make the sentience science sound settled in either direction — the uncertainty is the whole point. No footnotes, no citations, this is fiction.
