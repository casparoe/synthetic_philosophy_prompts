I have no academic training in philosophy, but for a few years I have been reading about Hempel's raven paradox in my spare time, and I have reached the point where the arguments on every side are familiar to me and yet nothing I read ever seems to settle anything. As I understand it, the paradox runs like this: the hypothesis that all ravens are black is logically equivalent to the hypothesis that all non-black things are non-ravens; a green apple is a non-black non-raven, and so, by the principle that a generalization is confirmed by its positive instances, it confirms the second formulation; and since equivalent hypotheses must be confirmed by the same evidence, the green apple confirms that all ravens are black. Everyone finds this uncomfortable, most people grant that the confirmation, if it exists, is vanishingly small, and yet the dispute about what has gone wrong — or whether anything has — has run for decades without resolution.

My own hypothesis is that the disagreement is not really about ravens or apples at all, but about two different things people mean by "confirm." One group means something like "raises the probability that the generalization is true"; another means something like "gives us a reason to think we have actually learned something about the subject matter." If that is right, the two sides could each be correct about their own question while talking past one another, in which case the paradox would be verbal. But I am not confident, and I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a verbal dispute about the word "confirmation"; a substantive dispute about which conditions any adequate account of confirmation must satisfy; a dispute about which observations count as evidence for which hypotheses; or a disagreement about starting intuitions — for instance, whether it is simply a datum that the green apple confirms the hypothesis at all, or whether that is precisely what is in question? Is there a hidden formal or probabilistic question doing the work, such that the impasse is really a theorem waiting to be proved or a distinction waiting to be drawn? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here — a sharper distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should we expect the debate to continue indefinitely because the question is ill-posed?

Please engage with what the actual positions are — the instance-confirmation view, the Bayesian treatment, and the various attempts to restrict or repair the equivalence condition — rather than giving me a survey of the literature or a reading list, since I cannot look anything up while I think this through. I want to understand the structure of the disagreement and what would move it.
