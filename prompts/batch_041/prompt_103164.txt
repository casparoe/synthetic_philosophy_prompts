My sisters wedding is in three weeks and I'm giving the toast, and I'm stuck. Our parents and most of the older relatives are devout and quietly believe marriage is essentially ordered toward having kids — they've been gracious about the whole thing, but I know they think this is at best an approximation of the real thing. My sister's own friends are mostly philosophers and queer theorists who'd hear any sentence shaped like "marriage is for..." as an insult. I need a four-minute toast that works for both halves of the room. Hard constraints: it can't pretend the disagreement isn't sitting right there in the room, it can't ask the older relatives to give up their view of what marriage is for, and it can't leave the couple with a watered-down "well, love is love" — by the end it has to be an unqualified blessing of this marriage, not a consolation prize. The move I want you to build it on: separate what the institution of marriage is *for* from what makes a particular marriage *good*, so the toast can grant the relatives' account of the institution's purpose for the sake of argument and still say something specific and true about what these two are actually promising each other. Use one or two concrete moments from their relationship (make them up, keep them plain), stay warm rather than lecturing, and finish on a line the whole room can drink to without crossing their fingers. Give me the toast, then one sentence on where the pivot happens.
