Okay, here's one I keep getting stuck on. I'm running a reading group session on Frankfurt cases and I want to use A Clockwork Orange (the film) as the anchor, but the obvious reading keeps bugging me. Everyone treats it as a free will tract: the chaplain tells the Minister they've robbed Alex of his free will, the Ludovico cure makes him physically unable to be violent, and the horror is meant to be that a man who can't choose isnt a man anymore. But thats just the principle of alternative possibilities, and the whole point of the Jones and Black case is supposed to be that PAP is false -- that you can be responsible for something you couldn't have avoided. So is the film just naive about PAP, or is it tracking something else? I want the second option taken seriously: does the film locate Alex's unfreedom in the lost alternatives, or in the fact that the aversion isn't his -- that his own will has been bypassed and rewired from outside? Is this a sourcehood/mesh story rather than a leeway story, and does it know which one it's telling? Two more things I'd like from you. First, build a Frankfurt style case inside the film's world -- a Black who would have guaranteed that Alex beats someone no matter what Alex decided -- and tell me whether its any good, in particular whether the film's world leaves the agent the little flicker of freedom that Frankfurt cases keep getting accused of needing (he at least tried, or at least decided). Second, the Ludovico case looks like the mirror image: no alternatives at all, and clearly no responsibility either. So does the debate need PAP for that intuition but not for the other one, or is that just having it both ways? Also, the novel has a last chapter the film doesnt use where Alex supposedly gets bored of the violence on his own, which if I'm remembering it right is basically a straight vindication of PAP -- does the film's ending (conditioning undone, Alex back in the mood, Minister propping him up) make it better or worse as philosophy? And where does the whole thing resist the reading I'm pushing, say so. I don't want a tidy allegory, I want to know where it breaks.
