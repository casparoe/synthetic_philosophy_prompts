Write an essay on whether the simulation argument makes 'fanaticism' about the far future better or worse. Quick recap of the two bits I mean, in case it helps: the simulation argument is the trilemma that either civilisations tend to die out before going posthuman, or posthumans don't bother running ancestor-simulations, or we're almost certainly simulated; and fanaticism is the charge that longtermists, who say the far future could hold astronomically many people, end up telling us to accept huge present-day costs for tiny probabilities of a huge payoff. My worry is that these two things interact in a way people havent really sorted out. One line: if we're simulated, the simulators can pull the plug, or the far future we're trying to shape isn't really ours to shape, so the expected value of far-future work collapses — the argument damps fanaticism down. The opposite line: simulated civilisations are cheap to run, so there are probably vast numbers of them, and that means even more future people's welfare is in play — the argument cranks fanaticism up. I want you to actually adjudicate this rather than just laying out both sides. Reach a verdict and defend it. And be honest about the weak point: tell me which consideration, if it went the other way, would flip your conclusion, and where your reasoning is most likely to come unstuck.
