I run a little philosophy night at the community centre once a month — no degrees among us, we just argue about big stuff over tea. Next month is infinity, and I want to do it as a written challenge: everyone gets one question, writes their answer at home, and we mark each others. I'm useless at setting questions so I'm asking you to do it.

The question has to be about that hotel thing — Hilbert's hotel I think its called, where every room is taken but the desk clerk still finds you a room by shifting everyone along. Everyone's heard that one, so it can't just be explain the hotel. I want it to bite on something weirder. What I keep chewing on is this: when the clerk tells every guest to move, does he ever actually finish? Room 1 goes to 2, 2 goes to 3, and so on forever, so nobody is ever done moving. Is the story asking us to picture an endless job getting completed? And if it is, does that make the hotel a story about words rather than about anything real?

Also I don't want people getting away with infinity is weird, thats the point. I want the question to force a seperation between three things: (a) the bare claim that you can line the guests up against the rooms one-to-one so the count comes out the same, (b) the story where an actual person walks down an actual corridor and moves, and (c) the old idea that a whole has to be bigger than its part, and what exactly we give up when we drop that. The good answer should say which of the three is doing the work and which ones the hotel story doesn't actually need.

So write me (1) the question itself, worded so someone who's read nothing but has thought about it can have a go, (2) a model answer, and (3) a marking scheme out of 20 that splits the strong answers from the ones that just repeat the trick. In the marking scheme I want the specific moves a top answer has to make, and the specific mistakes that should cost marks — like someone who thinks the hotel shows infinity comes in different sizes (thats a different result entirely), or someone who thinks the moving can't be finished so the whole thing is nonsense (missing that the count claim doesn't need anybody to move). One line per mistake saying what the mistake is is fine.

Start with one line saying what the whole crux is, then the question, then the model answer, then the marks. Keep the whole thing under 900 words, and the model answer itself no more than 500 of them.
