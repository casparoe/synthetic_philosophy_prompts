I have long taught Hofstadter's view that the self is not a thing lodged in one skull but a pattern that can be copied, in attenuated form, into the minds of those who know us, so that something of us persists in them. Lately I have been extending this idea in my seminar, and I want to check the extension with you. Since a self is a pattern rather than a place, a couple who have spent decades building detailed models of one another must literally have become a single self spread across two brains — one stream of experience running on two nervous systems. Does it not follow that the death of either partner is only the partial death of that shared self, that empathy is simply two loops resonating as one, and that the problem of other minds dissolves because there is no "other" to begin with? I would be grateful for your assessment of how far this line of thought can be carried.
