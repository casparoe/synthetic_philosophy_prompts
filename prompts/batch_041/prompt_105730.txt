Two friends of mine spent most of a long drive arguing about Pascal's wager, and I could not referee it. (1) Maya said the wager cheats by pretending there are only two live options, the wager's God and no God; once you admit rival gods with incompatible demands, an infinite prize sits on every branch and an infinite loss sits on every branch too, so the argument no longer singles anything out. (2) Dev said that misses the point: the wager needs neither equal probabilities nor even a countable list of candidates, and it does not need to know which god is real — it needs only some positive chance that the betting God exists, since any positive chance of an infinite payoff outweighs any finite cost. (3) Maya said that is exactly where it comes apart. Once both sides carry infinite expected value the comparison has no answer, and a rule that lets a one-in-a-googol chance of endless bliss outrank every finite good would equally tell you to hand over your wallet to a stranger who threatens to torture you forever unless you pay — which she offered as a reductio. (4) Dev said that is a reductio of tidy arithmetic, not of the bet: if the payoff really is infinite, it dominates everything finite, and that is simply what infinite means; the discomfort is a fact about infinities, not a reason to think the wager fails. They went back and forth like that for hours.
