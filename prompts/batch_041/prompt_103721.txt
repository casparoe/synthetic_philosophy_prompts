I've been reading a lot of adversarial-collaboration stuff on the blogs lately (the whole "write down what you both already agree on before you start yelling" genre) and I want to try it on a fight that keeps recuring in my group chat: are generations real? One friend is all in on cohort theory — Gen X got latchkeyed, Millennials got the 2008 recession, Gen Z got the phones, and these formative shocks shape you for life. The other thinks it's astrology for people who read Pew reports, and that "generations" are just marketing departments and lazy columnists chopping up a continuous distribution of birth years at whatever decade boundary feels catchy.

Write the joint statement those two could actually both sign. What do they agree on — about what a cohort even is, about whether "generation" picks out anything beyond birth year, about what we could in principle learn from data? Then the exact points they still disagree on, each phrased the way that side would want its own view worded (no strawmanning either one). For the cruxes, I'm pretty sure one is whether you can ever separate cohort effects from age and period effects, given the identification problem where you've got three variables and only two of them free — handle that honestly, including whether it's a genuine obstacle or just a convenient excuse for whoever's losing. Another crux is probably whether a real-but-weak effect still counts as "real." For each crux, give a case or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table. And if some of the fight turns out to be purely verbal — two different senses of "generation" doing the work — say which part and why.
