I am a high school student in our school's philosophy club, and next week I have to open the discussion on whether there can be reasons that no one has. The reading we were assigned leans hard on a comparison I want to get straight before I say anything in front of the group: it describes natural selection as a designer that works without a mind, an engineer who never sees the plans and never cares how the building turns out, and it argues from there that the living world is full of purposes — the heart is for pumping blood, the wasp's sting is for paralyzing its prey — even though no one designed the heart or wanted the wasp to sting. Our co-president then pushed it further: if nature can be full of purposes that nobody holds, it can be full of reasons that nobody thinks, so the wasp has a reason to sting, the river has a reason to carve its canyon, and a thermostat has a reason to click on; and if reasons can float free of thinkers like that, then we should be far less impressed by the claim that a moral reason has to be grasped by anyone. I am inclined to think the first step is fine and the second is not, but I cannot say where the slide happens.

Please audit the comparison. Give me the correspondence first — what plays the role of the designer, the plans, the goal, the beneficiary, the reason for acting, and the thing that could be mistaken — then the places where it breaks down: which parts of an actual designer the comparison has to drop, and which features of designing it quietly smuggles into nature. I would especially like to know which breaks matter for the two claims in play, the modest one about purposes in nature and the ambitious one about reasons floating free of thinkers, and which breaks are harmless. Finally, does the co-president's conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the argument the whole way?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question; a table of correspondences is welcome. Tell me how confident you are in each main claim and single out the point you are least sure about. Write in American English.
