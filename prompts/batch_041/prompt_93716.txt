I've been reading Pascal's Pensées in a reading group -- we've got to the fragments on justice and force, where he keeps circling the thought that justice without force is impotent and force without justice is tyrannical, and that since nobody could make either one complete on its own, people have settled for pairing them and calling the result legitimate. What I can't get out of my head is a pattern I keep meeting everywhere else, which I suspect is the same thing in a different dress, and I want to know whether it already has a name before I go inventing one. It goes like this: an institution is defending some rule, and it has two props, one of force and one of justice, and it never leans on both at once -- it alternates. Ask "why should anyone obey this?" and you get the justice prop: because the rule is fair, it treats everyone alike, it protects the vulnerable. Push back that the fairness is contested -- that reasonable people read the same facts the other way -- and the justice prop is quietly set down and the force prop picked up: well, it is the rule, it has been settled, and it will be enforced. Push on that -- settled by whom, enforced on what authority -- and you're handed back the justice prop again. The two are never on the table together, and each one only ever appears once the other has been knocked away. I can see the family resemblance to circular reasoning and to the is/ought gap, and I know legal theorists have spilled a great deal of ink on whether validity rests on enactment or on merit, but neither of those seems to be quite what I'm pointing at, because the thing I'm after is not that the justification is bad -- it's the specific choreography, the taking-turns, the fact that each prop is brought out precisely to cover the absence of the other and is then put away before it can be examined. Is there an established name for this move, or for the structure it presupposes -- that legitimacy is whatever holds when neither justice nor force will bear the weight alone? If there is one, I'd like to know how it is distinguished from mere circularity, from the positivist/natural-law dispute, and from the general observation that power dresses itself up as right; if there isn't, I'd like a term coined and defined, with the boundaries marked out -- what would count as the same move in a case where only one prop is ever in play, and what would count as a case that looks similar but isn't. Please don't stop at a label: what I actually want is the carving, the precise statement of what this thing is, what it is not, and where its edges lie. Write it in British English, and end the answer with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that gives the name or the coined term, followed by no more than fifteen words.
