A colleague and I have spent two years re-fighting one argument — whether spacetime is a substance or a system of relations — and we have agreed to write it up as an adversarial collaboration rather than continue talking past each other. I hold the relationist position: the world consists of material bodies and the spatiotemporal relations among them, and the felt need for an arena in which those bodies sit is an artefact of how we coordinate them. She holds the substantivalist position in its most defensible form: the metric is a genuine physical field in its own right, and there are spacetime points whose identity is not exhausted by facts about bodies and their relations — though she grants that there is no fact of the matter about which of two shifted, otherwise identical models is the actual world. We also agree, and this is the source of our trouble, that no experiment distinguishes our two pictures; everything we disagree about is downstream of that agreement.

Draft the document we can both sign. It should contain, in order: (1) the claims we both accept, stated so that neither of us concedes anything; (2) the precise points of disagreement, each phrased the way its own side would phrase it; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us; and (4) for each crux, the case, argument, or thought experiment we would both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect one crux is whether the substantivalist can say what work the extra entity does that the relations cannot, and another is whether the word real in our formulations is doing the work of fundamental.

Two matters I want us both on record about, whatever else we settle: what the ordinary bucket experiment is supposed to establish, and what becomes of the water in a bucket set spinning in a universe that contains nothing else. I am also curious whether anything turns on the difference between saying that there is no fact of the matter about which point is which and saying that spacetime points are not real things. If some part of our dispute is merely verbal, identify that part and say what we are actually disagreeing about once the vocabulary is fixed. Do not give me a survey of who has said what — I want the document itself. Close by saying how confident you are in the main claims you attribute to each of us, and single out the point you are least sure of.
