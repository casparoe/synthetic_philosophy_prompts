Alright, I've got about eighteen months, one co-author, and basically no budget, and I want to spend it on guilt vs. shame — the "which is the better moral emotion" question, and the weirder one underneath it, whether a culture can actually choose which of the two it runs on. I don't want a literature review or a reading list, I want help figuring out which of the open questions here is worth the time and which ones will just eat a year and produce nothing.

Here's the stuff I keep circling, so you know where I'm coming from. I buy the standard split at least roughly — guilt as a verdict on a particular act, shame as a verdict on the self, or on being seen — but I don't think it survives contact with cases. The person who can't forgive themselves for one thing they did isn't obviously feeling guilt-as-act-verdict; the person who feels dirty because of a relative's crime isn't obviously feeling shame about a self they chose. So one live question is whether that distinction is doing any real work, or whether it's two names for one thing with different audiences. Second: I keep running into the claim that shame is the worse emotion because it's about the whole self and so leads to withdrawal and hiding, while guilt is better because it's act-focused and leads to repair. But that seems backwards half the time — people repair out of shame constantly, and people sit in guilt without doing anything. So is the normative ranking of the two a real finding or an artifact of which cases people happen to pick? Third, and this is the one I actually care about: can a culture choose? I've been playing with a distinction and I can't tell if its real — between shame that's owed (you did something that merits it) and shame that's imposed (someone else's gaze, whether or not you did anything). And the choice question looks to me like it's really about whether a society can shift which of those it runs on, or whether all it can shift is which one it publicly authorizes while the underlying emotion stays put. There's also the possibility I find most annoying: that a culture can inflate shame — crank the audience and the standards until shame fires on trivia — and that this is what people mean by a shame culture, not that shame is intrinsically conformist. If that's right, the interesting variable isn't shame vs. guilt at all, it's calibration.

So given all that, which open questions should me and my co-author actually spend the eighteen months on? I want them ordered, and I want the reasoning: which are upstream of which, which are tractable for two people with no lab and no archive, which look deep but wouldn't change anything downstream, and what you'd expect to see in a year that would move one up or down the list. Also tell me which of my three framings — the distinction-doesn't-survive one, the normative-ranking-is-an-artifact one, the calibration one — is worth building on and which I should drop. And if there's a question in this area I haven't listed that you think outranks all of mine, say so and say why.
