I've been reading around the First World War and the long quarrel over how to apportion blame for it, and one image keeps doing an enormous amount of argumentative work: the Balkans as a powder keg, the assassination as the spark, the alliance system as the fuse running from one capital to the next. The moral usually drawn is that 1914 was less an act than an accident — a structure primed to go off the moment a stray match landed — and therefore that singling out one nation for primary responsibility (the thesis associated with Fritz Fischer) is a morality tale retrofitted to the events. I'm suspicious that the picture is carrying a conclusion it can't actually support, and I'd like you to audit it.

Give me the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the keg, the powder, the fuse, the spark, the bystanders — and then where the mapping breaks: which features of a real powder keg the situation lacks, and which features the image quietly imports (inevitability, passivity, symmetry of guilt, the sense that no one could have done otherwise). Then the same treatment for the rival picture, the one that says the room was full of gas because someone had been filling it for years, and that it still takes a deliberate hand to strike a match. Which of the two is doing genuine explanatory work, and which is just a mood?

Then the part I care about most, because I think it's the crux. Both pictures assume there is something there to be blamed — a nation, a system, a structure. When we hold a nation responsible for a war, what is the thing being held? The problem of many hands — that a decision distributed across dozens of offices and committees leaves no single pair of hands dirty enough to hold — seems to cut both ways here, and I want to know whether the powder-keg story genuinely exculpates anyone or merely relocates the guilt onto a collective that can neither be punished nor feel it. If the anti-Fischer conclusion survives the audit, say so plainly; if neither analogy earns it, tell me what a better one would be.
