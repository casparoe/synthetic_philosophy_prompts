a friend in my law-and-philosophy reading group sent round his one-paragraph summary of the Hart-Devlin debate and I can't tell how much of it to trust. here it is, more or less:

'Devlin's line was that any society needs a shared morality the way it needs a shared government, and that if you let one part of it erode the whole thing eventualy comes apart -- morality is a seamless web, so no piece can be loosened without loosening the rest. And since the shared morality is what keeps society from disintegrating, the law is entitled to punish immoral conduct, which is really just the harm principle applied to society instead of to individuals: the harm is the corrosion of the common morality. Hart's reply was that this is basically bunk -- people's moral views change all the time and society doesn't collapse, so there's nothing there for the law to protect, and he thought the state should keep out of private morality altogether. So Devlin is the "criminalize whatever the ordinary man finds disgusting" side, Hart is the Mill side, and Hart won because the disintegration claim is empirically false.'

which bits would Devlin happily sign, which would make Hart wince, and does the closing "Hart won because..." actually follow from the parts of the summary that are accurate? I'm inclined to think my friend is broadly right but something feels off, I just can't put my finger on it.
