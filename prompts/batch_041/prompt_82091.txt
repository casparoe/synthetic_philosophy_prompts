I retired after forty years as an experimental geophysicist and have spent the last few years reading philosophy of science, especially work on mechanisms. I keep stumbling over a question my old lab never had to ask: when a mechanistic anomaly detector flags a system, what makes the mechanism it flagged the same mechanism as one flagged in another system? Write a satirical Materials and Methods section, about 600 words, in the deadpan register of a journal article, for a study that claims to have detected a mechanistic anomaly in two systems by establishing that they share a mechanism. The humor should come from the actual difficulties of mechanism individuation: the choice of variables and coarse-graining, intervention sets, causal specificity, multiple realizability, degeneracy, and the way the reference class built into the detector's training distribution decides what counts as anomalous. Let the central joke be that the authors resolve the identity question by stipulation and then report it as a validation result. Invent a research group and a detector; do not cite real papers or researchers, and do not include a reference list. The piece should be funny to a philosopher of science who knows the individuation debate and still legible to a scientist who does not. Begin with the piece itself, with no preface or commentary.
