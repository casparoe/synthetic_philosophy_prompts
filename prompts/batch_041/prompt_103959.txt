I am a high school student putting together a one-page decision tree for my school's debate club, and I want it to sort drug-policy positions by what they actually claim rather than by the familiar labels of prohibition, decriminalization, and legalization. Build the tree from these branch questions: whether the state may coerce a competent adult who harms only himself; whether the objectionable thing, if there is one, is the act of use or the market that supplies it; whether a widely disobeyed rule is still a rule, and whether enforcement costs count among a policy's merits; and whether possession, sale, and production should be settled as one question or as three. The leaves must include criminal prohibition of possession; prohibition with diversion to treatment; depenalization, in which possession remains an offence but carries no custodial penalty; an administrative dissuasion panel sitting in place of a court; prescription or medical access to otherwise illegal drugs; sale through a state monopoly; and a taxed, licensed commercial market. Where a combination of answers leads to a position nobody defends, say so and diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate. Phrase every node so that a debater can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to.
