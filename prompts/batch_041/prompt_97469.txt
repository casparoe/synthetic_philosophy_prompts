Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the historiography of European history: period realists, who hold that the familiar divisions into a Middle Ages, a Renaissance, and a Scientific Revolution mark discontinuities that a good enough historiography would discover, and period constructionists, who hold that those divisions are imposed on a continuous record by the categories, boundaries, and timescales historians bring to it — useful for some purposes, misleading for others, and never tracking a boundary that exists independently of us. The two camps have argued for years without converging, and have agreed to produce a document instead of another exchange of essays.

The document should have two parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, phrased so that neither side has to concede anything it needs. Among them, include the claims both accept about the historical record itself, and — kept separate — the claims both accept about what is at stake in calling a period real, i.e. about what the disputed question even is. Second, a disagreements table. Each row should state: the sub-question in dispute; the realist's answer in wording the realist would accept; the constructionist's answer in wording the constructionist would accept; the crux, i.e. the further sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side; and, for each crux, a test, case, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Both sides have also agreed to assess one proposal on its merits, since it cuts across their usual lines. Treat a periodization as a lossy compression of the historical record: a scheme that groups many events under few labels, discarding detail in exchange for a shorter description. On this construal, are some periodizations objectively better than others — better in the way that a description that is shorter yet recovers more of the original is better — or does the ranking always depend on which features one chooses to compress and at what granularity? The realist and the constructionist are expected to answer differently, and the document should locate that disagreement exactly: does the compression proposal give the realist a principled sense in which a period boundary is discovered rather than stipulated, or does it merely restate the constructionist's point at one remove?

Finally, if any apparent disagreement turns out to rest on the two sides using a word differently, identify the word and say why the dispute is verbal.
