I'm about halfway through a novel set in a cannery town — five canneries, one bay, a fish stock everyone can watch shrinking — and I've written the town's collapse two different ways into two different chapters, and they keep coming out the same on the page even though I'm fairly sure their different in kind. Version one: whichever way the other canneries jump, any given cannery does better by running its nets an extra hour; the fish go, the bay goes, everyone ends up worse off than if they'd all shown restraint, and no single cannery could have saved the bay by being good on its own. Version two: a cannery only wants that extra hour if it thinks the others will take it; if it expects the others to hold back, holding back is plainly better for it, and everyone in town knows this about everyone else. Both chapters end with the same dead water and the same speech about how nobody wanted this. My hunch is that these are two different animals — in one the trouble is in what each cannery wants, in the other it's in what each cannery expects — and that the difference decides what a fix could even look like. Can you explain it to me properly, in the weeds: why the two get lumped together as one problem (all my characters say Moloch and wave their hands), what a remedy built for the first kind would fail to do for the second, and whether a remedy of that kind could actively make the second kind worse, because that's the chapter I'm stuck on.

And one more thing, because I don't want to write a scene that cheats: from the outside the two look identical — same bad outcome, same regret, same insistence that nobody chose it. So what would actually tell me which one I'm looking at? If the honest answer is that you often can't tell from the outcomes alone and have to go on something else, tell me what that something else is and how much it buys me. And if my hunch is wrong and these really are the same problem wearing two hats, say so and explain why, because then the book has a structural problem I need to fix before I write another word.
