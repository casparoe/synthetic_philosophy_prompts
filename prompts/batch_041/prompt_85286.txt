I read more alignment material than is good for me — the blogs, the workshop papers that get passed around, the comment threads under both — and I have been keeping a file of a pattern I do not have a name for. The pattern is this: a channel that was going to tell us something about a system's insides gets wired into the system's training objective, and then we go on reading the channel as though it were still a window. Three instances, in increasing order of my distance from them. Chain-of-thought started as a way of getting a model to externalize work it was already doing; it then became the main place we look to see whether a model is scheming; and then it became something we train on directly — and now I cannot tell whether the traces I read are reports or performances, or whether that distinction survives once the trace is itself a target. A friend who works on interpretability trained a linear probe to detect a particular concept in a model's activations, then used the probe as a penalty during fine-tuning so the concept would be discouraged; after training, the probe read clean and the behaviour the concept was supposed to explain was unchanged. The probe had stopped being a measurement of the model and become a component of it. And in RLHF, a signal that was meant to be a measurement of what people actually want became the thing being optimized, after which we read model outputs as evidence about what people want.

I know the neighbourhood, or think I do: Goodhart's law, Campbell's law, the observer effect, the Hawthorne effect, the Lucas critique, and the machine-learning observation that a prediction can move the distribution it predicts. But each of them seems to be about something adjacent rather than about this. Goodhart and Campbell are about a target losing its validity once it becomes a target, and they assume that the agents doing the optimizing are distinct from the thing being measured; the observer and Hawthorne effects are about disturbance, but not disturbance that is itself the product of an optimization pressure; the Lucas critique and performative prediction are about a policy or a prediction changing the world it describes, which is close, but they are about the world rather than about a channel that has been made part of the apparatus. What I keep wanting to name has, I think, three parts: the measurement sits inside the system being measured; the system is trained against it; and its readings are still consumed as testimony about the system rather than as a control signal.

So: is there a term for that, or for the failure whose structure that is? If there is not, I would rather have a coined term with a careful definition than an assurance that it is Goodhart with extra steps. Tell me what the nearest established concepts are, what each would have to be stretched to cover, which fits best, and which of my three instances are the same phenomenon and which merely rhyme. I also want the boundary cases — does a student who studies to the test count, does a firm that hires on a metric, does a thermometer we have converted into a thermostat and then gone on consulting as a thermometer? — and a description of what a clean case would look like, one where nobody would dispute that the channel has been captured and is still being read as a window.
