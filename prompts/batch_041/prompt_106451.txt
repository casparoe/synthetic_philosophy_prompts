I'm a third-year archaeology PhD and my supervisor has an eerily consistent track record on the one question that actually matters for my thesis: when are you allowed to say anything about what people *believed* from the stuff they left behind. Her calls, as best I can reconstruct them from seminar fights and my own chapter drafts:

- Big cemetery, dozens of graves, same treatment for everyone, bodies posed in ways that take real effort to arrange, grave goods made to be buried and good for nothing else. She'll say: shared belief in some kind of afterlife, no problem, infer it.
- A scatter of little figurines in a midden, no associations, no placement, no breakage pattern. She says no, that tells you nothing, people drop things.
- A whole region where for centuries everybody is buried head-north, no grave goods, no texts, no pictures. She says that's an institution, you can infer the beliefs behind it, the uniformity *is* the evidence.
- One pit at one site with smashed pots and a dog skeleton, no obvious function. She says no, there's a boring taphonomic story (it was a dump, the pots were seconds) and a one-off isn't a religion.
- A site where iconography, spatial patterning, and a decent ethnographic analogue all line up. Gold standard, she says, infer away.

Now here's my site and I can't get her to commit until I've written the chapter. One deposit, and it's the only one of its kind on the entire site, with nothing comparable for a few hundred km in any direction. A pit, carefully cut, lined with clean clay, holding about forty finely made pots that were deliberately smashed and then laid out in a spiral, plus a single polished stone worn smooth on one face. Sealed, never disturbed. There's no practical reading of it that makes sense, the pots were good ones (not seconds), there's no iconography to decode, and no living society does anything like it.

Predict her verdict on my deposit. But whats actually useful to me is the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her five earlier calls just as well, what each of those predicts about mine, which one you'd bet on and why, and which further case would really force the issue. I'm also not sure her cemetery call even distinguishes "they had a belief" from "they had a practice" — she slides between them and I want to know if that slide is doing work. If her track record genuinely underdetermines my case, please just say so instead of inventing a verdict for her.
