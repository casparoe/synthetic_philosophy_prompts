ok, Ive been reading alot about anthropic reasoning and I want a consistency audit of where I've ended up before I put any of it in writing. Here's the pile, in no particular order:

1. Self-locating credences are real and sometimes rationally required. I'm not one of the people who says "the fact that I'm me is true under every hypothesis, so it can't be evidence for anything" and then goes home.
2. I don't believe the doomsday argument. Learning that I'm somewhere in the first hundred billion or so humans doesn't move me much on how long humanity lasts.
3. The reason I don't believe it is the self-indication move: hypotheses that give rise to more observers give me more chances to be one of them, so the fact that I'm here at all is evidence for a bigger universe, and that cancels the doomsday shift.
4. I'm a thirder about Sleeping Beauty. I think the halfer's credence is either confused or at least can't be the credence they'd bet on.
5. But I think the presumptuous philosopher result is a reductio. If self-indication really works the way (3) says, then a philosopher sitting in a chair, reasoning a priori, gets to be near-certain the universe is the huge one rather than the modest one, just from the fact that he's an observer at all. I dont buy it and I wouldn't stake a nickel on it.
6. Credences are for betting. If I'm .999 on something, I should take the bets a .999 credence implies, at the odds it implies. I don't think there's a special class of credences that are true but exempt from action.
7. The reference class problem is fatal to the self-sampling assumption. Nothing non-arbitrary settles whether my reference class is humans, observers, observer-moments, things that can ask this question, and the doomsday answer swings wildly with the choice. So I'm not an SSA person.

Here's a case I made up that I keep flip-flopping on: a coin gets flipped tonight. Heads: I wake up tomorrow as usual. Tails: I wake up tomorrow as usual and an exact duplicate of me, same memories, wakes up somewhere else, and neither of us can tell which one we are. I wake up. My gut says it's still 50/50 on the coin — a copy of me existing in another room shouldn't make tails more likely for me. But the reasoning in (3) seems to say 1/3, and it's the same shape as (4).

So: what actually hangs together here, what only looks like it clashes, and what's the cheapest repair for each real tension? Order them by how bad they are, and tell me which single revision buys the most. Don't tell me everything is in tension, and don't tell me it's all fine — I want to know which pair is the actual problem. Also say how confident you are in your read, and single out the one thing in your answer you're least sure about. American English please.
