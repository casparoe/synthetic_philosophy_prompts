My friend Rosa and I got in a two-hour yelling match last night about Sen's liberal paradox and I'm still annoyed about it. My theory is she's running one rule and thinks she's running a different one, and I cant pin it down. Her record so far, all the same shape — one person's protected call against what other people want:

1. Roommates, one paperback. Mo thinks the book Dee's reading is trash, says it debases the whole apartment, can't stand having it in the house. Dee says he doesn't even care about the book and would happily toss it if it mattered that much to Mo — he just won't be told what he's allowed to read. Rosa: Dee reads whatever he wants. Mo's disgust at Dee's taste doesn't get a vote, and "it's in my home too" doesn't turn an aesthetic opinion into a claim on Dee.

2. Priya gets a nose ring, then a tattoo on her back. Her dad says it embarrasses the family and that he can barely look at her at dinner. Rosa: her body, her call, and his embarrassment is about her choices, not about his own life.

3. Flip it: Ben runs a leaf blower at 6am on Sundays in his own yard and Ana next door can't sleep. Rosa says "my yard, my call" doesn't survive contact with Ana's actual sleep — once your choice lands on somebody else's welfare it stops being just your business and the welfare side wins.

Now the case we couldn't agree on. Priya wants to donate a kidney to a stranger she matched with. Nobody in her family is sick; she just wants to do it. Her family is begging her not too. All their reasons are about themselves: they'd be terrified the whole time, if anything goes wrong they're the ones nursing her, she'd be at the back of the queue if she ever needs one herself. Nobody's saying it's gross or embarrassing or beneath the family — it's all "we're the ones who'd suffer." Priya says it's her kidney and her risk.

What does Rosa say? I want (a) the rules that fit her three earlier calls, (b) what each one predicts for Priya, (c) your best guess at what Rosa actually thinks and why, and (d) the next case I should put to her that would split whichever rules are still standing. Under 400 words, American English, and please skip the lecture on the paradox itself — I've had enough.
