I'm giving a talk at a joint math/philosophy seminar next week defending a view about Thomson's lamp that I think is the right way to kill the paradox. The view: the lamp has no determinate state at t=2, not because supertasks are impossible and not because the series is metaphysically indeterminate, but because the task only fixes the switch times and the initial state; it doesn't fix a completion rule. The state at the limit is whatever the state sequence converges to under the topology you put on the state space. In the discrete topology the sequence doesn't converge, in the trivial topology it converges to both on and off, and in a Sierpinski-like topology it converges to one of them. So the paradox is a choice point about convergence, not a contradiction about infinity. I also want to say this generalizes: every supertask needs a completion rule, and the completion rule is a substantive mathematical posit, not part of the task description.

The audience is mostly analysts who think in terms of limits and compactness, a couple of set theorists who care about nonstandard models and will bring up infinitesimals, one philosopher of math who likes constructive analysis, and a physicist who works on zeno behavior and thinks the whole thing is a physics problem. They are not Thomson scholars.
