You are a philosopher of medicine specializing in the naturalism–normativism debate over the concepts of health and disease. Please think carefully and reason from the positions themselves rather than from any literature you happen to recall; I do not need citations or reading lists, only a rigorous audit.

I recently came across this paragraph in a popular article about the philosophy of medicine, and I want to know how much of it I can trust:

"The naturalist holds that disease is a purely biological fact: a condition is a disease exactly when it is statistically abnormal for the organism and impairs its functioning, so diagnosing a disease requires no value judgment whatsoever. The normativist replies that this is naive. Disease is whatever a society decides to call disease; homosexuality was classified as a disorder a few decades ago and is not one now, which proves that the concept is entirely social. The naturalist's hope that medicine discovers objective facts about disease therefore collapses. In any case, the naturalist cannot explain why an elite swimmer's unusually large lung capacity is not a disease while a mild but nearly universal viral infection is, which shows that the so-called biostatistical approach fails on its own terms."

Break the paragraph into its distinct claims before you evaluate any of them. Then, claim by claim, tell me what a careful naturalist would accept, what is a defensible position being caricatured, and what is simply a misrepresentation — paying particular attention to what the naturalist criterion of dysfunction actually is, and whether the swimmer case really is a counterexample to the best version of that criterion. Then do the same for the writer's rendering of the normativist, saying where a careful normativist would object. Finally, assess whether the writer's conclusion — that naturalism is refuted and that disease turns out to be entirely social — follows from whatever survives correction, and close with a short verdict on how much of the paragraph a philosopher of medicine would endorse.
