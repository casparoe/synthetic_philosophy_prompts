im in a course on indian philosophy this term and we just did two weeks on the jains, mostly anekantavada — the many-sidedness thing, where no single statement can capture the whole of a thing, every claim is only true from a standpoint (a naya, a partial grasp), and the full truth is only had by an omniscient being, an arihant. the syat prefix they put on everything is supposed to mean "in some respect" rather than "maybe," and theyre adamant it makes them realists instead of relativists. anyway two people in my section, Sana and Eli, got into it on the walk back from class and i stayed out of it but i cant stop thinking about it.

Sana's move: the doctrine is relativism with extra steps and it cant survive being aimed at itself. if every judgement is only true from a standpoint, then the claim that every judgement is only true from a standpoint is also only true from a standpoint, so it gives you no reason to accept it unless you already do. and if they say it's the exception, that's just a claim to have stepped outside all standpoints, which is the one thing the doctrine says nobody can do.

Eli: you're mixing up perspectival with relative. the aspects are supposed to be really there in the object — a naya is a partial grasp of something that exists, not a truth that happens to be true for me. nothing follows about every view being as good as every other; a doctor's diagnosis and my guess are both partial and theyre not on a par. and they do think there's a complete cognition, so the claim that all standpoints fall short is anchored in something that isnt a standpoint. they even have a fixed list of the forms a claim can take — it is, in a way; it isnt, in a way; it both is and isnt, in a way; and then versions with "inexpressible" in them.

Sana: but thats the escape hatch doing all the work. to know my judgement is partial you need some grip on the whole, and by your own account only the omniscient have that, so it's a claim nobody who asserts it is in a position to make. also the aspects picture assumes there's a finished object with a definite set of sides, like a pie with a countable number of slices. the disagreements this is supposed to fix arent slice questions — is the self permanent, does the world have a beginning. saying it is and isnt in a way doesnt answer those, it just refuses to.

Eli: the "in a way" is meant to be cashed out, though — the pot is a pot now, seen from here, and is clay later, seen from there, so the respect is doing real work, it's not a shrug. and the blind men and the elephant thing is aimed exactly at your pie worry: the elephant is one thing, theyre not disagreeing about slices of nothing. the practical upshot is humility in argument, not that everyones equally right.

Sana: "the elephant is one thing" is itself a claim from outside the blind mens standpoints, so thats your own doctrine being used against you. and honestly the humility-and-tolerance reading smells like a modern retrofit — i think 20th century people read it back into a medieval logical apparatus that was mostly for winning debates with buddhists and vedantins.

Eli: fine, maybe the tolerance thing is reception history, but that doesnt touch whether the logic works. and you keep assuming a standpoint has to belong to a person — they seem to mean something more like a level of description, so "is and isnt" is two descriptions at different levels both coming out correct, not a fence-sit.

so: who's right about what here? go move by move and say who has the better of each exchange, then who's more right overall, and what both of them are missing. i dont need sources or further reading, just the argument.
