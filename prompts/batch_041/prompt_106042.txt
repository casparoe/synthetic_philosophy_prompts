Me and my friend spent two hours arguing about the affluenza case last night -- Ethan Couch, the Texas 16-year-old who killed four people driving drunk in 2013 and got ten years probation after his lawyers argued he'd never learned that actions have consequences because his rich parents never gave him a single boundary. My friend says that's just Strawson's Freedom and Resentment playing out: the court took the objective attitude, the public stayed in the participant attitude, and the outrage afterwards is his point about how you can't just stop having reactive attitudes. I think he's half right, and the half he's missing is the interesting half.

So map it properly first -- who's holding which stance at which moment, what the excuse actually is (it's one of Strawson's own examples, the kind where someone is the way they are because of how they were brought up, not a mistake or an accident), and whether a court adopting the objective attitude on the public's behalf changes anything. Then the part I care about: nobody's resentment actually got suspended, it just moved. People weren't less angry at the kid, they were furious at the parents, the psychologist, the judge, the money. Is there room in Strawson for resentment that migrates onto the excuser, or onto a whole system, or even onto a word like affluenza? My friend says that's just vicarious resentment; I say that's a cop-out, because the target isn't another agent's wrong, it's the excusing itself.

And the excuse-versus-exemption distinction people get from Watson -- is being raised with no boundaries an excuse that should get you off the hook, or an exemption that means you were never a candidate for the attitudes in the first place? Feels like neither to me.

Last thing, and be honest: where is Strawson just silent? The way I read him he's descriptive about the practice and never says which excuses ought to be accepted. If that's right, say so, and say what it does to anyone using him to argue about an actual sentence.

Dont need citations or who said what first, I want the analysis.
