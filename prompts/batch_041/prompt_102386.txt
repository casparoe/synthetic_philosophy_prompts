So my sister and I keep having this fight about whether there are things you just shouldn't be allowed to pay for, and I've realized I might be having two different fights at once without noticing. Half the time what bothers me is the money itself — that once you put a price on something it stops being the thing it was. The other half of the time what bothers me is that the person taking the money didn't really have a choice. I want you to build me a case that seperates those two, because right now I can't tell which one I actually believe.

Here's what I want it built around. Say somebody pays a person to sit in as their family at a big holiday dinner — show up, do the toasts, act like a cousin, go home. I made that up, but it seems like the kind of thing that probably happens somewhere.

Now give me four short versions of one dinner, and move two things independently:

(a) whether money changes hands or the stand-in does it for free as a favor, and
(b) whether the two of them are equals or whether the stand-in depends on the host for something big — their job, their apartment, their visa.

So: free between equals, paid between equals, free but the stand-in is dependent, paid and dependent. Everything else stays put — same people, same dinner, same behavior at the table, same amount of information, and in the two paid versions the stand-in doesn't actually need the money and could say no without anything bad happening to them. Keep it realistic, a couple sentences each, nobody's dying, no sci-fi.

Then do the work. For each of the four tell me what each of these three people would say and why:

1. someone who thinks paying for something destroys what it was,
2. someone who thinks the only real problem is ever power and need, never money,
3. someone who only cares about whether everybody ends up better off.

I want to know which of the four each one flips on, and whether any of them gets forced into a verdict they'd hate — like the money one having to admit the paid dinner is fine, or the power one having to admit the unpaid dependent version is fine. Also: does the money one actually flip only on money, or does it secretly care about the dependent case too? And if it does, is it really a seperate view at all, or just the power one in a costume?

Tell me honestly which single thing in the case is doing the work, what happens if I take it out, and how someone who likes whichever view comes off worst would try to poke a hole in the case.

What this is for: I'm trying to figure out which of the three I am. So a case that just restates the three positions back at me is no use — if the verdicts are baked into how you wrote it rather than falling out of the views themselves, it doesn't count.
