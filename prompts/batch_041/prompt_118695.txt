Two things happened in the last year that pushed me to ask this. A friend of mine who reads alot of history is now convinced that a certain government will fall inside a decade, and his case is that he's gone through four centuries of states and 'they all go in the end'. When I asked for the list it was mostly states that went; the ones that lasted he waved off as different, and 'different' always turned out to be something you can only spot after you already know how it ended. Then I realised I've definately been doing the same thing on a smaller scale: I've been trying to work out whether the company I work for will still be here in ten years, and I can get basically any answer I want by choosing who counts as 'like us'. Count the small software shops and we're doomed; count the firms that survived a bad patch and we're fine. Either way the comparison group gets picked after I already know how those cases turned out, and picked by someone who already has a view.

Is there a name for that? I know there's an old statistical worry about which group you count from, but that seems to be about not knowing the right population in the first place, whereas here the group gets assembled after the fact to fit a conclusion. I've also heard 'the outside view' and 'base rates' thrown around in these arguments and I don't think either is quite the thing I'm pointing at. What I want is the carving: what the nearest established ideas actually cover, where a legitimate analogy stops and this starts, and what you'd call it if nothing fits.
