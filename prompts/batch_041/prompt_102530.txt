I sit on the deployment review board at a mid-size health-tech company, and we vote next week on whether to ship our ICU triage module. It doesn't just rank patients by expected survival: when its scorers disagree, it runs what the engineers call a parliament. One scorer maximises expected lives saved, one refuses to deprioritise a patient for a trait they never chose (age, disability, and so on), and one gives extra weight to whoever is worst off. Each scorer gets a vote weighted by how confident our clinical advisory panel is in it, and the module ships the ranking with the highest weighted score. Two board members want the middle scorer ripped out — they say it sometimes blocks a ranking that would plainly save more lives, and that a machine has no business hedging between moral theories anyway; it should just do whichever thing is most likely to be right. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. Recommend whether we keep the parliament, drop it, or keep it with a hard cap on how many lives it's allowed to cost, give me a clear bottom line, and treat the losing side's strongest argument honestly instead of waving it away. American English, please, and don't be shy about telling me my framing is the real problem. (Yes, I know "parliament" is a stupid name, the engineers are definately too pleased with it.)
