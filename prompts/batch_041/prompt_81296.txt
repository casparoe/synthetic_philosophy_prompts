My opening shot: the more I think about it, the less your model looks able to do what it promises. Quarantine works on disease because the danger is present and mechanical — the sick guy is a hazard the way a loose ladder is a hazard, and you don't owe the ladder an explanation. But the guy in the box isn't a hazard, he's an agent, and the only thing that actually gets through to an agent is being addressed as one — told that what he did was wrong and that something is now owed for it. Take that out and you're not being humane, you're managing a population, which is exactly the attitude people have always hated about the systems you're trying to rescue us from. And here's the part that really nags me: if danger is the whole story, why does my verdict matter? Dangerous, lock him up; not dangerous, let him walk. Then what he *did* is nearly beside the point — and yet the law I'm about to swear to follow cares enormously about what he did and barely at all about his risk score.
