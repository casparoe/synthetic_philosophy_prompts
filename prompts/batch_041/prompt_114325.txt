I am a doctoral student working on the criteria of authenticity in historical-Jesus research, and I have reached the point where I need to state, as precisely as I can, what such criteria would have to do to be worth employing at all. Please write me that specification.

Set out the desiderata that any adequate criterion — or, more plausibly, any adequate set of criteria — for separating authentic from inauthentic Jesus material must satisfy. For each, give the case or consideration that motivates it, so that I can see what work it is doing and what would be lost by abandoning it. I expect the list to cover at least: the phenomena it must account for (the nature of the sources, the fact that our Greek texts stand at the end of a period of transmission, that the same saying recurs in different forms and settings, that some material is transparently the later church's product); the distinctions it must not collapse (between a saying and its setting, between the authenticity of an individual unit and the authenticity of the portrait built out of the units, between a saying's being early and its being authentic, between the historical Jesus and the kerygmatic Christ); and the results it must be consistent with, in so far as any are reasonably secure.

Then two things I care about more than the list itself. First, which of the requirements are genuine desiderata and which are merely the preferences of one camp — maximalist, minimalist, or otherwise — presented as method. Second, whether any of the requirements are jointly unsatisfiable. I have a suspicion, which I would like you to test rather than assume, that the demand for selectivity (a criterion with real discriminating power, able to reject as well as to accept) and the demand for coherence (the surviving material adding up to a recognisable figure) cannot both be met at once, and that the requirement that the criteria be usable without already knowing who Jesus was sits badly with the way the best-known criteria define themselves by contrast with the Judaism and the church of the period. Say which requirements conflict, and what that does to the whole project of using the criteria to settle who he was.

I would also like one requirement that is usually left implicit made explicit: that a criterion be calibrated against material we have independent reason to regard as late or secondary, so that we can be sure it is capable of returning a negative verdict, and that it not authenticate everything it touches.

Please give me the argument rather than a reading list: no references or bibliography. Close by saying which single requirement, if enforced, would eliminate the greatest number of the standard criteria, and what would be left standing.
