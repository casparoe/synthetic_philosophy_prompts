I teach a 16-student upper-level seminar on concepts and inductive inference, and I have one 75-minute session to fill. Design an in-class game that makes the students feel the underdetermination of a concept by a few examples — the moment when they realize the rule they all confidently "saw" was only one of indefinitely many consistent with every case they were shown, and that what made it the right rule was something in them rather than in the data.

The core should be a Bongard-style sorting task. Prepare roughly 24 cards, each showing a simple drawn figure (closed shapes, dots inside or outside, line segments crossing or not) or a short verbal description of one; deal them face up into two labeled piles, A and B, distinguished by a rule you keep hidden. Working in groups of four, students must write down the rule and may not ask you questions. Let them succeed quickly on the natural rule — the point is not to stump them. Then spring the twist: reveal a second rule, disjunctive and gerrymandered, that fits every card on the table just as well (for instance, if the natural rule was "more dots than corners," offer "more dots than corners, except for triangles with an even number of dots, which count as A regardless"). Then deal a few new cards and let the groups bet, with points, on which rule predicts them. The natural rule survives; the gerrymander fails. Close by having each group try to state what, exactly, ruled the second rule out — they will reach for simplicity, projectibility, or naturalness, and you can let them argue.

Give me the full runnable package: materials, group roles, the sequence with timings, a scoring scheme that needs no real stakes, the wording of the reveal, and a debrief that maps each beat onto a piece of the idea — hypothesis-driven perception at the aha, multiple consistent rules at the reveal, the new cards onto projectibility and inductive bias, and the group that defends the gerrymander onto the creativity of concept formation. Say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which feature of the idea the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself.

Keep the whole response to 650 words or fewer, in American English. State how confident you are that this design will actually produce the realization in the students, and single out the one element you are least sure of.
