I am drafting a short essay on Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain example and would like substantive comments before writing it up. My working notes are as follows.

Nozick's target is what he calls patterned, or end-state, principles of distributive justice — principles that specify how a distribution ought to look along some dimension, whether need, effort, desert, or equality. His own entitlement theory is meant to be historical rather than patterned: a distribution is just, on his view, if it arose through just acquisition, just transfer, and, where the past has been unjust, rectification.

The example itself: suppose some distribution D1 is just by whatever standard one favours. A million people, each free to spend their money elsewhere, voluntarily pay twenty-five cents to watch Wilt Chamberlain play basketball. Chamberlain ends up $250,000 richer, giving distribution D2. Every transfer was voluntary, and on the assumption that D1 was just, no one appears to have been wronged along the way, so D2 appears to be just as well. But D2 will not display the pattern that D1 displayed. It follows that no patterned principle can be continuously realized without continuous interference in people's choices, since any free transaction may carry the distribution away from the pattern. The same difficulty, I take it, afflicts historical principles too: any principle that tells us what people ought to end up with, including Nozick's own entitlement theory, implicitly fixes a distribution that voluntary transfers can disturb. And the principle of rectification that Nozick appends to his two principles of acquisition and transfer is a minor addendum that can be set aside, since in a market society nearly every holding traces back to acquisitions and transfers that were just.

The conclusion I want the essay to draw is that a commitment to any pattern is a commitment to perpetual interference, and therefore that patterned principles are untenable for anyone who takes liberty seriously.

I would like comments on two things in particular. First, accuracy: does this exposition get Nozick's own position right, and is the reasoning as stated a sound reading of his argument? Second, is the conclusion in the final paragraph defensible, or does it claim more than the example can support? Please identify the specific sentences that are wrong or too strong and indicate how they should be reformulated.
