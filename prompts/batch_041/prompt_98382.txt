Two colleagues — a constructivist who accepts only potential infinity and refuses to treat an infinite collection as a completed object, and a classical set theorist for whom a completed infinite is no more problematic than the number 7 — have spent a year arguing about Hilbert's hotel, bijections, and whether the axiom of infinity posits an object or merely licenses a procedure. Write the joint statement they should be able to sign. Begin with a single sentence naming the point the collaboration actually settled — the locus of the real remaining dispute — then give a numbered section of claims both accept: about the formalism, about what the phrase "same size" can legitimately mean, and about which moves neither side regards as live. Follow it with a disagreements table in which each residual disagreement is stated twice, once in wording that the side holding it would endorse; for each, name the crux it rests on and a case or argument both sides agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Treat at least these as candidate cruxes: whether a bijection can establish sameness of size at all, whether "there exists an infinite set" is a claim about objects or about rules of construction, and whether any super-task — a lamp toggled at halving intervals, or a vase that ends empty although a ball is added at every finite stage — tells against completed infinity. If part of the quarrel turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Format: the one-sentence bottom line first, then the numbered agreed claims, then the table.
