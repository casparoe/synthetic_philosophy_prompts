Two people in my department have been arguing for a year without converging. One holds that the climate problem is at bottom a pricing problem: a steadily rising carbon price, with the revenue recycled to households, is the indispensable instrument and everything else is secondary. The other holds that pricing of that kind is politically and physically inadequate, and that what is required is degrowth — binding caps and mandates, rationing where necessary, and a deliberate reduction in the throughput of the economy rather than the pursuit of green growth. Write the joint statement they could both sign. It should contain a numbered list of claims both accept, including about what the ultimate target variable is (emissions, energy and material throughput, GDP, welfare) and about which findings each side already treats as settled; the residual disagreements in a table, each stated in wording that its own side would accept and that the other would recognise as fair; and the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — with each side's prediction recorded in advance and, for each crux, a case, argument or measurement both sides agree would bear on it. Where you judge that they have been talking past each other rather than disagreeing, say so explicitly: I suspect part of the quarrel is over whether degrowth names a fall in GDP or a fall in throughput, and over whether a carbon-pricing advocate who also accepts sufficiency policies and shorter working hours has already conceded most of the degrowth case. Treat one further crux with care: whether rich countries' falls in territorial emissions represent genuine decarbonisation or partly offshored emissions, and whether consumption-based accounting changes the verdict on who is decarbonising. Neither side should be made to look stupid or insincere, and no claim should be included that either side could not defend in front of its own camp.
