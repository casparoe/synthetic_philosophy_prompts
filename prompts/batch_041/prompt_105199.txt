I write about science and ideas for a general readership, and I'm at work on a long essay about how we talk about uncertainty — why professions that live by prediction keep returning to an admission of ignorance, and whether the machinery of probability we have built since really absorbed what the older writers were after. Before I commit to a line, I want the strongest version of the other side to argue me out of my own view, so I'd like you to take a role and hold it.

Play Keynes — the Keynes of the Treatise on Probability and of his later reflections on uncertainty — and stay in character until I tell you to stop. Constrain yourself to the concepts he actually had, plus whatever I supply; if I use a piece of modern apparatus he wouldn't recognize, ask me what it means rather than quietly adopting it. He should be willing to grant particular points that are genuinely good, but he must not surrender the two commitments I intend to attack: that probability is at bottom a logical relation between evidence and conclusion, and that the weight of argument is a genuine second dimension of an evidential situation rather than another word for confidence. Give him his real resources — his doubt that all probabilities are even comparable, his sense that some questions are not merely low-probability but of a different kind altogether. Keep each turn to a paragraph or two, measured and a little donnish, so this feels like a conversation rather than a lecture. And write in continuous prose throughout — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just the way one writes a letter.

Here is my opening move. I mean to show you that the moderns have already absorbed both of your ideas and left nothing over. Begin with weight. Take the familiar pair of coins — one known to be fair, one about which you know nothing — both leaving you at one-half, the first resting, you say, on more weight. But a subjectivist can define weight for himself as resilience: how far your present opinion would survive further evidence, the revision still to come. That is a fact about your current state, computable from your degree of belief together with what you take the evidence to be able to do, and it shifts exactly where you wanted weight to shift — so weight turns out to be a shadow of probability, not a dimension beside it. As for the logical relation, the subjectivists abandoned that long ago; for them the only real thing is the degree of belief a coherent agent happens to have, and your coins and your urns never display anything else. So tell me plainly what your logical relation explains that their degrees of belief do not.
