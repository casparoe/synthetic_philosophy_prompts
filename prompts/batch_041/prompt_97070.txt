Ok so I need help writing a research proposal, and I should warn you I'm coming at this sideways. I spent 35 years as a physiologist — metabolic stuff, a bit of gut-brain work near the end — and retired two years ago. Since then I've been reading philosophy the way I used to read journals, which is to say obsessively and without much regard for what people who know better consider settled.

Here's what's eating me. Everyone tells me Nietzsche's remarks on food, climate, walking, and general regimen — the ones in the autobiographical book where he keeps going on about what he eats and where he lives — are either an embarassing sideshow to the real philosophy or just a sick man rationalizing his own bad health. But when I actually read them they look to me like a theory. A real one, with something like mechanisms, dose-response intuitions, and predictions. He treats regimen as upstream of thought: what you eat and where you live and how far you walk doesn't merely make you feel better, it determines what you are able to think. He has a phrase for this, the great health, which he seems to think you have to earn physiologically before you're entitled to certain ideas. And it sits right next to his attack on asceticism, which is the part that breaks my brain — he spends the whole third essay of the Genealogy trashing the ascetic ideal and then, later, hands us a regimen. A diet. Is that not the same move wearing a different coat?

What I want is a proposal — for a one-year fellowship at the philosophy department of my old university, they take outside applicants and I think a reformed experimentalist is a plausible sell — on whether this dietetics is load-bearing in his philosophy or detachable from it, and more sharply, on what it would even mean for claims like these to be right or wrong. Can you turn regimen-shapes-thought into something with a truth value that a physiologist could in principle test, or does that translation destroy the thing he was actually claiming? I want the proposal to (1) state the question so it has a determinate answer, (2) say why the obvious readings — it's autobiography, it's pathology, it's ancient dietetics reheated — don't settle it, (3) lay out what I'd actually do over the year, in order, keeping in mind I read German slowly and badly, (4) say what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing, and (5) be honest about whether a year is enough or whether I'm fooling myself.

Two things. Don't give me a bibliography or a reading list — I'll build that myself and I don't want you guessing at editions or page numbers. And lean into the physiology rather than running from it; the whole reason I think I have anything to say here is that I know what a mechanism looks like and I'm not convinced these passages lack one.
