I run the sovereign-risk desk at a mid-sized endowment -- we have positions in about forty countries, our investment committee signs off on a twenty-year horizon, and every quarter I have to hand them a single number per country: the probability that the current political order survives the next two decades. Right now the whole thing is vibes. My analysts read the news, write a paragraph, and then we all sort of converge on a number that feels right, and I know that's just narrative with a decimal point stapled to it. I want to rebuild it around the outside view -- reference classes, base rates, the whole apparatus -- but in a form my team can actually run every quarter, a day or two per region, without anyone having a stats PhD.

Can you write me that procedure? The actual sequence, in order. How to turn "will this regime last" into an event you can count. How to pick a reference class -- and I want real discipline here, becuase I'm convinced the choice of class is where all the cheating happens, so give me a ladder from broadest to narrowest (all country-years at the bottom, the country's own history at the top, or whatever ordering you think is right), a rule for when to move up or down the ladder, and a rule for what to do when two rungs give you very different numbers. How to get from a count of past cases to a base rate when the counts are small and the cases aren't independent. How to fold in the inside-view judgement my analysts are actually good at without letting it swallow the base rate. And where the procedure is allowed to stop and just say "no number."

Two fights I keep having with my team, and I want the procedure to take a side. First, age: plenty of these regimes have already outlasted the median case in their class by decades. Is a long-lived regime more likely to keep going, or is it overdue? Second, my twenty-year window is longer than almost any episode I can find to count, so am I just compounding a per-year hazard and calling that a forecast? Tell me when that move is legit and when it's a swindle. Part of why I'm asking is that I've been reading the empire-lifespan stuff on the side, and everyone seems to pick a different class of empire and land on a different answer, which is exactly my fear.

Then run the procedure on three cases so I can watch it work: (a) a small oil monarchy that's been stable for eighty years and happens to be our single largest position; (b) a country that just survived a failed coup attempt, where the class of "failed coups" is thin and I suspect the standard base rate is misleading; (c) a case where I think the procedure is going to tell me to cut a position I really don't want to cut, because the number comes out worse than what the committee is used to. Don't soften that one, I'd rather see it bite.

And be honest about the failure modes -- where the thing will spit out a confident-looking number that's garbage, and which steps are judgement calls wearing a lab coat. My team will treat this as box-ticking if I let them, so flag every place a lazy analyst can fake compliance.
