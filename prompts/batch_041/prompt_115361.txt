I'm a science journalist — for the past several years I've been writing about how people actually reason under uncertainty: forecasting tournaments, replication failures, the long-running mess over what a p-value is supposed to mean — and I'm now putting together an application for a three-month residential fellowship that funds public-facing work on rationality and decision-making. What I want to do with the fellowship is write a short working paper, maybe eight thousand words, on the Judy Benjamin problem, plus a long-form piece for a general readership, and I need help turning a half-formed hunch into a proper proposal: a question stated precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, an account of why the existing answers leave it open, the method, what a result would look like, what would count as the project failing, and a scope that is honest about three months of interrupted work. I'll add the citations myself, so don't worry about the literature or about reading lists — I want positions and arguments, not references. The case, as I carry it around: a soldier, Judy, is dropped into territory she knows to be divided into a Blue region and a Red region, with the Red region itself divided into Red Headquarters and a Red second area; she puts even odds on Blue versus Red, and within Red she puts three-quarters on Headquarters. A radio message then reaches her that says, in effect, that if she is in Red territory then she is in the Headquarters area — a conditional, delivered as a conditional. How should her degrees of belief change? The received answers come apart: minimising the relative entropy between her old and new credences, reading the message as a material conditional and conditionalising on it, and lifting the probability of the conditional itself each deliver a different posterior, and as far as I can tell the disagreement has never been made to go away.

My hunch, and the thing I'd like you to help me turn into something crisp, is that the disagreement is an artefact of not modelling the channel: everyone treats the message as a constraint on Judy's posterior while leaving the informant, the informant's reliability, and the semantics of a conditional utterance outside the model entirely. If you put the source back in — give it a stated track record, make what it says an event, conditionalise on that event — then either the rival rules collapse into one another or they don't, and either outcome tells you something. So the primary question I want the proposal to pose is whether the Judy Benjamin disagreement survives an explicit model of the information channel, and the secondary question is whether the disagreement matters for what Judy should *do* or only for what she should *believe* — the example began life as a decision problem, about finding her unit, and has been converted into a credence puzzle, and I half suspect that conversion is where the trouble starts. I'd also like the proposal to say why this is a better use of three months than the two obvious alternatives: arguing for one of the existing rules with a fresh round of intuitions, or inventing yet another update rule.

Concretely, the proposal should lay out what I would actually do week by week: build the smallest channel model that stays faithful to the example; derive each rival rule's answer inside that model; hunt for the conditions under which the rules coincide and the conditions under which they diverge; then re-run the case as a decision problem, with a plausible utility for finding her unit, to see whether the credence disagreement washes out or actually drives the choice. It should say what a good outcome looks like — ideally a clean characterisation, something like the rules agreeing exactly when the channel has a certain form — and what failure looks like, which I take to be the discovery that modelling the channel requires deciding the very question at issue, so that the dissolution is circular and the puzzle simply stands. It should also be straight about the fact that the paper is meant to be readable by people who are not decision theorists, since that is the whole point of the fellowship, and take a stand on the belief-versus-action framing rather than leaving it as a footnote.
