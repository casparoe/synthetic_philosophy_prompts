I recently participated in a sleep study that employed a reversible amnesia protocol, and the experience has made the Sleeping Beauty problem feel less like an abstract puzzle and more like a description of my week. Two friends of mine, one a committed halfer and one a committed thirder, have been arguing about it for months, and I want to force them to produce the document they should have written after a genuine adversarial collaboration. Please write that joint statement.

It should have four parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims. These should cover what both sides accept about the standard Sleeping Beauty problem: the setup, the credence question, what the amnesia does and does not do, what follows if Beauty learns the day or the coin outcome, and what is simply not in dispute. Second, a precise statement of their disagreements, with each side's position written in a formulation that side would sign. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, identify a case, argument, or test that both sides agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Fourth, a short prose section identifying any part of the dispute that is merely verbal and explaining why.

To keep this from being a rehearsal of the standard literature, the collaboration must also address a variant I will call the Variable Awakenings variant. On Sunday, Beauty is told that a fair coin will be flipped. If it lands heads, she will be awakened once, on Monday. If it lands tails, a second fair coin will be flipped: if that coin lands heads, she will be awakened twice, on Monday and Tuesday; if it lands tails, she will be awakened three times, on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday. After each awakening she is given an amnesia drug that erases the memory of that awakening. On waking, she does not know the day, the outcome of either coin, or how many awakenings she has had. She is asked for her credence that the first coin landed heads. Both sides should state that credence, and both should predict what she should do if, on each awakening, she is offered a bet on the first coin at 2:1 odds and must decide whether to accept, knowing that her payoff will be the sum of the payoffs of all bets she accepts.

Format the response as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table with columns for the issue, the halfer formulation, the thirder formulation, the crux, and each side's predicted outcome for the agreed test, and then the short prose section on verbal disputes. The whole document must be under 1,200 words. Do not include citations, references, or a reading list.
