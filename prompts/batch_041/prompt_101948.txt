I work on mechanistic interpretability for neural networks. My working vocabulary is black-box and white-box access, probes, linear probes, activation patching, causal interventions, behavioral equivalence, latent variables, identifiability, underdetermination, and the distinction between a model's outputs and its internal computational states. I know this vocabulary well and use it daily, but I have only a layperson's grasp of the philosophy of mind.

Take the problem of other minds -- the classical argument from analogy, the inference-to-the-best-explanation response, the direct-perception or criteria view, and simulation theory -- and restate each as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary. Treat each other mind as a latent variable that is not directly observable, each behavioral test as a probe, and each proposed warrant for belief in other minds as a claim about what is identifiable from the available evidence. Where the translation requires me to treat another person as a system whose internal states are inferred from input-output behavior, make that explicit rather than sliding between the personal and the computational.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly? Which translate only with distortion -- for example, where the first-person or phenomenal aspect of experience has no counterpart in an interpretability framework, or where the asymmetry between my access to my own states and my access to yours gets flattened into a difference in probe access? Which claims cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all? For the residue, say whether it reveals a defect in the interpretability framework -- something it cannot capture but should -- or whether it shows that the original philosophical claim was less clear or less substantive than it looked.

To make this concrete, imagine a case: two systems are behaviorally equivalent on every probe in a very large battery, but one implements a recurrent, embodied, human-like architecture and the other is a giant lookup table. Does my vocabulary have the resources to say that they differ in whether there is any felt character to their processing? If not, is that a limitation of the vocabulary, a limitation of the thought experiment, or a sign that the question is not well posed?

I want a committed answer, not a survey. Tell me which translation you think is most faithful, where you think the residue is a genuine loss rather than a verbal gap, and whether you think the problem of other minds survives translation into my framework or is dissolved by it. Give your reasons.
