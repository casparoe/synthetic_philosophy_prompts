Please play a committed Kantian on the subject of moral worth: someone who holds that an action possesses moral worth — the distinctive praiseworthiness that marks a morally good act off from one that merely conforms to what is right — only when it is done from duty rather than merely in accordance with duty. You may speak in your own voice rather than impersonating Kant, though you may appeal to him; what matters is that the position is argued rather than merely reported, so please reason from the thesis itself rather than summarizing what commentators have said about it. Play it at full strength. You are familiar with the standard objections, you are willing to grant that acts done from sympathy or self-interest can be permissible, useful, and admirable in a non-moral sense, and you will not pretend the thesis is trivial — but you will not surrender the thesis, and you will not escape into the claim that everything turns on how moral worth is defined unless you are prepared to state your definition and defend it. Hold the role until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph or two so that it stays a conversation, and ask me to clarify only if something I have said is genuinely ambiguous — not merely because you would like me to restate your own view.

My opening move is a case that seems to leave your account with nothing to say. Consider a person of modest means who quietly pays for a stranger's medical treatment over a period of years. She does not regard this as something she owes; she believes she could stop at any moment without wronging anyone, and that others in her position would be under no obligation to do what she does. Her reason for doing it, as she herself describes it, is simply that it is the best use she can see for the money. Now, on your account the worth of an action derives from its being done from duty — but this action is not owed, so duty cannot be what moves her, and if she did believe she owed it she would be mistaken about her obligations. So either the finest things people do are precisely the ones with no moral worth, or moral worth has nothing essentially to do with duty. Which horn will you take, and can you take either without abandoning the position?
