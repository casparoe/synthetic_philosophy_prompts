I am a novelist writing a story set in an immense library whose master catalogue is compiled by one rule: for every catalogue in the library, the master catalogue lists it if and only if that catalogue does not list itself. My editor calls the scene a logic puzzle and wants the clerk to enter the master catalogue into itself and be done. I want the scene to turn on a real limit, not a trick. The clerk has tried every variant: if the master catalogue lists itself, the rule says it must not; if it omits itself, the rule says it must. The library board proposes to solve the problem by declaring that the master catalogue is simply not one of the catalogues the rule ranges over, or by splitting the library into levels so that a catalogue can only list catalogues below it. Is either of those a principled fix, or are they just ways of changing the subject? What should the clerk conclude—that the founding rule was ill-formed, that there can be no single catalogue of all catalogues, or something else? I need her final position, and I need to know what it costs her to hold it. Please begin your reply with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
