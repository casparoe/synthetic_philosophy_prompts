I just got roped onto the board of the lake association where my parents have a place, and I keep running into something I dont have a word for.

Twenty-five odd years ago the walleye in our lake crashed and the old-timers pushed through a size limit plus an unwritten rule that you threw back anything under a certain length, and honestly it worked. The fish came back. The lake is about as good as anyone can remember it. Here's the part I can't name: the fish came back and the rule didn't. The people who kept it are mostly dead or moved to Florida, and the younger crowd fishes like the crash never happened, even though everybody agrees the fish are only back because of it. We wrote the limit into the regs again last year and its like it never existed before — nobody feels like they're breaking something they grew up with, they're just following a sign, and it took maybe five years for the new rule to be as ignored as the old one was obeyed.

I saw the same shape at my last job with the office kitchen. Someone finally put up a sign, it was clean for about a year, the sign came down during a remodel, and it went back to being disgusting faster than it ever got fixed — even though everyone had lived through the clean period.

What I'm after is whether there's a name for this: a shared resource that can be restored, and a habit of not taking too much that can't be restored along with it, or at least takes way longer, so the second time around you're starting from scratch even though the lake looks identical to how it looked back when the rule was strong. Is it just hysteresis? Is it the shifting-baseline thing? I know it's near the tragedy of the commons but that one is about taking too much, not about the restraint rotting away after the resource comes back. What I actually care about is the asymmetry — the resource has a recovery time, the rule has a seperate and longer one, and nobody budgets for it. Don't just hand me a label, tell me where the line is.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, no preamble and no summing up at the end. Items 1 through 4: the closest existing ideas, ordered from nearest to furthest, each a sentence or two on what it captures about my case and where it stops fitting. Item 5: your verdict — either the concept that actually fits and why, or, if none does, your own name for the thing with a one-line definition and one case that should be kept out of it. For each item say how confident you are, and tell me which one you're least sure about.
