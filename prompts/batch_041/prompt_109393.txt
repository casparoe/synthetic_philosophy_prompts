Exam question:
A research group proposes a programme called chronobiotic endocrinology. Its Hard Core states that all major mood disorders are caused by a single master circadian clock in the hypothalamus, and that the clock's phase can be reset only by ambient light. Its Protective Belt contains auxiliary hypotheses about receptor sensitivity, seasonal variation, and diagnostic thresholds. In its first decade, the programme predicts that a new class of drugs acting on a specific clock gene will relieve symptoms in a subset of patients, and that the effect will vanish in knockout animal models. Both predictions are confirmed. In its second decade, a large clinical trial fails. Defenders add an auxiliary hypothesis: a common antidepressant alters the clock's phase in a way that masks the drug's effect. They then derive a new prediction: patients carrying a particular polymorphism should respond only to a modified dosing schedule. That prediction is also confirmed. Using Lakatos's methodology, assess whether this programme is progressive or degenerating. Explain the roles of the Hard Core and Protective Belt, distinguish progressive from degenerating problem shifts, and state what Lakatos would advise the researchers to do.

Student's answer:
Lakatos's methodology says that a research programme has a Hard Core of central assumptions that are protected from refutation by a Protective Belt of auxiliary hypotheses. A programme is progressive when it predicts novel facts that are later confirmed, and degenerating when it only patches up anomalies after the fact. In the first decade, the chronobiotic programme is clearly progressive: it predicted the drug effect and the knockout result, and both were confirmed. The second decade is more complicated. The failed trial is an anomaly, and the defenders respond by adding an auxiliary hypothesis about the antidepressant masking the effect. This is a classic ad hoc rescue, so the programme has become degenerating. The fact that the new polymorphism prediction was confirmed does not change this, because the prediction was invented only after the anomaly and was designed to save the Hard Core. Lakatos, following Popper, holds that a programme that needs such rescue is pseudoscientific and should be abandoned. He would advise the researchers to give up the Hard Core and replace it with a new one, while keeping the Protective Belt, since the belt contains the empirically useful parts of the theory. The only thing the researchers did right was to keep making testable predictions; that shows they are not outright dogmatists, but it does not make the programme progressive.
