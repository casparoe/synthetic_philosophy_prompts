I'm putting together a prospectus for a one-year MA thesis (12-15k words, realistically 8 months of work) and I want to take a swing at the advance directive problem, but not down the usual path.

The usual setup: someone writes a directive refusing treatment in case of advanced dementia, later she seems settled and doesn't refuse anything, and we ask whether the earlier self gets to decide for the later one. The debate seems stuck between two autonomy claims -- the earlier self's interest in shaping her own life, and the later self's claim to be governed by her present values -- and I think that's why it goes in circles. My half-formed hunch is that we're cramming two separate questions into one. First: does the earlier self have standing to bind at all, i.e. is there any authority that runs forward across a large change in the person? Second: even if it doesn't, does the directive still give the later self a reason to comply -- because of reliance, because of the promise-like shape of the thing, because of the value of a life not being a series of unrelated selves? These can come apart, and I suspect a lot of the writing answers one while thinking it has answered the other.

Two things I think the standard debate underweights and want to fold in. First, transformative experience: the person writing the directive is deciding about a state of mind she cannot imagine from the inside, and that's a strange kind of ignorance to build a binding decision on -- compare a will, where we shrug at the dead hand's ignorance because the stakes are just money. Second, the disability critique: the worry that these directives get written by able-bodied people projecting onto a future disability and systematically misjudging it, which if true makes the whole thing not a neutral exercise of autonomy but a distorted one. I'd like to use the constitutional analogy too -- entrenched vs living -- to see whether self-binding across time is better modeled as entrenchment or as amendment.

Turn this into the prospectus: state the question so it has a determinate answer, say why the existing positions don't settle it, lay out the method month by month (what I actually read, what I actually write, where the argument gets tested against cases), say what a successful result looks like versus what would count as the project failing or collapsing into a literature summary, and be honest about whether this fits in 8 months or needs to be cut.
