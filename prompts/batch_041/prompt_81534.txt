I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and for the past several months I've been circling a cluster of problems that all strike me as versions of one question: whether the higher-level, symbolic or representational descriptions we give of a system can be said to do any causal work, or whether all of the pushing is done at the level of the substrate. Hofstadter's careenium is the cleanest version of it I know. Simms bounce around a table, magnetically clump into simmballs, and the simmballs then appear to interact with one another in ways that track features of the world outside; the question of who shoves whom around inside the careenium is supposed to admit two legitimate answers depending on the level at which you look, and the whole point is that neither answer is obviously the wrong one. What frustrates me is that the thought experiment is almost always deployed as a vivid illustration of a position rather than treated as a research programme, and I suspect the reason is that nobody has sorted out which of the questions it raises is upstream of the others. The metaphysical question about causal exclusion, the semantic question about what makes a pattern real enough to be referred to at all, the formal question about how to measure the causal efficacy a coarse-grained description retains relative to its fine-grained realization, and the methodological question about when a working cognitive scientist is entitled to invoke the symbolic level all feel entangled, but I can't tell whether they are genuinely entangled or whether I'm just confused about the order of explanation.

I have roughly a year to spend on this, in the form of a qualifying paper plus a summer, and I'd like to commit to the piece of it that the rest depends on rather than the piece that happens to be most discussed. So I'd like a dependency-aware prioritization of the open questions in this area: which one, if settled, would most reduce the pressure on the others, and which ones are downstream enough that a good answer would mostly be a restatement of something already decided elsewhere. Please defend the ordering rather than just listing items. I care about the criteria you use — whether a question is upstream of others, how tractable it is for someone with a philosophy-of-mind background and enough formal competence to read the coarse-graining and interventionist-causation literature but not to prove new theorems, how neglected it currently is, and how much would actually change downstream if it were answered — and I want you to say explicitly where those criteria conflict and how you'd trade them off. I'd also like an honest verdict on which of these questions are merely terminological, in the sense that they would dissolve rather than be answered once we had a better account of causation, since I'd rather not spend a year on a question whose apparent hardness is an artifact of how it's phrased.

Finally, tell me what would move items up or down this ranking over the next year or two. Concretely: what kind of result, argument, or formal development in the philosophy of causation or in the theory of coarse-graining would make the question you've put first suddenly look less central, or make something you've deprioritized look like the real crux? I'm less interested in a survey of positions than in a defended agenda with a clear view about what the field is getting wrong about the order in which these things should be settled. You can refer to arguments and positions as you like, but please don't bother with citations or reading lists — I'll track those down myself.
