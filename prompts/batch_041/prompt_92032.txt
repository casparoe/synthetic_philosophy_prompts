Suppose we try to isolate the Meno question from its usual instrumental framing. Two subjects hold the same true belief with the same content; they share the same evidence history, the same dispositions to revise, the same reliability about the matter going forward, and the same willingness to act and assert on it. The only stipulated difference is that one subject's belief meets a modal anti-luck condition (safety, or a comparable non-accidentality condition) while the other's does not, and we simply hold every downstream consequence fixed, so that no instrumental difference remains. The residual question is whether the first subject's state is better than the second's as a state, independently of anything it will go on to produce.

Give me your credence that the answer is yes — that there is a species of epistemic value attaching to knowledge and not to mere true belief which is neither instrumental (better future performance) nor merely pragmatic or attributive (a feature of our practices of ascribing knowledge or granting credit), but belongs to the state of knowing as such. Then decompose that number. I want it to rest on at least the following axes, each with a rough probability and a one-line justification:

(i) whether the Meno intuition about the value of knowledge survives once all instrumental differences are stipulated away, or evaporates as an artifact of the ordinary cases in which knowledge and true belief differ in their consequences;
(ii) whether the swamping objection is fatal — that is, whether any feature that could ground the extra value is one the mere true belief in my clean case also possesses;
(iii) whether epistemic value can be intrinsic or finally valuable at all, or whether veritism is correct that only true belief is valuable for its own sake and everything else is derivative;
(iv) how much of the apparent value difference is better explained by the social functions of knowledge — assurance, testimonial license, the termination of inquiry — rather than by anything in the believer's state; and
(v) how sensitive the answer is to the correct analysis of knowledge, given that the clean case presupposes that knowledge picks out a determinate state rather than a loose cluster.

Show how the sub-probabilities combine into your final number, and tell me which term the number is most sensitive to. I am at roughly 0.35 myself; if you land far from that, say which sub-question you think I have misweighted. If you think the question is ill-posed as stated, say why, then give a number conditional on the most defensible precisification you can supply. Finally, name the single argument or result that would move your credence most, and in which direction.
