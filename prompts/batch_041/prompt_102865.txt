For a while now I've been bothered by how the individualism-versus-collectivism argument gets conducted in popular writing, and the last time it came up it was over dinner with friends who had each read a different op-ed and spent an hour talking past one another. Someone says East Asian cultures are collectivist and Western ones individualist, someone else objects that this is a stereotype, and the argument never gets past duelling anecdotes — because nobody has separated the several different questions that the word collectivism is being used to answer all at once. I'd like a proper sorting instrument, in the form of a decision tree, or a taxonomy whose branches are each labelled with the question that splits them from their siblings, that takes a reader from their answers on concrete questions to a named position and shows how many genuinely distinct positions the crude East/West binary flattens together. Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names: first the questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then the actual positions located within that space, and wherever a combination of answers is left empty, a sentence on whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely never defended.

Keep these four things apart, since collapsing them is exactly what I am complaining about: whether the community is supposed to be ontologically prior to the person; whether social facts are supposed to be irreducible to facts about individuals; whether the good of the group is supposed to outweigh the good of the person; and whether rights and duties are supposed to attach to individuals, to families or lineages, or to groups as such. I also want the tree to have room for two options the usual binary cannot place. The first is a view on which a person is not a bounded unit at all but a composite of relationships, so that obligations and even parts of one's agency can be held by, or spread across, other people — which seems to be neither standard individualism nor standard collectivism, and which I would like placed rather than ignored. The second is a view on which both the individual self and the collective are denied ultimate reality, so that the whole debate is diagnosed as resting on a mistake. And I'd like the questions to be able to catch two cases that embarrass the geography: a Western position that is collectivist in the political sense, treating freedom as something achieved through collective self-rule; and an East Asian position that is individualist in the ethical sense, insisting on personal responsibility and self-cultivation while still holding that persons are constituted by their relations.

Aim for eight to twelve leaves, each carrying the position's name (or "unnamed" if nobody defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Phrase every question as something a reader could answer without already knowing which position it leads to — no question should simply ask whether they are an individualist or a collectivist. Finally, flag any two leaves your questions cannot separate, and tell me whether the surviving distinctions are enough to show that the East/West contrast, at best, picks out a single diagonal through a much larger space.
