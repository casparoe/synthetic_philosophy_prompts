I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and the case I have never been able to settle is the one where enormous numbers of mild annoyances are weighed against one person's agony. I have come to think my confusion has a nameable shape, and I want to turn it into a proposal. The debate runs together two claims under the heading of commensurability, and I suspect they can come apart. The first is that pleasure and pain admit of a common cardinal scale, so that sums and ratios of them mean something. The second is that trade-offs between them are bounded: for any finite amount of suffering, some finite amount of pleasure outweighs it. Lexical threshold negative utilitarianism looks to me like the position that takes the first locally and rejects the second globally — below some threshold of suffering, pains and pleasures are weighed on one scale, and above it a single pain outweighs any quantity of pleasure, however great. Both halves are doing work: the threshold blocks the conclusion that we should end all life, and the local scale blocks the conclusion that a stubbed toe outweighs a lifetime of happiness. If that reading is right, the two standard arguments are mirror images of one conflation. The case for lexicality — that no amount of bliss compensates for one agony — is an argument against boundedness alone. The case against it — that if you accept a scale at all you have accepted an exchange rate, and any exchange rate can be beaten by enough pleasure — is an inference from the scale to boundedness, which is exactly the step I want under a microscope.

Write me a short research proposal of the kind you would submit with a small grant application for a six-month independent project in value theory, on the question: can a value ordering be locally commensurable and globally lexical, and if so, what must the threshold be a threshold of for the position to hold together? State the question so that it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing positions leave it open, set out the method and why that method rather than the obvious alternatives, say what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing, and keep the scope honest for six months. I will handle citations, so no bibliography or reading list. And if the distinction between the two senses of commensurability is spurious — if a common scale really does force an exchange rate, so that the framing collapses — say so plainly rather than writing the proposal around a distinction that is not there. I would rather learn that now than in month five.
