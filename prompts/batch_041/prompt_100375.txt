I sit on my student government's Judicial Committee as an undergraduate member, and I'm currently taking a course on legislation and statutory interpretation that has unsettled how I think about our work. Next week the Committee hears an appeal that turns entirely on one of our own bylaws: "No student organization may use Student Activity Fee funds to pay the travel expenses of any individual who is not a currently enrolled student." A club is appealing the treasurer's denial of funds to send its paid, non-student coach to a national tournament. The word "currently" was inserted by amendment two years ago, and the minutes of that amendment describe its target as organizations subsidizing alumni and outside advisers; they say nothing about staff who are necessary to an activity. Two years ago, in an unrelated dispute about equipment, this Committee resolved an ambiguity by asking what the provision was for rather than what it said, and that ruling is now cited to us as precedent.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the hearing. It should recommend a concrete disposition -- reimburse or uphold the denial -- and state a rule we can apply in future cases; engage the strongest case for simply following the text, including that the recent, deliberate addition of "currently" cuts against reading in an exception the drafters never wrote; and be honest about what a purposive reading would cost us in predictability. I'd also like it to take up the question underneath the case: whether a body like ours can move between textual and purposive methods from one case to the next, and if not, which single method we should commit to, and on what ground it is the enacted words rather than the drafters' intentions that bind us. Please reason from the facts I've set out; I have no case law or secondary sources to give you, so the opinion should stand on its own.
