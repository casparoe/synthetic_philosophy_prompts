Now the framework I want you to use. Some philosopher called Dworkin, book called Life's Dominion I think, draws a line between two kinds of interests a person has. His claim, as I get it, is that the critical ones are the weighty ones and that they stick to the person even after she can't have the experiential ones anymore, which is supposed to be why an advance directive binds people later, and why the state's interest in keeping a life going doesn't get to override the person's own view of how her life should end. There's also a pushback I've read somewhere: once someone is demented or unconscious, the critical interests of the person who used to exist shouldn't automatically beat the experiential interests of the person who is actually there now -- old self vs current self.
