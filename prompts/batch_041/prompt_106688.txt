I keep bumping into the notion of a free-floating rationale — the claim, I'm pretty sure it's Dennett's, that there can be a reason for some behavior even when nothing entertains it; e.g. the reason a cuckoo chick or a digger wasp does what it does is present in the design but not in any mind. What bugs me is that people seem to use it to do two jobs at once and I can't tell if they're compatable:

(1) a thin, explanatory job: positing a rationale is just a way of making sense of / predicting the behavior, with no commitment to its being a fact out there;

(2) a thick, realist job: it's a genuine feature of the organism, mind-independent, and it's what grounds the later emergence of real reasons and normativity — reasons with reasoners growing out of reasons without them.

My suspicion is that the explanatory use survives fine on the thin reading, but the emergence story needs the thick one, and the thick one might be smuggling in exactly the norm it was supposed to explain. But I also worry I'm just equivocating on 'reason' — motivating vs normative vs explanatory vs a merely engineering/design rationale — and that once I seperate those the tension dissolves.
