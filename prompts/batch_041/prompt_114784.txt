Suppose I hold that insects very likely have morally relevant experiences — that there is, say, a one-in-ten chance that a bee's pain matters in the way a mammal's does — and that because insects number in the quintillions, the expected moral value of caring about them dwarfs almost any other cause. A colleague holds the opposite: that insect sentience is so uncertain that we should not let the arithmetic of enormous numbers drive our priorities, and that acting on it would be a kind of fanaticism. We have argued for a year and keep talking past each other. Write the joint statement we should have produced.

I want a document we could both sign. Please include:

1. A numbered set of agreed claims — what we both accept about insects' capacities, about what evidence could in principle bear on their sentience, about the magnitude of the numbers involved, and about the structure of the decision we face. These should be substantive, not platitudes; a claim that either of us would privately reject is worse than useless.

2. A precise statement of each remaining disagreement, written so that the side holding that view would accept the wording as a fair rendering of its own position. Include at least: (a) whether a small credence in sentience should be multiplied by vast numbers at all, or whether there is a principled credence floor below which we should treat an outcome as morally absent — and if such a floor exists, how it is fixed without arbitrariness; (b) whether moral weight is binary (a being either has experiences that matter or does not) or graded, and what follows for the calculation if it is graded; (c) whether the relevant probability is a single number at all, or a distribution over possibilities that should itself be integrated.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us. For each crux, give a concrete case, experiment, or argument that both of us would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it. Where you can, invent a specific study or scenario rather than gesturing at "future research": e.g., a finding that a particular neural structure is present in insects and absent in animals we already agree are insentient, or a case in which an insect-welfare intervention produces a measurable behavioral change, and say what each of us would expect to see and what it would do to our positions.

4. A short section on whether any part of our dispute is merely verbal — for instance, if we mean different things by "sentience," "probability," or "moral weight," say exactly which part and why, and whether the same wording would dissolve it.

Format the agreed claims as a numbered list, the disagreements as a table with a column for each side's own phrasing, and the cruxes as a separate numbered list with a prediction row for each side.
