I'm a novelist and I've got two friends who've been going at each other for about a year now, and I'm fairly sure they're mostly argueing past each other, so I want the document they should've written instead. They're both into philosophy of language. One of them — Theo — thinks a name is basically a pointer. "Marta" picks out Marta, that's the whole story, and the only thing the name contributes is the person it points at. The other one — Ruth — thinks there's a second layer, a "way the object is given to you," and that this layer is part of what the name actually *means*, not just some belief you happen to carry around. (I think these are the Millian and Fregean positions, but don't quote me on the labels.)

What set them off is my current book. Two things in it:

(1) Early on the narrator tells the reader that the man in the doorway is her father. Nobody in the story learns anything — the daughter already knows, the father already knows, the reader is the only mind that goes from not-knowing to knowing. So if there's information in "the man in the doorway was her father," the only mind that gained it was a mind outside the fiction.

(2) Later, the reverse. The reader has known since page three that two characters with different names are the same person. We watch one of them finally work it out. Nobody in the reader's position learns a thing; the character does.

Theo says these are just facts about who learns what when, pure psychology, nothing to do with meaning. Ruth says they show the "way of being given" has to belong to some particular mind, which is either fine (if you let the layer be mind-relative) or a mess (if you wanted it objective and shared between speakers).

I don't want a verdict on who's right. I want the joint statement they could both sign. So:

- the claims they genuinely agree on — about names, about an identity statement being true or false regardless of how you refer, about what still needs explaining, and about whether my two scenes are really the same puzzle as the usual morning-star/evening-star thing or a different one;
- the real disagreement, stated twice, once in Theo's terms and once in Ruth's, each version written so the person it describes would actually sign it;
- the cruxes, the sub-questions where settling it would move at least one of them. I'm pretty sure one is "is the informativeness a property of the sentence or of a mind," and another is "are the descriptions you associate with a name part of its meaning or just part of your beliefs." If there are more, add them;
- for each crux, a case or argument both of them would agree bears on it, plus what each one predicts about it. Use my two scenes if they fit; if they don't, invent something new — I'd much rather you make something up than pad it with examples everyone's seen a million times.

Two more things. If part of their fight turns out to be only about words — one of them using "meaning" for something the other calls "belief" — say which part, and why. And at the end, tell me where you think you've papered over a real disagreement, or where an "agreed" claim is secretly smuggling in one side's view, and what would make you throw the whole thing out and start over. I'd rather know where this document is likely to break than get something that just sounds tidy.

Format: numbered section of agreed claims, then a table for the disagreements and cruxes. Casual is fine, I'm not grading it, and don't bother citing anyone — I just want the argument.
