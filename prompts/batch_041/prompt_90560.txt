Compose a dialogue between two decision theorists of equal competence and equal good faith who disagree about whether coherence arguments establish that a rational agent must maximize expected utility.

The first theorist holds that preferences violating completeness or transitivity are irrational because they leave the agent open to a sure-loss sequence of exchanges. Her central case: an agent strictly prefers A to B and B to C but suspends judgment between A and C, and a trader exploits the gaps, extracting a fee at each step and returning the agent to a starting point she is worse off by her own lights. She concludes that rational preference must be representable by a single probability function and a single utility function, and that an agent with no such representation has no determinate policy to deliberate with at all.

The second theorist concedes that such an agent may lose money but denies that this establishes irrationality. She argues the pump works only by smuggling in a premise the first theorist has not earned — that the agent would rather close the gap than keep her money, or that she must accept each offered trade — and that suspending judgment between genuinely incommensurable or on-a-par options can itself be rational, so the completeness axiom is a convenience of representation rather than a demand of reason. As a positive alternative she proposes that the agent carry a set of admissible probability-and-utility pairs and treat an option as choiceworthy when it is optimal for some admissible pair, then presses the first theorist on whether this is a genuine escape from incoherence or merely a relabeling of it.

Have each character engage the other's strongest formulation rather than restating her own position. The first should offer a real reply to the resolute-refusal and incommensurability moves; the second should offer a real reply to the charge that a policy without a single ranking cannot guide action. Let them converge on the precise point where they part — a premise about what rationality demands, a factual claim about exploitability, or a disagreement over what a preference is — and close on the sharpest question still open between them.
