ok, I need a thought experiment built to order, and the spec matters more than the prose. It's for a seminar on the concept of law where I want students to see legality and morality come apart — but not in the usual direction, and not via the tired example. Domain: the Hart–Fuller fight, legal positivism vs natural law, the old question of whether an unjust law is still law.

Here's the target: a case where Hart and Fuller give opposite verdicts on whether some norm counts as law, but the usual engine is switched off — the norm's content has to be morally admirable, so neither of them can be disagreeing because it's wicked. The disagreement has to come from a formal/procedural feature instead, and I want exactly one such feature doing the work (promulgation, prospectivity, clarity, consistency, generality, or the congruence between what's on the books and what officials actually do — pick one, hold the rest fixed).

Constraints:
- one paragraph of scenario, contemporary and realistic. No Nazi grudge-informer stuff, no dictatorship, no secret police, no "evil regime, evil law." Think a modern regulator, a municipal bylaw, a professional body, a treaty body — but not the textbook examples.
- same norm, same officials, same citizens throughout; the only difference-maker should be the one formal feature you picked.
- dont stipulate the verdicts. They have to fall out of the two theories, and if the honest answer is that the theories don't actually diverge on your case, say so and fix the case.
- extra credit if you can make it come out the awkward way round — Fuller saying yes, that's law, Hart saying no it isn't — but only if it really works; if that direction needs fudging, tell me why and give me the standard direction instead.

Then the analysis: what Hart says and why, what Fuller says and why, which single feature is carrying the disagreement, what happens if you delete that feature (do they converge, or does it turn into a different dispute?), and how a defender of each would attack your case. And the bit I actually care about: tell me whether your case is a disagreement about the concept of law or just about this one legal system's rule of recognition — I suspect alot of these cases are the second thing dressed up as the first.

Epistemic bit: keep what's settled seperate from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't hand me a contested reading as consensus. In particular: whether Fuller really held that a rule failing the inner morality is straight-up not law, or only a defective/degraded law; whether Hart's rule of recognition can itself include a moral test (inclusive vs exclusive positivism); and whether the debate is about the concept of law at all or about how judges should decide hard cases. Flag those where they matter.
