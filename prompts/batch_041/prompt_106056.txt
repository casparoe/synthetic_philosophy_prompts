In a seminar on radical interpretation I asked whether we can really be said to understand a text — I had in mind a mystical or paradoxical text that appears to affirm and deny the same thing — or whether we are only projecting our own logic onto it, and a classmate cut me off with "you have to be charitable," as if that settled the matter. I keep meeting the same move: "charity" invoked to end arguments about whether some speaker, text, or culture means what we take them to mean. I want a careful map of the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making, laid out from the weakest to the strongest, so that I can see which claim was doing the work in that exchange and whether any of them actually licenses dismissing my question.

Separate at least these readings: (i) a working heuristic — assume agreement to get interpretation started, defeasible as evidence comes in; (ii) a methodological principle about which global interpretation is the better hypothesis; (iii) a constitutive constraint on ascribing any beliefs at all; (iv) a constitutive constraint on meaning or interpretability; (v) a claim about the possibility of genuine, deep disagreement; and any further reading you think belongs on the list. For each, say what the charity is being charitable about — truth, rationality, coherence, or something else — since that choice seems to change the whole picture. Then tell me which readings are near-platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, which are probably false, and which one people usually mean when they deploy the phrase to shut a question down. Finally, and this is the point of the exercise: does any reading on your list actually support the move my classmate made, or was he borrowing the prestige of a strong reading to do the work of a weak one?

If the way I have posed this is confused — if the slogan does not really split along these lines, or if the question I was asking was itself muddled — say so plainly rather than producing a tidy menu that papers over the problem. Where you do give verdicts, be explicit about how confident you are in each and where a Davidsonian or a Quinian would push back hardest.
