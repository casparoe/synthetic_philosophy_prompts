So I've somehow ended up on the selection commitee for a small foundation grant -- enough money for two postdocs, two years each -- and the remit is deliberately vague: the timing of conscious experience. Half the panel are vision people and they're already circling the color phi thing. You flash one dot, then a second dot somewhere else a fraction of a second later, and at the right spacing people report a single dot that travels across the gap and switches colour abruptly about halfway along the path. The weird part is that people report the colour change as happening before the second dot was even on the screen, which kicks off the usual fight: is that Orwellian (the experience is fine and something downstream -- memory, the report -- gets edited after the fact) or Stalinesque (the edit is upstream, so the experience itself is a construction and there's no pre-edit version to compare against)? Dennett's frame, roughly.

I suspect that fight is a tar pit and I want the money to go somewhere it'll actually do work. So: what are the open questions about the timing of experience, and which matter most? I want a ranked agenda for two postdocs, not a survey.

Two candidates I want you to take seriously, including the possibility that both are duds:

1. A colleague keeps calling it the three-clock problem -- the world clock, the experience clock, and the report clock -- and claims half the literature slides silently between them, so that "when did you experience it" and "when would you say it happened" get treated as the same question. Is that a real confusion or a terminological fuss?

2. My own hunch: temporal judgments are built for action, not measurement, so asking whether felt timing is accurate might be like asking whether a speedometer is accurate about the road. If that's right, the live question is what the timing is for, not when it really happened.

Also tell me where the postdiction stuff belongs in the ranking -- flash-lag, the cutaneous rabbit, chronostasis (that stopped-clock feeling when your gaze lands somewhere new), intentional binding -- and whether any of it is more then a cabinet of curiosities. And if the color phi effect is shakier than the textbook version suggests, say so, since half the panel's priors rest on it; I've heard there are deflationary accounts that get the effect out of ordinary visual processes like lateral inhibition without any exotic machinery.

For each item on your list: why it's upstream of other work, whether it's doable in two years by a postdoc who's a decent experimentalist but not a philosopher, what would move it up or down, and what you'd cut. No citations needed, I don't want a reading list.
