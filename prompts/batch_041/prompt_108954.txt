My view, which I haven't come across anywhere, though that might just mean I haven't read enough: what the hitchhiker promises isn't the money. He's asking the driver to bet on his character — on whether he's the sort of person who pays. So the promise is a transfer of the risk of being wrong about yourself. And what he owes afterwards isn't the cash, it's making that bet come true. Which means (a) he should definitely pay, and (b) nothing irrational is going on at all, so rational irrationality is a misnomer here — he's just settling a debt he took on the moment he asked to be trusted.
