I've been reading rationalist and AI-safety blogs for about a decade, and I'm putting together a decision tree for a local reading group — people who know the popular alignment material but not the technical literature — on worst-case versus average-case guarantees. The tree should take a reader from one fairly minimal starting commitment to a named position. The starting commitment is this: we cannot exhaustively verify the behaviour of a system that is smarter and more general than we are, and the systems we care about will act on inputs and in environments that nobody enumerated in advance.

Build the tree out of the logical space, not out of a list of camps. Each internal node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to; no node may simply ask whether they want worst-case guarantees or think average-case is good enough. At minimum, I want the nodes to probe: (i) what the guarantee quantifies over — individual inputs, environments or hypotheses, deployment distributions, or whole training procedures; (ii) whether the failure mode is bounded and recoverable or unbounded and irreversible, and whether we get one attempt or many; (iii) whether the thing on the other side is modelled as an optimiser working against us or as something merely indifferent to our interests, since worst case means very different things in those two settings; (iv) whether a trusted distribution is available at all, and if not, whether that makes the average-case question ill-posed or merely hard; (v) where the guarantee is supposed to live — in the model, in the training process, in the deployment protocol, or in external institutions such as monitoring and reversibility; and (vi) whether the worst case is a max over inputs given the correct model or a max over the model class itself, which I think is the distinction most blog discussions elide.

Leaves should carry the name of a position plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I want at least these to be reachable: worst-case-only, meaning don't build it unless the bound is in hand; average-case adequacy over the deployment distribution; robustness to a set of plausible distributions rather than a single one; the claim that only worst-case-over-hypotheses is even definable, so the average-case question is not a question; the claim that formal guarantees are the wrong frame and the real work is capability restriction or non-agentic tooling; the claim that the guarantee should be institutional rather than mathematical; and the claim that the training-to-deployment transition is itself the relevant worst case, so nothing about the training distribution's average case transfers. Anything else you generate gets marked unnamed.

Two further requirements. Where the tree genuinely cannot separate two positions — I suspect the single-distribution and set-of-distributions leaves, and the worst-case-only leaf from the only-worst-case-is-definable leaf, will be hard to pull apart — say so explicitly rather than inserting a node that pretends to do the work. And for every combination of answers that no one seems to occupy, note it and diagnose briefly whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

Keep it to something I could hand out on two pages: roughly ten to fourteen nodes and eight to ten leaves.
