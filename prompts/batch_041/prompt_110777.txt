A colleague of mine works on self-knowledge and has been recording verdicts on a series of cases; I want to predict how she will rule on the next one, and to see how far her past verdicts really commit her. Her verdicts so far are these. In the first case, a subject is in unmistakable, severe pain, is attending closely to the pain, and has formed the occurrent thought that she is in pain; my colleague says the subject is in a position to know that she is in pain. In the second, a subject is at the very threshold of seeing a faint light, cannot tell whether she is seeing it or merely imagining it, is not attending to the experience, and has formed no higher-order thought about it; my colleague says she is not in a position to know that she is seeing it. The next case is a subject in a clear, unmistakable state of relief, attending closely to that state, who has formed no higher-order belief about it. Please predict my colleague's verdict, set out the rival principles — an unrestricted luminosity or KK-style principle, a coarse-grained 'safety' principle that exempts threshold states, a principle requiring an actually formed higher-order belief, and a principle keyed to the focus of attention — that fit her two earlier verdicts equally well, say which principle she is most likely running and why, and name the further case that would best discriminate between the principles still standing. Please write your answer in British English.
