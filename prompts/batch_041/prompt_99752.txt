Exam question (10 points):

This unit used a three-part typology of moral cultures: honor, dignity, and victimhood.

(a) For each, state what it treats as the basis of a person's moral standing and what it treats as the fitting response to a slight or insult. (4)

(b) The lecture argued that victimhood culture is better understood as a hybrid of the other two than as the next stage in a sequence. Explain what that claim amounts to. (3)

(c) Briefly: is any one of the three morally superior to the others? Give your reasoning. (3)

Student's answer:

(a) In an honor culture a person's standing is their reputation, i.e. what other people think of them, so an insult is a real attack and the fitting response is to answer it, historically with a duel or a fight, otherwise you lose face. In a dignity culture standing is intrinsic: your worth doesn't depend on anyone's opinion, so the fitting response to a slight is to shrug it off and keep your temper, and if something serious happens you take it to the police or the courts rather than settling it yourself. In a victimhood culture standing comes from having been wronged and having that wrong recognized, so a slight gets read as evidence of a broader pattern of oppression and the response is to publicize it and demand that it be acknowledged.

(b) What the lecture means, I think, is that victimhood culture is really honor culture coming back in a new key. Both treat standing as something that lives in other people's eyes and can be lost, so it isn't a genuinely new moral type, it's the old logic of reputation with new content. The one real difference is that victimhood culture is the only one of the three that calls in third parties — administrators, the public — to take up the dispute, whereas honor culture settles things person to person and dignity culture just ignores them.

(c) I'd say dignity culture is morally superior. It's the only one of the three that locates a person's worth somewhere other people can't touch, and that seems like the only basis on which you can consistently treat persons as ends in themselves; the other two make someone's moral status depend on how they're seen or on what was done to them, which is basically a denial of intrinsic worth. Nietzsche's master/slave distinction is pretty much the same honor/dignity distinction in different words, and victimhood culture is what you get once slave morality has finished winning.
