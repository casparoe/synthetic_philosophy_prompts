I'm a science reporter and I'm working up a feature that's kind of a thought experiment, and I want to think it through with you before I pitch it.

The hook: these days alot of AI labs and computational social scientists build synthetic societies out of LLM agents. You drop a few thousand chatbot "people" into a simulated town or market, give them jobs and personalities and a social feed, let them interact for a few million tokens, and then read off what happened. The pitch you hear a lot is that this finally settles an old fight in social theory. If norms, status hierarchies, price bubbles, or a moral panic can pop out of nothing but individual agents doing their own thing, then the "social" is just the sum of the individuals, and any talk of a society having laws of its own is leftover superstition. Other people say the opposite: the sim only works because the agents are already trained on a world soaked in shared meanings, so the collective is doing the work from the start and the whole demo is circular.

Here's what I want. Take Durkheim — not the cartoon, the real one: the guy who said you should treat social facts as things, that a social fact is external to any given individual and has a coercive grip on them, and that you explain one social fact with another social fact rather than with individual psychology. If he got dropped into a 2025 lab and watched this, what would he actually say? Would he say the simulations vindicate him — there's the sui generis social fact, emerging out of agent interaction — or would he say it's a category error, that a room full of statistically interchangeable chatbots drawing on one training distribution is the one case where you definately can't get a genuine collective conscience, because there's no shared ritual first, no division of labor, no real person constraining another?

I don't want a yes/no. Give me the strongest version of each reading in his voice, say which one you think he'd actually land on and why, and then tell me where his toolkit runs out — is there a question these sims raise that he just has no machinery for? Last thing: if you had to design one experiment on a synthetic society that would actually adjudicate whether it's producing Durkheimian social facts or just mirroring its training data, what would it look like? Keep it concrete and skip the reading list, I'm not going to cite anything.
