Write an essay on whether truthlikeness — the idea that one false theory can be closer to the truth than another — is a notion we can legitimately use, or whether it should be abandoned. Reach a verdict and defend it rather than surveying the terrain.

The material you need is this. The original proposal, due to Popper, treats a theory's verisimilitude as a function of its true and false consequences: roughly, theory A is closer to the truth than theory B when A's true consequences include all of B's, while A's false consequences are all among B's. Tichý, Harris and Miller showed independently in 1974 that on this comparative definition no false theory is closer to the truth than any other, so the definition cannot do the work Popper wanted it to do. Repairs have gone in two directions: keep the consequence framework but count only consequences that are, in some technical sense, relevant or essential; or take the "likeness" literally and score a theory by the similarity to the actual world of the possible worlds it permits, averaged over them. Against the second family there is a general objection that the ranking is an artefact of vocabulary: construct a language with the same logical structure but a different similarity ordering among its predicates, and the ranking of two theories can be reversed while nothing about the world changes.

Do not gesture at the failure of the original definition; test it on a small case. Suppose the world is described by three independent facts A, B and C, that the truth is that A and B hold and C does not, and compare the false theory that A, B and C all hold with the false theory that none of them do. Work out the true and false consequences of each and say what the comparison shows — whether it destroys the consequence approach outright or only one formulation of it. Then say whether the similarity approach escapes the triviality result and whether it escapes the vocabulary objection, and whether any account you can defend is entitled to the vocabulary it uses. Your verdict should be one of these: truthlikeness is defensible as a measure; truthlikeness survives only as a comparative, informal notion with no metric; or the notion should be dropped. Say what your verdict implies for the claim that science makes progress toward the truth, and give the strongest objection to your own conclusion and answer it.

Write the response as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables.
