I'm a novelist, and I've gotten myself stuck on something I can't tell if it's a real problem or just me being cute. I'd like you to try to take it apart.

Here's what happens in the book. My character Mara is driving somewhere, and she has to get off at the second exit. The first exit is nothing, a dead end, zero. The second exit is everything she wants, call it 4. If she drives past both exits she ends up somewhere mildly fine, call it 1. Now the catch, and it's the whole point of the character: Mara is absent-minded in one very specific way. Every time she reaches an exit, she has no idea if it's the first or the second. The exits are indistinguishable to her, she doesn't remember any earlier decision, and she's got no notes, no passenger, no phone, no little gadget counting exits for her — just her own head, and her head cant do this job. So at the first exit and at the second exit she is in exactly the same state, thinking exactly the same thoughts.

Here's the view I've landed on and want you to attack. I think the only honest advice to give her is: dont take either exit. Keep driving, take the 1. Because the good outcome — the second exit — is exactly the thing she has no way to aim at. She can't be the sort of person who continues at the first and exits at the second, because whoever continues at the first and whoever continues at the second are the same person in the same frame of mind. So any advice along the lines of continue past the first, exit at the second is just cheating — it hands her a plan she has no way to carry out. The one thing she can actually count on is that if she never exits, she gets the 1. So: never exit. That's how I want the chapter to end.

So what would count against this? Give me the strongest objections, ranked from the ones that would kill the view outright down to the ones that are merely awkward, and tell me for each whether someone who thinks the way I do would have to concede it or could shrug it off. I'm especially interested in anything showing I've rigged the case so my answer wins by default — I have a suspicion I've smuggled in a rule like always pick the thing you can be sure of, and I dont know if anyone has to accept a rule like that. Also: does my view predict anything a rival wouldn't, or commit me to something a rival isn't stuck with? And finally, if there's some smarter move for Mara that I'm just not seeing — some way for her to do better than the 1 without ever being able to tell the exits apart — I really need to know, because it would change the whole ending. Alot rides on it.
