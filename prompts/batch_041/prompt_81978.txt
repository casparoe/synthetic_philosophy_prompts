Write a pantoum on the ontology of generations. The occasion is a dispute about whether labels such as Boomer, Millennial, and Gen Z pick out real kinds or are merely administrative cuts in a continuous distribution of birth years. I want the poem to be organized around a distinction from demography that has become important in the philosophy of social science: the age–period–cohort identification problem. From any single cross-section, a difference between age groups may be an effect of aging, an effect of the historical period, or an effect of the cohort's distinctive composition, and the three cannot be separately identified from that snapshot alone. Make the pantoum's form carry that problem. In a pantoum, lines recur exactly from stanza to stanza, but their meaning shifts with their new neighbors; use that recurrence to show how a line that sounds like a generational trait in one decade can look like a period effect in the next, and how the final return of the opening lines can make the identification problem visible rather than solved. Let the diction be plain enough for a reader who knows nothing of the technical literature, but keep the form exact: four-line stanzas; the second and fourth lines of each stanza become the first and third of the next; the closing stanza folds back into the opening; repeated lines are exact repetitions, not near-repetitions, so that the change is entirely contextual. Aim for at least five quatrains. Somewhere in the poem, give the strongest case for the realist side — that cohorts are real because shared formative events and institutions leave durable marks — and somewhere give the strongest case for the anti-realist side — that the word generation names a label imposed on a continuum, often for marketing or journalism, and that the apparent kind is an artifact of arbitrary cutoffs. Do not resolve the dispute. Let the refrain carry both possibilities at once, so that a demographer or philosopher of the social sciences would recognize the problem and a reader who has never heard of it would still feel its pull.
