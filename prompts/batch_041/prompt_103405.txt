I'm a novelist, not a political theorist, so forgive me, but I've been going around in circles on this and I need someone to tell me what I actually believe. Here are my gut reactions to a pile of cases about how we pick the people who govern us. (1) A friend pitched me his idea: fill a second chamber by lottery. Pull, say, three hundred names off the voter roll, they serve two years, they can never be pulled again, and they get real power over legislation. My honest reaction was that I'd probly take that over what we've got now. (2) Then he said the same lottery could decide whether we go to war, and I felt physically ill. Same people, same mechanism, same binding authority, and yet one of those I'd sign tomorrow and the other I'd chain myself to a fence over. (3) Juries. Random citizens, zero expertise, power over whether a stranger loses the rest of his life. Completely fine with it, have never once lost sleep over it. (4) A one-term limit for legislators: in, serve six years, out forever, never on a ballot again. I'm for it. (5) But a surgeon who's done the same operation for thirty years, or a bridge engineer, or honestly a novelist, I obviously want the veteran and not a rotation of fresh faces. I don't think politics is somehow exempt from that, so I can't tell you why I want the term limit for the senator and not for the surgeon. (6) Here's the one I keep flipping on. Compulsory service, like jury duty except for the legislature: everyone gets drafted once, three years, you can't recieve a pass and you can't run again afterward. Half of me thinks it fixes everything, no career, no donors, no ladder to climb. Half of me thinks it's jury duty for people who'd rather be anywhere else, deciding things they'll never have to answer for. (7) Small one to finish: I think a president should be barred forever from a second non-consecutive term, but I think a mayor who's doing a great job should be able to serve four or five terms if the voters keep wanting her, and I have no idea how to square those two.

So is there a principle behind this pile, or a couple of candidate principles I could choose between? What do they say about the cases I haven't thought about, like a lottery-selected body that can only propose and never decide, or a term limit on judges? And be honest about which of my verdicts the principle has to throw overboard, because I suspect at least one of them is just sentiment wearing a costume.
