I am preparing an episode of a small, technically inclined philosophy podcast, and my guest is a philosopher who defends a view she calls evidential cooperation in large worlds. The view holds that agents who are causally isolated from one another, whether in distant regions of a very large universe or in different branches of a multiverse, can nevertheless cooperate by adopting policies that are logically or indexically correlated, because choosing a cooperative policy is evidence that one's counterparts are choosing cooperative policies, even though no causal signal passes between them. She treats this as a result in decision theory rather than as a moral postulate, and she often insists that the real question is not whether acausal influence is possible but which reference class of counterparts one's policy is correlated with. I want questions that force her to specify the machinery rather than restate the conclusion. Please draft exactly five interview questions. Each item should be one or two sentences: a main question followed by a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I want at least one question each on the reference-class problem and how the view avoids vacuity; on whether evidential cooperation collapses into evidential decision theory or instead requires something like functional or updateless decision theory; on the parochialism objection and whether the view can avoid licensing cooperation only with agents sufficiently like us; on how it handles logical uncertainty and the probability that counterparts exist; and on whether the view has any normative upshot beyond instrumental rationality, for example whether it could ground obligations to create or accommodate possible cooperators. Return only a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, and do not ask for citations or a reading list.
