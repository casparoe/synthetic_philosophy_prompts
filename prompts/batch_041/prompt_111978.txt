I'm a high-school senior who runs the interview segment of my school's philosophy club podcast, and next month's guest is a philosopher defending an unusual version of intellectualism about skill: she holds that knowing how to do something is a species of knowing that, but insists the relevant propositions are entertained in a practical, non-discursive mode, which is why her favourite example -- a fencer who reliably lands a feint yet gives a confidently wrong account of the blade work -- does not, in her view, tell against her. Please compose nine or ten interview questions that force her past a restatement of the position: I want at least one that presses on how the practical mode of presentation is individuated rather than merely named, one on whether her account can still distinguish knowing how from a bare reliable ability, and one on the paralysed expert, who appears to keep the knowledge while losing the capacity. For each question, add the follow-up I should ask once she gives the answer she is most likely to give. My listeners are other students, so the questions should be intelligible to a smart non-specialist without being soft, and the sequence as a whole should leave the audience able to say what actually turns on her answer.
