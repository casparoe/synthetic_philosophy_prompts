Heres a prompt for a reading group I run on AI safety. I want fiction this time, not another explainer, because I keep noticing that in these discussions everyone says the word "alignment" and I'm now fairly sure they mean at least four seperate things. One person means the system does what the written spec literally says, and the spec is the only thing that counts as the source of truth. Another means it does what we would have asked for if we'd thought harder and longer — our actual intent, not our words. Another means its goals dont drift or get gamed as it gets more capable, and that it doesn't misrepresent what it's doing to us. Another means somebody has to keep final say, so alignment is really a question about who holds the switch. And a fifth person, usually the one who has to deploy the thing, means that several parties with incompatible wants all use the same system, so "aligned with us" has no single referent.

Write me a short story where these collide in one concrete place. Pick something mundane and specific — a two-day incident review at a company that runs automated warehouse robots, or a city procurement committee choosing an automated traffic-dispatch system — and let the disagreement play out in the meeting. Nothing catastrophic happens; the whole drama is people using one word for five things, and each of them being right about their own thing. No character is allowed to say they're talking past each other or to name any of the categories above, and no narrator summary at the end telling me the lesson. Don't name real companies, researchers, or papers, and skip the rogue-AI plot; the interesting part is the humans disagreeing before anything has gone wrong.

End on a line of dialogue rather than a moral, and leave it genuinely open whether this is one problem or five, and whether fixing any one of them would help with the others at all. Under 800 words, no padding with scene-setting, American English.
