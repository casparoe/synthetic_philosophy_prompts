A friend of mine who's deep into historical materialism keeps leaning on the building picture: society is like a building, the economy is the foundation, and everything else — law, politics, religion, art, the whole culture — is the superstructure sitting on top of it. Change the foundation and the whole thing comes down; repaint the upper floors and the foundation never notices. He uses this to argue that the culture war is a waste of time, that you can't get real change by reforming laws or winning hearts and minds, because those are just the upper floors and the economy underneath decides everything in the end. How far does the picture actually hold? Give me the correspondence table — what plays the foundation, what's load-bearing, what's the wiring and plumbing, is there anything that's neither — then where the metaphor breaks (does a foundation ever push back on itself? who ever builds a building bottom-up, foundation first and only then decides what the rooms are for?), what it quietly smuggles in about causation and direction, and whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. And if there's a better analogy for the same job, tell me what it is.
