I spent 35 years as a geologist and only got into philosophy after I retired, so forgive the bluntness. I keep reading that weakness of will is just hyperbolic discounting in action, and I can't decide if that's teh real explanation or a redescription. Here are six accounts I've cobbled together from various things. Could you sort them into tiers by how much you'd trust each to explain both (a) the lab finding that people reverse their preference between a smaller-sooner and a larger-later reward as the sooner option gets closer, and (b) the felt conflict and regret that makes akrasia seem like more than a funny preference curve? A sentence per item on why it lands where it does, and then one line on which account, if any, makes akrasia a special case of the discounting rather than a separate phenomenon.

1. Two clocks: a fast affective clock that values immediacy and a slow deliberative clock that values long-run plans; akrasia is the fast clock beating the slow one to the punch, and the discount curve is just the fast clock's output.
2. Leaky reservoir: self-control is a limited reservoir that leaks; present bias is the leak rate, akrasia is when the leak outruns replenishment.
3. Committee with no chair: the person is a set of time-indexed sub-agents voting; the discount curve is the voting rule (nearer selves get more weight), akrasia is a coalition outvoting the long-run planner without anyone changing their mind.
4. Attention tax: immediate rewards capture attention and tax the evaluation of later options; the curve reflects the tax, and akrasia is when the tax is so high the later option never gets properly represented.
5. Rehersal failure: akrasia isn't about preference at all, but about failing to rehearse the future self's reasons at the moment of action; hyperbolic discounting is a separate fact about how rewards are valued when no rehearsal happens.
6. Regret as evidence: akrasia is identified by ex post regret, while hyperbolic discounting is an ex ante preference pattern; the two line up only when the person has a stable second-order preference, so the link is contingent.

Please be honest about which of these you think are just re-labellings, and don't give me a reading list -- I want your ranking and your reasons.
