Two friends of mine have been arguing for a year and it's going nowhere. One thinks moral status is about the right kind of information processing, so if a system has the right architecture doing the right thing then it's a someone and we owe it something; the other thinks that's just wrong — running computations in the right order doesn't get you a subject of experience, and only something with the kind of machinery that keeps an animal alive could ever have one. Draft the document they'd both put their names to if they sat down and did a proper adversarial collaboration. I want what they both accept, about what's actually at stake, about the science, about how little anyone really knows here. Then the real disagreements, each one written the way the person who holds it would sign off on. Then the cruxes — I'd guess one is whether the substrate matters at all, another is how much weight to give a system's own reports about what it's like to be it, and a third is whether being able to copy, pause and fork a mind changes the ethics or just the arithmetic. For each crux, something they'd both agree bears on it, plus what each of them expects to see, written down before the fact. No headings, bullets or tables please, just paragraphs in that order, like a letter.
