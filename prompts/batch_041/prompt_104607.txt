There is a pattern I have been trying to find a name for, and I would like you to tell me whether an established one exists and, if not, to supply a term with a careful definition.

Consider a person choosing among options without a fixed standard of adequacy. She begins with a rough sense of what would be acceptable — say, an apartment that is affordable and within a reasonable commute. Each option she examines and rejects contributes something: a defect she had not previously thought to care about, which then becomes a requirement for everything she sees afterward. The second apartment has thin walls, so now quiet matters. The fourth has a landlord who seems evasive, so now the landlord matters. The fifth has an awkward layout, so now the layout matters. By the tenth, her requirements have grown into a list she could not have written at the outset, and she has ruled out every candidate — yet she does not experience this as having raised her standards. She experiences it as still searching, still hoping to find the one that is simply good enough.

Two things strike me about this. First, the process is neither maximizing nor satisficing as those are usually drawn. She never surveys the full set, so it is not maximizing; but there is no stable threshold that a candidate could meet, because the threshold is rewritten by the very candidates it rejects, so it is not satisficing either. Second, the moving threshold is not obviously a mistake. Some of the new requirements are genuine discoveries about what she values; others look more like rationalizations of an unwillingness to commit. From the inside, and often from the outside, the two are difficult to tell apart, and that ambiguity seems to be part of the phenomenon rather than incidental to it.

So: does this have an established name? If it is closest to something like aspiration-level adaptation, anchoring, or adaptive preference, I want to know exactly where the resemblance breaks — in particular, what the standard treatments assume about whether the aspiration level is fixed at the start, and whether they can accommodate a criterion set that grows endogenously through the search itself. If nothing established fits, coin a term and define it: what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie. In particular I want boundary cases — a case at the edge where you would say the description no longer applies, and a case where the same outward behavior (never settling, requirements accumulating) should be classified as something else entirely, such as ordinary learning or ordinary perfectionism.

Finally, tell me what would change your verdict. Which consideration would lead you to conclude that this is not a distinct phenomenon at all but merely satisficing or maximizing under a different description, and where in your own reasoning you would expect it to be most likely to fail?
