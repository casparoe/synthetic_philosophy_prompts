I spent 35 years as a field entomologist before I retired, and now that I've got the time I've been reading alot of philosophy, and theres one thing about my old trade I can't stop chewing on.

Here's the setup. In zoology we've got this rule that goes back basically forever: when you name a new species, you have to designate one single physical specimen as the type -- one actual pinned beetle in a museum drawer -- and that one dead individual is what the name is permanently tied to. Every later use of the name is supposed to trace back to that specimen. Which is strange when you sit with it. We use a species name as a general thing, true of millions of beetles spread across continents, but the official anchor of the name is one particular beetle, an individual, in a box.

And it gets worse. Sometimes that type specimen turns out to be a runt, or a freak, or a juvenile, or -- now that we sequence DNA -- an individual that doesn't even group with the population everyone had been slapping the name on for a century. The name doesn't budge. We just file a petition with the nomenclature commission and maybe get a replacement type designated. Meanwhile the species concept itself keeps getting redrawn -- lumpers vs splitters, biological species vs phylogenetic species, on and on.

So here's what I want to know. When we run this naming practice, what are we actually presupposing about universals -- about whether a species is a real general thing that the individual beetles somehow share, or just a convenient label we hang on a specimen, or something else all together? A working taxonomist would probably shrug and say "neither, it's just bookkeeping," but I don't buy that the practice is that innocent. Reconstruct the view of universals (or of kinds) that the type-specimen practice commits us to, if it commits us to any. Which rival views does it quietly rule out? Is the species being treated as a universal, as an individual (a particular lineage running through time), or as a bare name? And tell me which parts are strictly presupposed by the practice and which are just things we happen to believe alongside it -- because I suspect the practice is underdetermined and that several incompatible metaphysics would let us keep doing exactly what we already do. Last thing: if we swapped in a thoroughgoing nominalism, or a full-blown Platonic realism about species, what in the practice would actually have to change, if anything?
