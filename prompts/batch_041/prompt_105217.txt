I am a member of a small society whose governing charter consists of six articles, and I have been keeping a ledger of my verdicts on whether various rule-changes are valid. I would like to know what principle, if any, captures my verdicts, and whether the principle I have tentatively formulated survives them.

The charter:

Article I. Any article of this charter may be amended by a two-thirds vote of the members present at a regular meeting, provided that notice of the proposed amendment has been given at the previous regular meeting.

Article II. This article may not be amended or repealed.

Article III. The Chair's interpretation of this charter is final.

Article IV. This article may be amended only by a unanimous vote of all members.

Article V. No rule of this society may refer to itself.

Article VI. Amendments take effect immediately upon passage.

My verdicts:

1. At a regular meeting with two-thirds of the members present, and with notice given at the previous meeting, the members vote to amend Article I to require a three-quarters vote. I think this amendment is valid.

2. At a regular meeting with two-thirds present and proper notice, the members vote to repeal Article II. I think this amendment is invalid.

3. The members vote unanimously, with all members present, to amend Article IV to allow amendment by a two-thirds vote. I think this amendment is valid.

4. The members pass a rule that reads "This rule repeals Article V," with two-thirds present and proper notice. I think this repeal is valid.

5. At a meeting, the members vote to suspend the notice requirement in Article I and then, in the same meeting, vote to amend Article I. I think both votes are invalid.

6. The Chair, invoking Article III, rules that Article I's notice requirement is merely advisory and may be dispensed with. I am inclined to think the ruling is valid, but I keep going back and forth, because it seems to make verdict 5 unstable.

7. The members vote to amend Article VI to say that amendments take effect one meeting after passage. I think this amendment is valid, and that it takes effect immediately, so the delay applies only to future amendments.

8. Article I is amended, by the procedure it prescribes, to require a unanimous vote for future amendments. Later, an amendment is passed by a two-thirds vote. I think the later amendment is invalid.

9. The members vote unanimously, with all members present, to amend Article II to remove its self-entrenchment clause. I think this amendment is valid, even though a two-thirds vote to repeal Article II would be invalid.

10. A rule is proposed that says "No rule may be amended by a vote of less than three-quarters of the members present." The members pass it by a two-thirds vote. I think it is invalid.

My tentative principle is this: a rule-change is valid if and only if it is authorized by the rules in force at the time of the change, where authorization is determined by the amendment procedure then in force, and a rule that explicitly prohibits its own amendment cannot be amended by the procedure it prohibits. I am not sure this principle captures all my verdicts. In particular, verdicts 4 and 6 seem to strain it, verdicts 2 and 9 seem to require a distinction between a two-thirds vote and a unanimous vote that the principle does not yet make, and verdict 10 seems to be a counterexample to the principle's first clause.

Please tell me what principle or small set of principles best captures these verdicts. State the principle precisely, identify the case that motivates each clause, say what it predicts for cases I have not listed (for instance, a rule that says "this rule may be amended only by a unanimous vote" is amended by a unanimous vote of those present but not all members; or a rule that says "no amendment may reduce the quorum" is amended to reduce the quorum; or a rule that says "the rules are unamendable" is amended by a unanimous vote of all members), and tell me honestly which of my verdicts the principle cannot accommodate.
