I sit on the model governance committee at a mid-sized hospital, and we are about to deploy a model that scores patients for a scarce transitional-care program. A near-miss last month—a patient who should have been flagged and was not—has made the fairness question personally urgent for me. The vendor says the model is calibrated; the equity office wants equal error rates; the clinicians want something they can explain at the bedside; and I have been told there are impossibility results showing that some of these demands cannot hold together. I do not need the theorems restated or a literature review. I need the specification itself: what any adequate fairness criterion for this deployment would have to deliver before I can decide which demands to make and which to drop.

Write the desiderata any adequate fairness criterion for this deployment must meet. I mean the full list: the cases it must get right (a patient whose score falls just below the threshold; a group whose measured need is lower because of prior access barriers; an intersectional subgroup too small to estimate reliably; a patient who would benefit but cannot attend the program for non-medical reasons), the distinctions it must not collapse (group-level parity versus an individual’s claim to fair treatment; calibration versus error-rate parity; ex ante equality of opportunity versus ex post equality of outcomes; a criterion’s mathematical properties versus its institutional defensibility), and the results it must be consistent with (the impossibility results, the fact that our labels are proxies for need, the feedback loop in which today’s scores change tomorrow’s training data, and the fact that we do not record race and must rely on imperfect proxies). For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it. Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp. Most important: flag any pair or subset that cannot be jointly satisfied, and say what that impossibility forces us to give up. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the usual candidates—demographic parity, equalized odds, predictive parity, calibration, individual fairness—and why. A table is fine if it helps, but the reasoning behind each requirement matters more than the formatting.
