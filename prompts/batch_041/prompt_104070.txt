I am a high school student writing a history essay on how legendary narratives become treated as history. I have been thinking about cases such as the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus, where a story acquires a date, a place, and a cast of characters that later historians either debunk or try to trace back to a real kernel. Please describe the most important implications of this process for how we should treat any past event that is attested only in legendary or mythic narratives. In doing so, please bring in euhemerism—the reading of myths as distorted memories of real people and events—and the reverse phenomenon by which a folkloric or fictional figure is given a historical biography. Please separate what is settled among historians from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
