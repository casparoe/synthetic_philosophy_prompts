I've just started volunteering as a community mediator (housing stuff, mostly), and the training keeps telling us to respect people's preferences but challenge their biases. I can't for the life of me tell which is which, and it's starting to feel like the distinction is doing no work. My mate Dan, who's doing a PhD in moral psychology, gave me this argument and now I can't unsee it:

(1) Every moral judgement you make is caused by some psychological disposition — disgust sensitivity, empathy, in-group loyalty, a need for order, whatever.
(2) A disposition is either a preference or a bias.
(3) A preference is a disposition you're entitled to have; it doesn't claim to track independent moral facts. A bias is a disposition that distorts your tracking of independent moral facts.
(4) For any moral judgement, there are two causal stories that fit all the evidence: one where the disposition is a preference (you've chosen it, or it's constitutive of your values) and one where it's a bias (it was installed by upbringing, trauma, disgust, etc.).
(5) If two causal stories fit all the evidence, you can't non-arbitrarily call one the bias and the other the preference.
(6) So the preference/bias distinction isn't a factual distinction at all; it's just a way of saying whether you approve of the disposition.
(7) If it's just approval, then calling someone biased is circular: it means nothing more than that you don't like their preference.
(8) So in mediation we should drop the language of bias altogether.

I'd rather keep (1) and (3) than (8), because I do think some things are genuinely biases and not just preferences I happen not to share. But I can't see where Dan's argument goes wrong. Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what do I have to give up if I reject it? Could you write the answer in British English, please? And don't just tell me it's the genetic fallacy — I know that's the name for something like this, but I need to know which exact move is the fallacy and whether that leaves the argument's conclusion standing anyway.
