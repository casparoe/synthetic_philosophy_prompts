A friend who runs a delivery depot has been argueing himself into a knot over that line about a measure ceasing to be a good measure once it becomes a target. He wrote it out and wants me to find the loose thread:

(1) Whenever you reward people for pushing a number up, some of them will push it up in ways that don't move the thing it was supposed to track.
(2) So any number you make a target stops telling you about that thing.
(3) Any outfit that wants to improve has to use some number to tell whether it's improving.
(4) So any outfit that tries to improve ends up flying blind.
(5) But lots of outfits obviously do improve. So something's off.

I said the slide is between (1) and (3): (1) is about paying people on a number, (3) is only about watching one, and a number nobody's paid on stays honest. He says that's cheap — everyone in the depot knows which number the bosses watch, so watching it rots it just as surely as paying on it does; he calls the two the same disease whether the number is a whip or a window. And he says the disease comes in strains anyway: sometimes the number just turns noisy at the extremes you're now shoving toward, sometimes shoving it actually changes the thing underneath, sometimes people just lie. Now I can't tell if whip/window is a real line or a comfortable one. Which premise should he drop, and what does he have to give up to drop it?
