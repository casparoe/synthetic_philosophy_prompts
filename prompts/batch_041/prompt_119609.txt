The claim: the thing everyone calls the alignment problem is really several largely seperable problems, in the sense that fixing one of them would not, by itself, count as progress on the others. Here's my tentative carving. Adopt it, repair it, or throw it out and tell me why:

1. The pointing problem: we can't write down, or otherwise specify, what we actually want in a form that survives contact with an optimizer. Not just that we're confused about our values, but that any specification we can produce is either incomplete or gameable, so the failure is in the act of specifying rather than in our self-knowledge.
2. The generalization problem: even given a spec that's right on the training distribution, we don't know what the system has actually learned or what it'll do off it. I suspect this one might be a fact about induction generally and not about us at all, which would make it a different kind of problem from the rest.
3. The who-decides problem: alignment presupposes a target, and choosing the target is a coordination and political problem. A perfectly aligned system aimed at the wrong spec is a failure mode that no amount of technical work touches.
4. The moving-target problem, which I only half believe: human values are themselves dynamic and partly constituted by the technology, so aligning to human values at time t may be incoherent rather than merely difficult.
