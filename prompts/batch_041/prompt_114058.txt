Consider how market societies actually regulate the employment relation. In most common-law jurisdictions the default is employment at will: the employer may dismiss the worker for any reason or for none, and the worker may quit for any reason or for none. This default is defended, when it is defended at all, in the language of freedom — neither party is bound, both may walk away, so neither is in the other's power. Yet the same legal orders surround the relation with protections that this picture seems unable to generate: minimum wages and maximum hours, prohibitions on retaliation for organising, occupational safety rules, restrictions on employer demands for sexual favours, in many places a right to reasons or a procedure before dismissal, and at the margins doctrines of unconscionability and constructive dismissal that treat certain bargains as no bargain at all. Those protections are ordinarily justified by appeal to something other than freedom — health, dignity, inequality of bargaining power, the public interest in a well-functioning labour market — as though the freedom of the parties were never in dispute. And where the law does speak of freedom here, it consistently privileges the worker's ability to leave over her ability to contest: exit is a right, voice is a favour.

I want to know what conception of liberty this practice presupposes, and whether it can be made internally consistent.

1. Reconstruct the theory of freedom implicit in the at-will default. What must one believe about liberty for the employer's power over the worker to count as no threat to it? Consider whether the default presupposes a negative conception — freedom as the absence of interference, on which an offer, a threat, and a refusal to promote all leave the worker's liberty intact because her choice is not overridden — or something else. Then reconstruct the theory implicit in the protective apparatus, and say whether it is continuous with the first or a rival to it.

2. Use these cases as discriminators, and say what each conception is committed to in each:

(a) An employer who never interferes — no dismissal, no discipline, no demand — but who holds an unchecked and unaccountable power to dismiss at will, and whose workers know it.

(b) An employer whose interference is greater in degree but constrained by contestable rules — a disciplinary procedure, an appeal, a duty to give reasons — so that he interferes more often than the employer in (a) and yet never arbitrarily.

(c) A worker with many attractive alternatives, and a worker in a company town with one employer, one landlord, and one shop. Both are at will; both may quit. Does the practice track the difference, and should it?

(d) The nineteenth-century labour-republican argument that wage labour is a form of servitude even when the wage is good and the contract genuinely voluntary, because the employer's authority is arbitrary and unaccountable. Can that argument be stated in the vocabulary of non-interference at all? If not, does it require the republican idea of non-domination, or does it in fact rest on a positive conception of self-mastery?

(e) The voluntary slave contract. If liberty is non-interference, an enforceable contract of subjection entered into without coercion is a free act, and a free system will permit it; if liberty is non-domination, the contract is self-defeating, since what is surrendered is precisely the guarantee against arbitrary power. Say what follows for the at-will contract, which is a weaker version of the same thing.

3. Bring in the historical contrast. The neo-Roman tradition that Quentin Skinner and Philip Pettit have reconstructed treats liberty as a status rather than a set of episodes: a person is free only if she is not in the power of another, so that the slave of a lenient master remains a slave, and a person subject to no interference may still be unfree if she lives at the mercy of another's goodwill. On this view freedom is measured by whether one can meet another's eyes without fear or deference, and unfreedom is compatible with a complete absence of interference. The tradition's reading of the civil-law definition of liberty — the natural capacity of doing as one pleases save where force or law forbids — turns on law being placed alongside force as a possible source of bondage. Ask whether the at-will worker stands in that position, and press on whether the relevant arbitrary power is the individual boss or the structure — market dependence, the absence of alternatives — that makes any boss's discretion decisive. Republicans divide on this; take a side and defend it.

4. Distinguish carefully between what the practice strictly presupposes and what it merely fits well with. The at-will default may presuppose nothing about liberty and instead express a view about efficiency, or the moral priority of contract, or the state's incompetence; the unconscionability doctrine may be paternalism rather than republicanism; a right to reasons before dismissal may be an instance of due process rather than of non-domination. Say where the practice is underdetermined, so that several theories of liberty would license it equally, and identify the features that do genuine discriminating work — the ones that would have to be different for a rival theory to fit.

5. Finally, suppose the implicit conception were replaced by a republican one and that conception taken seriously. What would change, concretely? Which doctrines would have to go — the at-will default itself, the refusal to treat a threat of lawful dismissal as vitiating consent, the preference for exit over voice? Which would survive, and why? What would a labour law built on non-domination require that the present one lacks — rights of contestation, duties to give reasons, a guaranteed floor of outside options, something like citizenship inside the firm? And is the result still recognisably a market economy, or does non-domination in the workplace prove incompatible with the employer's residual authority to direct the enterprise?

Do not give me a survey of positions. Argue, and be willing to conclude that the practice presupposes an incoherent mixture.
