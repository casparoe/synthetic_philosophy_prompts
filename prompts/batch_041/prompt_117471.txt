Ok so for my AP World History class we have to do this "two sides of a debate" presentation and I got stuck with the question of whether societies actually collapse or whether that's just a word we slap on top of stuff that happened. My teacher put it on the board as "a society losing, fast and for good, a whole bunch of the things it used to be able to do," and then refused to say anymore, which was unhelpful. Anyway I want to write the actual dialogue we'd perform, between two people who disagree about this, and I want them to be evenly matched -- like two smart people who have both read alot and neither one is there to be a punching bag. I'm thinking of my friend Dana, who thinks collapse is a real thing you can see in the record (her test is that it has to be fast, it has to stick -- no bouncing back to the old level -- and you have to measure the loss against what that society could do before, not against some ladder where big centralized states are automatically the top rung, because she thinks that ladder is a sneaky value judgment), and my friend Marcus, who thinks "collapse" is always a story told by whoever ended up writing the history, and that the honest move is to stop asking whether a society collapsed and start asking who lost what and who was actually fine. So for him the Maya thing is mostly the collapse of kings and courts while the farmers kept farming and the north kept going, and Easter Island is a morality tale about people wrecking their own island that falls apart once you notice the rats and the timing of when Europeans showed up. Dana would say fine, but then some things really did disappear for everybody at once and Marcus has to deal with that instead of just re-describing it.

What I need is them actually arguing through at least two of these: the end of the Bronze Age, the Maya, Easter Island, and one modern one -- maybe a company town after the mill shuts down, or the Soviet Union breaking up, where the party definitely collapsed and a bunch of other people were pretty relieved. And I want a real fight, not a trading-facts session: every time one of them makes a move the other one has to answer that exact move, and if somebody shifts their ground or says something that doesn't fit what they said earlier, the other one should call it out. Also, here's the part I actually care about, because I noticed it while reading: they keep switching what the "unit" is without noticing -- sometimes it's a state, sometimes a whole population, sometimes an economy, sometimes just an institution or a way of life -- and I want that to be one of the things they fight about, with one of them eventually saying out loud that half their disagreement is that they've been quietly changing what's supposed to be collapsing. Each of them has to give up something real at least once, and Dana specifically has to handle a case where complexity drops but nobody in their right mind would call it a collapse, and Marcus has to handle a case where it's not just the elite who lose -- where almost everybody, top to bottom, loses something, so his "who lost what" move doesn't rescue him. Don't let it end with "well it's all just semantics, you're both right," because my teacher will kill me; it should end with them having narrowed it down to one specific thing they still disagree about, and I want a note at the end telling me what that thing is and which one of them I think is winning and why. No bibliography or citations or anything like that, I can't look stuff up and neither can they, so just keep it as two people talking. Like a page or so, and make the ending a little tense if you can.
