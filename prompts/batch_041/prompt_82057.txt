My sisters finishing her sentence next month, and the college-in-prison program she did her associates through is holding a little graduation ceremony for this years cohort. They've asked me to give the keynote and I said yes before I thought about what I'd actually say. The room will be a mix: graduates, a lot of family, the donors (lovely people, but they want a redemption arc), and a row of correctional staff who'll be listening to see whether I'm going to be a problem. What I want to say is that what we're celebrating is these people being answerable -- that treating someone as a person means holding them to it, and that "we understand, it wasn't really your fault, look at what you came from" is its own kind of insult. Strawson's participant vs. objective attitude is basically my whole thesis, but I don't want to just dunk on the objective attitude either, since his point is that we can't get along without it and it isn't always cold -- sometimes it's the most humane option there is. Please write the speech, 6-8 minutes, that holds all of that together without sounding like a seminar, and that keeps the donors and the staff in the room with me.
