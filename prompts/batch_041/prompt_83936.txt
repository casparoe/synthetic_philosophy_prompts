I have no training in philosophy, but I have spent a great deal of my life arguing about rules, and there is a pattern in those arguments that I have never seen named. I would like you to tell me whether it has a name, and if it does not, to name it and define it carefully.

The setting is always a word with a fuzzy edge. Take vehicle in a rule that bans vehicles from a park, or drunk in a rule about how much a driver may drink, or adult in a rule about who may enter. For each of these there is a range of clear cases, a range of clear non-cases, and a band in between where people disagree and where no measurement, test, or further inquiry seems able to settle the matter. Everyone I argue with grants that the band exists. What I keep noticing is that we do not have one way of talking about it. We have two, and we slide between them according to what we want the conversation to yield.

When we want to hold someone responsible, we talk as though the case had a single right answer that the person ought to have reached. A man rides a motorized scooter into the park, and the guard says he should have known the rule covered it, that he was over the line, that he does not get to hide in the gray. The band is treated as a place where the facts are perfectly settled and merely hard to see, so that getting it wrong is a real failure and grounds for blame.

When we want to excuse, or to avoid committing ourselves, the talk flips. The same guard will say that the rule simply does not reach a scooter, that there is no right answer to be had here, that you cannot fault a person over a case that has no answer, that it could have gone either way. Now the band is treated as a place where the facts run out altogether.

The same people do both, about the same words, sometimes within the same afternoon, and each is defended as if it were obviously true.

I have a rough sense that philosophers hold two standing positions about these bands: one, that there is always a fact of the matter and we simply cannot get at it; another, that there is no fact of the matter at all. What I cannot find is a name for the thing I am actually pointing at, which is neither of those positions but the practice of using both, one after the other, as the occasion demands, together with the question of what to make of that practice.

So: is there an established concept for this, or is it a gap? Either way I want the carving, not the label. In particular I want to know whether the slide is simply an inconsistency, or a coherent position that someone could hold on purpose, or a piece of ordinary pragmatic behavior that only looks like a contradiction once it is written down. And I want the edges. Does it still count as the same thing when the two ways of talking come from two different people rather than from one, and when the person who switches does so because they have genuinely changed their mind about the underlying facts rather than because the switch is convenient? If nothing established fits, give me a term and a definition that separates the phenomenon from the two positions above and from plain hypocrisy.
