Ok so I keep seeing people say that modeling knowledge as a set of possible worlds -- the agent knows P just in case P holds in every world they havent ruled out -- makes agents "logically omniscient," and that this is some kind of fatal flaw. I get the slogan but I dont actually understand why it follows or why it's supposed to be so bad, so please walk me through it properly.

Two things I specifically want untangled. First: why does that setup force the agent to know every logical consequence of what they know, including every tautology, and is that one fact or a bundle of separate facts? I suspect "knows all tautologies" and "knows everything that follows from what they know" and "believes one contradiction so they know everything" are not the same problem, and I want to know which of these the framework actually commits to and which are optional extras.

Second, and this is the part that really confuses me: the same feature seems to make it impossible to ever learn anything by reasoning. If I already know P and I already know if P then Q, closure says I already knew Q -- so what was the deduction even for? Is the problem of deduction the same problem as logical omniscience, or a different one that just happens to share a cause? And does a fix for one fix the other, or can you have a model that's not omniscient and still can't explain learning by inference?

To make it concrete, use this case: an agent who knows P and knows if P then Q but has genuinely never put the two together, and if you asked them "do you know Q?" they'd say no, sincerely. I want to know what the possible-worlds model says about them -- does it say they know Q, and is that a false prediction about the person, or is the model just quietly answering a different question (what their evidence supports, rather than what they've actually registered)? Then tell me: if I wanted a model that handles the deduction case and the closure cases, what would I have to give up, and is there a principled place to draw the line, or does every fix just relocate the problem somewhere else? I'd much rather understand the tradeoff than get handed a list of named fixes.
