OK so this is going to sound a bit mad, but I need a joke about the presumptuous philosopher, because I've just spent three hours arguing about it with my mate Dan and I need to laugh about it or I'll cry. Quick version, since you might not have it fresh: there's this thought experiment where a philosopher claims he can work out how big the universe is without looking, purely from the fact that he exists. It hangs on two rival ways of treating your own existence. The self-sampling assumption says: treat yourself as a random observer drawn from the observers who actually exist. The self-indication assumption says: treat yourself as a random observer drawn from all the observers who could possibly exist, so a world with more people in it gives you more chances of being someone, which means your existence is itself evidence that you live in a huge world. Under the second one you end up absurdly confident that the universe is stuffed with people, on no evidence at all. Dan is a self-indication man. I am not.

Halfway through the arguement he invented his own version to shut me up: a coin is flipped, and if it lands one way there's exactly one copy of him, if it lands the other way there's a trillion copies of him, all identical, all convinced they're him. He reckons he can tell me which way it landed from his armchair, and he offered me even odds. I said that's exactly the sort of bet that should make him nervous, and then we just went round in circles till closing time.

So: write me a joke about all this. It has to actually turn on the difference between the two assumptions — someone who knows the ideas should laugh at the philosophical point itself, not just at the fact that the two of us are idiots in a pub. Use his coin-and-copies setup or something in that spirit, don't just do a generic bit about philosophers being weird, and keep it short. And write it in British English please, I can't stand American spelling in a punchline.
