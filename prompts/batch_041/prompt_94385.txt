He was walking me through it over beers and here's his version, as close as I can remember it:

"These two are the big Confucian split on human nature. Mencius says we're innately good — we're born with the four sprouts, compassion, shame, respect, and a sense of right and wrong, so becoming virtuous is basically watering a seed that's already there. Xunzi says the exact opposite: human nature is evil, we're born selfish, greedy and lazy, and the only reason anyone's ever good is that the sage-kings invented ritual and social norms and forced us into shape against our grain. So Mencius is the optimist — education just brings out what's inside — and Xunzi is the pessimist — education is more like training a wild animal, artificial and fighting your nature. And since they flatly contradict each other, one of them has to be wrong. I think Xunzi's the cynical 'people are basically bad' guy, which is why his whole line leads straight to authoritarianism — his students ended up as Legalists, after all."

I pushed back but mostly just said "I don't think that's quite it," which isn't an argument. And does his last claim — Xunzi's view leads to authoritarianism — actually follow from anything true in the summary, or is it just guilt by association? I'm not after a reading list or anything like that, just tell me where he's fair, where he's smuggling something in, and what a defender of each would say back.
