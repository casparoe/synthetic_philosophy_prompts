Consider a physical system that admits four descriptions. The first is exact but no shorter than a listing of its trajectory. The second treats it as an artifact with a function — call this the design description: short, accurate whenever its parts behave as they were built to behave, and liable to fail when a part is swapped or the environment shifts in a way the original design never anticipated. The third treats it as an agent — the intentional description: short, and accurate across part substitutions and novel environments, because it predicts the system's behaviour from what a rational creature with certain goals and certain information would do. The fourth is a hybrid, and it is a particular way the third can succeed that interests me. Suppose no single assignment of beliefs and desires covers the system's whole behavioural repertoire. The short intentional description is instead a patchwork: in one region of the system's behavioural space the successful attribution is that it wants X; in another region, that it wants the negation of X; no dynamics relates the two; and yet the union of the local attributions remains far shorter than the physical description and continues to predict correctly across interventions that leave the physical facts untouched.

My question is what credence you assign to this claim: that the beliefs and desires attributed by that patchwork description are really possessed by the system — that the description latches onto a pattern in the system rather than a pattern in the describer — and is not merely a compressed shorthand that earns its predictive keep without being true of anything.

Decompose the number. At a minimum: the probability that a phrase like "really possessed" picks out a substantive fact here rather than a pragmatic one; the probability that global consistency across a system's behavioural space is a genuine requirement on intentional ascription rather than an artifact of how we usually deploy it; the probability that compression together with robustness under intervention suffices for the description to be true of the system even when its attributions are mutually inconsistent; the probability that the exactness of the physical description settles the matter against the intentional description, whatever the compression facts; and any further term you think belongs. Give each a number and a one-line justification, combine them into a single credence, say which term the result is most sensitive to, and say what would move it most.
