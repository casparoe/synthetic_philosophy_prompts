I'm on the ethics committee at a transplant hospital and I've been asked to give the closing remarks at our annual remembrance event for donor families — three minutes, standing up, in front of donor families, a handful of recipients, and a lot of ICU and transplant staff who have heard every version of this speech before. Here's my problem. Roughly half the donors we're remembering were declared dead on brain criteria, and half were declared dead on circulatory criteria after we withdrew treatment, and I have to say something true about when they died without ranking one group over the other and without turning it into a seminar. Our protocol is that we wait two minutes after the pulse stops before we call it; the hospital across town waits five; I've never found a sentence that makes that sound like anything other than a policy choice, and the families who were standing out in the corridor counting are exactly the ones who'll notice. And please don't have me say anyone 'was already gone' — that line works for the brain-death families and I'm honestly not sure it's true for the others, and it's the one thing I'd hate to be caught saying by someone who knows better. So: write the speech, in British English, and then give me a short JSON object after it with these fields: "speech" (the remarks themselves), "death_claims" (array of objects with "claim", "criterion_it_assumes" — "brain", "circulatory" or "neither" — and "who_in_the_room_might_hear_it_as_false"), "dodged" (array of objects with "issue" and "how_the_speech_gets_round_it_without_lying"), and "riskiest_line" (an object with "line" and "why" — the sentence most likely to be heard as endorsing one criterion over the other). Keep the whole thing under 400 words.
