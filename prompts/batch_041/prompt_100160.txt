I teach a seminar where a hard-line French republican — thinks laïcité is the only honest way for a pluralist state to hold together — and a Canadian-style liberal multiculturalist have been at each other's throats all term, and honestly they've been talking past each other more than arguing. I want to force them to produce the joint statement they could both actually sign. So draft it for me.

Set it up as an adversarial collaboration, not a debate. I want a numbered section of claims both sides accept — about what the state is for, what neutrality does and doesn't require, what's wrong with forced assimilation, what's wrong with a state that just lets a thousand flowers bloom, that sort of thing. Keep these non-vacuous. The mush where both sides agree everyone deserves respect is exactly what I don't want; every agreed claim should cost each side something.

Then the actual disagreements, each one written in wording the side who holds it would accept as fair — so the laïcité person signs off on the sentence describing their view, and so does the multiculturalist. No strawmen, no loaded verbs.

Then the cruxes. My hunch is that one is whether neutrality should be cashed out as neutrality of justification or as neutrality of effect, and another is whether cultural membership is a primary good the state must secure or merely an option it must not block. But tell me if those are the real joints or if there's something deeper underneath. And for each crux, give me a case or argument both sides would agree actually bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

If part of what they've been fighting about turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.

One more thing. I don't want the usual Rawls-vs-Kymlicka rehash — I've read that a hundred times. Somewhere in the statement, make them grapple with a principle I've been kicking around: that a state may promote a shared civic culture, but only through means equally available to every citizen on the same terms. Is that something either camp can genuinely live with, or does it secretly collapse into one side's position the moment you press on it? Work that out and say which way it falls.

Finally, be upfront about how confident you are in the positions you're attributing to each side — I'd rather see the uncertainty than a confident gloss — and single out the one point you're least sure of.
