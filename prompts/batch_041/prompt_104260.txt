ok so i want a poem about dennett's three stances and the real patterns thing, but i want the form to actually do the work instead of just name-checking it. heres the idea: three stanzas about the same small event -- pick something like a thermostat clicking on, or a chess engine making a move, or a person reaching for their coffee -- one stanza per stance. physical stance first: predict it from the parts and the laws, tedious and complete, the way an exhaustive bit-map of a grid is complete. then design stance: treat the thing as built to do a job. then intentional stance: ascribe beliefs and desires and predict from those. and the stanzas should get shorter each time, clearly shorter, because thats the whole dennett point -- a pattern is real when the description that catches it compresses better than the raw enumeration does, and the intentional stance is the compressy one. i also want one short phrase, exact same words, to survive all three stanzas, so that by the end the reader sees that whats still standing after you squeeze the description down is the pattern itself, and thats why its real without being a fiction and without being some further object sitting on top of the physics. dont let it collapse into a lecture at the end, but the last line or two should land that. maybe 20-30 lines total, rhyme if you want it, dont force it. someone who knows the three stances should read it and think yeah thats right, and someone who doesnt should still get a real poem out of it.
