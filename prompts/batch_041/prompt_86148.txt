Here is the background I'm starting from. Nozick's entitlement theory says that a person's holdings are just if they were justly acquired and justly transferred, and that whether a distribution is just is a matter of its history rather than of how the holdings compare to some pattern such as equality or need. His Wilt Chamberlain example is meant to show the cost of pattern-based views: let a great many people each freely hand over a small amount to someone they want to watch, and the resulting inequality is produced by nothing but free choices, so holding the pattern in place would require continually interfering with those choices. I take the load-bearing claims to be roughly these: liberty upsets patterns; voluntariness at each step transmits justice to the whole; no one is wronged where no one's rights are violated; and entitlement is not desert. Tell me if that is a fair reading.

Now I want to modify the example, because I think my modification is not merely a harder case but a structurally different one, and I want to know what follows from the difference. I work on a marketplace product, which is where the variant came from, but please treat it as philosophy and not as product advice.

In my version:

- The transfers are still voluntary, still individually small, and each one still leaves the payer better off than not paying would have, at the moment of paying.
- But the motive for paying is partly comparative: a large part of the value of paying is being one of the people who paid, so each additional payer raises the next person's willingness to pay, and the process feeds itself.
- The cascade degrades the free alternative that non-payers rely on, because the same limited pool of resources — venues, attention, whatever the scarce thing happens to be — is progressively bid away from it. The non-payers end up worse off in absolute terms than they started, even though nobody took anything from them and nobody coerced them.
- And the person who ends up with the money is not the performer. He owns the mechanism that coordinates the cascade — think of the owner of a matching algorithm rather than a star athlete. He does not create the value so much as sit where it collects; nobody's payment was made for anything he produced.
