I'm teaching a week on nudging in a policy ethics seminar and I want The Truman Show as the centrepiece, but every time I draft the lecture I get tangled up. The obvious reading is that Christof is the paternalist, Seahaven is the nudge, and the door at the end is autonomy winning. Thats too clean and I want something better.

So read the film through the actual paternalism and nudging literature and tell me where it lands. Things I want it to hit: the difference between paternalism about means and paternalism about ends; the defence that a nudge is fine because it leaves you better off by your own lights; the boost-versus-nudge distinction (influences that build your reasoning versus ones that exploit the fact that you're not using it); the transparency condition, i.e. would the intervention still work if you told the person; and sludge, the frictions that quietly make the wanted option the only live one. Truman's whole preference set is manufactered -- the fear of water was installed, the ambition to explore was corrected in a classroom -- so the by-your-own-lights test looks vacuous to me. Is that the film making a point, or the film accidentally showing that the test was never doing the work?

Scenes I want done properly: the studio light falling out of the sky and the radio explaining it away in real time (a nudge caught in the act, and the fix is another nudge); the run of obstacles when he tries to leave town; Christof's interview where he says the world outside is the sick place; and the last ten minutes at the door, plus the guards flipping channels after. And one thing I cant get past: the film is itself choice architecture aimed at us, and it wants us cheering when he walks out. Does that make it a nudge in the bad sense, or does it show the whole category is too broad to carry any moral weight? Where does the film resist the reading -- what does it simplify or get wrong about the debate? I'd rather have a reading that leaves the film intact than one that turns Christof into a textbook case. Also tell me what the film adds that the arguments alone dont. No citations needed, I know the literature.
