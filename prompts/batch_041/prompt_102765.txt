I hold the view that perceptual knowledge is "easy": it does not require any antecedent, non-question-begging justification for the reliability of perception, and the bootstrapping procedure — relying on perceptual beliefs to build up a track record and then inferring that perception is reliable — can yield genuine knowledge rather than mere circularity. I want to know what would count against this. Which intuitions about the bootstrapping cases (a subject confirming a gauge against itself, a perceiver vindicating perception by its own lights) must a defender simply bite the bullet on, and which are genuinely damaging rather than merely rhetorical? Which formal results about closure, the transmission of justification, or the structure of epistemic support would actually undermine the view, as opposed to restating the circularity charge in a new vocabulary? What would a defender themselves concede counts against them, and has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, is that a defect, or simply the price of any sufficiently general epistemology? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones I should find most persuasive.
