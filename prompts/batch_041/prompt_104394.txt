I've been reporting for a year on the guilt-versus-shame argument: the camp that holds shame to be a cruel, status-obsessed emotion a liberal society should outgrow, and the camp that holds guilt is inert on its own — that a culture which cannot shame has no sanction left for the wrongs it cannot catch. They keep talking past each other, and I want the document they should have written together. Draft the joint statement they could both sign: first, the agreed claims about what each emotion actually does — what each is tracking, how each responds to concealment, what repair each makes possible (and whether shaming that ends in reintegration is a different mechanism from shaming that ends in exile); then their remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; then the cruxes. I expect one crux is whether shame is constitutively about being seen, another is whether a society can deliberately choose its moral emotions or only inherit them, and a third is what would have to be installed to replace shame if it were dropped. For each crux, give a case or comparison both sides would treat as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance — I've been using a wrong that no one can ever discover and that harms no one, where guilt should still bite and, on some accounts, shame cannot. If part of the quarrel is verbal — say, one side using shame for a social sanction and the other for a lived experience — identify it and dissolve it rather than adjudicating it. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, with no citations or reading lists: the thing has to stand on arguments and cases.
