So this is going to sound like a strange thing to be hung up on, but here we are.

Three weeks ago I started recording myself at night. Long story: my girlfreind kept telling me I talk in my sleep and I didn't believe her, so I got one of those apps that switches the mic on when it hears you moving. Turns out she was right, and its constant. But twice now I've woken up with no memory of having dreamed anything at all, checked the app, and found a two-minute recording of myself in this flat, sleepy voice describing a whole scene -- people, a kitchen, what someone was wearing, what happened, in order. Not mumbling. Description. Like I was filing a report.

So my first thought was, fine, I had a dream and forgot it. Then I started wondering what it even means to say I had a dream when the only trace of it is a recording of me describing it at 3am that the awake me has zero access to. Which reminded me of an argument I half-rememeber from a philosophy course years ago. I think it was Norman Malcolm and Dan Dennett, and I've definately got who said which backwards, but the shape of it was: one of them was saying dreams aren't experiences at all, that the whole idea of 'the experience you had while you were asleep' is a muddle, because the only thing that makes something a dream is that it can be told when you wake up -- the telling is the criterion, not some inner show you were watching. And the other one pushing back that you can't settle a question like that by unpacking the word 'dream', that it's a real question about what's actually going on in there, and we might turn out to be wrong about it.

I want to see that fight done properly. Write me a dialogue between two people who hold those two positions -- the 'dreams aren't experiences, the question is confused' one and the 'you don't get to settle that by definition' one. Both should be sharp, and neither should cave; the second guy especially shouldn't just roll over, I suspect he has more to say than people give him. Make them go at my recordings specifically, because that's the case I actually care about:

- Does the recording count as evidence one way or the other? I described the thing in detail while half asleep and I can't remember any of it now. Did I have a dream, or did I tell a story?
- What is the difference, if any, between a dream I had and forgot and a dream I only know about secondhand, from my own voice?
- Press the first guy on the case where someone dreams something, never tells anyone, and never remembers it. Is there anything left of it? And if not, what is he saying about the thousands of nights of my life I remember nothing from?
- Somewhere in there, someone should ask whether any of this is about what we mean by a word or whether something is actually at stake -- because it feels like something is at stake to me and I can't say why.

Real back and forth, not two essays with a topic sentence in between. Each one should be answering what the other just said, including the low blows.

At the end, step out of it and tell me how confident you actually are that those are the two real positions in that debate and not a version you invented, and flag the one moment in the dialogue you're least sure either side would really accept. Don't cite anything -- I'm not going to look it up, I want the argument, not a reading list.
