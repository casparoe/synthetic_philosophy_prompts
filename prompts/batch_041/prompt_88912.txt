The literature usually treats the central puzzle as one about literally infinite populations or infinite welfare — fanaticism, the impossibility of a satisfactory aggregation, the repugnant conclusion at the limit — and traces the trouble to infinity as such. I suspect infinity is doing less work than is assumed: the same structural difficulties seem to reappear whenever the value at stake is unbounded rather than infinite, so that the real question is whether there is a principled way to decide between outcomes whose value is arbitrarily large but finite, and whether the infinite case is merely the limiting instance of a more general problem. Turn this into a proposal.
