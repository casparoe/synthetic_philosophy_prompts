Here's the question I set:

"Suppose an agent's credences are given by P. She has an experience that does not tell her for certain whether E, but shifts her credences over E and ¬E — say to 0.7 and 0.3. (a) Why can't she just conditionalize? (b) State the rule that applies instead and the assumption it relies on. (c) Does the rule make the order in which she receives a sequence of such uncertain reports irrelevant to her final credences? Explain."

And here's what the student wrote:

"The agent can't conditionalize because conditionalizing requires a proposition she's certain of. Her experience leaves her at 0.7 on E, not 1, so there's no E to plug into P(H | E) = P(H ∧ E)/P(E) — conditionalizing on E would force her to 1 and conditionalizing on ¬E would force her to 0, and neither is what happened.

The rule that applies is Jeffrey's rule. Take a partition {E_1, ..., E_n} and suppose the experience moves her credences over the cells to new values q_1, ..., q_n. Then her new credence in any hypothesis H is

P_new(H) = Σ_i q_i · P_old(H | E_i).

The thought is that the experience only changes how likely she takes the E_i to be; it tells her nothing new about how H bears on them. So the conditional credences P(H | E_i) are held fixed — they're 'rigid'. This rigidity is the assumption the rule leans on. It's often glossed as the requirement that we keep the same partition before and after the shift, and that's really what 'rigidity' amounts to.

As a sanity check, if the new distribution over the partition is certain — one cell at 1, the rest at 0 — the formula collapses to ordinary conditionalization, which is what you'd want.

On (c): the order doesn't matter. Each application just reweights the same conditional credences by the newest probabilities over the partition, and since the reweighting is multiplicative the sequence commutes — you land in the same place whichever order the reports arrive in. This is one of the nicer features of the rule, and it's why it doesn't have the trap that dogmatic conditionalization has, where certainty in one direction can never be undone.

The one thing I'd flag is that the q_i have to come from somewhere — the rule tells you how to move once you've got them, not how to read them off the experience."
