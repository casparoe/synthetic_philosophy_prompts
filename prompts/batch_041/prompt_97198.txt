I keep meeting the saying 'history is written by the victors' in a seminar on the origins of the Cold War, where a classmate uses it to shut down the question of who started it: the United States came out on top, he says, so of course the standard account blames the Soviets, and there is no getting behind that. I suspect the move is too quick, but I cannot say precisely where it goes wrong. Lay out the distinct claims that one sentence could be making — about which evidence survives, about who controls the telling, about whether there is any fact of the matter about who started the Cold War, and about what 'blame' is even doing in a historical explanation — and mark each as a platitude, a defensible but contested thesis, or false. Then say which of those readings my classmate would actually need in order to reach his conclusion, which are doing no work, and which he most likely has in mind. Present the readings as a menu rather than settling on one.
