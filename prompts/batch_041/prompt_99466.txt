I've been teaching myself RL off youtube and reading alot of the alignment writing that floats around online (I did a year of a CS degree before dropping out, so I'm not coming at this from a research background, I just think about it on the bus), and I've ended up with a pile of views about preferences and behaviour that I suspect don't all fit together. Here's roughly where I've landed: (1) when someone reliably picks A over B, that's basically just what it is to prefer A over B — a preference is a standing tendency to choose, nothing more spooky than that; (2) all the same, I think there's a real gap between what someone wants and what they actually choose, the addict who'd give anything to stop being the obvious case; (3) I think the most promising route to safe AI is to have the system work out human preferences by watching what people do, rather than us trying to write the objective down by hand — the inverse reinforcement learning stuff; (4) I'm not bothered that you can never pin down a unique reward function from behaviour, since you can add a term that depends only on the state and the best possible policy comes out identical, and generally the leftover freedom seems to be exactly the freedom that doesn't change any choices, so nothing real is being lost; and (5) honestly most human behaviour looks like it's driven by context, framing, habit and whatever happens to be in front of us, so I don't think most individual choices are tracking any stable preference at all.

What I want is an audit rather than a defence of any of it: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad is each one? Rank them by severity instead of listing them as all equally bad, and for each real clash tell me the cheapest way to patch it and what that patch costs me. And if I could only give up one of the five, which one buys me the most consistency? I've got a feeling two or three of these are quietly contradicting each other but I can never work out which, and my flatmate says I'm just doing philosophy with extra steps.
