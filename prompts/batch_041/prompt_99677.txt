I have no academic training in philosophy, but I have been trying to work out what David Hume would have made of the asymmetry argument associated with David Benatar. As I understand it, the argument says that when someone exists, their pain is bad and their pleasure is good; when someone never exists, the absence of pain is good even though no one enjoys that good, while the absence of pleasure is not bad because there is no one to be deprived of it, so non-existence is preferable to existence. What I want is a reconstruction of Hume's likely response, not a summary of his general ethics. In particular, given his view that moral distinctions arise from sentiment rather than reason, would he have rejected the asymmetry as incoherent because it treats the absence of pain as a good without any subject who approves or benefits? Or could his account of sympathy and the standpoint we take when we moralize allow him to make sense of impersonal goods? Please lay out the strongest Humean line of argument on each side, say which one you think he would actually have taken, and flag where the reconstruction is speculative.
