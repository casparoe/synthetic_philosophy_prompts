Ok, so for context: I write a newsletter on health policy and ideas — drug pricing, NICE-style cost-effectiveness thresholds, the value of a statistical life, discount rates for climate stuff — and over the years I've accumulated a pile of views that I'm now worried don't actually hang together. I can't see where the crack is, so I'd like you to find it for me.

(1) I buy the Harsanyi / veil-of-ignorance arguement: if you had to pick your life without knowing which person you'd end up being, treating each existing person as equally likely to be you, you'd choose whatever maximizes the total (equivalently, the average) of everyone's expected utility. My understanding is that once you grant a few mild axioms about rational choice under uncertainty plus a Pareto-ish condition, that aggregation rule isn't a heuristic, it's the only one on the table.

(2) I'm deeply suspicious of interpersonal comparisons. When a cost-benefit analysis puts a dollar figure on a statistical life, or a QALY on a year of dialysis, I think I'm looking at a convention, not a measurement.

(3) In actual policy fights I'm a prioritarian — I want extra weight on the worst off, and bare sum-maximizing spits out answers I'd be embarassed to publish.

(4) Fairness is at least partly about chances, not just totals. If one of two patients can have the drug, a lottery that gives each a real shot beats handing it to one of them for sure, even when the expected-utility totals come out identical.

(5) The really big weighting questions — how much extra weight the worst off get, how hard to discount future generations — have no fact of the matter. They're value choices, and no formalism is going to discover the answer.

So: which of these genuinely collide, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest thing to give up? Please rank the tensions by how bad they are rather than telling me everything is in tension with everything, and be specific about what would have to give in each case. Also don't tell me to just throw out the whole apparatus, I have a deadline.
