I'm putting together a handout for a law-and-philosophy reading group that's doing the old Hart–Devlin punch-up about whether the criminal law can go after conduct that harms nobody. I want a decision tree, eight nodes at most, that takes someone from 'yeah, some acts are just plain wrong even though nobody's hurt' to a named position, and I want each node to be a question about a concrete case rather than an ism, so people can answer honestly without already knowing where they'll land.

Five cases to build it round (I made them up, so the tree has to do the actual work): a pair of adult siblings who have consensual, contracepted sex in private and are perfectly happy; a competent adult who wants a healthy leg amputated by a willing surgeon, gets it, and is grateful afterwards; a man who has sex with the corpse of a woman who consented to it in advance, left no relatives, and whose wishes nobody else cares about; two adults doing elaborate slave-owner role-play in period costume, behind closed doors, with no audience; and two men having sex in a locked public toilet cubicle that nobody else ever uses.

The branch questions I care about: whether harmless wrongdoing exists at all; whether its wrongness is a reason for the criminal law or only for private reproach; whether the real ground is the act's intrinsic wrongness, the community's stake in its shared morality, the disgust it provokes, or harm to the agent himself; whether wrongness only counts in combination with some other liberty-limiting principle (pure vs impure moralism); and whether it matters that the act is done in private or in a place the public could stumble into. Leaves should include the Mill/Hart harm-principle line, Devlin-style social-cohesion moralism, pure legal moralism, the offence principle, paternalism, the public-wrongs view, and the position that harmless wrongdoing is real but never criminalisable.

Name the positions where a label exists, write 'unnamed' where nobody obviously holds it, and flag any leaves your tree can't really seperate. Also mark the answer-combinations nobody occupies and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, repulsive, or just unexplored. No citations needed, and I dont want a bibliography — one side of A4.
