ok so I'm in a seminar on consciousness and we just did panpsychism, and afterwards me and a friend built an argument in the pub that I can't stop chewing on, because if it works it says something insane about ethics. I need you to tell me which step to drop, and what dropping it costs.

Here's the setup. Panpsychism, the version I like, says experience is one of the fundamental features of the physical world: whatever is physically fundamental has some minimal bit of it, and the fundamental things are the quanta, i.e. the excitations of the fields. Then:

(1) Each quantum has a tiny bit of experience, and a composite system's experience is a function of the micro-experience of its parts — in the simplest version, more micro-experiencers means more experience in the whole.
(2) But in QFT the number of quanta isn't a frame-independent fact, as our TA kept insisting. It depends on the observer's motion and on which modes you expand the field in: the vacuum that's empty for me is a warm bath of particles for my accelerating twin (Unruh effect), and whether a detector clicks depends on the detector's trajectory.
(3) So how much micro-experience a region contains depends on the observer. The same patch of vacuum is empty of experience for me and crowded with it for my twin.
(4) Ethics can't be like that. Whether a process contains suffering can't depend on who's asking or how fast they're going — otherwise my twin, by firing her engines through empty space, could be bringing enormous amounts of suffering into existence, or destroying it, and which of the two it is would depend on her frame.
(5) So (1) can't be true as stated.

My friend says (4) is doing all the work and that it proves too much, since temperature is frame-relative in exactly the same way (an accelerating thermometer reads hot in vacuum) and nobody thinks thermodynamics is incoherent. She thinks I either give up (1) or accept that moral facts can be frame-relative, and I can't tell which one I can live with. I definately want to keep panpsychism, and I want to keep the thought that if there are micro-subjects at all then their suffering counts for something — otherwise the whole pan-sentience angle is dead on arrival. (Also, on the additive reading my kettle contains more micro-experiencers than my entire family, which I take to be a reductio of something.) Where does this actually go wrong, and what am I paying when I fix it?
