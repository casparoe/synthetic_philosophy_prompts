Write a dialogue between two philosophers going back and forth about Thomson's lamp — you know, the one that gets switched on and off at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ... and so has been flipped infinitely many times by t=1. One of them thinks the lamp just has no determinate state at t=1: the setup pins down the state at every time before 1 and says nothing about t=1 itself, and wanting an answer anyways is like wanting the 'last digit' of 0.999... . The other thinks a real lamp is either on or off at t=1 no matter what, and that our failure to read it off the flipping schedule is a failure of the toy idealization rather than a sign that reality is silent — nail down the actual physics (what a filament or a switch does when driven infinitely fast) and there will be a fact of the matter. Have them actually respond to each other over a few rounds instead of talking past each other. One good thread to work in: a variant lamp where each switch position is the average of the previous two, so the positions converge and everyone agrees what it's doing at t=1 — what does that show about whether the original dispute is really about physics, about infinity, or about what a description can pin down? Make both of them sharp; no strawmen.
