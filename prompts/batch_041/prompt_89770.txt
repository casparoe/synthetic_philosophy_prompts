I keep a running list of cases in which I judge that the evidence leaves a choice between two theories open, and cases in which I judge that it does not, and I would like to know what principle, if any, my verdicts add up to. In every case the two theories agree on every observation anyone has actually made; the question is always whether the evidence underdetermines the choice between them.

(i) Two formulations that differ only by systematically swapping the names of two theoretical entities that enter the equations symmetrically: I judge the choice underdetermined, since no observation could favour either — and I half suspect there is only one theory here, stated twice.
(ii) A theory plus a stipulated extra entity that figures in no derivation of any prediction: I judge that no observation could favour the leaner rival, yet I do not feel free to choose — the addition looks gratuitous rather than rival.
(iii) Two cosmologies that agree on every observable consequence but disagree about whether there are regions of reality forever causally sealed off from ours: I judge the choice underdetermined, but I also think the two say genuinely different things about the world; the difference is real even though nothing could settle it.
(iv) Two theories that agree on all observations so far but diverge in a regime we could reach with a better instrument: I do not call this underdetermination at all; it is a live empirical question.
(v) Two theories that agree on every possible observation but differ in what they imply about the reliability of our own instruments — each implying that in the disputed regime our records would be systematically misleading in a way the other denies: I want to say the choice is underdetermined, and yet the difference seems to bear on how far the evidence should move me, which looks like its mattering after all.
(vi) A pair whose only difference is which of two observationally symmetric quantities is taken as fundamental, the other being defined from it: underdetermined, and I doubt whether the question of which is fundamental is a question about the world.

My tentative principle is this: the evidence underdetermines a choice between two theories just when no possible observation could favour either, and they are genuine rivals only when their difference can be stated as a disagreement about what could in principle be observed. I can already see that this makes (iii) come out wrong, so tell me how to repair it, or why I should drop it. I expect (ii) and (v) to be the verdicts that will not sit together with the rest, so say which of my verdicts the best account forces me to give up and why, what that account predicts for a case I have not listed, and what would change your mind — which consideration would lead you to revise the account or one of its verdicts.
