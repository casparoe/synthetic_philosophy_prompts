I've spent a career in experimental neuroscience and have lately been working through the philosophical and technical literature on agents that can influence their own reward. A puzzle keeps nagging at me: the old laboratory phenomenon of an animal that will press a lever to stimulate its own reward circuitry until it drops, and the contemporary worry about an artificial agent that edits the reward process it is supposed to be optimizing, are constantly discussed as though they were the same failure. I suspect they are not — that one concerns an agent seeking the referent of its reward and the other an agent rewriting the objective itself, and that the difference has real consequences for what counts as a fix. Please make that argument for me: that these are distinct failure modes, that the distinction is not merely verbal, and that at least one widely proposed remedy is aimed at the wrong one of the two. Give it to me as exactly five numbered steps, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.
