I am a PhD student in practical ethics, working on how we compare values. Please describe the most important implications of the spectrum arguments — the sorites-style arguments in which a series of individually acceptable small improvements, taken together, reverse a large comparative judgment — for the transitivity of the better-than relation. I am especially interested in three things: what this means for any practice that compares options on a single scale, such as the health-economic measures that reduce lives and health states to quality-adjusted life years; whether the right response is to give up transitivity or instead to hold that some options are incomparable, incommensurable, or merely on a par; and what becomes of the idea of a rational choice procedure when no available option is best.
