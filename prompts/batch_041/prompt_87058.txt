Write a dialogue between two equally well-informed interlocutors who disagree about the Anthropocene. The first is a stratigrapher who argues that a geological epoch can be admitted only if it can be anchored to a single, globally synchronous marker in the rock record, which makes the mid-twentieth-century radionuclide fallout from atmospheric nuclear testing the only defensible candidate. The second is an environmental historian who argues that the Anthropocene is a value-laden act of periodization rather than a natural kind, that the human transformation of the planet is diachronous, and that geologists have no special authority to settle a question that is partly political. Have them genuinely engage each other on whether a diachronous process can qualify as an epoch at all, whether an event is a better category than an epoch, whether the geological timescale is as value-neutral as the stratigrapher claims, and who should get to decide. Neither should be a strawman.
