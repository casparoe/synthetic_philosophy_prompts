So my friend has run crisis-simulation games for a policy school for like fifteen years, and at this point she has really settled opinions about what makes a threat in a crisis actually do any work. Her casebook goes roughly like this. Run one: a team announced its red line on live TV, shut down its own back-channel, and basically torched its bridges in public — she scored them as having the stronger position and said the publicity was doing the work. Run two: a team let it be known that its leader was erratic and maybe not fully in command of himself — she said that bought them real leverage. Run three: a team wrote its retaliation into standing orders and handed the trigger to a mid-level commander with a narrow tripwire — she called that the single strongest move in the whole casebook. Run four: two teams with almost identical stakes in the disputed patch and roughly equal arsenals — she called it stable, said nobody would ever move, and was frankly bored. Run five: a team with a huge threat that it only ever whispered in a private channel — the other side walked all over them, and she said the threat failed precisely because it never went public. Run six: a team that had way more at stake than its opponent won without escalating at all, and she credited the stake, not any commitment gadget.

Now the team I'm coaching, in the next run, does this. It has genuinely handed a regional commander the authority to launch on a narrow tripwire — so backing down is, for real, no longer in the leader's hands — but it's kept the whole arrangement secret: no announcement, no declared red line, nothing public. And it has less at stake in the disputed region than the other side does.

What's she going to say? Is this team strong or weak? I care less about the verdict than about the rule she's actually running, what the other rules are that fit her casebook just as well, which of those rules the new run would actually seperate, and — if they really do come apart here — which further run I should set up next to tell them apart for good. Give me your best guess at her verdict and the reason you'd back it.
