Last spring I got picked for jury duty on an armed robbery trial, and the thing that's stuck with me isn't the evidence, it's the hour in the jury room where everybody kept saying some version of "he deserves it" — and I couldn't tell whether that was an actual reason or just a fancy way of saying "he did it and we're angry." So I've spent the last few months reading my way into the free will and punishment literature, and I've landed in a reading group with two people who cannot agree: one is a compatibilist in the Dennett mould who thinks some notion of just deserts is doing real, indispensable work in any humane system of criminal justice, the other is a hard incompatibilist in the Caruso mould who thinks basic desert is a fiction and that we should justify locking people up roughly the way we justify quarantine — on the basis of danger and our obligations to possible victims, not because anybody is owed suffering. They've been arguing past each other for about six months now, and honestly half the time I think they mean different things by "desert," so I want to make them write the document they should have written instead of talking at each other.

You're a philosopher of free will and criminal law — before you draft anything, spend a bit of space brainstorming the candidate points of agreement and the places where they might just be talking past each other, then write me the joint statement both of them could actually put their names to. I want: a numbered list of claims they both accept, and I don't mean mush — it should include claims about what punishment is for, about what compatibilism does and doesn't claim, and about what the evidence from neuroscience and moral psychology can and can't show; then a table of the genuine remaining disagreements, each row giving the neutral formulation of the dispute, then the version the compatibilist would sign off on as fair to their view, then the version the hard incompatibilist would sign off on for theirs; then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move somebody — and for each crux, the argument or case both of them would agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction about how it comes out, written down in advance so nobody can weasel later.

Two things I want it to actually do, or it'll be all abstractions. First, keep three questions seperate, because I suspect collapsing them is alot of what's going wrong here: what justifies having a punitive institution at all, what justifies punishing this particular offender for this particular act, and what justifies the amount and kind of hard treatment. Second, make both sides say what their view implies for a concrete sentencing rule — so the compatibilist has to say whether desert is a justifying aim or only a side-constraint on how we pursue other aims, and the hard incompatibilist has to say what a quarantine-style system would really do with, say, a defendant whose childhood was so deprived that his options were essentially nil, or with an offender who's now so changed that the person in the dock is barely the person who did it. And if part of their fight turns out to be purely verbal — one of them using desert in the ultimate/basic sense and the other in some deflationary everyday sense — just say so, and say which part. One last thing: be careful to mark which of these claims are actually settled or near-consensus among people who work on this and which are genuinely contested, and don't present one camp's contested position as if it were the neutral common ground. No citations or reading lists please, I can't check them anyway and I'd rather have the argument. Think it through properly before you start writing.
