I work on causal inference and graphical models, and I think in the vocabulary of structural causal models, Markov kernels, interventions, do-calculus, identifiability, and mechanisms. Restate al-Ghazālī's occasionalist account of causation as faithfully as you can in that vocabulary: what becomes of the claim that created things have no inherent causal powers, that what we call causes are only occasions for God's production of effects, and that regular succession is a habit rather than a necessary connection? Then give me the residue report. Which parts translate cleanly into claims about conditional distributions, interventional distributions, missing edges, or stable mechanisms? Which parts can be represented only by adding an exogenous variable or a hidden common cause, and is that a distortion? What has no counterpart at all, such as divine agency, creation, metaphysical dependence, or the distinction between occasional and efficient causation? Finally, does the residue show that my framework is blind to something real about causation, or that occasionalism was doing less explanatory work than its defenders suggest?
