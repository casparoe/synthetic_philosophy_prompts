Write the joint statement that a committed multiverse realist and a committed empiricist critic of non-empirical confirmation could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on string theory and the multiverse. The realist holds that the string landscape -- the vast space of possible vacua string theory seems to allow -- together with eternal inflation can be confirmed by non-empirical virtues such as explanatory coherence, the absence of plausible alternatives, and unification; the critic holds that nothing short of a risky empirical prediction confirms a physical theory. I want the claims both accept about what confirmation is, what the multiverse hypothesis asserts, what the measure problem (the difficulty of assigning probabilities across eternally inflating regions or vacua) does to its predictive content, and what the Boltzmann-brain problem (the worry that typical observers would be freak fluctuations rather than evolved beings) shows; then the precise residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording. Identify the cruxes, including whether the landscape is a prediction or a post hoc accommodation, whether explanatory economy can ever be evidence, and whether the dispute is partly verbal because the two sides mean different things by confirmation. For each crux, give a test, argument, or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Format as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
