I've been doing family history research for about four years now — Ancestry, the free census stuff, county records, the whole slog — and I've gotten back to the point where every generation gets thinner: a name in a parish register, a maybe, a story somebody wrote down in 1970. My brother-in-law is a philosophy grad student, and over Thanksgiving he told me philosophers have an old argument about exactly that, the regress problem, and that there are three answers to it. Foundationalists: the chain has to bottom out somewhere in something that doesn't itself need backing, like a first couple who are just there on the record with nothing behind them. Coherentists: forget about a bottom, the whole thing holds together as a web, branches loop back and support each other. Infinitists: it just goes back forever, no first ancestor, and that's not a problem, that's the answer. Is that sketch even right?
