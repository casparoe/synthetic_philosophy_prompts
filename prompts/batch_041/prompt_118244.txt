im putting together the last session of a night class on dream arguments — we do descartes' demon, then the zhuangzi butterfly bit, and i want to close with a poem instead of another handout. can you write me one? around 16 lines, free verse is fine, but with a hard constraint: two halves of eight lines each, and the second half has to mirror the first line by line (line 9 answers line 1, line 10 answers line 2, and so on). the first half is the man who dreamed he was a butterfly; the second half is the butterfly who dreamed it was a man. every line has to be speakable by either side — no giveaways like wings or hands or waking up in a bed — so the reader cant tell which voice is which until the swap makes them go back and reread. the last line has to be the pivot: a line that closes the poem and that both readings can hold at once, with neither winning.

it should land on the zhuangzi move rather than the descartes one. descartes wants a way out of the doubt; zhuangzi just says there must be some distinction between the two and then calls the whole thing the transformation of things, which is not a proof and not a shrug, its a shift in what you take yourself to be. so the ending should feel like the right place to stop, not like a dodge. also keep it specific to the two-way swap — no generic life-is-a-dream stuff, no all-is-illusion stuff. if it helps you can bring in the bit about how you only call a dream a dream after youve woken up, and how the same move can be made about being awake, but dont let that become the whole poem. no citations or anything, just the poem and maybe a short note on which line you think is doing the pivoting.
