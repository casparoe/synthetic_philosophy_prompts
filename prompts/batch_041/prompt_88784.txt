So I've been trying to wrap my head around Bradley's regress and the whole unity of the proposition business, and I think I follow the basic move: take a fact where a bears relation R to b. Something has to hold a, R, and b together so you get one fact instead of just a heap of three separate things. But if you say a further relation is what does the holding, then that relation itself has to be held to a, R, and b by yet another relation, and off it goes forever. Okay, fine, I see the infinite regress.

Here's where I get stuck though. Doesn't this actually show that the pieces of any proposition can never be genuinely joined up? Like, at every stage there's still something left over that hasn't been attached, so nothing ever becomes one single proposition, which would mean there aren't really any propositions at all — or at least none that are unified, and so none that could be true. But that's obviously wrong, since we think true thoughts constantly. Someone told me the fix is that the relation or concept is "unsaturated" and just grabs its terms, but that seems to me like renaming the problem instead of solving it. So how do people who believe in propositions actually get out of this? Do they just declare a stopping point and hope nobody asks why that's allowed? And if the regress really does rule out unity, why isn't everyone a proposition-nihilist? I feel like I'm either misreading the regress or misunderstanding what a proposition is suposed to be in the first place. Set me straight, and please answer in American English.
