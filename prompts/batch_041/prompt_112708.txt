I am a doctoral student in science and technology policy, and I have been trying to pin down a pattern that keeps surfacing in seminars on emerging technologies, though I suspect it is more general than technology. A group of actors—labs, states, firms, even a family—would each prefer that no one take a certain irreversible step, at least not yet. The step is not obviously wrong; one actor taking it might even be beneficial. But if several take it, or if it is taken prematurely, the result is worse for everyone than coordinated restraint. Each actor then reasons: I would rather wait, but if I wait, someone else may act—someone with worse judgment, less caution, or a stronger incentive—and then the outcome I feared will happen anyway, except that I will have lost the chance to shape it. So each has a reason to move first, and the most cautious actors may be the ones most motivated to move first, precisely because they fear what a less cautious actor would do. A concrete version: a lab has a protocol that would make a pathogen more transmissible, would prefer a global moratorium, but believes that if it does not publish, a less careful lab will, and with worse results, so it publishes with a justification. A domestic version: a group of friends all prefer not to raise a painful subject at dinner, but one of them fears another will raise it clumsily, so she raises it first, and the thing everyone wanted avoided happens anyway.

I have seen this compared to a tragedy of the commons and to a prisoner's dilemma, but neither seems right: there is no depleting resource, and the actors are not choosing between cooperation and defection in a single matrix so much as trying to pre-empt one another under uncertainty about who will move. Is there an established name for this configuration? If there is no name, or if the nearest names capture only part of it, I would like a careful carving: what distinguishes it from the tragedy of the commons, the prisoner's dilemma, a race to the bottom, and a plain arms race; what work uncertainty about others' intentions is doing; and whether the "most cautious move first" variant is the same phenomenon or a distinct one. I am not after a bare label—I want to know where the boundaries of the concept lie, which cases fall outside it, and what the concept would predict about a case where the actors are all equally cautious and all know it.
