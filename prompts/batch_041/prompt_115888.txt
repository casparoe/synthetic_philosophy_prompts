Ok, so I'm a novelist — historical fiction, mostly — and I'm in the middle of a book where one of my main characters is a professional gambler, which has sent me down a rabbit hole on betting and risk that I did not expect to enjoy this much. The trouble is that the more I read, the more I suspect my own views on this stuff don't actually fit together, and I don't have the math to tell whether I'm genuinely confused or whether the views themselves are the problem. So I'd like you to audit the package rather than teach me the topic. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) For anything money-like and repeatable — bets I'll face over and over, portfolio stuff, that kind of thing — I size by Kelly: bet the fraction of my bankroll equal to edge over odds, maximize expected log wealth, all that.

(2) I don't think the reason for (1) is that dollars have diminishing marginal utility in some mushy everyday sense. I think the real reason is the non-ergodicity argument — the thing people like Ole Peters push — where for a multiplicative process the time-average growth rate and the ensemble average are just different objects, and it's the time average you should care about. So on my view expected-value reasoning isn't merely risk-flavored, its actually the wrong tool for those processes.

(3) At the same time I'm fairly confident that expected utility maximization is the correct general theory of rational choice — that once you've fixed a utility function, maximizing its expectation is just what it is to be rational, and everything we call risk aversion lives in the shape of that function and nowhere else.

(4) But for one-shot, non-fungible stakes — whether to move cities for a person, whether to have a kid, whether to take a job that could very plausibly kill me — I think the Kelly/log stuff is a category error, and you should just take whatever has the higher expected utility, even when it carries a real chance of blowing up your life.

(5) And I think my "bankroll" should be cashed out broadly — not just dollars but my time, my health, my ability to keep writing — which makes me think Kelly-style reasoning should apply to a much wider class of choices than people usually apply it to.

I suspect some of these are in real tension and some only look like they are, and honestly I can't tell which is which. Could you go through it properly: which pairs actually conflict, how badly, and for each real conflict what the cheapest fix is and what I'd be giving up by taking it. If any of it turns out to be a purely verbal dispute I'd love to hear that too, because from where I'm sitting it all feels equally shaky. And please don't just reassure me that they're all compatible — if something has to go, tell me which one and why.
