Compare and evaluate two relativistic arguments against the objectivity of a global, flowing present: Kurt Gödel’s argument from his rotating-universe solution to the Einstein field equations, and the argument commonly associated with C. W. Rietdijk and Hilary Putnam from the relativity of simultaneity in special relativity. In your comparison, address the following questions. What precisely does each argument purport to show — that there is no unique global present, that temporal becoming is not an objective feature of the world, or that time is ideal in a stronger, mind-dependent sense? Gödel’s solution describes a homogeneous, rotating dust universe with a negative cosmological constant that contains closed timelike curves and admits no global time function; does the philosophical conclusion depend on the physical plausibility of that particular model, or can the conclusion be detached from it and supported by more generic features of general relativity? The Rietdijk–Putnam argument moves from the frame-relativity of simultaneity to the equal reality of all events; does this inference rely on contestable assumptions about transitivity or about the relation between simultaneity and reality, and are those assumptions defensible? Finally, consider an objection that both arguments conflate the absence of a global time function or a unique global present with the absence of objective temporal passage, and that passage might instead be a local or perspectival phenomenon that survives the relativity of simultaneity and the existence of closed timelike curves. Which of the two arguments, if either, withstands this objection, and which conception of time — Gödelian idealism, the block universe, or a local/perspectival account of passage — is best supported? Reach a definite verdict and justify it.
