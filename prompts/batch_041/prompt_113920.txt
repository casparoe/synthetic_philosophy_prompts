I belong to a small astrobiology reading group whose convenor has spent the term taking up proposed resolutions of the Fermi paradox one at a time and ruling on which of them she is willing to count as doing genuine explanatory work. Her record so far runs roughly like this. She counts as respectable, though in need of sharpening, the proposal that the emergence of technological intelligence requires a long chain of individually improbable transitions; she counts as respectable the proposal that we are simply early, in the sense that the universe is young relative to the timescales involved and that most star formation still lies ahead of us; she counts as respectable the proposal that worlds like ours are rare because of a conjunction of geological, orbital, and magnetic requirements. She rejects the proposal that civilizations are common but conceal themselves out of fear of predators; she rejects the proposal that a galactic club or a quarantine arrangement excludes us; she rejects the proposal that civilizations communicate by means our instruments are not built to register; she rejects the proposal that advanced civilizations deliberately run at low clock speeds in order to husband free energy against the far future; and she accepts, as a framing rather than as a resolution, the point that the silence constrains the product of civilization density and expansion speed rather than the existence of civilizations. Her stated grounds shift from case to case — now unfalsifiability, now the demand that every civilization independently arrive at the same choice, now the suspicion that a proposal is doing nothing except restating the datum — and I have never been able to get her to say which of these is the real test.

Next week the paper is one I have to present. It argues that the galaxy is in fact full of advanced civilizations and that they are invisible because any civilization reaching a certain level of capability converges on building enormous energy-harvesting structures, reprocessing and re-radiating its waste heat at long wavelengths and ceasing to leak radio, so that the signature to look for is a mid-infrared excess around old stellar populations rather than a signal in a radio band. What I want from you is not a verdict on whether that is true. I want to know what she will say about it; which rule or rules her record is jointly consistent with, since I am fairly sure that more than one will fit; what each of those rules predicts about the megastructure paper; which reading of her you would bet on and on what grounds; and finally, the case I should put to her the week after next, chosen so that it separates the rules that fit her record from one another, with an account of why that case separates them and the others do not. If her record turns out to be genuinely underdetermined with respect to the new paper, tell me what principle she would be committing herself to under each verdict, and how much of her apparent consistency is an artifact of the proposals she has happened to be shown.
