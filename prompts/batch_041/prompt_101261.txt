I am a control engineer by training, comfortable with the standard machinery of dynamical systems: state and trajectory, flows and vector fields, conserved quantities and invariants, reachable sets, open-loop versus closed-loop control, setpoints and feedback, controllability, and the distinction between a constraint the dynamics happen to respect and one that must be enforced by an external input. I have no formal instruction in philosophy. I would like you to take a position I know only in outline and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use.

The position is Robert Nozick's entitlement theory of justice and the argument he builds around Wilt Chamberlain. In outline as I understand it: a distribution of holdings is just if and only if it came about by just original acquisition, then by just transfers, with rectification of any past injustice where it occurred; the theory is meant to be historical, judging a distribution by how it arose rather than by how it looks now, in contrast to end-state or patterned principles that evaluate holdings against some current configuration (equality, or distribution by need, desert, or effort). The Chamberlain case is his test of patterned principles: begin from a distribution you already grant to be just, suppose a million people each voluntarily pay a quarter to watch Chamberlain play, and he ends up with a quarter of a million dollars more than anyone else. The resulting distribution violates the equality pattern, yet every step from the just starting point was a willing transfer. Nozick takes this to show that liberty upsets patterns, and that anyone committed to holding a pattern fixed must be prepared to interfere continuously with people's voluntary choices.

Please translate this into my framework, piece by piece. Treat acquisition and transfer as generating a trajectory through a space of holdings; treat the entitlement principle as a path-dependent property of that trajectory rather than a property of the current state alone; treat patterned and end-state principles as a setpoint or an invariant imposed on the state; and treat the Chamberlain argument as the claim that the free-exchange flow does not preserve that invariant, so that holding it fixed requires a closed-loop controller acting at every step rather than a one-time correction. Keep the translation honest: do not quietly import the assumptions of my framework and present them as if they were Nozick's.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I care about most. For each element, say what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. In particular I want your judgment on three things. First, Nozick seems to be doing normative work with the idea that a trajectory itself confers a claim — that coming about the right way makes a holding just — and I am not sure whether any control-theoretic notion (reachability, path constraints, integral cost) captures that or merely resembles it. Second, his argument leans on a transfer being voluntary, and my framework has no notion of voluntariness; an external input is just an input. Third, my framework is neutral between open- and closed-loop in a way that may be telling: a controller is simply another dynamical system, morally on a par with the plant, so within my vocabulary the claim that liberty upsets patterns threatens to collapse into a truism about non-invariance, carrying no evaluative weight. Tell me whether that collapse shows the argument has less content than it appears to, or whether it shows my vocabulary is missing something real — a distinction between an agent acting on their own holdings and an intervention imposed on them.

Close by telling me what would change your verdict: which consideration or argument, if it turned out one way rather than another, would lead you to a different residue report, and where your translation is most likely to have failed.
