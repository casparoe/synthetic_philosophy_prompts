I sit on the board of a small housing co-op and i've started noticing a shape that keeps recurring, and I can't find a name for it. Our rule for who counts as a member is basically 'you live here and you pay the costs', and that's what makes it us deciding who moves in rather than some outside body. Then a member pointed out that if we honestly applied that same rule to ourselves, the two basement renters and the live-in carer would get votes, and two of our own people who've been abroad for three years and pay nothing would lose theirs. Nobody wanted either result, so we quietly settled on 'whoever was already in.' Then I watched my union local do the identical thing while voting on whether to fold in a non-union shop: same principled-sounding membership rule, same discovery that applying it straight either lets the newcomers swamp the vote or ejects some of our own, same retreat to 'whoever's already in.' What gets me is that it doesn't feel like ordinary hypocrisy or gerrymandering, since nobody's breaking a stated rule -- the rule just can't be turned on the group that's using it, and there's no body above us to say otherwise, because deciding who we are is the one thing only we get to decide. The closest thing I've found online is the general puzzle about who gets to decide who belongs in the first place, but that seems to be about starting from nothing; this is what happens to a group that's already been running a while and has to keep re-deciding. Is there a name for this, and if not, coin one and tell me where its edges are -- what separates it from plain self-dealing, and what stays the same and what changes when the group is a co-op, a union, or a country. A bare label won't help me, I want the distinction. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning -- no preamble and don't restate my question.
