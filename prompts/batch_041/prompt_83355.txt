Bit of an odd one. There's a pattern I keep running into and I don't know if it has a proper name — "thinking outside the box" is way too vague for what I mean, so don't just tell me that.

Two times it's happened lately. First: a friend handed me one of those puzzles where you start with a string of symbols, there's a handful of rules for rewriting it, and you're supposed to reach some target string. I ground away at it all evening, trying longer and longer sequences of the rules, got nowhere. What actually cracked it wasn't a cleverer sequence. It was noticing that every string you can possibly produce with those rules has a certain property, that none of the rules ever mention that property — they just happen to preserve it — and that the target string doesn't have it. So the real answer was "you can't get there", and the reason for it lived entirely outside the rules. It was a fact about the rules, not a move in them.

Second time, less cute. I was debugging some code and kept patching the logic, and every patch broke something else. What fixed it was realising the design assumed something that simply isn't true of the data, so no amount of patching the logic was ever going to work. Again the escape wasn't a better move inside the game; it was stepping out and asking what the game can even say.

But here's the part I actually care about. Since I started noticing this, I see people *pretending* to do it. Someone's stuck, they announce they're "stepping outside the system", and then what they actually do is bolt one more rule onto the system and keep playing — which is just a bigger version of the same thing. Or they relabel everything in fancier words and call that a leap. And it's genuinely hard to tell the difference from the outside, because a real one and a fake one both leave the original problem looking different afterwards.

So: is there an established name for the real move — where the thing that unblocks you is a fact *about* the rules that the rules themselves can't state? And is there any principled way to tell a real one from a fake one? My guess is something like "it's real if it tells you that something can't be done, and fake if it just relocates the stuckness" — but I suspect that's too neat and only covers half the cases. I'd also like to know what the nearest existing ideas are, because I've got a feeling that "meta-level reasoning", "adding an auxiliary assumption" and "thinking outside the box" are all circling nearby without being the thing itself. A bare name definately won't satisfy me — I want to know where the edges are, and what it is *not*.
