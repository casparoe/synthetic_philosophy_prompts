Rank the pramāṇas that figure in classical Indian epistemology — perception, inference, comparison, verbal testimony, postulation, and non-apprehension — into tiers by how much independent epistemic work each one does: that is, how much genuine knowledge (pramā) an otherwise competent agent would lose if that pramāṇa alone were unavailable. Give a sentence for each explaining why it lands where it does, and state plainly which placements turn on a contested reduction — for instance, whether postulation collapses into inference, or non-apprehension into perception plus inference — ranking under the assumption that the reduction fails. Then re-rank the same six by how much of an infallible oracle's output each could certify as knowledge rather than mere true belief, given Nyāya's account of what makes a cognition a pramā, and note where the two orderings come apart.
