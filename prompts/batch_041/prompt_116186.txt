I run a fornightly formal epistemology reading group and we're doing a session on uncertain evidence — the case where you shift your credences without learning anything for certain (Jeffrey's candlelit cloth, and the update rule we all just call Jeffrey conditonalization). I'd like a decision tree the group can work through together: start from the bare commitment that some update has happened, and end at a named leaf. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names — nodes are questions, leaves are the positions that survive them, and 'unnamed' wherever a combination nobody defends is sitting there. Note briefly why each empty slot is empty: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Make sure these come out as leaves somewhere: (1) the view that there's no genuinely uncertain evidence, because you're always certain of *something* — your experience, your total observation, how things seem — and plain conditionalization does the rest; (2) Jeffrey conditionalization proper, where the conditional probabilities on the partition stay put (rigidity) and only the marginals move; (3) the friendly amendment that says the real rule is just 'move as little as you can from your prior, subject to whatever constraint the evidence imposes', so rigidity is the special case where the constraint is a fresh set of marginals; (4) the pessimist leaf, on which no rule is uniquely justified — the partition you choose changes the answer and successive updates needn't commute, so the update is underdetermined.

Nodes I want on the tree, because they're what actually splits people: whether you can always find a proposition you learn with certainty, and if so whether the certainty has to be about your phenomenal state rather than the world; whether the evidence constrains a marginal on a partition or something else entirely (a likelihood, a fact about your own reliability); whether rigidity is a substantive claim about your conditional credences or just the definition of the right partition — this looks circular to me, and I want the tree to force a choice; and whether the rule needs a diachronic Dutch-book or expected-accuracy justification, or is simply constitutive of having credences.

Use these at the relevant nodes: a blurry photo of a friend that moves you to 0.9 but tells you nothing for sure; a thermometer reading where what you learn is the error distribution rather than a marginal; and a case where you learn your colour vision in this light is worse than you assumed, so your conditional on 'it looks greenish' seems like it ought to move. Where two leaves can't be separated by any of these, say so instead of papering over it. 7–10 leaves, each a name plus one line on its most awkward commitment. And tell me how confident you are in the overall shape of the tree, and which single placement you're least sure of.
