Bit of an odd ask — I cover tech and ideas for a news outlet, and I've been circling the whole "when should an assistant refuse you" story for about a year now, and at this point I honestly can't tell which of my positions are load-bearing and which are just vibes. Before I file I want to know if my stuff hangs together.

Here's the package as I'd defend it:

1. Anti-paternalism, pretty strictly. An assistant shouldn't withhold help just because the thing you're asking for is bad for you. Protecting a competent adult from their own choices isn't a good enough reason to refuse — that's the whole ballgame for me.

2. Two cases where I'd want it to refuse anyway: if someone's clearly asking for help ending their life, or asking for a step-by-step way to keep starving themselves. And yet the same week I'd want it to help a hunger striker plan a long fast for a political protest, and even help them draft the op-ed explaining it. Same act, different reason. Yes, I know that one's in tension with 1, that's why I'm asking.

3. When it does refuse, it should say why in plain language. Not a canned "I can't help with that." If you're not going to help me, you owe me the actual reason.

4. It shouldn't enforce contested values. Where reasonable people disagree — meat, gambling, affairs, taking a job with a weapons contractor, whether to have kids — the tool should help you do what you've already decided, not nudge you toward what its maker thinks is right.

5. I floated this in a column and got yelled at: a one-time speed bump on irreversable requests. Not a refusal — one screen saying here's what changes if you go ahead, then it helps you if you still want it.

6. If it's legal for a person to help me with, and I'm not doing anything wrong, then the fact that someone else might misuse the same capability is not a reason to refuse me.

7. I own my conversations and should be able to export or delete them.

The replies always go straight to 1 vs 2 and call it a straight contradiction. Maybe so, but I want the full audit, not just the obvious one. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. Then tell me the single revision that cleans up the most, even if it isn't the worst one on its own.
