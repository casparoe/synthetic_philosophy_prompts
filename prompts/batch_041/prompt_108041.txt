I am putting together a handout for a graduate reading group on AI alignment, for a session on low-impact agents, and I would like you to build me a decision tree. The tree should take a participant from the starting commitment that a sufficiently capable optimiser will tend to alter the world in ways its designers did not intend, and route them to a position on the question of whether avoiding side effects can be given a determinate definition without a full specification of the values the agent ought to serve. Build it out of the logical space rather than out of a list of proposals: first work out which questions genuinely divide the possible answers, then say which positions, named or unnamed, occupy which leaves. The distinctions I care most about are these. Whether a side effect is relative to something — a plan, an objective, a baseline trajectory — or is treated as an absolute property of an action. Whether the reference point is inaction, the trajectory the principal intended, or the world as it stands at the moment of decision. Whether impact is measured as deviation in the physical state of the world or as reduction in the agent's own capacity to pursue its objectives afterwards. Whether the measure needs the true value function to be computed or only the agent's objective, so that impact is objective-relative but value-free. Whether the constraint is hard, imposed on the policy, or soft, folded into the objective as a penalty. Whether the agent's own later corrective action counts, so that an intervention is low-impact if it can be undone. Whether effects on the agent's own future options, including changes to its own hardware, count as side effects. And whether the defender takes low-impact measures to be worth having in their own right or only as a convergent proxy for something else, such as corrigibility or not disempowering the humans.

Give me roughly eight to twelve leaves, and make sure the tree can at least locate these four: a view on which impact is definable relative to the agent's objective without any value specification; a view on which it requires the full value specification, because a side effect is just any effect outside the intention; a view on which the notion is empty once one notices that every consequence is an effect of something; and a view on which low-impact is a useful heuristic for training or for building corrigible systems but not a definition at all. Each leaf should carry the name of the position if it has one, or be marked unnamed if not, plus a single sentence on that position's most awkward commitment. Where two of the distinctions I listed cannot be pulled apart by any question in your tree, say so explicitly and say what additional question would separate them. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, note it and say briefly whether that is because the position is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. I do not want citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; if you want to invoke a particular proposal, describe it rather than pointing me at a paper. Write the whole thing in continuous prose, the way you would explain it in an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — and make the routing explicit in words, saying for each question which answer leads to which next question and which answers terminate at a leaf.
