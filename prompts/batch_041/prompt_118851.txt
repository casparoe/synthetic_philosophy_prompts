ok so I'm in a seminar on hedonism right now and we spent a whole week on the heterogeneity problem — the worry that pleasures maybe don't share any single common feature (the pleasure of a hot shower vs. the pleasure of finally getting a proof vs. the pleasure of being petty and right), and the same for pains. I have to write a paper tying it to something real, and I want to do clinical pain measurement, but I'm not sure I'm seeing the philosophy in it properly.

Here's the cluster of practices I'm staring at. In basically every hospital now pain gets treated as a fifth vital sign: the nurse asks you to rate it 0 to 10, sometimes with the little faces chart, and that single number goes in the chart next to your blood pressure. Same scale for a burn, a migraine, a broken bone, period cramps, the ache after a breakup. Then health economists take numbers like that and fold them into QALYs and DALYs, so my toothache is supposedly commensurable with someone else's depression and we can trade them off. Meanwhile in animal research there are apparently standard scoring sheets where you don't ask the animal anything at all — you score ear position, whisker angle, how squinted its eyes are — and that spits out a pain number too. But for mental distress we do the opposite: separate questionnaires for depression and anxiety, no universal 0-10 sadness scale, nobody trading off "my anxiety is a 7" against "your grief is a 4."

So what does this whole thing presuppose about pleasure and pain? Does it need a common currency — that all pains share one thing (intensity? unpleasantness? badness?) that the number tracks and that's the same thing across kinds? Or can you run all these practices while being a full heterogeneity person and just treating the number as a rough proxy for "how much do I want this to stop"? I'd like to know which bits genuinely require the common-currency view and which merely fit well with it, and where the practice is underdetermined so several theories would license it. Also: is the animal behavior-scoring thing presupposing the same thing the human self-report is, or a totally different theory of what the state even is? And if heterogeneity is true, what actually has to change — multidimensional scales, no interpersonal aggregation, something else?
