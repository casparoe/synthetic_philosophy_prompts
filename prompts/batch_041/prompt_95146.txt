I'm in the third year of a doctorate and have spent most of the last year not writing it, and the problem has changed shape for me: it no longer looks like laziness, and I'd like your advice both on the practical question and on whether I've even got the diagnosis right. Here is the pattern. Every Sunday I lay out the week with real conviction — I believe the plan, I can say why each item matters, and I don't think I'm fooling myself about the difficulty. Most mornings I then lie in bed at 5:40 with the alarm going, and what I notice is that I don't experience the moment as a decision at all. I don't think "the plan was wrong"; I think "not now," and the plan feels like something a previous occupant of my body arranged. It's the same with money: I've saved almost nothing by resolving to transfer a little each month, but I've saved a decent amount through an account that sweeps the money out before I see it, and I can't honestly say I feel the difference as a difference in virtue. I never come to believe the goal was a bad one. I just don't do it.

What complicates it is that I can argue both sides of my own case. A friend of mine, who reads a lot of philosophy and is a bit of a menace, says that my future self is a different person, and that present-me has no more reason to sacrifice for him than for a stranger on the street — and I can't tell whether that's a serious thought or a very sophisticated excuse, especially since I notice that the person who benefits from it is always the one making the argument. I also put a website blocker on my laptop last term, and when I found a way around it I didn't feel like I'd cheated; I felt like I'd corrected an injustice, that some earlier version of me had no business legislating for me. Meanwhile a friend who runs marathons says, without cruelty, that I'd do the work if I really wanted it, and while that seems obviously false, I can't produce the argument against it. And there's a temporal asymmetry I can't shake: I'm happy to say my past self should have started earlier, but when I weigh an hour of work now against an hour of relief now, the work seems to be for someone I don't know, and I notice I can construct reasons on both sides depending on when you ask me.

So: what should I do about the dissertation, concretely — build more fences, try to change what I want, or accept that my stated priorities are simply not my real ones? And underneath that, which description of me is correct: one agent whose will is divided, a series of agents with overlapping but competing interests, or one agent whose actions are the truth and whose plans are the fiction? For whichever picture you favor, tell me what it implies about whether it is rational to bind my future self, and whether the fact that I can always find a reason to defect is evidence about my reasons or only about my rhetoric.
