I am a high school student and the host of a small podcast for our philosophy club. Next week’s guest is a philosopher who defends an occasionalist view of causation, roughly the position that God is the only genuine cause and that what we ordinarily call natural causes are merely occasions on which God produces the effect. I would like to ask eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having the guest restate the position. Please include at least one question on each of the following: the difference between an occasion and a cause, and whether occasionalism can distinguish its view from the claim that events are merely constantly conjoined; the problem of explaining why God produces the same effects on the same occasions if God is free and omnipotent; how occasionalism should be understood in relation to the natural sciences, especially whether it makes scientific explanation vacuous or merely reinterprets laws as descriptions of divine regularity; the problem of human agency and moral responsibility if God is the only cause; and the problem of evil if God is the cause of all events, including suffering. For each main question, please add a follow-up that is keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give, and make the sequence build so that the standard replies become progressively harder to use. The questions should press on the actual content of the position rather than asking for a summary, and they should not request reading lists, bibliographies, or citations.
