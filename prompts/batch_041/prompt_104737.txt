Take the Milgram obedience experiments and read them through Sartre's radical freedom and bad faith. The setup is familiar -- ordinary people in a lab, a guy in a coat telling them to keep going, the shocks ramping up, most of them going all the way while saying things like they can't stop, it's not their fault, they were told to. Sartre's whole thing is that this is exactly bad faith: you're free, you can't not be free, and "I had no choice" is the lie you tell yourself to dodge the anguish. So give me a real case analysis, both directions. Map it: who's actually free here, what's the facticity of the situation (the lab, the authority, the pressure) versus what's the free projection, and what does "I had to continue" look like as mauvaise foi. Then be honest about where the Sartrean reading strains -- does the experimenter and the escalating-prods structure describe something his vocabulary for facticity can't quite absorb? And flip it: does Milgram confirm Sartre (people really are free and just invent excuses) or is it closer to an anomaly that suggests the situation does most of the work and "radical freedom" is the thing misdescribing what happened? Don't hedge or just lay out both sides -- tell me which way you think it cuts and why.
