I've been trying to write down my position on the physical, design, and intentional stances, and I want a consistency check before I try to defend it. Here is what I hold.

(1) The physical facts fix what there is; anything else we say is true must be true in virtue of them.

(2) A pattern is real when it is a compressible description that supports better-than-chance prediction, and its reality is objective — a matter of compression and predictive gain, not of our convenience or interests.

(3) The intentional stance is a stance: to interpret a system as a believer is to adopt an interpretation that makes it rational, and there is no further fact of the matter about what it really believes beyond the pattern that interpretation picks out.

(4) Given (2), the beliefs and desires posited from the intentional stance can be perfectly real.

(5) A thermostat, or any system whose behaviour the design stance already captures, has no genuine intentional states.

(6) I am a physicalist, but I take consciousness to be a genuinely harder problem than intentionality rather than a further application of the same method.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look as though they are, and how badly? Rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad; for each, say what would have to give and what that would cost; and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
