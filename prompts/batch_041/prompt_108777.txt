So I've been going down a rabbit hole on A.J. Ayer, the logical positavist who thought claims about life after death were either meaningless or at least couldn't be verified. The thing that got me is that late in life he had a near-death experience -- his heart stopped, he was out for a few minutes, and he later described seeing a red light and some kind of beings, and said it left him less sure that death is annihilation, though he still didn't think it proved survival. I don't want citations or a reading list. What I want is a careful analysis of that episode through the lens of his own verificationist theory. By his own standard, what would even count as evidence for or against survival after death? Does his own experience meet that bar, or does it fail because it's private, unrepeatable, and only he had it? And does it matter that he's the one making the theory -- is there alot of tension when the verificationist has an experience that seems to point at the very thing he said couldn't be meaningfully asserted? Then flip it: what does the episode show about the verificationist approach to life after death? Does it confirm the approach, embarrass it, or show that the approach was never really equipped to deal with experiences like his? I want the mapping to be disciplined -- what exactly is the claim being tested, what would count as a test, and where does the theory just go silent -- not just a summary of what Ayer believed.
