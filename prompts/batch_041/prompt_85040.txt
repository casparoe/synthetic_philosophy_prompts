Please describe the most important implications of the following answer to the special composition question, which may be called the self-maintenance view: a plurality of objects composes a further object just in case its members stand in a closed loop of mutual causation that sustains a joint capacity to resist dissolution and to repair perturbations to that capacity. In particular, what does the self-maintenance view imply about (a) whether artifacts such as ships and tables exist as composite objects, (b) whether organisms have sharp spatial and temporal boundaries, and (c) whether social entities such as committees, firms, or states count as composite objects? Focus on the implications that matter most for persistence, part-whole causation, and the special status often assigned to biological individuals.
