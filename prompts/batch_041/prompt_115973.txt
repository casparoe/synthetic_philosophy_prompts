I am preparing a two-page proposal for a twelve-month research fellowship in political theory, and I would like you to draft it. The subject is the boundary problem: the question of who belongs to the demos, and by what right. My half-formed idea is that the debate has run together two questions that ought to be kept apart. The first is the inclusion question: by what principle should the boundaries of a demos be drawn — all affected interests, all those subjected to its coercion, all who hold citizenship, or something else again. The second is the constitution question: who is entitled to decide where the boundary falls, given that any procedure for deciding already presupposes a bounded set of participants. My suspicion, which I have not yet tested, is that once the two are separated the familiar charge that the second question is viciously circular loses much of its force — but only if one distinguishes two senses of legitimacy, a procedural one and a justificatory one, and says clearly which of them a boundary decision is supposed to enjoy. I am not confident the distinction survives contact with the hard cases.

Please turn this into a proposal. It should state the guiding question so that it has a determinate answer rather than being an occasion for surveying views; explain why the existing positions in the debate leave that question open; set out the method — what I would actually do, in what order over the twelve months, and how the labour divides between conceptual analysis and the examination of cases; and say what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing. Keep it tractable by anchoring the argument in a small number of genuinely contested cases, such as a secession referendum, diaspora voting rights, and the admission of new members to an existing demos, without letting the proposal drift into a policy memorandum. Include a short and candid paragraph on the strongest reason to think the whole approach is misconceived, and what I would say in reply. I will supply the citations myself, so refer to positions by name where that is useful, but do not give me a bibliography or a reading list.
