ok so i'm in a phil of physics reading group and we've spent like two months on the measurement problem, mostly on Everett. for the last session i said i'd bring a poem to close us out, since the last hour always turns into everyone re-litigating the same thing anyway. i've been trying to write it myself and it keeps comming out as an essay, so i'm outsourcing.

what i want: a poem about the Born rule problem in many-worlds. not an explainer dressed up as verse — a poem. the vertigo of it.

here's the shape i want, and the shape is the point: i want it circular. first line and last line identical, word for word, so you can't tell whether the poem has ended or just started over. because thats honestly the feeling. unitary evolution is perfectly deterministic, the schrodinger equation never hands you a probability, and then the whole decision-theoretic industry (Deutsch, Wallace, that crowd) goes and derives the Born weights from axioms about what a rational agent prefers — and half the field says wait, preference is already a probabilistic notion, you've assumed the thing you're proving. so a poem that loops is a poem about that. it should read as an argument that keeps restarting, not as a gotcha.

the one technical hinge i want in there: the word "measure" should be doing one job in the first line and a different job in the last. measure as the thing you take (a measure on branches, the born weights), measure as the thing you do. the line stays the same, so the shift has to come from whats in between. dont spell it out, just let the word pivot. and somewhere in the middle i want the failure of branch-counting to show up — the fact that you cant just count branches because "how many branches" isn't a well-defined question, it depends on how finely you carve the world up, and the carving is your choice, not the physics. but dont drop it in like a footnote, make it feel like the ground going soft.

length: 14-20 lines. no rhyme scheme, no meter required, but tight enough to read aloud at the end of a seminar, like 90 seconds. it can be in the voice of someone who accepts Everett and still wakes up at 3am about the probability thing. i dont want a smug poem and i dont want a debunking poem. leave the tension open. someone in the group who knows the literature should wince with recognition; someone who's just visiting should get the chills anyway.
