Please audit the following package of anthropic views for consistency. I want to know which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and for each genuine tension what the least costly revision is and what it costs. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and do not treat every difference as a conflict.

(1) I accept the self-sampling assumption (SSA): in self-locating uncertainty I should reason as if I am a random observer drawn from my reference class.
(2) I reject the self-indication assumption (SIA): a hypothesis is not made more likely merely by entailing that more observers exist.
(3) I regard the presumptuous philosopher as a decisive objection to SIA: if SIA were true, a theory on which the cosmos contains vastly more observers would be overwhelmingly confirmed by ordinary evidence, which is absurd.
(4) I think the Doomsday argument is sound in outline: learning that I am among the first N humans should shift my credence toward early human extinction.
(5) I think the correct reference class for SSA is not all humans or all biological observers but the evidence-bounded class: all observer-moments that share my current evidence.
(6) I think the fact that I find myself human rather than a Boltzmann brain—a spontaneously assembled brain whose apparent memories and evidence match mine—is strong evidence against Boltzmann-brain scenarios.
(7) I think we should not accept any anthropic principle that turns the size of the cosmos into a free evidential parameter.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the genuine tensions by severity. End with a final line beginning Verdict: followed by the name of the single commitment most in need of revision.
