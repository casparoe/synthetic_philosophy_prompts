I am a doctoral student in a decision theory reading group, and a friend who runs a small forecasting nonprofit has asked me to help her think through a design problem. The nonprofit rewards forecasters with a proper scoring rule, allocates resources across projects using a portfolio approach, and permits teams to revise their preferences over time as evidence comes in; it has twelve employees and a board that wants procedures that can be explained to non-specialists. My friend has absorbed enough of the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization to know that preferences violating the standard axioms leave an agent open to a sequence of choices that is worse by the agent's own lights, but she is unsure what follows for an organization whose members are not idealized expected utility maximizers and whose decisions are made collectively. A further complication, which we have been calling budgeted incoherence, is that each team's local preferences may satisfy the axioms while the organization's aggregate preference over portfolios cycles because teams weight the same outcomes differently; the nonprofit also cares about whether its procedures are coherent at the group level rather than only at the individual level.

Please describe the most important implications of the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization for the design of this nonprofit's internal decision procedures. In particular, I want implications that bear on how it should interpret its scoring rule, how it should handle preference revision and aggregation across teams, and what it should do when local coherence and global coherence pull apart. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
