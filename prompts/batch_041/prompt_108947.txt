I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I work on Bayesian models, and I keep running into Bertrand's paradox. As I understand it, the paradox shows that the principle of indifference is inconsistent: you can get different probabilities for the same event depending on how you parameterize the random chord, so any appeal to symmetry in assigning a prior is arbitrary. That worries me, because we routinely assume exchangeability and permutation invariance. I've also seen the suggested fix—require invariance under a transformation group—but that just seems to relocate the arbitrariness into choosing the group. Does Bertrand's paradox really undermine symmetry-based priors? If it doesn't, what exactly does it refute, and is the reference class problem the same issue or a different one?
