I follow politics and law stuff enough to notice a pattern that bugs me, and I want to know if there's a name for what the pattern is committed to.

Here's the pattern. In most US states it's a crime to have sex with an animal, and a crime to have sex with your adult sibling or your adult parent even when everyone involved is a grownup who consented and nobody gets physically hurt. Same deal with assisted suicide in most places -- illegal, even when the person is terminally ill, lucid, and asking for it, and even though refusing treatment or starving yourself is legal. Dueling is still on the books in some places. Bigamy is illegal even when all the spouses know and are fine with it. But we let people box each other half to death, we let people sell their plasma, we let adults get tattoos and cosmetic surgery and gamble away their savings. We don't prosecute adultery. Nobody raids a house because two consenting adults are doing something gross in it.

So the law seems to be doing two things at once: it mostly stays out of the business of protecting people from themselves and from each other's private consensual choices, but there's a whole category of stuff it bans anyway, and the only thing the banned stuff seems to have in common is that most people find it disgusting or degrading or beneath human dignity. It's not the harm -- there's no victim in the incest case that the parties aren't also the perpetrator of, and the animal can't complain and in some of these cases isn't even obviously suffering.

My question: what does this pattern actually presuppose about what the state is allowed to do? I keep hearing "the harm principle" thrown around like it's settled, but the practice doesn't look like the harm principle to me. Does the law's willingness to ban harmless-but-revolting stuff commit it to a real theory -- that society has a shared morality worth enforcing for its own sake, that letting this stuff slide would corrode the social fabric, that some acts are intrinsically degrading regardless of consequences? Or could you explain the whole pattern with something thinner, like second-order harms (normalizing it, slippery slopes, disgust as a proxy for hidden harm)? And which parts of the practice does the theory actually have to carry, versus which parts are just historical accident or inertia that any theory could accommodate?

Also tell me where it's underdetermined. If I replaced the "society's morality" story with a strict harm-principle story, what concretely has to change in the law -- what gets legalized, what stays, and what new stuff suddenly looks like it should be banned? And is there a version of the practice that's internally consistent, or is it just a mess that several incompatible theories all happen to license? I'm less interested in whether legal moralism is true than in what the actual law is quietly assuming, and whether the assumption is doing any real work or is just decoration on top of politics.
