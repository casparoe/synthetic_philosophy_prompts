I'm in a small reading group on markets and morals and I keep hitting the same wall. In the US its against the law to buy a kidney, and a lot of states make it illegal to jack up the price of water and generators after a hurricane. But it's legal in many states to pay a woman to carry your baby, you can be paid for plasma, sperm and eggs, and healthy volunteers get paid thousands of dollars to swallow an experimental drug. Meanwhile nobody minds when a rare painting sells for a hundred times what it went for last year. I dont want the standard commodification sermon, I want to know what theory these rules actually presuppose. Does the gouging law assume some prices are intrinsically fair, or only that desperation makes people unable to bargain? Does the kidney ban assume kidneys arent the kind of thing that can have a price, or just that paying for them produces bad results (exploitation, black markets, crowding out the people who give them away for free)? The parts that bug me: you can give a kidney but not sell one, and you can be paid for the labor of surrogacy but not buy the baby, so what do those asymmetries commit the law to? Reconstruct the view or views the pattern presupposes, tell me which bits are strictly required and which would sit just as comfortably with some rival theory, and say what each rule would have to turn into if you swapped in a plain consequentialist view. And if some of these rules rest on assumptions that can't all be true at once, tell me which ones.
