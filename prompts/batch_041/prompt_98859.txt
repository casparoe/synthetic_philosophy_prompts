Since my father's illness I've been reading about the project of abolishing suffering through biotechnology, and I've joined a reading group that has been circling the same argument for months without either side moving. The advocates hold that pain and negative affect are pure design flaws, intrinsically bad and additive, and that we should engineer them away as far as biology allows; they point to patients who report pain but are not distressed by it as proof that the badness can be peeled off the sensation. The critics grant every fact about suffering but insist that a creature incapable of it would have lost something essential, or that the goal is not even coherent; they say those patients only show the badness was never in the sensation to begin with. Both sides agree that a world of perpetually contented, pain-free beings would be missing something, and each treats that agreement as evidence for its own side. My hypothesis is that the impasse is not about the ethics of the technology but about whether suffering's disvalue is intrinsic or partly constituted by the sufferer's attitude toward it -- so the sides are not disputing one quantity but counting different quantities under one word. Is that right? If not, is this a hidden empirical disagreement (about what the technology could do, or about what else goes when suffering goes), a disagreement about which cases are the ones to be explained, or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? Diagnose it from what each side actually says, and tell me what would count as progress -- a distinction, a case, a result -- or whether none should be expected.
