ok so I'm a phd student in philosophy (ethics side, with some metaethics) and I need a short story for a reading group I'm putting together. the point is that it should actually be *about* a concept, not just a fable with a moral taped onto the end.

the concept is supererogation -- the acts that go beyond the call of duty, the heroic/saintly stuff thats praiseworthy but that nobody is actually required to do. giving a stranger a kidney, running into the fire, that kind of thing. the standard debate is whether the category is even coherent (if an act is morally better, why isnt it required?), and I really dont want the story to just rehearse that.

here's the twist I want you to build it around, which I think is underexplored. supererogation might be self-erasing. the second somebody actually does the beyond-duty thing, it stops being beyond duty -- it becomes the new baseline, the thing everyone's now expected to do, and whoever doesnt do it afterward is a bit of a monster. so the act of being a hero destroys the very category that made them a hero. call it the precedent ratchet. I want a story where the protagonist did the original beyond-duty thing and then has to watch, over years, their voluntary act get converted into an obligation that falls on everyone else, and eventually keeps falling back on them, again and again, each time at a higher level. the drama should be: was what they did still good? should they not have done it? can you do a beyond-duty act *for the sake of* keeping the category alive, and does that even make sense, or does trying to preserve the category just turn it into a different kind of calculation? I'd like the ending to genuinely stay open rather than collapsing into "and so we should all lower our standards."

length: maybe 1200-2000 words. third person, set it somewhere concrete and mundane -- a hospital ward, a small company, a village, a lab -- your pick, not a seminar room. the ideas should come through events and through what the characters argue with each other, not through a narrator explaining the philosophy. and please dont name-drop any philosophers or cite anything, just tell the story.
