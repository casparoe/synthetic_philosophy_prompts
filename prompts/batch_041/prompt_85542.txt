For a workshop on the boundaries of epistemic injustice, I need help deciding what belongs in the category. Below are eight candidate harms. Please rate each from 0 to 10 for how well it fits the core idea of an epistemic injustice — a wrong done to someone specifically in their capacity as a knower, as opposed to an ordinary mistake, a distributive unfairness, or plain bad luck — and then rank the eight from most to least central to the category.

1. Being disbelieved because of a hearer's prejudice about your social group.
2. Being disbelieved because the hearer needs to regard you as unreliable in order to keep their own conduct in a good light.
3. Lacking the shared vocabulary to make sense of an experience you have had, through no fault of your own.
4. Lacking that vocabulary because those with power have actively prevented it from being developed.
5. Having your testimony extracted and put to use as data while you receive nothing from it.
6. Being shut out of a profession by credentialing requirements that are not themselves epistemic.
7. Being repeatedly talked over in meetings.
8. Having your anecdotal evidence dismissed as mere anecdote by someone applying a standard they would not apply to their own evidence.

For each item, give one sentence explaining why it lands where it does, and flag any that you think are better treated as instances of some broader moral or distributive harm rather than as entries in their own right. Finally, if your top-ranked item strains the definition of the category I gave you, say so, and tell me whether the definition or the item should give way.
