I'm a second-year PhD student in a cognitive science program but my actual work is philosophy of mind, and I'm in a reading group that's been crawling through Gödel, Escher, Bach all semester. We just hit the Ant Fugue — the one where the Anteater's aunt turns out to be an ant colony named Aunt Hillary that he has actual conversations with, and the neat move is that some little cluster of ants scurrying around counts as a "signal" at the colony level even though it means nothing to any individual ant. My labmate has totally run with this. His line is: ants are to neurons as the colony is to the brain, so a colony can have a mind, so minds are substrate-independent, so none of us should be shocked when a big enough pile of models, or a city, or the internet "wakes up." He says Aunt Hillary is a mind, just a slow one — her thoughts take hours where ours take milliseconds — and that the only reason it feels weird is that we're biased toward brains, and that if you could only watch the colony's trail-writing you couldn't tell it from a person texting, so she passes a Turing test anyway.

I want to push back but I keep getting lost in the mapping. Can you do a real audit of the analogy? What corresponds to what — is an ant a neuron, or is an ant more like a whole small organism, which I think changes everything, since neurons aren't little agents with their own agendas and interests — and what plays the role of a synapse, a signal, memory, and the "binding" that makes it one subject instead of a crowd? Then the places it breaks: the colony seems to store its state in the pheromone trails and the nest itself, not in anything inside the ants, which starts to look less like a brain and more like a brain plus a notebook, or like a market; and there's no obvious spot where all the signals meet the way they do in a brain. Tell me which of those breaks actually matter for the conclusion he wants and which are just colorful differences, and whether the "substrate-independence, so the internet might be conscious" moral survives once the disanalogies are on the table. And if there's a better analogy for what a colony is, I'd love one, because "it's a brain" is doing alot of quiet work in that room and I don't think anyone's checked it.
