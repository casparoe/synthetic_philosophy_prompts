I'm in a historiography seminar and I've got to hand in a dialogue next week, so I want you to write it for me. Two grad students in the same seminar, both sharp, neither one a strawman. One is a structuralist — call her Priya — who thinks that once you've got Weimar's crisis, the war, the depression, the paramilitaries, the treaty grievance, the street violence, the person is basically a placeholder: somebody else fills the slot and you get functionally the same regime. The other, Tom, is a contingency guy: structures set the menu, he says, but the person picks the dish, and the particular dish here — the Führer cult, the party turning its terror inward on its own, the war in the east as a racial project rather than ordinary empire — was cooked by one guy and isn't just sitting there waiting for a replacement to pick up.

Don't let them trade the usual "great man vs. social forces" slogans back and forth. I want them to actually dig into each other's points. A few things I'd like them to fight about, add more if you see them:

- whether "would there have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler" even has a determinate answer until you fix how coarse the outcome is. Is a military junta in 1935 that rearms and tears up Versailles a win for Priya or a different outcome entirely? Have them coin a term for this resolution problem and then argue about whether it's a real distinction or a dodge.
- whether a successor inherits the movement or has to rebuild it. Priya says the movement is bigger than any one man and the slot gets filled; Tom says the specific features that made it so dangerous — the personal oath, the myth, the readiness to murder its own — were his handiwork and don't transfer to whoever's next.
- what would actually settle it. Have them propose a test and have the other one put a hole in it. I'd like at least one of them to concede something real by the end, even if it's small, and please don't end on a tidy "well, it's complicated" note.

Keep it maybe 900-1200 words, plain speech, not academic register, and make them answer each other's actual arguements rather than talking past each other. Then outside the dialogue, tell me how confident you are in the substantive historical claims your characters make and single out the one point you're least sure about.
