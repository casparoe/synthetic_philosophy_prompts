Ok, longtime lurker, first real post. I've slowly talked myself into the al-Ghazālī line on causation and I want someone to try to break it before I get too attached to it.

Here's the position. There's no necessary connection between what we call a cause and what we call its effect. When fire touches cotton the cotton burns, but the burning isn't produced by the fire; God produces it directly, and what we're looking at is a regularity he keeps up, not a power the fire has. Ghazālī's version (the seventeenth discussion of the Incoherence, if you want to check my memory) is explicitly metaphysical, not just epistemic -- he isn't saying we can't know the connection, he's saying the connection isn't there to be known, because the efficacy is supplied from outside the objects at every moment. So the fire is at most an occasional cause. Secular translation: swap God for whatever the base level of physics turns out to be, and the claim is that no macro object ever really does anything to another -- the regularities are all there is.

What I want is the defeaters. Not the lazy ones (the standard rundown that this is just Hume in a robe, or that it makes science impossible, neither of which I find convincing as stated). I want the things that should actually move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I want the ones a committed occasionalist would concede are real costs, not just the ones critics like to push.

Some stuff I keep going in circles on, and would like you to chew on specifically:

1. The interventionist point. Woodward-style: if X is a cause of Y then intervening on X changes Y in a way that conditioning on X alone doesn't. I don't see how occasionalism explains why intervention should be special at all -- if every regularity is just God's habit, why does one kind of manipulation track something the other kind doesn't?

2. My friend's arguement that occasionalism plus computational physics just equals "the world is being re-rendered every frame", and that this makes the view unfalsifiable by construction, since any observation whatsoever is just another thing God is doing. Is that a fair reduction, or is there a version of the view with actual empirical content? (Those are his words, I think he's cheating, but I can't say why.)

3. Concurrentism. Aquinas-ish: creatures have real powers, God just sustains them in being. If concurrentism can do everything occasionalism can do, does occasionalism have any motivation left besides the theological one about God's sovereignty?

4. What would a world with genuine causal glue look like that's different from ours, in a way you could actually notice from inside it? Because right now I can't name a single observation that distinguishes the two, and I suspect that's definately the whole problem.

5. The moment-by-moment re-creation thing. If God's custom just is the regularities, what's the status of the counterfactuals? Does "if the fire had touched the cotton it would have burned" get grounded in anything, or is that only a fact about the custom?

Then tell me honestly whether the view is well insulated, and if it is, whether that's a fatal problem for a view like this or just the price of doing theology with the physics left switched on. And if there's a version of the position that keeps the metaphysics but pays for its empirical content somewhere, I'd like to see what it pays with.
