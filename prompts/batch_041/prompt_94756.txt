I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade, and the habit I've taken from them is to ask what a claim would have to mean before deciding whether to believe it — which is how I've ended up stuck on what looks like a forced choice in the metaphysics of persistence. My instincts are with what I take to be the common-sense picture: a table, or a person, is wholly present at each moment of its existence, rather than being a long four-dimensional object that has a different temporal part at every instant. At the same time I'm persuaded by an argument I keep meeting, to the effect that special relativity leaves no privileged present, so that past and future events are as real as present ones. A friend and I spent an evening trying to work out whether these two commitments can coexist, and we came away convinced that they cannot. The argument we hit on was this: if the past is as real as the present, and I am wholly present now, then either my past self is also wholly present — so that there are, absurdly, two of me — or else I must be a four-dimensional thing whose earlier and later states are merely parts of it. Since two of me is nonsense, it looked as though accepting the relativity argument forces me into perdurantism and out of the common-sense view.

I'd like you to tell me whether that reasoning holds, and if it doesn't, precisely where it comes apart. I've also noticed that the people who take the perdurantist line don't seem to speak with one voice: some describe persisting things as four-dimensional worms and others as successions of momentary stages, and I can't tell whether that is a real disagreement or merely two ways of saying the same thing. And I've read that perdurantism is supposed to solve the puzzle of how a single thing can be one colour and then another by parcelling the incompatible properties out to different temporal parts — which strikes me less as a solution than as a relocation of the problem. So: are eternalism and endurantism genuinely incompatible, does the relativity argument bear on the question of persistence at all, and if there is a way to be an eternalist who still holds that objects are wholly present at each moment, how does that view cope with the reality of my past and future states? I am not after a reading list or references — I have no way to look anything up — I want the distinctions and the argument, laid out so that I can see the joints. Please answer in British English.
