I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and we've spent the last three weeks on the Turing test and behavioral criteria for mentality, and a friend dropped an argument on us over coffee that I've been chewing on ever since. It goes like this. (1) Having a mind is nothing over and above being the kind of thing whose behavior is best predicted and explained by attributing mental states to it. (2) My thermostat's flipping the furnace on and off is best predicted and explained by saying it wants the room at 21 and believes the room is currently colder than that. (3) So the thermostat has a belief and a desire. (4) Whatever the thermostat has, my cat has more of — more states, finer gradations, more of them wired to one another — but the difference is complexity, not kind. (5) If the only difference between having a mind and not having one is the complexity of the state-to-behavior map, then there's no principled place to stop, and anything with any state-to-behavior map at all has some mental life. (6) A rain gauge's water level tracks the rainfall the way a person's report would; a mousetrap responds to a mouse the way an angry person responds to an intruder. (7) So the gauge and the trap have beliefs about the weather and about mice, and so does a river carving a canyon and a planet holding to an inverse-square law. (8) A planet doesn't want anything. (9) So (1) is false.

I'd like to keep (8) — that feels like rock bottom to me — and honestly I'd like to keep (1) too, because I have no idea what the extra ingredient would be if (1) turned out false, and the moment I posit one I owe an account of how I ever know that other people have it rather than just behaving as if they did. So please don't give me a cheap dismissal of step 2; I want to know which step I should actually give up, why it looked fine when we read the whole thing out loud, and what giving it up costs. If keeping (1) forces me to say the rain gauge beleives it's raining, tell me that and tell me how much that hurts. If keeping (8) forces me to find a threshold I can't locate, tell me that instead. And my friend keeps insisting that every position here just bites one bullet or another — so walk me through what the functionalist, the behaviorist, the eliminativist about belief, and the panpsychist each have to pay, and whether any of them gets to keep both (1) and (8) without cheating. I want the reciepts, not a survey.
