My partner and I have a stupid recurring argument about our dog and I want an outside ruling. Dog knocks the bin over, then slinks under the table with her ears back. My partner says she feels guilty. I say no, we're just putting a face on a dog, theres no little courtroom in her head. Last weekend a friend of ours who did a biology degree waded in and said we're both missing the point. The question, she says, isn't whether theres guilt in there, it's whether talking about the dog as if she had wants and beliefs lets you predict what she'll do next better than talking about nerves and muscles would. If it does, the guilt-story is real in the same way "this room is warm" is real even though you can't point at a temperature. That seemed smart to me for about a day, until I noticed I can also describe our Roomba as "wanting" to get back to its dock, and that compresses the data fine too. So now my partner says great, then everything is real, which is the same as nothing being real.

A third friend says the right order is: first work out what the thing is built to do, and only then ask what it's trying to do. My partner says that's circular, since the only way you know what a dog is "built for" is by watching what dogs do.

What I want is the tiebreaker. Is there a real difference between the dog case and the Roomba case, or is it just that one story is more flattering? And if it all comes down to compression, what stops me from inventing a mental vocabulary for literally any object, since you can always write a short story about something after you've already seen the data? Is there some extra condition, like the description has to survive when you re-carve the system a different way, or it has to be one you'd have found without knowing the answer in advance? Our friend also said something about how evolution gives things purposes without anyone doing the intending, and that this is supposed to make the whole picture less creepy, but I didn't follow it, and I don't know if it changes anything that the Roomba was designed by a person while the dog wasn't. Walk me through how to actually settle this, and tell me whether your test gives the same verdict on an ant colony "deciding" where to nest, and on a chess engine "wanting" to keep its queen.
