I teach a graduate seminar in normative ethics and am setting the final exam. Please draft one ninety-minute essay question on the distinction between doing and allowing harm, together with a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme out of 20. The question must not be answerable by reciting the standard examples of that debate.

I want it to turn on a structural feature rather than a well-worn case. The case it presents should be one in which the only thing standing between a person and death is an ongoing, human-maintained process — a pump keeping a mine dry, a ventilator, a drainage tunnel — so that switching the process off and failing to keep it running produce the same death, while the process was itself, from the outset, an intervention in what would otherwise have been the lethal course of events. Candidates should be required to say what the doing/allowing distinction is a distinction between; whether it can be drawn at all without a prior notion of the natural or baseline course of events; and whether it survives the observation that such baselines are typically the residue of earlier human interventions. They should also have to take a stand on whether the distinction does any independent moral work once the baseline problem is on the table, rather than merely relabelling the verdicts.

Candidates should not be asked to cite or name any author, and the model answer should be self-contained rather than resting on any particular text. The marking scheme should name five must-hit points with the marks each carries, give a one-line note on the characteristic error at each, and state in a sentence or two what separates an A answer from a B.
