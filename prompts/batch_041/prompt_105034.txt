I've been going down a rabbit hole on the old Meno problem — true belief gets you to Larissa just as well as knowledge does, so what exactly is supposed to be better about knowledge? Everyone agrees knowledge is better, nobody agrees why. I've got a pile of candidate answers and I want you to rank them, strongest to weakest, as answers to that specific question. Not "which is most fashionable" — which one actually explains what knowledge has that a lucky true belief lacks.

The candidates, roughly:

(a) Knowledge is more stable. It won't get knocked over by the next bit of counterevidence, whereas the lucky true belief is fragile.
(b) Knowledge is creditable to the knower — it came out of their own competance, so it's an achievement in a way the lucky belief isn't.
(c) Knowledge is more valuable because you can transmit it, teach it, and build on it, whereas a lucky true belief is a dead end you can't put to further use.
(d) There's no deep story here: knowledge is true belief plus a bit of decoration, and the demand for extra value is a pseudo-problem we should stop asking about.
(e) My own stab: knowledge is better because the knower can answer challenges. They can say why, or at least reliably re-derive it, whereas the lucky believer's confidence is a blank wall.

Put these in tiers, say in a sentence or two why each lands where it does, and tell me which of them, if any, gets hit by the swamping worry — the thought that if the truth of the belief is doing all the work, then bolting anything else on can't add value. Then tell me whether my stab at it, (e), is really just a dressed-up version of (a) or (b) or whether it's doing something neither of them is. Finally, if you had to defend exactly one of these in a seminar and the room was hostile, which would you bet on, and what's the cheapest objection you'd expect to get?

Answer in British English please.
