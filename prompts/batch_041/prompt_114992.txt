I help run a weekly policy debate group at my university — about 20 students, mostly philosophy and law-adjacent, and we keep getting into the same mess whenever sex work law comes up. Everyone in the room says they're for decriminalisation, and then it turns out they mean completely different things: one person means the Nordic model with buyers criminalised, another means the German/Netherlands legalisation-and-regulation approach, another means the New Zealand approach (which I think is its own thing but I'm not sure exactly how it differs from full decriminalisation), and someone else means full decriminalisation of everything including managers, landlords, and platforms. We also have the abolitionist/end-demand people who agree with criminalising buyers but for totally different reasons than the public-health people, and a couple of libertarians who think the state shouldn't be involved at all. I want to make a one-page decision tree that sorts people by what they actually think rather than by which label they like. Can you design it? Maybe 12-14 branch nodes and 7-9 leaves. Each node should be a concrete question or claim someone can answer without already knowing which position it leads to — not "are you a decriminalisationist?" but things like whether buying should be a crime, whether selling should be, whether managers/landlords/advertisers should be, whether mandatory registration or health checks are acceptable, whether the state's main goal is reducing prevalence or improving working conditions, whether sex work is inherently harmful or just another job with bad labour protections, whether criminalising buyers reduces demand or just shifts harm onto workers, whether legalisation should exclude migrants/undocumented workers, whether the sex worker's consent can be genuine under economic pressure, and whether exit services should be funded instead of or alongside legalisation.

The tree should be built from the logical space first and then locate the named positions in it, and I'd like you to flag any branches where two positions collapse into the same answers or where a combination is unoccupied — and say whether you think that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody has bothered to defend it. For each leaf give the position name (or "unnamed") plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Also, if the tree can't separate two views that people in my group insist are different, please say so rather than pretending the questions do the work. And don't just list countries and their laws; I can look those up. I want the diagnostic questions that get at the underlying disagreements. I'm not a lawyer, so some of my terminology is probly off — fix it where it matters, but keep the questions in plain language a first-year could answer.
