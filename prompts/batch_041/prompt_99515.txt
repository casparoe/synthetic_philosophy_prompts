Two people in my cohort got into it after the set theory seminar and asked me to referee, and I honestly dont know enough to. It was about Hilbert's hotel.

(1) He said the hotel is basically a reductio: if a hotel is full and can still take a new guest, then full has stopped meaning anything, so the story shows that the size of an infinite set is something we decided rather than something we found — Cantor picked the bijection criterion and then acted surprised at where it led. (2) She said no, the only lesson is that intuitions trained on finite sets don't carry over, and the bijection criterion isn't arbitrary anyway: size has to be invariant under renaming, because if the way a set happens to be described can change its size, you're not talking about size anymore. (3) He said that's circular, since size must be invariant under renaming is just the bijection criterion restated. He also said the part-whole principle (a proper part is smaller than its whole) isn't a mere habit, because there's real technical work — numerosities, I think he said — that assigns every set a size on which the evens really are fewer than the naturals, so you can keep the part-whole principle as long as you're willing to give up bijection-invariance. And he said the hotel story quietly assumes you can enumerate the rooms and shuffle infinitely many guests; without the axiom of choice you can have an infinite set with no countable subset at all, so there's a hotel that's infinite, full, and can't take even one more guest. (4) She said numerosities are a different notion of size, not a rival to cardinality, and that the choice stuff is a red herring since the hotel is about the natural numbers, which are provably enumerable, so the shift is free.

Can you adjudicate this move by move — which moves are good, which are mistaken, and what each of them is missing? I'd rather have a rating per move than a verdict on who won overall.
