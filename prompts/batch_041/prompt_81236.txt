I am a high school student and a member of my school's philosophy club. I am preparing a presentation on antinatalism and the asymmetry thesis, and I want to make sure my own views are coherent before I present. I have written down my verdicts on a set of cases, but I cannot find a single principle that accommodates all of them. I would be grateful if you could help me identify the principle, or the small set of candidate principles, that best captures my verdicts, and tell me honestly which verdicts I would have to give up.

Here are the cases and my verdicts.

1. A couple can take a pill that will cause them to conceive a child who will live a happy life. I think it is permissible to take the pill and permissible not to. Not taking it is not a moral failure.

2. A couple can take a pill that will cause them to conceive a child who will live a life of unrelenting suffering. I think taking the pill is morally wrong, and not taking it is good.

3. A couple can take a pill that will prevent the conception of a child who would have lived a happy life. I think taking the pill is not wrong.

4. A couple can take a pill that will prevent the conception of a child who would have lived a life of unrelenting suffering. I think taking the pill is good.

5. A doctor can either cure an existing patient's painful condition or bring a new happy person into existence. I think the doctor should cure the patient.

6. A doctor can either cure an existing patient's painful condition or prevent a new person from being born into a painful condition. I think the doctor should do either; both seem equally good.

7. A world with one happy person is not better than an empty world.

8. A world with one happy person and one suffering person is worse than an empty world.

9. A world with a million happy people is better than a world with one happy person.

10. If a happy person already exists, making them happier is good. But creating a new happy person is not good in the same way.

11. Suppose you can either create a happy person who will experience a lifetime of bliss, or prevent a suffering person from being created who would experience a lifetime of agony. I think you should prevent the suffering person.

12. Suppose you can either create a happy person or make an existing person slightly happier. I think you should make the existing person happier.

13. Suppose a machine can retroactively erase a happy person from existence, so that they never existed and no one remembers them. I think it is not wrong to use the machine. If the machine would erase a suffering person, I think it is good to use it.

14. Suppose a couple can have a child who will be happy, but the child will also cause a small amount of suffering to an existing person, for example by using resources that could have cured a stranger's headache. I think they should not have the child. But if the child's happiness is enormous and the stranger's headache is trivial, I am less sure.

My tentative principle is that suffering is always a reason against bringing someone into existence, while happiness is never a reason for bringing someone into existence, but once someone exists, their happiness matters as much as anyone else's. Does this principle fit my verdicts? If not, what should I change? What does it say about a case I have not considered: a world with no suffering and no happiness versus a world with one happy person and no suffering? Or a case where you can create a happy person who will never suffer, but who will be the only being in the universe? Please also tell me which of my verdicts the principle forces me to abandon, and why.
