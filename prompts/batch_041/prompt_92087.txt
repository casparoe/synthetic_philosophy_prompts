I've been in a reading group on collective intentionality for about a year now and we keep hitting the same wall. The setup, as I understand it: Bratman says a shared intention is built out of ordinary individual attitudes — each of us intends that we do the thing, the intentions are interdependent, each of us intends it partly because the other does, and we're disposed to help each other out if things go sideways. Searle says that can't be the whole story, because a we-intention is its own primitive kind of intentional state, not an I-intention with extra beliefs bolted on, and he thinks that's a fact about our biology, not a convention. Gilbert says both of them are missing the thing that actually matters, which is joint commitment — once we're jointly committed you have standing to demand that I do my part, so obligations and entitlements come in from the start rather than being added later. And then there's the we-mode vs I-mode vocabulary floating around that seems to cut across all three positions without anyone agreeing on where the joints are.

Nobody has moved in a year. Every stock example gets claimed by everybody: two people going for a walk together, singing a duet, pushing a stalled car out of an intersection. My hypothesis is that this isn't one disagreement but three stacked on top of each other — one about what's actually in the head when someone acts jointly (an architectural or empirical question), one about what makes a bit of behavior count as joint in the first place (a question about the explanandum), and one about whether obligation is constitutive of joint action or just a common side effect (a normative question that can't be settled by psychology). If that's right, then alot of the arguing is people answering different questions while using the same words, and the we-intention debate may be closer to verbal than anyone wants to admit.

So diagnose it. Is the claim that we-intentions are irreducible even a psychological claim, or is it a claim about how we ought to describe things? Does Gilbert's obligation get built into her analysis in a way that makes her dispute with Bratman not a psychological dispute at all? I'm told infants do things like joint action before they could possibly have these concepts — does that count for Searle's primitiveness or against it? Is there a deflationary option, that attributing we-intentions is just a predictive stance and the question of whats really in the head is misposed, which would dissolve the whole thing rather than settle it? And where does the team-reasoning stuff in game theory fit — people apparently reasoning as a team instead of each maximizing — is that the same axis or a different one? I dont need a survey of the positions, I need a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, and an honest answer about what would count as progress: a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, or just admitting it's a normative dispute wearing psychological clothing.
