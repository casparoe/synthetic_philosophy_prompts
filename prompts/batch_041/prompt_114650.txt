I am a professor of metaphysics, and next month I will present a paper to my department's metaphysics reading group. I want a Q&A preparation sheet, not a restatement of the paper. The thesis I will defend is that "fundamental" should be treated as a role rather than a rank: to be fundamental is simply to be the terminus of a grounding chain, and once fundamentality is understood as a role, the well-foundedness of grounding (every chain bottoms out) and the completeness of grounding (everything non-fundamental is grounded) come apart. I argue that well-foundedness is false — the counterexamples I call hanging chains, in which each member of a chain is grounded by the next with no terminus — while completeness can be preserved, so that a picture with no fundamental level can still be fully explanatory and can serve as a successor to the Principle of Sufficient Reason. The audience is the reading group: roughly eight people, most of them committed grounding realists who treat grounding as a primitive, hyperintensional relation and who hold that a chain which fails to bottom out is simply unintelligible; two deflationists who regard grounding talk as a dressed-up way of talking about ordinary explanation and who will press me on whether hanging chains are anything more than an artifact of my formalism; and one philosopher of science who thinks the whole debate projects the habits of set theory onto the world. They are impatient with formal apparatus and will want the argument in prose, and they are unsentimental — they will say the weakest thing out loud rather than wait for me to notice it.

Give me exactly five numbered items, with no introduction, no conclusion, and no headings, in American English. Order them from the objection that does the most damage to my case to the one that does the least, and make the fifth item the question I am hoping nobody asks. In each item, state the objection in the words the colleague who would raise it would actually use, then, in a sentence or two, tell me what I should concede, what I should hold, and where that reply is soft.
