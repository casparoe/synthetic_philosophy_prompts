Alright, I've been going down a rabbit hole on echo chambers vs epistemic bubbles — the usual line being that a bubble is when you're just missing information, and an echo chamber is when you're missing it because you've been taught to write off everything outside as a lie. Fine, I buy that. But I keep running into something that dosn't fit either box and I want you to compare it properly against the usual two.

Let me call it the 'mumble room' for now, since I don't have a better name. In a mumble room: nobody's cut off from info, nobody's been told the outside is lying, everyone can read whatever they want. The catch is that almost nobody says what they actually think out loud, and everyone assumes the room agrees with whoever's loudest. So the room's 'position' is really just the two loudest people, and everyone else privately thinks something else but figures they're the weird one. From the outside it looks like a totally sealed-in, self-assured little group. From the inside it's actually full of quiet dissent that never gets said.

So: is the mumble room an echo chamber, an epistemic bubble, both, or a genuinely diffrent animal? I want you to actually compare the concepts, not just define them — where do they overlap, where does the usual distinction break down, and what does the mumble room need that neither of the other two has? And a second thing I can't sort out: is the real problem the loud minority, or is it the silent majority all assuming they're alone? Those feel like seperate diagnoses and maybe seperate fixes, so tell me which one the mumble room actually is.
