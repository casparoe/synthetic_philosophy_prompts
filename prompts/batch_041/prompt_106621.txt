In my seminar on Darwin and Turing, we discussed a thought experiment: a system trained by reinforcement learning from human feedback can reliably classify arbitrary sentences of propositional logic as theorems or non-theorems, yet its internal states contain no representation of inference rules or validity. One classmate, Mira, argued that this is a clean case of competence without comprehension, and that our hesitation to call it reasoning is residual dualism: if the system matches every counterfactual we can state about theoremhood, Turing's imitation game leaves no further fact about whether it understands. Another classmate, Dev, replied that this only relocates the comprehension: the training signal was supplied by humans who understood theoremhood, so the system's competence is a product of their comprehension; the Darwinian and Turing inversions show how comprehension can be outsourced, not that it is absent. Mira countered that by Dev's standard no evolved capacity could ever count as comprehension, since natural selection's fitness was not comprehended by the organism. Dev answered that this conflates selection with instruction: natural selection carries no representational content, whereas human feedback does, and that difference is doing all the work. They have asked me to referee. Please adjudicate their exchange move by move, saying where each is right, where each overreaches, and what the other is missing, then give a verdict on which is closer to the truth about what the Darwinian and Turing inversions actually establish.
