I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have spent the past several months reading about Confucian ethics on my own, and there is one claim I keep running into that I cannot get past: that a person is not a private self who happens to occupy various roles — daughter, neighbour, teacher, mourner — but is in some strong sense made out of those roles, so that there is no core self standing behind the relationships waiting to be discovered.

My difficulty is that I suspect this is several different claims bundled together, and I cannot tell which one is doing the work. One reading seems obviously true: my habits, my sense of what matters, even what I notice about other people were formed by the particular people who raised me and by the expectations attached to my positions. A stronger reading seems to say something else — that once you subtract the roles there is nothing left at all — and I cannot tell whether that is meant as a claim about what a person is, a claim about what a person owes, or a claim about how selves are in fact built.

The case that stopped me is my own performance of these roles. Much of what I do in them is scripted: the polite formulas, the expected gestures, the bedside manner. Sometimes I mean it and sometimes I do not, and I have noticed that after enough repetition I usually come to mean it. That seems to run in the direction the account wants. But it also seems to describe a well-trained performer, and I cannot see what distinguishes a person from someone going through the motions very convincingly. My impression from what I have read is that the tradition wants both of two things — that the outward forms without the right inner state are worthless, and that the right inner state is itself cultivated by practising the outward forms — and I cannot see how those two are compatible.

Please explain what the claim that persons are constituted by their roles actually commits you to, separating the metaphysical, the moral and the psychological readings and saying which of them the account genuinely needs. Then tell me what would have to be true of sincerity for the two halves about ritual to hold together, given that sincerity is neither the raw material the roles work on nor merely the residue of performing them well.
