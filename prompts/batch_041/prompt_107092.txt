I've been going back and forth with a mate about whether a model that only ever reads text can mean anything by its words, and he thinks the symbol grounding problem is a non-problem. Here's his arguement, tidied up:

(1) A symbol means something only if it's grounded — hooked to the world rather than just to other symbols.
(2) If a symbol is hooked only to other symbols you get a regress, so at the bottom some states must be grounded directly in the world.
(3) To be grounded directly in the world is to stand in the right kind of causal connection to the thing meant.
(4) But all a causal connection to X amounts to is reliable co-variation with X.
(5) Nearly every physical state reliably co-varies with something — a nail rusts when it rains, a dune's shape tracks the prevailing wind.
(6) So nearly every physical state is grounded, meaning is everywhere, and the nail means rain.
(7) That's absurd, but every premise looks fine to me, and if I drop (3) I've got no account of what "direct" grounding even is. So either meaning is everywhere or nothing means anything. Where does this go wrong?
