I need a consistency check on my philosophy of mind views before a seminar, and I'd rather find the cracks myself than have them found for me. Here's where I've landed:

- Physics only ever tells us about structure: relations, dispositions, how things are hooked up to each other. It's silent about what the things are in themselves. And the only intrinsic natures I have any grip on are experiences. So the intrinsic nature of physical stuff is experiential (the usual Russellian move, if that helps).
- Which means I'm still a physicalist. On this picture panpsychism doesn't add a new fundamental property or a new causal power, it just says what the physical properties are like from the inside. So I'm not worried about causal closure.
- Every fundamental thing has some experience, its own tiny point of view. Micro-subjects are as fundamental as micro-properties.
- But I also think there's really only one subject and it's the cosmos. Your experience and mine are the cosmos experiencing itself through a local window; the micro-ones aren't subjects in their own right.
- I dont believe in brute emergence of the experiential from the non-experiential. That's the whole point of going panpsychist in the first place: if experience is fundamental, nothing has to emerge.
- The combination problem is the real problem. I split it in two: getting many micro-qualities to fuse into one quality (a chord out of notes) I think is tractable; getting many micro-subjects to fuse into one subject I don't think is just unsolved, I think it's unsolvable, at least for the micro version.
- Being a subject is all or nothing. Nobody is 30% a subject.
- And I don't think you need a self-model to be conscious; a bee can hurt without representing itself as the hurt-er.

Does this hang together? Go through the pairs and tell me which ones are genuinely in tension and how bad each is, and which ones only look like they are and why. For the real ones, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? Rank them by severity instead of telling me everything is equally bad, and finish by telling me which single revision cleans up the most.
