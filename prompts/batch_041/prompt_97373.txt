I have been living with the hole argument for a few months now, and it has stopped being a topic and become a personal problem; before I commit my own position to paper I would like an audit. Here is the package I hold. (1) Substantivalism: the points of the spacetime manifold are genuine individuals, not mathematical artifacts, and the metric is a field borne by them. (2) Leibniz equivalence: diffeomorphic models of general relativity represent one and the same physical situation, so the diffeomorphism invariance of the theory is gauge rather than physical. (3) Determinism: the laws, together with the complete state of the world on a Cauchy surface, fix the whole history. (4) Haecceitism, which I have always taken to be too obvious to need defending: two worlds can differ by nothing beyond a permutation of qualitatively identical individuals, because individuals carry identities over and above their qualitative profiles. (5) The hole argument is a serious argument and not a verbal trick: a substantivalist who lets points keep their identities independently of the metric really does face indeterminism inside the hole. My own suspicion is that the fault line runs between (1) and (2), since I cannot see how to be a substantivalist and still treat diffeomorphic models as one world. Which of these actually conflict, and which pairs only seem to? For each real conflict, say what has to give and how costly that is, rank the tensions by severity, and tell me whether any single revision cleans up the most.
