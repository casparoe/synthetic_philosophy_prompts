I work in the stats office of a small country, and last month we dropped four places in the world happiness rankings and my minister has basically declared a national emergency about it. He wants a plan to get us back into the top ten by the next round, and he's already decided what the plan is: grow GDP, and run a public campaign telling people the government isn't as corrupt as they think it is. I'm the one who has to write the thing, and I'm stuck on two problems.

First, what is that ranking even measuring? As far as I can tell the only number that decides our place is one question -- picture a ladder from 0 to 10, where's your life right now -- and all the other stuff (income, social support, life expectancy, freedom, generosity, corruption) is just things they use afterward to explain the scores. So the thing we'd be "topping" is people's answers to one weird question. Should a country even aim at that, and would my minister's plan move it?

Second, our numbers are pretty good and I think I know why. People here have never expected much. My aunt says she's satisfied with her life and I believe her, but I honestly don't think she'd say anything different in almost any situation she found herself in. So is she happy, or just used to things? And if those are two different things, which one is the ladder actually picking up?

Tell me straight: what do I put in the plan, and should I tell him out loud that chasing the ranking is a bad idea even if it works? Assume I cant go read a pile of studies, I just need someone to think it through with me.
