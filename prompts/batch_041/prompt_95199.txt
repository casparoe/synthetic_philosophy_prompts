I am a software engineer — mostly machine learning and formal verification — with no formal training in philosophy, and I keep returning to what I understand is usually called Wigner's puzzle: the fact that mathematics developed for its own sake keeps turning out to describe the physical world far more precisely than anything seems to entitle it to. I do not want a reading list, or a survey of who has said what. I want you to assess a set of candidate explanations, several of which I have assembled myself, and to be willing to say that some of them are empty.

Here are the candidates:

(a) The world simply is mathematical, or participates in a mathematical order, so the fit is not a coincidence requiring explanation at all.

(b) Survivorship: we keep the mathematics that works and quietly discard the rest, so the observed fit is a selection effect rather than a fact about the world.

(c) Compression: any world regular enough to contain observers must be compressible, and a mathematical description is just a compression, so the fit is close to tautological.

(d) Cognitive fit: our minds were shaped by a world they had to navigate, so the structures we find natural should be expected to track that world.

(e) Construction: physics is practised by people trained in mathematics, and mathematics supplies the only vocabulary in which a theory can be stated; on this view mathematics shapes what will count as a physical theory rather than merely describing one.

(f) Category mistake: the fit feels mysterious only because we expect it to have a reason; there is no further fact here to explain, and the puzzlement is an aesthetic residue.

(g) Structure: physics only ever delivers relations and structure, never intrinsic natures, and mathematics is the study of structure, so the fit is a matter of definition.

(h) Plenitude: mathematics is the study of all possible patterns, and physics selects one of them, so of course the selected pattern is mathematical.

Please do the following.

1. Rate each candidate from 0 to 10 for how much of the puzzle it genuinely explains, rather than merely restating or relocating it, and give a one-sentence justification for each score.
2. Rank the candidates from strongest to weakest overall, and say plainly which of them you think does no real work at all.
3. Identify any two or more candidates that are really the same explanation in different clothing, and any pair that cannot both be true.
4. For each candidate, say whether it could ever be shown to be wrong — as an engineer I care a great deal about whether an explanation is capable of failing — and flag any that are unfalsifiable in principle.
5. Finally, give a single number between 0 and 1 for how much less mysterious the puzzle feels once your top-ranked explanation is accepted, with a sentence on why it is neither 0 nor 1.

Please be concrete and do not hedge: I would far rather have a ranking I can argue with than a balanced summary.
