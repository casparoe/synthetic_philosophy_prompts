I've been reading rationalist blogs and adjacent forums for about fifteen years, and there's an argumentative move I keep running into that I have never seen named. I'd like you to tell me whether it corresponds to something established, and if not, to carve it precisely enough that a name becomes possible.

The move is this. Whenever a mechanistic or deflationary account of some mental phenomenon is on the table, introspective reports get recruited as evidence — but only when they point in the deflationary direction. Someone says they sat and looked and found no decision, only the action already underway; this is received as a datum about how the mind works, a window onto the machinery. Someone else says they looked and found a genuine act of will; that report is treated as confabulation, its occurrence explained away by the very mechanism under dispute. The same faculty is authoritative when it embarrasses the robust view and worthless when it embarrasses the deflationary one. I have watched the same flip in arguments about emotion (a report of a felt reason is data; a report of a felt cause is construction) and about perception.

What makes me doubt this is just the familiar unreliability of introspection, or plain confirmation bias, is that the debunking is usually offered as a finding rather than a stance: the person takes themselves to be following the evidence, and the asymmetry is invisible from the inside. Nor is it, as I understand it, the introspection illusion — that seems to be about the gap between how well we think we know ourselves and how well we know others, whereas here the asymmetry runs not between self and other but between reports that flatter one theory and reports that flatter another.

So: does this have a name? If something established covers it, I want to know how far the fit goes and where it breaks — whether it is best seen as a special case of something broader (motivated reasoning, a debunking argument gone circular) or as a distinct evidential failure with its own structure. If nothing covers it, define it so the edges are visible: how it differs from the general claim that introspection is unreliable, from confabulation, from the introspection illusion, and from ordinary cherry-picking; whether the flip must occur within a single reasoner or can be distributed across a literature; and whether a report can be evidence about mechanism at all, or only about content. I won't be satisfied with a bare label — the carving is the point.

Finally, tell me what would change your verdict: if you decide nothing established fits and you coin a term, what would you have to find to conclude the phenomenon was really just an instance of something already known, and where is your reasoning most likely to be wrong?
