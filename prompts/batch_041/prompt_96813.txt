Until last winter I would have described myself as a physicalist without hesitation. My father was then diagnosed with early-onset dementia, and over the months since I have watched a person I know turn, by degrees, and without any line I could point to, into someone else — the lights on the whole way down. That experience, more than any argument, moved me toward the view that experience is fundamental rather than produced: that there are micro-experiences all the way down and that the one I am having now is somehow made of them. I can accept both ends of that picture. What I cannot get past is the combination problem — how the many micro-experiences become the single experience I am having — and I would like an honest assessment of the going answers rather than a survey of them.

Sort the five responses below into tiers, from the ones you would actually stake the view on at the top to the ones that merely restate the problem at the bottom, and give one sentence for each explaining its placement. (a) Positing a further fundamental relation whose entire job is to bind micro-subjects into a single subject. (b) Holding that the micro-level is not experiential at all but has proto-experiential features that jointly yield experience. (c) Holding that only qualities are fundamental and subjects are not, so that the binding must produce a subject rather than merely collect qualities. (d) Holding that there is one cosmic subject and that the real puzzle is how it divides into you and me. (e) Holding that there is no genuine unity to be explained and that the problem is an artifact of how we describe ourselves.

The ranking should not be on general plausibility. I want it on how each response fares in the reverse direction of the problem, which is what my father's case has made concrete for me: if micro-subjects genuinely bind into one subject, then as the binding machinery degrades piece by piece, what becomes of the subject? Does one subject become two, or half of one, or must something switch off all at once? For each of the five, state what it is committed to saying about that transition, whether the commitment is coherent, and where that lands it in the tiers. If you think one of them cannot survive the question at all, say so and put it at the bottom.
