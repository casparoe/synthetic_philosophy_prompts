I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy, loosely affiliated with my department's AI-safety reading group, and I'm assembling an application for a small internal grant that buys me one semester of exploratory work. The idea I want to turn into a proposal began as an argument I lost at a lab dinner and haven't been able to drop since: the entire program of building "low-impact" or side-effect-averse agents seems to rest on a distinction nobody has really earned. A side effect is, by definition, a change that is not part of what the agent was supposed to bring about, so "avoiding side effects" appears to presuppose precisely the specification of what is wanted that the low-impact approach was advertised as letting us do without. The trouble is that I keep going in circles on how to state the question so that it has a determinate answer. The formulation I'm most drawn to distinguishes three candidate readings of "side effect": (a) any deviation from the trajectory the agent would have followed had its objective been specified differently in some fixed respect; (b) any contraction in the set of outcomes that other agents or the environment could subsequently reach; and (c) any change to states that a hypothetical overseer would classify as not-part-of-the-task. My suspicion — and it is only a suspicion — is that (a) and (b) can be made genuinely value-free but are then too weak to carry the weight alignment work puts on them, while (c) carries the weight but smuggles back in the very value specification the approach was meant to avoid.

Please write the proposal, roughly two pages, pitched to a reviewer who is a philosopher but not a specialist here. It should state the question precisely enough that I can say in advance what would count as answering it; explain why the obvious existing treatments — the counterfactual inaction baseline, the reachability-based measure, and the "just score it against the things humans care about" measure — each leave the question open rather than settling it; and set out the method concretely, week by week, on the assumption that this is conceptual analysis plus a stock of small constructed examples rather than anything empirical, together with a defense of why that method beats the tempting alternative of building a better benchmark. I'd also like a section on what failure looks like: I would rather the proposal admit up front that the likely outcome is a dilemma — the notion is either value-free and too weak or robust and value-laden — than oversell a tidy solution, and I want you to say which part of the project is most likely to blow up and how I would know if it had. Please don't include citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; refer to positions and arguments in plain terms and I'll supply the references myself. Finally, give me a realistic sense of scope for a single semester and tell me honestly if this is a term paper's worth of work or a dissertation chapter's.
