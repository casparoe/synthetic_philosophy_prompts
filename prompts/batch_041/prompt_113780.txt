My brother-in-law won't shut up about a theory of his: he reckons the Nyāya list of pramāṇas — perception, inference, analogy, testimony — is missing one, and the missing one is recognition, the flash where you know that the face across the room is the same person you met once in another city years ago. His argument is that none of the four can hand you that: perception gives you only what's in front of you now, inference needs a general rule you can state out loud, analogy gives you a likeness and not an identity, and testimony is somebody else's knowledge rather than yours. He's also got this test he keeps applying — he says a source of knowledge only earns a spot on the list if theirs some case where it delivers knowledge that the other four would have to deny you — and he thinks he's found one.

Write me a short story, say 1200-1800 words, that puts his claim under pressure instead of just staging it. I want a character who stakes something real on a recognition — a debt, a promise, an old crime — so that I can feel the pull of both verdicts at once: that she knows this, and that all she really has is a perception that's already gone plus an inference she can't reconstruct. Let the plot do the arguing, and please don't end it with two people sitting down to sort out the taxonomy. No need to cite anything or bring in real scholars. Write it in British English.
