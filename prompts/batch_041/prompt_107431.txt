I'm a third-year PhD student working on cultural evolution, and I keep encountering the same move in my reading group. A colleague in my cohort argues roughly as follows: a meme is to culture what a gene is to biology. Ideas are replicators; they vary, they are transmitted with some degree of fidelity, and they are differentially copied, so cultural change simply is Darwinian selection operating on a second kind of replicator. He then uses this to license a fairly strong claim: that we can explain the persistence of a belief or a practice without any appeal to whether it is true or useful, because what survives is whatever copies best, and that the units of culture consequently have interests of their own that can come apart from ours. He will cheerfully say that a bad argument can outcompete a good one for purely Darwinian reasons, much as a virulent strain can outcompete a mild one, and that asking whether the surviving idea is warranted is a category mistake.

I would like to audit the analogy rather than wave at it. Begin by laying out the correspondence in continuous prose: what plays the role of the replicator, the phenotype, the environment, fitness, mutation, drift, and selection, and say which of those roles the cultural case fills cleanly and which it fills only by stipulation. Then tell me where the mapping breaks. I have in mind the absence of any clean genotype/phenotype distinction in culture, the fact that cultural transmission is often reconstructive and Lamarckian rather than replicative, the presence of guided variation and of a designer inside the system, and the suspicion that a meme's fitness depends on precisely the psychological facts the Darwinian story is supposed to explain rather than presuppose. Separate the disanalogies that actually threaten his conclusion from the ones that are merely colorful. Finally, tell me whether the strong claim survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was quietly carrying the conclusion the whole time, and if the latter, sketch a better analogy for what cultural evolution is really doing.

Please write this as continuous prose, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables, in the manner of a long and careful email to a colleague.
