So I've got this friend — met her at a LessWrong meetup years ago, she's the type who rereads the Sequences for fun and has opinions about decision theory she'll share whether or not you ask — and we've been working through classic paradoxes together over the last few months. Her verdicts have been weirdly consistent, and I want to spring a new case on her, partly to settle a bet and partly because I'm fairly sure she's running a rule she hasn't noticed she's running.

Her calls so far:

- Two envelopes, both sealed, one contains twice the other, you pick one and can switch blind: don't switch. She says the "always switch" argument is a con — it assumes the other envelope is 50/50 half-or-double, which quietly assumes a flat prior over all possible amounts, and there's no such prior.
- Same setup but you open your envelope and see the amount, and you're told absolutely nothing about how the amounts were chosen: still don't switch, same reason, and she's definately very firm on this one.
- Pascal's mugging: don't pay. The giant-utility-tail is smuggled in on a prior she doesn't think is legitimate, and she won't do it.
- St. Petersburg: she'd pay a small fee to play, but she's adamant the "infinite expected value" story is a con and she would not pay a big fee.
- Newcomb: one-boxer, take the one box.

New case. You open your envelope and this time you see $20, and — crucially — you ARE told exactly how the pair was generated: a fair coin was flipped; heads put {10, 20} in the envelopes, tails put {20, 40}, then they were shuffled and you picked one at random. That's the entire mechanism, a fully proper prior, no sleight of hand.

So: what does she say? Does she switch? I want you to (a) predict her verdict, (b) lay out the candidate rules that fit her earlier calls — I think at least two do, one being "I only ever objected to improper priors, give me a proper one and I'll happily do the EV math," the other being something like "in any two-envelope-shaped setup, don't switch, the whole thing is a shell game" — and say what each predicts for the $20 case, (c) tell me which you'd bet on and why, and (d) name the single further case that would actually settle which rule she's got, i.e. a case where the two rules come apart and her answer would be diagnostic.

Also — and this is the part I really care about — is there a chance she's just incoherent? The no-switch stuff and the Newcomb one-boxing might not be the same underlying rule at all, and I'd like you to say so plainly if the earlier verdicts don't hang together as well as they look like they do.
