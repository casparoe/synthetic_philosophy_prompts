I write a science-and-ideas column, and for the next one I want a parody product review -- think Wirecutter but grumpier -- of a startup's app that promises to "reverse-engineer" any biological or behavioral trait and tell you the selection pressure that built it. I spent a week with it: male nipples, the human chin, the gaps between the arches in a cathedral, my own compulsion to file this column on a Friday. Confident adaptive story every single time. Can you write the review, 600 words or so, deadpan and getting steadily more tired? The jokes need to come out of the real adaptationism fight rather than generic tech-review snark: why "it's useful" doesn't mean "it was selected for," what a byproduct/spandrel is and how it differs from an exaptation, why these narratives are so hard to falsify, and what actually reverse-engineering a trait would take. One gag I'd love: the settings menu offers only strong selection, very strong selection, and sexual selection, with drift and developmental constraint grayed out. A brief Pangloss cameo welcome. No citations or reading lists, please -- I dont want it reading like a lit review.
