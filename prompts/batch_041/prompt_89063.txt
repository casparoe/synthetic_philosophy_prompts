But I think there's an actual tension worth taking seriously, namely: Schopenhauer says the other arts copy the Ideas, and the Ideas are the immediate objectifications of the will, so painting and poetry are one step removed from the will and music is supposedly not removed at all — it's a copy of the will itself. My problem is that on his own account the will is one, indivisible, and outside the forms of representation, including space and time and therefore plurality. So what is it for a temporal, articulated, plural thing like a piece of music to copy something that has none of those features? And when he gets concrete and says melody is like the life of the will, moving from desire through satisfaction to new desire, isn't he smuggling in phenomenal temporality and individuation that the will is precisely supposed to lack? Also the harmony/bass stuff seems to map onto grades of objectification, which are Idea-level structures, so now I can't tell whether music is supposed to be will-level or Idea-level or somehow both.
