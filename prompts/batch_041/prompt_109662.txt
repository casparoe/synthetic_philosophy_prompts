Take a strengthened ring of Gyges: its wearer is undetectable, so no legal, reputational, or social cost can attach to anything done while invisible, and it also erases the wearer's memory of those acts, so no self-image cost attaches either. What is your credence that a randomly chosen adult in a modern liberal democracy, wearing this ring for a year, would commit at least one act that they would themselves classify as seriously unjust if it were described to them cold?

Decompose the number rather than asserting it. Roughly how much of ordinary compliance with justice rests on (a) expected detection and sanction, (b) self-image and self-consistency, (c) direct concern for others, and (d) internalized habit that survives even when its usual supports are removed? Say which of these the ring actually strips away, whether it plausibly changes what the wearer wants (so that the counterfactual agent is no longer quite the same person), and how much rides on where one sets the threshold for "seriously unjust." Give a figure for each term and show how they combine — don't force a product if the terms aren't independent — then name the term the final number is most sensitive to, together with the evidence or thought experiment that would move it most. My own credence is 0.7; a friend's is 0.25. Tell us where that disagreement most likely lives.
