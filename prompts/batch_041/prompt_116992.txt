My cousin is finishing a doctorate in philosophy, and for the past few months she has let me bring her cases to argue about. I am a high school student; the practice began as preparation for our school's ethics competition, but I have kept it up because I find it genuinely absorbing. She is scrupulous about consistency, so I have been recording her verdicts on questions about bringing people into existence. Here is what I have so far.

— A couple learns that any child they conceive will live in continuous, severe pain and will die before school age. Her verdict: it would be wrong to conceive, and such a child would have been harmed by being brought into existence at all.

— A couple who would have raised happy, healthy children decide instead to remain childless and to spend the money on travel. Her verdict: nothing wrong has been done; no one has been wronged; and she will not say that the world is the poorer for it or that some possible person was deprived of anything, since there was no one there to be deprived.

— A woman who has had a good life says that she is glad to have been born. Her verdict: she may say that, but it does not establish that her existence was a benefit to her; had she never existed, that would have been no loss to her.

— A surgeon must put a patient through several weeks of intense pain in order to save her life. Her verdict: permissible. The pain is a real harm done to someone, but the patient's life is worth saving, and the harm is justified by it.

Next week I intend to put this case to her. A couple must decide whether to conceive now or to wait. A child conceived now will inherit a condition that will cause her a stretch of intense pain in her twenties, unresponsive to treatment, from which she will recover fully; for the rest of her life she will be healthy, will have close friends and work she loves, and will say at forty, if asked, that she is glad to have been born. If the couple waits two years, they will conceive a different child, who will not have the condition. They would love either child. She has never told me which general position she holds; all I have are her verdicts.

Tell me what she will say about the couple who conceive now, and whether she will say the child has been harmed by being conceived. I want the reasoning more than the verdict. Name the rule, or the several rules, that fit her record so far; say what each of them predicts about this new case; tell me which rule you think she is actually running, and why you prefer it to the others; and give me the further case that would best expose which rule she is running, that is, the case on which the rules that fit her record diverge most sharply.
