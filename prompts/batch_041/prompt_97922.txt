I'm a novelist, about two-thirds through a book about a mayor who does something genuinely awful to keep her city from tearing itself apart, and I've developed a bad case of second thoughts about the moral spine of the whole thing. So when two friends got into it at dinner about basically my plot, I leaned in. Then I couldn't referee to save my life, so I'm handing it to you.

The setup was this. Maya said: if it was the right call, then he's got nothing to feel guilty about — guilt is a verdict, it says *you did wrong*, so you can't both stand by the decision and carry the guilt. If he really feels it, some part of him knows it wasn't right. And guilt isn't a costume you put on for the voters.

Dev said that's the fantasy of clean hands. The whole reason we call them dirty is that you can be right to do it and still be stained by it. A man who can order a thing like that and sleep like a baby is exactly the one you should be scared of.

Maya came back: then "right" is doing two jobs at once. Either it was justified, in which case the guilt is just irrational residue he ought to work through, or it wasn't, in which case he's a criminal with good PR. "It was right, but he should still feel guilty" is trying to bank the moral credit both ways.

Dev: no, it's that some goods can't be weighed on one scale. He traded one value for another, and the guilt isn't a verdict that he chose wrong — it's the debt left over from the thing he sacrificed. You don't get to make a sacrifice costless just because the other option was worse.

Maya: fine, but then the debt is owed to who? The people he stepped on are dead or ruined. Guilt that helps nobody and changes nothing is self-indulgence — worse, it's a way of feeling sensitive while keeping the profits.

Dev: and a politician who feels nothing will do it again the first time it's convenient. The guilt is the only thing standing between dirty hands and no hands at all.

Maya: now you've admitted it's instrumental. That's a reason to *want* him to feel it, not a reason it's *warranted*. Those are seperate questions and you keep sliding between them.

That's where the table left it and where I'm stuck. Go through it move by move — who's right at each step, not just who wins on points — and tell me what both of them are missing, because I suspect its something about what kind of thing guilt even is.
