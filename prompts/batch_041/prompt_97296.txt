The standard story is that you can throw out any strategy that's strictly dominated — by another pure strategy, or by a *mixture* of them, which is the part that always felt like a magic trick to me — and then you do it again on the smaller game, and again, on the grounds that it's common knowledge that everyone is rational and everyone knows everyone knows it, etc. Fine. But two things bother me. One, when the dominance is only *weak* rather than strict, the order you delete in can change what survives — I'm fairly sure I've seen a case where going in a bad order deletes the whole strategy set. Two, even in the strict case, the jump from "everyone is rational and knows it" to "nobody plays a deleted strategy" seems to smuggle in something extra, and I can never tell whether that's a real epistemic gap or just me being sloppy.

Here's what I actually want. Give each a rough probabilty and a line of justification, say how they combine into your final number, and tell me which term I should be pushing on and what would actually move it.
