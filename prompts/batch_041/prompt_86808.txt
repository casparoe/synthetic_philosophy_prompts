I run a monthly philosophy reading group for graduate students from other departments — mostly scientists and engineers, none of them trained in metaphysics — and next month's session, seventy-five minutes, is on the identity of indiscernibles. Don't explain the principle to me; plan the session.

I want the dependency order, with reasons: which pieces must be in place before the question even makes sense to them, and what each later piece leans on. My worry is that they will hear the whole thing as a question about whether you could tell the two spheres apart, so tell me where in the sequence that reading has to be killed and what has to precede it for the kill to work.

For the carrying example, I've decided to stipulate my own pair of cases rather than lean on the stock one. Case A: the familiar empty world containing just two iron spheres, alike in diameter, colour, temperature and everything else. Case B: a world in which one of the spheres is a perfect duplicate struck from the other and both persist side by side. Case B is the lever I want the session to turn on, because the causal history discerns the pair — so I want the plan to show how the verdict flips with the property vocabulary we allow ourselves, and thereby to relocate the question from how many things there are to what counts as a property. Tell me what the carrying example must have and must lack for that to land.

Then the misconception map: the conflation of this principle with the indiscernibility of identicals; the epistemic reading; the assumption that spatial location settles the matter; and the triviality trap, where they conclude that two things always differ because each has the property of being itself. Give me the order they will arise and the exact point in the sequence to head each one off.

Finish with one question I can put to the group at the end that would show whether they've got the question rather than just followed the cases, and tell me what to leave out at this level — and say so out loud to them — including the quantum-particles application and the apparatus of haecceities, with your reasons. No readings.
