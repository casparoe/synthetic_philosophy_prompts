Here's roughly what he said:

"Superrationality is Hofstadter's fix for the prisoners dilemma. The dilemma only exists because each player asks what's best for me, and the answer is always defect. But if you ask instead what should a player in my exact situation do, the answer has to be the same for both of us, so you both cooperate — it's basically Kant's categorical imperative dressed up in game theory. Which is why it only bites in the one-shot case: once the game repeats you dont need any of this, because tit-for-tat is the equilibrium of the repeated game and cooperation becomes rational in the ordinary sense. So superrationality is really a footnote — it tells you what to do when you'll never see the other guy again. And the other thing people forget is that it doesn't even apply to real players, since it only works against a perfect reasoner who's guaranteed to land on the same conclusion as you, and that basically never happens, so it's a thought experiment with no teeth."
