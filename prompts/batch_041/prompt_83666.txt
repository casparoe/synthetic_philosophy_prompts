Last week I lost an argument to a friend — a management consultant who has lately taken up the neuroscience of consciousness — and I have not been able to reconstruct a reply to it since. His claim was that the internal communications protocol he has installed at his firm has made the firm itself conscious. The protocol, as he described it, forces every team's output into a single shared channel of deliberately limited capacity, holds whatever wins the resulting competition in that channel long enough for the firm to report on it, and broadcasts it back to every team, whose subsequent behaviour is steered by it. This, he insisted, is exactly what global workspace theory says consciousness is: a limited-capacity workspace in which a coalition of processors wins out and its content is made globally available, which is why the contents we are conscious of are the ones we can report, remember and act on. His firm satisfies every clause of that description; its employees are the unconscious modules that feed the workspace; and the firm therefore has something it is like to be, which is why its rivals do not.

I would like the piece of satire I was too slow to write at the table. Please write the text of a certification issued by a fictitious accreditation body, the Bureau of Broadcast Sufficiency, which audits organisations for consciousness under a standard of its own invention, the Broadcast Sufficiency Standard. Keep it deadpan, in the register of a standards document or a set of terms of service, and keep the comedy inside the theory: someone who works on consciousness should find the clauses fair rather than see the objections stated outright. The piece must be exactly five numbered clauses and nothing else — no title, no preamble, no closing note — with each clause one or two sentences. Across the five, let the standard quietly expose what my friend's criteria leave out: that global availability is a claim about access rather than about experience; that an audience for the broadcast is either a homunculus or else the true seat of the consciousness being certified; that reportability is at best evidence of experience and not the thing itself; and whatever else you can make land in the space of five clauses. Do not mention any actual researcher, laboratory, institution, or publication.
