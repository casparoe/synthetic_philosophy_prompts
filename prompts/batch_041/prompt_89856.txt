For two years a reading group I run on free will has been deadlocked between two of its members, and I have lost the ability to tell whether they are even disagreeing about the same thing. One of them holds that determinism and moral responsibility are compatible, because what matters is that an action issue in the right way from the agent's own reasons and character and that she be responsive to reasons in the relevant sense, so the truth or falsity of determinism is beside the point. The other holds that this is a dodge: if the whole causal history of the action was already fixed before the agent was born, then she is not the ultimate source of what she does, and no amount of reasons-responsiveness repairs that, since responsibility of the blaming kind requires that she could have done otherwise in a sense that the compatibilist's conditional reading of that phrase does not capture. They have read the same papers and the same cases; the first always replies that this is just the ultimacy intuition again, and the second always replies that this is just changing the subject. Neither has moved in two years, and each thinks the other's counterexamples are question-begging.

My own suspicion, which I would like you to test rather than assume, is that this is not one disagreement but two questions wearing a single name: a metaphysical question about the actual causal sequence that produced an action, and a normative question about what licenses the reactive attitudes of blame and resentment. If that is right, then the apparent counterexamples each side marshals may be doing no work at all, because each is addressed to the question the other is not asking. But I am not confident, and I want to know whether the residual disagreement is instead a genuine clash of bedrock starting intuitions that no argument can touch, whether a hidden formal result about the relation between determinism and sourcehood is quietly doing the settling, or whether the whole dispute is ill-posed and progress should not be expected.

Give me your diagnosis as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion. I want the kind of disagreement you take this to be and the specific claims on each side that support that reading; whether any empirical or formal question is doing hidden work; whether the impasse is a sign the question is malformed or merely that it is hard; and, as the fifth point, what if anything would count as progress given your diagnosis.
