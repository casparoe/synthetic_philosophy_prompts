I am in my final year of secondary school and am writing an extended essay in philosophy of mind. In our reading group we have been trying to understand why the project of naturalising mental content has never really settled, despite the fact that the main proposals have been on the table for decades. The two families of view I keep returning to are informational or indicator semantics, on the one hand, and teleosemantics, on the other. Informational semantics treats a state as representing whatever it reliably carries information about, so content is a matter of causal covariance; teleosemantics treats content as fixed by the biological function or proper function of the state within a system, so content is a matter of what the state was selected to do. Both are meant to be naturalistic, both accept roughly the same range of examples, and yet they seem to give different answers to the same questions.

The dispute has stalled in a way I find frustrating. Consider misrepresentation: informational semantics seems to make it hard to say how a state can be about something it does not reliably covary with, while teleosemantics seems to make it hard to say why a selected mechanism could not be systematically misleading. Consider the disjunction problem: if a state is triggered by both F and G, what makes it represent F rather than the disjunction? Consider Swampman: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate with no evolutionary history seems to have all the same inner states but no proper functions. Each side has a well-known reply, but the replies seem to satisfy only those who already accept that side's starting point. A classmate has proposed what she calls consumer teleosemantics: content is fixed neither by what the producer state covaries with nor by what it was selected to indicate, but by what the consumer mechanism was selected to treat as the condition for its own successful use of the signal. I cannot tell whether that is a genuine third position, a restatement of functionalism, or simply a redescription of ordinary teleosemantics.

I would like a diagnosis rather than a survey or a verdict on which side wins. Is this a substantive disagreement about the nature of representation, a verbal disagreement about how to use the term content, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? Is there a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work, perhaps about the possibility of a purely causal theory of normativity, or about whether selection history is doing any explanatory work that current causal structure cannot do? If the impasse is genuine, what would count as progress: a distinction, a counterexample, a formal result, a change of question, or an argument that no progress should be expected because the question is ill-posed? Please make the diagnosis concrete by engaging with what each side actually says about misrepresentation and the disjunction problem, and please answer in British English.
