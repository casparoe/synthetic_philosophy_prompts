I teach the second half of a large introductory ethics course, and I would like a single handout that does the work of a lecture on the utility monster: the case Nozick introduced in Anarchy, State, and Utopia (1974), in which a being is so constituted that the welfare it gains from any marginal resource vastly exceeds what others lose by its taking that resource, so that an unqualified sum-of-welfare morality appears to direct us to sacrifice everyone else to it. My students arrive already knowing the punchline, that utilitarians have to bite the bullet, and consequently learn nothing. I want the handout to sort them instead, by their own answers to concrete questions, into the positions people actually hold about the case, including the ones that have no popular name.

Please write me a decision tree. Requirements:

1. A single entry point, and the first node must be answerable by a student who has never heard of utilitarianism, consequentialism, or Nozick. Every node must be a concrete case or a claim about the case; no node may ask the reader to endorse a theory by name (nothing like "do you think welfare should be summed?"), and no node may presuppose familiarity with any of the literature.

2. Between ten and fourteen nodes and between eight and twelve leaves. Each leaf carries (a) a label, using a standard name where one exists and a descriptive one otherwise, (b) one sentence stating the commitment that distinguishes it, and (c) one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

3. The tree must route through at least the following cases, each doing real work rather than decorating the diagram:

- the monster as an unremarkable neighbour rather than a science-fiction creature, so that one can ask whether the reader's verdict survives the loss of the exotic framing;
- a case with no single monster but a very large number of ordinary people each gaining a small amount, with the same total, so that one can ask whether the objection attaches to monstrosity or to aggregation;
- a monster with a bounded welfare function, which satiates and gains nothing from further resources, so that one can ask whether the reader's verdict rests on a contingent fact about psychology;
- a continuous dial running from the monster's advantage at ten thousand to one down to one-point-one to one, asking where, if anywhere, the reader's verdict flips, and thereby whether the reader's position is lexical, threshold-based, or gradual;
- a monster whose vastly greater welfare-capacity follows from its being a fast-running digital mind rather than a biological fantasy, so that one can ask whether the case remains a fantasy at all;
- the suggestion that the monster is a mirror, that the relation the case describes already obtains between us and some existing class of beings, so that the reader's horror is bad faith.

4. The leaves must include at least the following: bullet-biting; a concave or prioritarian value function; lexical or threshold priority for persons; the separateness-of-persons or otherwise non-aggregative response; the claim that the case is not well formed because welfare admits no ratio scale; the claim that it is well formed but false of any actual creature; graded moral status, on which the monster simply has more of whatever grounds status; incommensurability, on which there is no common currency in which the monster's gain could outweigh the others' loss; the mirror reading; and one leaf for a position your tree can reach but that you believe nobody actually occupies.

5. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and say why the case at that node fails to distinguish them.

6. Mark where a leaf survives only on one understanding of welfare (hedonistic, desire-based, objective-list), and note which leaves can be rebuilt against any currency, since a monster can be tailored to any theory that aggregates something.

7. Close with a short section on the answer-combinations the tree permits that nobody occupies, diagnosing each as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

8. Do not attribute a view to a named philosopher unless you are confident of the attribution; an unattributed label is better than a wrong one. I want no citations and no reading list.

Finally, tell me in two or three sentences which node you take to be load-bearing, the one where the disagreement really turns, and whether my tree has anywhere forced a genuine disagreement into a merely verbal one.
