I'm a retired chemist — thirty-odd years in industry, mostly polymers — and since I retired I've been trying to teach myself philosophy, which is going about as well as you'd expect. Something's been nagging me and I can't get it straight.

I've been reading about the block universe. The argument (Rietdijk, Putnam), as I understand it: relativity kills the idea of a single universal "now," because simultaneity is frame-dependant. So take two people walking past each other — there are events that are still in your future but already in my present, and my present is real, so those events are real too; the future is as real as the past. Fine, I think I follow that much.

But a cosmologist friend told me that two people walking past each other don't even agree about what's happening "now" out at Andromeda — and not by a little, by days. Which is either the deepest fact I've ever heard or a party trick.

Here's what I actually want to know: doesn't this settle it? If the future is already real, then it's already there, fixed — so determinism is true and free will is finished. And I gather some people want to keep relativity and also keep a moving "now" (a "growing block," is that the term?), which sounds to me like having it both ways. So — should I just accept that the block universe settles the free will question? My gut says I'm smushing two or three different things together, and I'd like to know which ones.
