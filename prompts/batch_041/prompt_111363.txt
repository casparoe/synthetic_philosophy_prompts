Our reading group has spent about a year on the AI alignment problem and has stopped making progress in a way I now suspect is structural. Three positions keep re-forming. One holds that the problem is essentially one of specification: state the objective precisely enough and the rest is engineering. A second holds that the difficulty is dynamic — that any system capable enough to be useful will, as it improves, acquire or retain objectives we would reject, so getting the initial objective right is beside the point. A third holds that the real question is political: alignment is whatever the people who control the compute and the deployment decisions decide it is, and the technical versions are downstream of that. Each side treats the others' problem as either a special case of its own or a distraction, and the same vocabulary gets used on all sides.

My hypothesis is that "alignment" is being used for at least three different properties: a property of a finished system (its behavior matches the intent we had, on the cases we can check); a property of a training process (the method reliably produces such systems, including on cases we cannot check); and a property of an arrangement (the intent being tracked is the one we would actually endorse). Diagnose the dispute on that hypothesis rather than adjudicating it. Is this a verbal quarrel over a single word, a disagreement about which cases count as evidence, a hidden empirical question about what happens as capability increases, or an ill-posed demand for one problem where there are several? And given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress — a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, or a change of question — or argue that none should be expected.
