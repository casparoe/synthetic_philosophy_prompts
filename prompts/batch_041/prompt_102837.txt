A few of us have been running a reading group on Hofstadter's strange-loop writings, and two of the regulars have been circling each other for weeks over the careenium thought experiment. Let me fix the setup so we are all talking about the same thing: the careenium is a vast arena in which enormous numbers of tiny, simple entities — simmballs — jostle and flip among a handful of local states according to fixed micro-rules. Nothing in the arena "knows" anything; each simmball responds only to its immediate neighbours. Yet because of how the simmballs are arranged and interact, large, stable configurations of them can be read, from a higher vantage point, as a system of symbols: certain configurations stand for things, and one symbolic configuration reliably gives rise to another, which in turn rearranges the underlying simmballs. The question that has split the group is whether it is ever literally true that the symbols push the simmballs around. One of my friends is a committed substrate purist: all the pushing is done by simmballs on simmballs, the symbolic story is a convenient description and nothing more, and any sentence in which a symbol is the subject of a causal verb is at best a useful shorthand and at worst a category mistake. The other is an equally committed pattern realist: the higher-level patterns have genuine causal potency, and saying that a symbol caused a rearrangement of the substrate can be straightforwardly true — not a figure of speech, and not a mere re-description of the micro-story. They have been talking past each other for weeks, and I would like the document they should have produced instead.

Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the substrate purist and the pattern realist. I want, first, a numbered list of agreed claims: what both sides can sign about the setup as described, about what would even count as a symbol's pushing the substrate around, about how the two levels are related, about which of each side's formulations the other is willing to grant as innocent, and about what the dispute is plainly not about. Second, the precise points on which they still differ, each stated in wording that the side it favours would accept as its own position — so the purist would sign the sentence describing purism and the realist would sign the sentence describing realism, neither feeling that the other's phrasing has smuggled in a premise. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. I suspect one crux is whether "causation" is here being used in a difference-making sense or a productive-pushing sense, and another is whether the causal completeness of the simmball level forces the symbolic level out of the causal story or leaves it a place to stand; but I may have mislocated the real fault line, so tell me if so. Fourth, for each crux, a test, case, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about how it comes out written down in advance.

Finally, if any part of their quarrel turns out to be merely verbal — two ways of talking rather than two theses that could diverge in what they predict or explain — say which part it is and why, and say plainly where the residual, non-verbal disagreement actually lives.
