I am writing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the philosophy of time, and I need one essay question on Gödel's rotating-universe argument for the ideality of time, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The exam is closed-book; students will have 90 minutes for this question and are expected to write about 800 words.

The question must not be answerable by reciting the familiar story that Gödel's 1949 solution to Einstein's field equations contains closed timelike curves, that these would permit time travel, and that Gödel therefore concluded that time is unreal. That story is both too easy and, as a reconstruction of Gödel's actual argument, misleading. What I want is a question that forces students to distinguish at least three things: first, the existence of closed timelike curves in the Gödel universe; second, the absence in that universe of a global, objective simultaneity relation or cosmic time; and third, the further idealist conclusion that there is no objective lapse of time or becoming. The question should require students to say which of these does the real work in Gödel's argument, to reconstruct the argument as a valid or near-valid piece of reasoning with explicit premises, and to identify the premise that a critic should attack.

The question should also make students confront the following specific issue: Gödel's argument is sometimes said to tell against the A-theory of time, according to which there is an objective present and an objective passage from future to past, rather than against the reality of time as such. Students should be asked to assess whether the argument, properly reconstructed, tells against the A-theory only, or whether it has force against any view that treats time as a fundamental feature of the world. They should also be asked to consider whether the empirical question of whether our universe is rotating matters to the philosophical conclusion, and if so, how.

Finally, the question should ask students to evaluate one important objection: that even if there is no global simultaneity relation in a Gödel universe, there could still be a local, observer-relative lapse of time, and that this is all the objectivity that time requires. A good answer should say whether this objection succeeds, and why or why not.

Please write:

1. The exam question itself, phrased as a single essay prompt with clear sub-questions.

2. A model answer of about 800 words that would receive full marks. The model answer should be self-contained, should not rely on quotations from Gödel or from secondary literature, and should make the argument's structure explicit.

3. A marking scheme out of 20 marks. The marking scheme should name five or six specific must-hit points, assign marks to each, and for each point give a one-line description of the mistake that would lose marks. It should also distinguish an A answer from a B answer in terms of the depth of the reconstruction and the handling of the objection.

Do not include any bibliographic references or reading lists. The model answer should be written as continuous prose suitable for a student to emulate. The marking scheme can use bullet points.
