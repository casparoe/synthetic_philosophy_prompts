I have been trying to reconstruct an objection to Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, and the reconstruction has turned into something stronger than I intended: an argument that the methodology cannot perform the task Lakatos designed it for. I would like to know which step to give up and what giving it up costs.

Here is the argument.

(1) On Lakatos's account, a research programme is appraised as progressive or degenerating by reference to its problem shifts. A shift is theoretically progressive if it predicts some novel fact; it is empirically progressive if some of those novel predictions are corroborated. A programme that merely accommodates the facts it was built to accommodate, without generating corroborated novel predictions, is degenerating.

(2) The negative heuristic of a programme forbids directing modus tollens at its hard core; recalcitrant results are absorbed instead by the protective belt.

(3) Hence for any programme and any apparently refuting observation, there is some modification of the belt that accommodates the observation while leaving the core intact.

(4) Nothing in the idea of a belt-modification restricts it to a single adjustment. A defender is free to add, in one move, whatever auxiliary hypotheses are needed both to secure the accommodation and to entail some further prediction.

(5) So for any programme and any refutation, there exists a belt-modification that accommodates the refutation and yields a novel prediction.

(6) Therefore for any programme there exists a modification of it that renders it theoretically progressive, and, if the novel prediction is subsequently corroborated, empirically progressive as well.

(7) Whether a programme is progressive or degenerating is therefore a matter of which modifications its defenders happen to choose, rather than a property the programme bears independently of those choices.

(8) And since no programme can be shown to be degenerating -- whatever the evidence, some progressive-seeming modification is available -- Lakatos's criterion cannot discriminate rational science from pseudoscience, or fruitful research from a degenerating enterprise.

(9) A methodology of science that cannot draw that distinction fails at its own task and collapses into the anarchism it was meant to answer.

I am willing to give up (9), since I do not think Lakatos promised a decision procedure. What I would rather not give up is (1): if (1) goes, I no longer see what the framework is for. Tell me which premise or step to reject, why it looked plausible in the first place, and what the remaining options cost. I would also like to know whether the argument is invalid or merely expensive to resist -- that is, whether there is a step I can simply deny, or whether every exit requires surrendering something I would prefer to keep.
