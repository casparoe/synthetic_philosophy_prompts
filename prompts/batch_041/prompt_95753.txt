I'm a journalist who writes about science and ideas, and I'm at work on an essay that reads Miloš Forman's Amadeus through Schopenhauer's philosophy of music rather than the customary God-and-grace angle. Schopenhauer's arresting thesis is that music is the exception among the arts: where the others render the Ideas, music copies the will itself, which is why it moves us without concepts and why he can imagine it subsisting even if the world did not. I want the film read against that thesis with the friction left in — above all the awkwardness that Schopenhauer treats art as a release from willing while describing music as a copy of the very thing we are meant to be released from. Give me the reading as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Have them take up, in this order: Salieri's experience of Mozart's music as the will's own voice rather than a picture of anything; the gap between the will-less genius Schopenhauer describes and the coarse, embodied Mozart the film shows, and whether Salieri's mistake is to confuse the vessel with the capacity; whether the God Salieri bargains with does the work of Schopenhauer's blind will or competes with it, and what the film gains and loses by moralizing the source of the music; the one scene that most resists the reading, where the film insists on biography, authorship, and personal desert in a way the philosophy dissolves; and finally what, if anything, the film adds to the debate that Schopenhauer's text cannot supply. Keep it grounded in scenes and choices a reader who has seen the film will recognize, and don't lean on quotations.
