My old PhD supervisor is a behavioral geneticist and she's been marking up drafts with the same red pen for thirty years, so I'm fairly confident I can call her verdicts before she writes them. Her record: wrong to say that because height is about 80% heritable the 20th-century rise in average height must have been genetic. Wrong to say that because a reading test is 70% heritable, teaching kids to read better can't shift the average. Right — not a trick, she insists — that how much parents read to their kids is itself heritable, since a child's temperament pulls different parenting out of the same two parents. Right that the heritability of a vocabulary measure is higher in well-off familes than poor ones, because up there there's less environmental spread for the genes to compete with. And wrong to say that a trait being 90% heritable means it must come down to a handful of genes.

This week's draft argues: the heritability of a school-readiness measure is lower among poor kids than rich kids, so poor kids' scores are more responsive to environment, so a preschool program should buy more there than in affluent districts. Does she sign off? What rule is she actually running, what other rules fit all five verdicts just as well, what does each predict here, which would you bet on and why, and give me one further case that would separate them.
