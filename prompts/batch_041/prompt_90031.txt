Rough shape of what I'm after, so you dont drift. It has to fix what the connectives mean well enough to explain why disjunction and existence behave as they do -- a proof of A or B has to be a proof of one of them, not merely a proof that one of them is true -- and it has to explain that, not just permit it. It has to say what a proof from an assumption is, since the BHK story is told for closed proofs and the introduction rule for the conditional takes you from an open derivation to a closed one. It has to distinguish canonical from non-canonical proofs without collapsing the distinction, because no mathematician ever writes down a canonical proof and that's exactly what normalisation is supposed to buy you. It mustn't be a finite truth-table job -- I'm fairly sure no finite matrix gives you intuitionistic propositional logic, so anything that amounts to one is dead on arrival -- and it mustn't be a three-valued 'some sentences are neither true nor false' story either, because that misdescribes the position: 'not refuted' has to come apart from 'proved' without becoming a third truth value. It has to respect the asymmetry with classical logic, so classical reasoning comes out right for the stable/decidable fragment and wrong in general, and the double-negation translation gets explained by the semantics rather than looking like a trick that happens to work. And it has to stay neutral on the questions the logic deliberately leaves open -- I don't want a semantics that quietly decides for or against the extra principles people bolt on (continuity, computability, that sort of thing) just because of the metatheory it's built in; relatedly I want a verdict on whether proving the completeness results classically is question-begging for an anti-realist or just harmless machinery.
