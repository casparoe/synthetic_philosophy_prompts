I run a small comparative-ethics reading group that is mostly analytic philosophers with no background in South Asian traditions, and we keep getting stuck on a disagreement I would like to turn into something the group can actually work through rather than merely assert. One member holds that karma is simply cosmic desert plus reincarnation and thinks the idea is therefore both familiar and obviously dubious; the rest of us suspect the phrase conceals several quite different views, at least one of which is more philosophically interesting than the slogan suggests. What I want is a decision tree for the group: a classification instrument that begins from the minimal commitment nearly everyone in the room will grant, that a person's circumstances are at least partly a fitting response to what that person has done, and fans out into the positions actually available on karma as a theory of moral desert. Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of schools, giving me first the diagnostic questions, then the combinations of answers they permit, then the positions located in that space, and phrase every branch node as a question a reader can answer without already knowing where it leads.

The distinctions I would like the tree to turn on are these: whether the fitting response requires a subject who is the same person at the moment of action and at the moment of response, which seems to be where the whole thing either holds together or falls apart; whether karma is an impersonal, law-like causal mechanism or the verdict of an agent who knows and judges; whether it must span lives or can operate within one, and whether the books can balance only if rebirth is real; whether the desert attaches to intention and character or to the act's objective quality, including ritual and bodily acts; whether the causal link is inexorable or can be interrupted, transferred, or discharged by something other than the original agent's own later action; and whether desert is meant retributively, so that the suffering is warranted, or merely causally, so that it is a result rather than a fitting punishment. I would like the leaves to include at least a theistic-verdict view, an impersonal-law view, a no-self causal view, an act- or ritual-based view, a merit-transfer view, and the deflationary reading on which karma is only a folk label for social and psychological feedback and carries no desert claim at all. Where a leaf has no defenders, mark it unnamed and tell me briefly whether the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored; where two leaves come out indistinguishable under your questions, say so rather than forcing a distinction.

The combination I personally find hardest is the one that keeps the causal chain real and morally loaded while denying any persisting subject who is the same person at the two ends of it; I would like the tree to make plain whether that is a genuine position or a contradiction, and if it is a position, which of my other branch questions has to be answered a particular way to keep it stable. Aim for five to seven branch nodes and eight to twelve leaves. Do not give me reading lists, citations, or the names of historical schools unless a name is genuinely doing work as a leaf; I care about the shape of the space, not the bibliography.
