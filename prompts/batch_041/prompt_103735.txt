I'm assembling a proposal for a one-semester MA thesis in philosophy of medicine, and I'd like you to write it. The topic I want to pursue is whether the biostatistical theory of disease — the naturalist view on which health is species-typical functional organization and a disease is an internal state that departs from it — can accommodate disorders of evolutionarily novel capacities. Reading and arithmetic are far too recent for selection to have shaped dedicated mechanisms, so on a strict construal there is no species-typical design for a reading capacity whose impairment could count as a dysfunction; yet specific learning disorder with impairment in reading is diagnosed, treated, and theorized as a disorder of precisely that capacity, and the same structure recurs for dyscalculia and, arguably, for conditions bound up with novel environments. My half-formed thought is that the naturalist's best response is to distinguish a mechanism's failing to perform the function it was selected for from a capacity's failing to perform a culturally supplied function that it is biologically underwritten to serve, and that once that distinction is drawn the novel-capacity cases stop threatening the account and instead force a choice between two versions of it that are habitually run together. I want the proposal to work out whether that thought survives contact with the cases, or whether they show that the naturalist cannot get a usable design-based notion of function for a capacity with no evolutionary history — in which case the terms of the whole naturalism/normativism dispute need renegotiating rather than one side winning.

Take a definite line on which way the argument should go instead of laying out the options neutrally, and give your reasons for taking it.
