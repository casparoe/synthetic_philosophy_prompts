ok so I'm trying to finish a short paper and I want you to be brutal about the argument. Here's the gist of it.

Fitch's paradox, the short version: if every truth is knowable, then every truth is known. Suppose there's some truth p that nobody actually knows. Then "p and nobody knows p" is itself true. If every truth is knowable, then it's possible for someone to know that conjunction. But knowing a conjunction means knowing each conjunct, so our knower would know that nobody knows p. Knowledge implies truth, so it would be true that nobody knows p -- and yet they just came to know p. Contradiction. So the knowability principle, together with two boring assumptions about knowledge (that it's factive, and that it distributes over "and"), hands you the absurd conclusion that there are no unknown truths.

Here's my move. The proof treats "knowable" as a property a proposition has, the way truth is a property it has. But when an anti-realist says a truth is knowable, I don't think they mean that some possible agent could occupy a mental state with that content. I think they mean the truth is recognizable -- there's some possible body of information that would settle the matter. Those two things come apart in exactly the case Fitch picks. A body of information that settles "p and nobody knows p" does not have to be a body of information that also registers the higher-order fact that whoever assembled it knows the first-order fact. The proof quietly assumes the verifying state is transparent about its own epistemic standing, and I don't think the anti-realist ever signed up for that. Drop that assumption and the paradox goes away, while the knowability principle survives in a form that still rules out truths no possible inquiry could settle.

2. Is the transparency assumption really seperable from verificationism, or does the anti-realist need it just to state their position? If they need it, then I've found a different way to refute them and I should probably say so.
3. Does my watered-down knowability principle still exclude the verification-transcendent truths the anti-realist wanted to exclude, or is it so weak that a realist can just nod along and go home happy?
