Bit of an odd one for me — I'm a science journalist, I usually write about this stuff rather than present it, but I've been roped into giveing the closing talk at a small AI conference next month. The room will be mostly industry ML engineers, a scattering of alignment/safety people, and two philosophers of technology who I'm told enjoy a fight.

The talk is my own argument, so it's not anything the audience will have read. Everyone draws the tool-AI vs agent-AI line by capability — does it plan, does it act, does it have goals. I want to draw it by whether the objective is swappable. A tool is something you can point at a new goal and it's still the same system; an agent is something whose objective is constitutive — swap it and you haven't redirected it, you've replaced it. Two consequences I'll be pushing: the advice to just build tools rather than agents is false comfort, because what makes a system safe turns out not to be tool-hood in the old sense; and some things everyone calls tools are agents on my criterion, and vice versa.

So prep me for the Q&A. I want the six or seven objections I'll actually get, in each camp's own vocabulary, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they do — including the one where they're right and I should just concede it up front, and the one I'm hoping nobody asks. For each, what I say back in 90 seconds and a line on where my reply is soft. Also name the weakest joint in my own position, the one I should walk in ready to defend rather than pray they miss. No reading list please, I've got a week.
