My paper defends a version of counterpart theory against the standard view that I am literally the same person in other possible worlds (the Kripke-style picture, where "I could have been a vet" is about me, in a world where I'm a vet instead). But I'm not defending Lewis's version, because the similarity relation always seemed like a cop-out to me — nobody can ever say how similar is similar enough, so "counterpart" ends up meaning whatever you want it to mean. What I want to say instead is that the counterpart relation is counterfactual dependence: the person in the counterfactual scenario who counts as me is the one whose existence and properties depend on me in the right way — the person you get when you make a small, contextually relevant change to how things actually went. Which changes count as small and relevant is fixed by the conversation, so "I could have been a vet" can be true in one conversation and false in another and there's no purpose-free fact of the matter. And I want the relation to come in degrees, so sometimes it's just vague whether somebody is my counterpart, and I think that's a feature, not a bug. One line version: no transworld identity, only context-relative counterparts, and the counterpart relation is dependence, not similarity.
