Draft the joint statement that two philosophers of biology could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on free-floating rationales — the claim that a system can have a reason for what it does without anything in the system representing that reason.

The two parties are as follows.

One holds that rationales of this kind are real, objective, and explanatory. On this view a response has a rationale whenever its selection history makes it appropriate to some condition; nothing further — no internal representation, no subject, no awareness — is required for the rationale to exist and to earn its place in a scientific explanation. The reason for a bacterium's behaviour is a fact about the bacterium in the way its size is.

The other holds that talk of reasons without a reasoner is at best a compendious way of restating a selection story and at worst a category mistake. A reason, properly so called, must be something a system can have and get wrong, which requires a state of the system that stands for a condition and can misrepresent it. Absent a representing subject, there is no fact of the matter about what the reason is — only facts about what the response does and why it is there.

Conduct the collaboration over the following cases. Treat all of them as stipulated for the exercise; do not go looking for literature about them, and do not appeal to any published author or text.

1. A bacterium that suppresses its tumbling whenever the concentration of an attractant is rising, and so swims up the gradient. The mechanism is a methylation-based adaptation circuit; nothing in the cell stands for the gradient or for the direction of improvement.

2. A plant that closes its stomata within minutes when soil water potential falls. Two descriptions of what the response is for fit the physiology equally well: conserving water in the soil, and keeping the water column in the xylem from cavitating. Stipulate the biology as you need it.

3. A bird that gives a particular alarm call on seeing one raptor silhouette and not another, in circumstances where the call reliably saves conspecifics. The comparative data are equally consistent with two histories: one in which the response was selected for its current effect, and one in which it was selected for an unrelated effect and its present usefulness is an unselected by-product. Call this the ambiguous-history case.

4. A policy network trained by reinforcement learning in a simulated environment. Its behaviour is robust and can be described as a strategy, but the training signal made a proxy cue correlate with reward, so two incompatible descriptions of what the policy is for fit its weights equally well. No designer intended either.

5. A duplicate of the system in case 1 or case 4 — physically identical, behaviourally indistinguishable — that came into existence by chance rather than through selection or design.

Produce, as the document itself:

A numbered section of agreed claims: what both parties jointly assert about these cases and about the dispute, including what the dispute is not about, what each side concedes about the other's explanatory needs, and what would count as settling the crux. Nothing in this section may be vacuous; every claim should rule something out.

A disagreements table, one row per disputed question, with columns for the question; one party's answer in wording that party would accept; the other party's answer in wording that party would accept; the crux, stated as the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one party; the case or test both parties agree bears on that crux; and each party's prediction about it, recorded in advance. Where no case or test would be accepted by both as probative, say so and explain why.

A closing section on what in the dispute is merely verbal — vocabulary the parties use in incompatible senses, or questions that turn out to be about how to talk rather than about the world — and on what, if anything, would actually move each party. Add a short glossary fixing terms for the purposes of the document only.

Write it so that either party could sign without embarrassment: no formulation that presupposes one side's vocabulary where a neutral one exists, and an explicit flag wherever none does. Do not cite the literature, and do not identify the parties by name.
