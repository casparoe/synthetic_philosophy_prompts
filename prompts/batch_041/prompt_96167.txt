I did an MSc in epidemiology before I defected to journalism, so I think in counterfactuals, confounding, effect modification, spillovers, transportabilty — that sort of thing. There's a worry philosophers bang on about, consequentialist cluelessness: that once you try to trace the knock-on consequences of anything you do — giving a fiver to someone begging, buying the cheap jumper, voting — the causal chain runs off into the dark and you've no idea whether you've done good or harm, and it never resolves no matter how much you learn. Restate that as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, then give me the residue report: what survives the translation, what only goes through if you fudge it, and what can't be said in epi-speak at all. I'm especially interested in whether the E-value move — ask how strong an unmeasured confounder would have to be to explain away your result — is doing the same work as whatever the philosophers' answer is, or whether cluelessness just is the claim that no such bound exists. And tell me whether the leftover is a gap in my vocabulary or a sign the philosophers are dressing up ordinary ignorance as something deeper.
