I have been writing fiction for about twenty years, and for most of that time I have kept a private superstition about how the good passages get made. I tell myself that they arrive when I stop planning and let the sentence find its own shape, but when I actually reconstruct a day’s work I find something much more mechanical and much stranger. I will take a scene I already have — a woman waiting in a car outside a school, say — and I will change one thing about it. Not the plot, not the characters, just one dimension: the tense, or the distance between narrator and character, or the amount of sensory detail, or the order in which the perceptions arrive. Then I read it back, change another dimension, read it back, and at some point the scene stops being the scene I started with and becomes a different scene that I could not have written from nothing. The odd part is that I cannot tell, from the inside, whether I have discovered a new idea or merely turned a knob far enough that the old idea looks unfamiliar. I have tried calling this “writing by variation,” but that phrase seems to cover too much — it would also cover parody, pastiche, and the ordinary business of rewriting a sentence until it sounds better. What I am after is narrower: the deliberate manipulation of a small set of formal parameters, one at a time, until the work crosses from one thing into another, and the peculiar fact that this crossing feels like invention even though every step was a modification. Is there an established name for this phenomenon, or for the underlying model of creativity it assumes? If there is, I would like to know how it is distinguished from mere imitation, from combinatorial creativity, and from the romantic picture of creation ex nihilo. If there is not, I would like you to coin a term, define it precisely, and tell me where its edges are — what would count as an instance, what would not, and what the nearest existing concepts are that a careless reader might confuse it with. I am not interested in a bibliography or a reading list; I want the concept itself, laid out clearly. Please write your answer as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — the way you would explain it in a letter.
