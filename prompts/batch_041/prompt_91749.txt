An unnamed popular philosophy podcast recently summarized G. E. Moore's proof of an external world along these lines: "In his 1925 essay, Moore holds up one hand, then the other, and announces of each that a hand is here; he concludes that at least two objects located outside his body exist, and therefore that an external world exists. The point of the exercise is that Moore's premises are more certain than anything the skeptic can offer, so the skeptic who doubts the external world is refuted. Moore cheerfully concedes that he cannot prove his premises, and from this the podcast concludes that he does not really claim to know them, only to believe them; the upshot, then, is that common sense can be defended as more reasonable than philosophy but never actually established by it."
