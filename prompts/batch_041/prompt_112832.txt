I have been working through the "low impact" idea on my own — the thought that a system should do what it is asked and otherwise leave the world alone — and I want to know whether "leave the world alone" can be given definite content without first settling what is worth caring about. Please design a case that tests this.

Make it ordinary and realistic: one helper (a person or a machine, since the point is the concept rather than the hardware), one request, one small setting — a kitchen, a workshop, a corner shop — and two ways of carrying the request out. The two ways must satisfy the request equally well, and must differ outside it. Arrange them so that two plainly stated ways of measuring disturbance rank them in opposite orders: (a) count how much of the world is changed, with no regard to what was changed; (b) count how much of what anyone cares about is changed, with no regard to how much. State the unit each measure is counting in, and make sure the inversion genuinely holds in those units rather than being asserted.

Then give each measure's verdict on the two ways of acting, show where the disagreement comes from, and say what in the request itself, if anything, tells you which measure to use. Add a brief note on whether the verdicts change when disturbance is measured against the world as it stands at the moment of the request rather than against the world as it would have been had the request never been made.

Close with: the one feature of the case that is doing the work; what becomes of the disagreement if that feature is removed; the strongest defence available to someone who insists that "avoid side effects" is a perfectly usable instruction as it stands; and how confident you are in each claim you make, singling out the one you are least sure of.
