I am a high school student organizing a philosophy club exercise: an adversarial collaboration between a counterpart theorist, who denies literal transworld identity, and a defender of transworld identity. Please draft the joint statement they could both sign. Start with a numbered list of agreed claims about possible worlds, de re modality, rigidity, and what it is for an individual to be the subject of a modal predication. Then give a disagreements table: the precise point of divergence, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own position; the crux that would move at least one side; and for each crux a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance. I want the statement to be useful even to someone who has never read the primary literature, so please avoid depending on particular books or names. To keep it from being a standard textbook summary, build the cruxes around two specific puzzles: a world in which someone qualitatively indistinguishable from a given person, with the same apparent history, is nevertheless said to be a different individual; and a world in which the counterpart relation is not one-to-one and may be non-transitive, so that one person has multiple counterparts and one counterpart has multiple originals. Make clear which parts of the dispute are genuine and which might be merely verbal. Please state how confident you are in each claim and flag the single point you are least sure of.
