Compose a sestina about the illusionist view of consciousness, in which the six end-words are all drawn from the vocabulary of a graphical user interface—window, cursor, icon, folder, trash, desktop—and the form itself enacts the view's central claim: the interface is all we can manipulate, and its terms keep returning because there is no stepping outside it. The poem should distinguish the illusionist thesis that phenomenal consciousness is a user illusion from the stronger eliminativist thesis that we do not have experiences at all; a reader who knows the debate should see that the poem does not conflate the two, and an illusionist should recognize the meta-problem—why the illusion of phenomenality arises—as the poem's real subject. Do not use the words experience, feeling, or qualia anywhere except in the envoi, where they should appear only to be marked as part of the illusion. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind would not wince.
