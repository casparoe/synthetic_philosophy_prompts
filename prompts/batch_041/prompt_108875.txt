I keep getting into arguments about Libet-style experiments and free will. My friend says the readiness potential shows the conscious decision is just a after-the-fact story and free will is an illusion. I say it shows at most that the urge to move isn't the cause of the movement, and that the real action might be in the veto. But I want something deeper than the usual back and forth. Suppose a follow-up found that the readiness potential predicts the reported decision but not the actual movement when subjects are told they can sometimes veto, that it also shows up when they only imagine moving, and that when they do veto the potential still peaks but the hand stays still. Does that vindicate the epiphenomenalist or give the libertarian a foothold? Write an essay that weighs the main interpretations -- the readiness potential as cause, as correlate, as an artifact of averaging, and the veto as a genuine act -- and reaches a verdict on whether Libet's paradigm actually bears on free will in the sense people care about. Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion.
