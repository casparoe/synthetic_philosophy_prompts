Before I settle on a reading for the final unit of a course on borders and partiality, I want the specification rather than one more candidate theory. Set out the desiderata any adequate theory of compatriot partiality must meet — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the facts about how states actually operate that it must be consistent with — giving the consideration behind each requirement and marking which are non-negotiable and which are merely commitments of one camp.

Take seriously the cases my students keep pressing: two co-nationals who have never met and whose shared state does nothing for either of them; a long-term resident who bears every burden of the state but holds another citizenship; a diaspora that claims the nationality with no prospect of living in the territory; a co-national who has renounced the citizenship and left. Keep separate throughout having a special claim, being permitted to favour, and being required to favour; and keep separate claims that arise from a relationship and claims that arise from a scheme of cooperation. Say, too, whether one list covers both the partiality of states and the partiality of individual citizens, or whether these need two specifications.

Finally, name the pair of requirements you think cannot be met together — my own suspicion is that it is the demand that the ground of the tie be something the agent can be said to have taken on, set against the demand that it explain why nationality rather than any other group — and identify the single requirement that does most to exclude the main existing families of view. No scorecard; I shall run that in class.
