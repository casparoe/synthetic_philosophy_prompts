I'm a retired geologist — forty years of rocks, and since I hung it up I've been reading moral philosophy in the evenings, badly and slowly. There's a pattern I keep bumping into and I've never found a name for it, and I'm starting to think either there isn't one or it's hiding under a name I don't recognise.

Here's the thing. Somebody has a strong reaction to a practice — the yuck reaction to lab-grown meat, say, or a gut sense that a certain kind of deal is dishonest even though nobody's been lied to, or a conviction that a way of treating animals is degrading. When we happen to approve of the reaction we call it a moral conviction, a considered value, and we say it deserves respect and shouldn't be messed with. When we don't approve we call it a bias and we go hunting for the psychological or evolutionary story that produced it, as if finding the story settled the matter. The odd part is that the story is always available in both cases — every moral conviction anyone has ever held has some causal history, disgust, upbringing, group loyalty, whatever. So the origin story can't be what separates the value from the bias, or nothing would count as a value. But in practice that's exactly the move people make, over and over, and it usually works on the room. I've watched a colleague dismiss someone's objection as 'just their culture' in one breath and defend his own as reasoned in the next, and I don't think he was being cynical, I think he genuinely couldn't see it.

Second thing, closer to home. When I introspect I can't tell the difference from the inside. My irritation at a neighbour's behaviour and my principled objection to it feel identical — same heat, same certainty. Nothing inside says 'this one is just conditioning.' And I notice I'm much more willing to go looking for a deflating explanation of other people's convictions than of my own.

So: is there an established name for this — the problem of telling a legitimate preference or value from a bias, when both have the same kind of origin and the same feel from the inside, and the only sorting device anyone seems to actually use is whether they already agree with the reaction? I know about the genetic fallacy, and I've gathered there's a literature on 'debunking arguments' in ethics, but what I'm after seems narrower than that, or maybe broader: not 'does origin undermine a moral belief' in general, but the prior question of how you ever seperate the reactions that are up for debunking from the ones doing the debunking. Does it have a name, am I confusing it with something else, and if there's no name, what would you call it so it doesn't just collapse into 'bias' or 'preference'?

What I don't want is to be told 'that's just reflective equilibrium' or 'that's the genetic fallacy' and left there — I want the carving. Where is the line, if there is one, and what sits at the edges of it? Is my preference for chocolate over vanilla in the same category, and if not, what work is the word 'moral' doing there? Sorry for the length, I'm probly overthinking it.
