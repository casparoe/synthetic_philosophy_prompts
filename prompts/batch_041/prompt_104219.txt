My roommate says the story is basically a refutation of metaphysics: if the dreamer can always turn out to be dreamed, there's no floor, no bottom level, no "really real" layer, and anyone who thinks reality rests on something fundamental is just the guy in the story before the last paragraph. I think that's way too quick, and I want a real critical reading of the story, not a vibe.

Here's the angle I'm after. Read it through grounding and fundamentality — grounding being the relation where one thing holds *because of* another in a non-causal, non-merely-modal way, the way a table's existing is grounded in the wood being arranged like that, or a moral fact is grounded in whatever makes it the case; and the thing that trips people up is that mere supervenience isnt enough, since two things can covary without one holding in virtue of the other. The question I actually care about is the well-foundedness of grounding: can a chain of "in virtue of" relations descend forever with nothing fundamental at the bottom, or does something have to be ungrounded?
