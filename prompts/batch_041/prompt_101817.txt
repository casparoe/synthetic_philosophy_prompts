Hi — I'm in year 12 and I help run the philosophy club at my school (it's basically me and my mate Josh, and Josh mostly comes for the biscuits). We've just finished a unit on pragmatism, the Peirce vs James thing about truth, and for my project I'm allowed to do a creative piece instead of an essay. I think I've got the basics: Peirce says a belief is true if it's what the community of inquirers would eventually converge on if inquiry went on long enough, so truth is more like a regulative ideal, something the long run aims at; James says a belief is true if it works, if it pays off in the way of our thinking, if it has what he calls cash value — though I know James didn't mean 'whatever makes you feel warm inside', and Peirce wasn't saying truth is just whatever we happen to agree on either. My problem is that everyone in my class keeps collapsing it into 'truth is whatever works for you', and I'm pretty sure that's wrong for Peirce and only half-right for James, and I want to write something that shows the difference actually matters without sounding like a textbook. Also I read somewhere that Peirce ended up renaming his own version 'pragmaticism' because he thought James's version had got too cosy with wishful thinking — I don't know if that's exactly right but it's the kind of thing I'm after.

So here's what I want: a satirical agony-aunt column, like the sort of thing you'd find in a weekend magazine, where the aunts are two sisters — one a Peircean, one a Jamesian — and they take turns answering the same letters from readers. Around 800 words, deadpan, no winking at the reader, with a title and a one-line standfirst like a real column, and the humour should come from the philosophy itself rather than from the aunts being silly. The letters can be everyday things, but the answers should be genuinely Peircean and genuinely Jamesian, so that someone who actually knows the literature would laugh because the positions are being taken seriously, not because the aunts say 'truth is subjective'. I'd love it if the jokes land on the real sticking points: the Peircean aunt having to cope with the fact that the long run never actually turns up, the Jamesian aunt having to cope with two contradictory beliefs that both 'work' for different people, whether a belief that helps you get out of bed can be true, that sort of thing. At least one letter should end with the two sisters giving answers that can't be squared with each other, and I definately don't want either of them to be a strawman — they should both be defensible on their own terms, even if the reader can see where they clash.

You can make up the readers' problems, but a couple of ideas if it helps: a girl whose boyfriend has moved abroad and who keeps believing he'll come back because believing it gets her through the day; a lad who thinks his late dad would be proud of him even though there's no evidence either way; someone trying to decide between two jobs and convinced that whichever one she picks will reveal her 'true' calling. Oh, and maybe the magazine has given the column a title like 'Is It True For You?' — that kind of thing. I'm not allowed to use the internet for this project (school rule, annoyingly), so please don't tell me to go and look stuff up — just write the column. It's due the week after half term, if that matters.
