I'm TAing a first-year seminar on experimental philosophy and I want a handout that sorts students by what they think is really going on with the Knobe effect -- the chairman who waves through a policy that happens to help/hurt the environment and only gets called intentional in the harm case. Make it a decision tree, and make every branching question a concrete case they can answer without already knowing which label it leads to, not stuff like 'do you think the effect is a pragmatic error or a real feature of the concept?'. So branch on things like: does the asymmetry still show up when the side effect violates a norm that isn't moral -- a bit of etiquette, an office dress code, a purely statistical norm; does it survive swapping 'intentionally' for 'deliberately' or 'knowingly'; does it show up when the agent is a committee or a thermostat rather then a person; does it hold when you strip all blame out and just ask what caused the outcome. The leaves should be the actual positions: the one that says the folk concept of intentional action genuinely is asymmetric, the one that says it's a performance glitch and the underlying competence is symmetric, the one that says the effect is really blameworthiness leaking into our attributions, the one that says normativity is baked into the concept itself, plus any leaf you think earns its place and an explicit 'nobody can stand here' leaf if a branch dead-ends. One line per leaf on its most awkward commitment, and flag any two leaves the tree can't actually pry apart.
