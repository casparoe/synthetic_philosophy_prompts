I dont know if this is a real distinction or just me, but here goes. I've been reading a lot of military history in the evenings — not the academic kind, the sort you pick up at an airport — and I keep tripping over a particular kind of sentence. "The decision to hold the ridge was the decision that lost them the war." "That was the last real chance to stop it." Nobody standing on that ridge could have said either of those things — not because they were stupid, but because they weren't true yet. They're true now, and they're true *of* the ridge, but only because of what happened afterwards. And the books aren't cheating when they write them: they're not saying anyone should have known, and they're not blaming anyone. They're describing what happened, and the description only works if you already know how it turned out.

Then I noticed the other half of it, which is the bit that actually bothers me. I tried writing one of these campaigns out keeping only what someone on the ground could have known as it happened — no "turning point", no "beginning of the end", nothing that needs the ending. What came out wasn't a worse history. It wasn't a history at all. It was a log. So these sentences don't look like decoration that a strict historian could just strip out; it looks like the thing we call a history is partly made of them.

Is there a name for that kind of sentence? I've had a look around and everything I turn up is either hindsight bias or presentism, and neither of them is it, definately. Hindsight bias is about being unfair to people in the past, and these sentences can be completely fair. Presentism is about judging them by our own values, and this has nothing to do with values. A sentence can be perfectly accurate and still be unsayable at the time.

And is there a name for the second half — for the fact that a record with none of those sentences stops being history and turns into a log? If there's no name for either, tell me what you'd call them and where the edges are, because the name isn't really what I'm after. Specifically: is this one phenomenon or two? Does the same thing turn up outside history — a doctor writing up a case can surely say "the first sign of what killed him", and a biographer can say "the woman who would one day..." — or is there something special about history? And is the puzzle about truth, or only about what you're allowed to say? I'm not after a reading list, just the shape of it.
