I am a third-year student taking a course on practical rationality, and we have just finished the unit on money pumps. I have to open a seminar presentation next week, and I would rather begin with a joke than with the standard three-good cycle, which everyone in the room has already seen. Would you write one for me?

I want the joke to turn on something my seminar spent an entire session on: that the synchronic pump and the diachronic pump are two different arguments travelling under one name. The synchronic pump needs intransitive preferences — A preferred to B, B to C, C to A — and needs nothing beyond a willingness to trade at each step. The diachronic pump needs no intransitivity at all; it needs only that your preferences reverse between the moment you choose and the moment the offer arrives. In that second case, as my professor put it, the victim can be perfectly rational at every single moment and still finish where she began, holding less of whatever she was spending. That is the part I would like the joke to reach, because it is the part that makes the pump more than a story about foolishness.

Three requests. First, do not let the currency be money. The general form is a willingness to pay some cost in order to move along a cycle of preferences, and I want the joke to pump something else — goodwill, apologies, evenings, favours, whatever fits — so that the point does not depend on the phrase doing the work. Second, if you can manage it, make the butt of the joke the sophisticated agent rather than the naive one. The naive victim being taken is the easy joke; I would rather the humour come from the agent who sees the reversal coming, pays to restrain herself, and ends up paying anyway, or from the agent who sticks to her plan and therefore has to refuse, at the moment of the offer, to do what she most wants. Third, it must be a joke and not an illustration: a setup and a punchline, short enough to say aloud, and accurate enough that nobody in the room winces.

Please do not name any theorists or cite any literature; I want it to stand on its own for people who have never heard the term. It should also still land for a friend of mine in the audience who has never heard it.
