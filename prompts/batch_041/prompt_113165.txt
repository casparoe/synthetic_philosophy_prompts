Here's a thought experiment I keep chewing on, and I want the most important implications of it. Solomonoff induction weights each hypothesis by 2 to the minus its program length — the universal prior. There's a worry, call it the malign prior worry, that this prior hands non-trivial weight to very short programs that just hard-code or simulate your entire observation history, which means the posterior can end up dominated by worlds where your observations are basically a setup and the real world is something else entirely (or nothing at all). For the sake of argument, grant that the worry goes through. What are the most important implications of this for a consequentialist who already belives the far future has astronomicaly large expected value and that we should be willing to make serious sacrifices now for it? I'm mainly interested in whether it undercuts the action-guidingness of far-future reasoning, whether it changes how much you should trust your own observations when you update, and anything else you think matters more than those two.
