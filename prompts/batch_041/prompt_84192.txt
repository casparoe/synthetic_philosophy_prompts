I am organizing a three-year conceptual working group on the competitiveness constraint and the alignment tax, with funding for roughly four philosophers and two workshops per year. The guiding worry is familiar: if a lab, firm, or state unilaterally bears the capability cost of safer systems, it may be outcompeted by less cautious actors, so safety becomes self-undermining. But I want the group to avoid rehashing that slogan. I propose we split what is usually lumped together under the alignment tax into four distinct burdens: a capability burden, meaning what safety costs in raw performance; a coordination burden, meaning what it costs to move only when others do; a legitimacy burden, meaning what it costs when safety measures cannot be publicly justified to those bound by them; and an epistemic burden, meaning what it costs when safety measures make systems harder to evaluate or understand. Which open conceptual questions about these four burdens most deserve the group's time, and in what order? I care less about a comprehensive list than about the dependency structure: which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable for philosophers rather than ML researchers, and which would change the group's priorities if answered one way rather than another. Also tell me which of the four burdens is most likely to be a distraction, and why.
