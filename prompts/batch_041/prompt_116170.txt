I'm a novelist and I've got a book where the gimmick is a town that's quietly started putting a price on everything, so I keep pitching my friend Rosa — she teaches moral philosophy and reads all my drafts — little markets and asking if they're kosher. Her verdicts are weirdly consistant: cash for a kidney, even between two happy consenting adults, no; doubling the price of bottled water in the week after a flood, fine; selling your vote for money even when it wouldn't change the result, no; selling your old trophies on eBay, fine; a paid surrogate pregnancy, no; scalping concert tickets at five times face value, fine.

The chapter I'm stuck on needs a case she hasn't ruled on yet: a company where you pay a monthly fee and get a stranger who calls you every week, remembers your birthday, checks in when you're sick — does the stuff a close friend does. Everything's disclosed, the staff aren't desperate, they like the job, nobody's lied to.

Which way does she jump? More usefully, what rule is she actually running? I can see at least two that fit every verdict above — one about markets corrupting the meaning of certain goods, one about who gets hurt or pushed into it — and I don't think they agree on this one, so say what each predicts, which you'd bet on and why, and what other case I should sneak into the next draft to find out which rule she's really got.
