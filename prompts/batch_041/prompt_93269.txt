I am presenting a paper at a joint workshop next month, and I want to walk in with a Q&A sheet rather than a rebuttal. The room divides about evenly between two camps. The first is modal logicians and proof theorists, who know the incompleteness results cold, care about whether a circularity has actually been discharged, and will not accept a hand-wave about what an agent can trust. The second is AI-safety researchers who work on self-modification and on how an agent can justify relying on a more capable successor; they care about the one-shot case, about what happens when the successor's reasoning is opaque, and about whether the trust relation can be gamed. One decision theorist will be present for reasons I have not established.

The thesis I am defending is that the Löbian obstacle should be dissolved rather than solved. I take the standard setup to be common ground: for a theory T strong enough to formalize arithmetic, with □ the provability predicate, adding the local reflection schema □φ → φ would let T prove its own consistency, and by Löb's theorem an instance of that schema is derivable only where its consequent is already a theorem; hence an agent cannot prove the soundness of its own or its successor's proofs in general, and cannot license action on a successor's conclusion by that route. My move is to stop asking for a theorem. Self-trust, on my account, is carried by what I call a trust ledger: the agent records the occasions on which it acted on a conclusion it had derived and the conclusion held, licenses further self-trust by induction over that record, and declines to act on a successor's conclusion whenever the successor's derivation draws on the ledger. I claim this is not the reflection schema in disguise — the ledger is deliberately not a theorem of T — but that it gives the agent a non-circular basis for relying on its successor everywhere except the self-referential cases.

Prepare the seven or eight objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they do, each phrased the way the person who will raise it would phrase it and labelled with the camp it comes from; for each, a two-minute reply and a note on the weakest joint in that reply. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks, tell me which objection I should simply concede rather than fight, and flag any place where my own formulation invites an objection I could have avoided by stating the position differently.
