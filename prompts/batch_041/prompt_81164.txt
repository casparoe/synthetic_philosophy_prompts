I keep getting dragged into the same fight about the master-slave dialectic and I want to force it to a real disagreement instead of two people talking past each other. The two camps, roughly:

1) The labor reading: the payoff is that the slave, not the master, ends up with self-consciousness, because he works on the world and comes to see himself in what he's made. The master is a dead end, since the only recognition he gets comes from someone who isn't in a position to withhold it.
2) The mutual-recognition reading: no, the point is that recognition has to run both ways between equals. Labor without that is just coping. So the slave doesn't get there either — both parties are stuck, and the thing only resolves when the relation itself gets transformed.

Build me a thought experiment engineered so these two come apart in one specific direction: the labor reading says the subordinate party comes out ahead of the dominant one, and the mutual-recognition reading says they come out the same, i.e. both stuck. Constraints: modern and realistic (workplace, hospital, family, prison — no literal masters and slaves, no fight to the death, nobody in the case has read Hegel), scenario in one paragraph, the subordination has to be structural rather than one person just being a jerk, and the labor has to produce something that's genuinely the worker's own. Don't rig it by making the dominant party a sadist or the labor obviously degrading — I want a case someone in the mutual-recognition camp would look at and say "yeah, fine, that's my kind of case."

Then: give each reading's verdict on the case and why, name the single feature doing the seperating work, tell me what happens if I strip that feature out (does it collapse into a case both sides agree on?), and how a defender of whichever reading comes out worse would redescribe the case to defuse it. American English, and feel free to be blunt about which reading you think is actually doing more explanatory work.
