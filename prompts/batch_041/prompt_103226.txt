I have been reading a good deal of epistemic game theory and would like a worked case rather than a survey. Build me a finite two-player normal-form game, with payoffs of the sort two people dividing chores or bidding on a shared apartment could actually face, that separates two readings of common knowledge of rationality. Under the weaker reading — common knowledge that neither player uses a strictly dominated strategy — iterated elimination should leave more than one cell standing, so the assumption predicts nothing in particular. Under the stronger reading — common knowledge that neither player uses a weakly dominated strategy — a single cell should survive, but only for one order of elimination; a different order should kill a different cell. Give the matrix, run both eliminations in full, and report what each reading predicts. Then tell me which structural feature of the payoffs is doing the work, what I would have to change to make the two readings agree again, and how someone who denies that avoiding weak dominance is part of rationality would argue the unique prediction is an artifact of the order. The case is for a post arguing that common knowledge of rationality names a family of assumptions rather than one, so the verdicts have to be derived from the game rather than stipulated.
