I can know and love a recording for years, and then learn something about the person who made it; not a note has changed, and yet the thing begins to say something else. Lines I had always heard as affectionate start to read as confessions. A song I took to be about tenderness turns out to have been written for someone the singer treated badly, and what I heard as warmth I now hear as performance. What puzzles me is that this is not simply the loss of my taste for it. Sometimes the work becomes more interesting, as though it had acquired a dimension it did not have, and I still do not want to spend time with it, so the change in what the work says and the change in my willingness to consume it come apart. Nor is it only about condemnation: when I admire someone I find their work warmer and more generous than I suspect it would seem to me if I had never heard of them, and the same shift happens with discoveries that have nothing to do with morality, as when a painting turns out to be a forgery and people say it looks different. So a fact about the maker seems to become a fact about what the made thing means. Is there an established name for this? The advice to separate the art from the artist is advice, and the warnings I have met about reading a work through its author's intentions seem to be about a different mistake, so neither quite covers it. If some existing concept fits, say which one and where it fails; if nothing does, propose a term, define it, and mark its edges — does it apply to a building whose architect did something monstrous, to a work whose maker is unknown, or to a piece made long before the fact came out?
