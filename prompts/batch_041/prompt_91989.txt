I am a high school student, and the philosophy club I belong to has spent most of this year circling a single question without ever settling it: when a large language model outputs a sentence such as "I believe the library closes at six," is there any sense in which the system holds that belief, or are we only speaking as if it did because doing so makes the conversation easier? Our two camps have become fairly well defined. One side takes its cue from the intentional stance as Daniel Dennett described it: if treating the system as though it had beliefs and desires lets us predict and explain what it does, then that is all there is to having them, and asking whether the beliefs are "real" is a question with no further answer. The other side insists that a belief must play a particular kind of role in a system — it must be revisable in light of evidence, connected to the system's other attitudes, and capable of guiding action — and that a system whose words are drawn from the statistical structure of human text rather than from an updating model of the world fails that test. A third group suspects the whole argument is about how we use the word "belief." A fourth treats it as an empirical question about what is actually happening inside the network, to be settled by inspecting its internal representations rather than by arguing about concepts.

My own hypothesis is that the first side and the second are not disagreeing about the same thing: one treats belief as a status conferred by predictive success, the other treats it as a kind of state with a hidden structure that a system either has or lacks, and the shared word conceals the difference. What I want is a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Which kind of disagreement is this really — verbal, substantive, a hidden empirical question, or two different questions travelling under one name? Does the fact that a language model's fluent behaviour is inherited from human text make the intentional stance's usual defence, predictive success, cheaper here than it is for ordinary people and animals? And given your diagnosis, what would genuinely count as progress — a distinction, an experiment, a change of question — or should we expect none? Please argue from what each side actually says rather than giving me a survey of positions.
