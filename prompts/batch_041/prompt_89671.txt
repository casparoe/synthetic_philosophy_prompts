I'm writing up my axiology for a graduate seminar and want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the package I currently hold.

1. There are objective facts about which outcomes are better than which; 'better than' is not a projection of our attitudes onto a neutral world.
2. Value is a single ordering of outcomes: any two outcomes are comparable, so that one is at least as good as the other, and 'at least as good as' is transitive. ('Better than' is transitive as well.)
3. The Repugnant Conclusion is false. Concretely: A is 10 billion people at welfare level 100, on a scale where 0 is a life not worth living and 100 is a flourishing human life; Z is on the order of 10^40 people at level 1. A is better than Z, and better than every intermediate world in the sequence that reaches Z by repeatedly doubling the population and lowering the level by one. The vastness of Z cannot outweigh its poverty.
4. Adding people whose lives are worth living to an outcome never makes that outcome worse.
5. There is no lexical threshold anywhere in that sequence: no quality of life is so important that no increase in the number of people enjoying it could compensate for a small loss of it, and welfare admits arbitrarily fine gradations.
6. Some comparisons are neither better nor worse nor equal, and not indeterminate either: two outcomes can be on a par, so that neither is better than the other and no verdict of betterness is available in either direction.

What I want is an audit, not a survey. Which of these can be held together? Which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be? Order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give, what that costs the rest of the package, and which single revision would clear up the most.
