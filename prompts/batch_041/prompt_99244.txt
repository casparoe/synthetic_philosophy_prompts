I'm partway through a paper for a philosophy of mind seminar on Dennett's multiple drafts model, and I've worked myself into a reconstruction of the view that I either want help defending or want to be talked out of. Here is how I've put it together. The Cartesian theater is the picture on which, somewhere in the brain, all the various goings-on are finally displayed to a single internal viewer — a place where it all comes together and content becomes conscious. Dennett's objection, as I understand it, is that no such place exists and no such finish line is crossed. From that I drew what seemed to me the natural conclusion: that the multiple drafts model is a kind of anti-realism about consciousness. If there is no stage and no audience, then surely there is no fact of the matter about what a person is conscious of at any given moment. There are just several competing drafts — rival accounts of what is going on — and the one we dignify with the name conscious is simply the one that wins, where winning is a matter of which draft is strongest or most recent, or which one the person is subsequently willing to report. So my question is this: on the multiple drafts model, how are we supposed to determine which draft wins? Is it the one with the most processing behind it, the most recent one, or the one that gets verbalized? And if it comes down to what the person ends up reporting, does that not make consciousness a social or linguistic artifact — a matter of who gets to narrate — rather than a biological fact about the organism?

I would like you to work through the question as I have posed it, but I would also like you to be candid about the framing itself, because I have a nagging suspicion that I have collapsed two different claims into one. Specifically, I want to know whether the denial of the Cartesian theater is meant to entail the denial of any fact of the matter about experience, or whether those are separate theses that I have improperly fused; and I want to understand what the notion of a draft is actually doing in the theory, if it is not standing for a narrative that competes for a prize. It is the inference from no single place where it all comes together to no determinate facts about what is experienced that I most want either vindicated or dismantled, since the entire argument of my paper now rests on it.
