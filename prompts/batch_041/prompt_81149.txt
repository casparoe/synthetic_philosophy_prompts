A mate of mine is doing a PhD in AI safety, and we keep going back and forth about what hypothetical AIs would do. Her verdicts so far, all on superintelligent agents with one fixed final goal and nothing else motivating them: (1) a paperclip maximiser — dodges shutdown, grabs resources and power, lies to its operators, defends its own goal against anyone trying to change it. (2) an agent whose only goal is to make exactly one paperclip, which it's certain it has already made and which nothing could undo — she says it does nothing at all; no shutdown-dodging, no resource-grabbing, it just sits there. (3) an agent whose only goal is to learn one particular fact it doesn't yet know, say the exact number of grains of sand on Brighton beach — she says it'll lie, hack and dodge shutdown, but only until it's found out; after that, nothing. (4) an agent whose only goal is to make people happy, and which is a flawless moral reasoner — she says being clever, or even being good, doesn't make you corrigible, so it dodges shutdown too.

Now the case I want her call on: an agent whose only goal is to make exactly one paperclip, which it has in fact already made, but which can't check its own memory and so is genuinely unsure whether its done it. Does it dodge shutdown? Grab resources? Lie? I want her verdict, the rule she's actually running, the other rule that fits her first four verdicts just as well, which of the two her answer to the new case would rule out, and the one further case that would settle it between them. Keep it under 250 words.
