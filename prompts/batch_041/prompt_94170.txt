I'm a PhD student working at the border of aesthetics and epistemology and I've been living in the aesthetic testimony literature for a chapter of my thesis, and the whole optimism/pessimism fight feels stalled to me in a way that isn't just the usual slow grind. The pessimists say that when a trusted critic tells you a film is beautiful, that can't justify you in judging it beautiful -- at best it tells you what she thinks -- and the optimists say testimony transmits justification here just like it does anywhere else. Both sides have all the standard moves by now (the acquaintance line, the you-can't-outsource-your-taste line, the comparison with testimony about other people's experiences) and nobody's budged in years. My hunch is they're not even contradicting each other: one side is asking whether you end up with justification for a content, the other whether you end up with the standing to be a judge rather than a reporter of someone else's verdict, and "aesthetic justification" is doing two jobs under one word -- or maybe the pessimist intuition is really tracking something about understanding or the value of first-hand experience and not about justification at all. Is that diagnosis right, or is there a definately real substantive disagreement I'm flattening? And given your answer, what would actually count as progress here -- a distinction, a reframing, or should we just stop expecting any? I'm not after a survey, I want the disagreement itself diagnosed.
