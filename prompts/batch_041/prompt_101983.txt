ok so this is a weird ask but bear with me. For civics class we have to run a debate night and the topic is the whole statue / street name fight — my town has a couple of these, a statue of a guy who founded the local college but also did some genuinly awful stuff, and a street named after a scientist who had gross views about people. Everyone in class already has an opinion but nobody can actually say what their opinion is, they just yell keep it or tear it down. I want to hand out a one page thing that sorts people into the real positions by asking them about cases, not by asking them which label they like.

So: make me a decision tree (or a taxonomy, whatever fits better) where every branch is a question that splits the positions, and phrased so a random student can answer it without already knowing the name of the view it leads to. The leaves should be named positions, and if some leaf is one that nobody actually defends, just say so and say why — is it incoherent, unattractive, or just that nobody's thought of it yet. Also flag any two positions my tree can't really tell apart.

Some things I definately want the tree to force people to decide, because I think these are the real fault lines and I never see anyone ask them straight:

- is the problem the person's worst act, their overall record, or neither — i.e. nobody deserves a monument in the first place?
- does a statue keep doing something every day it stays up, or is it just a rock whose meaning got frozen back whenever it was put there and we can renegotiate it now?
- is a street name a different thing from a statue, because a street name is also just an address, so renaming it costs people money and mail headaches, whereas a statue is pure honor?
- can a plaque or a move to a museum or a counter-monument actually fix the thing, or is that a cop-out that keeps the honor?
- do the dead have anything at stake in being taken down? can you even wrong a person who's gone?
- and this is the one I care about most: is a monument kind of a promise from the past to the future, so pulling it down breaks something, or is that just romantic nonsense?

I don't need readings or who-said-what, I need the actual sorting thing itself. Keep it to like 7 or 8 branch points and no more than 9 leaves.
