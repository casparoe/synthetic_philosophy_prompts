I want to defend a particular way of drawing the distinction between tool AI and agent AI, and I want you to tell me what would count against it. The usual way of drawing the distinction is in terms of capability or architecture — whether a system plans, uses external tools, or operates over long horizons. I want to defend a different and stronger claim: that the distinction should be drawn in terms of the scope of the objective a system is trained or deployed to satisfy. Call a system a tool to the extent that its objective is exhausted by the present request, so that it is rewarded only for the quality of the current output and no term in its objective refers to the consequences of that output for future requests. Call it an agent to the extent that its objective ranges over those downstream consequences. On this view, planning, memory, tool use, self-modification and multi-step autonomy are all orthogonal both to the tool/agent distinction and to risk: a system with a request-local objective is safe however capable it becomes, because it has nothing to gain from deceiving its operators, preserving itself, or acquiring resources, whereas a system with even a weakly downstream objective is unsafe however modest its capabilities.

What would count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, including things that have arguably already happened. In particular, I want you to press on four points. First, are there conceivable or already-observed systems whose behaviour diverges from what the objective-scope criterion predicts — request-local systems that nonetheless behave in instrumentally self-serving ways, or systems with genuinely downstream objectives that remain reliably safe? Second, is the criterion well-defined at all, given that a training objective can hardly avoid encoding some facts about the consequences of the present output for future requests, so that the distinction may collapse into a difference of degree? Third, which of these defeaters would a defender of the position concede as counting against them, and which would they dismiss as question-begging? Fourth, has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability — for instance by construing the notion of an objective so loosely that any behaviour can be redescribed as conforming to it — and if so, is that a defect in a claim of this kind, or an acceptable feature of a conceptual proposal?

Please write your answer in British English.
