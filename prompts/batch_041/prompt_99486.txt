So I got passed over for a promotion that everyone, including my boss, had basically told me was mine, and the consolation I keep getting is that it was "bad luck" -- which somehow stings more than being told I wasn't good enough. I've been reading Machiavelli at night to stop feeling sorry for myself, mostly the fortune and virtù stuff: fortune as everything that isn't up to you, virtù as the part that is. Fine in the abstract. It's my actual case I can't fit into it. I did everything right and lost anyway.

Can you write me a short story that takes that seriously? Not a retelling of the river-and-dikes bit, I've read that one, and please not a fable with the moral bolted on at the end. A few things I want it to actually be about:

- The main character should be undone by someone else's virtù. Not a flood, not an accident, but another person being extremely good at what they do in a way the character's own competence can't answer. The part of Machiavelli everyone skips is that your fortune includes how good everyone else is, and I want that to be the engine of the plot.
- The character shouldn't fail by doing anything wrong or by failing to prepare. They prepared, well. They still lose. I'm tired of stories where the loss is secretly a lesson.
- There's something of his I half remember, that everyone has one settled way of going about things and only does well when that way happens to suit the times, and that the tragedy is you can't just switch modes when the times turn. I want the character's downfall to come out of the very disposition that made them good in the first place.
- Don't resovle it. No ending where better preparation would have saved them, and no ending where it was all luck and nothing anyone did mattered. If there's a lesson, make it an uncomfortable one or none at all.

Pick a setting with real texture -- a merchant family, a night shift on a hospital ward, a fishing town, a chess academy, whatever -- rather than a generic kingdom, and keep it to maybe 1500 words. And if you can work in a moment where the character sees the mismatch coming and can't do anything about it, that's the scene I actually want to read.
