I am an undergraduate in a course on Plato's late political thought, and next week we are hosting a visiting scholar who argues that the treatment of wine in the Laws is not a senile concession to human weakness but Plato's most serious contribution to moral psychology: the drinking party is a controlled institution for habituating the spirited part of the soul, continuous with the Symposium rather than opposed to it. I have been asked to prepare the interview questions, and I want them to press the position rather than let it be restated. Please draft eight to ten questions.

I need at least one question on how this reading squares with the Republic's censorship of drunkenness in the guardians' education, and a follow-up for whatever answer he is most likely to give; at least one on whether the drinking party reveals a man's character or manufactures it, since the Athenian's talk of testing seems to presuppose the former while his talk of training presupposes the latter; and at least one on what the institution accomplishes that ordinary habituation in pleasure and pain cannot, given the Athenian's own account of education. I would also like a question or two on the analogy between testing courage in battle and testing temperance at the symposium, and a closing question on whether the whole scheme treats citizens as patients to be managed rather than as reasoners who could be persuaded.

For each question, include a brief note on the reply he is likely to offer and the follow-up that should meet it.
