ok so I've got to circulate my aesthetic profile before the workshop and I'd rather find the cracks now than in the room. Here's where I've landed.

(1) I think there are aesthetic facts. Some works really are better than others; calling something kitsch isn't just me reporting my own gag reflex.

(2) I'm a pessimist about aesthetic testimony. Nobody can tell you a film is a masterpiece and thereby put you in a position to know it. You have to encounter the thing yourself.

(3) In practice I defer constantly and think that's fine. I'll take the sommelier's pick, I'll skip a novel because a friend whose taste I trust hated it, and I'll tell someone to go see a film I haven't seen, on the strength of what I've heard, without thinking I've been dishonest or unjustified.

(4) But I also think criticism can be a real source of aesthetic knowledge. Reading good writing about a painting has made me see it as good, not merely told me that it is.

(5) I don't buy the ideal-critic picture. There's no privileged vantage point; the standard is whatever the people who actually care about the form converge on, provisionally and revisably.

(6) I'm an optimist about moral testimony — taking "X is wrong" from someone who knows is fine by me.

So: which of these actually pull against each other? Rank the tensions by how bad they are rather than giving me six equally weighted worries. Tell me which pairs only look tense and why they're fine, and for the real ones say what the cheapest repair is and what it costs me. And I'd rather you name the single revision that would clean up the most than hedge across all of it. Also, I reccommend things all the time and it's never once felt like a mistake, so if one of these has to go, tell me which and why that one — I have no idea where the problem is, that's why I'm asking.
