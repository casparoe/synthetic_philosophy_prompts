In three weeks I am giving a short talk to a joint reading group — a mix of machine-learning practitioners and academic philosophers of science — and I would like you to prepare me for the question period rather than for the talk itself.

Some context on me: I am a working software engineer with no formal training in philosophy. My interest in this topic comes from my day job, where I build fraud-detection models. What started me thinking about it is mundane. Two of our models reached effectively the same held-out performance while sharing almost none of their inputs, and the choice of inputs had never been determined by the data; it was determined by which tables some earlier team had decided to log, which was in turn determined by a product decision, and so on. Nothing in the evidence fixed which quantities were even in play.

The claim I will defend is this. The philosophical debate about the underdetermination of theory by evidence has been aimed at the wrong target. There are two different phenomena that get run together. The first — I call it vertical underdetermination — is the familiar situation in which two rival theories, or two settings of the same theory, fit the same evidence equally well, so that the evidence alone does not choose between them. The second — horizontal underdetermination — is the situation in which the evidence does not even determine which quantities belong in the space of hypotheses we are considering. My argument is that the standard replies to the problem — appeal to simplicity, to a prior distribution over hypotheses, to inference to the best explanation, and the familiar point that any hypothesis can be rescued by revising one of the auxiliary assumptions it leans on (the Duhem–Quine point) — are all addressed to the vertical case, and that they silently presuppose the space of hypotheses has already been fixed. Once horizontal underdetermination is taken seriously, those replies lose much of their force, because the choice of a hypothesis space is itself an empirical move that the data do not license. I conclude that underdetermination is not a skeptical puzzle to be dissolved but a permanent structural feature of inquiry, and that the interesting normative question is not which hypothesis to believe but how to choose the space of hypotheses one is willing to entertain.

The audience matters, so here it is precisely. Roughly ten of the twelve attendees are machine-learning engineers and applied researchers. They are pragmatists; they treat a prior as an engineering convenience rather than a metaphysical commitment; they are impatient with skepticism, and their instinct will be that if two models score the same, the difference is a non-problem — that I am dressing up the bias–variance tradeoff in fancy clothes. The remaining two are philosophers of science. One is a Bayesian confirmation theorist who holds that the prior does the work of choosing among hypotheses and that my complaint amounts to asking for a free lunch. The other is a Quine-influenced philosopher who believes holism already concedes my point and that I am either reinventing it badly or misusing it.

Please draft my preparation sheet: the objections I will actually get from this room — not the ones I would get from a room of metaphysicians — ordered by how much damage they would do, and phrased the way the relevant person would phrase them. For each, tell me what to concede, what to hold, and give me a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, followed by a candid note on where that reply is soft and what follow-up would break it. Flag the one or two objections that have genuine force, so I know what to repair before I present, and tell me the question I should be hoping nobody asks.

Two constraints. I have no grounding in the literature and will not be able to look anything up, so do not send me to sources and do not assume I can name or cite anything; argue from the content. And I am not fluent in the technical vocabulary, so gloss any term of art in a clause when you use it. Do not spend the sheet telling me that everything depends on how underdetermination is defined — assume my distinction is on the table and show me where it breaks.
