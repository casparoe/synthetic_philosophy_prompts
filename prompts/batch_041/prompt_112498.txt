Here is the problem:

Maxwell's demon is sometimes said to show that the second law of thermodynamics is merely a statistical regularity that a sufficiently intelligent being could beat, and sometimes said to show nothing of the kind. Explain how the demon's procedure appears to threaten the second law, and explain how the threat is defused. In your answer, (a) state precisely what quantity appears to fall, and by how much per bit of information the demon gains about the gas; (b) identify the step in the demon's operation at which the compensating entropy increase must occur, and say why it must occur there rather than somewhere else in the procedure; and (c) use the Szilard one-molecule engine to exhibit the accounting over a single cycle, and say what becomes of the accounting if the demon never clears its memory.

Here is the student's answer:

The demon sits at a partition in a box of gas and works a small door. Fast molecules arriving from the left are let through to the right, and slow molecules arriving from the right are let through to the left. Nothing else about the box changes, and the door itself is massless and frictionless, so operating it costs no energy. After a while the right half is hotter than the left, even though the box began at a single uniform temperature. The entropy of the gas has therefore fallen, in an isolated system, with no work supplied from outside, which is exactly what the second law forbids. Nor is this a harmless bookkeeping matter, since the temperature difference can drive a heat engine; we would be extracting work from what is effectively a single reservoir at uniform temperature, which is what the Kelvin-Planck statement rules out. Quantitatively, the gas loses k ln 2 of entropy for every bit of information the demon gains about it, so the sorting is best understood as the conversion of information into negentropy.

The defusing move is to notice that the demon has to know which molecules are which, and that knowing is a physical process. To decide whether to open the door, the demon must first measure the molecule, and a measurement is not a free act of observation: the detector must couple to the molecule and register a result, and registering a result is a real physical transition in a real device. That transition costs at least kT ln 2 per bit recorded, dissipated as heat, where T is the temperature of the apparatus. This is Landauer's principle, and it is the whole resolution of the paradox: the entropy generated in the detector and its memory is at least as large as the entropy removed from the gas, so the total entropy of the gas, the demon and its apparatus never decreases. The demon's intelligence buys it nothing, because intelligence implemented in matter is still matter.

The Szilard engine exhibits the same accounting in miniature. A single molecule is confined to a box; the demon determines which half it occupies; a partition is inserted; and a piston is attached to the half containing the molecule, so that the molecule does kT ln 2 of work as it expands against it. The demon has thus extracted work from a system that started at a uniform temperature. But the bit recording which half the molecule was in has to be cleared before the engine can run again, since the memory must be returned to a standard state before it can be reused, and erasing one bit dissipates kT ln 2 as heat. The demon therefore breaks even at best, and the books must come out exactly even, because the second law allows no net decrease of entropy in a cycle. If the demon never clears its memory, the process is simply not cyclic, and the entropy it has not paid for sits in the memory rather than disappearing, so the second law is untouched.
