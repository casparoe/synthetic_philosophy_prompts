I am writing a long piece on blame — on why we hold one another responsible, and on what we are doing when we do — and before I start interviewing I want the specification on the table rather than buried in the footnotes. Set out the desiderata that an adequate account of moral responsibility, of the resentment, indignation, gratitude and hurt we take up towards one another, would have to meet: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the features of our practice it must explain rather than explain away, and the findings it must not contradict. Attach the motivating case or consideration to each requirement, and say which are genuine desiderata and which are merely the parochial demands of one camp. I want an account good enough to adjudicate the cases I am actually reporting, not merely one that is elegant.

Two phenomena should be handled explicitly, since they are what made me want to write the piece. The first is the person who accepts the excuse and still finds the self-blame apt — the surgeon whose patient dies through no one's fault, the driver who was not at fault — and who, offered the chance to have the feeling removed, would refuse. The second is the person who takes offence at being excused: who hears that they were not themselves, or that anyone would have done the same, as a demotion rather than an acquittal, and insists on the blame. An account that treats the absence of blame as always the kinder outcome has, I suspect, missed something; say what it has missed and what it must add.

Finally, flag the pair of desiderata you judge cannot be satisfied together — my own suspicion is that the requirement that blame track what was within the agent's control cannot be squared with the requirement that the account vindicate, rather than correct, the practice it describes — and say which single requirement does the most to rule out the current contenders.
