ok, I want a consistency audit on my views about induction and simplicity before I embarrass myself in a reading group. Here's the pile:

(1) Occam's razor is a genuine epistemic norm. When I say a simpler hypothesis is more likely to be true I mean it, and I don't think its secretly a fact about how I like to write things down.

(2) Solomonoff induction is the correct idealization of (1): assign each hypothesis prior 2^-(program length), and you get the predictor that converges on any environment some program can generate. That convergence is what I mean when I say simplicity is truth-conducive. The universal prior isn't a heuristic standing in for a prior — it is the prior.

(3) Kolmogorov complexity is objective in the sense that matters. The invariance theorem says switching universal machines changes K by at most an additive constant, so rankings of hypotheses aren't artifacts of my description language. Related verdict: if two programs both reproduce my data perfectly and one is 20 bits shorter, I think the shorter one is genuinely more likely to keep predicting right, not just cheaper for me to write.

(4) I take no free lunch seriously. No inductive method beats every other method across all environments. Every prior is a bet that some environments are more likely than others.

(5) "Model plus data given the model" is bookkeeping. There's one compression problem, and the "model" is just whichever part of the code I decided to call the model. A short model isn't a separate epistemic virture on top of a short total code.

(6) I know none of this is computable and I don't lose sleep over it.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they clash, and how bad is each real clash? Rank them by severity rather than telling me they're all in tension to different degrees. For each real one, what's the cheapest repair, and what does that repair cost me elsewhere — in particular, if I patch (3), what happens to (1)? Then tell me if there's a single revision that cleans up the most, and which commitments I'd have to read differently rather than drop. Don't just tell me they operate at different levels.
