I'm in my third year, taking a course that sits between philosophy of science and interpretability, and my final paper has to be a specification rather than an argument: I need to write out what any adequate theory of mechanism identity would have to deliver, for one specific purpose — mechanistic anomaly detection. Please act as my philosopher of science here, someone who works on mechanistic explanation in the sciences and has also spent real time with the interpretability literature, and think this through carefully rather than handing me the standard list. Sketch a long list of candidate requirements to yourself first, then prune it down and justify what survives.

Background, so you know exactly what I'm after: the idea behind mechanistic anomaly detection is that you have a system whose behaviour you trust, and you want to flag when it does something produced by a different internal process than the one that produced the trusted behaviour — even when the behaviour itself looks fine. So everything turns on when two episodes of processing count as the same mechanism and when they count as different. That is the theory I need desiderata for.

Lead with your bottom line: your first sentence should name the single requirement you think does the most work in excluding bad theories, and your first paragraph should say whether you think the full list is jointly satisfiable. No preamble, no restating of my question. Then give the requirements, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp.

I want the list built out of cases, so here are the phenomena I've been chewing on and that the theory has to get right:

- Two networks trained from different random initialisations that compute the same function by visibly different internal routes: one mechanism or two?
- A system that gets a task right by a generalising route, and a system that gets the same task right by a route that looks like a lookup table: behaviourally indistinguishable, and exactly the case anomaly detection is meant to catch.
- A sub-computation that survives fine-tuning but whose job in the larger computation changes — the same part, now doing something else.
- The same algorithm implemented at two different levels of description, say the same arithmetic routine realised once in a feedforward network and once in a network with recurrence. Is mechanism identity level-relative or level-neutral?
- A computation that is real but not localised: no single unit or small set of units carries it, because it lives spread across a great many units that are each doing several jobs at once.
- A computation you cannot intervene on cleanly, because the only interventions that change it also change something else you'd rather hold fixed.
- A computation genuinely reused across two tasks, versus the same input-output pattern arising twice by coincidence.

The requirements should cover at least three things: the distinctions the theory must not collapse (mechanism vs. function vs. algorithm vs. implementation; type vs. token; being present vs. being responsible; sameness vs. mere similarity of role); the results it must be consistent with (identical behaviour doesn't entail identical mechanism; massively over-parameterised systems admit many circuits for one function; parts can be non-separable; mechanisms nest inside one another); and the operational constraint that whatever sameness turns out to be, you have to be able to test for it by intervening on a system you can run but whose internals you only partly understand.

The part I most want is the no-go. I have a suspicion that you cannot simultaneously have (i) a criterion neutral about the level of description and (ii) a criterion you can actually test by intervening, because any intervention has to be typed at some level; and a second suspicion that a criterion invariant under every behaviour-preserving change collapses mechanism into function and so defeats the purpose, while a criterion sensitive to every parameter change makes every training step a new mechanism. Tell me whether either suspicion is right, which requirement has to give if so, and what the least costly repair looks like.
