Two people in my metaphysics reading group have been going round in circles for months: one is a counterpart theorist (no such thing as one and the same individual in two worlds — "she could have been a pilot" is about a different, similar individual in another world), the other thinks that's a dodge and that de re modality is about the very same individual existing in other worlds. They've agreed to try an adversarial collaboration, and I said I'd help draft it. Write the joint statement they could both sign. Have them bracket the metaphysics of possible worlds entirely — they agree to take worlds, and quantification over them, as given — so the whole fight is about how modal claims about particular individuals get represented. I want: a numbered section of claims both can sign (about ordinary de re claims, about the necessity of identity, about what isnt actually in dispute); then the disagreement stated twice, once in wording each side would put its own name to; then the cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether the counterpart relation is just doing the work of identity under another name, and another is whether its inconstancy — it's a similarity relation, so which counterpart you get can shift with what's salient — is a virtue or a defect. For each crux, give a case both sides agree bears on it and record each side's prediction in advance. Two cases I want called explicitly: (a) a person in another world with the same life story but different biological origins — is "she could have had different parents" true? (b) an individual with two equally similar counterparts in a single world — is the corresponding modal claim true, false, or neither? Finish by saying which part of the dispute, if any, they now think was verbal.
