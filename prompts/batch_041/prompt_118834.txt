I've been chewing over this idea of a "safe Pareto improvement" and I think I've built an argument that the whole notion is empty, but I can't tell whether the argument is a cheap trick or whether the idea really is empty. Here's the thought behind it, as I understand it: when two agents bargain without knowing each other's preferences, there can still be changes that are guaranteed to leave both of them at least as well off, because the change is better for each of them under every preference profile the other might have. I'd like that to work. Here's my arguement that it can't.

(1) Say a change P is a safe Pareto improvement over the status quo if and only if, for every preference profile consistent with what the agents know about each other, each agent weakly prefers the outcome under P to the status quo outcome.
(2) What the two agents know about each other's preferences is exhausted by the fact that each is rational and each would take a strictly better deal if one were on the table.
(3) Now take any change P that alters the outcome under at least one profile.
(4) Given (2), nothing rules out a profile under which Ann ranks the outcome under P strictly below the status quo, so the set of consistent profiles contains one; call it π.
(5) By (1), P counts as a safe Pareto improvement only if Ann weakly prefers the outcome under P to the status quo under π as well.
(6) She doesn't. So P isn't a safe Pareto improvement.
(7) Since P was arbitrary, no change that alters the outcome is a safe Pareto improvement, and the notion is empty.

I'd like to deny (7), since the whole point was supposed to be that there are non-empty cases, and I'd like to keep (1) if I can, though I'm not wedded to it. So something in the middle has to give. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost me — what does the notion look like afterwards, and what work can it still do? If you think the argument is actually valid and one of the premises just has to go, fine, tell me which one is cheapest to give up and what I'm paying for it. Tell me how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the part of it you're least sure about. No need for references, I just want the diagnosis. American English please.
