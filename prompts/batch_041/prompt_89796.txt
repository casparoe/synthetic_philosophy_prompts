I've been reading about Boltzmann brains lately and I think I've more or less landed on a view, but I want a sanity check on whether it actually hangs together before I present it at a seminar. Here's where I am.

(1) I take the standard worry seriously. In a universe that just keeps going and keeps producing vacuum fluctuations, random observers should eventually vastly outnumber the ones who formed the ordinary way, and I don't think you escape that just by pointing out how improbable a fluctuation is — infinity is patient. (2) All the same, I think I'm almost certainly not a Boltzmann brain. I've got a long, detailed, internally consistent stream of experience, and that seems way more likely to come from a normal evolutionary history than from something that popped into being with a full set of false memories. (3) I think there's a fact of the matter about the right measure over observers, even if nobody has found it yet — it's not just a matter of convention. (4) I think a good measure shouldn't make our observations evidentially worthless; if your favorite measure implies that everything you've ever seen is noise, that counts against the measure. (5) I don't want the whole topic to be unfalsifiable — if nothing could ever count against the Boltzmann brain conclusion, I'd take that as a mark against it.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for the real conflicts? And be straight with me about (5) specifically — what would count against the conclusion, and is my version of the worry actually insulated from all of it?
