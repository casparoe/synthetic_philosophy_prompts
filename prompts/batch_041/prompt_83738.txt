The contrast Strawson draws between the participant attitude and the objective attitude is usually taught as a dichotomy: either one addresses a person as a fellow member of the moral community, or one steps back and treats them as an object of explanation or management. But the objective attitude is better understood as a band than as a point. At one end sits the dispassionate stance of the observer, who explains conduct in causal or statistical terms and asks nothing of the agent; at the other sits the manipulator, who is intensely engaged with the agent's behaviour but engaged with it as something to be steered. The interesting conceptual work gets done in the middle of that band.

Give me an example of a concrete, presently existing practice that occupies the middle. It should be a practice in which people are routinely dealt with in a way that is neither dispassionate explanation nor straightforward manipulation, and yet is plainly not the participant attitude either. Avoid the stock illustrations — young children, the severely mentally ill, addicts, brainwashed agents, the criminal law, and psychotherapy — and avoid resting the example on a single dramatic case; I want a standing institutional, commercial, or technological practice.

Then say exactly what conceptual issue the example exposes, addressing at least the following:

- Is the objective attitude a single stance or a heterogeneous family? If heterogeneous, does the participant/objective contrast survive at a higher level of generality — engaged versus detached — or does it collapse?
- In your practice, is anyone still being held responsible? If so, does that show that responsibility can survive the withdrawal of the reactive attitudes, and does that refute a constitutive reading of the reactive-attitude account of responsibility or merely a definitional one?
- Does the practice address the person in the second person while withholding the participant stance? If it does, what does that do to the assumption that the two stances are exclusive and exhaustive?
- What would distinguish a genuinely intermediate practice from manipulation wearing a friendly face — that is, what would show that I had chosen a bad example?

Finish by saying which of these issues is the deepest, and what turns on it.
