I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, and I keep encountering a structure in dispositional ascriptions that I don't have a name for. Suppose a substance is disposed to dissolve in water. The standard fink case is one where the stimulus removes the disposition before it can manifest; the standard mask case is one where an external condition blocks manifestation while the disposition remains. My case is different: the substance dissolves only when the water is agitated, and the agitation that triggers dissolution also disperses the substance so rapidly that it never reaches the concentration at which we would count it as having dissolved. The disposition is still attributed, the trigger is the normal one, and nothing external blocks it; the manifestation is self-limiting because the trigger's own effects prevent the completion of the manifestation. A second case: a person is disposed to be generous when they feel secure, but the act of giving makes them feel insecure, so the generosity is real but never runs its full course. Is there an established name for this? I know about finks and masks, but this seems to be neither: the disposition isn't removed before manifestation, and the blocker isn't an independent mask but the manifestation process itself. I'm not after a label; I want to know whether it's a distinct category, what its boundaries are, and how it differs from self-finking dispositions, antidotes, and merely partial manifestations.
