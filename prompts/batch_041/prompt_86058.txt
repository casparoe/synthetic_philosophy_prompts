You are to act as a philosopher of science with specialist expertise in the metaphysics of explanation and in the reductionism debates in biology and cognitive science. I am a philosophy professor designing a seminar session, and I need a rigorous, self-contained piece of conceptual analysis rather than a survey. Please think carefully and work in stages: first brainstorm the several ways the distinction I describe below might be drawn and note where they come apart; then commit to the reading you think is best; then test that reading against the cases I give. Do not supply citations, reading lists, or references — I care about the concepts, not the bibliography, and you should not guess at attributions beyond the distinction itself. Where the material forces a choice between readings, say so explicitly rather than smoothing it over.

Here is the issue. Consider the familiar distinction between cranes and skyhooks, and the accompanying warning against greedy reductionism. As I understand it, a crane is a higher-level feature that is itself built by lower-level, mindless processes, so that invoking it does not smuggle in anything unbuilt; a skyhook is a source of order that is supposed to do explanatory work without anything below it having paid for that order. Greedy reductionism, by contrast, is a charge against explanations that try to reach the bottom too quickly — that announce that some higher-level phenomenon is nothing but some lower-level process while skipping the intermediate machinery that would connect them. What I cannot get clear on is how these two commitments fit together. If nothing enters the world except by being built up from mindless parts, it looks as though any higher-level explanation must eventually be cashed out downward, which is exactly what the anti-greedy-reductionism warning tells us not to demand. So on what basis do we keep a higher-level explanation while rejecting a skyhook, given that both are, in some sense, explanations that stop short of the bottom?

I would like you to do three things.

First, state precisely what the crane/skyhook distinction is a distinction between. Is it a claim about what exists (whether a given higher-level feature really is produced by lower-level processes) or a claim about what an explanation accomplishes (whether it earns its explanatory keep, compresses the data, supports prediction and intervention)? Or is it both, at different levels?

Second, locate greedy reductionism relative to that distinction. Is it a failure on the same axis as a skyhook, the mirror-image error, or an orthogonal failure — a matter of explanatory patience rather than of what is posited? A colleague proposes the following test: an explanation is a skyhook just in case it could not, even in principle, be given a lower-level mechanism, and it is greedy reductionism just in case it claims to have supplied such a mechanism without actually specifying one. Assess that test: does it capture the two notions, conflate them, or misdescribe one of them?

Third, use these cases as probes, and say for each whether it is a skyhook, a piece of greedy reductionism, a legitimate higher-level explanation, or none of these — and, crucially, what feature of the case drives your verdict:
(a) A persistent social institution is explained by its being functional for the group, with no account of how individual-level processes could generate or maintain it.
(b) A cognitive capacity is explained by observing that it is nothing but neurons firing.
(c) A population's traits are said to have changed because the new trait was needed for survival.
(d) The clustering of prices in a market is explained by arbitrage, stated entirely at the level of prices and traders.
(e) A trait is attributed to a gene for it, where the same sentence might be a crane or a case of greedy reductionism depending on whether the intervening developmental and behavioral machinery is specified.

Finally, give me a usable diagnostic: a procedure I can hand to students for telling, in a live case, whether a proposed higher-level explanation is a crane or a skyhook, and whether a proposed reduction is legitimate or greedy. If you conclude that the distinction is less stable than my framing assumes, or that my framing presupposes a false dichotomy, say so and explain why.
