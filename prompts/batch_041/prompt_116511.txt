For years I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums, and I want to write a dialogue for a post that gets past the standard "the axiom of choice is controversial because of Banach–Tarski" summary. The speakers are two working set theorists, equally sharp, neither of them a strawman: one is a classical mathematician who works in ZFC and treats the axiom as a legitimate, unremarkable part of the iterative conception of sets; the other works constructively and regards it as a substantive existence claim that is simply false. I want the exchange to turn on the hard questions rather than the famous paradoxes: whether "existence without a witness" is a coherent notion at all; the fact that in a constructive set theory with separation, full choice already yields excluded middle, which suggests the two of them are really disagreeing about logic rather than about sets; and what the classical side has to concede — that giving up choice is precisely what buys measurability and the Baire property for every set of reals. Have the constructive side press the objection that the classical defence of choice is circular, since it leans on a notion of definability that cannot be stated for the language as a whole, and have the classical side answer that objection rather than dodge it. Each character should respond to the other's actual points rather than to a caricature. Close with a short paragraph from whichever speaker you think has the better of the argument, saying which single consideration would change their mind and where their case is most likely to break down.
