I've been reading decision-theory blogs and forums for long enough that my views have hardened into a package, and I'd like a consistency audit rather than a sympathetic reading. Here is roughly where I've landed.

(1) Credences are probabilities, and rational choice maximizes expected utility with respect to them. I take the content of "rational" to be exhausted by something like: your preferences are representable by a utility function together with a single probability function, and you update that function by conditionalization.

(2) The Ellsberg pattern is rationally permissible. Given an urn of ninety balls — thirty red, and sixty others that are black or yellow in an unknown proportion — I would rather bet on red than on black, and I would rather bet on black-or-yellow than on red-or-yellow. I do not regard this as a bias to be trained out of myself; I think it tracks something real about the difference between risk and ambiguity.

(3) The sure-thing principle is a genuine requirement of rationality. If two acts yield the same outcome in some state of the world, then the value of that common outcome should not affect my preference between the two acts.

(4) There is no separate faculty for handling ambiguity. An "unknown probability" is just higher-order uncertainty: I have a credence over what the first-order probability is, and that is the whole story. There is nothing left over for a distinct attitude toward ambiguity to attach to.

(5) I am a causal decision theorist, and I think Newcomb-style problems show that evidential decision theory is simply mistaken, not merely a rival package that happens to score differently.

(6) Exploitability across separately considered choices is not by itself a failure of rationality. A set of preferences can be such that a clever bookie could take money off a sequence of me, and still be preferences I am entitled to hold.

What I want from you is an audit: which of these actually conflict, which pairs only look like they conflict, and how severe each real conflict is. For every genuine tension, say what has to give and what the cheapest repair costs me elsewhere in the package. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as uniformly bad, and note explicitly any pair whose apparent incompatibility dissolves once the terms are made precise. I have a suspicion that the real problem is not the pairing I would name first, so I would rather you work through the set than confirm the obvious.
