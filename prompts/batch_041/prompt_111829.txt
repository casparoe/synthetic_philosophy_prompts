I have been assembling cases in which something is said to refer to itself in the Gödelian way, and my verdicts on them seem to me to have a shape I cannot yet state. A program that prints its own source code: genuine self-reference. A program that prints the sentence "this program prints its own source code": not genuine, since it describes itself from the outside and nothing in its workings turns back on itself. A compiler that compiles its own source code: genuine. A compiler that was merely produced by the previous release: not genuine; that is a lineage, not a loop. A statute containing a clause that specifies how that statute may be amended: genuine. A constitution that declares itself the supreme law: not genuine. A thermostat wired to the furnace it controls: not genuine. A sentence of arithmetic that asserts its own unprovability: genuine, and the paradigm of the kind. A picture of two hands, each drawing the other: not genuine, since nothing in how the picture was made is governed by what it depicts. A language model retrained on transcripts of its own earlier outputs: not genuine, even though the causal loop is real, because I deny that anything in the model represents the model to the model. And I accept the further step that Hofstadter takes in Gödel, Escher, Bach, that a mind is a loop of this kind, though I cannot say what in it does the representing.

What principle, if there is one, delivers these verdicts? State it precisely, giving for each clause of it the case above that motivates the clause and the verdicts it yields on cases I have not considered, including these two: a program that correctly prints its own length, and a court that must rule on the validity of the amendment that created it. Then tell me honestly which of my verdicts the principle forces me to abandon — in particular, whether the program that prints its own source and the self-trained model can both be sorted as I have sorted them.
