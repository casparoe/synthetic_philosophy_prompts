I'm putting together a reading-group packet on Hegel and war, and what I want is the document that never actually gets written: the joint statement that two people who've been arguing about this for two years could both put their names on. The two parties are, first, someone who thinks Hegel's treatment of war in the Philosophy of Right is a serious and defensible philosophical position — the claim that war is not simply to be treated as an absolute evil, plus the addition where he suggests that long stretches of peace let a nation stagnate and that war can preserve something like the ethical health of a nation, with the image of winds keeping the sea from going foul — and, second, a Kantian-flavored cosmopolitan who thinks that whole line is at best poetic noise and at worst a dangerous glorification of violence that any decent ethics should throw out. They keep talking past each other, and I suspect a lot of it is that they're using the same words for different things.

Please write the document they should have produced. I want:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — things both sides can sign, including about what Hegel actually asserts (as opposed to what he's popularly accused of asserting), about what the state is for in his system, and about what a defensible position on war would even have to look like.

2. A disagreements table. For each row, state the disagreement in neutral wording, then give each side's own formulation of its view in wording that side would actually accept, then say whether the disagreement is genuine or merely verbal. If part of it is verbal, say which part and why.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. I'd bet some of them are: whether the ethical-health claim is an empirical prediction about what happens to nations or a conceptual point about the state's relation to its members; whether the claim is supposed to apply to all wars or only to wars fought for the right reasons; whether it's about the character of individual citizens or about the vitality of an objective ethical order; whether the winds-and-sea image is doing real argumentative work or is just rhetoric; and whether any of this gives Hegel a view about which wars may legitimately be started.

4. For each crux, a concrete case, historical example, or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about that case written down in advance. Something like a state that has stayed out of war for a very long time, or a plainly unjust war that nonetheless mobilized a society, or a defensive war that did neither.

Two constraints: no block quotes from the text, since I don't want anyone half-remembering wording — paraphrase instead. And no reading lists or citations; if a specific passage matters, describe what it claims in your own words. Keep the whole thing in American English, and keep the agreed-claims section from going vacuous — if a claim is so weak that both sides would sign it without giving up anything, cut it or say why it's still worth recording. I'd also like it if the document ends up showing that some of their original arguemants were about two seperate things wearing the same word.
