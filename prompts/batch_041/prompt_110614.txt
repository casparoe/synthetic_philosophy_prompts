I recently retired from experimental physics and have been spending my new free time reading philosophy. I am trying to write down my views on the demarcation problem for a seminar, and I would like a consistency audit first. Here is what I believe. (1) A theory is scientific only if it is falsifiable, where falsifiability is assessed relative to the auxiliary assumptions and instruments we actually have: it must forbid some observable outcomes. (2) The familiar underdetermination point is correct: no hypothesis is tested in isolation, and a failed prediction can always be absorbed by revising an auxiliary assumption, so falsification is never logically compelled. (3) The proper unit of appraisal is not the isolated theory but a research program, and what marks a scientific program is a record of successful novel predictions rather than an ability to accommodate data already known. (4) Historical sciences such as evolutionary biology and cosmology, and speculative parts of physics such as string theory or multiverse cosmology, are not pseudoscience even when their central claims cannot now be directly falsified; they are scientific because they are continuous with successful theories and are supported by consilience, unification, and indirect evidence. (5) In public and legal contexts—creationism, astrology, homeopathy—we need a reasonably sharp criterion, and falsifiability is the one we should use. I would like to know whether they can all be held at once; if not, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Finally, tell me which single revision would resolve the most.
