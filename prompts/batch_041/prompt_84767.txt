Please give me your credence in the following claim, stated precisely enough to be wrong: across the class of large-scale historical outcomes of the last five centuries — which coalitions win major wars, which economies industrialize first, which regimes and religions survive — the coarse-grained outcome is not attractor-dominated but micro-sensitive, meaning that in at least half of such cases there existed some perturbation available to a single ordinary person within about a year of the outcome that would have reversed the coarse-grained result.

Give me a number, then the decomposition that produces it. At minimum, separate: (i) the share of macro-historical regularity attributable to structural constraint — geography, demography, technology, path dependence — versus genuinely open outcomes; (ii) whether the underlying dynamics are stochastic or deterministic-but-chaotic, and whether that distinction survives coarse-graining at all; (iii) whether coarse-graining typically absorbs micro-divergence, i.e. whether history has wide attractor basins, or instead amplifies it; (iv) whether counterfactuals of this kind are well-defined, and whether reading them interventionistically rather than as claims about similarity between possible worlds changes the answer; and (v) the reference-class problem — what counts as a single ordinary person's perturbation, and whether the claim collapses into trivial truth or trivial falsehood as that class is widened or narrowed.

Assign each term a rough probability, say how they combine, name the term the final number is most sensitive to, and tell me which piece of evidence or argument would move it most. My own credence is about 0.25.
