Write me a joke about teleosemantics -- the view that what a mental state is *about* is fixed by its history, i.e. what it was selected to do (its proper function), rather than by whatever happens to be triggering it right now. The punchline should actually turn on that, so someone who's read Millikan or Dretske would laugh and someone who hasn't would be kinda lost. Heres a scenario you can use, or ignore it and do your own: a startup trains an image classifier, one neuron fires for cats, everyone's happy. Then they copy the exact same weights into a brand-new, never-trained network, and that neuron also fires for cats. The engineer says obviously both networks represent cats -- same weights, same behavior. The teleosemanticist says no: only one of them means anything at all, the other is basically a rock that happens to light up when a cat walks by. Make that funny, or come up with something else that lands the same point. A few lines is plenty, and bonus points if you can sneak in why a frog's bug-detecting neuron means fly and not fly-or-little-dark-speck.
