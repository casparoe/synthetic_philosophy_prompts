I retired a few years ago from a career in experimental physics, and for the last two of those years I have been reading contemporary metaphysics with more enthusiasm than discipline. I have come around to a picture I would put as follows. Besides the causal relations my old colleagues and I spent our working lives on, there is a distinct, non-causal relation of grounding, in which one fact, entity, or property exists or obtains because of others; that relation is irreflexive, asymmetric, transitive, and hyperintensional (two things can be necessarily equivalent without either grounding the other), and it is explanatory rather than merely modal, so that the obtaining of the ground does not exhaust what it is for the grounded thing to obtain. The picture then adds a further claim, which is the one I actually care about: grounding is well-founded. Chains of dependence terminate in a fundamental level — things that have no grounds — so that reality has a bottom story, and everything else is ultimately grounded in that bottom level. I am also drawn to the idea that there is one grounding relation rather than several, though I hold that part more loosely.

What I want from you is a map of this picture's exposure. What would have to be true for it to be false, and which of the things that would count against it are decisive rather than merely awkward? I have some candidates of my own, and I would rather have them assessed than catalogued, along with anything I have missed. Circular structures, in which two facts ground each other or a fact grounds itself. Structures with no minimal elements, whether because the chain descends without end or because it is dense, so that there is something between any two levels. Cases in which the grounded and the ground are modally inseparable, which seem to me to threaten the explanatory surplus of grounding over plain necessitation, and cases in which grounding appears to relate things that are not really distinct. The deflationary suggestion that grounding talk is a regimented way of speaking about explanation or supervenience and names no relation at all. Rival pictures that keep grounding but reject well-foundedness — for instance one on which there is a single fundamental entity of which everything else is a part, or one on which every level is as fundamental as any other. And a nagging one: whether well-foundedness is even the property I should care about, or whether the bottom story needs something stronger, such as a compact and unified basis, a small set of generators rather than a sprawling heap of ungrounded facts; and whether a totality fact, if the view needs one, is itself something that requires a ground.

Rank what you give me from fatal to merely awkward, say explicitly which of these a committed defender would concede and which they would dismiss as question-begging, and tell me whether the picture as I have stated it risks anything at all or has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability — and if the latter, whether that is a vice in this case or the unavoidable price of a view at this level of generality. One thing I would find especially persuasive, and I will be honest about my leanings: an argument that the fundamental level does no explanatory work that the grounding relation alone could not do, or that the bottom story is a projection of our explanatory practices rather than a feature of the world. I came to this from effective field theory, where the working lesson was that no scale is privileged and each description is a description of the same world at a resolution we chose; I cannot tell whether that lesson transfers, or whether the demand for a fundamental level is simply the metaphysical descendant of the demand for a final theory, and I would like to be told which.
