I've been lurking on LessWrong and the associated blogosphere for like ten years now, which means I've absorbed a lot of arguments and produced basically zero fiction, so I'm trying to fix that. The thing I keep chewing on is the precautionary principle — not the courtroom version, the vibe version, the one people invoke as "don't do the new thing unless you can show it's safe." What gets me is that everyone I know applies it to the stuff they were already scared of and never to the stuff they're used to, which smells like status quo bias wearing a lab coat.

So here's the story I want. Pick a concrete setting — a town council, a biotech startup, a biosecurity agency, a family, whatever you think has the most teeth — and commit to it. Somebody in that setting sincerely adopts the rule that you don't introduce a new thing (a technology, a policy, a drug, a move across the country) unless you can rule out serious harm from it, and the story follows what that rule does to them over a couple of years. I don't want a hypocrite or a villain who's secretly in it for the money — I want the honest, good-faith version, because that's the one that's actually scary. The pressure I'm most interested in is that the rule seems to eat itself: refusing to act is itself an action with consequences, so a strict reading would have to forbid its own enforcement. And I want to see whether the characters can find a version of the principle that survives that — is there a real difference between "don't act until proven safe" and "scale your precautions to how bad and how hard-to-undo the harm would be"? Reversibility feels load-bearing to me and I can't articulate why.

Two things I want from the story specifically. First, a scene where the reversibility move actually bites: someone objects to a change, and another character asks whether they'd object just as hard if the situation were the other way around — i.e. if the change were the status quo and reverting it were the proposal — and it's genuinely unclear who's right. Second, no cop-out ending where caution is either vindicated or condemned wholesale. I want the story to show what the principle gets right, not just what it gets wrong.
