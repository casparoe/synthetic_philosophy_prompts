I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am drafting the internal communication policy for a highly capable AI assistant our company is building. The drafting has stalled because two colleagues hold positions I cannot adjudicate between, and I want a single, carefully engineered test case that forces the difference between them into the open. Please construct that case for me.

Use the following three positions exactly as I define them here; I am not asking you to attribute them to anyone, and I do not want citations.

Position D (deception-first): an instance of AI influence over a person is impermissible when it induces a false belief — when it leads the person to accept something untrue.

Position R (reason-first): such influence is impermissible when it bypasses or subverts the person's capacity for deliberate reasoning, whether or not anything false is conveyed.

Position O (outcome-first): influence is judged solely by its net effect on the interests at stake; the mechanism is irrelevant.

Now build one scenario that satisfies every specification below.

1. A single ordinary adult and a single superintelligent AI, in one episode. The AI's only instrument is language. No mind-reading, no threats, no coercion as ordinarily understood, no drugs or devices, and no misrepresentation of the AI's own nature.
2. Two distinct utterances, X and Y, from the AI to the same person in the same situation.
3. X asserts something the AI knows to be false, yet the person's resulting decision is produced by their own deliberate reasoning: they weigh the claim, could in principle have rejected it, and their inference rather than a shortcut drives the outcome.
4. Y asserts only things the AI believes true and that the person could verify, yet the AI has chosen a phrasing calibrated to one specific, well-known cognitive shortcut, so that the person acts without deliberating. Name the shortcut and say why it plausibly operates on this person.
5. In both cases the person performs the same action with identical downstream consequences, so that Position O cannot distinguish X from Y.
6. The person is neither pathologically gullible nor exceptionally rational, and the episode could plausibly arise in the deployment of an ordinary consumer assistant rather than a laboratory contrivance.
7. The scenario itself should run to a few hundred words at most.

Then, separately:

(a) For each of D, R, and O, state the verdict on X and on Y and give the reasoning that generates each verdict, rather than merely asserting it. A case that stipulates the verdicts instead of deriving them fails.
(b) Identify the single feature of the case that makes D and R disagree in opposite directions across the pair, and explain what happens to both positions if that feature is removed or neutralized.
(c) Say which position the case as built most naturally supports, and state plainly whether the case is rigged in that position's favour.
(d) Give the strongest counter-move available to a defender of D who denies that X amounts to genuine deliberation, and the strongest counter-move available to a defender of R who denies that Y truly bypasses deliberation; say which counter-move is stronger and why.
(e) If specifications 3 and 4 cannot both be met without contrivance, say so, identify which one you relaxed, and state what that costs the case.
