I retired from a biology lab a few years ago and I've been catching up on philosophy ever since, mostly through a small reading group that lets me pick the topic now and then. My turn is coming up and I want to do the self-amendment puzzle: what happens when a system of rules contains a rule about how to change the rules, and nothing stops that rule from being turned on itself. There's a game from 1982, Nomic, where amending a rule is itself a legal move and players can even ammend the rule saying that rules must be obeyed; the same structure shows up in constitutional law with amendment clauses, with clauses that declare themselves unamendable, and with rules that lower the supermajority needed to change them. What I want for the group is a decision tree that sorts people by what they think is actually going on, not by which -ism they happen to like. So each node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing where it leads, phrased as concretely as I can make it — can the clause authorizing amendment be used to amend itself, and if a system forbids that, does the forbidding clause really hold against a later vote to repeal it, that kind of thing. The leaves should be the positions, named where there's a name going around (the presupposed basic norm, the rule of recognition as settled official practice, self-embracing authority so there was never a puzzle, both rules genuinely valid so accept the contradiction, the regress you hit if you insist on ascending to a meta-rule), and marked unnamed where there isn't one. Tell me which branches have nobody living in them and why — incoherent, or just unattractive, or simply unexplored. And flag any two leaves the tree can't really separate on these questions; I'd rather know than have it papered over. Eight-ish nodes is plenty, since the whole thing has to fit on one page as a handout.
