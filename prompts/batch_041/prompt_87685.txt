I'm building a little collection of philosophy-flavored writing prompts and I need one more, and this ones been bugging me. Write me a mock customer-support FAQ page — deadpan, corporate-helpdesk voice, the kind of page that answers questions nobody wanted to ask — for a consumer service that scores people on how much they deserve. Something like the Merit Account, run by a bureau. Treat the score exactly the way a credit score gets treated: you can check it, dispute it, it affects your loan rates and your kids' school placement, and there's a phone tree you can't escape.

Rules of the game:

- Roughly 800 words. Headline, then Q&As, plus one "related articles" sidebar with three fake article titles.
- Never break character. No winking, no "obviously this is absurd." The FAQ writer sincerely believes the system is fine and is just explaining it, and the reader should still be able to tell what's being skewered.
- The jokes have to come from the philosophy being right, so someone who's thought hard about desert laughs instead of wincing. In particular the page should have to grapple with, at minimum: (a) what the score is measuring against — deserved relative to *what* baseline, and what happens when a user actually asks that; (b) the fact that nobody can observe the counterfactual where you didn't recieve your advantages, and how the bureau papers over that gap without admitting it's guessing; (c) the regress — if effort is what earns you credit, and effort flows from a character you didn't pick, then the bureau needs a second score for how much of your first score was really yours, and the FAQ should handle that recursion the way a real help page handles a billing edge case; (d) the seperate difference between deserving something and being entitled to it, and why the bureau's legal team cares a lot about that distinction; (e) some version of the self-undermining problem — if your score shapes your circumstances, then less and less of what you do is attributable to you, so scores should be shrinking, and the page needs an answer for why they aren't.
- Invent whatever internal machinery you need (tiers, forms, a hotline, a "provisional deservingness" status for minors). Don't cite real papers or philosophers — the voice shouldn't sound academic, it should sound like a water utility.
- Don't name real people or companies.
- Finish on a question whose answer quietly gives the game away without ever admitting it.
