I'm a grad student in early modern European history and I keep getting into the same fight with a friend who works on late antiquity, and I finally want to settle it properly instead of just reenacting it. The fight is about whether the periods we both use all the time — 'the Middle Ages', 'the Renaissance', 'the Scientific Revolution' — are real features of the past or just convenient boxes that historians built and then forgot they built. She keeps saying things like 'the Renaissance is a nineteenth-century invention' and I keep answering 'fine, the label is, but something really did happen in Italy in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries,' and we never get any further than that. I don't think either of us is being dumb, I think we're genuinely arguing past each other, and I suspect at least part of it is a fight about the word 'real' rather than about the past. So here's what I want: put on the hat of a very careful philosopher of history — someone who could referee this dispute without secretly rooting for one side — think it through step by step before you write anything, and then draft the joint statement that my friend and I could actually both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Call the two camps the period realists and the period nominalists (or whatever you think the honest names are), and make the document the kind of thing we could put in a drawer and pull out the next time we start yelling.

I want the agreed-claims part first, numbered, and I want it to be substantive rather than mushy — not 'periods are useful in some contexts' boilerplate, but the actual things both of us should concede, e.g. about what a period label is doing, about whether conventionality rules out truth, about what kind of evidence could ever settle a boundary date, about the difference between a period being arbitrary and a period being merely chosen, and about whether the micro-level continuity that medievalists love actually threatens the macro-level discontinuities that modernists point to. Then a disagreements table: each row the exact point still in dispute, with each side's position written in wording that side would accept as its own, and a column saying whether you think it's a genuine disagreement or a verbal one (and if verbal, what the two of us are actually disagreeing about underneath). Then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of us — and for each crux a concrete case or piece of evidence that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance (something like: if you're right about X, we should expect to find Y in the sources, and if I'm right, Z). Please treat the three periods separately where they need it, since I have a hunch the Scientific Revolution behaves differently from the other two and I want to know if you agree. If you conclude that a chunk of our fight is just about two senses of 'real' — mind-independent versus non-arbitrary — say so plainly and tell us which claims survive once we fix the word. And be honest if one side looks like it's holding an indefensible line; I'd rather know.
