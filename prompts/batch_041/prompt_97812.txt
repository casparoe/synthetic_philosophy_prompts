Okay, I'm in a philosophy of science class right now and there's this pattern I keep running into that I can't find a name for. It goes like this. Someone gives a genuinely satisfying explanation of some higher-level thing using only the parts and how they interact -- why a gas heats up, why a flock of starlings turns all at once, why a market panics -- and then in the very next sentence says the higher-level thing is 'nothing but' those parts. But those two claims don't seem to sit together for me. The part-by-part story always needs one very specific arrangement, and meanwhile the higher-level description is the one that actually does the predicting, and it stays true even when you swap the parts out entirely (fish instead of birds, particles instead of people). So the macro story is doing work the micro story can't, at the exact moment someone's telling me it's just the micro story. Is there a name for this exact squeeze -- the gap between 'you can derive the higher level from the parts' and 'the higher level is nothing over and above the parts'? I've heard 'reduction,' 'emergence,' and 'multiple realizability' thrown around but none of them feels like quite the thing I'm pointing at. If there's an established term, tell me, but honestly the label is the least of it -- I mostly want the carving: what this phenomenon actually is, which nearby ideas it is definately not, and where its edges are, with a couple of cases that sit right on the boundary. Please don't just hand me a word and stop.
