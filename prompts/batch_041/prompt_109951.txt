I'm a novelist — not a scientist, so forgive the lay framing — and I'm writing a character who does human behaviour research, which means I've spent the better part of a year reading pop evolutionary psychology and listening to people fight about it at parties. There's a move I keep running into and I don't have a word for it.

Example one. Someone tells me people are more willing to help blood relatives than friends, because helping relatives is a way of getting your genes into the next generation. Fine, I say, but where I grew up people leaned on neighbours and friends far more than on cousins. Reply: ah, but the machinery reads kinship cues, and where those cues are noisy it switches over to reciprocity. OK. Example two. Someone tells me we're wired to like sweet and fatty food because calories used to be scarce. I say plenty of people in cheap-calorie environments don't behave that way at all. Reply: right, the taste is calibrated to local abundance, it dials down where food is everywhere.

Do you see what's happening? The claim turns out to be conditional, and the condition is always supplied after I've waved the awkward fact at it. It's not that the claim is vague — the mechanism story is usually quite specific. It's that all the specificity has migrated into an if-clause that can be rewritten to fit whatever I bring, and the rewriting gets presented as sophistication rather than as a patch. Each new conditional is individually reasonable, so nobody ever counts it as a cost.

I know the usual complaint about these explanations is that they're just-so stories, and I've also heard unfalsifiable thrown around. Neither quite feels like the thing. A just-so story is a story with no evidence; what bothers me is a story that swallows evidence, including evidence against it, by growing a new conditional each time. And I don't think it's just an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis either, because the conditional isn't an assumption bolted on beside the claim — it's inside the claim about the mechanism.

So: does this have a name? And if it doesn't, can you give me one, with a definition sharp enough that I can tell it apart from (a) a genuinely conditional or facultative adaptation, where the if-then really was part of the original claim and could have failed, (b) the ordinary business of patching a theory, and (c) the just-so story complaint. I'm told philosophers of science have a whole vocabulary for theory-patching, but I don't know it and I'm not after a reading list — I want the carving, not the label. What exactly is this thing, what isn't it, and where are the edges. Also, tell me how confident you are in all of it, and single out the one point you're least sure of.

Lay it out in this order please:

1. Short answer — two or three sentences. Do I get a name or not.
2. The pattern, said back to me — about 60 words, so I can check you've actually got it.
3. The nearest things that already have names — the long part. Two or three of them, a paragraph each, saying what each one is and precisely where it misses.
4. A name and a definition — your term, a one-line definition, and two boundary cases, one that should count and one that shouldn't. Around 150 words.
5. Confidence — how sure you are overall, and the single point you're least sure about.
