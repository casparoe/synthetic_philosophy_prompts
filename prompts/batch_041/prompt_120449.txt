I work on probabilistic machine learning. My working vocabulary is priors and posteriors, likelihood functions, observation channels, hierarchical and latent-variable models, variational objectives and the ELBO, temperature scaling and calibration metrics, knowledge distillation and soft targets, label smoothing, confusion-matrix and other label-noise models, and the ordinary recursive or online update stories. I can write a generative model of a noisy observation process and condition on the observation without difficulty. I have no philosophical training and no appetite for one; what I want is a translation.

I have been told about a position in epistemology that I can render only loosely. Here is my rendering, which I expect to be partly wrong — correct it as you go. Sometimes an agent does not learn anything for certain. Instead of coming to be sure that some proposition holds, the agent comes to have a new probability distribution over a set of mutually exclusive and exhaustive alternatives: she finds herself at 0.7/0.3 over two hypotheses rather than at 1/0, and this new distribution is itself the evidence, not a noisy report of it. The rule for absorbing it: leave the agent's conditional credences given each alternative exactly as they were, and change only the probabilities of the alternatives themselves, so that her new credence in any proposition A is the average of her old conditional credences in A given each alternative, weighted by the new probabilities of the alternatives. The rule is named after Richard Jeffrey, who proposed it, and is called probability kinematics or Jeffrey conditionalization. The accompanying claim is that this is the general case, with ordinary conditioning on a certain proposition as a degenerate special case.

Please do two things.

First, restate the position as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary. In particular, tell me what the update rule is in the language of likelihood functions and observation channels: whether a Jeffrey update can be written as an ordinary Bayesian update with some likelihood, and if so, what that likelihood is a likelihood of and where it came from. If the position insists on a likelihood that is not derived from any model of the evidence-generating process, say so plainly and say what licenses it instead.

Second, give me the residue report. I want the seams to show, in both directions.

1. What translates cleanly, item by item: partition and categorical target, credences and predictive distribution, rigidity and — if there is anything — the invariant I actually preserve when I distill or train on soft labels, uncertain evidence and the soft target itself.

2. What translates only with distortion. Two things bother me. The rigidity condition looks like the whole content of the rule, and I cannot find its counterpart: when I train on soft targets I preserve no conditional distribution, I just minimize a divergence to the target. And the rule seems to require that the new probabilities be assigned to cells of a partition fixed in advance, whereas a great deal of what I learn arrives in another shape — a constraint on a conditional distribution, a relative ranking, pairwise preferences, an ordering — and I see nothing in the rule that takes such input. For each: is this because my setting has no analogue of the constraint, or because I am violating the position's norm, so that the position would call my targets irrational rather than merely different?

3. What cannot be expressed at all. My hunch is that the position is normative in a way my vocabulary is not: my soft targets are chosen because they lower a loss or improve calibration on held-out data, not because they are the credences a rational agent is required to have. If that hunch is right, name the normative content I have no word for, then tell me whether my inability to state it is a gap in my framework or a sign that the original claim is thinner than it sounds.

4. The reverse residue, which is what I care about most: is there anything I do routinely that the position has no room for? Consider at least these. (a) Targets produced by my own model — pseudo-labelling, self-training, iterative distillation, EM-style bootstrapping — so that the source of the new probabilities is the same system that is supposed to absorb them. (b) Targets that are not a distribution over a partition at all: multi-label targets, targets over a continuum, a full next-token distribution over a vocabulary whose alternatives are not exhaustive in the way the rule needs. (c) The case where the new distribution comes from a teacher I believe to be systematically miscalibrated, which in my world invites a reliability model and therefore ordinary conditioning on the observation. For each, say whether it is a genuine counterexample to the position, merely outside its scope, or something the position would forbid and I do anyway.

5. Finally, an order question. Two updates of the kind described need not commute: absorbing one uncertain observation and then another can leave a different distribution than absorbing them in the opposite order, whereas conditioning on two certain propositions in either order lands in the same place so long as both are kept. Say what this does to the position — defect, feature, or artifact of how I set the example up — and whether my own practice has the same disease, given that the order of training examples, or of two distillation rounds, also changes what I end up with.

Do not quietly import my framework's assumptions and present them as the position's: that a target distribution is whatever minimizes a loss, that a predictive distribution is a claim about the world, that a model's output is evidence. Where my vocabulary forces a choice the position would refuse to make, say so, and tell me which choice the position would make instead.
