I write about philosophy and science for a general-interest magazine, and I'm putting together a piece on utilitarianism and its critics. A philosopher I interviewed gave me his version of the main objection to the view, and I want to know how much of it I can safely put in print. Roughly what he said: utilitarianism asks you to picture society as one big person and add up everybody's happiness into a single total; Rawls showed that this ignores the fact that people are seperate beings, which is what the "separateness of persons" objection means; so the view is refuted, and that's why hardly anyone defends act utilitarianism anymore while rule utilitarianism survives, since rules don't add up welfare across people the same way. He also said this is basically the same complaint as the one about not being able to compare happiness between people, since both come down to the fact that you can't get inside anyone else's head.

That feels too neat to me, and I suspect some of it is doing the argument's work for it. Take on the role of a philosopher who specializes in normative ethics and give me an audit I can actually use. Before you write anything, think it through in your own words: what is the objection really claiming, what is it an objection to, and what are the strongest replies a sophisticated utilitarian would make? Then go claim by claim: what's accurate, what's a popularizer's simplification that a specialist would wince at, what's flatly wrong, and whether any of it is a different objection wearing this one's clothes. I especially want to know whether the act/rule bit is a real distinction here or a fudge, and wether the last point about comparing happiness is the same objection or a separate one. No citations or reading lists please, just the argument, and don't be polite about it if he's mangled it.

One more thing, since you're already in the weeds: if the objection depends on my boundaries as a person mattering a lot, what happens to it if personal identity turns out to be shallower than we assume? Is that a serious threat to the objection or a side issue?
