I'm writing a term paper for my seminar on judgment and decision-making, on the dollar auction and escalation of commitment, and before I take a position I want the space of positions mapped out. Please give me a taxonomy of them as a decision tree: each branch node should be a claim that splits the positions below it, each leaf a position, named where it has a name and marked 'unnamed' where it does not, and each leaf should carry one sentence on the commitment it has the hardest time defending. Build the tree out of the questions first and locate the positions in the resulting space afterwards, rather than working from a list of labels.

Let it separate at least the following. Whether the escalation is a departure from the agent's own instrumental rationality, and if so which kind: a miscalculation of sunk costs, a greater willingness to gamble once the agent is already behind, or the service of a motive such as self-justification or reputation, which alters what the agent is maximizing rather than introducing an error of arithmetic. Whether the escalation is instead rational given the agent's incentives, either because continuing is the best reply to the other side's continuing or because quitting would carry information about the agent that the agent does not want carried. Whether the trap originates in the agent or in the rules of the game, since in the auction the second-highest bidder pays as well, which makes continuing the losing bidder's best reply. And where the remedy is supposed to sit: individual precommitment and refusal to enter, or changing the game so that the trap is not there to fall into. I suspect that last distinction cuts across the others rather than lining up with them, and I would like the tree to show whether that is so.

Phrase every branch node as a question I could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to. Where a combination of answers is occupied by nobody, say so and diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; I am particularly interested in 'a mistake act by act, but a useful disposition overall,' and in 'rational, and yet the remedy is still individual self-binding.' Use the dollar auction as the running case throughout, and where a position survives only by redescribing the auction rather than explaining it, flag that. I care about the shape of the space rather than about citations or reading lists, so skip those. Finally, flag any two leaves your tree cannot separate.
