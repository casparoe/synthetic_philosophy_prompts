I am a senior analyst in the research department of the central bank of Marisia, a small open economy that maintains a hard peg to the currency of its much larger neighbor. Marisia has three dominant sources of foreign exchange: tourism, a copper and lithium export enclave, and remittances from citizens working abroad. The Monetary Policy Committee meets monthly and repeatedly faces the same class of question: an external shock or a domestic monetary expansion has disturbed the money supply, the trade balance, or the reserves, and the committee must decide whether the disturbance will self-correct along the lines Hume described, whether it needs a policy response, and what response is consistent with Hume's understanding of money and trade. The committee has asked me for a reusable decision procedure, not an essay on Hume.

Please write that procedure. It should be a checklist or decision tree in words, with questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it must hand the decision to the committee because the remaining question is one of values or political economy rather than economics. It must be usable by a staff economist who has read Hume once and not since, in under an hour, and must produce a one-page memo with a recommendation. The governor must sign off on any recommendation that involves changing the peg, imposing capital controls, or drawing down reserves below the statutory floor.

The procedure should encode at least these Humean commitments: money is a means of exchange and a measure of value, not wealth itself; an increase in the money supply tends in the long run to raise prices rather than output; a trade surplus or deficit triggers reserve flows that alter relative prices and tend to correct themselves under specified conditions; and trade is not a zero-sum contest. But it must also state the conditions under which Hume's mechanism fails or is overridden: sticky prices and wages, capital mobility, sterilized intervention, a reserve-currency issuer, dollarized domestic contracts, and political constraints on internal adjustment. It should distinguish shocks that are monetary from shocks that are real, and temporary from permanent. It should say when the correct recommendation is to do nothing and let adjustment occur, even when that is politically painful.

Run the procedure on three cases: a sudden surge in remittances that raises reserves and the domestic money supply; a collapse in the world price of copper and lithium that turns a trade surplus into a deficit; and the large neighbor imposing a unilateral tariff on Marisian tourism and agricultural exports. For each, show the steps, the answers, the recommendation, and the point at which the procedure stops or refers to the committee. At least one case should produce a recommendation the committee will not like, and you should say why the procedure nevertheless gives it. Then identify the hard cases the procedure cannot settle, where the committee must choose among values or bear political costs, and say honestly where the procedure is likely to misfire. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; I need the procedure itself.
