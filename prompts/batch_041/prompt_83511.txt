so my flatmates and I did this dumb thing last weekend that I half-remembered from some pop-game-theory book my brother left at our place. The setup: we all threw in cash to make a $300 pot, and then the rule was that anyone who wants a cut has to write down a number of "tickets" — you can claim as many as you like, no limit, costs you nothing — and then the pot gets divided by the total number of tickets claimed. So if all five of us claim 1 ticket, we get $60 each.

What actually happened: everyone claimed somewhere between 15 and 40, we each walked away with about three bucks, and we all sat there feeling like idiots.

The part that's still bugging me is something my friend Jenna said, which is definately true: if we all claim the same number, it doesn't matter what that number is. Pot divided by five either way. So the number is arbitrary, there's nothing to argue about, no coordination needed. And yet we all cranked it up anyway. And when I try to reason it out I go in circles: if everyone's claiming 1, then me claiming 2 gets me 2/6 of the pot instead of 1/5, which is more for me and a little less for each of them. So "let's all just claim 1" isn't something anyone has an actual reason to stick to — not because people are greedy or cheating, just because the better move is sitting right there the whole time. But "everyone claim as much as they want" obviously gives us all nothing.

Can you explain what's really going on here? It doesn't feel like the usual prisoner's dilemma or tragedy-of-the-commons story, because nobody has to trust anybody and every symmetric outcome pays all of us exactly the same. Is there a name for this shape of problem? And one more thing — the version I'm thinking of assumed everyone involved is perfectly rational and knows everyone else is too, and the claim was that this makes it *easier* to solve. That seems backwards to me. Who's right?
