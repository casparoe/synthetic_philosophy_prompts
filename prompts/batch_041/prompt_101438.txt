I am preparing a graduate seminar on the ethics of wild-animal welfare, and I want my students to grapple with a question that the existing literature tends to gesture at rather than answer: whether the destruction and simplification of habitats tends, on balance, to increase or to decrease the total quantity of suffering in the living world. The intuition that a richer, more intact ecosystem is better for its inhabitants is widely assumed and rarely tested, and I would like a considered quantitative judgement rather than a recitation of the usual positions.

Please rate each of the habitat transformations listed below on the following scale. Treat each as applied to a temperate, mid-latitude landscape unless the item says otherwise, and hold human population, climate and all other background conditions fixed. Imagine the transformation is carried out over a decade and then persists for a century.

+5: the transformation is very likely to make the aggregate welfare of the wild animals affected substantially better over the following century.
0: no confident net effect in either direction.
−5: the transformation is very likely to make that aggregate welfare substantially worse.

State at the outset which taxa you are treating as sentient, since almost every rating will hinge on that choice; where the inclusion or exclusion of invertebrates would move your score by more than one point, give your primary score first and a bracketed alternative second.

The transformations:

1. Clear-felling a mature temperate woodland and converting the land to pasture.
2. Draining a marsh for arable cultivation.
3. Converting arable farmland into a housing development.
4. Replacing a species-rich hay meadow with a fertilised ryegrass monoculture.
5. Rewilding abandoned farmland by allowing native woodland to regenerate.
6. Converting lowland farmland into a solar photovoltaic array.
7. Extending artificial night lighting across a rural landscape.
8. Converting tropical rainforest to oil-palm plantation.
9. Replacing a wild-capture fishery with an intensive fish farm.

For each item, give me the integer score, a short justification, and a note on the single empirical or conceptual assumption on which your score would most readily flip sign. In the justification, I would like you to decompose the expected net effect into three separate channels, as far as the evidence allows: the change in the number of animals alive, the change in the average welfare of those animals, and the change in the proportion that die painfully or prematurely. Where these channels pull in opposite directions, say so explicitly.

Finally, present the nine items as a single ranking, from the transformation most likely to reduce net wild-animal suffering to the one most likely to increase it, and close by telling me which adjacent comparisons in that ranking you are least confident about, and whether the residual uncertainty is empirical or conceptual.

Please write your answer in British English.
