I need to turn a half-formed idea into a two-page research proposal for an internal one-semester grant (small money, mostly my own time and maybe a half-time RA). Would you write the proposal for me? Heres the idea, such as it is.

The old question is how someone can judge that A is better than B and still do B. There are two registers of answer that I think quietly talk past each other. The philosophical one treats it as a defect of the will or of the practical syllogism — the judgment is intact, the action just doesn't follow. The behavioral-econ one treats it as an unstable preference: the agent's ranking at t1 differs from their ranking at t2, and akrasia is just the name we give to preference reversal when we're the ones doing it. Fine. But I don't think the second one explains the thing the first one was pointing at. Someone who is akratic reports the same ranking throughout — they don't experience themselves as having changed their mind about what's best; the better option stays better in their eyes the whole time and they just... don't do it.

My hunch, and I can't tell yet wether it's a real result or a cheap trick: the present-bias weight in the quasi-hyperbolic model — the beta that gets slapped on the immediate reward — doesn't have to be read as a time preference at all. You can read it as an attentional weight, the salience of whatever is in front of you at the moment of choice, independent of when it happens. If you do that, the model stops explaining akrasia and starts dissolving it: nothing has gone wrong with the agent's preferences, they just have an attention system that over-weights the near. Which either kills the phenomenon or relocates it somewhere more interesting.

The wrinkle that makes me think it might actually be worth a semester: regret. Akratics regret it, and regret is prima facie evidence that the ranking did flip and then flip back — which hands the phenomenon right back to the economists. So the proposal needs to say what would decide between the reversal reading and the salience reading, and I suspect the answer isn't more armchair work.

A precise statement of the question, one that has a determinate answer; why the two standard readings leave it open rather than settling it; what I'd actually do month by month (I'm happy for the method to be mostly conceptual with one small vignette study that the RA runs — that's what the money is for); what a positive result would look like; and what would count as the project failing. Finish with an honest paragraph on whether this is really one semester of work or whether the framing collapses as soon as you start pulling on it.
