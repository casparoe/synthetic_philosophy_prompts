My friend and I have been going back and forth on this for about a month now and I want to figure out if my position even holds together before we pick it up again. He's a hard Calvinist, I've ended up somewhere in Molinist territory (though I've never actually read Molina, so this is just where I've landed), and here's roughly what I think:

1. Before God decided to create anything at all, he already knew exactly what every possible free creature would freely do in every situation it could be put in. That's the middle knowledge thing, and it's what lets me say God is in charge of the world without him rigging it.
2. Free choices aren't caused or determined by anything outside the chooser. God actualizes a world with me in it; he doesn't make me do things.
3. If I freely do A in some situation, then in exactly that same situation I could have done otherwise. I take that to be what freedom is.
4. Nothing about God depends on creatures. He doesn't learn anything, and his knowledge isn't a part of him that could have gone differently — his knowing is just his essence. I'm classical about this stuff.
5. God is perfectly good, and the evil he permits doesn't count against that, because there are reasons for it that we're in no position to see.

My friend says the whole thing is a mess. He says (a) if God knew what I'd do then I couldn't have done otherwise, (b) nothing makes those counterfactuals about me true in the first place, and (c) it makes God the author of sin. He can't be right about all three, but I can't tell which ones are real problems and which are just him being Calvinist. So: which parts of my package actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me the cheapest fix and what it costs me. If one single revision would clean up the most, tell me which. And tell me how confident you actually are in your verdict — flag the one point your least sure about.
