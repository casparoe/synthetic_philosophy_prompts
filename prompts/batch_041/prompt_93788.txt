In my aesthetics reading group we've been circling why anyone seeks out sad art for months now, and it's the same five moves everytime: catharsis; the sadness is secretly pleasant; the emotions in fiction aren't real so there's nothing to explain; you're not enjoying the sadness, you're enjoying being the kind of person it moves; and you're not enjoying anything at all, you just value the exercise. Nobody's budged, and I'm starting to think the dispute is malformed rather than hard. My suspicion: 'enjoy' is doing at least three jobs — that we voluntarily come back to the thing, that the experience on the whole is one we'd choose again, and that the sadness itself feels good — and each camp quietly buys one of those and redefines the others away. Second suspicion: the cases aren't a natural kind. Horror runs on fear and disgust, tragedy on pity, sad songs on nothing but sadness, so each solution works beautifully for one sub-class and falls over on the rest, which is why people keep citing different examples at each other. A friend also insists the state in question isn't sadness-with-a-plus-sign but a separate emotion, being moved, pleasant in its own right — if that's right the whole paradox might be a labelling error. So diagnose it for me: is this a verbal fight about 'pleasure', a substantive disagreement about the structure of emotion (are the emotions even real ones? does an emotion have to have a valence at all?), or three different questions wearing one hat — why we choose it, why we value it, and what's good about it? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, some empirical work on wanting versus liking, or just conceding that the question is ill-posed? Skip the tour of the solutions, I've read them.
