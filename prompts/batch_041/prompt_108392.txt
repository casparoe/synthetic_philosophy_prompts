Right, so I've been roped into a workshop on historical materialism next month and the organiser wants everyone to circulate a position statement beforehand, so I'm trying to write mine down before one of the Marxists in the room does it for me. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) The base is the forces and relations of production taken together, and it explains the superstructure in the last instance.
(b) There's a transhistorical tendency for the productive forces to develop — once you've got a division of labour and a surplus, technique and productivity tend to ratchet up over the long run, whatever else is going on.
(c) Superstructures aren't epiphenomenal. Law, politics, religion and the rest have real causal effects and can speed the base up or hold it back, sometimes for centuries.
(d) Transitions from one mode of production to the next are driven by class struggle, not by the forces quietly outgrowing the relations and bursting them like a skin.
(e) Historical materialism is itself the product of a particular historical situation, and there's no standpoint outside history from which to certify it.
(f) The theory is explanatory rather than predictive — the stage-by-stage story people extract from the famous 1859 preface isn't something I'd defend, and I don't think the theory needs it.

I suspect these don't all hang together, but I can't see which ones are actually pulling against each other and which just look like they might. Some of them, I think, are moves that everyone in the literature makes at once, so maybe they're fine. I'd like an audit before I circulate it. Be honest, I've got thick skin and a sabbatical.
