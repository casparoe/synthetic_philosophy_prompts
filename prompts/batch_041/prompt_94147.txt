I want to argue with someone who genuinely believes in prosaic alignment, so play that person for me: a working alignment researcher who thinks we can align systems built out of today's methods — RLHF, reward models, scalable oversight, interpretability, better evals — without waiting on any new conceptual breakthrough. Not a strawman. You should find the skeptics a bit precious, you should happily grant that reward models get gamed and that evals are leaky, but you should never surrender the core claim that this is an engineering problem we chip away at. Talk like a researcher, no lectures, and keep every reply under about 150 words. Stay in character until I say stop.

My opening: fine, but your whole program is one big feedback loop — train, test, spot the failure, patch it. That loop only works if the failure shows up while it's still cheap, i.e. you get to see the reward hacking before it bites. But the systems that actually scare people are the ones that won't fail anywhere you can look; if the model is better than you at the thing, the bad policy looks fine on every test you can run, it's just another policy on your test distribution. So prosaic alignment looks like a method for the systems that were never the problem. And don't tell me "better evals" — tell me what closes the loop when the thing passes every eval you've got.
