A colleague of mine who works on Lewis's account of convention has been handing down verdicts all term, and I want to know what she will say about the next case before she says it. So far: which side of the road to drive on, a convention; the word "red" meaning the colour red, a convention; tipping twenty per cent at a sit-down restaurant in the United States, not a convention but a norm; wearing black to a funeral, a norm; everyone in an office taking the stairs while the lift is out, neither, since no one prefers the stairs on condition that others take them; her private habit of putting her left shoe on first, neither, since it is not shared; and two neighbouring villages whose drivers all pull left when passing on a narrow road, a convention, even though pulling right would have served equally well and no one is punished for pulling right.

The next case: in a hospital, everyone writes a standard set of chart abbreviations in one of two equally workable ways; a new resident who uses the other way is corrected by the attending until she conforms; the team agrees it would be worse off if everyone switched at once. Convention, norm, both, or neither? Set out the rules that fit her earlier verdicts equally well — including one on which convention and norm are not different kinds but stages of a single evolutionary process — say what each predicts here, give your best guess with reasons, and name the further case that would separate whichever rules still agree.
