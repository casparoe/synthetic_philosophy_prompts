I'm writing a section comparing the BHK reading of the connectives with the propositions-as-types / Curry–Howard reading, and I keep going back and forth on whether these are one idea or two that just happen to line up. BHK: a proof of A→B is a construction that takes a proof of A to a proof of B, a proof of A∨B is a proof of one of the disjuncts plus a tag saying which — informal, epistemic, and 'construction' is supposed to be doing real work. Curry–Howard: a proof of A→B is a term of function type, so a proof is just an object in a formal system and 'construction' means 'term'. My suspicion is that the second is what you get when you try to make the first precise, and that something gets lost on the way: the BHK clauses can't be stated without already presupposing a notion of proof (you have to know what counts as a construction of what), and once you formalize them you've got a formal system rather than a justification for one. Can you compare the two and tell me whether Curry–Howard really inherits BHK's conclusions while dropping the motivation behind them? And if so, whats left of the idea that intuitionistic logic is forced on us by a theory of meaning, rather than just being one option we happen to like?
