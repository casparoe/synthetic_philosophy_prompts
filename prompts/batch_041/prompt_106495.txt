I write about AI and science for a general readership and I'm trying to put together a piece that actually stakes out a position instead of doing the usual "but are they really thinking?" song and dance. I've got an argument I want to make and I want you to build the strongest version of it for me.

Take the role of a philosopher of mind who works on representation and has read way too much of the interpretability literature. Think carefully before you write, and start by brainstorming out loud: give me the three strongest objections to what I'm about to say, then argue past them.

Here's the claim. When someone says a neural network "represents" a concept — a feature, a direction, whatever — they're leaning on evidence that can't carry the weight. Split the evidence into two kinds. Read-out evidence: you train a probe on a layer's activations and it decodes whether a sentence is about a city or a country, or you find a direction that tracks truthfulness. Write-in evidence: you intervene, patch activations, steer along the direction, ablate, and the behaviour changes the way the concept says it should.

My line is that read-out evidence is nearly free, and therefore nearly worthless — a flexible enough probe will pull almost anything out of almost any layer, and what it pulls out is as much a fact about the probe as about the network. Fine, so the real action is in write-in evidence. But write-in evidence is confounded in a specific way: the thing you intervene on isn't a clean unit, it's a bundle glued to everything correlated with it, so a successful intervention doesn't isolate a representation, it perturbs a neighbourhood. Which means the realist move — I poked it, the behaviour moved, therefore it represents X — has a hole exactly where the interesting part is.

So I want you to argue that the honest position is anti-realist or quietist: networks don't represent things in the sense the interpretability crowd needs, and we should stop talking as if they do until someone can deliver interventions that are orthogonalized against everything else the representation is tangled up with. If that demand is impossible in principle, say so and argue the position is even stronger.

Don't just recite the realism-vs-instrumentalism map, I've read that stuff. And don't hedge — I'd rather have a sharp wrong argument than a balanced one. Anticipate the reply that representations were never supposed to be clean isolated units anyway (distributed, overlapping, superposition and all that) and say why that reply doesn't save the realists.
