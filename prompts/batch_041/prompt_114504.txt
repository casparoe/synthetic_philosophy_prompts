Exam question:
In a repeated even-money betting game, you win with probability p > 1/2 and lose with probability q = 1 - p. Your current wealth is W, and you may bet any fraction f of your wealth each round. The Kelly criterion recommends betting f* = 2p - 1.
(a) Derive this recommendation from the expected logarithm of wealth.
(b) Explain in what sense this strategy maximizes long-run growth, and distinguish the relevant average from the expected wealth average.
(c) Evaluate the following claim: Because Kelly maximizes long-run growth, it is the uniquely rational strategy for any risk-averse agent with a known edge. A rational agent should always bet full Kelly; betting less is either a mistake or a concession to emotion. In your answer, discuss the role of the utility function and the distinction between full and fractional Kelly.

Student's answer:
Let W_n be wealth after n bets. If we bet fraction f, a win multiplies wealth by 1 + f and a loss by 1 - f. The expected log wealth after one bet is E[ln(W_1/W_0)] = p ln(1+f) + q ln(1-f). Differentiating with respect to f gives p/(1+f) - q/(1-f) = 0, so p(1-f) = q(1+f), hence p - pf = q + qf, so p - q = f(p+q) = f. Thus f* = p - q = 2p - 1. This is the Kelly fraction. By the law of large numbers, (1/n) ln(W_n/W_0) converges almost surely to p ln(1+f) + q ln(1-f). So maximizing the expected log is the same as maximizing the almost-sure exponential growth rate of wealth over many bets. This is a time average, not an ensemble average: the expected wealth E[W_n] is maximized by betting everything (f=1), but that leads to ruin almost surely, while the typical trajectory grows fastest at f*. Kelly therefore maximizes the median of the wealth distribution, which is why it avoids ruin and is the uniquely rational choice for a risk-averse agent. Since log is concave, maximizing expected log is just expected utility maximization with log utility. But any concave utility function represents risk aversion, so any risk-averse expected utility maximizer would choose the same fraction f*. The only reason to bet less than full Kelly is if you have non-log preferences or if you are irrationally loss-averse; fractional Kelly is a psychological compromise, not a requirement of rationality.
