Write a joke about repugnant markets. The joke should turn on the difference between two grounds for objecting to a market: that it is coercive, and that it corrupts the good being exchanged. Set it in a firm that sells repugnance offsets, by which customers pay the firm to buy up and retire the supply of a contested good—for instance, kidneys offered by living vendors—so that no one can sell one. The firm insists it is not commodifying kidneys but decommodifying them. The joke should be funny to someone who knows the commodification debate, not merely to someone who finds organ sales distasteful; the punchline should depend on the fact that the offset itself has a price.
