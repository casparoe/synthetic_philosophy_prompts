My partner and I moved into a housing co-op about a year ago — 40 units, everyone owns a share — and somehow I ended up on the commitee that's rewriting the bylaws. The big fight is over who gets a vote on the stuff that actually matters: rent adjustments, whether we sell the parking lot to the developer next door, how we spend the reserve fund. Right now only the lease-holding members vote. But there's a bunch of long-term subletters who've lived here five, six years, pay into the maintenance fund every month, know everyone's dogs by name, and they're pushing hard to be included. Then two people who moved in like six weeks ago also want in, and someone floated letting the teenagers who live here vote on the garden and common-room budget but not on rent, which feels either obviously right or obviously insane depending on the day.

The thing that's really bugging me is that our committee basically appointed ourselves when the old one dissolved, so we're the ones deciding who gets to decide, which even I can see is a little circular. I keep drawing the line by vibes — "people who are really part of this place" — and every time I try to turn that into an actual rule it either swallows everyone or turns into a popularity contest. My roommate says I should just stop, because any answer is whoever's holding the pen picking their own side, so there's no non-circular way to settle it and I'm wasting my time. I don't think he's totally wrong, but I also don't think "no principled answer" can be the end of it, since we still have to run the vote and somebody has to make the list. So: is there a defensible way to work out who belongs in the electorate here, and if so, how do I apply it without quietly rigging it toward the outcome I already want? Walk me through how you'd actually decide the subletter and teenager questions, not just that it's complicated.
