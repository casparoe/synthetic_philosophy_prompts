I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I would like help thinking through a recurring disagreement in my design reviews rather than just asserting my intuitions. A senior colleague of mine, my tech lead, has spent years ruling on whether a system or a person made the right call under constraints, and I have been collecting her verdicts. I want to predict what she will say about a new case, and to understand whether her pattern of judgments is even coherent.

Her verdicts so far, each of which she has defended as the rational call:

- A service that returns the first configuration meeting its latency target, rather than the fastest configuration it could have found, where she agreed that the exhaustive search for the true optimum was too expensive to justify running.
- A job scheduler that uses a greedy heuristic instead of solving the full combinatorial optimization, which would have taken hours she considered wasted.
- A developer who ships the first bug fix that passes the test suite rather than the cleanest available fix, where the cleaner fix would have required a substantial refactor.
- A recommendation model that stops at a checkpoint whose accuracy clears a threshold instead of continuing to train toward a better one at real compute cost.
- A search feature that returns the first acceptable match rather than ranking all matches.
- A pipeline that, having already produced an acceptable result, runs a long additional optimization pass for a marginal gain and consequently misses its deadline, which she called not rational.

The new case she will have to judge: a routing component stops and returns the first route that satisfies its travel-time budget. Unknown to the component, a strictly better route, several minutes faster, was already sitting in its own cache and would have cost one additional and essentially free comparison to retrieve. She is asked whether the component's stopping was the rational call.

Please do the following. First, articulate the competing rules that all fit her earlier verdicts; I suspect there are at least two, one holding that a choice is rational whenever it clears a pre-set acceptability bar, and one holding that a choice is rational only when it makes the best use of the resources the agent actually had. Second, state what each rule predicts for the new case. Third, give your best guess at her verdict and the reasons for preferring it. Fourth, tell me which of the earlier verdicts are genuinely load-bearing and which are merely consistent with the rules. Fifth, name the single further case that would most cleanly separate the candidate rules, and say what each rule would conclude about it. Where her track record genuinely underdetermines the new case, say so, and tell me what additional commitment she would be taking on with each possible verdict.
