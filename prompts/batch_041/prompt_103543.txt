ok so i've been reading way too much rationalist blog stuff lately and I'm trying to get a handle on the whole worst-case vs average-case thing in AI alignment for our reading group, and I want to do it as a decision tree / taxonomy instead of yet another essay nobody reads.

Here's the knot I keep running into. Everyone says you want your AI to be "safe," but that word is doing at least two jobs: (a) there's some guarantee that holds no matter what input, adversary, or distribution you throw at the system, or (b) it does well in expectation over the distribution you actually expect, with the bad cases rare enough that they wash out. And the real fight isn't just which of those is better — it's whether they're even the same kind of thing. Is worst-case supposed to be a proxy for "a smart optimizer will find the worst case anyway," or is it a separate decision rule for when you can't stick probabilities on things (Knightian uncertainty, the Ellsberg vibe)? People slide between those and I think it's causing a lot of the confusion.

So: write me a decision tree where each internal node is a question a person can answer without already knowing which camp it drops them into, and each leaf is a named position (or 'unnamed' if nobody actually defends it). Build it from the logical space first — the questions and which combinations of answers are even coherent — and then locate the real positions inside that space. Flag any pairs of positions your tree can't separate instead of pretending it can.

Stuff I specifically want it to pull apart:

- someone who thinks worst-case matters because a sufficiently good optimizer just finds the worst case (worst-case is the right *model of the adversary*) vs someone who thinks worst-case matters as a rule for deciding under unquantifiable uncertainty (no adversary needed at all).
- someone who thinks expected value is the whole story and the scary cases are just small-probability terms (fine or not, depending on the numbers) vs someone who thinks EV breaks down here because you're multiplying a made-up probability by a made-up disvalue.
- the person who separates worst-case over *inputs* from worst-case over *training/deployment distributions* from worst-case over *which agent you actually built* — i.e. adversarial-example robustness is not the same question as distributional shift, which is not the same as "this whole training paradigm is one draw from a space of possible paradigms." I feel like people mash all three together.
- the ML-brained person who says average-case is fine as long as train and test distributions match and thinks the worst-case crowd is just doing adversarial-robustness paranoia.

Also give me one branch on the meta question of whether worst-case should be a *constraint* (don't do anything that could be catastrophic, full stop) vs a *term in the objective* (minimize the max — maximin) vs a *tie-breaker*, because I think people collapse those three and they're clearly not the same thing.

Invent at least a couple of toy cases for the diagnostic questions, don't just recycle the obvious ones. And tell me which answer-combinations come out empty and why — incoherent, just unattractive, or nobody's bothered to explore them yet.

Keep it to like 12-15 leaves max, it needs to fit on a handout.
