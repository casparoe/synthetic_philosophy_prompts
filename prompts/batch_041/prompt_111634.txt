Ok, I need help settling an argument I lost over the holidays and cannot stop chewing on, so this is part philosophy question, part please-tell-me-where-the-bad-step-is.

Background. Three months ago I got a job writing and grading prompts for a company that trains chatbots. Most of what I do is write prompts and then score two model answers against each other. Part of the job is writing a few sentences of justification, and I've noticed I keep producing things like 'the model thinks the user wants X' or 'the model is trying to dodge the question,' and aparently that's just how everyone on the team talks, my supervisor approves them without blinking. Fine, whatever. Then at my parents' place my brother, who is halfway through a philosophy MA and is insufferable about it, says every one of those sentences is literally false, and then says the same reasoning makes them false when I say them about myself. He built me this argument on the back of an envelope and I cannot find the bad step. It goes:

(1) A state is about something -- a coin, a cat, a country -- only if something makes it about that thing rather than some other thing. Otherwise the aboutness isn't in the state, we're just reading it in.
(2) Take any physical state, a neuron firing or a transistor switching. There is a complete description of it under which it is about nothing at all: it's just an event with causes and effects, and nothing in that description mentions coins or cats or countries.
(3) So whatever aboutness a state has isn't in the state itself. It comes from outside it, from an interpreter who assigns it.
(4) Now, an interpreter is either a physical system or it isn't. If it isn't, if it's something whose states are just about things without anyone assigning them, then we've already assumed the very thing we were trying to explain.
(5) If it is a physical system, then by (2) and (3) its own interpreting states need an interpreter of their own.
(6) So the interpreters either go on forever, or they stop at something that is about things by itself.
(7) If they go on forever, no state is ever finally about anything -- aboutness is always relative to some further interpretation and never lands. If they stop, then some state is about something all by itself, with no mechanism doing the work, which is just magic with a nicer name.
(8) Magic is not an option. So we take the regress: nothing is ever determinately about anything.
(9) Therefore 'the model is trying to dodge the question' isn't literally true, and neither is 'I believe my sister is still angry with me.' There is no fact of the matter about what any state is about, only more and less convenient ways of talking.

I can live with (9) applied to the model. I cannot live with (9) applied to me; it seems flatly false that my belief about my sister isn't about my sister. And here's the bit that makes me want to tear my hair out. My brother's actual view is that the whole distinction between real, original aboutness and borrowed, derived aboutness is a myth -- that's the whole point of his vending machine example. The machine is rigged to take only real US quarters; he says ship it to Panama and it cheerfully takes Panamanian quarter-balboas that fool its sensor, so whether its internal state 'means quarter' or is 'mistaken' depends entirely on whose interpretation you adopt, and my beliefs differ from its states only in how many people are already on board with the interpretation. But if (3) and (5) are right, then derived aboutness doesn't make sense either, because something has to do the deriving, and by (5) whatever does the deriving needs a deriver of its own. So it looks to me like the argument, if it goes through, eats his own position before it eats mine. I just can't tell which line is doing the work, and he won't tell me, he says figuring out which premise to give up is the whole exercise.

So: which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Don't just tell me a step is false -- tell me why it looked fine to me when he said it out loud, and what I have to give up if I cut it. If there are two different cuts available, say one that saves only my beliefs and one that saves the machine's states too, price both. I'd rather keep (2) than give up the application of (9) to me, if that's even coherent -- tell me if it isn't. And tell me what my brother gets to keep if I make whichever cut you recommend.
