Some context first: I'm the most junior member of a small committee in a philosophy department that's just been handed an oddly specific pot of money -- enough for one two-year postdoc plus a workshop series -- and the dean has earmarked it for "cross-cultural variation in philosophical intuitions." None of us on the committee are experimentalists; we're all armchair people, so we're a bit at sea about what would actually be worth funding. The dean is open to a broad reading of the topic, so we can stretch it. I've been asked to bring a shortlist plus a defense of the ordering to the next meeting, and I'd rather not just reproduce whatever the last ten years of x-phi headlines have been.

Here's what I want from you: what are the live open questions in this area, and which two or three would you actually put the money behind? I care much more about the reasoning than the list. For each candidate, tell me how upstream it is (does settling it change what a lot of other work in the area even means?), how tractable it is for a group that mostly won't be running studies themselves but can hire someone who will, whether it's neglected or already crowded, and what kind of result over the next two years would move it up or down. A few things I keep going back and forth on, to give you a sense of the space: whether cross-cultural differences are really differences in intuitions or differences in what the words and cases mean to the respondents; whether the right lesson is to abandon the method of cases altogether or just to relativize it, i.e. keep the method but localize which intuitions count; whether there's any defensible remnant of "universal" philosophical claims once the variation is on the table; and whether any of this bears on the epistemology of philosophy or only on its sociology. If some of those are red herrings or rest on a confusion, say so and tell me what I should be asking instead.

Two constraints worth factoring in: the postdoc will need to publish in ordinary philosophy journals, not just x-phi venues, and the workshop is meant to pull in people from outside the area (an ethicist, a metaphysician, maybe a linguist). So part of the ranking is which questions a general philosophical audience would recognize as mattering, not just which are hot inside the subfield. Give me the shortlist, the ordering, the criteria you used, where those criteria trade off against each other, and what would have to happen for you to reshuffle the list. And please be honest about which of these questions are the sort that get people tenure but don't seperate the real issues from the noise.
