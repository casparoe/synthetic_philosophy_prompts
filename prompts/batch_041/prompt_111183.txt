Ok so I've got a talk in about three weeks at a small joint session our department runs with the decision theory group, plus two cosmologists who come over from the physics building for it, and I need help prepping for the discussion afterwards, not for the talk itself. Here's my thesis. Causal decision theory says an act is rational if it has the best causal consequences. Evidential decision theory says pick the act with the best expected value conditional on your doing it, which makes it matter that your choosing it is evidence about what other agents who deliberate like you, or who face a structurally identical choice, end up choosing. In a one-shot prisoners dilemma with someone whose reasoning tracks yours, that's the boring old argument for cooperating with no repeat play. My claim is that the someone doesn't have to be reachable, or even in the same epoch. If the world is big enough that there are lots of agents whose choices are correlated with mine because they're instantiations of the same kind of deliberation, then my defecting is evidence that they defect too, and the real cost of defecting includes that spread. So repetition and the shadow of the future aren't the only things that can make cooperation rational, correlation of deliberation does the same work across any distance. I'm using that for three things: one-shot defection is much more expensive than it looks, the standard who-cares-you-can't-affect-them line is really just CDT restated, and the interesting question about cooperating with distant future people or distant civilizations is an evidential question rather than a causal one. The move I'm most attached to: CDT asks what would happen if I alone changed, but in a large world the thing to hold fixed isn't me, it's the whole class of deliberators whose output is correlated with mine, and that class is enormous.
