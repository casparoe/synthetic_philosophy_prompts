Give me an example of such a conceptual issue. It should be specific enough to be philosophically interesting but not depend on obscure texts or on citations. Reconstruct the issue in plain terms: identify the competing interpretations of Mill's late position, explain how his advocacy of worker cooperatives, his interest in land reform and the taxation of the unearned increment of land, his worries about population pressure, and his insistence on experiments in living and the development of individuality pull in different directions, and say what turns on choosing one interpretation over another. I am especially interested in an example that shows why Mill's late socialism is better understood as a conditional or transitional position than as a straightforward endorsement of collective ownership, and that brings out the conceptual cost of that conditionality. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; assume the reader knows the basic outlines of Mill's liberalism and utilitarianism but not the details of his late socialist writings.
