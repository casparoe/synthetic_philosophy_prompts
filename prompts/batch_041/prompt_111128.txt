Right, I'm rebuilding my Lakatos lecture and I'm bored stiff of the textbook version where every research programme is just hard core plus protective belt plus positive heuristic and then everyone nods. I want a real worked case instead, and I've picked the peptic ulcer story: Warren and Marshall in the early 80s claiming that most ulcers are caused by a bacterium living in the stomach lining, the gastroenterology establishment treating the whole thing as crankery for a decade or more (the going view being that the stomach is more or less sterile and ulcers are an acid and stress problem), the self-experiment, and then the slow reversal -- antibiotic cures working, a consensus statement in the mid 90s, eventually the Nobel. Don't go looking things up, I'm giving you the shape of it and I only care about the analysis.

Three things. First, reconstruct it as a Lakatosian research programme, and do it for both sides: what's the hard core on each side, what's sitting in the protective belt, and did either side actually have a positive heuristic or was it just belt-patching all the way down? Then, and this is where I want you to go beyond the standard framework, use the Proofs and Refutations vocabulary too. Where is there monster-barring, where is it exception-barring, and is anything that happens better described as lemma-incorporation? Give me specific moments, not gestures.

Second, the verdict, and be careful with it. Was the bacterial programme progressive or degenerating at the point when it was being dismissed? I want you to seperate progressive in Lakatos's technical sense -- novel excess empirical content, some of it corroborated -- from progressive in the loose sense of turned out to be true. If they come apart here, tell me how, and tell me which one Lakatos is actually entitled to.

Third, the bit I care about most: where does the framework misdescribe the episode? I'm thinking of the single dramatic self-experiment doing rhetorical work that no methodology accounts for, the funding and patent side, the fact that the decisive move looks like a clinical trial rather than a theory-driven novel prediction, and whether the later Nobel is just the retrospective rationality Lakatos was trying to get away from. So: does the case support his no-instant-rationality line, or does it show his criteria can be run backwards to bless whoever won? Don't be polite if you think the framework is being gerrymandered onto the case.
