The topic is reasons without reasoners, i.e. free-floating rationales (I'm fairly sure it's Dennett's phrase but dont hold me to which book). Here's the thing I keep banging my head against: there obviously IS a reason a trait is the way it is -- the reason a wing is that shape has something to do with flight, the reason a sting works has something to do with defence -- and yet that reason was never in anyone's head, not the organism's, and not a designer's either, since selection did the work. So you get a rationale that floats free of any reasoner. My biologist friends say this is just a fancy word for anthropomorphising, and on my bad days I half agree, which is exactly why I want to teach it properly instead of just asserting it and hoping.
