I'm in the middle of a graduate seminar on confirmation theory, and I've been asked to open a session on the problem of old evidence with a case study of my own devising, since the stock examples have been discussed to death. The puzzle, as I understand it, is this. An agent has known some proposition E from the beginning; her credence in it is 1; so conditioning on it does nothing, since P(H | E) = P(H), and yet it seems plainly true that long-known evidence sometimes supports a hypothesis enormously, and that an agent is not simply confused in feeling that it does. Two repairs are usually offered. The first relocates the comparison to a counterfactual agent: the support E gives H is measured by how much higher the agent's credence in H stands than the credence of an otherwise identical agent who has not yet learned E. The second treats the newly appreciated implication as the evidence: what the agent learns, when the old evidence at last registers as support, is a logical fact — that H entails E, or renders it probable — and she reconditions on that fact within a re-partitioned space. I want a case in which these two repairs come apart, and in the direction I find most interesting: the counterfactual repair reports a spectacular confirmation, the logical-fact repair reports almost none, and it is the second verdict that seems to me right.

Please design the case to my specifications. (i) One hypothesis H, one item of evidence E, and two ideally rational agents: a veteran who has known E from the start, and a newcomer who meets H and E together for the first time. (ii) A realistic setting of your choosing — a clinic, a materials lab, a fisheries office — in one paragraph, with no science fiction and no appeals to forgetting, irrationality, or shifting standards; E is an ordinary proposition, not an experience. (iii) The veteran's credence in E is 1 throughout. (iv) The feature I care about: H's content or its parameters were fixed by E, in the sense that H was framed so as to accommodate E, so that the fit between the two is for the veteran something she has known since she first framed H. (v) For the newcomer, learning E raises the credence in H substantially. After the scenario, do the bookkeeping: give the prior and posterior credences for the veteran, the newcomer, and the counterfactual veteran, and show why the counterfactual repair must report a boost large enough to carry H to near-certainty — or, if you think the comparison is simply undefined here, say exactly what has to be stipulated to define it and what the stipulated comparison then yields. Show why the logical-fact repair reports almost nothing, name the structural feature that is doing the work, and then tell me how a defender of the counterfactual repair ought to attack the case — I expect the reply to be that I have smuggled in a difference between the actual and the hypothetical agent over and above the absence of E — and whether that reply can be made good without giving up the repair's claim to measure the support the veteran actually has. Finally, tell me what becomes of the divergence if I drop (iv) and let H be framed before E was ever known, so that E's fit with H was a genuine prediction rather than an accommodation, and say whether the asymmetry between veteran and newcomer is defensible or whether the case shows the puzzle to be unresolved.
