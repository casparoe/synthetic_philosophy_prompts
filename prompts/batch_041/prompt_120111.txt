I'm painting a hand-lettered alphabet mural at my kid's school, all 26 letters big on a wall, in a wobbly slanted style I made up. Two rules I set for myself: every letter has to be drawable in one continuous stroke without lifting the brush, and every letter's main loop has to be the same width as every other letter's loop. My friend who's helping me says my 'a' isn't an 'a' — she says it reads like a 'd' turned around, or a 'q' with a hat. I say it's fine, because in the word it's sitting in, next to the other letters, everyone reads it as an 'a.' She also thinks the 'g' should have the fancy two-story tail like you see in a newspaper, because that's what a real g looks like, but I used the simple round one with the straight tail, the kind in kids' books. Honestly I read somwhere that most people can't even say which way the tail on the two-story g curls, which makes me think we don't really know these shapes as well as we think we do, which is wierd. Who's right about the 'a'? And is there a principled way to tell when a shape I've drawn is still the letter I meant, versus when it's turned into a different letter or just a blob? I keep feeling the answer has something to do with the rest of the alphabet — like an 'a' is an 'a' partly because of what it isn't — but I can't get it straight. One more thing: I want this to be the kind of style where, if someone asked me to draw a letter that isn't in English at all, or a made-up symbol, I could do it and it would still look like it belongs to the same alphabet. Is that even a coherent thing to want, and does it bear on the 'a' question?
