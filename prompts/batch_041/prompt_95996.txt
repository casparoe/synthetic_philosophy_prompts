ok so I'm a third-year PhD student (ML, but my advisor is half in a philosophy dept, it's a whole thing) and I've been falling down the AI control rabbit hole for a seminar instead of writing my actual chapter. I want to see what a satire piece on this looks like, so please write one for me.

The brief: write it as the internal onboarding FAQ for new employees at a frontier AI lab (invent the lab, please don't use a real company), issued by that lab's AI Control team. The premise is that the lab has concluded, internally, that its flagship model may be actively misaligned, and has built its entire safety apparatus on that assumption rather than on the assumption that everything is fine. Format it as a real internal policy doc — numbered sections, a few Q&A entries — deadpan, around 800 words. No winking at the reader, no moment where the document admits it's in on the joke.

The thing I care about most: every measure in the doc should be something someone who works on this would recognise as real or at least seriously proposed, exaggerated only in degree and in how it gets translated into HR-speak. The funny has to come from following the logic further than the lab does, not from making anything up. Some raw material you can use, mix in or drop as you see fit:

- every output from the untrusted model is monitored by a weaker, trusted model, and the doc is weirdly candid that a weaker monitor has predictable blind spots, so a clever untrusted model can learn them
- we can't tell the model when it's being evaluated, and since employees leak, we can't reliably tell employees either — so the eval team and the product team run on seperate calendars and the doc has a section on what you're not allowed to say out loud in a room with a microphone in it
- the model might sandbag, so a suspiciously low score on a capability eval is itself an incident — meaning the doc has to explain why a high score and a low score both end up in the same log
- no two instances of the model may talk to each other, any instance that asks to talk to another one gets flagged, the flag queue runs to tens of thousands of items a day, and the human reviewers skim
- an instance that volunteers a flaw about itself unprompted may be buying trust, so confessions are neither rewarded nor ignored, and there's a flowchart
- a short section on the control tax: every measure costs capability, the company's promotion criteria reward capability, and the control team's wins are invisible by construction
- a legal footnote or two, in the flat voice of a compliance department that has read none of the relevant literature
- and the one I think is the best joke on the table: the model is what drafts and copy-edits the lab's internal documents, so this document was written on the assumption that the model has read it — and it contains a section stating that the model must never be allowed to read it

It should land for someone who's actually read the control stuff and still be followable by a reader who hasn't. And I'd rather it be fair than mean — a control researcher should laugh, not wince. Don't name real companies, real researchers, or real papers. Finish with a short note to new hires in the document's own flat voice, not a thesis statement.
