So my reading group has been going back and fourth on Malthus for weeks and I finally built an argument to settle it, except now I think it proves way too much and I can't find where it breaks. Here it is.

(1) Malthus's actual core claim is a conditional, not a dated forecast: if population grows geometrically while subsistence grows arithmetically, population will press against the food supply. The conditional itself doesn't say when.
(2) A claim that doesn't say when can't be refuted by what happens on any particular date.
(3) So the last two centuries of population and food both rising aren't evidence against the core.
(4) Any date we attach -- "by 1850," "by 2100" -- is an auxiliary assumption, not part of the core.
(5) Auxiliary assumptions can be swapped out indefinitely without ever touching the core.
(6) So nothing that ever happens can force us to give up the core. The theory is compatible with every possible history.
(7) And that's exactly what people mean when they say Malthus was "right but early." So he's right but early no matter how history unfolds.
(8) But by the same reasoning every failed long-run prophecy is right but early: the doomsday cult, the peak-oil guy, the people who swore the internet would kill cities. We don't grant them that, and we're right not to.
(9) So either Malthus wasn't right but early, or we owe the same courtesy to all of them.

I'd really like to keep (1) through (4), because I do think there's a real mechanism in Malthus and I don't want to just wave the whole thing away as pseudoscience. So: which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? And if it's (5) or (6) that's rotten, tell me what actually separates a legitimate rescue of a theory from a desperate one, because I can't find a criterion that doesn't also let the cult off the hook.
