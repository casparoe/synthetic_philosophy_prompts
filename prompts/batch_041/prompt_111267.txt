For the past month I have been reading about the Great Divergence — why sustained industrial growth began in Britain rather than in China — and about the older question that goes by Needham's name, why modern science did not arise there. I keep having the sensation that the whole dispute is a badly posed version of a problem I solve for a living. My day job is causal inference in epidemiology: I think in counterfactual outcomes, exposures, confounding, estimands and directed acyclic graphs, and I am fluent enough in that vocabulary to be suspicious of it.

Restate the debate in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Take "Britain industrialised first" as the outcome. Say what the exposure or treatment would have to be; whether the usual candidate explanations — coal, colonies and overseas demand, secure property rights, an unusually high-wage economy, the examination system, the state's suspicion of merchants — can be represented as interventions at all; what the counterfactual China is supposed to be; and what the estimand is. Where my framework's ordinary requirements (units, well-defined treatments, a fixed contrast, and the demand that a cause be something one could in principle intervene on) simply fail to apply, say so rather than fudging them.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want: which moves in the historians' argument survive the translation intact, which survive only as distortions, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. My own guess is that the residue concentrates in one place. The historians' question is contrastive — why Britain rather than China? — whereas my machinery takes the contrast as given and asks only about the size of an effect. Tell me whether that is a defect of my framework or a sign that the disagreement is less substantive than it looks; whether some other choice of estimand could absorb the contrastive structure; and whether the criterion by which one cause is singled out from a standing background of conditions has any analogue in what I do. Where something genuinely cannot be said in my vocabulary, name it precisely rather than retreating to "historical complexity". I do not need names, dates or a reading list; I want the translation.

Format: under 750 words. Lead with a one-sentence bottom line. Then a table with one row per candidate explanation and three columns: the candidate; its best translation into my vocabulary, giving the exposure or intervention and what the estimand becomes; and a verdict of clean, distorted, or no counterpart. Then the residue report in prose.
