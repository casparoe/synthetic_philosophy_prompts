My friend Nadia is not an archaeologist — she dropped out of a history degree about ten years ago — but she's read an enormous amount about prehistory and she has really firm opinions about when you're allowed to look at a find and say "this tells us what they believed." I keep having this argument with her and I want a proper answer, so please act like a philosopher of archaeology — somebody who thinks hard about how you get from objects to beliefs and institutions, not just somebody who knows a lot of digs. Think it through carefully and start by brainstorming the possibilities, dont just give me a gut answer at the top.

Her track record, the way I'd summarise it:

1. Deep cave paintings — the ones far in the dark where you'd need a lamp, similar animals over and over, cave after cave: she says yes, that's real evidence of a shared practice, they meant something by it. "You dont crawl a kilometre underground just to doodle."
2. Bogs where people put in swords, cauldrons, gold rings, over and over for centuries, bog after bog, and a lot of the metal was deliberately bent or snapped first: yes, she says, obviously a deliberate practice, that's not stuff getting lost.
3. Little clay figurines of people and animals, found broken in ordinary household rubbish, some of them reused as scoops: no, she says, that's toys and trash, everyone wants it to be a cult.
4. Normal graves — pot at the feet, a couple of beads, a flint knife, the pot usually worn and mended: no. "That's grief, maybe. Its not proof they thought they'd wake up somewhere."
5. A single pierced animal tooth, alone in a cave with nothing else around it: no, that could be anything, a lost thing, a kids thing, you cant build a religion on one tooth.

Now the new thing I want to ask her about. In one region people have been digging graves for a hundred years and they've found thousands, and they're all basically number 4 — plain, same layout every time, worn pots. Except one. One grave is cut down into solid bedrock, sealed over with a big stone slab, and inside is a person with a wagon, a huge cauldron, a gold ring round the neck, and a pile of miniature things — little vessels, a tiny boat, a copper axe so thin you could bend it with two fingers — all of it clearly made for the grave, none of it ever used or usable. Cutting that chamber and sealing it would have been weeks of hard work for a lot of people.

Two things I want her verdict on: does it show they believed in some kind of afterlife, and does it show there was a chief or a ranked society, somebody who could get other people to do that work.

So: what would she say about the tomb, and why? Then the bit I actually care about — what rule is she really running? Give me at least two other rules that fit everything on her list just as well as the one you pick, say what each of them predicts about the tomb, and tell me which you'd bet on and why. And then tell me what I should go looking for that would tell the rules apart — what kind of find would make her say one thing if rule A is right and the opposite if rule B is. You dont need to cite anything or give me a reading list, I cant look anything up right now, I just want the reasoning. And if you think she's wrong somewhere, say so.
