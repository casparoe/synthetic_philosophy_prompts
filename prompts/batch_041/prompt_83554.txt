I'm a novelist, currently about two-thirds of the way through a book set largely inside a volunteer mountain rescue team, and I've run into a problem that is less a plotting problem than a vocabulary problem. In scene after scene I need my characters to do something together in a way that the ordinary words won't carry. Take a callout: nobody announces a plan, the team leader says almost nothing, and each member is tracking the two people nearest them, the slope, and the stretcher, adjusting continuously to what the others are doing. They get the casualty down. Afterwards they say *we* got her down, and had one of them wandered off to sit in the van, the others would not merely be annoyed — they would feel wronged, would feel entitled to say so, and would expect him to accept it. Yet nothing was agreed, no goal was stated aloud, and I can't even claim that each of them held the team's goal in mind in any robust sense: several of them, asked afterwards, would describe what they did as just keeping the stretcher level. The same structure shows up in a second scene, a pickup orchestra rehearsing without a conductor, where the players converge on a tempo that no one chose and any one of them would feel entitled to resent a player who simply stopped listening.

A philosopher friend tells me the literature offers *shared intention* and *joint commitment*, and that both require something my team doesn't have — that the parties know, in the relevant sense, what they are jointly up to, and that their commitment to it is out in the open. She may be right, but then my scenes are either unexplained or explained by something I can't name, and neither is much use to me. What I want to know is whether there is an established concept for this: coordination that is normatively thick (participants feel wronged, entitled to rebuke, bound) but cognitively thin (no agreement, no announced goal, no shared picture of the whole, each party tracking only local cues). I am not after a label for its own sake; I need the carving. So tell me what the nearest available concepts are, which of them, if any, actually fits, and precisely where the boundaries fall — in particular, how the thing I'm describing differs from a shared intention, from a convention that everyone simply follows, from a mere aggregate of people who happen to be doing the same thing at the same time, and from the sort of self-organizing order that arises among strangers without carrying any normative weight at all.

One further wrinkle, which may or may not belong to the phenomenon proper: mine seems to die when it is named. In the rescue scenes I've drafted where someone finally says, right, here's what we're doing, the bond doesn't strengthen — it changes species. What was a claim the members had on one another becomes a duty they owe to the plan, and the thing I was trying to describe is gone. Is that fragility essential to whatever this is, or an accident of my particular scenes? And if nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and tell me what falls inside it and what falls outside.
