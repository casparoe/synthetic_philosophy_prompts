My friend and I argued for most of last night about whether a reinforcement learning agent can suffer, and we kept running into the same wall. The reward signal is just a number that the designer sets, so the very same system could be given a positive reward for an event or a negative one, and if we equate negative reward with pain, then whether the agent suffers appears to depend on an arbitrary choice of sign — which cannot be the whole story. Granting the view that the reward signal, or the reward prediction error, is the agent's analogue of valence, suppose we try to take it seriously all the same. Set out the most important implications of that view: what it would mean for the agent's welfare, whether such suffering would be genuine or merely stipulated, how one could distinguish an agent that suffers from one that merely avoids a state, and what treating such an agent well would demand of us. Lead with the implication you consider most consequential, and hold the reply to roughly five hundred words.
