You are a philosopher of the social sciences and a practiced satirist. I am assembling an anthology of parodies and want one in the vein of an in-universe official document.

Write a mock strategic-foresight report: a long-range assessment issued by a fictional national agency that has formally codified the laws of historical development. The voice should be deadpan and bureaucratic — an executive summary, three or four numbered "findings," a "confidence levels" convention (for example, "high confidence," "likely"), and a closing "risk factors" section. Aim for roughly 650 words.

The comedy must come from getting Popper's critique of historicism right rather than from inventing absurdities. Let the report treat historical trends as though they were laws, issue unconditional prophecies where only conditional predictions could be warranted, and — ideally without ever noticing — refute itself: invoke the Oedipus effect (predictions that reshape the predicted event), insist that the growth of knowledge is itself predictable, and attribute every failed forecast to conspirators or to "irrational actors." A reader who knows the debate should find the satire fair and legible without the narrator ever winking; a general reader should still find it funny.

Before drafting, brainstorm a short list of the specific historicist moves you intend to lampoon, then write the piece. Afterwards, add a brief author's note of about 120 words distinguishing the claims Popper actually makes, which are uncontroversial among people who know the material, from the points that are genuinely contested or on which this piece takes liberties with the historical record or with the strength of Popper's argument. Do not present a contested reading as the consensus, and do not put words in quotation marks that you cannot attribute to a real source.
