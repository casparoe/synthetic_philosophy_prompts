A catastrophe-risk analyst at a reinsurance firm must update the firm's probability model whenever a broker sends a note reporting that, while the broker cannot say whether the cedent's portfolio is in the Red region or the Blue region, if it is in the Red region then the probability that it is in the Headquarters sub-region is one half — a report about a conditional probability rather than a categorical fact. The analyst has one working day to respond, is not a trained probabilist, and the result must be signed off by the head of risk. Write a decision procedure the analyst can run on any such note, grounded in the Judy Benjamin problem and the general problem of updating on conditional-probability reports. Return exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: first, the procedure's prerequisites and the questions the analyst must answer before starting; second, the ordered steps of the procedure, including how to represent the report formally; third, how to compute the updated probabilities under at least two defensible update rules and how to proceed when they disagree; fourth, a worked application to two notes, one whose reported conditional matches the analyst's prior and one whose reported conditional does not, including at least one case where the procedure yields a result the analyst finds unwelcome; fifth, the cases the procedure cannot settle, where the firm must choose a rule on policy grounds rather than evidential ones, together with a one-line caveat the analyst should attach to any output.
