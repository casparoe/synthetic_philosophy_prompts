I've been trying to get my head round Harsanyi's aggregation theorem and I'm now fairly convinced it basically ends the argument about utilitarianism. Here's my reading of it: take any group of people whose preferences satisfy the expected-utility axioms, add the requirement that the social ranking goes along with unanimous individual preference (Pareto), and the only consistent way to aggregate is to add up their utilities. So surely prioritarians and Rawlsians — anyone who wants to tilt things towards the worst off — are just being inconsistent? They either give up Pareto or they admit they're rejecting a theorem that falls out of axioms nobody sensible denies. It also looks like it answers Arrow in a way: Arrow says aggregation is impossible, Harsanyi says it's fine once you let people rank lotteries. So where am I going wrong, because I dont see how a non-utilitarian can stay in the game here — is distributive ethics that isn't utilitarian just mathematically confused?
