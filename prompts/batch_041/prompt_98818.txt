The topic I've been handed is Otto Neurath's picture language, the Isotype thing. Don't explain Isotype to me, plan how I should teach it.

What I need: the order the ideas have to come in and why each one depends on the one before -- starting from where they actually are (they think this is a retro visual style, a design trend to mine for mood boards) and getting them up to the part that's actually philosophically interesting, which I take to be that Neurath built a picture language to do work he thought verbal philosophy kept botching, and yet was dead clear it could never be a full language, only an aid riding alongside words. The one chart or example that should carry the whole session: I was going to build it on the repetition rule, the thing where you never enlarge a symbol to show a bigger quantity, you just multiply the same symbol -- one figure stands for so many thousand people. Is that the right spine, or should the carrying case be something else? Tell me what it needs to have and lack to do that job. Then the misconceptions they'll bring or form, in the order they'll form them, and the exact point in the sequence where I should head each one off. My guesses: that Isotype is just proto-Tufte infographics; that a picture language is transparent and self-explanatory and needs no learning (which is backwards from Neurath, who insisted even the simplest symbol only works by convention); that the one-symbol-equals-N-units rule is a neutral technical choice rather than a loaded one; and that this is basically Carnap's logical ideal language when it definately isn't. Add the ones I'm missing. Also the check at the end that tells me whether the interesting idea landed rather than just the design history, and what I should openly say I'm skipping at this level.
