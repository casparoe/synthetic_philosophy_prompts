I recently had an experience that has made the Pasadena paradox feel less like a philosopher's toy and more like something I need to have a settled view about. A family member is negotiating a settlement whose payout structure is, without going into the details, a Pasadena game in all but name: a fair coin is tossed until the first head appears, and if that happens on toss n, the payout is (−1)^(n+1) · 2^n / n. The expected value series is the alternating harmonic series, which converges — to the natural logarithm of 2, if you sum the terms in the obvious order — but only conditionally, so rearranging the terms can make it sum to any real number whatsoever. The expectation is therefore not well-defined in the sense that matters for decision: its value depends on an ordering of outcomes that nothing in the situation itself supplies. My family member keeps asking me what the offer is worth, and I keep finding that the honest answer is not a number but a story about why there is no number.

Write a short story of about 1,000 words in which a character confronts a Pasadena-like offer. The story should not explain the paradox didactically; it should dramatize it. Let the character's attempts to reason about the offer — to compute an expectation, to find a principle, to decide — be shaped by the fact that the expectation is undefined. You may set the story anywhere and populate it with anyone, but the central tension should be the one the paradox creates: a decision that seems to demand a number, where no number is available, and where the alternatives to having a number (refusing to decide, picking arbitrarily, invoking something else entirely) each have their own costs.

Then add a section titled Commentary on the choices made, of about 400 words, explaining what you were doing: why you chose the setting and characters you did, how you decided to represent the undefined expectation without turning the story into a lecture, and which aspect of the paradox you think the story best captures and which it necessarily leaves out.
