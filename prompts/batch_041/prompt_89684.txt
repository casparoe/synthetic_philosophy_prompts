so my older sister is home from uni for reading week and she will not shut up about this book she read by Douglas Hofstadter called I Am a Strange Loop (pretty sure its from 2007, dont hold me to that) and specifically this thought experiment in it called the careenium. the way she tells it: imagine a billiard table the size of a warehouse, except instead of normal balls its absolutely packed with millions of tiny magnetic marbles just careening around, bouncing off each other and off the walls (I think thats where the name comes from). each marble on its own is stupid and simple. but they stick to each other into little clumps, and clumps stick to other clumps, and if you zoom way out you start to see that certain shapes of clump keep re-forming and breaking apart in a really regular way — she says the marbles are called simms and the clumps are simmballs, and that a simmball basically stands in for a concept or a symbol. also the walls of the table change how bouncy they are depending on what's happening in the outside world, so information gets into the system that way. her big question, which she keeps asking me like I'm supposed to know, is: inside the careenium, who is actually pushing who around? because you can say its just marbles knocking marbles forever, and the clumps are nothing over and above that. or you can say the simmballs are the ones doing the pushing, since the marbles wouldnt move the way they do if the clumps werent forming in the first place. she claims this is secretly the whole mental causation / free will thing in disguise and its been living in my head rent free for a week.

for philosophy club next month I have to write a dialogue between two people arguing about exactly this (theres only like 8 of us and honestly two of them just come for the biscuits, so it needs to be readable). could you write it? person A thinks only the substrate really pushes anything and the symbol level is just a handy way of describing the mess — a pattern, not a pusher, and patterns dont have muscles. person B thinks the symbol level genuinely does causal work, and that it isnt a coincidence the marbles end up where they end up, and that if you deleted the simmballs the marbles trajectories would actually be different, which sounds like pushing to them. both of them need to be properly smart and neither should be a pushover — I really dont want one of them to be there just to go oh wow good point and switch sides at the end. I want them picking up each others actual sentences and turning them around, going back and forth, and using concrete bits of the careenium (the walls that change bounciness, a particular clump that keeps re-forming, a single marble getting nudged somewhere new because of a clump that dissolved three seconds earlier). it should end without a clean winner but with both of them agreeing on what the real crux actually is, and maybe with one of them admitting their view has a cost the other one hasnt been able to pay either. also can you have them bring in one ordinary example so its not all abstract — like a laptop running a program, or someone buying a train ticket because they believe the 6 o'clock train exists — and can you keep the whole thing to around 900 words? thanks!!
