I've been kicking around a verison of the dark forest hypothesis that I don't think gets enough attention. The usual story is everyone hides, because anyone who reveals themselves gets wiped out. But I think that's an unstable equilibrium. Silence is cheap and ambiguous -- it probly means you're weak, or plotting. The stable thing, I think, is a small number of beacons: civilizations that deliberately broadcast a costly, verifiable commitment not to expand, maybe enforced by a dead-man's switch that punishes their own expansionists. The signal has to be expensive enough that a civilization which intends to expand can't afford to fake it. So the sky should be mostly dark but with a few rare, bright, short-lived lanterns. What would have to be true for this to be false? I want the observations, arguments, and formal results that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be. What does this predict that the standard dark forest doesn't, and what does it have to explain away? What would a defender of this view have to concede as counting against it? Also be honest: is this just unfalsifiable hand-waving once you add the caveat that the beacons are rare, and if it is well insulated, is that a problem for a view like this? And tell me what would change your mind -- which argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. I'd personally find a game-theoretic proof that the beacon equilibrium is unstable the most persuasive kind of objection.
