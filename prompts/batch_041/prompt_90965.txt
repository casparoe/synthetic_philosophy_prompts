I have been trying to formulate a principle about when two physical theories should be counted as describing the same ontology, and I would like you to act as a philosopher of science and metaphysician to stress-test it. By accessible information I mean the totality of facts that any possible embedded agent could in principle condition on in deliberation—not merely what humans can measure now, but what any finite or idealized observer could learn from within the world, including facts about their own location, memories, and decision-relevant probabilities. The principle, which I will call the Accessible-Information Identity Principle, says that if two theories agree on all accessible information, then they should be identified as the same ontology; any residual differences are artifacts of description, not ontological distinctions. I am not a verificationist: I allow that inaccessible facts may be real in some thin sense, but I deny that they mark out distinct ontologies. I want to know what would count against this principle, and I want the answer to be a map of its exposure rather than a general survey of underdetermination.

Please think carefully and begin by brainstorming the strongest candidate defeaters before organizing your answer. Sort them from fatal to merely awkward. For each, say whether it is an observation, a conceptual case, a formal result, or a development in physics or decision theory; what the Accessible-Information Identity Principle predicts that its rivals do not; and whether a defender of the principle would concede it as counting against them or dismiss it. I am especially interested in cases where inaccessible differences seem to do explanatory work—for example, a global ledger-swap that permutes an inaccessible hidden variable while leaving every local fact and every decision-relevant probability invariant; an epiphenomenal global property posited to explain why the laws are as they are; self-locating or indexical facts that are accessible to one agent but not another; gauge-related differences that are often called mere redundancy; and measure or symmetry considerations in cosmology that might make some inaccessible facts relevant to prediction. Also address the distinction between inaccessible in principle, inaccessible to any finite agent, and merely inaccessible to us now, and whether the principle can draw that line without collapsing into a triviality.

Finally, tell me honestly whether the principle risks being unfalsifiable or insulated by qualifications, and whether that is a problem for a principle of this kind. I personally find the ledger-swap case the most troubling, but I suspect the defender can bite a bullet there; I want to know where the principle is genuinely exposed, not where it can be patched. Argue from first principles; I do not need citations, reading lists, or bibliographies.
