ok so I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar session on pain and why it's bad — the hook is that neurological condition where patients report pain, can point to exactly where it is, rate it as intense, and yet it doesn't bother them at all; they don't seem to mind, they may not even pull away. The session is about what that does to our story about why pain is bad. I want a decision tree, not an essay.

Start the tree from something everyone in the room already grants — that these patients are reporting something and aren't just lying or mixed up about words — and branch only on questions a student can answer without already knowing which view they're being led into. So the nodes should be about the case, not the labels. Not "are you a representationalist?" but "when the patient says it hurts, is there a felt quality there that they're reporting, or only a judgement about their body?" Roughly 8–12 nodes, one page.

Leaves: I want at least these six to land somewhere as leaves, each described in a sentence, with a name only if there's a standard one and "unnamed" if there isn't: (1) the badness is baked into the sensation itself, so a pain that doesn't bother you is barely a pain; (2) the badness is a separate conative state — a desire/aversion tacked onto a neutral sensation; (3) the pain experience itself is a kind of command to protect the body, so its badness just is that directive; (4) the pain represents the bodily state as bad for you; (5) these patients aren't in pain at all; (6) pain isn't one natural kind so the question is malformed. If any of those can't be cleanly placed, say so rather than forcing it.

Then run two extra cases through the tree, because I think they split things that the plain asymbolia case leaves glued together: (a) a hypothetical drug that leaves the sensation bit-for-bit as it was but kills the "get away from this" pull for an hour; (b) a patient who says the pain is intense and that she'd rather keep it than lose it. For each, say which leaves predict what — including which ones say she's no longer in pain, and which say she is but it isn't bad. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't seperate.

And I want the empty cells called out: if some combination of answers has no defender, tell me whether that's because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or nobody's bothered yet. Also flag any node where the answer depends on an empirical claim about the patients rather than a philosophical one, since I don't want to smuggle that in. No citations, don't make me reference anyone.
