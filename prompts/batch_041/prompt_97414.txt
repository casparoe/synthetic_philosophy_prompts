for my film club blog I said I'd make one of those "which one are you" quizes about why we cry at movies even though we know the characters aren't real, because we argue about this every single week after screenings and I'm tired of going in circles. can you write it for me?

8 questions, each one a concrete case, 2-3 answer options each, worded so someone can answer without knowing any of the labels. I want these cases in there somewhere: a character dying in a film you've already seen five times; being way more wrecked by a biopic with actors then by a documentary about the same real events; jumping at a horror scene when you know fine well nothing can hurt you; feeling bad about a choice you made in a video game; crying at a story you made up yourself for a D&D campaign; feeling nothing when a character dies but getting upset about a stranger in the news; being sadder at the same sad scene on a day you were already in a bad mood.

Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions. These four need to show up: (a) we're not really feeling it, we're pretending to feel it as part of the game of make-believe; (b) we really do feel it, and thats exactly the problem, we're being inconsistent; (c) we really feel it but there's no inconsistency, because feelings can take thoughts or imagined stuff as their object and don't need beliefs; (d) we really feel it and it isn't even about the character, it's about the film/work itself. For each position add one line on the thing that's hardest to defend about it. If two of my cases can't actually tell two of these apart, say so instead of faking a clean key, and if some answer pattern doesn't land on any real position, tell me what's weird about it. I don't care who said what, no names or dates needed, just the positions.
