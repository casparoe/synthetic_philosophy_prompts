The episode: through the 1940s and 50s Austin went after the arguement from illusion as it was actually deployed by Ayer and Price — the argument that because a straight stick in water looks bent, or a white wall looks yellow under sodium light, and because the experience is (allegedly) indistiguishable from the veridical one, we must in both cases be aware of something bent or something yellow, i.e. a sense-datum. Austin's published lectures, Sense and Sensibilia, don't so much refute the premises as refuse the question: they go after the way the argument leans on the "looks F" idiom, as though "looks bent" reported an object rather than hedging a claim about the stick. Whether Ayer ever had a real reply, or whether the thing just fizzled, you can tell me. The lens: disjunctivism, specifically the denial of what's standardly called the Common Kind Claim — that veridical perception, illusion, and hallucination are all fundamentally the same kind of event, differing only in their causes and in whether their objects exist.
