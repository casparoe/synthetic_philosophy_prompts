I write backend code for a living and have zero formal philosophy training, so excuse the framing. I'm putting together a short piece for a zine a friend runs, on this word I keep bumping into, sphexishness. The picture I have in my head is a digger wasp: she drags a paralyzed cricket up to her burrow, but before hauling it inside she goes in first to check the burrow, leaving the cricket on the doorstep. Nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's inside and she comes back out, finds it, drags it to the door again, and now has to go check the burrow all over again. Do it again and she does the whole routine again. Apparently you can keep this up basically forever and she never skips the inspection step. People hold her up as the poster child for mindless rigidity, the opposite of free will. (A friend who does biology says the repeated check might actually be the sensible thing to do and that the wasp story gets told worse than it really is, but I don't know what to do with that, and I don't want the poem to just be me debunking a story I half-remember.)

What I want is a pantoum about her. You know the form: lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the last stanza is supposed to curl back around to the poem's opening line, so the whole thing closes into a circle. Thats exactly why I want a pantoum and not something else, the form itself should be the reset loop. Four or five stanzas, four lines each, lines kept shortish and roughly even, rhyme it if you can without it sounding like a greeting card.

The thing I actually care about: don't let it turn into a smug poem about how dumb bugs are. The wierd part is that I'm the one standing there moving the cricket, running the same move over and over to see whether she'll vary, and that's a loop too, and I'm not obviously outside of hers. At the end I want the circle to almost close but not quite, one line returning slightly changed, so a reader can't tell whether that counts as her finally doing something new or just the same machine with a bit of wear on it. Someone who knows the actual debate should read it and not wince, and someone who's never heard any of this should still get a good poem out of it.

After the poem, give me a short JSON block with no prose around it, fields: "form" (the form you used and any place you broke the pantoum rules on purpose), "reading_i_lean_on" (which take on the wasp the poem bets on), "the_tension" (one sentence on what the poem refuses to resolve), "where_it_could_be_wrong" (the consideration or bit of evidence that would make this framing of the wasp/human contrast fall apart, and your honest guess at how likely that is), "line_numbers_doing_work" (the lines where the repetition is carrying the idea rather than just the meter).
