I've been turning over the "unilateralist's curse" on my own for a few weeks now — the claim that in a group where anyone may act on their own judgment, and each member acts whenever they personally reckon the act worth doing, the group will keep doing things that none of them, taken together, would have signed off on, because it only ever takes one of them. I'd like a piece of satire that a specialist would find fair and a newcomer could still follow.

Write a 550–650 word op-ed in the voice of an invented founder-and-productivity guru — a confidence evangelist of my own making, not modeled on any real person — who is fed up with the curse being used as an excuse for hesitation. Straight-faced, with no irony detectable in his own voice, he argues that the cure for too much uncoordinated action is more of it: trust your gut, ship first, let the timid catch up. The humor should come from the mechanics rather than from making him a buffoon: every anecdote he proudly tells and every rebuttal he offers should be a clean specimen of the curse that he never recognizes — he reads one person's private calibration as if it settles the group's risk, he assumes an act that's usually harmless is harmless in the tail, he treats each additional volunteer as another unit of expected gain while forgetting that a single success is enough and each extra actor adds only downside, and he files any call for coordination under cowardice. Keep it deadpan throughout, with no winking at the reader.

Close with a brief afterword that steps outside his voice: say where the piece takes liberties with the idea or leans on a contested reading of it, how confident you are that the satire is faithful to the underlying logic, and name the single point in the piece you're least sure of.
