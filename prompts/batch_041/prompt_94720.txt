Set it between a Peircean and a Jamesian, both at the top of their game. Peirce's line: truth is what inquiry would converge on in the long run, it's a claim about how things stand independent of any believer's comfort or payoff, and if that means no finite inquirer ever actually holds the truth, fine, that's the price of not confusing truth with what's nice to believe. James's line: truth is something that happens to an idea, it's what's good in the way of belief, it has to cash out in a life, and an ideal limit nobody ever reaches is just a fancy way of never answering the question.
