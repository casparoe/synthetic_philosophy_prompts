for philosophy club im supposed to write a dialogue where two people argue, and I want to do the buridan's ass thing — the donkey that starves between two identical piles of hay. Only I dont want the boring version everyone does. My setup: two people standing in front of two doors that are exactly identical, with a 30 second timer, and when it runs out both doors lock forever and they get nothing. Character 1 says: if the doors are truly equal then there is literally no reason to walk through one instead of the other, so if you move at all it was a coin that moved you — you didnt choose, you just didnt stop the coin. Character 2 says thats a fake problem: walking through A is completely rational even though you have no reason to prefer it, and the demand for a reason to break the tie is where the mistake is. Make them actually go back and forth, each one answering what the other just said, no talking past each other, and neither of them is allowed to be the dumb one. Stuff I want them to fight about: does it change anything if you can switch doors after picking, or if the choice is irreversible? Can you decide in advance that when its a tie you always take the left one, and does that make the actual pick rational or does it just move the arbitrariness somewhere else? And if flipping a coin is a perfectly fine way to choose, why is the donkey a problem at all? Let them get somewhere, but dont force a winner if one of them honestly has to give ground.
