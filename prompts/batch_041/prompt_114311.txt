So I'm writing a feature about the way management theory keeps reinventing Gödel — every couple of years someone in an airport book discovers that organisations are self-referential and that the org chart is a living document — and I want a satirical sidebar to run alongside it. Can you write me a mock internal memo, say 600-700 words, from the chief of staff at a company that has just reorginised itself as what Hofstadter called a tangled hierarchy, in the Escher Drawing Hands sense: two hands drawing each other, the loop only closing because there's a level underneath it that isn't part of the loop at all, the inviolate level, the one where the artist actually sits and which the hands themselves can't see. The memo should announce the reorg to staff in deadpan corporate register, and it needs to get the machinery right rather than just name-drop it: the difference between the levels that are allowed to tangle and the level that fixes the rules and can't itself be in the tangle, why nothing inside a system can fully account for itself from inside, and what actually happens when the company, being thorough, draws the person who draws the org chart into the org chart too. The twist I want is that the memo is itself one of the hands — whoever is writing it is doing the drawing, so they're standing on the inviolate level, which is exactly the problem they're describing. Don't let it turn into a lecture; the philosophy should be doing the work of the jokes, and no real companies and no quotes from real people, invented or otherwise. Take a definite line on the ending — either the company spots the trap and quietly reinstalls a level it can't see, or it doesn't — pick one and commit, don't hedge in both directions. Then a couple of sentences at the end on where the piece takes liberties with Hofstadter or leans on a reading that people argue about. Keep the whole thing in continous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, just paragraphs like an email.
