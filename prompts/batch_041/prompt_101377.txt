Suppose Pierre Duhem could be briefed on a debate that postdates him by nearly a century. String theory appears to admit an enormous landscape of possible vacua, and many of its proponents infer that our universe is one region of a vast multiverse; so far, however, the framework has produced no distinctive prediction we can test. Some philosophers of science now hold that such a theory can still be confirmed non-empirically: since no rival framework able to unify gravity with the other forces has been constructed, the sheer absence of alternatives is itself treated as evidence for the theory.
