I am an undergraduate in a course on relativity and the block universe, and I am struggling with a passage from McTaggart’s 1908 paper The Unreality of Time that we read alongside the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument seems to show, from the relativity of simultaneity, that all events are equally real—that the world is a four-dimensional block in which the distinction between past, present, and future does not correspond to anything in reality. McTaggart, as I understand him, would say that this gives us only a B series, a series of events ordered by earlier and later, and that a B series without an A series cannot be time at all. I follow the general shape of that claim, but I cannot get clear on the passage below, and I would like help understanding exactly what McTaggart is arguing and what it implies for the block universe.

If, then, a B series without an A series can constitute time, change must be possible without an A series. Let us suppose that the distinction of past, present and future does not apply to reality. Can change apply to reality? What is it that changes?

Could we say that, in a time which formed a B series but not an A series, the change consisted in the fact that an event ceased to be an event, while another event began to be an event? If this were the case, we should certainly have got a change.

But this is impossible. An event can never cease to be an event. It can never get out of any time series in which it once is.

My questions are these. First, what does McTaggart mean by change in this passage, and why does he think that the only candidate for change in a B-series world is an event ceasing to be an event? Second, is he right that the relations of earlier and later are permanent, and does that permanence really rule out change? In the block universe, we still say that a poker cools, that a child grows, that a fire burns out. If McTaggart is right, are these not changes at all, or is he using change in a narrower sense? Third, how does this passage bear on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument? If the block universe is a B series without an A series, does McTaggart’s argument show that the block universe is not a temporal world, or does it show that the block universe must be supplemented with an A series—which McTaggart thinks is contradictory? I would like a careful reading of the passage that distinguishes what McTaggart is entitled to claim from what would be an overreading, and that explains how the B-series/A-series distinction is supposed to map onto the block universe debate. I am not looking for a bibliography; I want to understand the argument.
