I'm running a formal-epistemology reading group and we keep rehashing "Bayesian vs not" at a level thats useless to anyone. Can you make me a decision tree I can hand out that starts from "I've got some evidence but I'm not certain of it" and ends at a named updating rule? Each branch node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing which rule it leads to — whether you have a whole new probability distribution over the partition, or only a likelihood for the evidence, or only a vague feeling, that sort of thing. I want leaves for: Jeffrey conditionalization / probability kinematics, likelihood-ratio or virtual-evidence updating, conditionalizing on the proposition that you had the experience at all, just refusing to update until the evidence firms up, and at least one non-probabilistic rule (ranking theory, Dempster-Shafer, whatever you think earns its place). Use concrete cases at the nodes — the blurry-photo goldfinch credence of 0.6, a witness whose report is itself shaky, and a case where two uncertain observations give you a different posterior depending on which one you process first. That last one is the real crux for me, so make sure some branch separates people who think the final posterior shouldn't depend on the order of successive uncertain updates from people who shrug and accept that it does. Each leaf gets the rule's name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and if the tree can't actually separate two leaves (I suspect "refuse to update" and "wait for firmer evidence" collapse into each other) then say so instead of fudging it. Also flag any branch combination nobody seems to occupy and guess whether thats because it's incoherent or just unexplored. Aim for 7-9 leaves. Casual, it's a handout not a paper.
