I'm putting together a handout for a reading group on split-brain cases, and I want it to be a one-page decision tree rather than another list of -isms. Start everyone at the boring shared premise -- in the classic commissurotomy patients each hemisphere can respond, report, and in some cases act at cross-purposes -- and build outward from there with questions, not names. The distinctions I actually care about: does the case show a split in phenomenal unity, or only in what each hemisphere can access and report? do the two streams ever come apart inside a single action (one hand doing up the shirt while the other undoes it), or only on tasks they can't share? is the disunity something the surgery produced, or just what you'd expect to find in any intact brain if you could only ever ask one hemisphere at a time? and is the thing that's supposed to be unified a subject, a person, or an agent -- because if the person stays one while the subjects go to two, does that buy you anything? Every node has to be answerable by someone who doesn't already know which leaf it leads to. Leaves are the positions, one line each on the ugliest commitment, and I want a leaf for the deflationary 'no fact of the matter' option. Also tell me which combinations of answers nobody occupies, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just untried, and flag any two leaves your tree can't really separate. Aim for 8-10 leaves, and assume the audience knows the usual 'one mind or two?' framing already, so dont rest anything on it.
