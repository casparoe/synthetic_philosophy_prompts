My editor says theres something wrong with him; I say he's fine. I've realized I dont actually know the shape of this debate, so build me a decision tree of the positions on moral testimony — whether you can get moral knowledge by taking someone's word, and what (if anything) is lost when you do.

Build it questions-first, not names-first. Each branch node should be a question I could answer as a novelist without already knowing where it leads — something like: is what he gets from her *knowledge* or just true belief; does acting rightly require acting for the right reasons; is the loss epistemic at all, or moral/practical. Leaves I need: nothing is lost; the deed is fine but he can't be praiseworthy; what's lost is specifically moral *understanding* rather than knowledge; the worry is about his agency, not his beliefs; and the deflationary "this is just trusting your doctor" line. Flag any pair of leaves my tree can't actually separate, and any combination of branch-answers nobody occupies — is it incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?

Run it on my sister case throughout, and tell me where the case distorts things — a real relationship has power, intimacy, history, and the difference between him deferring on her *verdicts* vs. never learning to weigh reasons himself might be the real issue. Be opinionated: dont hand me a survey, tell me which position is actually right and why, even if its the unfashionable one. I'm not a philosopher so ease off the jargon, but don't flatten the distinctions.
