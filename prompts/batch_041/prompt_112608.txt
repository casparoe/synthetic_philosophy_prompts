Write a satirical op-ed, around 500 words, in the voice of the director of a fictional grantmaking foundation who has just gotten into infinite ethics and is now arguing, in perfect deadpan, that his foundation should blow its entire endowment on a single project — a one-in-a-googol shot at producing literally infinite value. The humor has to come from him getting the philosophy right rather than making things up: any nonzero chance of an infinite payoff dominates every finite good, so yes, he'll happily defund the childrens hospital; he knows this gets called fanaticism and has a slick comeback ready; and his grant committee keeps deadlocking because the two projects they're choosing between both have infinite expected value and he can't say which infinity is bigger. Let him stay cheerfully, consistently committed the whole way through — no winking, no quiet retreat into common sense at the end. Don't name any real philosophers; someone who knows the debate should grin, and someone who doesn't should still see where the logic is dragging him.
