I retired from a chemistry lab about six years ago and since then I've been teaching myself philosophy of science on my own — confirmation theory and probability mostly, since that's closest to what I used to do. I've been reading alot about Jeffrey conditionalization, the rule for updating when your evidence isn't certain, and I'm definately missing something, because the whole thing looks to me either trivial or illegitimate. I'd like you to tell me which parts of my worry are real and which are me misreading.

Here's my case. I run an assay and the readout comes back a color I'd have to call probably positive — I'd put it at 70/30. Fine. I take it I've learned something. What have I learned? I've learned that I'm 70% confident the assay is positive. So by ordinary Bayesian lights I should condition on that fact: treat my credence that the assay is positive being 0.7 as the proposition that just became certain, and update on it. That's the whole move, as far as I can see.

But the way the rule gets written up, it isn't that. It says: take your old conditional credences in each hypothesis given each possible evidence state, and average them, weighted by your new credences over the evidence states. Now, either that's the same thing as what I just said and the fancy name is doing no work, or it's a machine for updating on evidence you admit you don't have — which is exactly what I was always taught you can't do. Garbage in, garbage out. If I can't write down how likely that color readout would be under each hypothesis, I don't see what entitles me to move my credences at all. I'd sooner just call the assay inconclusive and leave my numbers where they were.

And there's a third thing that really nags at me. As I understand it, the answer depends on how I carve up the possible evidence states. If positive and faint positive are one lump, do I get a different posterior than if they're two? Because if so, that's not a rule, that's a Rorschach test, and it can't be right.

So where am I going wrong? Is Jeffrey's rule just conditioning on the fact that I'm 70% sure, or is it something else entirely? And if it's something else, what exactly have I learned in that assay case that licenses the update?
