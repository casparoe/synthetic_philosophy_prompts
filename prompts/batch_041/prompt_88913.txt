A classmate drew what he took to be a sharper conclusion from this: that whatever information a real market price carries is parasitic on the presence of participants who are mistaken, liquidity-driven, or otherwise not fully rational, and that a market of ideally rational traders who commonly knew this would be informationally empty. I want an actual number, and I want the reasoning decomposed rather than asserted. Finally, I'd like your verdict on a meta-question: is my classmate's formulation best read as a substantive thesis about real markets, or merely as a restatement of the theorem's own assumptions wearing the costume of a conclusion about them? Think carefully, and don't smooth over the places where the question may be less determinate than it sounds.
