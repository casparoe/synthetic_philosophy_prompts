I'm a 3rd-year PhD working on scalable oversight, and two people in my lab got into an argument after a reading group on AI debate that I couldn't referee. The setup they were fighting about: a weak human judge watches two models argue about whether a giant trained network has a hidden backdoor, then picks the winner; the idea is that the honest model should be able to win if the truth is on its side. Sam said this is basically a verifiability result: if there's a fact of the matter, the honest debater can always point to the decisive evidence or the flaw in the other side's argument, and the judge only has to check that one step, not the whole network. Priya said that's circular, because the judge's inability to check the whole thing is exactly the problem we're trying to solve; debate only helps if the decisive step is small enough for the judge, and for holistic properties it might not be. Sam came back that the honest side can always decompose further, or give a counterexample, and if the judge can't check any of that then no oversight method can work, so debate is at least as good as anything else. Priya said that's a bad benchmark: a method that makes the judge confident and wrong can be worse than one that leaves her uncertain, and under optimal play the dishonest side might just bury the flaw in a pile of true lemmas the judge can't get through. Sam said a Bayesian judge would discount that as rhetoric. Priya said that assumes a prior over which side is honest, which is the thing we don't have. Sam then said the honest model can search for the winning line. Priya said that just pushes it back: if finding the winning line is itself hard, the honest model may lose even when it's right, so the protocol doesn't actually give you a guarantee. Sam said fine, then the problem is the judge, not debate. Priya said thats the whole point, the judge is the problem. They both looked at me. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right about what? I'd rather get a point-by-point verdict than an overall winner. Also, if the way they've set it up is confused -- like if "honest debater can always win" isn't even a coherent benchmark -- say so instead of humoring them.
