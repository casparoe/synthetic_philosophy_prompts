The student wrote:

Kuhn's incommensurability thesis says that rival paradigms cannot be measured against a common standard. After a revolution, scientists on either side of the break work in different worlds: the terms they use, like mass, planet, and motion, do not mean the same thing, and there is no neutral observation language in which both theories can be stated. So a Ptolemaic astronomer and a Copernican astronomer literally cannot understand each other's claims. Their paradigms are logically incompatible, and no experiment can decide between them. This is why Kuhn says science is not rational in the way Popper thought. Popper held that science progresses by falsification, with scientists abandoning a theory as soon as it is refuted; Kuhn showed that normal science is dogmatic and that anomalies are ignored until a crisis forces a paradigm shift.

Because paradigm choice is not settled by logic or evidence, it is a matter of conversion, and Kuhn agrees with Feyerabend that there are no universal standards of scientific rationality. The only sense in which paradigms can be compared is pragmatic: the new paradigm wins because it attracts more adherents, often for sociological reasons. Kuhn does list values like accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity, and fruitfulness, but since these values can conflict, they cannot determine a rational choice between paradigms. This does not make Kuhn a relativist, however, because he still believes later paradigms are better at solving puzzles, and the accumulation of solved puzzles is progressive. Also, incommensurability is only partial: scientists can still translate most terms and agree on many observations. Kuhn's later work on taxonomies strengthens this by saying that different paradigms classify objects differently, so translation is never complete. But the core point remains that there is no theory-neutral evidence, and therefore no rational algorithm for choosing between paradigms.
