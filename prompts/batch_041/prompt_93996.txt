My friend's a philosopher of science and she has very settled intutions about when a thing really has a disposition and when it's just been rigged to look like it does. Her calls so far: ordinary window glass, fragile; the same glass welded inside a steel box, still fragile; glass that's been annealed so it genuinely won't shatter, not fragile; a sugar cube dipped in shellac so water can't get at it, still soluble; the same cube after it's been chemically converted into something inert, not soluble; a charged wire under thick insulation, still disposed to conduct; a charged wire hooked up to a switch that would drain its charge the instant a conductor touched it, not disposed to conduct; and a dead wire hooked to a gadget that would connect it to a live source the instant a conductor touched it, so a bulb would light, which she says is not disposed to conduct.

The new one: an ordinary pane of glass with nothing done to it, except that there's a gadget sitting next to it which would, the instant anything struck the pane, spray it with a fast-setting foam that would stop it breaking. Fragile or not? And what I actually want is the rule she's running, the other rules that fit her past calls just as well, what each of those predicts about the new case, which one you'd bet on and why, and what case would settle it.
