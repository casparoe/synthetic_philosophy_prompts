I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I've been asked to circulate a short position statement before my reading group takes up illusionism. What I want is a consistency audit rather than encouragement: tell me where the real trouble is, not that everything fits.

My commitments:

(1) Illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. There is no phenomenal character in the sense the realist intends: no intrinsic, ineffable, subjective feel constituting what it is like to undergo an experience. What exists instead are introspective representations that systematically misrepresent us as having states with such properties.

(2) Introspection is quasi-perceptual. It is generated by a dedicated monitoring system, carries content about our own states, and, like perception, is fallible; but as a rule it is reliable about the states it represents.

(3) Transparency. When I introspect an experience, I find the properties I take the world to have — the redness of the tomato, the shrillness of the bell — and no further intrinsic features of the experience itself.

(4) Representational realism about experience. Perceptual experiences have genuine representational content with accuracy conditions, and in favourable conditions they are veridical about the world.

(5) I am not an eliminativist. Consciousness is real; it is what distinguishes a creature that is awake and responsive from one that is not; and the word should be kept.

(6) The meta-problem is empirically tractable. We can explain why creatures like us come to believe there is an explanatory gap, in terms of the format and functional role of our introspective representations, without positing phenomenal properties; and the gap itself is an artefact of how introspection presents its objects, which dissolves once we stop treating those presentations as windows onto intrinsic properties.

Which pairs here are actually in tension, which merely look so, and how severe is each real tension? For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost; then say which single revision would resolve the most while costing the least. Please rank rather than flag indiscriminately, and keep the whole reply under 500 words.
