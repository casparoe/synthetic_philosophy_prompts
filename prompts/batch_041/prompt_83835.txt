I hold a two-part view about rational credence, and I want an honest map of how exposed it is. The first part is that a rational credal state is a set of probability functions rather than a single one, and that the set represents genuine indeterminacy in what the evidence supports rather than ignorance about some precise degree of belief I could in principle introspect. The second part is a restriction I have not seen defended in quite this form: my imprecision is local and generated. I am sharp wherever symmetry or a stable frequency settles the matter, and imprecise only elsewhere; and my set is never the vacuous set of all distributions but always the members of some explicit parametric family — a range of values for a bias parameter, or a handful of models I could write down and defend one at a time — whose value I take the evidence to leave open.

What would count against this? I want defeaters of every kind: formal results, cases where the view returns the wrong verdict, rivals that account for the same phenomena more cheaply, and consequences that even a friend would concede are ugly. Several I already suspect and want assessed rather than merely listed. Dilation, in which learning something leaves the posterior set for a proposition a strict superset of the prior set for it, so that information makes me less precise than I was — and in particular whether it breaks the local/generated restriction by leaking imprecision from the parts of my credence I am vague about into the parts I take to be settled. The suggestion that any genuine evidential symmetry should force sharpness, so that symmetry does not license a spread but forbids it. The fact that a set of probabilities is not yet a theory of action, so that the view needs a decision rule, while the standard candidates — maximality, E-admissibility, maximising the lower expectation, interval dominance — each misbehave somewhere; whether that zoo is a research programme or a symptom. The possibility that a rival keeping sharp credences and adding a second-order aversion to ambiguity, or a non-additive capacity, explains Ellsberg-style behaviour without any imprecision at all. And the worry that an agent whose set is wide enough can never be moved by data.

Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and say plainly which a defender of my view would concede as counting against it and which would be dismissed as a misdescription. Say also what my view predicts that a sharp Bayesian's does not, if anything. Where the view is insulated rather than exposed — because the choice of family and rule is free enough to fit almost any behaviour, or because the claim that the evidence underdetermines the credence is not the kind of claim anything could refute — say so, and say whether that insulation is a defect or simply what a theory of indeterminacy should look like. I would be most moved by a demonstration that the local/generated restriction cannot be held, or that it commits me to something no one would accept.
