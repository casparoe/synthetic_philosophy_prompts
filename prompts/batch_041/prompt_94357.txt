For the past three years, my closest friends and I have eaten dinner together almost every Friday, and for as long as any of us can remember we have split the bill evenly, whatever each person happened to order. The arrangement began, as best I can reconstruct it, with a single evening when one of us had left his wallet at home and another quietly covered the table; afterwards it simply became what we did, and in all that time no one has ever proposed an alternative. Our group has lately grown, though, and one of the newer members — who orders sparingly and drinks only water — has begun saying, half-jokingly, that she is subsidizing the rest of us. Last week she tried paying only for her own meal, and the table went quiet; someone covered the difference without being asked, and nothing was said about it afterwards.

I am genuinely torn. Part of me wants to support her. But the friend who has always been the loudest voice at the table treats anyone who hesitates to split evenly as stingy, and I notice that most of us go along with him less because we agree than because we would rather not be mocked. I also like the even split: it spares us the arithmetic and the awkwardness, and there is something pleasant about a table where nobody is keeping score. To complicate matters further, we are spending the summer with another group of friends who do the opposite — each person pays for exactly what he or she ordered — and by August the two groups will have to settle on one way of doing things or the other.

What should I do? Should I back her, and if so, how should the change be made? And beyond that, I would like to understand what sort of thing our Friday arrangement actually is: is it a rule we are genuinely bound by, or only a habit that has hardened because none of us ever questioned it — and does it matter that not one of us ever agreed to it in the first place?
