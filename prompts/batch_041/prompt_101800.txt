We're doing a session on anthropics — the self-sampling vs self-indication assymptions, and the presumptuous philosopher, the guy who figures he can settle from his armchair whether the universe is big or small just because one theory predicts more observers. I want to close with a poem, 20-30 lines, free verse, spoken by that philosopher. The constraint I actually care about: make it cumulative, each stanza repeating all the lines that came before and adding one new one, so it grows by a line every time and by the end he's buried in his own echoes. That's meant to be SIA doing its work (more copies, more credence) rather than just being announced, and it should make the self-sampling reply feel like the one line he keeps not adding. Don't let it resolve into a verdict, I'd rather it stay queasy. And if that formal trick is just a gimmick, or if I've got which assumption makes the universe more crowded backwards, say so plainly instead of humouring me.
