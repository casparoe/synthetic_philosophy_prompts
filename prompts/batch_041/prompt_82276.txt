I've somehow ended up on a small advisory thing for our government about the world happiness rankings, and I want to figure out what I actually think before the first meeting, mostly by going with my gut on cases. The measure, as I understand it, is basically one question: picture a ladder, 0 to 10, top rung is the best possible life for you, bottom rung is the worst possible life for you, where do you stand. About a thousand people per country, three years of responses pooled together, and countries a few places apart are basically tied -- the error bars overlap. Bhutan, of all places, isn't even in the table.

My gut verdicts on ways we could climb it:

- Minimum wage up so the bottom decile has more money: fine, obviously, and nice if the score moves.
- Shorter work week at some cost to GDP: fine, a real gain, I don't care that the economists wince.
- Capping how much foreign TV and instagram people see, so the top rung stops looking like a Malibu mansion: cheating, I think, even though I can't point to anyone it hurts.
- Same for a government campaign telling people to want less: cheating, pretty sure.
- But my grandmother's village, where nobody had much and nobody expected much, and where you'd get a 9 out of most people on that ladder: not cheating at all, I think they're genuinely content.
- Suppressing survey returns from the unhappy province: not fine, that's just lying.
- Letting in a lot of cheerful immigrants so the average goes up: feels like cheating for "how's the country doing" purposes, but everyone who moved here is better off, so I can't say anyone was harmed.
- A decade where incomes double, everyone's life genuinely improves, and the score doesn't budge because the top rung rose too: I'd say the country did well and the ranking failed to notice.
- Spending real money on treating depression: counts as raising happiness for real, though a cynic would say we just changed what people tell a stranger with a clipboard.
- We climbed four places this year: don't care, that's noise.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What I keep comeing back to is "is anyone being fooled or manipulated?", but the grandmother case breaks it -- nobody there is fooled, they just don't know what else exists -- and the immigrants case breaks it too. What I want: a principle or two, stated precisely, with the case that motivates each clause, what it says about a case I haven't listed (a voluntary, non-coercive, well-funded national "want less" campaign, Stoic flavoured, score goes up, nobody worse off), and an honest note about which of my verdicts it definately can't save. And then the bigger thing: if the principle comes out one way, does that mean these rankings measure happiness at all, or are they measuring how well people are doing relative to what they've been led to expect? Because if it's the second, I want to know whether topping the thing is still worth trying, or whether we should be telling people not to bother.
