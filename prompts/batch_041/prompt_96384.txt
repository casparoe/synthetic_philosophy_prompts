ok so in two weeks I'm giving a talk at an agronomy department's one-day symposium on pollinator and soil health, and I'm the only person there from a philosphy dept. The room is basically: a handful of IPM extension people who spend their lives telling farmers what to spray and when, two ecotoxicologists, two reps from a beekeepers' association, one soil ecologist who works on mesofauna and who I'm told is quite scary, and someone from an environmental NGO who I suspect came to hear me say the correct thing about neonics. Twenty minutes plus fifteen for questions.

My thesis, roughly: the moral case against neonicotinoids has been built around the wrong animal. Honeybees are managed livestock, and a lot of the save-the-bees framing is sentimentality about a domesticated species we truck around by the pallet. The deaths that should be doing the moral work are the non-target soil and litter arthropods that a systemic seed treatment reaches whether or not there's actually a pest problem that year. Second move: if you take those deaths seriously, the conclusion isn't a ban, it's rebuilding risk assessment around a different ledger, something like deaths per hectare per unit of yield, rather than lethal-endpoint tests on a handful of test species. So it's half applied ethics, half methodological proposal. I'll be honest up front that my numbers are borrowed from other people and I don't know how soft they are.

Also, and I'm not sure I want to say this out loud: I think if you apply the ledger consistently it probably doesn't come out cleanly in favor of organic either, since tillage does a number on soil fauna. That's the part I've been dodging in drafts.
