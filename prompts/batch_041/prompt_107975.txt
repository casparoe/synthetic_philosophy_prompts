I'm a retired behavioural ecologist — thirty-odd years on social insects, mostly ants, a bit of paper wasps — and since I packed it in I've been reading my way into philosophy, mainly the philosophy of religion and the literature that tries to treat religion as a natural phenomenon rather than as something handed down. I could use a hand, and I'd rather have it from someone who wont let me get away with anything.

Here's what I want. Take the live disagreement about how religion is to be explained naturalistically — whether it's a proper adaptation shaped by selection, or a by-product of cognitive machinery that evolved for other purposes, or a cultural item that spreads because it's catchy and gets reshaped every time it's passed on, or a group-level adaptation that turned up once societies got too big for personal reputation to police anything — and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually think in. That means colonies and castes, kin selection and colony-level selection, sterile workers, division of labour, queen pheromones and worker policing, those transitions where previously free-living units give up breeding on their own account and become parts of a new higher-level individual, spandrels and exaptations, drift, frequency-dependent selection. Two things from my own game that I'd like you to run hard. First, we're fussy about cues versus signals: a cue is just something another animal can read, a signal evolved to be read. I suspect a good half of your by-product-versus-adaptation row is that distinction wearing a hat, and I want to know if I'm right. Second, I've got a hobby horse — I keep wanting to call a sacred symbol a pheromone, a signal that reorganises the behaviour of every receiving unit and means nothing outside the state of the colony. Try it on properly and don't be polite about it.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Which bits go across cleanly, which go across only if you bend them, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I'm thinking of the content of a belief as against its function; whether anyone actually believes it; religious experience and what it's like from the inside; the fact that religious claims purport to be true and that people quarrel over them as if truth mattered; the authority of religious norms; the difference between a person's faith and a colony's state. For each residue, say whether the fault lies with my vocabulary — a real feature of religion that ant-talk can't reach — or with the original claim, which perhaps was never clear enough to translate. Its the interesting part, so spend your effort there.

Two housekeeping things. I can't look anything up, so no reading lists, no citations, no 'see X 1998' — if a point can't be made without pointing at a paper, just tell me that and move on. And be honest about your own translation: where are you most likely to be smuggling the philosophers' assumptions in and calling it translation, and what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would make you say the residue is an artefact of my framing rather than a genuine gap?

Take it slowly, and start by brainstorming the candidate mappings before you commit to any of them. Two hats, please: evolutionary biologist and philosopher of religion, and don't let either one do the other's job. Answer in British English. I'm a hobbyist with a decent memory and no patience for hedging, so give me your actual verdict.
