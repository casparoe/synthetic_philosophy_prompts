My friend Priya has very settled opinions about which collectives have minds, and I want to pin down her rule before she sees our new build. Her track record so far: a single neuron, no mind. A human brain, mind. A single ant, no mind, obviously. An ant colony, mind — she's adamant that Aunt Hillary is real, that you can actually have a conversation with a colony if you ask it the right way. A beehive, mind. A starling murmuration, no mind, because each bird just tracks its six or seven neighbours and "nobody's home." A slime mould that solves a maze by leaving trails, no mind. A big corporation, no mind — she says the "corporate mind" is a useful fiction and everything the firm does is really done by its employees. A stock market, no mind. A split-brain patient, she says two minds. A sleeping person, one mind.

Now here's the new case we're about to show her. We built a swarm of matchbox-sized robots, each running a dumb twenty-line program with no memory and no idea what the swarm is doing, and we tuned the local rules until the swarm does everything a real colony does — forages, switches jobs when the ratio of foragers to nurses drifts, builds bridges out of its own bodies, even "fights" rival swarms by massing. Structurally it's ant-for-ant the same as the colony she already blessed, except it's silicon and wire instead of ants. Will she say it has a mind?

What I actually want: the rule she's most likely running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, what each one predicts about the swarm, which way you'd bet and why, and what further case would settle it if the swarm turns out not to. Don't just tell me yes or no.
