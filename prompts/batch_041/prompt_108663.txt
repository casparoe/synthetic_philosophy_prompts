my reading group keeps circling a view one friend calls lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism, and before we argue about whether it's right I want to nail down what any theory of the relative value of pleasure and pain would have to deliver. the view, roughly: there's a line in the badness of suffering. below it, pain is commensurable with pleasure and the two can be traded off at some rate. above it, no amount of pleasure on the other side makes up for it -- the suffering trumps lexically, the way the first digit trumps everything after it in a number. so a mountain of mild goods can't buy out one episode of the bad stuff, but a mountain of mild goods can buy out a pile of mild bads.

what I want first is the spec, not the verdict. give me the requirements list for an adequate account of how pleasure and pain compare. the cases it has to get right: a long dull ache versus a short sharp one; one person's agony versus a very large number of people each getting a moment of mild unpleasantness; a day of real misery versus a lifetime of low-grade contentment; a pain you can shrug off versus one that swallows everything else in the person's life. the distinctions it can't collapse: pain the sensation versus suffering, intensity versus duration versus how much it actually matters to the person, comparability versus commensurability, the within-a-life scale versus the across-people scale. and the facts or constraints it has to stay consistent with: that we do make these tradeoffs constantly in medicine and in animal welfare budgets, that pain relief has diminishing returns, that some people report the sensation and don't seem to mind it much.

then tell me which of these are genuine requirements and which are just the prejudices of one camp. mark the pairs I can't have together. i'm especially suspicious of the combination of (a) lexical dominance above a threshold, (b) comparing options by expected value under uncertainty, and (c) any non-negligible chance of threshold-level suffering actually occurring -- if those three can't be held at once, say so, and say which one people usually give up and whether giving it up costs them the view. also, which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the popular positions on offer, and is there any way to state the lexical idea so it survives that requirement? skip the survey of who says what, i dont need a reading list, I want the criteria and the collisions between them.
