I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I need a case built to order for a paper arguing that at least some of the joints the sciences carve are fixed by classificatory decisions rather than by the world alone. Construct one realistic case — a paragraph, hypothetical but of a kind that could actually arise in a working science, no invented substances and none of the water/jade/tiger/phlogiston standbys — in which three accounts of kindhood give three different verdicts about the same category, and give those verdicts because of the structure of the case rather than by stipulation. The accounts: (1) intrinsic-cluster, on which a kind is a set of things sharing a cluster of intrinsic properties sustained by some mechanism; (2) origin, on which kindhood is a matter of shared causal history and requires no intrinsic resemblance; (3) ratification, on which a category is a kind for a science just in case that science's authorized classificatory procedure licenses treating it as one. Take the hard direction: (1) says yes, (2) says no, and (3) says yes for reasons that do not reduce to (1). Then set out each verdict and the reasoning behind it; name the feature of the case that is doing the work and say what happens to the disagreement if that feature is removed; and give the strongest reply available to the origin theorist, in particular whether they can convert the shared intrinsic structure into a common cause, and whether that reply lands in your case. A category whose membership is fixed by a standing authoritative body is fair game.
