I chair our state sentencing commission, and at next month's meeting we must vote on a petition from the public defender's office to add a new mitigating factor to the guidelines: substantially diminished free will. The petition is backed by a neurobiologist's report in a pending aggravated-assault case; the report attributes the defendant's conduct to childhood lead exposure, poverty, and adverse childhood experiences and concludes that his actions were causally determined by those factors. The defense bar argues that if the will is not free in the libertarian sense, the guidelines' assumption of desert is unjust; the prosecutors reply that generic determinism is true of every defendant and so cannot mitigate without collapsing the guidelines. Our guidelines already list duress, diminished capacity, and youthfulness as mitigating factors, and the petition asks us either to add a fourth category or to reinterpret diminished capacity to cover what it calls reasons-responsiveness deficits. The commission is split. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete disposition -- adopt the factor as written, reject it, or adopt a narrower alternative -- and give reasons the other commissioners can argue with. Engage the strongest version of the losing side, not a caricature. You may use the distinction between free will as ultimate origination and free will as reasons-responsiveness, and the idea that the varieties of free will worth wanting are capacities for rational self-government rather than exemption from causation. Do not include citations, reading lists, or references to the literature. Begin the memo with your recommendation in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
