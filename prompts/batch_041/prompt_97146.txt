I'm giving a talk in three weeks to my department's philosophy of language reading group and I need a Q&A prep sheet, because I keep getting blindsided in these things. My thesis: the white-horse essay that comes down to us in the Gongsun Longzi -- the bai ma fei ma thing, white horse is not horse -- is not the cheap equivocation everyone treats it as. I think it's a real claim about kind terms: that a kind name like horse picks out the kind in a way that doesn't distribute down to its determinate subkinds, so white horse can fail to be identical with horse even though every white horse is, obviously, a horse. To make this non-vacuous I've been pushing a distinction I call the naming/covering gap -- horse covers the white horse, in the sense of being true of it, without naming what the white horse is. That gap is the whole paper.

The audience is the problem. It's mostly analytic phil of language: two committed Fregeans who will want to know what the sense/reference story is supposed to be here, a guy who works on generics and mass/count, and someone doing natural-kind semantics who thinks this is just the determinate/determinable distinction badly translated. Then there are two sinologists from East Asian studies who've been invited and who think the recieved text is a patchwork and that any logical reading of it is annachronistic.

Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come, phrased the way each camp would phrase it -- sinologists from the text-history side, analytic people from the semantics side -- with a two-minute reply for each and a note on where my reply is soft. Tell me which objections have real force versus which are just turf, what I should concede up front before anyone even asks, which single reply has to land or the paper is dead, and the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
