I am preparing materials for a graduate seminar on interpretation and need a thought experiment constructed to order. The target is a three-way divergence among actual intentionalism, on which the meaning of a text is fixed by what its actual author intended; conventionalism, on which meaning is fixed by the linguistic and literary conventions governing the text; and hypothetical intentionalism, on which meaning is fixed by the intention that an ideal reader, fully informed about the text and its public context but not about the author's actual mental states, would be justified in attributing to the author. I want a case in which actual intentionalism and conventionalism agree on one reading while hypothetical intentionalism yields a different reading, and not because the ideal reader is merely ignorant of some fact the actual author knew, nor because the author is lying, nor because the text is ambiguous or ironic. The case should be realistic: a single sentence from a novel, a poem, a letter, or a legal document, with an ordinary publication or delivery context, and no longer than a paragraph. The divergence should be generated by a feature of the public context that is genuinely available to the ideal reader but that points away from the author's actual, conventionally encoded intention. You may invent a fictional author and work, but do not rely on a private language, a stipulated private meaning for a public word, or a supernatural device.

Build the case, then give the verdict of each of the three positions and explain why each verdict follows from that position's own commitments. After that, tell me which feature of the case is doing the work; say what happens to the three verdicts if that feature is removed; and explain how a hypothetical intentionalist would try to resist the example, either by rejecting the description of the ideal reader or by arguing that the case concerns significance rather than meaning. Finally, note whether the case still works if the author is dead or unavailable, and whether it matters that the text is literary rather than legal. Please write the whole answer in British English, and do not simply assert that the positions disagree: show the disagreement.
