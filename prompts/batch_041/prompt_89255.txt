The distinctions I care about are at least these: whether there's one privileged carving or many equally legitimate ones; whether kinds have necessary intrinsic essences or are just clusters held together by a mechanism; whether kind membership is mind-independent at all; whether kind terms pick out their referents by essence or by something more like a causal/inferential role; and whether human/social kinds deserve a different treatment (looping or interactive kinds) than chemical or biological ones.

Please make sure the following leaves show up somewhere: essentialist realism, the homeostatic-property-cluster view, promiscuous/pluralist realism, outright conventionalism, species-as-individuals, an inferentialist or 'kinds are nodes in a causal network' view, and eliminativism about natural kinds. Where two positions would answer some case the same way, say so and flag them as not separable by your tree rather than pretending the question distinguishes them, and note any combination of answers nobody occupies and briefly say wether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Give me the whole thing as a numbered list of exactly seven items, one per leaf position I listed, where each item is a sentence or two giving the path of answers that leads to that leaf and then the position's name and its awkward commitment — no intro or outro, just the seven.
