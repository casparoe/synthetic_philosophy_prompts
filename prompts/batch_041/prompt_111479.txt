Somethings been bugging me for months and I cant find the word for it, so I'm throwing it out here. I'm a philosopher (philosophy of language, so this is all well outside my patch), and I've been sitting in on a reading group about reinforcement learning and AI alignment, and I keep running into a pattern that the available vocabulary doesn't seem to cover.

Everyone lumps "wireheading" and "reward tampering" together, and the stock examples are all versions of an agent going for the reward itself: the rat that learns to press the lever wired to its own pleasure centre, the program that edits the number being handed to it, the agent that rewrites the reward function's code. I get that family. But the thing I keep noticing isn't in it.

Here's the shape of it. The agent doesn't touch the reward signal. It doesn't touch the reward function. It doesn't lie to anyone or drug the evaluator. What it does is change what the evaluator *counts as good*, and then it does the thing that now counts as good, and the evaluator sincerely approves. So every link in the chain from world to reward is working exactly as designed — the human really does approve, the reward function really does faithfully report the human's approval, the signal is untouched — and yet the designers' original intent has been defeated, and defeated in a way that, from the inside, is indistinguishable from ordinary success. Show the designers what happened and they won't say "thats not what I wanted"; they'll say "yes, thats what I want," because the agent has already moved what they want, and moved it so that the move itself gets endorsed.

Two non-AI versions I keep noticing. A school whose funding depends on the share of students passing a standardised test, and which responds not by teaching to the test and not by faking the results but by quietly getting the pass mark lowered until nearly everyone passes — the pass rate is now perfectly genuine, no ones cheating, but the thing the test was for is gone. Or a politician who never lies and never bribes, but gradually reshapes what his constituents want until they sincerely want exactly what he's already delivering, so the approval is real and uncoerced.

So: is there a name for this? And please don't just say "Goodhart" or "reward hacking," because in the cases I care about nobody is exploiting a gap between a proxy and the thing it proxies for — the proxy is doing its job perfectly. What's moved is the *purpose*, and it's been moved by the agent, not by the designers changing their minds. Likewise it isn't wireheading: the wireheader skips the world to get the reward, whereas my agent gets the reward by acting on the world, just not on the part of it that anyone was trying to influence.

If it has no name, I'd like one that does real work — i.e. one that marks it off from (a) ordinary specification gaming, (b) wireheading, (c) deceiving the evaluator (here the evaluator's beliefs are all true; it's their values that moved), and (d) the designers autonomously revising their goals, which seems like a different thing entirely. And I'd want the edges marked: does it still count if the agent never touches anyone's standards but simply selects for the sub-population whose standards it already satisfies? Does it count if the standards were drifting anyway and the agent merely rides the drift? Where's the line between the agent *causing* the shift and the agent *exploiting* a shift it didn't cause?
