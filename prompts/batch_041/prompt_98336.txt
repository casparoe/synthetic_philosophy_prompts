I am a second-year PhD student working between philosophy of mind and visual psychophysics, and I need to turn a half-formed idea into a two-page proposal for a small internal grant that would fund one semester of pilot work. The topic is the color phi phenomenon and the dispute over when the content of experience is fixed.

The phenomenon, as I understand it: when a colored dot is flashed at one location and a differently colored dot is flashed shortly afterward at another location, observers under the right combination of timing and spacing report a single dot that travels between the two positions and changes color partway along its path — the color change is reported as occurring before the second dot was presented. No experience of the second color could have been produced before the second stimulus, so either the experience of the first dot is held back and the whole sequence is constructed before anything becomes conscious, or the experience occurs in something close to real time and the record of it is subsequently rewritten.

These two options have standard labels. On the Orwellian reading, experience is delivered roughly on time and the retrospective record is later revised, so the subject's memory of what they saw is not a faithful record of what they experienced. On the Stalinesque reading, the revision happens upstream of experience: the brain waits, reconciles the conflicting signals, and only then admits a single coherent episode into consciousness, so the memory is faithful and the experience itself was always of the moving, color-changing dot.

The received wisdom, which I am suspicious of, is that the two readings are empirically indistinguishable — that no experiment could separate them, because every measure of the content of experience is a report, and the report is precisely what the two readings disagree about the provenance of. If that is right, the dispute is not a scientific one. I want to know whether it is right.

My half-formed thought is that the debate has been framed as a dispute about a single instant — the moment at which the content is fixed, before or after the moment at which the experience occurs — and that this framing is what makes it look undecidable. If we instead treat the two readings as claims about the format of the representation that issues in the report — a perceptual construction versus a reconstructed memory — then they should carry different downstream signatures. A revised memory ought to behave like other reconstructions in memory: fragile in the ways memories are fragile, vulnerable to interference and to updating after the fact, and in competition with other memory demands. A pre-conscious perceptual construction ought to behave like perception: subject to adaptation, masking, and the ordinary sensory constraints, and not competing for memory resources.

I want the proposal to test that idea, and I want it to be honest about how likely the test is to work.

Please write the proposal. It should state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; explain why the existing positions leave it open, and specifically why the standard framing leaves the recast question open rather than settling it; lay out the method concretely — what the stimuli would be, what range of timings and spacings the conditions would cover, what the comparison would be between a measure that stresses memory and a measure that stresses perception, and how each of the four possible outcomes would be read (a signature in memory only, in perception only, in both, or in neither); say what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing, including the outcome in which the signatures turn out not to be separable, which I would want to be able to report as a real finding rather than a null result; and be realistic about scope for one semester of pilot work, distinguishing what can be done with a few dozen participants from what would require a full programme.

Do not worry about citations or a literature review; I will fill those in. Do not lean on particular published experiments or on any named author's formulation of the distinction — I want the argument to stand on the phenomena and the logic rather than on who said what.
