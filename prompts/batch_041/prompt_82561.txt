Something's been niggling me for years and I've never found out weather the bit that bugs me actually has a name.

The setup is a puzzle I read in some maths book. A group of people are all offered the same deal, and how well you do out of it depends on how many other people take it up. The version I remember — I cant recall the exact mechanics, so bear with me — is a lottery you can enter as many times as you like, where every entry you send makes the prize a bit smaller for everybody, so it's always tempting to send one more. Play it the obvious way, by working out what everyone else will probably do and then doing slightly more than that, and you all end up sending an absurd number of entries and the prize thins away to nothing. But there's a second way of thinking, where instead of asking what's best for me given what they'll do, you ask what's best if everyone who reasons the way I do does the same thing — and that way you land on the good outcome.

Here's the bit I've no word for. The second way of thinking only works if I can draw a circle round the people I'm meant to be reasoning on behalf of. And I've got no idea where the edge of the circle goes. Round the whole room, and I'm treating some bloke who's never heard of the puzzle and doesn't care as my mirror image, which can't be right. Round just me, and the second way of thinking collapses back into the first one and the good outcome vanishes. My mum used to say "what if everyone did that?" when I dropped a wrapper in the street, and that's the same move — but she drew the circle by hand, she meant everyone who was thinking about dropping litter just then, not literally everyone. Same shape as "my one flight makes no difference" against "what if everyone did that": nobody ever says where the edge is, they just draw it wherever it suits them.

So does the circle have a name? I don't mean the puzzle, or the second way of thinking — I'm fairly sure those have names and I'm not fussed about them. I mean the circle: is there a name or a concept for the fact that you're forced to draw one and there's no principled place to put the edge? If there isn't, could you give me one and tell me where it stops — how is it different from just choosing a bad comparison group, or from the ordinary business of deciding which category to count yourself in when you look up a statistic? And what about when I'm a bit sharper, or a bit more patient, than the man next to me: is he inside the circle or not? It feels like the whole trick hangs on the answer and nobody ever gives it.
