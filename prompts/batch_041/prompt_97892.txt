OK so in my environmental history seminar we keep going round in circles about whether things like the Little Ice Age, or a big volcanic eruption, or a plague germ can count as 'historical actors', and my mate keeps saying the whole thing is basically a theatre: humans are the cast, and climate and volcanoes and germs are just the set — the scenery — and you don't credit the backdrop for the plot. He reckons that settles it: calling a volcano an 'actor' is a category error dressed up, and any history that does it is quietly smuggling in determinism. I'm not convinced the theatre comparison actually does the work he wants it to. Can you lay out the correspondence properly — what's meant to map onto the cast, the set, the script, the stage directions, the audience — then show where the mapping really breaks (a backdrop doesn't shift and kill people, a germ has no intentions but an awful lot of causal oomph, the 'set' sometimes rewrites the 'script', and so on), and then say whether his conclusion survives once those breaks are on the table, or whether he needs a better comparison. And please seperate out what's genuinely settled among historians and philosophers of history from what's a live dispute — don't palm off a contested line as the consensus.
