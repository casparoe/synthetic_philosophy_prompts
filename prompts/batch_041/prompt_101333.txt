I am applying for a one-year research fellowship in the philosophy of mind, and I need a two-page proposal built around a question I have only half worked out. Here is the seed. In Hofstadter's image of the careenium, a vast table is covered with countless tiny magnetic marbles that careen about, clump together, and break apart; at the coarse-grained level these clumps settle into recurring, quasi-stable formations — the simmballs — which Hofstadter treats as symbols, and which he pictures as mirroring and interacting with the world outside the table. The image is built to force a question about which level does the pushing: do the marbles move the simmballs, or do the simmballs move the marbles? Hofstadter's own answer is that both descriptions are true, but that the simmball description is the one that actually explains. I suspect that the dispute which has grown up around this image — and around downward causation more generally — is in part a collision between two questions that get run together: a metaphysical question about what actually causes what, and an explanatory question about which description of a single causal transaction is the one worth giving. I want to use the careenium as a controlled case to prise these apart, and to see whether a position can be built on which the simmball level is genuinely causally efficacious — makes a difference — without our having to posit any causal force over and above the marble-level ones, and without falling back on the claim that simmball talk is merely a convenient gloss.

Turn this into a proposal with: a statement of the question precise enough to have a determinate answer; an account of why the standard positions — those that treat higher-level causation as fully real and those that treat it as a description, a fiction, or an epiphenomenon — leave the question open rather than one of them already settling it; the method, setting out what I would actually do over the year, month by month, and why the careenium rather than the usual neural or economic examples is the right test case, including what happens if one weakens the stipulation that the marble-level dynamics are causally complete, and whether any counterfactuals about the system hold at the simmball level but not at the marble level (or the reverse) and what would follow if none did; what a successful outcome would look like, and what would count as the project failing — I would rather find out now if the question is likely to dissolve into a verbal dispute; and an honest assessment of whether the work fits a single year, naming the part most likely to overrun. Do not include a bibliography or citations; refer to positions and arguments as needed and I will supply the references myself. Please write the proposal in British English.
