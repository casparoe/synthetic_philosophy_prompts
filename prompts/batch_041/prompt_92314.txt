Write a joke whose humor depends on Max Weber's thesis about the Protestant ethic and the spirit of capitalism. The joke should be intelligible to someone who knows the thesis — the claim that a theology of predestination, by denying any causal role to human works in salvation while demanding constant assurance of election, fostered a this-worldly asceticism that rationalized labor and, unintentionally, produced the accumulation and the hardened economic order that eventually dispensed with the religious impulse that had driven it. The punchline should turn on the substance of that argument, ideally on one of two things: its ironic structure (a doctrine that denies the efficacy of works generating a disciplined, work-driven way of life that outlives the doctrine), or the methodological puzzle Weber leaves open (whether a system of values is a genuine cause of the associated conduct or merely its after-the-fact rationalization). You may set the joke wherever you like — a family firm, a modern startup, a productivity cult — but keep the punchline tied to the specifically Weberian content: a reader who did not know the thesis should find the joke puzzling rather than mildly amusing, and stripping out the predestination-and-assurance mechanism should leave nothing funny behind. Avoid the obvious workaholic-Calvinist and Protestant-guilt templates; the humor should not be available to someone who has never heard of the argument. A short setup and punchline is fine; it need not be long.
