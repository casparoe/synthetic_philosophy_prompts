In a reading group about the Darwin/Wallace simultaneity thing, someone waved off the whole priority dispute with "great minds think alike" — the implication being the discovery was coming anyway, so who got there first is a footnote. Someone else then said the idea was "in the air", which sounded like the same claim but I suspect isn't. I want the first sentence taken apart properly: what are the distinct precise claims "great minds think alike" could be making — about minds, about how the world was arranged, about credit — which are flat platitudes, which are substantive and actually defensible, which are just false, and which of them would really license "so the prioirty question doesn't matter"? One wrinkle I want handled: does the inevitability attach to the fact discovered, or only to the particular formulation/argument/evidence that ended up published, and does that distinction change the verdict on priority? And is "it was in the air" the same claim, a weaker version, or a different one entirely — plus, when someone tacks on the other half of the proverb ("and fools seldom differ"), which reading of the first half does that undercut? Lay it out as a menu with verdicts rather than settling on one reading. Also say how confident you are in each verdict and flag the one you're least sure about.
