I'm a novelist working on a book where a village decides by show of hands whether a stranger poisoned the well -- no court, just what people saw -- and the wrinkle is that each person's confidence in their own read goes up or down depending on how many of the people around them seem to agree. (Yes I know that's not how real deliberation works, it's a novel.)

Before I write the scene I want the spec, not the scene. What would an adequate theory of "when does a group's majority answer actually track the truth" have to get right? Not the standard line about a majority of better-than-chance independent voters being likely right -- I want the requirements list. The cases it has to handle (my confidence-feedback village, plus ordinary juries, plus a panel whose members genuinely differ in how competent they are, plus a case where there's no fact of the matter to be right about at all). The distinctions it can't collapse -- independence of votes vs. independence of minds, being a competent judge vs. getting it right this once, sincere vs. strategic voting. And any results it can't contradict, spelled out in words, no symbols please.

Tell me which requirement is doing the most work and which ones are just negotiable depending on what you want the theory for, and if some of them can't all be met at the same time, say so -- that's the part I'd most want to steal for the scene. Since I'll be leaning on this, say how confident you are in each claim and flag the one you're least sure about.
