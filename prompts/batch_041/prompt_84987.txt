Right, some context first. I'm a backend engineer, no formal philosophy training past one undergrad module I mostly slept through, but I've been sitting in on the philosophy of mind reading group at the university near me for about a year now, and they've made the mistake of giving me a slot next month to present something of my own.

Here's what I want to defend. Sphexishness has been misfiled. You know the wasp — paralyses a cricket, drags it to the burrow, goes inside to check the burrow, and if you nudge the cricket an inch while she's in there she comes back out, drags it to the entrance again, goes in to check again, and will keep doing that for as long as you keep nudging. Everyone tells that story as the punchline: she's a machine, we're not.

My claim is that this is the wrong shape. Sphexishness isn't a property of agents, it's a property of levels. Any finite agent is sphexish at some depth — the wasp at zero, me at three or four, a language model at whatever it happens to be. So the live question isn't whether something is sphexish or free, it's how far up a nudge has to travel before the routine actually changes, and free will worth wanting is just that depth being big enough. I've got a half-baked operational test for the depth, which I'm calling sphex depth: perturb the situation in a way that doesn't change what the agent ought to do, then count the levels the perturbation reaches before behaviour changes at all. Second test: run the agent in a near-copy of the situation and see if it can tell it's a near-copy.

The room I'm presenting to: mostly analytic philosophers of mind, three or four of them Dennett-flavoured compatibilists who think reason-responsiveness is all you need for free will; one proper hard determinist who thinks the whole debate is a mistake; a roboticist who builds subsumption-architecture robots and will probaly tell me I've just reinvented Brooks; someone who works on insect cognition and who I'm fairly sure will say the classic wasp story is a fable that field work has already killed; and one ML person who'll say I've described meta-RL with extra steps.

So: prep me for the Q&A.
