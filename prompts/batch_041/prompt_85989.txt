Ok so I'm putting together my own view on Rawls for a seminar and before I present I want a consistency check, not a summary of the theory — I've done the reading.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. The difference principle is the right principle for the basic structure: inequalities are just only if they work to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged.
2. What justifies it is the original position. Parties who don't know their own place, their talents or their conception of the good, who are mutually disinterested and can't put probabilities on who they'll turn out to be, would go for maximin instead of gambling.
3. What makes that stable rather than just a rational bet is reciprocity — the worst off have to be able to affirm the arrangement and carry the strains of commitment, and that only gets off the ground if they count as participants in the cooperative scheme in the first place.
4. This isnt just about nation-states. The same reasoning goes through globally, so there's a global difference principle and the global rich owe the global poor whatever maximally benefits them.
5. People with severe cognitive disabilities are full subjects of justice with the same kind of claim as anyone else, not a leftover to be handled by 'practical' theory after the principles are already fixed.
6. Future generations too — they should be behind the veil, and maximin should cover them.
7. I'm with Rawls that this is a political conception and not a comprehensive doctrine; the point isn't to get everyone to agree about the meaning of life.
8. Two verdicts I'm confident about: if making the better off worse doesn't help the worst off, that's just levelling down and it isn't required, even if it makes things more equal; and a doctor who emigrates from a poor country to a rich one for a better life isn't doing anything wrong, even though it plainly leaves the worst off worse off than they'd otherwise be.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and how hard? Order them by how serious the conflict is — I don't want a list where everything gets flagged as 'in tension'. Where something has to give, tell me the cheapest repair and what it costs me. And be explicit about which points are settled among people who work on Rawls and which are live disagreements; dont let a contested reading come out sounding like the standard view.
