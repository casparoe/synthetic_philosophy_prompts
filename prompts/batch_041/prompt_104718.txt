My friend is fine with all that — he just steelmanned my own position into this, and now I'm stuck.

(1) No statement is confirmed or disconfirmed on its own. What meets experience is the whole web together. I'm not going to defend this one, I think it's just right.
(2) So for any statement, and for anything experience throws at it, there's some adjustment elsewhere in the web that lets you keep it. Quine's own line here is that no statement is immune to revision, which I read as: there's always a move available.
(3) So experience never forces you to drop any particular statement. Whatever comes in, you keep the statement and pay somewhere else. And that's meant to be the whole death of the analytic/synthetic distinction — "No bachelor is married" isn't a different kind of sentence from one about brick houses on Elm Street, it's just one we'd hate alot more to give up.
(4) If that's right, then a physicist who saves his theory by blaming the detector and a crank who saves his theory by blaming the conspiracy are doing the same thing. Both are adjusting elsewhere in the web.
(5) So there's no epistemic difference between doing science and rationalizing, only a difference in which adjustments the community will put up with. Epistemology of science collapses into sociology of science.
(6) And the move eats its own tail: if any statement can be kept come what may, then holism can be kept come what may too, so if the argument works I've got no reason to accept it either.

I'd ditch (5) in a heartbeat if I could, but I can't seperate (2) from (5) without stopping at (3), and (3) reads to me like (2) said slower. My prior is that self-application arguments are a cheap trick, so if the real cut is at (6) tell me why I'm wrong about that. Which step do I cut, and what does cutting it cost me?
