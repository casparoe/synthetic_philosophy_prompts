I'm in a slightly odd position: I did a CS undergrad, jumped to philosophy for a masters, and now I'm somewhere I more or less get to invent my own thesis area, and I keep circling back to the frame problem. The trouble is that everything I find is either the 1980s version (McCarthy and Hayes style formalizations, the old non-monotonic-logic puzzles, Dennett's robot that can't stop drawing irrelevant conclusions about the colour of the walls) or the opposite -- a hand-wave that predictive processing, or just scaling things up, has dissolved the whole thing. Meanwhile friends of mine who work on LLM agents say the frame problem is alive and well and is basically the retrieval/context problem, which I find either very deep or completly deflating depending on the day.

What I actually want from you is an agenda, not a survey, and definately not a reading list -- I can find the literature myself. Suppose someone had two or three years and a modest budget to push this forward on the conceptual side only (no new experiments, no benchmarks, no new formalisms unless the formalism is doing real philosophical work). Which open questions would you put at the top, and in what order? I'm after the reasoning more than the list: which questions are upstream of which -- which ones, if settled, would make the others much easier or much less interesting -- which are genuinely tractable for a philosopher rather than an engineer, which look deep but are really engineering problems in disguise, and which currently fashionable framings you'd quietly drop. And tell me what would move something up or down your list: a result, an argument, a change in how the AI systems we care about actually work.

One thing I want you to take seriously, and I'll be annoyed if you just nod along: a friend in my group insists that the frame problem just IS the problem of relevance, and that relevance can't be specified from inside the system at all -- you need a body, a world, a practice, some background that was never represented in the first place. I genuinely can't tell whether that's a deep insight or a way of refusing to do the work. And if the way I've set this up is confused -- if "the frame problem" is now three or four unrelated problems wearing one name, or if the live version has migrated somewhere I haven't thought to look -- say so plainly rather than humouring the framing. Keep the whole thing under 800 words.
