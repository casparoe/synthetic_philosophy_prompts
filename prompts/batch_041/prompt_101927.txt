The standard gloss is "so utilitarianism follows from very weak assumptions." My essay says the opposite: the load-bearing assumption is the one that gets the least scrutiny, namely that society (or a social planner, or the impartial observer) has a well-defined preference ordering over lotteries that satisfies the same axioms an individual's does. So the theorem, at best, shows: *if* you already accept that social evaluation must look like the expected utility of a single agent, *then* you get weighted utilitarianism. I then try to extend this to the parallel impartial-observer argument, where I say the equiprobability assumption (the observer is equally likely to be any of the people) is doing the same agent-ifying work to get the weights equal, and that's why I think the two arguments share a hidden premise.
