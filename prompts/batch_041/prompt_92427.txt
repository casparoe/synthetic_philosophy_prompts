I have been reading rationalist and LessWrong-adjacent blogs for years, and the debates that frustrate me most are the ones that run for a decade because the participants never separate a real disagreement from a merely verbal one. So I would like you to act as a philosopher of metaphysics specializing in mereology and material constitution, think carefully before you commit anything to the page, and then help me do that separation properly.

The Special Composition Question asks under what conditions some things compose a further thing. Two well-defended answers seem to talk past each other. The universalist holds that any plurality of things whatsoever has a fusion, so composition is unrestricted and automatic. The organicist (the answer often associated with van Inwagen) holds that some things compose a further thing exactly when their activities together constitute a life, so that the only composite objects are living organisms and there is no object composed of a rock and a tree.

Draft the joint statement that a committed universalist and a committed organicist could both sign after a genuine adversarial collaboration. I want four things. First, a numbered list of the claims both would accept -- about simples, about what the question is even asking, about which cases are uncontroversial, and about what would count as an argument against each view; make these substantive rather than vacuous. Second, the precise residual disagreements, each phrased in wording that the side holding it would endorse. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. Fourth, for each crux, a test case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Two test cases I want both sides forced to confront, because I suspect they press on the neatness of the life criterion: colonial organisms such as the siphonophore (the Portuguese man o' war, whose specialized zooids are clones that together function as a single individual) and aggregative slime molds (whose solitary amoebae assemble into one migrating slug when starved). Finally, tell me whether you judge any part of the dispute to be merely verbal -- for instance, a disagreement about whether the word object is being used univocally -- and if so, which part.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Keep the whole response to no more than 700 words.
