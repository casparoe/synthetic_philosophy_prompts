I keep seeing people say a safe-ish agent should be "low impact" -- it shouldn't disturb the world more than its task requires, shouldn't rack up side effects. But "side effect" only seems to mean anything relative to an intended effect and to some baseline of what would've happened anyway, and both of those look value-laden. The cleverer version I've run into tries to define impact without naming any good outcome at all: score an action by how much it shrinks the set of futures the agent can still steer into, measured against a do-nothing baseline -- so all you need is a metric over futures, not a utility function. Please explain whether that actually buys you a value-free notion of side effects, or whether the value has just been relocated into the baseline and into the choice of metric (e.g. does nudging someone's opinion count as much as moving a rock around?). And explain why a bare physical description of the world seems to contain no side effects at all -- "side effect" looks like it needs a notion of what the action was for, which drags intention back in. If the honest answer is "you can't define low impact without something close to a full value spec," tell me what that does and doesn't doom in the low-impact reseach program. Say what would change your mind and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
