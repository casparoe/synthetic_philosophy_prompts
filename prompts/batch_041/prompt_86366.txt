I am writing the final exam for a graduate seminar on the ethics and safety of advanced AI, cross-listed with philosophy of technology. It is a closed-book, 75-minute in-class exam and the students have no internet access, so the question must be self-contained: everything the student needs to reason with has to sit in the stem.

I want one long essay question, worth 20 marks, on the crux below, together with a model answer and a full marking scheme.

The crux. Low-impact or side-effect-avoiding agency is proposed as a safety constraint that can be imposed without writing down the agent's true objective; the whole appeal of penalizing side effects is that the constraint is supposed to be neutral about what the agent is ultimately for. The question I want students to face is whether that is possible: whether "avoid side effects" can be defined without a full specification of value, or whether the constraint must quietly import one. I am not interested in the trivial observation that any constraint is value-laden in some sense. I am interested in whether it needs a full specification of value, and in where exactly the residue of value sits.

Because the students cannot look anything up, supply the technical material in the stem as three functionally stated definitions, with no attributions, dates, or paper titles — I do not want the exam or the model answer to turn on who said what:

(i) an impact measure that scores an action by how much it shrinks the set of world-states reachable within a fixed horizon, relative to the set reachable under a default or inaction policy;

(ii) a measure that scores an action by how much it reduces the agent's ability to attain objectives drawn from a broad reference distribution over objectives;

(iii) a definition on which a side effect is a change to the environment that the agent's own objective does not value but that a human evaluator would.

Then require three parts, in this order, with the marks as given.

(a) [5 marks] Reconstruct the strongest argument that "avoid side effects" cannot be defined without a full specification of value. Say which premise carries the weight — that no baseline is privileged without a value judgment, that any purely formal proxy is satisfiable by degenerate policies, or something else — and distinguish the claim that the constraint requires some value-laden commitment from the claim that it requires a full specification of value. Say which of the two the argument actually establishes.

(b) [10 marks] Evaluate the three definitions against that argument. For each, locate precisely where value enters and whether it can be quarantined. Include at least one concrete failure mode in which an agent minimizing the measure does something a human would find unacceptable, and at least one in which the measure is vacuous rather than mistaken. Then say whether the three differ in kind or only in where they put the same difficulty.

(c) [5 marks] Answer the original question — can "avoid side effects" be defined without a full specification of value? — and state what would have to be true for your answer to be wrong. If your answer is that it can be done only weakly, specify the residual burden and say why it falls short of a full specification of value.

For the model answer: roughly 700 words of continuous prose, written the way a first-class candidate would write under time pressure, signposting each move so the examiner can see it, and taking a definite position in (c) rather than hedging. No names of authors, papers, or research programmes, either in the model answer or in the exam question itself; the argument has to stand without them.

For the marking scheme: five must-hit points, with the marks each is worth, summing to 20; for each point, one line on the mistake that typically costs candidates the mark; and a short closing statement of what separates an A answer from a B answer, in terms of specific moves rather than general quality.
