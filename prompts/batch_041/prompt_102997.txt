A friend who knows I have been reading philosophy of mind lent me a chapter from a popular cognitive-science book that surveys the debate over cognitive penetration. I do not trust my own grasp of the literature well enough to know how much of it to take seriously, so I would like an audit. The chapter's central passage runs roughly as follows: "For most of the twentieth century, psychologists assumed that perception is a fixed, bottom-up process, sealed off from what we believe and want. The past thirty years have demolished that picture. We now know that perception is cognitively penetrable: our beliefs, desires, and expectations literally alter what we see. Hungry people see food-related words more quickly; ambiguous figures flip depending on what we are told to expect; partisans literally see the same video as showing very different things. Because what we perceive is soaked in what we already think, there is no neutral observation basis, and scientific disputes can never be settled by the evidence alone — the old ideal of a theory-neutral datum is finished. The author adds that this vindicates the predictive-processing view, according to which perception is a kind of controlled hallucination, and that it refutes the modular picture of the mind that Fodor defended."

I am inclined to think the chapter is broadly on the right track, which is exactly why I want to be careful. Sentence by sentence, which of these claims would a philosopher who works on cognitive penetration endorse, which are popular simplifications, and which are straightforwardly mistaken? I am especially interested in whether the empirical examples actually support the strong thesis the author draws from them, and whether the step from "some perception is influenced by cognition" to "there is no neutral observation basis" is legitimate. Finally, the author treats the question of whether a given effect should count as perception or as judgment as a merely verbal dispute that can be set aside. Is that fair, or does that distinction do real work in the debate? Please be concrete about where the summary goes wrong rather than merely gesturing that matters are complicated.
