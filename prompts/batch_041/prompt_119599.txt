You're a philosopher of science who works on dispositions and tendencies, so I'm hoping you can help me name and carve something up. Amatuer reader here, no formal training, so forgive the loose talk.

Here's the pattern that keeps bugging me. Something is supposed to have a tendency -- a kid who's allergic to peanuts, a bridge that's been judged unsound, a compound that's unstable -- and the way we'd normally check is to run the trigger: feed the kid peanuts, drive trucks over the bridge, warm the compound up. But in the cases I keep hitting, the trigger never gets a chance to run, and not because some outside thing steps in to stop it. It's because the tendency itself, or people's belief in the tendency, keeps clearing the trigger out of the way. The kid never goes near peanuts, and the reason he never goes near them is the allergy. The bridge is closed, so nothing heavy ever rolls over it. The compound is only ever handled cold, by people who know exactly what it does when it warms up. And in some of these cases we point to the avoidance itself as evidence that the tendency is real, which feels like it should be circular but doesn't quite feel wrong either.

Is there a name for this? And is it really the same thing as the ordinary case where some external interferer blocks a thing from showing what it can do, or is it importantly different? I'm not after a label so much as the carving: is the tendency still there, what exactly separates the case where the blocker is downstream of the tendency from the case where the blocker is downstream of everyone believing in the tendency, and does the avoidance count as evidence for the attribution or does it just make the attribution empty? Brainstorm the nearby ideas before you commit to an answer. And be explicit about which parts of this are settled among people who study this kind of thing and which are genuinely contested -- don't hand me one camp's answer dressed up as the consensus.
