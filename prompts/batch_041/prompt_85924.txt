Where I've landed:

1. The only thing that's bad in itself is suffering. Pleasure isn't a good in its own right — it's more like the relief of a lack. Minimizing suffering is the whole of the story.

2. But I don't want the crazy version of that, so I've put a floor in, which I call the "noise floor," because in the lab that's the level below which you can't tell signal from nothing. Below the noise floor, suffering is absolutely protected: no amount of anyone's pleasure, however enormous, justifies adding even a sliver of suffering down there. Above the floor, ordinary weighing is fine — you can trade suffering off against pleasure like normal.

3. I'm still a scientist underneath, so I think pleasure and pain are the same kind of thing with opposite signs. It's all just valence, one scale in principle; you could put a number on any experience and add it up.

4. And I think there's a fact of the matter in every comparison. Give me any two possible outcomes and either one is better than the other or they're equal. I don't believe in incomparability — I don't think two things can be genuinely on a par, neither better nor worse. That always struck me as a philosopher's way of saying they hadn't measured it yet.

5. Verdict on a case: I would not press the button that painlessly ends all sentient life on earth, not even to guarantee that nobody ever suffers again. That seems obviously wrong to me.

6. Another verdict: I'm glad my kids exist, I think having them was fine, and I don't think it's wrong to bring more people into the world who'll have decent lives — even though every one of those lives will contain some suffering.
