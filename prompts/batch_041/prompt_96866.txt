For a seminar on scientific realism I want the criteria fixed before we argue about candidates. Write out the desiderata that any version of structural realism — any account that locates what survives theory change in structure rather than in the entities a theory posits — must satisfy to count as an adequate account of the sciences, where the account has to do justice at once to the manifest success of our theories and to the historical record of their being overturned. For each requirement, give the case, argument, or historical episode that motivates it, and say plainly what kind of requirement it is: a constraint that binds any such account, or a commitment of one camp that a rival could reasonably reject.

Treat one further demand as binding, and work out its consequences rather than waving it away as an objection. Whatever is claimed to be carried across theory change must be something that not every domain of the right size can be given for free. If the preserved content is identified with a bare structure — relations with no further constraints — then, unless the account adds something that restricts which domains can bear that structure, any collection of objects of the appropriate cardinality can be assigned it, and the claim that the structure is preserved comes out true of nearly anything and so says nearly nothing. State what an account has to add in order to escape this, and whether the addition is compatible with the other requirements on your list.

Then do three things. Flag any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and say what the conflict forces one to give up. Identify the single requirement that eliminates the most candidate positions. And briefly — only briefly — say how the leading structuralist options fare against the list.

Be explicit about your own footing throughout. Name the requirement you are least confident is a genuine desideratum, and say what argument or evidence would lead you to strike it, and whether striking it would dissolve the conflict you identified. End with a one-sentence summary, then a final line of the form \answer{...} naming the one requirement you would give up first if the list proved over-full.
