I am assembling a sorting instrument for a graduate seminar on whether patriotism is a virtue or a vice, and I want it built as a taxonomy of positions rather than as a survey of named authors. Please construct it in two stages.

First, lay out the logical space as a decision tree whose internal nodes are the questions that genuinely separate positions, each phrased so that a respondent can answer it without already knowing which position the answer leads to. I want the spine to run through at least the following commitments, though you should add, drop, or reorder them if the logical space demands it: (a) whether patriotic attachment is a species of loyalty, and whether loyalty is a virtue at all; (b) whether patriotism necessarily involves a belief in one's nation's superiority, or whether a moderate form can be defined that does not; (c) what the object of attachment is — a people, a territory, a political order or constitution, a set of ideals, or an unfinished project; (d) whether any special obligations to compatriots can be justified, and if so by associative or role obligations, by fair play and reciprocity for benefits received, by gratitude, or not at all; (e) whether the virtue is purely motivational or must also issue in effective action; and (f) whether the attitude is unconditional or is held subject to conditions the nation can forfeit.

Then locate the actual positions as leaves: at minimum robust patriotism, moderate or conditional patriotism, constitutional patriotism, the cosmopolitan rejection of patriotic partiality, patriotism as loyalty to ideals (including the variant on which loyal dissent is the truest patriotism), and the outright vice view. Give each leaf a single sentence naming its most awkward commitment. Where the tree leaves a combination of answers unoccupied, mark it and diagnose briefly whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and say why.

Two constraints. First, do not lean on a catalogue of authors or works — I care about the positions, not the bibliography, so name a thinker only if a position is unintelligible without its label. Second, build the diagnostic questions from concrete cases rather than abstractions: a person who loves her country's landscape, language, and food but regards its government as an enemy; an emigrant who retains patriotic feeling for the country she left; someone who criticizes her country constantly and calls that criticism the truest patriotism; a soldier who obeys an unjust war out of loyalty; a naturalized citizen who feels the pull of two nations at once. Use cases of this kind to force the branches, aim for roughly eight to twelve questions, and tell me which case is the sharpest discriminator for each split.
