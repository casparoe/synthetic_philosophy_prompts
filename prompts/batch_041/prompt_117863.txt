I serve as the staff analyst to the public safety transition committee of a mid-sized city council. In two weeks the committee votes on an ordinance creating a Department of Community Safety and transferring two functions out of the police department: response to non-criminal mental-health and substance crises, and low-speed traffic enforcement. The new department would be unarmed, but its responders would hold statutory authority to detain a person involuntarily for up to two hours pending clinical assessment. Two features have split the committee. The department would be staffed largely by a private contractor under a renewable city contract, with the city retaining only an audit right. And the ordinance is silent on what a responder may do when a detainee physically resists; the police union reads the silence as forbidding force of any kind, while the sponsor reads it as leaving the question to the department's own policy.

Draft the written opinion I will circulate before the vote. Recommend a specific course of action, name the amendments I should move if I recommend passage, and say what the committee should do at the vote if those amendments fail. Reason from the question sitting underneath the ordinance rather than from any camp's slogans: what it is about a use of force, including a detention, that makes it legitimate; whether that legitimacy can be delegated to a contractor; and whether the authority in question belongs to the police department, to the state, or to the community. The committee contains three recognizable positions — amend and pass; strip the transfer and fund reform inside the department instead; reject the ordinance and begin planning to replace the department's core functions with non-police institutions entirely — and I need the strongest version of whichever position you reject, not the version its critics prefer to attack. Be concrete about what the ordinance should say about resistance, and about what a two-hour detention power does to the rest of the design. I don't need references or a literature review; I need a decision I can defend at the table.

State how confident you are in the recommendation and in the reasoning supporting it, and identify the single point in the memo you are least sure of, along with what would change your mind.
