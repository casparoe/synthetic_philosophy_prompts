For several years now I have been drawn into a quarrel that properly belongs to my colleagues in the history faculty, and I have come to think that the reason it never resolves is philosophical. I am a philosopher by training, and the controversy that still attaches to Fritz Fischer's name — the long argument over whether Germany bears the primary responsibility for the outbreak of the First World War — has come to seem to me a dispute in which the participants are not, despite all appearances, disagreeing about the facts. My half-formed idea is this: the question 'who started it' is not a question about events at all but about counterfactual baselines. To say that a state started a war is to say that, holding some background course of events fixed as the normal or expected one, the war would not have occurred but for that state's action; and because historians tacitly fix different backgrounds — the balance of power, the alliance system, the arms race, the domestic pressures on each cabinet — they reach different verdicts while agreeing on the chronology. If that is right, then the dispute is not a failure of historical method but a disguised and undefended dispute about which counterfactual baseline it is appropriate to hold fixed, and about whether there is any principled way of selecting one.

I should like you to turn this into a short research proposal. I am applying for a small internal grant that would buy me a single term free of teaching, and the application is capped at 1,500 words. What I need from you is: a precise statement of the question, sharp enough that it has a determinate answer; an account of why the existing positions on responsibility for the war leave it open, since on my reading the participants simply assume a baseline rather than argue for one; the method — what I would actually do with the term, week by week, and why conceptual work of this kind is the right approach rather than, say, more archival history; a description of what a genuine result would look like and of what would count as the project failing; and an honest assessment of whether this is really a term's work or whether I am overreaching. One further thing I want you to take seriously, because it is the part that interests me most: if the choice of baseline cannot be made principled, that is itself a result. It would mean that 'who started it' is not a well-posed historical question, and that the ethical and political uses to which such verdicts are put — reparations, national memory, the whole vocabulary of guilt and innocence — rest on something considerably shakier than those who invoke them suppose. Do not trouble yourself with citations or a reading list; I know the literature well enough and shall supply the references myself. Please write in British English.
