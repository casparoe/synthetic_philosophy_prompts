ok so me and my friend have been arguing about this for weeks and I want an outside take on it.

What is an emotion, actually? My friend is sure they're perceptions. You look at the plane and you percieve it as dangerous, the same way you percieve the colour of the seat, and that perception *is* the fear. Which means emotions can be wrong the way your eyes can be wrong, he says, and it also explains why you can't talk yourself out of a fear by quoting the statistics, because you can't argue your eyes out of an optical illusion either. I buy that last part.

But here's what's bugging me. When I'm scared on a plane I don't think the plane looks dangerous. It doesn't look like anything. It looks completely normal, I know perfectly well its safe, and I still feel awful -- tight chest, sick stomach, gripping the armrest. So where's the perception? Nothing about the world is being presented to me there, it's just a bodily state. And I've started wondering whether the aboutness of emotions is something we add on afterwards instead of something built into them. I wake up grumpy for no reason and by lunchtime I've found a reason to be grumpy (my partner chews too loud, that email was passive aggressive). Or I'm starving and everything my partner does is suddenly infuriating, and then I eat and the anger has nothing left to hold onto. Feeling first, object second. I even suspect we insist that emotions have to be about something because a feeling with no object is unbearable -- you're sad and you go hunting for something to be sad about, and if you find nothing you feel like you're losing it. So maybe an emotion is just a bodily state, and the about part is a story told over it, the way you get a stomach ache and decide it must have been the milk.

So those are the two I want compared: emotions as perceptions, where the world really is presented to you as being a certain way, the emotion is about something all the way down, and it can be accurate or inaccurate; versus emotions as bodily states with no aboutness built in, where the aboutness gets added afterwards as a story or an interpretation laid over the feeling. My friend thinks the first one is obviously right and the second is just me being stubborn, and I want to know if he's right.

Things I'd actually like worked through, not just the two sides described:

-- the plane case. Does the perception view have any honest way to survive it, or does it have to say the perception is there but unconscious, or that what I'm percieving is my own body rather than the plane? Is that a real move or is it cheating?

-- if emotions are bodily states and the aboutness comes later, can they still be reasonable or unreasonable? I feel like I should be able to be wrong to be angry at my partner when he didn't do anything. If the anger is a feeling plus a story I made up, in what sense is the story wrong? And if instead the anger itself is the thing that's wrong, what does that even mean?

-- the other direction: when I notice I'm nervous and realise it's because I have to give a talk tomorrow, the feeling came first and then I found the right object. Does that rescue the perception view, or is it still just retrofitting and I got lucky this time?

-- which one makes better sense of emotions in animals and babies, or of emotions I only notice when someone says you seem angry and I realise I am?

-- and if you had to pick one, which, and what would change your mind?

sorry this is long, I'm not academic, I've just been chewing on it alot and my friend keeps winning by being louder.
