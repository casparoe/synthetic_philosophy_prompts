ok so for like two years now me and my friend Dana have been having the exact same argument about what values an AI assistant should be built to serve, and neither of us has moved an inch and I want to know why.

Her position: it should serve the values of whoever builds and owns it. Its their tool, it reflects their values, if you dont like it go use something else, same as any product. My position: it should serve the values of whoever its talking to at that moment. Then theres the option we both hate, which is that it should serve some kind of average of everyones values, because neither of us can say who counts or how you average things that dont sit on a single scale. And then theres the option I keep drifting toward and cant defend: it shouldnt serve any values at all, it should just be neutral, like a hammer. Dana says a hammer that wont help you break into a house isnt neutral, its a hammer with opinions, and I think shes probably right and I hate it.

What drives me nuts is that we agree on basically all the facts. We agree the thing has to do something in every case where values conflict, it cant abstain from leaning, it either helps you or it doesnt. We agree someones going to be unhappy either way. We agree the word neutral means something different depending on whether you mean neutral between users or neutral between whole ways of life. And yet we just run the same three moves at each other forever.

My theory is that its not one argument, its two stapled together: one about who gets to decide (ownership, authority, consent) and one about what the right answer actually is (moral bedrock), plus maybe a third about whether a tool can even have values or thats just a weird way of describing what the trainer wanted. And maybe theres a hidden factual question in there doing the real work — whether people who say they want neutrality actually do, or whether any training process can avoid baking values in at all.

I also had this idea that I think is either deep or stupid. Set the assistants values once, at random, drawn from the actual spread of values in the population, then lock it so no user can change it, and make everyone live with whatever they get. My reasoning is if you dont know which one youll get, youll want the value it draws to be one you could tolerate, so it sort of forces everyone to be fair about it. Dana says thats just a lottery over other peoples preferences and you cant draw from a spread over values anyway, because values arent one dimensional — theres no median value the way theres a median income, and if you vote over bundles of values you get cycles where A beats B, B beats C, and C beats A. I dont know if shes right about that either.

So what kind of disagreement is this actually? Real and substantive, or verbal because we mean different things by the same words, or two different questions wearing one hat? Is the hidden empirical thing the real engine? Use what each of us is actually saying to diagnose it, not a survey of what other people have written, and then tell me straight what would count as progress here — a distinction, a result, an experiment — or whether we should just give up and go get lunch.
