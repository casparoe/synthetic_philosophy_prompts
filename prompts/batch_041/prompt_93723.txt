Write an essay on a controversy in Pascal's political thought. In the Pensées, Pascal pairs justice with force: justice without force is powerless, force without justice is tyrannical, and the two must therefore be joined. Yet he also says that we cannot make what is just strong, and that what has in fact been done is to make what is strong just, with custom rather than any insight into desert grounding the authority of law. He adds a three-tier picture: the people take established power to be just, those who think themselves clever see through this and despise it, and the wise honor established power again, though for reasons the people would not recognize.

Does this leave Pascal with a coherent standard of justice, or does it collapse into a refined version of the doctrine that might makes right? Address three points in particular. First, is the wise man's endorsement of the established order the same belief the people hold, held for different reasons, or a different belief altogether? Second, does Pascal's theology — fallen human nature, and the subordination of the political order to the order of charity — supply a standard of justice independent of force, and can that standard do any work within politics? Third, is the claim that law rests on custom self-undermining when Pascal himself, as a Christian critic, exposes the vanity of earthly justice?

Weigh the strongest case for reading Pascal as a political realist against the strongest case for reading him as an Augustinian who describes the fallen world without endorsing it. Reach a conclusion and defend it; do not end on a non-committal note.
