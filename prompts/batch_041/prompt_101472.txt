Explain what work the notion of common knowledge is doing in the game-theoretic treatment of rationality. Why are iterated elimination of dominated strategies and backward induction standardly said to require not merely that each player is rational, nor merely that each knows the others are rational, but that rationality be common knowledge in the full infinite-hierarchy sense? Then address a circularity I find troubling. If "rational" means "plays a best response given one's beliefs," and those beliefs concern what other rational players will do, the content of "everyone is rational" seems to depend on the very solution the assumption is meant to license. So is common knowledge of rationality a substantive constraint on the players, or a restatement of the equilibrium in epistemic dress? Distinguish these, and say what the infinite regress accomplishes that a fixed-point formulation -- each player rational given a shared conjecture about play -- would not. Finally, does weakening common knowledge to mere mutual knowledge of rationality change any prediction, or only the epistemic gloss we place on it?
