Okay so this is a wierd one and I'm not even sure it's a philosophy question but a couple of my friends insist it is, so here goes. I'm a backend engineer (mostly Go, some ML on the side) and for the last two summers I've been doing volunteer weekends with a small group that runs a trail-camera project in the state forest about forty minutes from me. It started as a nerd thing — I wrote the pipeline that tags the footage — and then last winter I trained a classifier that flags clips where an animal looks sick or hurt: limping, mange, really thin, that kind of thing. Which was fine until it actually worked. Now I have numbers, and the numbers are grim. In one valley, something like a third of the adult deer clips show signs of a skin parasite, and there's a whole category of clips of fawns that just don't make it to fall. That's a lot of suffering, and nobody caused it, which is the part that's messing with me.

Anyway, the group is split and I'm supposed to present the data at our meeting in three weeks, and I think they want a recommendation from me, which I did not sign up for. One faction wants to actually do stuff: feeding stations through the hard months, some kind of treatment for the mange, and the big fight — paying a trapper to take maybe fifteen coyotes out of that one valley over two winters, because our own footage says the fawn mortality down there is basically all predation. The other faction, led by the guy who founded the group and who I honestly respect more than anyone I've met doing this, says we should touch none of it. His line is that we watch and we document, and the second you start feeding and trapping you're not conserving anything, you're running a zoo with extra steps, and you'll wreck the exact thing you love. Then he says the thing I cant get out of my head: the animals aren't ours, and a deer dying of parasites in the woods is not a moral emergency the way a dog dying in someone's front yard is, because there's nobody responsible for it. He also claims the state agency will yank our research permit the minute we start trapping, so we'd be trading the one thing we're genuinely good at — knowing what's actually going on out there — for one trapping season.

My gut says he's drawing a line that doesn't survive contact with the spreadsheet: suffering is suffering, and now I'm the guy who knows about it, which feels like it ought to count for something. But I also can't shake the worry that if we start managing that valley we end up with a feedlot full of habituated deer, and that "we made it better" is just the story we tell ourselves afterwards. Two things keep coming up in our group chat that I have no answer for. The grad student says we should only fix problems we caused, and then points out the mange outbreak is probably worse because of the mild winters and the runoff from the new subdivision, so maybe we did cause some of it. And the retired bio teacher says if we knock down the coyotes we'll just end up with more deer than the winter can feed, so the suffering doesn't go away, it only changes address. What's actually at stake in this fight — I feel like there's a real argument on both sides and I don't have the words for it — and how should I decide? And if you think we should intervene, how do I answer the founder, because his point about nobody being responsible is the one with the most pull on me and I genuinely don't have a comeback.
