I am writing a paper for a comparative politics seminar on whether Sweden or Norway is the more liberal of the two, and I have reached the point where I no longer trust the question as I have been asking it. Before I compare anything, I want the specification: the requirements that any account must meet if it is to settle the question — or to establish that the question cannot be settled as posed. Please write that specification as a standalone section of roughly 1,200 words, in prose, with a short table if it helps. No references; I shall supply those.

Set the purpose first. The standard of adequacy is relative to what the account is for, and mine is a seminar paper whose readers will accept a ranking only if it survives the obvious objections, and will accept a verdict that there is no determinate ranking only if the reason is conceptual rather than merely prudential. So state at the outset what form an answer could take — a full ranking, a partial order, a conditional ranking under declared weights, or a demonstration that the question equivocates between distinct senses of 'liberal' — and what each of these would commit me to.

Then the requirements themselves. For each, give the consideration or case that motivates it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. I expect the list to cover at least the following, though I would rather you reorganised it around your own view of what matters than ticked it off:

1. Cases the account must get right, at the level of the polity rather than only the statute book — including arrangements that sit awkwardly on a simple more/less liberal axis, such as a state retail monopoly on alcohol, the criminalisation of purchasing sexual services, or an immigration regime that is generous in one period and restrictive in the next.

2. Distinctions it must not collapse: between liberalism as a property of a state's institutions, of its inhabitants' attitudes, and of its public culture; between the level of liberalisation and its trajectory, since a ranking that holds for one baseline year may reverse for another; and between the liberalism enjoyed by citizens and by denizens, where resident non-citizens' local franchise and social entitlements may separate the two countries more sharply than anything about the majority's opinions.

3. Structural constraints on the comparison itself: the unit of comparison (state, region, person), measurement invariance across the two cases, and the aggregation rule, given that any weighting of dimensions encodes a contested conception rather than neutral arithmetic.

4. Whatever the account must remain consistent with: the readings on which Nordic welfare arrangements are a vehicle for individual autonomy rather than a departure from it, and the observable gaps between policy and opinion running in both directions.

Finally, say which single requirement does most to exclude the popular answers (economic-freedom indices, personal-freedom indices, values surveys, readings of the two constitutions), and identify any subset of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. If a demand for a determinate overall ranking is inconsistent with one of the distinctions in (2), that is the result I most want, and I would rather see it stated plainly than have the list quietly trimmed to fit. State the specification for Sweden and Norway, but do not let it collapse if the pair were changed to Canada and the United States.
