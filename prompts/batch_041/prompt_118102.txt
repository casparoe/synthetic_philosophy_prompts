I keep running into the same move in popular history writing and I cant find a name for it. Something big happens in the natural world -- a volcano goes off and the next summer is cold and the harvest fails, or a disease arrives -- and pretty soon it's being credited with something huge downstream: a famine, a wave of riots, a revolution, the end of a whole labour system. Then you check other places that got the same volcano or the same plague, and either nothing happened there or the opposite happened. The plague is supposed to have killed off serfdom in one half of Europe, except it reached the other half too, and there serfdom got tighter. The rescue move is always the same: the place that came apart was already fragile, stretched, on the brink, so the natural event was only the spark, the last straw. Fine as far as it goes, but then the fragility is carrying the explanation, and nobody mentions it until after we know how things turned out -- had the place held together we'd of called it resilient and told that story instead. So one event ends up explaining both outcomes, and the volcano gets top billing while the actual work is done by a background condition that only gets named in hindsight. A few of these writers go further and call the germ or the climate an actor in the story, and I honestly can't tell whether that's a metaphor or a claim. Is there an established name for any of this? I'm fairly sure it isn't just correlation isn't causation, and it isn't environmental determinism, because the careful ones explicitly deny that the outcome was inevitable. If there's a term of art for the trigger/background split, or for the way this particular package goes unfalsifiable, great, but what I actually want is the carving: what separates the volcano caused the collapse from the volcano was a condition the collapse came through, and does that line survive if you press on the counterfactuals? Dont just hand me a label.
