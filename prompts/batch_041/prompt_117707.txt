Novelist here, not a philosopher, so be gentle. I'm writing a scene where two friends make a deal and I cant stop turning the shape of it over. One of them thinks the worst thing happening in the world is factory farming; the other thinks it's tenants getting priced out of their neighbourhood. Each thinks the other's obsession is a distraction. They can't agree on anything, so they trade instead: she gives up her Saturdays canvassing for a rent cap she doesn't care about, he gives a chunk of his salary to a charity that buys out farms. Neither has moved an inch on what actually matters to them. Each gave up something small to get something big. And both feel grubby about it afterwards, because each has now put real effort behind a cause they think does harm.

Is there a name for this? It isn't compromise the way people usually mean it -- nobody split the difference, nobody softened their line on their own issue. It also isn't quite the thing where two politicians swap votes on unrelated bills, though I can see it's close, and if it is different I want to know exactly how. What I'm really after is the moral shape of it: it's a trade in wrongs, and it looks both obviously good (both parties are better off by their own lights) and slightly rotten, since each is now complicit in something they condemn. So does this have an established name? If it does, tell me what the term covers that my case doesn't, and vice versa. If it doesn't, coin one and show me the edges: what separates it from buying an indulgence, or from paying someone else to carry your moral burden, and what becomes of the deal when it lands on people who aren't in the room.
