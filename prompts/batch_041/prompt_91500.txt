2. Mental causation is real. My deciding to leave is a cause of my leaving, not a mere shadow cast by the brain state that's doing the real work.
3. The physical is causally closed. Every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause.
4. No systematic overdetermination. When my decision and my brain state both get cited as causes of my arm rising, that's one cause described twice, not two causes.
5. Multiple realizability. The same belief can be implemented in different physical ways, and that's my main reason for refusing to be a reductionist.
6. Interventionism about causation. X causes Y roughly when an intervention on X would make a difference to Y.
7. Causal realism. There's a determinate, interest-independent fact about what causes what. It doesn't shift around with what we happen to be explaining.
8. Causal inheritance. A mental property has whatever causal powers its physical realizer has. It doesn't add any, and it doesn't lose any.
