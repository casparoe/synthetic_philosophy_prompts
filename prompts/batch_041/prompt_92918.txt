I am a graduate student in philosophy of science, and I keep running into a structure that I am fairly sure must have a name, though I have not found it in anything I have read on underdetermination or on scientific realism. Here is the structure. A theory comes in two layers: one that fixes what any agent working inside the theory could ever measure, and another — extra entities, extra variables, extra structure — which the theory's own laws prove can never be learned about from the inside. The second layer is not merely unmeasured, and not merely hard to reach the way a distant galaxy or a particle beyond our accelerators is: the dynamics guarantee that no experiment could distinguish one state of the extra layer from another, so its state makes no difference to any fact anyone could ever come to know. In the hidden-variable theories where the extra variables are provably distributed in just the way that makes the statistics come out as the ordinary no-hidden-variable theory predicts, no measurement can ever catch them out; the same shape turns up whenever a model contains a sector that no signal from our sector can reach, or a parameter that the model itself proves unrecoverable from any data the model can generate, or an initial condition that the dynamics provably erase. What puzzles me is that the extra layer is not idle either: the theory is written in terms of it, the behaviour of the observable layer is derived from it, and those who accept the theory take it to be doing explanatory work. So we get a standing disagreement — some say the theory is about the extra layer, others that it is a device for computing the observable layer — and no evidence bears on it, not even in principle, because the theory itself certifies the extra layer's invisibility. This has also left me unsure how to do something I assumed was easy, namely saying what a theory says there is.

What I want is not a label but the right carving. Does this structure have an established name, or a small family of names, and which of them fits best? Underdetermination is the obvious candidate, but it seems to concern two rival theories with the same empirical consequences, whereas here there is a single theory with an internal division, and the inaccessibility is a theorem of the theory rather than a limit on us. Unobservable entity is too broad: an electron is unobservable, but the theory tells us its effects are recoverable from what we can see, whereas here the theory tells us the opposite. So the nearest concepts each seem only partly right, and I would like to know where the boundaries fall: does it matter that the inaccessible layer is load-bearing for the theory's formulation rather than a dispensable ornament, and is there a separate name for the case where the theory proves a layer inaccessible as opposed to merely failing to make it accessible? If nothing covers exactly this, coin a term, define it, and say what would have to be true of a case for it to fall outside. The edges are what I need, since the point I want to make in my chapter turns on the difference between information that is unavailable and information that is unavailable by the theory's own decree.
