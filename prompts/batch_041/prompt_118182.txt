My friend and I got into it about wether taxation is theft, and I wrote our argument out properly, and now I think it eats itself. Tell me where it goes wrong.

(1) Taking something from its owner without their consent and without a valid claim of right is theft. (2) A valid claim of right has to come from somewhere: either the owner's consent, or a rule the owner is bound by. (3) I never consented to the tax code, and I'm not bound by rules I never agreed to, so the state's claim to my income can't rest on consent. (4) So it would have to rest on some pre-political entitlement the state has over me and my stuff, and it doesn't have one. (5) So the state's taking of my income is theft, and theft is wrong whatever the statute book says. (6) So taxation is wrong.

Fine so far, I thought. But (3) works just as well against the state's power to decide which of two people owns the field in the first place, and against courts, contracts, currency, and land registries, none of which I consented to either. So by (2)-(4) the state has no valid claim of right to draw the boundaries of my property at all, which means there's no determinate "my income" for it to steal. The pre-tax figure isn't sitting out there in nature with my name on it; it's a number the whole legal apparatus produces. So the very steps that give me "taxation is theft" also give me "there's nothing there for anyone to steal," which I can't swallow. Where's the bad step? I'd rather keep (1) and keep the thought that there's a real difference between the state taking my money and my neighbour taking my bike, but I want to know what I'm paying for that. Also tell me how confident you are in your diagnosis, and which step you're least sure about.
