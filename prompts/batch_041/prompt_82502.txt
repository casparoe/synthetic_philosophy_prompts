So I'm giving the student commencement address at my college this May -- small liberal arts school, maybe 400 of us graduating, families in folding chairs on the lawn, president says a few words first and then it's me for about eight minutes. Which would be fine except I've spent the last two years as a fairly card-carrying lexical-threshold negative utilitarian and I cant just switch it off at the podium. Here's roughly where I sit, so you know what you're working with: I think pleasure and pain are commensurable up to a point -- a lousy headache is worth trading against enough mild good stuff, sure, no drama there -- but above some threshold the commensurability breaks. Really bad suffering isn't outweighed by any amount of the good stuff; it dominates lexically, the way an infinite disvalue would sit in the ledger and just flatten everything on the other side. And I'm not even sure the threshold is a property of a single experience or of a whole life-total, which is a fight I keep having with my roommate and haven't settled. Anyway. The awkward part is that a commencement address is basically a machine for saying the future is bright, go get em, it all works out -- and by my lights the ledger's already been blown past, by suffering that's already happened and suffering that's waiting for some of the people sitting in those chairs, and no amount of diplomas or careers or grandkids compensates for it.

I don't want to lie to that crowd. Not the families, not the faculty who know exactly what I think, not the friends who've been yelling at me about this at 2am. But I also can't stand up there and deliver a lecture on the impossibility of compensation to a field full of 22-year-olds in rented gowns whose parents drove six hours to watch them walk. So what I want is a real commencement speech that a lexical-threshold negative utilitarian could actually give without flinching -- one that takes the occasion seriously, gives the audience something to hold onto, and still doesn't say a single thing I'd have to walk back afterward. Is there a version of "congratulations, go well" that survives contact with the view? Write me the eight minutes. And if the tension can't be fully resolved, I'd honestly rather you show me exactly where it snaps than smooth it over with nice-sounding stuff I don't believe.
