In my ethics class my teacher keeps repeating 'the road to hell is paved with good intentions' whenever anyone suggests that what matters is trying to do the right thing rather than what actually happens. Lately she has sharpened it into something like 'because the consequences of anything you do ripple outwards for ever, nobody can ever really know what they are doing', and she calls this the problem of cluelessness. She takes it to show that judging actions by their consequences is hopeless, and that we should give up on that way of thinking altogether. I suspect there is something in this, but I cannot tell how much, and the word 'know' is doing a lot of quiet work.

Could you set out the different precise claims that slogan could be making, arranged from the trivial to the genuinely radical, and give a verdict on each — true, defensible, overstated, or simply false? I would especially like to know which of the readings would actually support her conclusion that consequentialism is hopeless; whether 'cannot know' here means 'cannot in practice calculate', 'cannot even assign rough probabilities', or something stronger still, such as the future being too chaotic for probabilities to attach at all; whether the argument, if it works, would not sink every other way of judging actions just as easily; and whether the moderate reading — that we often cannot foresee how things turn out — is enough to reach her conclusion or whether she needs the radical one. Please lay the readings out as a menu I can take to her, noting for each which version of the slogan the people who repeat it usually mean.
