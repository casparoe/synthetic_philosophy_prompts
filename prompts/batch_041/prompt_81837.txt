I'm a third-year PhD student working on Williams and the integrity objection, and I'm workshopping the middle chapter with my supervisor next week. I'd rather you tear into the core move before she does. Notes, not a full draft:

The standard framing: the objection is about deliberation. Husband saves wife; if his deliberating includes the thought that impartial morality permits him to favor her, he's had one thought too many, and the rescuing should have flowed from the relationship itself. The standard reply is that this confuses the agent's deliberation with the agent's justification — the justifying thought need never cross his mind, so the objection is unfair to the consequentialist. I think that reply is too easy and I want to earn the right to reject it.

My move: the thought that's one too many isn't the one he has, it's the one he could be asked for. Rebuild the whole thing around answerability instead of deliberation. New case, I'm calling it the transcript test: it isn't that we'd be appalled to overhear him deliberating impartially, it's that if someone asked him at the funeral why he went in for her, the answer that impartial morality gave him permission would fail him — not because it's false but because he can't sincerely avow it as *his* reason. So the objection is about the first-personal availability of reasons, not about the temperature of his psychology.

Corollary I'm attached to and nervous about: this makes the objection general rather than moral. Test case — a mathematician who works on a problem because it's hers versus because it's objectively the most important open problem. If the same complaint shows up there, then integrity is about self-identification rather than morality, which might be a bullet too big for me.

What I'm worried about: that I've just reinvented the two-tier view (impartial justification, partial deliberation) and moved which tier the thought lives on. Is my relocation a genuine advance or a redescription?

What I want: don't hand me a list of worries and let me pick. Take a definite position on whether the relocation is a real improvement or a dodge, say which of these three seperate pieces is load-bearing and which is decoration, and give me the one objection you'd lead with if you were examining me. If you think the transcript test is rigged, say so and tell me what case would break it.
