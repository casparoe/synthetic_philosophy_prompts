For a year now, two colleagues in my reading group have argued about pure time preference without either moving: one is a temporal neutralist who holds that no one's well-being should count for less merely because it comes later, the other holds that a positive rate of pure time preference is defensible once the moral question is separated from the uncertainty and growth-related components that usually travel with it. Draft the joint statement their adversarial collaboration should produce. Begin with the claims both can sign: what precisely is and is not at issue; the cases they agree are the test cases; and their shared account of how the ordinary dispute conflates pure time preference with the consumption discount rate, with uncertainty, and with diminishing marginal value. Then give the residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording, including whether the real disagreement concerns the moral status of temporal position or the rationality of caring unequally, and whether either position is at bottom a claim about the weight of a person's interests rather than about the value of outcomes. Set out the cruxes -- I suspect the burden of proof is one, and the analogue with spatial distance another -- and for each, name the argument or case both sides agree bears on it and record each side's prediction in advance, along with what each would accept as evidence or argument against their own view. If part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. Write it as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
