I design large-scale distributed systems for a living, and I'm fluent in the vocabulary of nodes and their local state, message passing and gossip, consensus and coordination overhead, private and asymmetric information, mechanism design, and the distinction between a system that computes a known objective and one that searches for or discovers it. I've been reading about the socialist calculation debate, and I want the positions of Mises, Hayek, Lange, and the later incentive critics restated in that vocabulary as faithfully as the restatement can be done -- not as a loose metaphor but as a genuine translation, keeping three claims apart that are usually run together: the calculation problem (whether the optimum is computable at all), the knowledge problem (where the relevant information lives and whether it can be transmitted), and the incentive problem (whether the agents will act on it once it is known).

Two things should shape the translation. First, the debate has been revived by the claim that modern computation and machine learning could let a central planner solve the allocation problem directly; I want to know whether that revival is answering the problem Hayek actually posed or a different and easier one. Second, I want the Austrian idea that the market is a discovery procedure rather than an optimization procedure taken seriously rather than quietly redescribed as stochastic search.

Give me exactly five numbered items and nothing else -- no introduction, no conclusion, each item a sentence or two and telegraphically dense if you like. Four of them should each carry one move of the debate (Mises's impossibility claim, Hayek's knowledge argument, Lange's parametric-price response with its Central Planning Board, and the incentive critique) into my vocabulary, saying explicitly where the translation fits without strain and where you are selling me a fudge. The fifth should be the residue report: what in the debate has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all, and whether that absence marks a gap in how I think about systems or a place where the economists' disagreement was doing normative work that no engineering vocabulary could carry.
