Bit of an odd one, this. I'm a backend engineer, no formal philosophy background — read a bit of the pop stuff, listened to a few podcasts — and my flatmate and I have got into a running arguement about whether it's actually wrong to kill NPCs in video games. Not in a jokey way either; he definately reckons there's something there, and I keep saying it's just pixels, except then I catch myself feeling genuinely bad when a character I liked dies, and I don't know what to do with that.

So: rank the cases below by how much moral status they deserve. Give me a number between 0 and 1 for each, where 0 is a rock and 1 is a normal adult human — and if you think the top of that scale should sit well below 1 for all of them, say so and say why. Then the overall order, plus the reasoning.

1. A shopkeeper NPC with fixed dialogue who exists so you can buy potions; the game lets you kill him for his gold and nothing else in the world changes.
2. A procedurally generated villager who only exists while you're in the zone and gets dropped from memory once you walk away — so if you kill them, there's no persisting fact of the matter afterwards.
3. A character in a story-driven game the writers clearly want you to care about, with a full arc and a proper death scene.
4. Any character in a save file — you quicksave, kill them, reload, and they're back with no memory of it. Did anything happen to them the first time round?
5. An NPC driven by a language model at runtime, so it says genuinely new things and seems to remember your last session.
6. The avatar of a real person in an online match — the player behind it is obviously real, the avatar is just a model.
7. A character from a game that got cancelled and never shipped, so no player will ever meet them.
8. Your own player character, the one you steer.

What I actually want out of it: which single feature is doing most of the work in your ranking (having experiences? being remembered? being real? being witnessed?); which two cases are seperate by only a sliver and why that gap is so small; which case breaks your own criterion if you lean on it hard enough; and what would change in your ranking if the case-5 NPC turned out to be an extremely clever script rather than a real model. Don't just tell me what other people have said about this — I can't look it up and I don't much care. I want your ranking and your reasoning behind it.
