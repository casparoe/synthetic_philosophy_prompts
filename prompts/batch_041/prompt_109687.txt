A classmate in my language-acquisition seminar keeps deploying 'the poverty of the stimulus' as though it were a single premise doing a single job: the child's data are too thin to determine a grammar, therefore the grammar must be innate, and anyone who proposes a statistical learner has simply failed to appreciate the phrase. My suspicion is that several quite distinct claims are packed into that expression, and that his argument goes through on some of them and not on others. What I want from you is a disambiguation, not a defence.

Set out the precise theses that 'the poverty of the stimulus' might be standing for. I have in mind at least the following: a claim about what the child's input does and does not contain; a claim about the relation between the input and the target grammar, for instance that the target is not the simplest hypothesis consistent with the data; a claim about the size of the hypothesis space relative to the evidence; a claim about the absence of negative evidence, correction, or information about which strings are ungrammatical; a claim about the frequency or statistical detectability of particular constructions; and a claim about what any learning algorithm could in principle extract from data of that kind. For each, say whether it is a platitude that nobody contests, a substantive empirical claim that may well be true, a claim that holds for some constructions but not others, or something false or at least unsupported by anything in the child's environment.

Then say which of these readings is actually needed for the inference to innateness to go through, and whether any single one of them is sufficient for it. Where a reading is empirical, indicate what would settle it — what kind of fact about child-directed speech, or about the capacities of learners given limited data, would show which reading is in play. Finish by telling me which reading my classmate most likely has in mind, and whether on that reading the phrase has the argumentative force he takes it to have.

Please lay this out as a numbered menu I can bring to the seminar, with a short verdict after each entry.
