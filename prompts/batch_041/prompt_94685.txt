You are an expert on Foucault's analysis of disciplinary power and on the practical ethics of workplace monitoring, and you are good at turning theory into something a non-specialist committee can actually run. I chair the technology oversight panel of a large hospital trust. We vet roughly one staff-monitoring proposal a month, we have two hours per proposal and no Foucault scholars among us (a clinician, a union representative, a data protection officer, an IT lead), and we must issue a written opinion to the board.

Please build us a procedure: an ordered set of tests, each with a question, what counts as a sufficient answer, and what follows from it, for deciding whether a proposed monitoring technology works as a disciplinary apparatus in Foucault's sense rather than as ordinary management, audit or contractual oversight. Begin with a short paragraph setting out which features you take to be essential and which you will drop. Keep the distinctions that do real work here: the three instruments of discipline (hierarchical observation, normalising judgement, and the examination); the difference between a norm generated from the population's own distribution and a standard imposed from outside it; whether the compulsion runs by itself through the mere possibility of being seen or requires someone actually to be watching; and whether the system accumulates knowledge about individuals — cases, dossiers, rankings — which then feeds back into how those individuals are handled. Be explicit that Foucault's account is diagnostic rather than a moral yardstick, and mark the exact point at which the procedure must stop and hand the matter back to us as a question of value, with the options we would be choosing between.

Then run the procedure on two proposals: (1) ambient listening software in outpatient clinics that transcribes consultations, scores each clinician's communication against a norm computed from the trust's own distribution, circulates the scores to peers, and puts the bottom tenth into mandatory coaching; (2) handheld scanners at a distribution depot that enforce pick rates and re-sequence the work, keeping no individual record beyond the shift. Finally, tell us where the procedure will misfire and which finding we will not like. No bibliography is needed. Write in British English.
