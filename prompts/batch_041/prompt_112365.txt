After a long argument with a friend who is a committed but unsystematic political reformer, I want to know what she will say about a new proposal. Her verdicts so far: she supports a six-year lifetime cap for state legislators; she supported an independent redistricting commission whose members are chosen by lot from a pool of volunteers; she opposed replacing our city council with a one-year citizens' assembly chosen by lot, arguing that amateurs would be captured by staff and that voters could not punish them; she opposes lifetime appointments to the state supreme court but supports retention elections; she supports a recall procedure for mayors; and she supports paying legislators a decent salary while abolishing their pensions. The new proposal would replace the state senate with a chamber selected by lot, whose members serve one four-year term, cannot be reelected, receive a salary, and have a professional research staff, but cannot be recalled and face no individual electoral accountability; its bills would still be subject to a referendum. What will my friend say about this proposal, and what should she say if she wants to be consistent? Identify the competing rules that fit her earlier verdicts -- for example, anti-careerism, popular accountability, deliberative competence, and anti-incumbent self-dealing -- and say what each predicts about the new case, which rule I should treat as her real one and why, and what further case would best distinguish those rules. Please answer in American English.
