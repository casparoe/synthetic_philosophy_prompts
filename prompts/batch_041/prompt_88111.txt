Im on the naming and monuments panel in Kestrel Bay — small city, five of us, we handle maybe six files a year. I need help with next month's, but the real problem is that after four years I still can't tell what rule we've actually been running, and I'm the one writing the recommendation.

Roughly what we've done:

1. Took the Rooke statue off the plaza. Colonial merchant, fortune came out of the plantation trade, and he paid for the library and the first hospital; the inscription calls him 'founder of the city's prosperity'. We didn't destroy it — it went into the county museum, in a room the descendant association co-wrote — and we moved $400k from the plaza sale into their housing fund.

2. Kept Kell Way. She founded the children's hospital. She also destroyed a rival doctor's career over a priority dispute in the twenties and he died broke. We added a plaque and a note in the registry. Nobody has ever complained about the plaque.

3. Renamed Pyle Elementary. He's honored for 'opening the valley', which is the dispossession campaign, and it's basically the only thing he ever did. The renaming was part of the settlement where the district handed the old building over to the tribal community.

4. Kept the Merrit memorial — lifeboat captain, pulled about forty people off the point. In 2019 a letter surfaced showing he beat his wife. Nobody reads that statue as being about that and there's no group it's an insult to, so we left it and added nothing.

5. Kept Fenwick Avenue. He's the banker who redlined that district in the thirties; the street is named for him as the man who brought the streetcar out there. The neighborhood association has been asking us to change it for years and we voted 4-1 to keep, basically because the city had committed nothing to that neighborhood and none of us wanted to hand them a new sign and call it justice. I'm still not sure about that one.

I can fit at least three different rules to all five of those and they aren't the same rule. Now the new one.

Hale Bridge, the old pedestrian bridge over the creek in Ridgeway Park. Named in 1911 for Ossian Hale, the city engineer who designed the water and sewer system that ended the typhoid outbreaks — the bridge is only there because the main had to cross the creek. Still in service; the water workers' union is named after him. At 22 he was one of the leaders of the mob that burned the Black neighborhood down by the creek in 1889 and killed eleven people. That neighborhood was never rebuilt. The descendants' association is three blocks away and has formally asked us to rename the bridge. The historical society will pay for an interpretive plaque. There's no repair package on the table — nothing budgeted. One contested archival letter suggests Hale quietly gave to the relief fund afterward; the historical society says yes, the descendants' association says it's a receipt for forty dollars and a thank-you note.

So: which of the rules I might be running do you think we're actually running, what does each say about the bridge, which do you bet on and why, which of the five is the closest analog, and what should we put in front of ourselves so we're forced to admit which rule we're using — i.e. what case would split them.
