Design a single thought experiment that can be told twice — once as one person's Ulysses contract, once as a political community's act of constitutional precommitment — while holding the preference structure and the temptation fixed across both tellings.

Your case must satisfy all of the following.

First, the individual's act of self-binding must be rational ex ante by her own lights: no paternalist, no external authority, no one imposing it on her — she binds herself, and she has reasons for doing so that you can state.

Second, the later reason to escape the binding must not be weakness of will. It must be a genuine and sincere change in what she values, and — this is the hinge — a change she could have foreseen at the time she bound herself. The case must therefore say what could justify binding oneself in full knowledge that one's future self may sincerely and reasonably want out.

Third, no supernatural or science-fictional machinery: no time travel, no brainwashing, no perfect predictors, no hypothetical enforcement bureaus. The setting must be one an ordinary adult could actually occupy — a workplace, a family, a club, a town.

Fourth, the same structure, transferred to the collective, must yield the opposite verdict on whether the binding is legitimate, and the asymmetry must fall out of the structure itself — the difference between a single person persisting through time and a body whose membership turns over — rather than from your stipulating different preferences, different stakes, or a different mechanism.

Fifth, the scenario itself should be compact: a short paragraph at most.

Then answer in exactly five numbered items, with no introduction and no conclusion — the scenario allowed a short paragraph, each remaining item a sentence or two:

1. The scenario, in both tellings.
2. What the view that the past self is sovereign — that a person's earlier act of will has standing over her later self, because the later self is not a different person — says about each telling, and why.
3. What the view that only the present self's will can ground a reason to act — so that a past act of will carries no special authority over a present agent — says about each telling, and why.
4. The single feature of the case that does the work, and whether removing it collapses the case into the ordinary Ulysses story and erases the individual/collective asymmetry.
5. How a defender of whichever view fares worse on your case would attack it.
