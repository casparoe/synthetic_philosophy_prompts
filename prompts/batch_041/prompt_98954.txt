I am a PhD student TAing a graduate seminar on collective responsibility, and I have a stack of problem-set answers to grade this week. I would like a grade and a set of marginal comments for one of them. The problem I set was:

"Suppose a harm occurs if and only if at least 5,000 of a pool of 10,000 agents each perform the same act, and suppose that in fact all 10,000 perform it. (i) State the inefficacy objection to the claim that any one of them acts wrongly. (ii) Evaluate the reply that appeals to expected contribution. (iii) Evaluate a reply that appeals to complicity or participation rather than to consequences. (iv) Say what changes if exactly 5,000 act, and what changes if the agents are uncertain how many of the others will act."

The answer to be graded:

"Part (i). The inefficacy objection holds that no individual act in this case makes a difference. Had any one agent refrained, the threshold would still have been crossed by the remaining 9,999, so her act is neither necessary nor sufficient for the harm, and the harm would have occurred just the same. Since an act can be wrong only in virtue of the difference it makes, and since here no act makes any difference, no individual acts wrongly; whatever wrongdoing there is attaches to the 10,000 as a collective rather than to any member. The objection should be read as metaphysical rather than epistemic: it is not that we cannot tell what any individual contributed, but that there is nothing for a contribution to consist in.

Part (ii). The expected-contribution reply denies this. Even if no act is a difference-maker ex post, ex ante each act raises the probability of the harm: a world in which this agent acts and the others' conduct is uncertain is more likely to cross the threshold than one in which she does not. Each act therefore carries expected harm, and that is enough to make it pro tanto wrong. I take the reply to succeed only where the agent is confident that enough others will act, since only then can her act be credited with membership in the set that produces the harm; under genuine uncertainty her act is one candidate among many and carries no weight. So the reply rescues individual wrongdoing in the easy case but not in the hard one.

Part (iii). A complicity reply says that the participant is responsible not for the harm as such but for her participation, which is objectionable in its own right and independently of what the others do. This is a coherent position, and it does not depend on any marginal difference the act makes. Its cost is that it must explain what is objectionable about participation when the participation is not itself harmful, and it is not obvious that this can be done without reintroducing the consequentialist reasoning that part (ii) relied on.

Part (iv). If exactly 5,000 act, each act is necessary for the harm, and the inefficacy objection lapses. If the agents are uncertain how many of the others will act, the case is intermediate, and the strength of the expected-contribution reply varies with the perceived likelihood of being pivotal."

Please assign a grade (I use a standard A–F scale) and, more importantly, write the marginal comments I should attach to each part, indicating which errors are serious enough to move the grade and which are matters of completeness or presentation. I need the comments specific enough that the student can see exactly where the reasoning goes wrong, and I would rather have a short list of precise objections than a general impression of the answer.
