Having spent a career in molecular biology and the past several years reading philosophy of biology, I still cannot get a clear grip on the claim that DNA is a code, or carries information, or functions as a program; the philosophers seem to assert and deny it in ways I cannot reconcile with the machinery I know. Please restate the principal positions in that debate in the vocabulary I actually trust: source and channel coding, redundancy and error correction, lookup tables, encoders and decoders, and the contrast between a program and its data. My own naive picture, carried over from instrument software, is of a compressed archive together with a bootstrap loader — the sequence as the payload, the ribosome as the runtime, the cell as the machine — and I would like that picture tested alongside the others. For each position, tell me what translates cleanly into these terms, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no counterpart there at all. Then give me the residue report, and in particular tell me where "aboutness" falls: the suggestion that a sequence is about the organism, or about its own fitness, is the part I cannot make the engineering do, and I want to know whether that marks a defect in my vocabulary or a sign that the disagreement was less substantive than it appeared.
