Here's the seed. Everyone argues about brain death vs circulatory death as if we were picking the correct criterion for a single thing called death. I want to try the opposite: that 'death' quietly indexes two different things — the end of the organism as an integrated system, and the end of the person or the mind — and that most of the shouting is because people slide between the two without noticing. The twist I actually care about is irreversibility. Both criteria lean on it, but irreversibility is relative to what we can currently do, which means any criterion is secretly indexed to a tech level we don't have yet. So take a case: someone meets the whole-brain criterion today, and fifty years from now the tissue is regrown and something with their memories wakes up. Were they dead in the intervening time, or only 'dead for now'? I don't want to settle that by intuition, I want it as the pivot.

The question I want the proposal to nail down is roughly: is death a technology-relative status, i.e. something you can be provisionally and later not be, and if so does that push it toward being a policy construct rather than a metaphysical category — or is there a residue that stays metaphysical no matter what the machines can do?
