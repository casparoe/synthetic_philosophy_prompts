I've been reading up on that old fight between Malcolm and Dennett about whether dreams are experiences, and I think I've got the basic shape of it: both of them are saying that when you dream you aren't really having any experiences — theres nothing it's like to be asleep and dreaming, and the dream narrative is something you cook up rather than something you undergo. Fine, I can accept that. But then it hit me that this makes dream reports basically worthless, right? If there was never any experience there, then when I say I dreamed I was being chased through a parking garage, I'm just confabulating, and I have no more reason to believe I actually had that dream than to believe some story a stranger told me. So how am I supposed to trust any of my memories of dreams? Should I just write off all my dream recall as noise, or is there a way to seperate the genuine dreams from the confabulated ones? And does it even make sense to say I had a dream at all, or should I stop talking that way? Also — does this mean lucid dreaming is impossible? Because if dreams aren't experiences then there's nobody in there to become lucid in the first place.
