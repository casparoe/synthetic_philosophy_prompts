I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training, trying to work out what I actually believe about my own small contributions to large harms. Here are my gut verdicts, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, they add up to. Eating one burger at a cookout where the rest of the meat would be thrown out anyway: fine. Eating meat most days for years: not fine, even though I can't point to any single meal that did harm. One short flight a year: fine. Flying long-haul for work most months: not fine. Skipping my vote in a presidential election in a state that's a foregone conclusion: fine. Skipping my vote in a town referendum I know will come down to a handful of ballots: not fine. Running a gas leaf blower in my own yard for an hour: fine. But being one of a hundred households whose combined runoff kills the creek at the end of the street, where the creek only dies once the hundredth household starts draining: the hundredth household seems to me to have done something worse than the first, even though no single household's runoff mattered on its own. And in a race that could genuinely go either way, where my vote has about a one-in-ten-thousand chance of deciding it, I feel I should vote, but I can't tell whether that's the chance or just that voting is a habit I'd feel bad about dropping.

I've been trying out a principle: an act is wrong when it's part of a practice that, taken as a whole, causes serious harm and I know it. That makes the single burger fine, since the meat was headed for the bin, and the regular meat-eater wrong, which is what I want, but it also makes the leaf-blower user wrong, which I don't want. The rival principle, that an act is wrong only when it makes a difference to the harm, gets the leaf blower and the safe-state vote right but seems to let the regular meat-eater off, which I can't accept. Tell me which of my verdicts I have to give up, give me the cleanest principle that saves as many as it can along with the case that motivates each part of it, and say what it implies for two cases I haven't settled: someone who eats meat about ten times a year, and someone who flies monthly but buys offsets for every flight.
