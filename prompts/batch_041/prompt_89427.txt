Ok so I'm putting together a case for a seminar on whether a colony can have a mind — the Aunt Hillary thing — and I want a case that actually does some work instead of just gesturing at the famous ones. Here's the framework I want it built inside, and yes I made it up, thats on purpose. Three marks, call them broadcast, rehearsal, and stakes. Broadcast: information picked up by one part of the system becomes available to every other part fast enough to be used in a single decision. Rehearsal: the system can run its model off-line, i.e. simulate an option without committing to it, and the simulation can change what it eventually does. Stakes: some of the system's states are ones it has an interest in attaining or avoiding — there's something it's like to be it, so it can be helped or harmed. I don't need these to be the right marks, I need them to be the marks we're using.

Build me a colony case that has broadcast and rehearsal in a way nobody in the seminar can wave away, but where the stakes mark comes out absent on the best reading — and make that absence fall out of the setup rather than just being announced. Constraints: no new physics, no "the ants turn out to be neurons" trick, no perfect consciousness-detector, and please dont recycle the usual furniture — no neuron-by-neuron replacement, no China-brain, no uploading. The colony's outward behavior should be person-like in at least one genuinely demanding respect (it can be argued with, say, or it holds a grudge over months), so the case isn't won by making the colony stupid; but it should be clearly not person-like in the stakes respect. A paragraph or so is plenty.
