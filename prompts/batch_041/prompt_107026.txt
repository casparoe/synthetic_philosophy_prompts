Hey, so I'm the co-president of the philosophy club at my high school and we just finished a joint unit with the bio department called "what is life," and my bio teacher said I could design the end-of-unit quiz since I'm the one who keeps arguing with her about whether viruses count as alive. I want one big essay question, and I need you to write the question, a model answer, and a marking scheme, because she wants to see how I'd grade it. My problem is that every question I've seen just makes people list the textbook characteristics of life and then they get full marks without thinking. I want something where you actually have to decide something and defend it.

Here's my case, tell me if it works or fix it if it doesn't. Say scientists find this thing deep in a cave (or on Mars, whatever). I'm calling it a shard. It's a mineral formation that slowly grows by pulling in dissolved minerals and energy, it heals its own cracks by regrowing, and every so often it sheds a chunk that grows into a new shard. The new shards come out slightly different from the parent, and the ones that happen to grow faster end up shedding more chunks, so over thousands of years the whole population changes, basically like it's evolving. But there's no cells, no DNA, no proteins, no membrane, nothing it regulates on the inside — it's just crystal. And then you've got a mule, which is definately alive but can't have babies.

So can you write (1) the essay question, (2) a model answer of about 400 words, and (3) a marking scheme out of 20 marks that names the specific moves a top answer has to make and the specific mistakes that should lose marks, with how many marks each thing is worth? The question should force students to decide whether the shard is alive, and then make them say what their verdict commits them to about the mule and about whether "life" is really one thing or a few different things. And please don't let them get away with "it's not alive because it isn't made of cells" as if that settles anything. It's a 40-minute in-class essay, so the model answer can't run long. Keep the whole response under 800 words and write it in American English. Thanks!!
