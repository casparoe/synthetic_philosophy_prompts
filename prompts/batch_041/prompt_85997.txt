I teach an upper-level philosophy of science seminar and I'm drafting the final exam. I want one essay question on Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes that cannot be answered by reciting the hard core/protective belt distinction. The question should require students to apply Lakatos's criteria for progressive and degenerating problem-shifts to a fictional research programme, and then to confront the problem that those criteria seem to be retrospective: if we can only tell after the fact whether a programme was progressive, what guidance does the methodology offer to scientists working in real time? To keep the question concrete, use the following capsule case: a cognitive-psychology programme whose hard core holds that memory traces are standing waves in a continuous neural medium and that retrieval is a resonance phenomenon; its positive heuristic generates interference models; at stage one it predicted a previously unobserved retrieval-induced forgetting curve, later confirmed; at stage two it failed to predict a spacing effect and its adherents saved the core by adding a dampening hypothesis that made no new predictions; at stage three a rival connectionist programme explained the spacing effect and predicted a priming asymmetry that the wave programme could only accommodate after the fact. Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. The exam is 90 minutes and the question is worth 20 marks.
