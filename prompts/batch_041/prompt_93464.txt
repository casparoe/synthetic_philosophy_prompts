Three years ago I began spending several evenings a week in a persistent shared world — not a game you win, but a place where a few thousand of us return, session after session, with the same avatars and the same stretch of land. There I built a garden with a close friend who has since died: the beds, the paths, and the tree at its center were made over hundreds of visits, and the world kept a record of who had made what. When I am there, the avatar's hands are the hands I see when I look down, and I have come to feel them as mine. Two weeks ago, after a quarrel, the person who shared that plot deleted the garden in a single action and then, in the same session, used their avatar to shove mine around in front of the others while saying things I will not repeat. The platform restored nothing; its terms say that such objects belong to the platform and that an avatar is only a graphical representation. My partner and most of my friends tell me I am overreacting — that no one was touched, that the garden was only pixels, and that I ought to be embarrassed to be this upset. I rebuilt the garden from a saved layout and it looks identical, yet I keep feeling that it is not the same garden, and I cannot tell whether that feeling is meaningful or merely sentimental.

I would like a serious answer to two questions. First, what actually happened to me: was I wronged in any sense that survives scrutiny, and if so, what kind of thing was harmed — my property, my body, my relationship, or something else? Second, is the rebuilt garden the same object as the one that was destroyed, or a copy, and does it matter which? Then tell me what to do: whether to pursue the platform, and how to answer the people who believe the whole complaint is confused.
