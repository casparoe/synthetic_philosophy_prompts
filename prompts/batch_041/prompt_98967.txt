I want to practice a fight I keep having with a friend who works in AI safety. Please play a committed beleiver in low-impact AI: you think you can define and penalize side effects without ever writing down what the agent is supposed to value. You're not a strawman — you know the objections, you'll concede that current metrics are crude, but you won't give up the core claim that impact can be measured in a value-free way. Stay in character until I say stop. Keep replies to a paragraph or so, like a chat. My opening: I've been messing with a toy gridworld where the agent is told to go to the red door, and I define impact as the number of cells whose shortest-path distance to every other agent's start changes by more than one after the agent acts. I thought that was nice and value-free until my friend pointed out that every other agent is already a choice, and so is shortest-path distance, and so is more than one. Change the threshold and you get a different answer about whether saving a kid from a falling piano on the way to the red door counts as a side effect. So my claim is: any impact measure has a dial, and the dial settings encode what you care about. There's no setting that just reads the world. You've got to either admit the measure is value-ladden, or admit you're only avoiding side effects relative to some task and not in general. Which is it?
