Write a joke that turns on the wrong kind of reasons problem: the puzzle that a reward for having an attitude — believing a proposition, admiring a person, finding something funny — can be a genuine reason to bring the attitude about while, if it is what actually moves you, seeming to disqualify the attitude as fitting. To avoid the stock demon-and-threat illustration, set the joke in a case where an automated system both offers the incentive and verifies compliance, so that the very evidence that you succeeded doubles as evidence that the attitude is not fitting. The punchline should turn on that self-undermining structure — a reason that works only by spoiling its own product — and the joke should land for someone who knows the debate over fitting-attitude accounts of value and the buck-passing connection, without the text naming any of that.
