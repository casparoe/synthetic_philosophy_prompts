I host a small podcast on philosophy of science, and next weeks guest is a philosopher of physics who thinks string theory plus eternal inflation is 'confirmed' in a weaker but still real sense. Her label for it is non-empirical confirmation: you're entitled to accept a theory when it has no viable rivals, when it unifies things that used to be separate, and when it solves problems it wasn't built to solve -- even if it hands you no novel prediction you could check anytime soon. Her extra twist is that the demand for a distinctive new prediction is a leftover from an older picture of how theories get tested, and that once you're Bayesian about where priors come from, the demand loses most of its bite. I want to push her on this without turning it into the usual 'is the multiverse science' segment everyone's already heard.

Draft me eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I want at least one on the no-alternatives argument -- how does she know the alternatives were exhausted rather than just not yet invented, and does it matter that the people judging the alternatives are inside the program? At least one on the measure problem: if different measures give different predictions and the measure gets picked partly because it gives sane answers, why isn't that fitting the curve? One on the fine-tuning inference to a multiverse, aimed at the objection that going from the fact that this roll came up double six to the conclusion that there must have been a lot of rolls is a fallacy, plus the usual comeback that the inference is fine when what you're conditioning on is there being an observer at all. One on Boltzmann brains: if the framework makes it overwhelmingly likely that we're momentary fluctuations rather than evolved physicists, doesn't it saw off the branch its own support is sitting on? And one on what 'confirmed' is supposed to add over 'worth pursuing', if she's happy calling the whole thing a research program.

Keep each question short enough to read aloud, and don't have her restate her position in the question. At the end, tell me honestly which of your questions is the weakest and what she could say that would make you think the whole non-empirical-confirmation line is a dead end -- I'd rather know where you're guessing than get a sequence that sounds airtight.
