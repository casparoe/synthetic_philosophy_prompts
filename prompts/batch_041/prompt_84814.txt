Here's the situation. I found an apartment listing for a place I really want, and over the past week I've done the full paranoid routine: a video call where the guy walked me through the actual unit, a buddy of mine who lives in that neighborhood walked past the building and confirmed the address and the name on the buzzer, the name on the listing matches the name on the lease PDF he emailed me, he answered a weirdly specific question about the radiator. So I feel like I know the guy has a key to 3B and that the listing is a real listing. Then my friend Dana, who reads the same blogs, said: sure, but do you know it's not a scam? Because if it were a scam, would any of your evidence have come out different? Scammers have keys — they rent the place for a weekend. They do the video tour. They send plausible paperwork. And annoyingly she's describing my actual mental state: the "it's not a scam" part does not feel like knowledge to me, even though it follows from stuff I do feel like I know, by a step so trivial it's embarrassing. Then she hit me with the reductio — by my own standard I can't know my wallet isn't being lifted right now, or that the text from my mom was actually from my mom — which supposedly means either I know nothing or I'm being inconsistent. I can't tell if this is a real puzzle or if she's just doing a bit at my expense.

I have a nagging feeling there's a name for exactly this shape of problem, that I read a blog post about it at 1am years ago and forgot. What I want is someone to tell me whether my instinct — I know the local fact, I don't know the implied fact, and that asymmetry isn't just me being wishy-washy about the word "know" — has a respectable defense, and if so what's doing the work, and where the defense gets ugly.
