I teach in a medical humanities program and in three weeks I have ninety minutes with a mixed group of psychiatry residents and hospital chaplains on advance directives. I don't want the ethics explained to me; I want a plan for explaining it. The session's real subject is not the paperwork but the authority of an earlier self over a later one, and I need the sequence: which idea has to be in place before the next one can even be stated, and why each step depends on the one before it; the single case that should carry the whole ninety minutes; the misconceptions this audience will bring or form, with the precise point in the sequence at which each should be headed off; the one question to put at the end that would show the idea landed rather than that they can recite the rules; and what I should deliberately leave out, with a word to them about why I'm leaving it out.

The hard constraint is on the carrying case. I want both selves fully competent, so that the audience cannot escape into a competence rule. That excludes the familiar dementia case — the woman who years earlier said she would not want to live like that, and who now, in the disease, is cheerful and content — because its whole grip comes from the later self's incapacity; and it excludes the Ulysses-contract case, whose grip comes from the later self's impairment. I want a competent earlier self repudiated by an equally competent later self. So tell me what the case you choose must contain, and what it must not contain, if the session is not to collapse back into a lesson about capacity.

Assume I know the standard literature: skip the survey and spend your effort on the dependency structure and the misconception map. My guess is that the first misconception to kill is "honoring the directive simply is respecting autonomy, so the real clash is autonomy versus welfare" — tell me whether that is the right first one to kill, and in what order the rest arrive. Finally, say how confident you are in the plan, and single out the one claim in it you are least sure of.
