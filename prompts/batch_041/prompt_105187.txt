A pile of my gut calls about what a speaker said versus what they only meant. I want to know if one principle is behind them, or if I have to give one up.

1. My roommate walks in from the balcony in January and says 'It's cold out.' I take that as a request to shut the door, and I dont think he said it.
2. He reads my draft and says 'some of these paragraphs are good.' He meant not all of them. Not said.
3. 'My landlord's dog is huge but gentle.' The sentence conveys a contrast between the two, but it doesn't say there's one — you could use those words about a dog that's huge and gentle with no tension at all. Still, '...but gentle, though theres no tension between the two' sounds like a contradiction.
4. 'She took the job and moved to Tulsa.' Unlike case 3, I think the order is just part of what she said. But '...and moved to Tulsa, though not necessarily in that order' sounds perfectly fine to me.
5. 'Her old landlord called.' She never said she had a landlord, she let it be understood — and yet 'Her old landlord called, though she never had one' is contradictory.
6. I ask my kid to tidy up and she says 'I have homework.' I hear that as a refusal with a reason, not as a report about homework, and she could add 'not that that's why I'm not tidying' without contradicting herself. Meant, not said.
7. The one I flip on: my boss signs off with 'I'm sure you'll do great things.' Some days I hear a hint that I should start looking; other days its just a pleasantry. I genuinely can't tell if something was implicated there or if I'm reading it in.

I've read a little Grice, so my working principle is the cancellation one: if you can tack on 'but I don't mean to suggest that' without contradicting yourself, it was meant but not said; if the addition contradicts you, it was said. That gets 1, 2, and 6, and I'm fairly sure it kills 4, which is why I don't trust my call on 4. It also gets 3 and 5 wrong by my lights, and I have no idea what it does with 7.

So: is there a principle that gets all seven, or do I have to drop a verdict? Where mine fails, give me the repaired version and be straight about what it costs. Say which of my calls yours can't accommodate, and what it predicts for a case I haven't given: someone says 'thanks a lot' right after you spill coffee on them.

450 words max.
