I've been going in circles with two friends for like three months about whether clearing habitat is actually good or bad for wild animals, and I want you to tell me what kind of disagreement this even is, because we can't tell anymore.

Heres the shape of it. One of us says habitat destruction is obviously terrible — it kills a huge number of animals, wrecks populations, drives extinctions. The second says wait, most wild animals probaly have pretty grim lives anyway (starvation, predation, disease, exposure, parasites), and if you clear a forest you end up with fewer animals overall, so maybe there's less total suffering even though there's a lot of dying. The first says but the animals that die suffer horribly, while the ones that never get born aren't harmed by not existing, so you can't just count the missing animals as a benefit. Then someone says that just shows the whole comparison is between populations that don't share any individuals, so there's no baseline where "the same animals" are better or worse off — and we go around again.

We basically agree on the ecology: habitat loss kills a lot of individuals, shrinks populations, sometimes wipes species out. We agree suffering is bad. Nobody's budged. So give me a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this really an empirical standoff (we don't know whether wild animals' lives are net positive or negative)? A population-ethics fight (whether adding lives is good, bad, or neutral, and which comparison we're entitled to make — cleared land vs. intact forest vs. never existing at all)? A clash of two moral frameworks, one that cares about species and ecosystems as such and one that counts only individual welfare? A confusion between doing harm and letting it happen? Or are we just answering different questions under one name — one of us asking "is this bad for this animal", the other "is this bad on net"?

My working hypothesis is that it's mostly a population-ethics disagreement wearing an empirical costume, and the ecology is doing almost none of the work. Tell me if thats right, what each side would actually have to give up if it is, and what would count as progress here — a distinction, some measurement we could in principle make, a decision about which baseline to use — or whether the question is just underdetermined and we should stop expecting a clean answer.
