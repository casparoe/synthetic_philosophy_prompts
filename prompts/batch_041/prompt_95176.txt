My in-laws are Spanish and we just spent two weeks with them outside Granada, and I came home sort of wrecked by it. My wife's grandfather was on the losing side of the civil war, did time in prison, then spent the rest of his life not talking about it, and the family has mostly carried that forward -- the one aunt who brings it up gets treated like she's being deliberately rude. What got me is that this isn't just my in-laws being weird; its the whole country, or it was. After Franco died everyone agreed to look forward instead of back -- the amnesty, the so-called pact of forgetting -- and then forty-odd years later it all came apart anyway: the exhumations, the fights about the Valley of the Fallen, the memory law, Franco getting dug up and moved. So the forgetting didn't even work. It just postponed.

I've been reading Margalit on the ethics of memory and Rieff's In Praise of Forgetting, and I want to actually run the Spanish case through both of them, then run it back the other way. What does Margalit's duty to remember say about a national deal like that? Genuine question, because my understanding is that his duty attaches to people in thick relations to each other -- family, friends, that sort of thing -- and I honestly dont know whether a country of strangers counts, or whether that's exactly the contested bit. And does Spain vindicate Rieff or embarrass him? His line is roughly that the cult of memory does more harm than good and that forgetting is often the healthier default -- but here the forgetting didn't take, the past came back anyway and angrier, so I can't tell if that's evidence against him or just evidence that they did the forgetting wrong.

Then flip it: what does this case do to the theories? Where does Margalit have to stretch to reach it, where does Rieff, and where do both of them just go quiet because the case isn't the shape they were aiming at? And be concrete about who the rememberer and the forgetter actually are here -- the state, the family, the victim, the perpetrator's grandkid -- because I suspect that's doing most of the work and nobody at that table agrees on it.
