Six months ago I had a falling-out with my manager, and ever since I have had the persistent, uncomfortable sense that I can see the contempt in her face — not merely that I judge her to be contemptuous, but that her face now looks contemptuous to me the way a face plainly looks tired or surprised. A colleague insists I am only reading her uncharitably, and that if we could swap eyes I would see exactly the same neutral expression she does. I genuinely cannot tell whether my perception itself has changed or whether I am only interpreting an unchanged look differently, and it matters to me: if it is the latter, I feel I owe it to myself to stop and correct for it, whereas if I am truly seeing something, I am no longer sure what correcting would even mean. Two details that puzzle me: the impression is strong when she is across the table from me but absent when I look at a photograph of her taken before the falling-out, and it fades when I am rushed or distracted. Could you help me think this through as carefully as you can? Please begin by brainstorming the different things that might be going on here — I suspect there is a real philosophical distinction I am fumbling — then give me your best account of which is actually happening, what would count as evidence either way, and whether the line between seeing and interpreting is as clean as it feels. Take the role of a philosopher of perception with a solid grounding in empirical vision science, be concrete rather than gesturing vaguely at the power of expectations, and do not simply advise me to keep an open mind.
