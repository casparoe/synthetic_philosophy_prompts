im putting together a handout for a translation workshop and i want a poem to open it with. the theme is the thing Hofstadter spends hundreds of pages on in Le Ton beau de Marot: youve got a short poem with a fixed shape -- rhyme, a set meter, a line that keeps coming back -- and you have to carry it into another language, and you can save the shape or save the sense but saving both is where the bodies are buried. i dont want a poem that just talks about this, i want the form to actually do it. so: a poem of maybe 20 lines with a refrain that returns four or five times, and each time it comes back you change exactly one word -- a synonym swap, a substitution made only to hit the rhyme, a word picked because nothing else would scan. by the final return the line should look the most literal and have lost the most. keep it rhymed and metered the whole way, make the drift feel like moves a real translator would actually make, and make it hold up as a poem for someone who's never thought about translation at all. a translator should read it and wince.
