I sit on the marriage-recognition commission of a small jurisdiction whose statute says only that marriage is "the union of two persons for life." Our precedents so far: we recognized the marriage of two men in their seventies who married chiefly for companionship and to pool pensions, and our written reasons said that the absence of children, and the fact that the couple did not describe their bond as romantic or sexual, were not our concern; we recognized the marriage of a same-sex couple who had lived on different continents for years, on the ground that their mutual commitment and interdependence were genuine; we refused a marriage between a man and a woman who testified that they married only to obtain a residency permit and intended to dissolve it once it was granted; and we refused a three-person union, though our reasons stressed that one member had been pressured into it and that the household was not characterized by mutual care rather than that the number three was itself disqualifying. The petition before us is from two men, both asexual, who have been in a committed, exclusive, non-sexual partnership for a decade and now wish to marry, stating that they intend a lifelong union of care and shared life but no sexual relationship. Predict how we should rule if we are to be consistent with ourselves, identify the candidate rules our precedents might be running and what each predicts here, give your best guess with reasons, and name the further case that would best distinguish those rules.
