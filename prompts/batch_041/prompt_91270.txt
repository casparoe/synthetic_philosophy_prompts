I'm a fourth-year PhD student in formal epistemology, and I've agreed to contribute a piece of deadpan satire to a humor issue of our department's graduate journal. I'd like you to draft it.

The piece should be Section 7 of the Epistemic Compliance Manual of a fictional firm: an internal policy document governing how employees are to revise their credences when the evidence they receive is uncertain. The target of the satire is not a person but a practice — the habit, shared by a certain style of formal epistemology and by institutional life generally, of presenting a kinematics rule as a complete epistemology of uncertain evidence, a procedure that confers the look of rigor while leaving the substantive epistemic work unaddressed. The compliance frame should carry the joke: norms of rationality administered as HR policy, with forms, appeals, and a legal department that has opinions.

Keep it deadpan. The humor has to come out of the actual content rather than out of winking, so that a reader who has taught this material recognizes that the author understands it and finds the satire fair, while a reader who hasn't can still follow the joke about bureaucracy. In the manual's own flat idiom, the piece should get the following right:

- The rule itself: for a partition of the possibilities, the new credence in any proposition is the sum, over the cells, of the new credence assigned to that cell multiplied by the old conditional credence of the proposition given that cell, with the conditional credences within cells held fixed. The manual should present this as standard operating procedure for the redistribution of credence.
- The input problem: the new probabilities for the cells arrive from outside. The rule tells you how to redistribute, not what to redistribute on. Make this a clause the firm treats as a virtue — the firm issues procedures, not credences — while letting the joke expose what that leaves untouched.
- The limiting case: when some cell goes to probability 1, the procedure collapses into ordinary conditionalization, so the uncertain-evidence case is the general one and the certain-evidence case its degenerate instance. Get this right, and if you can, have the manual draw the wrong institutional moral from it.
- The certainty trap: employees who assigned probability 1 to something the firm later needs to deny are beyond help; give this a clause (the regularity point sometimes called Cromwell's rule) and a grievance procedure for those who have run afoul of it.
- Partition choice: the result depends on which partition is selected, so the manual should require the partition to be filed before updating and leave the choice to professional judgment.
- One worked example: a compliance scenario in which an employee is, say, 60/40 about the accuracy of an internal report and must update without becoming certain either way, walked through in the manual's numbered prose.
- One clause in which the legal department concedes, in asides, that the promised immunity from a diachronic Dutch book is not a property unique to the firm's official procedure.

Roughly 700 words of parody, numbered as clauses, with a title and a one-line mission statement for the office. No citations, no bibliography, no reading lists, and no real scholars or living people as targets; the firm and its officers should be invented. Don't gesture at a literature the reader is assumed to know — the technical content has to be carried by the manual itself.

Then close with a short editorial note, clearly separated from the parody and in your own voice, that sorts what is settled from what is genuinely contested: the form of the rule and the rigidity condition on one side, against the things people who work on this actually disagree about — whether the rule deserves to be called a rule of learning from experience at all rather than a coherence constraint on any shift one might choose to make, whether rigidity is a substantive requirement or an artifact of how the problem is set up, whether immunity from a diachronic Dutch book is distinctive of this procedure, and whether updating with no new experience is a defect of the account or simply what radical probabilism amounts to. Do not present any of these as the consensus. Say plainly where the parody exaggerates or quietly takes a side.
