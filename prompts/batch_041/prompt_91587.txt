Next month I'm giving a paper at a small two-day workshop our cognitive science institute is running on minds and machines, and I want to be ready for the discussion, not just for the talk. The audience is not AI ethics people. Its mostly the animal cognition crowd — people doing nociception work on cephalopods and crustaceans, a couple of bird-cognition folks — plus two RL researchers from the CS side who think the whole "AI welfare" conversation is anthropomorphic nonsense, and one very senior comparative psychologist who has spent forty years insisting you can't infer anything about experience from behaviour or from internal goings-on you can't independently validate. Here's what I'm defending: what makes a system morally considerable is neither its substrate nor its functional architecture as such, but what I call the stake condition. Some of its internal states have to be answerable to the system's own continuation — they were shaped (by selection, by learning, by design) because of what happened to the system when they occurred, and they still function to correct for threats to that system's persistence or integrity. Feeling isn't built into the account anywhere. So a bacterium squeaks past, a mouse passes easily, a thermostat doesn't (its states serve the room, not itself), and a large model trained on text with a reward signal indifferent to whether the model keeps running fails the condition — however articulate it is, and whatever it says about its own inner life. I also think the status comes in degrees, which is going to get me in trouble somewhere. A colleague who read the draft said it sounds like the old idea that a trait's function is whatever it was selected for, wearing a new coat; I don't think that's right but I need a better answer than "I don't think that's right."

You know this area far better than I do — animal sentience, philosophy of mind, the function debates — so I'd like you to write me my Q&A prep sheet, and think first about who in that room will care about what before you start drafting. Order them by how much damage they'd do me, say which ones have real force (I would much rather concede something early than get cornered on it), give me a roughly 60-second reply to each with one line on where that reply is softest, and tell me the one question I'm praying nobody asks.
