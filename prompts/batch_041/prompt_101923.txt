I sit on the graduate curriculum committee of a physics department. A proposed cross-listed seminar, Probability Without Collapse, has been submitted for approval. Its instructor proposes to teach that the Born rule can be derived from the many-worlds interpretation by a symmetry argument about branch counting, and that this derivation is now the standard view; the syllabus says students who still worry about probability are in the grip of an obsolete Copenhagen picture. A reviewer on the committee objects that the probability problem in Everettian quantum mechanics remains open, that branch-counting arguments either presuppose equiprobability or equivocate between branch count and measure, and that approving the seminar as written would present a contested research program as settled. The committee must decide at its next meeting, in two weeks.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before that meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a clear time frame for any revisions, and it should engage the reviewer's strongest point seriously rather than dismissing it as Copenhagen nostalgia. In doing so, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; reason from the ideas themselves.
