ok so ive ended up with a view i cant shake and i want to know what would actually count against it, not the stock objections i can already answer. here it is. im an occasionalist, roughly al-ghazali's version: no created thing has any causal power of its own, God directly brings about every event, and the regularities we call laws of nature are just God's habit of making one sort of thing follow another. the necessity we think we feel in a causal connection isnt out there in the things — its just the constancy of the habit. so far so ash'arite, i know.

two things i dont want to give up though. first, i think physics is discovering real structure in the world, not just summarizing what we happened to observe. second, i think that when i say the fire burnt the cotton im saying something literally true, not a useful fiction.

what i want: what would have to be true for this whole thing to be wrong? not that its unfalsifiable — i already know no regularity can touch it, since any regularity is just God's habit. i mean the deeper stuff. does it predict anything a rival doesnt? is it really different from saying events are regularly conjoined, with God bolted on as a fifth wheel? do my two add-ons actually fit, or am i quietly a humean with extra steps? and heres the case that bothers me: suppose God changed his habit tomorrow and fire just stopped burning, forever. nothing in the world would be violated on my view — wed just call it a new law. if nothing could ever tell us the difference between a world where God is holding it all together and one where he isnt, is my view doing any work at all?

rank the objections by how much damage they do, say which ones a defender like me would have to concede rather than explain away, and tell me honestly if theres anything that would move me. the thing that would move me most is a rival that explains the regularity better than God's habit does, so tell me if one exists.
