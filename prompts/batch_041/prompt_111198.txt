ok so i'm trying to write a position piece on generations and i want a consistency check before i embarrass myself. here's where i've landed. i'm a realist about social kinds: if a category supports projectible generalizations — you can make successful predictions about new cases — and earns its keep in causal explanations, it's real, even if it's historically contingent and fuzzy at the edges. i think generations in the sociological sense are real in that way: cohorts whose formative years were shaped by a shared set of events and conditions. i think the pop labels — boomer, millennial, gen z — are mostly marketing constructs and i don't want to be a realist about those. i take the age-period-cohort identification problem seriously: age, period and cohort are linearly dependent, so you can't just read cohort effects off a cross-sectional difference, and alot of generational talk is unfalsifiable. but i still think there are genuine cohort effects in political socialization — a big formative event leaves a durable imprint that shows up later. i also think social kinds are real partly because of a looping effect: once people are sorted into a generation, they start to act in ways that make the label more true. and i think a real social kind has to be one that people themselves recognize and use to understand their own lives. i'm not sure which of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do. can you audit the set, order the real tensions by how bad they are, say for each what would have to give or what the cheapest patch is, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. no headings, bullets, bold or tables please, just write it out like you're explaining it to me in an email.
