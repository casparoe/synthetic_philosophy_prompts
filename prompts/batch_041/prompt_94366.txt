I write about science and ideas for a small outlet, and a colleague on the same beat just put out a newsletter piece on the 'gender-equality paradox' that I've been asked to respond to on a panel. Here's his argument as he states it. Study after study finds that the more gender-equal a country is, the fewer women go into STEM — the places with the best records on women's rights, Finland and Sweden and Norway, are the ones where women are least likely to become engineers. He calls this a natural experiment in what women choose when nothing is holding them back, and says it shows the STEM gap comes down to differences in what men and women are interested in, not discrimination or culture; he adds that the pattern even shows up in countries that only became more equal in the last couple of decades, which he says rules out culture for good. From there he draws three conclusions: the money we spend on diversity programs in STEM is wasted, the gap is really a sign that women in these countries are free, and egalitarian societies paradoxically produce more gendered choices — which he says is a problem for anyone who assumed equality and gender-blindness go together.

I have a feeling that most of his indivdual sentences are the kind of thing you could find in a press release, but that the whole thing doesn't add up, and I can't put my finger on where. Go through it and tell me what someone who actually works on this would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong. I definately want to know whether his three conclusions survive once the shaky bits are fixed, and whether 'fewer women go into STEM' is even the right way to describe the finding. No reading list, thanks — I've got the studies, I just want the audit. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and don't restate my question.
