My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether common knowledge of rationality is doing any real work in game theory, and it ended in a stalemate: I kept insisting it is the load-bearing assumption behind backward induction and the epistemic foundations of Nash equilibrium, and she kept insisting that the assumption is either so weak that no real agents ever satisfy it or so strong that it silently presupposes the solution concept it is supposed to justify. I would like to convert the argument into something productive. Suppose I had a year, a couple of collaborators, and no teaching, and meant to spend it on epistemic game theory — what are the genuinely open questions in the area, and which should come first? I care less about the inventory than about the ordering: for each candidate, say why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is with the tools that already exist, and whether settling it would change anything outside the technical literature or merely tidy up a formalism. One specific worry I want you to place in the ranking: whether common knowledge of rationality is even the right target for computationally bounded agents, or whether the fixed-point, possible-worlds formulation should be replaced by something defined over the agents' programs or strategies — is that a real frontier or a red herring? And tell me what evidence or result over the next year or two should move each item up or down the list.
