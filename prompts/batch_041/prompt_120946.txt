Hofstadter's careenium is a frictionless billiard table crowded with countless tiny magnetic marbles — simms — that clump into larger, slower-moving aggregates, simmballs; the simmballs have their own dynamics, and on the higher-level view the simms recede into the background. Hofstadter uses this to argue that the symbolic level is not a mere epiphenomenal shadow of the marble level: simmball patterns genuinely constrain which simms go where, so it makes sense to say that the higher level pushes the lower one around, and that this is the sense in which a self or a symbol can be a real cause. I want an audit of the analogy as an argument for that conclusion. Give the correspondence table — what plays the role of the simms, the simmballs, the magnetic forces, the billiard-table walls, and the observer's higher-level view — then say where the mapping breaks. In particular: is the simmball's influence on its constituent simms anything more than the sum of the simm-simm forces, and if not, does that undermine the claim that symbols push the substrate rather than merely redescribe it? Does the analogy need the simmballs to be causally autonomous in a way the physics forbids, or can it get the conclusion from constraint and boundary-setting alone? Finally, tell me whether the intended conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and what a better analogy would be if this one cannot carry it.
