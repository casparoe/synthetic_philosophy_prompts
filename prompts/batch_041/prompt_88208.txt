Is there a name for this? I keep running into it in medicine and social science and I've stopped being sure it's the same thing each time. The pattern: you build a classification scheme, it gets used to sort people, and the sorting itself changes the people being sorted, so that a few years later the scheme fits better than it did at the start -- not because it was refined, but because being sorted into a category nudged people toward becoming better examples of it. Two instances that bug me. (1) A screening questionnaire gets rolled out; the people who score above the cutoff start reading about the diagnosis, reorganize how they describe their own feelings around it, and the next round of screening finds more of them, which is taken as evidence the questionnaire is sensitive rather than as evidence it's now measuring a different population than the one it was built on. (2) A category that once picked out a small, distinctive group ends up, after a couple of decades of being applied generously, naming a broad and ordinary cluster of traits -- and nobody can tell whether the underlying condition changed, the boundary moved, or the population moved to meet the boundary. The part I find unsettling is that from inside the practice this looks like the scheme working, not like the scheme helping to manufacture its own evidence; there's no dishonest step anywhere, just a loop where each pass makes the next pass look more accurate.

I know Hacking wrote about this general area, and "looping effect" is the phrase I'd reach for, but I can't tell whether that's exactly what I'm describing or something adjacent. So: is there an established term for the specific configuration where the classified population drifts toward the classification without anyone intending it and without the drift being visible as drift? If it's just the looping effect under another name, say so and tell me which parts of Hacking's version I'm getting wrong. If it's something narrower, name it and define it, and tell me how it differs from the looping effect, from a self-fulfilling prophecy, and from ordinary measurement reactivity (where people change just because they know they're being observed -- I don't think that's quite it, because here the change persists and feeds back into the scheme itself). I'd rather have the distinctions drawn sharply than get a label, so please don't stop at the name. And keep separate what's actually settled in the literature from what's contested -- I have a feeling the existence of these loops is agreed on and almost everything about how they work is not, but I don't know if that's right.
