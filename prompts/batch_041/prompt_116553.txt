So I've gotten into fishing this summer — mostly catch and release on a small river near me — and the more I learn the rules the less I understand what they're actually for. Depending on the season, the species, and the size of the fish, the rules sometimes *require* me to put it back and sometimes require me to keep it, and everyone calls the whole system conservation. There's also this elaborate etiquette around the fish themselves: wet your hands first, don't touch the gills, use barbless hooks, hold it in the current till it kicks off, ten seconds out of the water max. But then the same people will kill an invasive crayfish or a mink without a second thought, because it's invasive, and nobody blinks. And when a fish swallows the hook deep or is bleeding, the advice is basically just keep it, it's going to die anyway. Also there's this thing where the wild is supposed to be the wild — you don't "interfere," you don't feed them, you don't rescue the injured one you can see — which sits oddly next to all the hands-on care in the unhooking.

What I want to know is what view of value the whole practice is quietly built on. Is it that only populations and stocks and species matter and individual fish don't? Is it that a fish's suffering just doesn't count, and the etiquette is only about not damaging the resource? Or is it something about wild animals being outside the moral community in a way pets and farm animals aren't? Can you reconstruct the implicit theory, and seperate the parts the practice actually *needs* from the parts that are just a comfortable fit? Where's it underdetermined — could several different theories license all of this, and if so which ones?

Then the harder bit: if you swapped in a view where the suffering of individual wild animals was the thing that mattered, what would have to change? Not just the fishing — what does the current setup assume about the wild in general that a wild-animal-welfare view has to deny?

One request: seperate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested, and don't sell me a contested view as the consensus. I suspect a lot of what I'd be told here is one camp's line dressed up as the obvious answer.
