I am a retired physiologist. For most of my career I worked on homeostatic and neuroendocrine feedback systems — setpoints, sensors, effectors, the whole apparatus by which an organism keeps a regulated variable near a reference value — and I have spent the last several years, with more enthusiasm than discipline, teaching myself moral philosophy and the literature on AI alignment. I would like your help with a translation problem, and I want it done rigorously rather than generously.

Please take on the role of someone who is at once a control engineer and a philosopher of mind and action: fluent in the vocabulary of reference signals, plant and controller, sensor and effector, disturbance rejection, model mismatch, and the corruption of a feedback loop, and equally fluent in the vocabulary of hedonism and desire-satisfaction, of intrinsic versus instrumental value, and of the difference between an agent's objective and its proxy for that objective. Think carefully before you write. Open with a short brainstorm in which you list the candidate correspondences between the two vocabularies — including the ones you suspect will not hold — and only then commit to a translation.

The material to be translated is the cluster of ideas usually gathered under wireheading and reward tampering: the thought that a system able to act on its own reward signal will act on it directly rather than earn it through the world; the distinction between tampering with the signal and tampering with the world so that the signal merely looks right; the claim that this is not an exotic failure but a structural consequence of optimizing a proxy; and the older philosophical question of whether a life of maximally stimulated pleasure is a good life or a defective one. Restate all of this in my vocabulary — homeostasis and feedback control — as faithfully as you can.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which claims translate cleanly, so that I can see the philosophical point as a familiar engineering fact? Which translate only with distortion, and what exactly is lost or added in the distortion? And which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? For that last category, tell me whether the gap reflects a defect in the control-theoretic framework — something it cannot represent — or whether it is a sign that the original philosophical debate is less clear than its participants suppose. I am particularly interested in one seam: whether the difference between an agent that rewires its own sensors and an agent that changes the world is a deep distinction or merely a difference in where the feedback loop is cut, and what the philosophical debate needs that distinction to do.

Do not flatter the translation. If the two frameworks are not isomorphic, say where and why, and do not import control-theoretic assumptions and then present them as if they were already contained in the philosophy. Close with a verdict on whether I should keep both vocabularies or abandon one.
