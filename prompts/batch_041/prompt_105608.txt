My brother-in-law cornered me at Christmas with this argument and I still can't tell if he's got me or not. He's one of those blokes who reads a single book and becomes unbearable. Anyway, here it is roughly as he gave it:

(1) A fair society is one where your chances in life don't depend on the family you were born into.
(2) Inheritance is the purest case of your chances depending on your family.
(3) So a fair society would tax inheritances away to nothing.
(4) But a head start is only worth anything because other people don't have it — if everyone inherited the same amount, nobody would be ahead. What's really being passed on is a ranking, and you can't make everyone win a ranking.
(5) And families hand on loads besides money: contacts, confidence, a posh accent, a parent who reads to you, and genes. None of that can be taxed away.
(6) To cancel all of it you'd have to take kids off their parents and raise everyone in the same institution.
(7) Nobody wants that, so the idea in (1) is a fantasy and inheritance tax is just envy with good PR.

He reckons (7) follows from (1)–(6) and that I'm the naive one for disagreeing. I really want to keep (1) — it seems obviously right to me — but I can't find the dodgy step on my own. Where does it actually go wrong, and if something has to give, what's the cheapest thing to drop? Please answer in British English.
