The twist is that I want them to consider whether Bentham's own appeal to "the general happiness" ends up relying on a background conception of what is natural or fitting in human life, which his utilitarianism officially disowns.

Please write the essay question itself, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that seperates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain -- e.g., an A answer must say why the secondary mischief argument is not just a empirical prediction but a normative one about the kind of society we want, while a B answer will just say "Bentham thought it was harmless." And for the marking scheme, give a one-line note on how candidates typically get each point wrong. I'm aiming for a 60-minute exam question, so the model answer should be doable in that time if you know the material.
