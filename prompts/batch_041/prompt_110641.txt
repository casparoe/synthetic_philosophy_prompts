Please explain a conceptual issue in the theory of liberty that textbook treatments of the positive/negative distinction usually pass over: whether being unfree requires that someone actually constrain you, or whether it is enough that someone holds an unaccountable power to do so. To keep the discussion concrete, fix two terms. Call a constraint occurrent when it is actually imposed on some option of yours, and standing when nothing is imposed but someone retains an unaccountable capacity to impose something. The test case is unexercised power: a worker whose employer could block any of her choices at will, need not justify doing so to her, and never in fact does — contrasted with a worker who is obstructed often, but only by rules she has an effective say in making.

Answer in exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. (1) Say what the occurrent/standing distinction amounts to and why the two workers are meant to come apart on it. (2) Explain why an account of freedom as the absence of actual interference must deny that the first worker is unfree, and how it might try to absorb her case by counting a constraint as present whenever interference would obtain in nearby possible worlds; then say whether that absorption succeeds. (3) Explain the non-domination condition that the unexercised-power case is built to isolate, and whether the relevant arbitrariness is a matter of the power not tracking the agent's interests, of its not being answerable to her, or something else — using a case that pressures each reading. (4) Explain what turns on the dispute: in particular, whether the Hobbesian observation that law is itself interference forces one side to concede that law as such reduces liberty, and whether freedom here is better understood as a feature of a person's options or of her status. (5) State the strongest objection that the standing-unfreedom side is tracking security or status rather than freedom, and say whether the distinction can be drawn so as to survive it.
