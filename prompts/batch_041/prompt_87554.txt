Here's the thing I keep tripping over. In genomics there's a recurring fight about how much of the human genome is functional. A lot of evolutionary biologists went ballistic and said no, that isn't function, that's just chemistry happening; real function means the sequence is there because selection kept it there, and the conserved part of the genome is a small fraction of that.

But then the same field does the opposite thing constantly. In comparative genomics you call a region functional precisely because it's conserved across species, and if it isnt conserved you write it off as nonfunctional even when it's plainly doing something. And in anatomy everybody says the appendix is vestigial, that we don't know what it's for — which is odd, because its got lymphoid tissue in it and clearly does something in the body.

So: what do these practices presuppose?
