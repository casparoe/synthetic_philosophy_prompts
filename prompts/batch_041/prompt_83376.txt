I'm a second-year student in a course on the social construction of kinds — race, gender, money, and the like — and I keep running into a pattern in the material that I can't match to any single concept we've been given, so I'd like your help pinning it down.

Consider two cases. In the first, a category is a mark of disrepute — say a stigmatized diagnosis, or a criminal classification. Because being placed in it is costly, people who might fall under it avoid being recorded, and those who are recorded learn to hide the tell-tale signs; when cases do surface, they tend to cluster in families and tight communities where concealment fails. The category therefore looks rare, sharply bounded, and clustered — the profile of a natural kind.

In the second case, a category is a mark of honor or belonging — an admired ancestry, a prestigious credential, a fashionable identity. Because being placed in it is advantageous, people claim it and adopt its markers. The category therefore looks common, fuzzy, and diffuse — the profile of something merely conventional, or of "everyone is a little bit this."

What puzzles me is that the underlying move looks the same in both: a classification reshapes the people it classifies, and those people then generate the very evidence used to justify the classification. Yet the two cases produce opposite signatures — rarity and sharpness versus ubiquity and blur — apparently turning only on whether the category is shameful or prized. And in each case the signature is then read back as a fact about the category's nature, when it is downstream of the social process.

So: is there an established name for this valence-dependence — for a single feedback loop between a classification and the classified that runs in opposite directions depending on whether the category is stigmatized or valued, and thereby manufactures opposite kinds of evidence about its own naturalness? If there is, tell me what it is and exactly which parts of what I've described it covers and which it leaves out. I suspect the nearest things I know — self-fulfilling prophecies, and the general idea that classifications of people loop back on the people classified — each capture only one direction or drop the valence entirely, and I'd like you to say whether that suspicion is right. If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it in a sentence, and give me two cases that fall just outside its boundary so I can see where the edge is. I'm not asking for a reading list; what I want is the carving — what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where it stops. Please keep the whole answer under 400 words.
