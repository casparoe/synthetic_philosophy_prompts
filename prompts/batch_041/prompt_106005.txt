My friend and I just spent most of a night arguing about whether things persist by being wholly present at each moment or by having temporal parts, and I came away unable to tell weather we disagree about anything at all. We agree about every fact I could name — what's on the table, what happened to the cup yesterday, what we'd each say if it got squashed — and still we kept talking past each other: he says the cup is one thing that endures, I say it's a four-dimensional worm and 'the cup' is just a stage of it, and neither of us could produce a case the other felt obliged to explain differently. The part that really jammed us was how one and the same wholly present cup can be hot at one time and cold at another without being both hot and cold simpliciter; he says that's just relativizing to a time, I say that's either trivial or a quiet retreat. My hypothesis is that this isn't really a fight about what exists so much as a fight about what 'the same thing' at two times is supposed to mean, and that we're each counting in a different way and calling the other's count wrong. So: is this a verbal dispute, two people answering different questions under one heading, or a genuine disagreement about what there is? And whatever it is, is there anything that would actually move it — a distinction, a case, a formal result — or is it just permanently stuck, and why?
