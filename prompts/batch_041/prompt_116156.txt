for a reading group thats finishing the Phenomenology i need a short poem, two voices, master and bondsman, no labels -- people should be able to tell who's speaking from what they say alone. heres the constraint i want, and its the whole point: every stanza is a two-line unit split between the voices, one line each, and a rhyme can only ever be completed across the gap between them, so neither voice is ever allowed to rhyme with itself. a rhyme only exists if the other one answers it. the masters lines should end on things -- what he holds, consumes, enjoys -- and the slaves on doings, shaping, forming, the work; because thats the actual hinge, the master only reaches the object through the slave and so gets no satisfaction from it, while the slave finds himself again in what hes made, but only one-sidedly, and only after the fear of death has shaken him apart. dont let the slave come out as a hero either, what he wins isnt mutual. then break the rule at the end: the master goes quiet before the last rhyme closes and the slaves final line is left hanging unrhymed. i dont want a resolution -- on my reading that section doesnt land in mutual recognition at all, it slides off into the unhappy consciousness, and the poem should stop where it stops. 20-30 lines, no fixed meter.
