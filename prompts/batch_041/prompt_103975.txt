I'm drafting a position statement for a working group on advance directives and the authority of the earlier self, and I'd like the package stress-tested before I circulate it. This is roughly where I have landed:

(1) The authority of an advance directive rests on the value of autonomy, understood as a person's interest in having their life as a whole go according to their own settled conception of how it should go.

(2) That interest is held by the person while competent and reaches forward in time, so a valid directive binds a later stretch of life even when the person can by then no longer form or revise any such conception.

(3) Autonomy is a matter of self-governance and not merely of having preferences: a preference counts as autonomous only if the agent can reflect on it and endorse it. Preferences voiced in advanced dementia, where that capacity has lapsed, are therefore not autonomous preferences.

(4) All the same, the contentment or distress of the demented patient is a genuine welfare interest of the very same person and must be weighed against the directive; where honoring the directive would impose substantial and unremitting distress, the directive should give way.

(5) Personal identity consists in psychological continuity, and continuity comes in degrees; there is no fact of the matter about the precise point at which the earlier person ends and another begins.

(6) I reject the move that recasts the demented patient as a different person from the one who wrote the directive, and with it the inference that the directive forfeits its authority once dementia is severe.

Please audit this rather than restate it. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what that concession would cost. I'm more interested in discriminating among the tensions than in a flat list, so make clear where the real pressure lies; finish by naming the single revision that would relieve the most pressure at the least cost.
