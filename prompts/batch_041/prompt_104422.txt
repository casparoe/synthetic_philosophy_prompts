I'm a novelist, and in a few weeks I'm on a panel where I plan to defend something unfashionable: that what a novel means is fixed by what its author meant. Not my private plan while I was writing though — I've been calling the relevant thing the design intention, meaning the intention a competent reader can reasonably attribute to me that makes the whole text cohere, and i think that's a public fact about the book, recoverable from the book itself and from the rest of my work (the corpus, not my diary). The room will be mostly lit-crit poststructuralists who think intention is a fiction we killed off decades ago, plus a couple of analytic philosophers of language who'll want to drag me into Grice and speaker-meaning and probably think I'm re-deriving their wheel badly. Write me my Q&A prep: what each side will actually throw at me, in their own vocabulary, ordered by how much damage it does, which objections have real force and which are just tribal, what I should concede up front, a two-minute reply to each, where the reply is soft, and — the one I'm definately dreading — the question about whether my own characters can end up meaning more than I intended them to.
