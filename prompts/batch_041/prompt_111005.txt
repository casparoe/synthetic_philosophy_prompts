For a dissertation chapter on personal identity and mind uploading, I want the adequacy conditions for a theory of personal identity written out before I take up any more of the accounts on offer — that is, what a theory of what we are has to deliver if it is to be usable on gradual replacement at all. Give me the desiderata: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the background facts it must not contradict, each with the consideration that motivates it, and say of each whether it is non-negotiable or a commitment of one camp. The standard cases — fission, teletransportation, the ship of Theseus, the one-step scan-and-copy — I take as read; I want the weight placed on these instead:

- neuron-by-neuron replacement of a brain by functionally equivalent silicon, at whatever tempo, with no detectable moment of transition and no behavioural discontinuity;
- a variant in which each original unit is not destroyed but kept running in parallel throughout, so that both systems are active and both have an equal claim to the memories;
- a variant in which no new parts are introduced at all: the same matter is disassembled, held apart, and reassembled in the same arrangement;
- and a variant in which the mechanism doing the replacing is itself gradually replaced while it works, so that nothing in the situation stays fixed long enough to be the thing that does the preserving.

For each requirement say why it is one; then tell me which single requirement excludes the most candidate theories, and which pair looks jointly unsatisfiable. I suspect the real trouble is not any one case but the collision between (i) there being a determinate fact about who survives and (ii) the theory having to treat original and replacement symmetrically — I want to know whether that is right, or whether the pair that actually collides is a different one. A brief scoring of the obvious contenders against the list — neo-Lockean psychological continuity, animalism, and a pattern or four-dimensional view — is welcome, but keep it to a few lines; the list is the deliverable. Close with a final line beginning "Verdict:" naming the requirement, or pair, that no adequate account can satisfy without giving something up.
