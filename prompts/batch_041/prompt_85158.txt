ok, need to get this out of my head before friday. I sit on the advisory committee a state senator put together on the filial-support bill — the one that would make adult kids legally on the hook for a slice of a parent's nursing-home or home-care costs, with a hardship exemption that the county has to run. She thinks it just writes down what families already owe each other. Committee's split down the middle and I'm the one drafting the recommendation we vote on Thursday and then send to her staff.

Two things I need it to do.

First, the rationale. The bill's preamble leans on a real idea and I want you to test it rather than restate it: that being someone's child isn't a feeling, it's a role, and roles carry duties; that the duty is graded — you owe your own more than you owe strangers; and that this is a fit basis for a law, not just for sermons. The draft actually invokes the older version of the thought, that you start with your own elders and then extend the same treatment outward to everyone else's, and I want to know whether that extension is doing any work or wether the graded part is just what the sponsors wanted to hear. The other half of the committee says impartiality is the only defensible baseline — need should route the money, not DNA. (They keep dragging in the old Mohist objection to Confucius, which I'm not convinced either side actually understands.)

Second, be concrete, because the draft defines the duty by role and then never says who holds it. Who counts as a child — adopted, step, the one raised by her grandmother because dad was inside, the one who hasn't spoken to him in twenty years? What happens when the siblings' incomes are wildly different? And half the committee thinks this lands hardest on daughters and on the poorest kids, and that a virtue you can't pay for quietly turns into a debt you can. Don't paper over that — I need the strongest version of it in the memo even if we recommend passing.

Give me a bottom line: pass, pass with amendments, or kill. If it's amendments, write the actual language for the definition section and the exemption, and tell me which of these two cases the amended bill should be able to handle and which it should carve out: (a) a daughter whose father left when she was two and who is now the only solvent relative; (b) three siblings, one a nurse earning 60k, two on disability.

It's a memo, so keep it short enough that the senator's staff will actually read it, and give me the reasoning rather than a survey of the literature.
