I'm a second-year PhD working on the metaphysics of time and I've been roped into doing a session for the film-and-philosophy course here. I want to run Nolan's Memento through Borges's views on time — not the usual memory-and-identity angle, but the harder one: Borges's claim, out of Berkeley and Hume, in the essay where he tries to refute time outright, that there's no real succession, only isolated presents, and that the self persisting through them is a fiction too. Leonard looks like that thesis made flesh: no continuity, a self reassembled from notes and tattoos, each moment sealed off from the one before. Give me a reading that takes the film and the philosophy equally seriously — where does it dramatise the Borges view better than his own prose does, and where does it quietly need something he denies, like a persisting someone who can be wronged, desert that survives across time, a revenge plot that only holds up if the man punished now is the man who did it? And is the reverse-chronology structure doing any real philosophical work or is it just a trick? If you think the film ends up refuting Borges rather than illustrating him, say so — I'd much rather have the awkward reading then a tidy one.
