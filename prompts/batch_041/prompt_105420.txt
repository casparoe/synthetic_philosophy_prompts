My old metaphysics lecturer's handout on Nozick says this, and I copied it down almost word for word: 'Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge is justified true belief plus a fourth condition -- the belief has to track the truth, i.e. it couldn't easily have been false. The extra condition is externalist, so you don't need to know that you know. And because tracking is closed under known entailment, Nozick can turn the skeptic's argument around: you know you have hands, you know that entails you're not a brain in a vat, so you know you're not a brain in a vat. The one bullet he bites is the grandmother who'd believe her grandson was well no matter what the family told her; Nozick has to say she knows, which shows tracking is too weak, so he ends up a fallibilist who rescues ordinary knowledge at the price of letting some lucky true beliefs count as knowledge.'

The overall shape seems right to me but there's alot riding on that closure sentence and I have a nagging feeling it's backwards, and the 'justified true belief plus a fourth condition' bit doesn't sit right either. Go through it claim by claim: what would someone who actually defends Nozick nod along to, what's a distortion, and does the last sentence -- that he saves ordinary knowledge by counting lucky beliefs as knowledge -- actually follow from the parts that survive? Also flag anything else I should be suspicious of even if it sounds authoritative.
