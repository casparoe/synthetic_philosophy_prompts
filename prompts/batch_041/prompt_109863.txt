I am a doctoral candidate working on hinge epistemology, and I want to settle a long-running disagreement with a colleague by getting a number I can argue with rather than a survey of positions. The proposition I am asking you to price is this: some transitions between hinge frameworks are epistemically rational, in the sense that they are licensed by standards of justification available to the community undergoing the transition, and are not merely causally or pragmatically explicable — a matter of coercion, conversion, drift, or a change in what the community happened to care about. Give me two credences: (a) that such a transition is possible in principle, and (b) that at least one actual historical transition was of that kind.

Work with at least the following terms, adding or deleting as the problem requires, and tell me which you deleted and why:

1. Content. Are hinge commitments propositional enough to stand in justificatory relations at all, or are they enacted certainties of a kind that reasons cannot govern? If the latter, say plainly whether the target proposition is false or merely ill-formed.
2. Redescription. The riverbed passage in On Certainty (§§96–99) leaves the division between bed and current deliberately unsharp: "The mythology may change back into a state of flux, the river-bed of thoughts may shift. But I distinguish between the movement of the waters on the river-bed and the shift of the bed itself; though there is not a sharp division of the one from the other." For any alleged hinge change, what is the probability that it survives redescription as a movement of the water rather than a shift of the bed?
3. Non-circularity. Can a hinge be revised by reasons that do not already presuppose it, or does every candidate justification for a hinge-level transition borrow the very certainty under revision?
4. The bridge. Can predictive success, anomaly reduction, or coherence with neighbouring certainties count as epistemic reason for a hinge, or does treating it that way change the subject from justification to expedience?
5. Determinacy. Is there a fact of the matter about whether a given historical transition was licensed by the community's own standards, or is that phrase doing no work? Do not average over readings silently; price the indeterminacy as a term.
6. Case. Taking the abandonment of absolute simultaneity, or of Euclidean certainty about physical space, as your candidate: how likely is it that this was a genuine hinge change, and how likely that it was reason-driven rather than interest-driven?

For each term give a number and one line of justification, then state your combination rule, the term your final figure is most sensitive to, and your least confident term. My own credence in (a) is about 0.30 and my colleague's is about 0.75; tell me which term that gap most likely lives in.

I also hold a stronger view I would like priced separately — the asymmetry thesis: hinge commitments can be rationally lost but never rationally acquired, since acquisition proceeds by accretion or habituation rather than by a community's recognising a reason, whereas relinquishment can be reason-governed. Give me your credence in that, and say whether accepting it should lower (a), (b), or neither.

Finally, tell me which single term, if it moved, would move (a) most, what evidence or argument would move it, and what would count as settling this question rather than merely redistributing the weights.
