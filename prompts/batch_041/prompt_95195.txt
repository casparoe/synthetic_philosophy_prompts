At the same time, the same panel will happily treat a statement as known if someone points out that it's the Gödel sentence of a theory we accept and that we can see it's true even though the theory can't prove it. So we seem to trust human insight that goes beyond any formal system, but we don't trust a machine that checks a formal proof we could in principle survey. What philosophical commitments does that practice presuposes? Does it presuppose that mathematical knowledge has to be first-personal, or human-surveyable, or something like that?
