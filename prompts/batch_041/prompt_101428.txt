Bit of an odd request. I spent 35 years as a geochemist and retired a couple years back, and to keep the brain busy I've been going to a philosophy reading group at the library. We just did a few sessions on free will and blame, and I came away more confused than I went in — in a good way, I think.

What struck me is that everyone in that room was answering a different question than everyone else, and nobody noticed. One fella kept saying that if my neurons did what they did because of the big bang, then I can't be blamed for anything. Another said the whole thing is a red herring because blaming and praising are just things we do to each other, and asking whether they're deserved is like asking whether a handshake is deserved. A third thought determinism was simply false. And our host kept going on about reactive attitudes and insisting you can't even ask whether a person is responsible without first asking whether the practice of holding people responsible is one we want to keep around.

So I want to build the group a proper decision tree — a flowchart that takes somebody from a small number of plain commitments and drops them out at a named position. Not a list of -isms. I want the branches to be questions a person can actually answer without already knowing the labels. Like a dichotomous key, which is what we used to ID minerals back in the day: you don't need to know the mineral to walk the key, you just answer the questions and it falls out.

Start from something almost anyone would grant — say, people sometimes do things on purpose and we sometimes get angry or grateful at them for it — and branch from there. Each node should be one question with two, maybe three, answers. The questions need to be the kind where the two sides of the debate genuinely part ways, and I'd rather they be concrete cases than abstractions. So don't ask "are you a compatibilist?"; ask whether a man raised from birth in a cult that made him cruel should be resented the same way as a man who chose cruelty for fun, and let his answer route him.

Things I want the tree to be able to land on as leaves (these are roughly the positions floating around the group, so put them in, or tell me why a question can't reach them):
- determinism is true and it kills responsibility, full stop
- determinism is false, and that's what saves responsibility
- determinism is beside the point because responsibility was never about could-have-done-otherwise to begin with
- responsibility talk is a tool for shaping behavior and we should keep it on those grounds alone, desert be damned
- the whole framework is an illusion and we'd be better off without it
- keep blaming and praising, but quietly stop believing anyone actually deserves it
- we can't get outside our blaming practices to evaluate them, so "is it justified?" is a malformed question
- plus one or two more that you think belong there and that I've missed

I also want at least one node about the difference between a person who does wrong and a person who is merely dangerous or broken — the case where you'd stop resenting them and start managing them instead, like a faulty instrument. That distinction seems to do a lot of work and I'd like it to fall out of the branches rather than just get assumed.

Two more asks. First, note any combinations of answers the tree can't place, and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, or merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Second, if any two of my leaves above really can't be seperated by any honest question, tell me straight rather than fudging it.

Keep it to about eight to ten leaves and make it something I can hand out on a single page. I'm definately not looking for a bibliography or who said what first — I just want the key.
