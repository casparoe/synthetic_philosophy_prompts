I don't have any training in physics or philosophy, but for about a year now I've been arguing about the fine-tuning of the physical constants with friends who are as unqualified as I am, and we keep going in circles because none of us has ever said what an explanation of the whole thing would even have to accomplish. So before we argue about a designer versus a multiverse versus just accepting it as a brute fact, I want the specification. Write me the desiderata for an adequate account of the fine-tuning: what any explanation of the apparent fine-tuning of the constants has to deliver in order to count as adequate, with the consideration or case behind each requirement, and with a note on which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of one camp or another.

To be concrete, here are the things that keep tripping us up, and I want each of them handled as a requirement rather than waved away. If we call the values improbable, we seem to need some way of measuring how large the space of possible values is and where the actual value falls in it, and that seems to depend on whether we describe a constant by its value, by its logarithm, or by some other equivalent description — so is the improbability a fact about the world or a fact about how we chose to describe it? Should the target be life, or carbon chemistry, or anything complex enough to wonder about the constants, since these give very different probabilities? Does the fact that no one could have observed a universe without observers cancel the datum or leave it untouched? Is the fine-tuning one improbability or a pile of separate ones that multiply into an absurdity? Does an explanation have to rest on something we already have independent reason to believe, and if so, how does a multiverse survive that demand any better than a designer does? And can the account say what would count as evidence against it, or is it the kind of thing that nothing could count against?

Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out the candidate explanations we keep naming, and whether any two of the requirements cannot be satisfied at the same time — if some pair is jointly unsatisfiable, that is the result I most want to see. Please don't send me a bibliography or citations; I'm working from what I can reason out on my own.
