I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind and I'm drafting a position statement on situated cognition for a seminar. Before I commit to it, I want the package stress-tested. Here is where I've landed.

(1) The parity principle is the right criterion for the cognitive: a process counts as cognitive when it plays the functional role that a corresponding intracranial process would play, and location by itself carries no weight. (2) So Otto's notebook really is part of his memory — not a tool he consults but a constituent of the remembering; cognition extends. (3) I distinguish embedding from extending: causal scaffolding that shapes cognition is not the same as constituting it, and only the latter is at issue in (2). (4) I accept that the coupling-constitution objection names a genuine error to avoid — you cannot get constitution for free out of causal coupling — but I don't think it lands on a properly stated parity principle. (5) I'm a functionalist across the board: mental states are individuated by causal-functional role, not by physical substrate. (6) But I'm an internalist about phenomenal consciousness: experience is realized by brain processes, and there is no extended phenomenal experience.

Do these hang together? I want an audit, not a survey. Which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and for each real tension what the cheapest repair is and what it costs. Order the tensions by severity, and tell me which single revision would resolve the most. I don't yet know which of these I'd surrender, so I'd rather you locate the pressure than tell me what I already believe. Be explicit about what would change your verdict: which consideration, if it turned out a certain way, would move you to a different diagnosis, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
