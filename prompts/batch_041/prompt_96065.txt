I need a short research proposal — four pages, one semester, no teaching relief — for a fellowship application. The subject is the argument, usually filed under the names Rietdijk and Putnam, that special relativity makes the block universe unavoidable. In my own words: because simultaneity is frame-relative, any event outside my past and future light cones — the 'elsewhere' region — is simultaneous with my here-and-now in some inertial frame; so if 'real' just means 'simultaneous with something present', every elsewhere event is as real as this room, and objective becoming is an illusion. My half-formed suspicion is that the argument's weight rests entirely on how we read 'real' in that conditional, and that both available readings disappoint. Read it as absolute and the premise is unmotivated by physics; read it as frame-relative and 'real' begins to look like 'up' — objective, perfectly respectable, and useless for concluding that the future exists. Write the proposal: state a question with a determinate answer, explain why the standing positions on the argument don't settle it, say what I would actually do with a semester and why the work has to be conceptual rather than a fresh formal treatment of spacetime structure, and say what would count as the project failing — including the possibility that I end up showing the argument is a definition dressed as a proof. Positions and arguments only; I'll supply the citations myself.
