Write a mock advice column for an invented self-help guru whose entire method is iterated elimination of dominated strategies applied to personal decisions. Around 600 words: three short letters from readers, each with the columnist's reply, deadpan throughout, the guru never winking at the reader. The comedy has to come from the game theory being exactly right, so build the letters around the genuine subtleties rather than the slogan. One reader should be told that although no single alternative open to them beats the option they are already living, a certain probabilistic mixture of two other alternatives does, so their option is dominated and must go, and the reply should make it clear that the dominance is by a mixed strategy and not by any pure one. A second reader's case should turn on the fact that when dominance is only weak, the order in which options are eliminated can change what survives, so that the columnist, having given opposite advice to two readers whose situations are structurally identical, can save face by eliminating in a different order without ever conceding that the method itself is doing anything wrong. A third letter can raise what is left once everything eliminable is gone, and whether what remains is any kind of resting point. Let the guru's voice stay serene and faintly smug; let a reader who knows the difference between strict and weak dominance catch what is being sent up, while a reader who does not can still follow the letters. The target is the practice of running a solution concept over ordinary life, not any real person.
