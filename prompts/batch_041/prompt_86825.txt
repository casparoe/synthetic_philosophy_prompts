setup. two parties are about to interact — think two countries negotiating a treaty, two firms setting a shared standard, two AIs, two roommates splitting chores, whatever. we get to be the referee who rewrites the rules of their interaction before they play. the question is when a rewrite is a good idea.

here's the standard i want you to chew on. call a rewrite 'safe' (in my own words, dont worry about whether it matches something in the literature): a rewrite is safe if it guarantees that every party ends up at least as well off as they would have in the original setup, no matter how each of them reacts — where 'reacts' means each party takes whatever they were originally going to do and adapts it to the new setup in any way that falls within some fixed set of 'reasonable adaptations' we're willing to countenance. if a rewrite is safe in that sense, the pitch is great: you dont need anyone to be nice, rational, or to share your values, you just rewrite the game and everybody is covered.

now the thing i cant stop chewing on, and what i want the essay about. 'safe' is doing a LOT of work, and it's doing it through that phrase 'reasonable adaptations'. make that set tiny — only the most obvious ways of translating old behavior into the new game — and your 'safe' rewrite is only safe against people who read the new game the way you do. someone who reads it differently can walk straight off a cliff, and it's not their fault, it's yours for having called the thing safe. make the set huge — every conceivable adaptation — and almost nothing nontrivial is safe anymore, because you can always invent some perverse adaptation that ruins the point, so the standard collapses into 'do nothing'. so it looks like the whole idea is either toothless or self-defeating depending on a choice we have no principled way to make.

your job: write an essay on whether this safe-rewrite standard is a coherent guide for designing real institutions (treaties, contracts, platform rules, constitutions, pick what you like), or whether it should be scrapped in favor of something else. give the strongest version of the pro-safe-rewrite case its due — i think the real appeal is that it sidesteps the whole 'but will they cooperate?' mess, which is genuinely attractive — then put it up against the 'either vacuous or arbitrary' objection above. dont cop out with 'it depends on the context'. reach a verdict: either (a) the standard is fine, but only under a specific, defensible restriction on what counts as a reasonable adaptation — say exactly what the restriction is, and why it isnt just gerrymandered to save the idea — or (b) it isnt salvageable and you'd replace it with something you spell out. and keep it concrete: invent a small two-party scenario with actual payofs and use it as a running example, dont stay abstract the whole way through.
