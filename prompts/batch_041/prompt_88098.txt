I am looking for the name, if there is one, of a pattern in how explanations get ranked, and I would settle for a careful definition rather than a label.

Here is the pattern. When I compare explanations by how much they have to specify — the fewer independent facts the better, which is roughly how I compare models in my work — the ones that put an agent behind the data keep winning, and they win for reasons that have nothing to do with whether any such agent exists. 'Something wanted these observations to come out as they did' is short to state, accommodates any observations whatsoever, and then, at no additional cost, predicts that the regularity will hold in cases nobody has examined, because the agent will go on wanting it. The mindless rival pays separately for every further case: it must specify the regularity, then specify it again for the unexamined region, and again beyond that. The premium is largest exactly where it is most embarrassing. The instruction 'arrange for these to come out heads' is shorter than the run of heads it accounts for.

I meet this in three places. In a long run of coincidences, 'it is being arranged' is briefer than the honest alternative and, unlike the honest alternative, survives each new coincidence without revision. In model comparison, if I permit a hypothesis that says the data were selected by something with an interest in how they look, it fits everything and costs almost nothing to write, while the hypothesis that has to describe the actual pattern costs a great deal. And in the old design argument, the standard complaint is that the designer is a superfluous addition; by the accounting I am using, one agent with one aim replaces an enormous number of independent facts, so it is the naturalistic alternative that looks expensive.

What I want to know is whether this particular asymmetry — the systematic brevity, and the free extrapolation, of hypotheses in which an agent whose aim is defined over the observations themselves is doing the arranging — has an established name and an established treatment; how it differs from the problem of induction, from the charge that conspiracy theories are unfalsifiable, and from the gardener of the old parable, who is made invisible, then intangible, then undetectable, until the claim about him has quietly emptied out; whether it implies that a device built on this ranking would expect any unexamined corner of the world to be under someone's management; and whether the premium can be removed without an ad hoc tax on agents. If nothing established answers to it, I would like a definition with edges, and a case on each side of them.
