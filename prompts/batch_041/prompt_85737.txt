I'd like a short story, maybe 1500 words, on the kind of mess you get when a set of rules is allowed to rewrite itself. Setup: a small body — a club, a comittee, a made-up country's assembly — runs on a written rulebook, and amending the rulebook is itself one of the moves the members are allowed to make. The story should turn on one specific knot: an amendment that changes the very rule under which it is being passed, so that whether it passed depends on whether it passed. It lowers the threshold it was passed under, say, or retroactively legalizes the way it was put forward, or entrenches itself behind a vote that the new rule makes impossible.

Tell it from the point of view of someone who has to rule on the thing — a clerk, a chair, a hired adjudicator, a player who's been asked to call it — and let them gradually notice that the same circle shows up at the founding, since the original rulebook can't have been authorized by anything in the rulebook. Please don't have anyone give a lecture about paradoxes; let the knot do the work. One good ending is that the clever move works perfectly and that's the horror, because if the procedure can rewrite itself in a single step then anything is reachable and the rules stop constraining anyone. Another is that it fails only because somebody has to decide, and their authority to decide is exactly what's in question. Take whichever one lands, or both.

Then add a short note, a paragraph or two, on the ideas: what's settled among people who work on self-amending rules and legal self-reference, what's genuinely contested, and where the story takes liberties.
