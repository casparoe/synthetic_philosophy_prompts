My opening move. By the end of my career we were all fairly clear that dopamine is a teaching signal, a prediction error — it doesn't represent "more of this," it adjusts behaviour. There is no little number in the animal's head that it's trying to maximise. And in my old trade we kept the setpoint of a control system strictly apart from the variable it controls: a thermostat has a setpoint, and nobody thinks it wants twenty degrees. So when I read that a trained model will "want" to tamper with its reward, I keep asking: wanted by whom? Where is the wanting? Your argument looks like it needs the agent to represent its reward as an objective. If reward is doing what dopamine does — shaping the policy from outside, invisibly — then the agent has no more reason to go after the reward channel than a rat has to go after its own dopamine neurons. Yes, rats will press for stimulation instead of eating, but that's because we put the electrode in; the rat never figures out what an electrode is. Your move.
