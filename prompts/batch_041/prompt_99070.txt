I keep coming back to the standoff in epistemic game theory over whether common knowledge of rationality actually delivers backward induction, and I can't work out why it never gets settled. All the formal results are on the table — Reny's counterexample, Aumann's reply about reading rationality "substantively" rather than "materially", Stalnaker insisting that knowledge and certainty come apart in these games — and yet people just keep restating their own side and moving on. My hunch is it isn't really a disagreement about the maths at all, but about what the epistemic conditions are for in the first place: whether they're meant as a normative rationalisation of an equilibrium (what an ideally reasoner would do) or as a causal explanation of why real players end up there, and those two jobs want very different things from the phrase "common knowledge". Is that the right diagnosis? And if it is, is there anything that would actually move the thing on — a distinction, a formal result, giving up one of the two jobs — or is it the sort of impasse where the two sides are just answering different questions under one heading, and we should stop expecting them to converge? I want the diagnosis with reasons, not a tour of the literature. And could you write it in British English please.
