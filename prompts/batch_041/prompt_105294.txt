I want a case that makes that vivid, not a hand-wave.

In case REU isnt at your fingertips, here's the idea in my own words: to score an option, you take its worst possible outcome as the anchor, then walk up through the better outcomes one at a time, and for each one you add the amount by which it improves on the outcome just below it — but weighted not by that outcome's probability, rather by your risk function r applied to the probability that the option does at least that well. r takes probabilities to weights, it's increasing, r(0)=0 and r(1)=1. If r(p)=p you're back to plain expected utility with no extra risk attitude. If r sits below the diagonal in the middle (convex), the agent underweights the upside and comes out risk averse; if it bows the other way (concave) they're risk seeking. The whole selling point is that this risk ingredient does work that, in EU, has to be smuggled into the shape of the utility function.
