ok, so I've got to present my version of modal realism at our local meetup in a couple weeks and I'm trying to write it down as a list of things I actually believe rather than a summary of somebody else's book, and every time I do this I get the nagging feeling that two or three of the items quietly contradict each other. I've been reading this stuff on and off since the Sequences era and I can never tell whether the friction I'm feeling is a real problem or just me being sloppy. So here's the package, roughly in order of how confident I am:

(1) Possible worlds are concrete — same kind of thing as the world we're in, not abstract representations, not maximally consistent sets of sentences, not useful fictions. (2) Worlds are causally isolated: nothing in one world ever causes anything in another, and there's no cross-world causation even in principle. (3) 'Actual' is indexical, like 'here' or 'now'. Our world isn't metaphysically special, its just the one we're in. But I'm not a relativist about any of this — I still think there are objective facts about what's possible and necessary, not just facts about our language. (4) A world is a maximal causally connected whole: two bits of stuff are in the same world iff some chain of causes links them. I'm attached to this one specifically because I think what makes something concrete is being the relatum of causal relations, so the unity relation for worlds should be causal rather than spatiotemporal. (5) Plenitude comes from recombination: any way of patching together parts of worlds — as long as you don't duplicate a particular and don't demand two incompatible arrangements of the same parts — is a way some world is. (6) So there could have been alien properties: properties that nothing in our world instantiates and that aren't built out of the properties our world happens to have. (7) De re modality runs on counterparts: I'm not in any other world, only things very like me are, so 'I could have been a plumber' is true in virtue of a counterpart of mine being one. (8) There's no answer to 'how many worlds are there' — the question assumes the worlds form some kind of definite totality and I don't think there is one.

For what it's worth I'm a card-carrying naturalist about all of this and I don't think any of it commits me to spooky extra furniture — worlds are made of whatever physics is about, they're just not hooked up to us.

What I want isn't a verdict on which item is false. I want the audit: which pairs are genuinely pulling against each other, which ones only look like they clash and why the appearance is misleading, and for each real tension what the cheapest repair is and what it costs me — because some of these I'd give up in a second and some I'd definately fight for. Rank the real tensions by how much damage they do to the package as a whole. And be upfront about your confidence: tell me how sure you are of each verdict, flag the single one you're least sure about, and say if any of them only bites given an assumption I haven't stated. No citations needed, I just want the argument.
