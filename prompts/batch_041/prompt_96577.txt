I'm in a seminar on collective action right now and I've gotten a little obsessed with one thing. Everyone I talk to treats the tragedy of the commons as basically a solved problem: open access means overuse, so either privatize the resource or have the state regulate it, and the only interesting wrinkle is Ostroms cases where a small tight-knit group with a long shadow of the future manages it themselves. I want a thought experiment that scrambles that picture, and I want you to build it to order.

Here's the spec. Give me two cases, as realistic as you can make them (a real kind of resource, ordinary people, no sci-fi, no gods-eye technology, no aliens), identical in every respect except the ownership arrangement. In Case A the resource is under pure open access: no ownership, no rules, a big anonymous crowd, and every single user is a textbook rational egoist — no altruism, no warm glow, nobody ever meets the same person twice, no talking, no coordinating, no punishing, no reputation, and no concern for their own future (they take what they want and leave for good). And yet the resource does not get destroyed; the crowd keeps using it indefinitely without wrecking it. In Case B, take the same resource, same people, same preferences, same technology, but now it is fully privatized: clear exclusive rights, enforced boundaries, no open access, no poaching, owners free to do as they like. And the resource gets wrecked. The depletion in Case B has to fall out of the owners' own rational choices — not outsiders, not sabotage, not a government doing something dumb.

Then do the checks I actually care about. First, name the feature of the resource that is doing the work, and test it by toggling: strip that feature out of Case A while keeping open access and everything else the same, and show me the collapse; then tell me honestly whether that same feature is what wrecks Case B, or whether B needs a second, different feature. I would rather have the honest two-feature answer than a fake single-variable one. Second, in Case A the resource has to be genuinely depletable, not just too big to matter — tell me what small, realistic change (a cheaper extraction technology, a demand spike, a shift in what people want) would tip it into collapse, and show the tipping. The resource is basically unlimited is a cheat, don't use it. Third, walk through what the standard open-access logic predicts for each case and what the standard property-rights story predicts, and point to the exact step in each where the reasoning comes apart, then say what happens to both cases if you neutralize your feature (I expect them to fall back into the textbook pair, and I want to see that). Fourth, give me the best shot a defender of the standard view has at absorbing all this — is Case B a real counterexample to privatization fixes it, or does it just expose a precondition the theory always quietly assumed? If it is the latter, say so straight, because that's half what I suspect and I'd rather know than get a clever-sounding dodge. One page, maybe a little more, short enough to read out loud in seminar.
