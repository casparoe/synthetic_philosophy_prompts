I am a philosophy PhD student working on risk and precaution, and before I commit to a framework in my dissertation I want to settle what an adequate theory in this area would actually have to deliver. Rather than another defense or critique of the precautionary principle, I want the specification first: the full list of desiderata that any theory of precautionary decision-making must satisfy, each with the case or consideration that motivates it.

I want the list to cover at least the following.

The cases the theory must get right: a novel technology with a low-probability but catastrophic downside; a precautionary restriction that forecloses a technology which would have prevented a comparable harm; a choice where the precautionary option and the risky option carry roughly equal expected harm; a hazard that is reversible and one that is not; and a case in which the threshold of serious or irreversible harm is itself uncertain.

The distinctions it must not collapse: risk versus ambiguity or model uncertainty; reversibility versus magnitude; a precautionary presumption versus the burden of proof that such a presumption shifts; the rational-choice reading of the principle versus the legitimacy reading, on which precaution constrains the imposition of risk on parties who have not consented to it; and action versus omission, if that distinction can be made to survive at all.

The puzzles it must dissolve: the apparent regress of taking precautions against the harms of precaution; the fact that inaction is itself a decision with consequences, so that precaution cannot be identified with doing nothing; whether the principle is a decision rule, a presumption, or a procedure for allocating the burden of proof; and the problem of fixing a threshold for precaution without circularity.

The results it must remain consistent with: whatever decision theory establishes about the inadmissibility of lexicographic or unboundedly weighted values, and the plain fact that no agent or polity can assign unlimited weight to every possible hazard.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it and mark whether it is a genuine constraint on any theory or merely a commitment of one camp. Then say which requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, if any; if the list is jointly unsatisfiable, I want to know exactly which pair does the damage and why. Finally, and briefly, how expected-utility maximization, a maximin or catastrophic-risk rule, and a strong precautionary principle each fare against the list, and which single requirement eliminates the most candidates. A table is fine for the last part.
