ok so i'm a backend dev and i've fallen down a rabbit hole reading about epiphenomenalism. my understanding of it: the feeling of pain, or of red, is a byproduct of the brain stuff and it doesn't cause anything downstream. the neorons fire, you say ouch, you post about it, and the actual sensation is just... along for the ride. cool, fine. but then somebody pointed out the obvious problem: i just wrote a whole paragraph about my sensations. if the sensation can't push on anything, how did it end up in the paragraph? and the answer is supposedly that the same brain event that made the sensation also made the paragraph, and the paragraph is about the sensation the way a shadow is about the thing casting it, not because the sensation reached out and wrote it. which is a neat answer but also kind of a horrible one.

i want a poem that lives inside that horrible spot. form i want: a pantoum - the one where lines 2 and 4 of each verse come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next verse, and the last verse folds back into the first so the whole thing closes on itself. i'm picking it on purpose: in a pantoum the lines come back on a schedule no matter what they mean, the layout is doing all the work and the sense just has to cope. i want the poem to make that feel like the paradox - the report keeps getting produced, right on time, and the feeling it's supposedly about is never the thing driving it.

voice should be the feeling itself, if thats not too weird - the sensation that keeps getting described and never consulted. don't name any philosophers or theories and don't cite anything, i can't verify that and honestly neither can you. the words epiphenomenalism or qualia don't even need to show up if the poem doesn't want them.

one more thing: i want one line in there where the speaker takes credit, something like "i made him say it", and i want the form to quietly undercut that line - the reader should be able to tell the line is false because the poem has already shown who's really in charge. someone who knows the puzzle should grin at that. someone who doesn't should still get a good poem about being a passenger in your own head.
