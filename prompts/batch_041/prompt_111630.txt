For a seminar on constitutional design and rational choice, I am trying to specify what any adequate theory of precommitment must deliver. The theory should cover both individual Ulysses contracts (Odysseus bound to the mast, addicts signing away access to funds, patients precommitting to refuse treatment) and constitutional precommitment (entrenchment clauses, supermajority amendment rules, independent central banks). Write the desiderata as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. For each, give the motivating case or consideration and say whether you regard the requirement as non-negotiable or as a negotiable demand of one camp. Include at least one case where the precommitting self and the later bound self disagree about what counts as the device's failure—for instance, a revocation clause that permits exit only if a trustee certifies the later change of mind as authentic. For one of the five requirements, name another requirement it cannot be jointly satisfied with, and in one item identify the requirement that does the most to exclude otherwise popular accounts. Do not provide citations or a reading list; I want the specification itself.
