I host a small metaphysics podcast and next weeks guest is a philosopher who's been defending a stubborn line on the statue-and-the-clay for years. Her view: the statue and the lump of clay are two numerically distinct things occupying the same place at the same time, and the reason they differ modally (the lump survives being squashed, the statue doesn't) isn't grounded in any difference in their material or in any other fact about them -- she thinks modal properties are only ever had relative to a sort of thing, so the lump can survive squashing as a lump and the statue can't survive it as a statue, and there's no further fact that determines which sortal each one falls under. I want 8-10 interview questions that stop her from just restating this. At minimum I need one on the grounding/explanatory worry (what makes it the case that a given thing counts as a lump rather than a statue, if nothing intrinsic does), one on counting (how many things are on the pedestal, and is she going to say the question is ill-formed?), one on whether making modality sortal-relative makes it conventional or mind-dependent (would there be no statues in a world with no thinkers, and is that a cost?), one on how her line differs from the older moves that say the two are identical but only contingently, or that identity itself is sortal-relative, and why hers is better, and one on whether a deflationist who calls the whole thing a verbal dispute has a point. Give a one-line follow-up to each question, keyed to the answer she'd most likely give.
