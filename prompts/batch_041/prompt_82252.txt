Next month I'm presenting at our department's metaethics work-in-progress group and I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a pep talk. My paper's thesis: the standard evolutionary debunking argument against moral realism only goes through if you buy what I'm calling the tracking assumption — that a moral belief counts as justified only if the mechanism that produced it tends to track moral truth — and that once you drop that assumption in favour of a coherentist account of moral justification with some role for moral seemings, the debunking argument loses its grip. The part I'm nervous about is my claim that this escape route isn't ad hoc, because the tracking assumption turns out to be doing no independent work anywhere else in our epistemic life either. The room is small and I know exactly who's in it: two committed non-naturalist realists who think debunking arguments are the single most serious threat to their view and who'll be quietly delighted if I've accidentally done their work for them; one philosopher of biology who's convinced the whole genre rests on garbage evolutionary psychology and will go after the empirical premises before the philosophy; and two first-order ethicists who dont care about metaethics and will ask, in some form, what any of this actually changes for normative theorising. Give me the prep sheet as exactly five numbered items and nothing else — no intro, no closing. For each item, name the objection in the words that particular audience member would really use, give the gist of my two-minute reply in a sentence or two, and flag where the reply is soft or where I'm basically bluffing. Order them worst-first by how much damage they'd do me, and make the fifth one the question I'm hoping nobody asks. Blunt, no padding.
