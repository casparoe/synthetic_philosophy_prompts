I write about science and ideas, mostly long features — the kind of piece where there's a philosopher on page four and a lab on page nine — and I'm three months into something on grief and I keep circling a thing I don't have a word for. I'd rather not invent a term in a magazine without checking that one doesn't already exist, so: is there a name for this, and if there isn't, what would you call it?

The thing itself. My friend D. died two years ago. He was the person I rang before every big decision, and about six months after he died I noticed I was still ringing him — except not remembering what he'd said about some earlier case, actually producing his verdict on cases he never lived to see. I got a job offer last month and I knew at once what he'd have said about it, and why, and it wasn't a guess or a reconstruction; it was more like an argument that ran in his voice and came out the other side with an answer I hadn't had before. His widow says the same is happening to her, except her D. gives different answers than mine. We compared notes on one thing and our two D.s flatly contradicted each other, and each of us was certain ours was the real one. And the thing in my head has started to drift — it'll endorse something D. would have hated, I'll notice the drift, and then I can't tell whether that's him or me. I can't do it with anyone else. My father's also dead and there's nothing there, just memories, so it isn't the dead-live-on-in-us thing in general. It happens with living people too: I have a version of my old editor in my head who still cuts my sentences, and he's alive and well and would be surprised to hear it.

I know people talk about minds spilling out into notebooks and phones and calendars. That's not it — a notebook doesn't argue back. I also don't think it's just memory or imitation, because memory hands you the past and this hands you new verdicts about new situations. And I don't think it's the usual personal-identity stuff either, because there's no question of which one is really D., he's dead, obviously; the question is what these two partial things are and what relation they stand in to each other and to him.

So: does this have a name, and is there a literature I've somehow missed? If the honest answer is that the nearest terms are adjacent things — sharing intentions, or the self being social all the way down, or minds reaching past the skull — then tell me exactly where each one falls short, because in the piece I want to be able to write that this is one thing and not the other, on the grounds that the other doesn't have the feature where the copy argues back and drifts. If nothing fits, coin something. Define it so it covers D. but not my father. Tell me where the edges are: does it need the person to be dead? Does it need love, or would contempt do? Can it run in three heads at once, and does it matter that they disagree? And if my D. drifts far enough, is there a point where it stops being him — and is that a fact about the world or a decision about words?

I don't beleive a reading list will help me, I'll never get through it. I just want the carving.
