I'm a junior and I'm in this course called Minds, Machines and Evolution, which is honestly kind of a mess — we went from Darwin to Turing to a guest lecture on neural nets in about three weeks — but it's left me noticing a pattern that I don't have a word for, and it's bugging me.

The pattern is a way of arguing, not a fact about the world. Somebody takes a thing that looks like it needed understanding and explains it with a process that understood nothing. Nobody had to see anything for eyes to end up seeing; the ones that worked just left more copies. Fine, that's an ordinary explanation, no complaints. But then the same explanation gets turned around and used as a verdict on the very thing it explains: since we can get the look of purpose without anybody having any purpose, the purpose was never really there. That's one direction — the explanation drains the mind out of the thing explained. The other direction is the Turing one: instead of arguing about whether the machine has the inner whatever, you point out that it does the outer thing, and now whoever says it lacks the inner thing has to say what else is supposed to be missing. I saw a version of this in the neural net lecture too, where people fight about whether a model that plays a game well 'really' understands it. So one direction drains, the other shifts the burden. I have a feeling these are the same trick run backwards, and I also have a feeling that one of them is legitimate and one is a cheat, but I can't tell which is which and I suspect I've got it exactly inverted.

Is there a name for this move? A friend said it's just emergence, and my TA mumbled something about teleonomy, but both of those seem to be about the systems themselves rather than the argumentative step I'm describing, so I don't think either is what I'm after. I don't want a label and nothing else — I want to know what the move actually is, whether the two directions are one move or two that merely look alike, and whether the draining direction is a real inference or a fallacy that's borrowing credit from the explanation next to it. Take a definite position and defend it, don't just lay out the options for me. Under 400 words, and American English please.
