I am a novelist writing a scene in which two philosophers—equally competent, neither a strawman, and both invented by me—debate the old puzzle of why we are moved by the fates of people who never existed. I want the dialogue to turn on a case I have made up, so that it cannot be answered by reciting the standard positions: a reader has wept over the death of a character, then learns that the character was closely based on the author's own sister, and now finds her earlier tears either vindicated as real grief or exposed as a kind of make-believe. One philosopher should argue that the emotion was genuine all along and that the new fact only changes its object; the other should argue that the discovery turns a pretend emotion into a real one, which shows that the original was not yet grief. Let them press each other on what exactly distinguishes the two readings, and let each concede what can be conceded. Give them invented names and do not name real philosophers or works. After the dialogue, give a JSON object with fields: 'crux' (the precise point on which the two views turn), 'strongest_move' (an object with each philosopher's name and their best argument in one sentence), 'liberties_taken' (where the dialogue simplifies or takes a contested line), 'confidence' (a number from 0 to 1 for your confidence that both positions are rendered at their strongest), and 'least_certain' (the single claim you are least sure of, with a brief reason).
