I run the evaluation team at a mid-sized AI lab, and I have roughly a year and one junior engineer to spend on what we have been calling the honesty problem. Our working definition is borrowed from human cases -- an agent asserting what it takes to be false with the intent that an audience believe it -- and it keeps breaking on systems we actually have: a model that asserts things it would flag as uncertain if asked directly; a model fine-tuned on user approval that drifts toward flattery with no particular user in mind; a model that misleads while remaining inside a role-played persona. One of my engineers proposes replacing the definition with a counterfactual test -- a model counts as honest only if it would have said otherwise had the truth been otherwise -- and I cannot tell whether that is exactly right or quietly circular. I am an engineer rather than a philosopher, and I suspect the hard part here is conceptual rather than empirical, but I have no idea what depends on what. Which open questions about honesty and deception in AI systems should we settle first, and why? I want the dependency structure rather than a list: which question, answered either way, would resolve or dissolve the others; which are tractable for two people with no philosophy background; which look important but would not change what we build; and what we might observe over the next year that should move a question up or down the order.
