Ok this one is a bit of an odd request so bear with me. I'm not an academic. I work as a data analyst for a city council, I did a sociology degree about fifteen years ago and never went back, and for the last year or so I've gotten weirdly obsessed with those annual world happiness rankings that get reported every spring. I'm applying to a small foundation that gives money to people outside academia to work on a public-policy question for a year part time. They want two pages: the question, why it matters, what I'll actually do, what I'll produce. I have a half-formed idea and I need you to turn it into the proposal.

My idea, roughly. The rankings ask people to imagine the best possible life and the worst possible life and place themselves on a ladder between them, then average that up by country and sort the countries. Everyone argues about whether the number really measures happiness. But I think that's the wrong fight, or at least it's only half of it, and here's the bit I can't shake: the thing being ranked, the country's position, is not the same object as the thing being measured. A measure can be good and still be a terrible thing to aim at, and I think the two questions have been quietly glued together in every argument I've read about this. So I want to pull them apart.

Three things I want the proposal to carry, in my own words since I don't know the literature:

First, the same-number-two-causes problem. If the score is people comparing their lives to the best and worst lives they can imagine, then there are two completely different ways to raise it. You can make lives better, or you can move what people take to be normal and possible, by managing expectations, narrowing what people see, changing what gets talked about. Both show up as the same number. I want to know whether anything could tell them apart in practice, and if nothing can, that seems like a big deal that should be said plainly.

Second, the positional thing. If what you care about is the rank, then it's zero sum in a way the underlying good isn't, and every country aiming for the top is incoherent because at most one can get there. So "should a country try to top them" can't be a general recommendation in that form. The sensible version has to be something like: should a government treat its position as a performance indicator, and what's the difference between that and treating the underlying thing as the goal.

Third, who is the country that's supposed to be trying? A government? A statistical office? Everyone at once? That changes the answer and I think people slide between the readings without noticing. Also I want the proposal to take seriously that once a score becomes a target, the behaviour that moves the score changes, so maybe the measure stops meaning what it meant, which makes "does it measure happiness" a question with a date attached to it.

What I'd actually do over the year, month by month, is the part I need the most help with. I can rebuild the index from published numbers in a spreadsheet, build a small toy example where a country raises its score by making lives better and another does it by moving the reference point, and then try to work out what evidence would separate them. Then maybe two or three case studies of countries that moved a lot, using only public data. Then the normative half. Then write it up. The deliverable should be something a minister could actually use plus an honest list of what I couldn't settle.

Please state the question so it has a definite answer rather than being a topic, explain why the obvious answers don't settle it, tell me what would count as the project succeeding and what would count as it failing, and be honest about whether this is too big for a year part time and where I should cut.

Don't include any citations or a reading list. I can't get at journals anyway and I'd rather not have references I can't check. If you need to refer to a position just describe it in plain words.
