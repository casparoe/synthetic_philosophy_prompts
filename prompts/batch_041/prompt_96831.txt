Write a short story set in a small museum's conservation lab. A restorer is handed a lump of unfired clay that's catalogued as a 19th-century artifact — it was never fired or shaped into anything, it's just a ball of clay that someone donated as "the last material a famous potter ever touched." The director wants her to sculpt it into a commemorative bust for the museum's anniversary, arguing that the clay and the bust would be made of the exact same physical stuff either way, so nothing is actually lost. The restorer thinks this is nuts: if she reshapes it, the artifact she was hired to preserve is gone, even though not a single gram of matter left the room. To complicate things, the clay isn't even neutral material — some earlier owner had pinched a crude little face into one side of it, so it's arguably already a (bad) sculpture, not just a lump. Then a curator from the lending institution shows up and demands the "original object" back in its registered condition. Build the story around the idea that two things can share all their matter and still be different things, and let it end without resolving which of them is right. Keep it under about 1200 words, and write it in American English.
