Hi — I'm in third year and I'm the student rep on the panel for our "Archives, Evidence and Power" module, so they've asked me to draft a mock exam question for the revision pack. They actually use these, so it needs to be a real one.

Can you write me one essay question, 45 minutes, 20 marks, on archival silence and who gets recorded.

What I want it to test: in week 6 we ended up sketching this rough grid on the whiteboard and I'm fairly sure its not in any textbook, so people can't just recite their way through it. The grid is that there are at least four different things we lazily call a "silence": (1) the thing was never recorded at all; (2) it was recorded but the record is gone; (3) the record survives but is unreachable — uncatalogued, illegible, in a script nobody working on it reads, stuck in a backlog; (4) the record survives and is easy to find, but its genre only lets the person appear in a degraded mode — as a debtor, a defendant, a vagrant, a number in a wage-arrears book. I want the question to force them to say which of these a historian can actually claim to detect and what would count as evidence for each, rather than just writing "silences exist and that's political".

I'd also like them to have to say something about reading against the grain — whether it recovers anything or just bounces the state's own categories back at you — and to be honest about cases where you genuinely can't tell a silence from a gap.

So: the question, a model answer around 550-650 words, and a marking scheme that names the specific moves that separate a first from a 2:1, with marks for each (total 20), plus one line per point on the usual way people miss it.
