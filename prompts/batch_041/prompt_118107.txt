Bit of a weird request. I spent 35 years as a field biologist working on insect sensory ecology, retired, and for the last two years I've been reading philosophy of mind, mostly on whether you can get real content out of biological function (the teleosemantics crowd — Millikan, Dretske, that lot). I've sketched a short essay and I'd like your comments before I write it out properly.

The claim: teleosemantics says a state means what it means because of what it was selected to do — a frog's detector means fly because detecting flies is what it got recruited for. Fine as far as it goes. But nearly every signalling system I actually spent my life on is an exaptation or an outright manipulation: a harmless hoverfly wearing wasp stripes, a cuckoo chick begging in its host's accent, a mate preference that some other species has learned to hijack. In those cases the history that's meant to fix the content and the current job of the state come apart, and they can come apart in opposite directions. So I want to say the content of a state is fixed by what the consumer's response needs to be true for the response to work, not by what the producer's ancestors were selected for. If the bird's flinch only pays off when the striped thing stings, then the state is about stinging things, whatever the stripes used to mean.

What I'm unsure about: (1) whether I've just relabelled something already in the literature — I have a suspicion I've re-invented a wheel; (2) whether I lose the very thing teleosemantics was built to give me, namely systematic misrepresentation, since "what the response needs to be true for it to work" sounds suspiciously close to "what would make it work", which may make error impossible or make error the wrong kind of thing; (3) whether exaptation is really the hard case here or just a case the standard account handles fine if you tell the history carefully enough; and (4) whether my frog example is doing what I think — the old worry about whether that detector is about flies or about small dark moving dots seems to bite me harder than it bites the people I'm arguing against.

Comments please, accuracy first, then whether the central move is interesting or just confused. Exactly five numbered comments, a sentence or two each, no intro and no closing summary.
