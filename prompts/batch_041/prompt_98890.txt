I've been going down a bit of a rabbit hole on something called evidential cooperation in large worlds, and I can't tell if it's a genuinely distinct idea or just ordinary reciprocity wearing a sci-fi hat. The rough idea, as I understand it: if the universe is big enough, there are probably lots of other agents out there running something like the same decision procedure as you, so what you choose can count as evidence about what they'll choose even though nothing you do can causally reach them. The upshot is supposed to be that you might have real reason to 'cooperate' with these strangers — not defect in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma you'll never actually get to play with them, pay into things on the assumption that other instances of your reasoning are paying in too — because your cooperating is evidence that they cooperate. It feels like Newcomb's problem scaled up to cosmic size, and it seems to lean on the kind of decision theory where you do what's evidence of a good outcome rather than what causes one. My flatmate reckons it's just dressed-up tit-for-tat: you scratch my back, I scratch yours, and once you strip out the window dressing it's the same old reciprocity with a philosophy degree. I don't think that's right but I can't quite say why.

Could you compare the two properly for me? I want to know what evidential cooperation in large worlds actually needs in order to get off the ground, versus what reciprocal cooperation needs — what has to be true about the other agents, about your own decision procedure, about whether your action is really evidence for anything — and then where the two come apart. Is evidential cooperation just reciprocity with the causal links filed off, or does it do something reciprocity structurally can't? And if it does, what's the price of admission — does it only work if you've already swallowed a particular theory of rational choice, does it commit you to cooperating with agents you'd never in a million years want to, does it collapse into 'everything is evidence for everything'? I'd rather the comparison bite on the mechanics — what each one is actually doing under the bonnet — than stay at the level of vibes, and I'd like you to be honest about which parts of the case for evidential cooperation are doing real work and which are just the largeness of the world doing the heavy lifting.
