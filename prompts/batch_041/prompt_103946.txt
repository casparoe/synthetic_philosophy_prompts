Ok so I got into this argument at a party and I can't let it go. This guy there — history grad student, the type who says "problematic" unironically — was holding forth about how every national identity is built on a lost cause. He had this whole list going: the Confederacy, Masada, Kosovo 1389, Dunkirk, and he kept saying it's the same machine every time, a defeat gets ground up and turned into glue. He even had a term for it, "chosen trauma," said some psychologist came up with it, the idea that a group latches onto a historical wound and it becomes part of who they are, and that the wound has to be kept alive on purpose.

I pushed back but I did it badly and now I want the proper version. Can you write me a dialogue between two people who both actually know what they're talking about, where one defends his "it's all the same machine" line and the other says no, you're lumping together things that shouldn't be lumped. I want the second one to make the case that the real variable isn't defeat at all, it's what the story is being asked to do now — that Dunkirk and the Confederate Lost Cause are doing completely different jobs, and that calling them both "lost causes" is a way of not having to say which jobs are defensible. And I want the first one to have real answers, not just fold. Like, he should be able to come back with: the moral content is a seperate question from the mechanism, and the mechanism is what you asked me about; and press the point that the distinguisher is smuggling in a moral verdict and dressing it up as analysis, which is exactly the move he accused me of.

I want real back and forth, they should answer each others actual points instead of talking past each other, and neither one should be a dummy. Then, outside the dialogue, a short note: which of the two you find more convincing, how confident you are in that, and the single point in the whole thing you're least sure about.

(One thing to be careful of — don't let it turn into a debate about whether the Confederacy was bad. Nobody in the dialogue is confused about that, and if that becomes the issue it's just a boring fight. The interesting disagreement is about the category.)
