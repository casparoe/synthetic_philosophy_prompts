My family's been feuding for like six months over something that happened at my cousin's wedding and I keep getting drug in as the referee, so I want to actually think it through instead of just picking whoever I already agree with.

My uncle Ray gave a toast and made a joke about my mom's cooking — said the turkey was so dry it could soak up a whole bottle of gravy. Big laugh. My mom was hurt. Since then three people have told me how she should handle it:

My grandfather says Ray insulted our family in public, and mom has to demand an apology in front of everyone who heard it, and if he won't give one we stop inviting him to stuff. You can't let people walk on you or it never stops.

My wife says it was a dumb joke at a wedding and everyone was drunk, and the dignified move is to act like it didn't land. Getting in a public fight over a turkey joke makes mom look smaller than Ray did. Be gracious at the next thing and it dies on its own.

My sister says mom is being bullied (this is like the third time Ray's pulled something) and she should take it to the family group chat and get the aunts and uncles to formally agree that Ray's behavior is out of line and set a rule. She says "just let it go" is what people always tell the person who got hurt, and it mostly protects whoever did the hurting.

Honestly all three of them sound right about something and wrong about something. Is there a real principle for saying which of these is the right response here — or is one of these three whole ways of dealing with being wronged actually morally better than the others? Please don't give me a wishy washy "it depends," I want to know what actually makes one of them the better answer.
