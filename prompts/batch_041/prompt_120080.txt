You are a philosopher of psychiatry retained as staff ethicist to a national psychiatric association's nosology review panel; I chair the panel, and in two weeks we vote on a proposed addition to the next revision of our manual. Our working label is "scripted dysphoria": persistent distress and impairment organized around a person's immersion in an online community that circulates a detailed public script for the condition — its triggers, its expected course, its symptoms. Field trials show the proposed criteria are reliable, clinicians find them useful, and the advocacy groups that helped write the script are pressing hard for inclusion. One member wants to file it as a culture-bound syndrome rather than a diagnosis proper.

Draft the short opinion memo I will circulate before the vote. Open with your recommendation — include the category, exclude it, or include it in a modified form, and if modified, specify the mechanism and a review timeline. Then give the reasoning, including an honest account of the strongest case on the losing side.

Confront head-on the difficulty I keep circling: this may be a kind whose members change in response to being classified — the looping effect Ian Hacking described. Our validation machinery (field trials, prevalence estimates, discriminant validity against neighboring diagnoses) treats the target as fixed while we test it. If classifying the population alters the population, tell us what we have actually validated, and whether the evidentiary bar for a self-modifying category should differ from that for one that is not. Do not summarize the literature on social construction or attach citations; argue to a decision. Think the tension through before you commit, but the memo itself should lead with the bottom line. Maximum 500 words.
