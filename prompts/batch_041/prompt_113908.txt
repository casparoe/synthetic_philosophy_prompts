I've been trying to figure out what I actually mean when I say one false theory is closer to the truth than another, and my gut verdicts don't seem to come from the same place. Newtons mechanics vs Aristotles: Newton is obviously much closer even though both are false. Phlogiston vs the oxygen theory of combustion: oxygen is closer by a mile. But then two versions of a nineteenth-century ether theory, one where the ether is a continuous fluid and one where it's granular, same predictions everywhere, both false in the same way — and I want to say neither is closer, there's just nothing there to compare. And a theory that says the earth is six thousand years old vs one that says 4.5 billion when it's really 4.54: the second is closer, even though I'd guess the first racks up more true consequences (seasons, tides, day and night, all of that).

The case that really bugs me: a theory that gets one big structural thing right — inheritance runs through discrete particles that get shuffled and never blend — but is wrong about nearly every detail, versus a theory that gets a hundred small things right but says inheritance blends like paint. I say the first is closer. Flip it though: a theory that gets the big structure right but also posits one extra unobservable entity that isn't there, versus a theory that posits nothing extra and gets the big structure slightly wrong. Now I feel like I'm just counting things, and the counting doesn't match what I said a second ago.

A friend who does philosophy of science says that if you spell 'closer' out as 'has more of the true consequences and fewer of the false ones', there's a proof that no false theory comes out closer to the truth than any other false theory, which can't be right given how I feel about Newton versus Aristotle.

So: is there a principle that gives me all of these verdicts, or nearly all? State it precisely, tell me which clause was bought with which of my verdicts, and then what it predicts about cases I haven't listed — a theory right about the mechanism but wrong about the magnitudes versus one right about the magnitudes but wrong about the mechanism, and two false theories that differ only over something we could never observe. If I have to give one of my verdicts up, say which, and if the honest answer is that 'closer' is partly a matter of what I care about rather than a fact about the theories, say that too.
