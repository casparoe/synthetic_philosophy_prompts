Ok so I'm putting together a session for our ops/data people and I want to head off the thing where somebody says "Goodhart's law" and everyone nods like that's the end of the conversation. What I want out of you is a dialogue I can actually read out loud, between two people who both really know what they're talking about and who disagree about what the law even says.

Setup, so the dialogue has something to bite on. Right now we grade our regional managers on tickets closed per week, and it's gone exactly how you'd guess: everything gets closed in twenty minutes and reopened as a fresh ticket, so the number is beautiful and the customers are furious. My director's proposed fix is a gadget I half believe in. Instead of one metric, managers get scored on how much other regions are willing to pay, out of their own budgets, to have that region's process copied over to theirs. Her argument is that you can't game that, because to game it you'd have to fool every other region's staff, not just a dashboard — and if you can fool all of them, you've arguably built something they actually want.

Write the dialogue between (a) someone who thinks Goodhart's law is a structural fact about any sufficiently powerful optimiser — so the market idea doesn't escape it, it just relocates the gaming one level up, and the honest conclusion is that you can only steer with numbers so far and after that you're back to judgment and relationships and people telling you the number is fake — and (b) someone who thinks that's mystical fatalism dressed up as a law, that what we call Goodhart's law is a grab bag of ordinary design failures (lazy proxies, no audits, slow feedback, nobody with the standing to say the number is garbage) and that the director's market is a genuinely better design rather than a shell game. Both of them should be good. I don't want the second one to be a naive tech bro and I don't want the first one to be a guy who just says "incentives, man" and folds his arms. Make them take each other's actual points and come back at them — including the one that bugs me most, which is that the new metric might just reward managers who are good at being impressive to other managers.

A few things I want in it. First, let them notice that "Goodhart's law" is being used for at least three different claims and that this matters: a metric degrading because people start optimising it, a metric degrading because optimisation drives you to the edge of the distribution where the old correlations don't hold, and a claim that's true by definition if you just define the goal as the metric. Second, each of them should say at some point the specific thing that would change their mind — not "well, I could be wrong" but the observation or argument they'd have to run into. And then at the end, a short note from you on where the dialogue leans on a contested reading of the law, since the two of them don't agree on what it says in the first place. (It's named after some British economist who was worried about money supply targets, which is a funny place for it to have started.)
