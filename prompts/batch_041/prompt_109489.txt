For my philosophy-of-biology elective I have to run a session on whether disease is a biological fact or a value judgment, and I would rather my classmates work out which view they actually hold than pick a label they happen to like. Please write a sorting quiz for me: eight short cases, each describing a condition or trait, each with two or three answer options about whether it counts as a disease and on what grounds, phrased so that someone can answer without having heard of any of the theories. Choose cases that pull the underlying questions apart instead of lining up with one another — something statistically unusual but harmless, something common and unwanted, something once classified as a disease and now not, something that causes trouble only in certain environments or lines of work, something the person is glad to have, something whose status as a malfunction depends on which comparison group you pick, and something where the person has no detectable malfunction yet is plainly unwell. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto five positions: disease as biological dysfunction; disease as dysfunction plus harm; disease as a matter of values through and through; disease as a concept we should abandon or replace; and the view that the fact/value split is a false choice, because a concept can be evaluative and still objective. Include one answer pattern that fits none of the five and say what someone holding it would have to believe, and flag any pair of positions my cases cannot separate. Keep the quiz and the key under 1,000 words.
