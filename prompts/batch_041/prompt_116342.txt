Our reading group has been going round in circles for about a year on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and I want to force the issue by writing up the document we should of produced months ago. The two camps: (A) the block-universe lot, who think the argument goes through and that talk of a moving present is either confused or a mere indexical, and (B) the presentists, who think it smuggles in assumptions about how 'real for' composes.

Write the joint statement that the two of them could both sign. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept — about what the Minkowski formalism itself says, about which steps in the argument are pure logic and which are substantive premises, and about what would count as evidence either way; (2) the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording that the side holding it would actually accept; (3) the cruxes; (4) for each crux, a concrete case both sides would definately accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. No citations or reading lists — I'll fill those in myself. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a table.

Two things I want built in, because I think they're why we're stuck:

First, the argument seems to run two premises together: (i) if an event is real according to some observer, then it's real, full stop; and (ii) 'real according to' is transitive, so chaining observers gets you from my here-and-now to an event in my own past. Make each side say which of the two it rejects, and whether rejecting (ii) on its own is enough to escape the conclusion. Related: does the argument need observers at all, or is 'real for an observer' doing work that a purely geometric relation could do just as well? If the observer drops out of the story, say what's left.

Second, I've been defending a half-baked view I call local presentism: every observer has a genuine present, no two observers disagree about what's happening now where they overlap, but co-reality isn't an equivalence relation — it isn't transitive, so there's no global now and no block. Is that stable, or does it collapse into the block view on one side or into a privileged frame on the other? Test it on a case like this: three events A, B and C where one inertial frame makes A and B simultaneous, a different frame makes B and C simultaneous, and A and C are timelike separated with C in A's future. What does the local presentist say about C? Is there a coherent way to say 'yes for the middle link, no for the chain'?

And say plainly if any of the disagreement is just verbal — if they're using 'exists' in different senses and the whole thing would evaporate under a bit of semantic ascent, then say which part and why, don't just wave at it.

Write it in British English, and don't hedge everything into mush — I want claims sharp enough that someone could actually disagree with them.
