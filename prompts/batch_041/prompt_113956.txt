I am writing up my position for a workshop on fanaticism and the limits of expected-value reasoning, and I should like an audit of my own commitments rather than a survey of the literature. Here is where I have landed. (1) Practical rationality consists in maximising expected value with respect to my actual credences; I do not think small probabilities may be discounted, and I do not think the value of outcomes has a ceiling. (2) I would not pay Pascal's mugger — the stranger who asks for my wallet on the strength of a threat whose astronomical magnitude, multiplied by its minute probability, is said to swamp every other consideration, and who can always raise the stakes until the arithmetic favours compliance — and I do not think I would be irrational in refusing. (3) I am a longtermist: I take the expected value of the far future to be astronomically large, and I take quite small probabilities of influencing it to be sufficient reason to give away a substantial part of my income. (4) I do not believe there is a fanaticism clause licensing me to ignore tiny probabilities of enormous payoffs; the machinery that makes longtermism compelling is the machinery that drives the mugging. (5) My resistance to the mugger rests on two thoughts: that the probability his threat is genuine is not merely small but unearned — a placeholder for ignorance rather than the output of any model with a track record — and that the making of the demand is itself evidence against it, since anyone able to do what he threatens would have no need to ask; my credences about the far future, by contrast, are earned, and no one is making a demand. (6) I do not think it is legitimate to refuse to put a number on the mugger's threat; if I decline to state a credence, I forfeit any claim to be an expected-value maximiser.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the cheapest repair for each genuine tension and what does that repair cost? Please order the tensions by severity, identify the commitment I am most likely to be holding only because I have not followed it through, and say which single revision would clear up the most. Do not simply reassure me that the package is consistent; where you think a tension is only apparent, say precisely what is doing the work of dissolving it, and where you think a commitment is doing no independent work, say so.
