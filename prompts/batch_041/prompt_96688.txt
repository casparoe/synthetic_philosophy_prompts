Bit of an odd one. I retired three years ago after 32 years in analytical chemistry (spectroscopy, nothing glamorous) and I've spent a lot of that retirement working through philosophy books, which has probably done me no favours. Anyway, this is a real decision I have to make this month and I keep going in circles on it.

I'm picking someone to manage my retirement money. I know, boring, bear with me. There are two finalists and I know them both, it's a small town. Their twelve-year records are basically identical — same returns within a rounding error, both sat through the 2020 crash and the bad stretch in 2022 without blowing up, and I've checked, they're not secretly holding the same index fund.

The difference is this. Dale can explain himself. He'll tell you why he's in this and out of that, he has a written process, he's trained two juniors who now do something like what he does. Rosa can't. I've asked her three times and the best I get is that she can see when a thing is about to go bad. She's not being coy, she genuinely can't say more than that. For what it's worth, Dale says he learned half his process by watching Rosa for a decade.

And here's the part that nags at me: when I asked Dale about a call he made in 2016, he gave me a reason that flatly contradicts the reason he gave me in 2019 for the same call.

Everybody I've asked says the same thing. If the records are the same, flip a coin. My gut says thats wrong — I spent my whole career trusting the people who could tell me how they'd know they were wrong, and I never once regretted it — but I can't tell you what the explainer is giving me that the other one isn't, if their results come out the same. If its only that I'd sleep better, thats not a reason to pick him. My wife also points out, unkindly, that I've never been able to explain why I'm any good in the lab either.

So: is there anything real here, or am I just nostalgic for the lab? And which one should I pick?
