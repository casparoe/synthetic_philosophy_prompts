I'm putting together a prospectus for my dissertaion (philosophy of language / epistemology) and I need it to come out around 2000-2500 words. Here's the half-baked idea I've been carrying around.

The whole fight over whether assertion is governed by a knowledge norm, a truth norm, a belief norm, or a justification norm feels stuck, and I think I know why. Everyone keeps testing the candidate norms against the same three or four cases -- lottery propositions, Moorean conjunctions, selfless assertions -- and those cases just don't discriminate, they all have some story to tell. My hunch is that the debate is quietly running together two different questions: what makes it permisisible for someone to make an assertion, versus what makes it the case that the assertion is defective or criticizable once it's been made. I want to split those apart and then test the candidate norms on each side separately. If the knowledge norm turns out to be right about permissibility but the truth norm right about defectiveness (or some other split), that would actually explain why each camp can point to cases that seem to refute the other.

And I want a fresh case class to work with, because I'm sick of lottery cases. What about assertions whose whole point is to move a joint inquiry along rather than to pass along information -- a researcher thinking out loud "the failure is probably in the caching layer" during a group debugging session, a lawyer offering "the motive was the inheritance" as the theory of the case, a mathematician saying "assume for contradiction that the sequence converges." These aren't (always) insincere and they aren't (always) mere suppositions, but they also don't obviously satisfy any of the proposed norms, and I think they'll put pressure on the act/content distinction.

Precise statement of the question, why the current positions leave it genuinely open (not just under-argued), the method -- what I'd actually do and in what order across six months, including how I'd decide which cases count as the core data -- what a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing. Also a realistic scope check: is this a paper or a dissertation, and if the split I'm proposing turns out not to be a real distinction, is there anything left?
