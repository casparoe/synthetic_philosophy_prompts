I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy or genetics, and I have somehow ended up responsible for the written assessment in a short course on behavioural genetics and its social uses. I need one exam question, answerable in about 45 minutes and roughly 700 words, that cannot be passed by reciting the standard warning that heritability is not destiny, along with a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme.

Build the question around this scenario. A fictional study of twelve-year-olds' arithmetic scores reports a heritability of about 0.85 in a country where schooling, nutrition and after-school care are unusually uniform, and about 0.25 for the same test in a country where those things vary enormously. The estimates come from comparisons of identical and fraternal twins. A commentator concludes that in the first country differences in arithmetic are "mostly genetic, so extra tutoring will do little", while in the second they are "mostly environmental, so that is where the money should go".

Ask candidates to do five things: say precisely what the figure of 0.85 measures and what it does not; explain why this pair of numbers is what the underlying variance model predicts rather than evidence about how strongly genes shape arithmetic, and whether the tutoring claim follows; say what these two within-country estimates can and cannot tell us about why the two countries differ from one another; explain what happens to the "mostly genetic" reading if the same gene variants tend to arrive together with the same surroundings, or if the same variants have different effects in different surroundings, and which assumptions of the twin method the scenario itself gives reason to doubt; and say what evidence would actually settle whether tutoring helps, and how a defensible version of the commentator's claim would be worded.

The marking scheme should consist of five must-hit points worth four marks each, a one-line note for each on the error that typically costs marks, and a statement of what separates an A answer from a B answer. Please end with a single final line of the form \answer{...} containing, in a short phrase, the misconception the question is designed to catch.
