I write literary fiction — three novels in, no philosophy training to speak of — and my whole method is close third person: the camera never leaves my protagonist's head, no omnicient asides, and any moral verdict the reader walks away with has to arrive disguised as the character's own rationalization. I keep circling Kierkegaard's Abraham, the one where the ethical gets teleologically suspended for something higher and the poor guy can't explain himself to Sarah, to Isaac, to anyone. Restate that problem as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually work in — interiority, scene vs summary, what the reader can and can't be told, sympathy as a machine, the gap between what a character believes and what the book is arguing. Then give me the residue report: what carries over cleanly, what I'd have to fake or distort, and what has no counterpart in my toolkit at all — and tell me whether that leftover means my vocabulary is missing something or that Kierkegaard's paradox is doing less work than it sounds like.
