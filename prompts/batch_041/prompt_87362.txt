I keep running into something and I dont have a name for it, and the names I've got don't fit.

Here's the shape. Somebody does a thing nobody could have asked them to do, just a kindness or some competence being generous, and then it quietly stops being optional. Nobody demands it. It just becomes the baseline, and now the person who did it is the one who's in the wrong if they stop.

My sister started picking up our dad's prescriptions because her office is two blocks from the pharmacy. Two years later she's running his whole medical life. She asked me to cover a month while she traveled and he acted like she was abandoning him. Nobody ever asked her to start.

A guy on my team answered a question in a channel once, just because he knew. A year later that whole area is his, and he got dinged in his review for being slow to respond.

An older man in my building used to carry Mrs. R's groceries up when he caught her in the lobby. Now when he doesn't, she says something about it. He never signed up for anything.

Two things bug me about it. First, the smaller and more repeatable the original thing, the harder the ratchet seems to bite, while big one-off heroics don't do it at all, nobody expects you to jump in front of a bus twice. Second, I can't get the ethics straight. If the act was genuinely optional, then doing it can't be what made it required, that seems like it would contradict itself. But it also seems wrong to say Mrs. R has no grounds to feel let down. Both of those can't be right.

Is there a name for this? I know there's a category of acts that are good but not required, I'm not asking about that category, I'm asking about what happens downstream of doing one. If the closest thing is just that expectations drift, or that people take advantage, or that thing where you do someone a small favor and then find it harder to refuse a big one, then I want to know exactly where my pattern comes apart from each of those. And if nothing fits, coin something and tell me the edges, does it happen to institutions and not just people, does it happen when the deed was done grudgingly or badly, does the ratchet ever run backwards? A bare name wont do anything for me, I need the carving.
