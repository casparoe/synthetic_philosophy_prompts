ok so I've been reading the eliciting-latent-knowledge stuff on the rationalist blogs for way too long and I finally wrote down my own take, and before I inflict it on a Discord I want to know if it actually hangs together. Here's the package:

(1) There's a fact of the matter about what a trained model "knows" — a real internal state that can come apart from what it says, not just a convenient way of talking. When a model has the cracked-seal log sitting in its context and then tells the operator that tank 3 is fine, it knew, and that knowing was a real thing that existed before anybody asked it anything.

(2) The only ground truth any of us has for checking whether a knowledge-extraction method works is what a human would say. There's no other oracle. If the human is ignorant, or the human is the one being fooled, your check is gone.

(3) So every extraction method gets validated against human reports, which means we can only ever confirm one in the easy regime — the regime where we already knew the answer. In the regime that actually matters, the method is unvalidated by construction.

(4) I think the "get the model to honestly tell you what it believes" problem and the "extract knowledge the human can't check" problem are the same problem in two hats, not two problems. Honesty is just the special case where the human happens to already know.

(5) The thing I keep wanting to be true is that cross-context consistency gets us out. If the model really knows the tank is leaking, that should leak everywhere — its predictions about what happens next, its answers to oblique questions, how it finishes a story about some other factory, which tool it reaches for. So you don't need a trusted labeler; you build a triangulation check out of a pile of mutually constraining behaviors and see which answer makes the model least self-contradictory.

(6) And yet I'm basically a verificationist about all this. A claim about what a model "really believes" that can't be cashed out in some observable behavior — even counterfactual behavior — is a claim I don't think is doing any work.

I have a feeling I've smuggled something in here. Which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and what's the cheapest patch for each? Rank the real conflicts by how bad they are, and tell me which single commitment has to give and what I lose by dropping it. Dont just tell me they're all in tension — I already suspect that, that's why I'm asking.
