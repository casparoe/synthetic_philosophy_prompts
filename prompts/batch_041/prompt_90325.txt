I'm a software engineer and I own the "giving" feature in our app — the round-up-your-purchases thing, where we pool the money and feature a handful of charities every quarter, and users can pick which one their round-ups go to. Right now we pick the featured ones by vibes plus whatever has the cleanest numbers on the charity-evaluator sites, and its been bugging me, because I've been reading about effective altruism and the pushback against it and I think our process is basically a case study in everything the critics complain about. So I want to turn the critiques into an actual decision procedure my team can run, since none of us has a philosophy background and we're not gonna go read a stack of papers.

The complaints I keep running into, roughly: (1) measurability bias — we fund whatever comes with a nice number attached and quietly skip stuff that's hard to quantify even if it might matter more; (2) the "earning to give" advice, where the move is take the high-paying job and write a check, which people say lets you off the hook and doesn't change anything real; (3) the institutional one — the orgs doing the evaluating are kind of grading their own homework, so a charity can look amazing in the spreadsheet and still be a mess internally, and nobody outside can really check.

Here's what I need. A procedure we can actually run every quarter, on the shortlist, in about an hour, by people who don't know the jargon. Ordered questions, what counts as a good-enough answer to each, where it just stops with a no, and where it has to hand the call to a human because it's a values question and not a facts question. Assume we can't get real numbers for a lot of things — plenty of our candidates don't have a trial and never will — so the procedure has to say what to do when the number isn't there, instead of just dropping those. Label which of the three complaints each step is meant to address, so I can tell if we're just paying lip service. And be honest about which steps are real reasoning vs. me dressing up a gut call as a procedure.

Two constraints: legal will kill anything that looks like political advocacy or lobbying, so that's off the table no matter how good it is; and our community manager picks the final list, with our CEO able to veto, which he has done.

Then run it on these three, which are close to what our shortlist actually looks like: (A) a global-health org with published trials and a clean cost-per-life-saved number; (B) a small mental-health org in a region with no good data, so the numbers are basically guesswork but the need looks huge; (C) a well-known org with a slick site, big claims, and impact figures we can't independently verify, but it's popular with our users and the CEO loves it. One of these is the one I personally want to fund, so don't be surprised if the procedure tells me I'm wrong — I'd rather find out now.

Also tell me what the procedure can't do, where it'll produce garbage, and whether any of the three critiques don't actually apply to what we're doing (I suspect at least one of them doesn't map onto us at all).

Write it in American English, and skip the citations and reading lists — I can't look anything up anyway.
