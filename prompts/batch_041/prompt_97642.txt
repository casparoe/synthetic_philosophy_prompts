I'm putting together a handout for a seminar on standing to blame and hypocrisy, and I want a sanity check on my taxonomy before I inflict it on students. I've sketched six views on what grounds your standing to blame someone, and I want you to tier them — S/A/B/C/D/F, or just an ordering, whatever's cleaner — by how defensible you find each, with a sentence or two each on why it lands where it does. The views:

(a) Ledger view: standing is a balance you build by being good and burn by being bad; you can blame someone only if your balance beats theirs on the relevant trait.
(b) Reciprocity view: you have standing iff you'd accept the same blame from them were the roles reversed.
(c) Authorship view: standing requires that you've publicly endorsed the norm and put yourself under it.
(d) Epistemic view: standing is just being in a position to know the wrong occurred; hypocrisy is a red herring.
(e) Eliminativist view: there's no special standing to blame at all, only whether the blame fits and whether voicing it is wise.
(f) Relational view: you can only blame people you're in some norm-governed relationship with.

Tier them, and at the end tell me which one you think is doing the most hidden work in real-life blame fights without anyone naming it.
