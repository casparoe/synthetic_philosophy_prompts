So I volunteer at a community mediation centre — I have no background in any of this, no law degree, just the weekend training they give you, and I sit in a room with two people who've fallen out and try to get them to a written agreement. Probaly two or three of these a month, and the thing that keeps coming up is promises. Not contracts, nothing with money in escrow, just: he said he'd do X, I planned my life around X, he didn't do X. And I have to sit there and help them work out whether the promise still counts, and honestly I'm making it up as I go.

I've read alot about this on my own and I want to actually use it. What I've picked up: that a promise isn't just a prediction plus guilt, it's supposed to hand the other person some kind of authority over a bit of your future self; that Hume thought promising was an invented tool, something we made up because it's useful for living together, not a deep fact about the universe; that Rawls talked about promising as a practice with rules, so whether you're inside the practice at all comes before whether you're bound by it; and there's an argument I half remember that the real injury in a broken promise isn't the loss, it's being treated as someone whose plans don't have to be counted. I'd like all that turned into something I can run in a 90 minute session.

So give me a procedure. The questions to ask, in order, one at a time, in plain language I can say out loud to two upset people who are already talking over each other. For each question, what counts as a real answer and what's just them relitigating. Mark clearly the points where the procedure stops and the answer is "the promise stands, and here's why" or "the promise is dead, and here's why" — and mark the points where the honest answer is that this is about what they owe each other rather than about what's true, and I have to hand it back to them. Assume I have no authority, nothing to enforce with, the room is small and emotional, and sometimes I'm doing this over the phone with ten minutes notice. Also assume I can't look anything up mid-session.

Then run it start to finish on three cases:

1. Two brothers. The older one promised the younger half the proceeds if the younger did the gutting and rewiring on a house. The younger one did it, two years of weekends. Then the older one's wife got sick, and that half is now the difference between her being cared for at home and going into a facility. The older one wants out.
2. A woman promised her dying mother she'd never put her father in a home. Five years on the father has dementia, she's the only one doing any of it, and she's coming apart. She wants out. Her brother says a promise to a dying woman is the most binding kind there is.
3. A guy promised his mate he'd back up his story about where he was the night of a crash. I know I'm not a lawyer and this isn't my job, but he's asking me whether he still has to keep it.

I want at least one of those three to come out in a way I won't like, and I want you to say so out loud rather than softening it.

Finally, tell me where the procedure will misfire: what it can't tell apart, what kind of promise it'll get wrong, and whether any step in it is just my values dressed up as a test. One page, in order, so I can keep it in my bag.
