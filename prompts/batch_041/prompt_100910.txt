I hold the view that Hofstadter's superrationality is the correct decision rule for symmetric problems, and I want a serious map of its exposure. Take the Platonia case: twenty people are told that if exactly one of them sends a telegram by noon tomorrow, that person wins a billion dollars, and if zero or more than one telegram arrives, nobody wins anything; the superrational prescription is for each to send with probability one in twenty. What I want to know is what would count against treating that prescription as the rational one -- sorted by how decisive it would actually be, not merely how often it gets repeated.

I have in mind several candidate defeaters and want them weighed rather than listed. First, that superrationality is just evidential decision theory with an extra and unearned premise of identical reasoning across agents. Second, that it silently relies on a correlation between my choice and the choices of others that exists in the toy cases only because the agents are stipulated to be replicas of one another, and that in real strategic situations this correlation is either absent or wishful. Third, the empirical record: when the Luring Lottery was actually run, with readers invited to send a postcard stating how many entries they wanted for a prize of one million dollars divided by the total number of entries, some contestants submitted entries expressed as enormous numbers -- one reportedly filling a postcard with an expression for the largest value it could denote -- and the prize was annihilated. I want an honest verdict on whether that outcome is evidence against the rule or only evidence about who happened to be reading. Fourth, that the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability by restricting its scope to symmetric games among reasoners who reason alike, so that any counterexample is reclassified as an asymmetric case or as an instance of flawed reasoning.

Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which a committed superrationalist should concede as counting against them and which they should dismiss, and say plainly whether the unfalsifiability charge is a genuine defect in a view of this kind or a price worth paying. I would find the second objection the most threatening if it could be made precise, so give it particular attention: say exactly what the view needs to be true about the relation between my deliberation and yours, and what would show that this relation fails in the cases where superrational and equilibrium reasoning diverge.
