Ok so I'm trying to pin down my view on whether generations are real kinds and I want you to try and break it. My view: Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z and the rest aren't real kinds at all — they're artifacts of our habit of slicing continuous birth years into roughly 20-year bins, and nearly everything people credit to "a generation" is really an age effect or a period effect wearing a cohort costume, which is hard to rule out because age, period and cohort are tangled together and a single snapshot can't pull them apart. So the labels are convenient at best.

What would count against this? Sort the defeaters by how much they'd actually hurt: what would the evidence have to look like for a generation to be a genuine kind, which parts of that might already be in place, and which objections to me are just restating the thing I'm denying? I'd like the honest version of whether I've made myself unfalsifiable — if every apparent cohort difference gets reclassified as age or period, is anything left that could count against me? And is "real cohorts, fake generations" a coherent stopping point or a cop-out?

For what it's worth, what would move me most is someone showing the boundaries are discoverable rather than stipulated — that you could in principle find where one cohort ends and the next begins, instead of just drawing a line at 1980 or whenever.
