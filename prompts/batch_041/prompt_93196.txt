For a seminar on commodification I have written up my own position and want a consistency check before I present it. Here is what I hold.

1. A voluntary transaction between competent adults is presumptively permissible; the burden of justification rests on whoever would prohibit it, and neither the fact that a good is valued non-instrumentally nor the fact that a transaction strikes people as distasteful is by itself enough to discharge that burden.

2. There should be no legal market in human kidneys.

3. Anti-price-gouging laws are unjustified: in the aftermath of a disaster, sellers should be free to charge what the market will bear, since rising prices both ration the scarce good and draw in new supply.

4. Buying and selling votes should be illegal, and this holds even when the transaction is voluntary, transparent and free of deception.

5. What is wrong with commodifying a good, when it is wrong, is that putting it on the market changes what it is: it turns a good of one kind into a good of another, and it is that alteration of meaning, rather than any harm to third parties, that is the wrong-making feature.

I cannot tell whether these hang together. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each? For every genuine tension, say what would have to give, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please answer in British English.
