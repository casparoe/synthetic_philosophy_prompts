My lab's reading group has spent the last few weeks on the debate over releasing frontier model weights, and we keep running into the same move: someone says that releasing weights is just open-sourcing, so the standard case for open software carries over — more people can inspect the artifact, defects get caught faster, the ecosystem innovates on top of it, and keeping it closed mainly protects incumbents rather than the public. The conclusion they want is a default presumption in favor of release, with the burden of proof falling on anyone who would withhold. I would like an audit of the analogy itself rather than of the politics.

Begin with an explicit correspondence: what plays the role of the source code, the compiler or build system, the maintainer, the license, the user community, the bug report, and the patch. Then identify where the mapping breaks, and sort the breaks into those that bear on the safety conclusion — that scrutiny, and therefore safety, follows from release — and those that bear only on the innovation conclusion.

I am especially interested in two things. First, the inspectability premise: the open-software case assumes that those who hold the artifact can read it and see what it does. What happens to the argument when the artifact is a set of parameters whose behavior is opaque even to the lab that produced it, so that a release is closer to shipping a compiled binary than to publishing source? Second, irreversibility: a repository can be patched, forked, deprecated, or license-restricted after the fact, whereas a released weight set cannot be recalled, and I would like to know how much of the argument depends on the possibility of a recall.

There is also a narrower objection in the group that a weights-only release is not open source at all, since what would let an outsider understand or reproduce the system — the data and the training pipeline — is precisely what is withheld. Tell me whether that objection strengthens or weakens the analogy as an argument for release.

Finally, say whether the software analogy, once its disanalogies are on the table, still supports any general presumption in favor of release, or whether it was carrying that conclusion on its own. If a different analogy better fits the specific question of the marginal risk added by a weight release, propose one and give me its correspondence table as well.
