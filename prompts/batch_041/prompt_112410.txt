I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep running into a pattern I can't quite name. France has a legal 35-hour week, but the people doing the high-status work are often exempt and end up working way more than that. Japan has karoshi, death from overwork, and a culture where long hours are read as a sign of seriousness. So in both places you get a formal right to leisure sitting right next to a moral culture that treats overwork as a virtue, and the people who burn out are often the ones with the most freedom to set their own hours. What philosophical commitments does that arrangement actually presuppose? Does it assume work is a calling and leisure is a reward you earn, that only some people need protecting from themselves, or that a right to rest is compatible with a duty to keep producing? I definately want the implicit theory reconstructed, and I want to know whether the practice is coherent or just a compromise between incompatible ideas. What would have to change if we took the right to leisure seriously for everyone?
