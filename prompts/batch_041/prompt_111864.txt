I'm a retired chemist (polymer guy, forty years at a bench) who's been filling his retirement with philosophy, and free will has me hooked in a way that's honestly a little embarrassing. My old lab mate and I argue about it every Thursday over lunch — he's a hard determinist and I can't even tell you what I am — and lately I've noticed my gut verdicts on the thought experiments don't line up the way I'd have bet they would. So here's what I'd like from you: read off my verdicts below, tell me what principle (or small set of competing principles) actually captures them, say what each principle predicts about cases I haven't listed, and be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to give up. I know the famous version of this with the neuroscientist and the implanted device, that's roughly where I got started, but I've been fiddling with it and now I've got a pile of my own.

Here are my gut calls. (1) The classic setup: a neuroscientist has wired my brain so that if I were about to decide against doing something, he'd make me do it — but I decide to do it on my own and he never lifts a finger. I say I'm fully responsible; the device is just irrelevant scenery. (2) Same dormant device, except that the reason I decide it on my own is that this same neuroscientist, years ago, quietly rewired my values so that I'd want to. Now I feel like it's somehow not really mine, even though nothing forced me at the moment. (3) No device anywhere, I just do it because of the ordinary character my parents and my school and my own choices built over fifty years — fully responsible, no hesitation. (4) A tumor pressing on a bit of my brain makes me do the exact same act, with the same deliberation running through my head — I don't hold myself responsible at all, or barely. (5) I stay in my chair and do nothing, and it turns out there was a dormant device that would have hauled me up if I'd ever started to move, so I never actually could have gotten up — I still think I'm responsible for staying put. (6) A man says he'll kill me unless I do it, and I do it — diminished, but not zero. (7) Same threat, but suppose I'd have done it anyway that afternoon even if he'd never opened his mouth; now the threat seems to stop excusing me altogether. I keep going back and forth on whether 1 and 4 can both be right, since in both of them I couldn't have done otherwise, and whether 2 and 3 can both be right, since in both my character is doing the work.

My half-formed principle is something like: I'm responsible when the act flows from my own settled reasons and character, and not responsible when something bypasses or overrides that from outside. Does that survive contact with all seven? It seems to make a mess of 2 versus 3 (the rewiring is character too, isn't it?) and of 4 (the tumor is arguably just a fact about me now). Also tell me what it says about one more case I've been chewing on: a man with a craving he wholeheartedly endorses, who doesn't want to be rid of it and uses because he wants to — is he responsible, and does your principle get him right, or is that where it breaks? I'd rather have a clean principle that loses a verdict or two than a baroque one that saves everything, so don't be shy about telling me which of my seven to drop.
