I don't have any philosophy training, I've just been reading stuff online and arguing with friends, and I want a sanity check on my views about disagreement and self-doubt before I get into it with someone. Could you act like a philosopher who actually specializes in the epistemology of disagreement and higher-order evidence, and genuinely think this through instead of just listing positions? Feel free to brainstorm first. Here's where I've landed:

1. If someone who seems just as informed and just as sharp as me disagrees with me about something, I should move my confidence toward theirs -- not all the way, but some.
2. For any given pile of evidence, there's exactly one level of confidence that's the rational one to have. It's not like two people with the exact same evidence can both be perfectly reasonable while disagreeing.
3. If I find out my reasoning was distorted -- I was furious, or half asleep, or I'd already made up my mind -- that's real evidence about the thing I was reasoning about, so it should lower my confidence in the conclusion, not just in my reasoning process.
4. I think you can rationally believe something while also thinking that believing it isn't actually rational -- you can see you're not being reasonable and yet, honestly, staying put is the right call. I've seen a word for this, something like akrasia.
5. At the same time, I don't think someone pointing out that I was biased should make me instantly cave. It's one more thing to weigh, not a trump card.

Do these hang together? I want to know which ones are actually pulling against each other and how badly, which ones just look like they clash but really don't, and for each real conflict what the cheapest way out is and what I'd be giving up to take it. Please rank the conflicts by how bad they are instead of saying they're all roughly fine, and tell me whether one single change would clean up the most.
