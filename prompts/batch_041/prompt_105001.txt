Last night a friend and I argued for several hours about whether two people who disagree about fundamental values can ever reach a compromise that leaves each of them better off — the way two parties holding different goods can both gain by trade — and we kept sliding past each other. I would like you to reconstruct the dispute as a dialogue between two speakers, each defending one of the positions we were pressing, so that I can finally see where the real disagreement lives.

The two speakers should be, first, a proponent of the view that there are genuine gains from trade in moral compromise, and second, a proponent of the view that the very idea is confused, because values admit of no common measure and therefore no rate of exchange. Make them equally competent and equally serious: neither should be a strawman, and each should answer the other's actual points rather than restating his or her own. Let them concede where a point lands and change tack where it does not, and let the exchange run long enough that a reader can feel the argument bend under its own weight.

I want them to press especially on the following, since these are the places where our own argument kept getting stuck:

- the difference between compromising about what to do and compromising about what to value, and whether the first can be rational while the second is not;
- whether a person who holds several values at once — including, say, valuing the friendship itself or the other person's good — can be made better off by a package deal even though each of her individual values is worse served by it;
- whether the middle position is a third thing that neither party actually endorses, and whether that is an objection to compromise or simply what compromise is;
- whether talk of gains quietly imports a common currency of value that the no-trade view denies, and whether the trade analogy can be rebuilt without one;
- and whether, once all this is laid out, the two are really disagreeing about values at all, or about the structure of practical reasoning.

Then, after the dialogue, give me a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- question: a one-sentence statement of the precise question the dialogue turns on.
- speaker_positions: an array of two objects, each with name, core_claim (one sentence), and strongest_argument (one or two sentences).
- agreements: an array of strings, each a claim both speakers come to accept.
- cruxes: an array of objects, each with issue, what_would_move_the_first_speaker, and what_would_move_the_second_speaker.
- unanswered_objections: an array of objects, each with speaker_who_raised_it, target, and objection.
- assessment: an array of objects, each with issue, better_supported_view (the name of one speaker, or "unresolved"), and reason.
- open_questions: an array of strings.
