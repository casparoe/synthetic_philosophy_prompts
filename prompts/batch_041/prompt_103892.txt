For a decision-theory reading group on acausal blackmail, I would like a pantoum of three or four quatrains. The setup the poem has to carry: an agent is threatened by a party that can neither affect it nor be affected by it — no causal channel runs either way — yet the threat stays live, because the threatener is a good predictor running a similar decision procedure, so the agent reads its own choice as evidence about what the threatener has already done. The standard defense is a surrogate goal: before any threat arrives, the agent takes on an additional, trivial goal and comes to care about it as much as about what it really values, so that a threatener who menaces the surrogate gets the same reaction as one who menaces the real thing, and the blackmail buys nothing. Write the poem in the voice of an agent who has just adopted such a goal, addressing the blackmailer. Let the form carry the argument: since a pantoum reuses each stanza's second and fourth lines as the next stanza's first and third, let the threat come back wearing the agent's answer, and let the closing quatrain restore the opening lines so the poem ends where it began, at the fixed point a predictor would have found. A reader who knows the setup should be able to tell which recurring line is the threat and which is the surrogate, and to see why, by the last stanza, the two are hard to distinguish.
