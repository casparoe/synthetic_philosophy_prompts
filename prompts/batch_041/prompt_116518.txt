A colleague of mine is a moral responsibility skeptic in Galen Strawson's mould. She accepts the Basic Argument: you act as you do, in any situation, because of the way you are; to be ultimately responsible for what you do you would have to be ultimately responsible for the way you are, and for the way you were when you came to be that way, and so on without end, so that ultimate responsibility would require being the cause of oneself. Her conclusion is that no one is ever ultimately responsible for anything, whether determinism is true or not.

What puzzles me is her record on the neighbouring question of whether a deed is genuinely the agent's own. She insists this is not the responsibility the Basic Argument targets, yet her verdicts on it are anything but uniform.

A woman of unremarkable upbringing acts on a cruelty she reflectively endorses and would not wish to be without: the deed is hers.
A man in whom a surgeon installed a violent urge, which he detests and fights, and which he acts on only when exhausted and incapable of resistance: the deed is not his.
A man whose cruelty grew out of an ordinary but brutal upbringing, who deplores that streak in himself, who was under no compulsion that night, and who could have stopped at any point: the deed is his.
A woman whose tireless helpfulness was installed by a manipulator who wanted a compliant assistant, and which now runs on regardless of what she thinks or wants: the deed is not hers.

The next case put to her is this: a man whose ambitions and loyalties were installed by a surgeon, who now wholeheartedly endorses them, would refuse any offer to have them removed, and would give them up if he came to see sufficient reason to.

Predict her verdict. Set out the rules that fit her four earlier verdicts, say what each predicts here, and give me the one you would bet on with your reasons. Tell me which further case would separate the surviving rules. And tell me whether the Basic Argument she accepts leaves room for this whole line of distinction or flattens it, and if it flattens it, what she would have to say to keep it.
