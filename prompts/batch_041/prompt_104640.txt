I'm a teaching assistant for an undergraduate course on moral psychology and metaethics, and I've been asked to grade a short-answer exam before the professor finalizes the section grades. The exam question was: "Much of the evidence about how ordinary people make moral judgments comes from WEIRD populations, and people in other societies often weigh intentions, outcomes, purity, and authority quite differently. Does this cross-cultural variation give us reason to reject moral objectivism — the view that some moral claims are true or false independently of what any person or culture believes? Defend your answer."

The student submitted the following answer, reproduced in full: "The evidence of cultural variation is a real problem for moral objectivism. Since most of our data come from WEIRD samples, our own moral intuitions may just reflect our particular upbringing rather than tracking any universal truth, and objectivists cannot dismiss the variation as noise or as measurement error. Work on the moral/conventional distinction shows that morality is at bottom a matter of convention, since which acts count as moral violations shifts from group to group. And because objectivism is the claim that there are universal moral truths, objectivists owe us evidence that all cultures converge on the same judgments — which no one has ever provided. So the cross-cultural evidence supports relativism and shows objectivism to be a parochial Western view."

Please assign a grade and then give me the specific comments that justify it: which claims are sound, which are wrong, and which sound plausible but don't actually hold up. I want the comments keyed to particular sentences rather than a general impression, and I want you to be explicit about anything a hurried grader might let pass. Write it as continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — in the form of a note I could send to the instructor.
