I am a graduate student in philosophy, and I have been spending a lot of time thinking about a decision problem that a friend in the mathematics department introduced to me. The problem is this: a fair coin is tossed until it lands heads. If it lands heads on the first toss, you receive -2 dollars; if on the second toss, you receive +2 dollars; if on the third toss, you receive -8/3 dollars; if on the fourth toss, you receive +4 dollars; and in general, if it lands heads on the nth toss, you receive (-1)^n * (2^n)/n dollars. The expected monetary payoff, if you sum the outcomes in the order of increasing n, is the sum from n=1 to infinity of (1/2^n) * (-1)^n * (2^n)/n, which simplifies to the sum from n=1 to infinity of (-1)^n/n. That series converges to -ln 2. But it converges only conditionally: the series of absolute values is the harmonic series, which diverges. Because the series is conditionally convergent, the terms can be rearranged to make the sum equal any real number whatsoever, or to make it diverge to positive or negative infinity. So the game seems to have a well-defined expected value of -ln 2 only if we sum in the natural order of n, but that order is not privileged in any obvious way. I find this deeply puzzling, and I would like you to describe the most important implications of this game for the theory of rational choice under uncertainty.

In particular, what does it imply for the principle that a rational agent should maximize expected value? Does it undermine the very concept of expected value as a guide to action, or can expected value be salvaged by some refinement? What are the implications for the distinction between risk and uncertainty, and for the use of expected utility theory in economics and public policy? I am especially interested in implications that are not merely philosophical curiosities but that might affect how we model decision-making in practice, such as in cost-benefit analysis, in the design of insurance contracts, or in the regulation of financial products with complex payoff structures. Please be concrete and explain the reasoning behind each implication.
