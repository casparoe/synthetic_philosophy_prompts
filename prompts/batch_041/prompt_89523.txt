Write a short dialogue between two philosophers on whether dreams are experiences, with both speakers competent and neither a strawman; I want a real exchange rather than a polite summary of two positions. I have gone back and forth on this for years, and my difficulty is that each side seems to be answering a different question, so I want to see them forced onto the same ground.

The first takes the Wittgensteinian line: a dream is not an experience but a way of speaking; the dream is constituted by the waking report; nothing counts as checking a dream report except against another report; and talk of dream experience borrows the grammar of seeing while omitting the machinery. The second holds that dreams are hallucinatory experiences of an entirely ordinary kind, occurring while the subject is asleep, and that the first has mistaken our criteria for talking about dreams for the thing being talked about. Each should concede whatever is genuinely right in the other's last move and then press the weakest claim in it, and each reply should address the point actually just made. Neither may win by definitional fiat, by an appeal to ordinary usage, or by quietly changing the subject.

The material I want them to fight over, because it is where my own thinking stalls: sleep researchers wake subjects out of REM sleep and take dream reports on the spot, so the report is sometimes made during the sleep period rather than after it; sounds and touches delivered to a sleeping subject turn up in the dream that is then reported; some sleepers report knowing, while dreaming, that they were dreaming; and yet nothing verifies a dream report except further reports, or recordings that at best correlate with what was reported.

Force each of them to say what evidence would count for their side and what they would have to deny. No citations, no name-dropping: the argument has to be carried by the cases above. Keep it to roughly eight exchanges, with no narration or scene-setting, and leave it unresolved but narrowed, so that by the last exchange the two of them have located the single claim that one of them has to be wrong about. I care about the crux, not the prose.
