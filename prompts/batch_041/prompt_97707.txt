My half-formed idea is this. The standard picture of supererogation treats it as a category of action that is morally good but not required, and it usually illustrates this with heroic or saintly sacrifice. But many of the acts we actually praise as going beyond what is expected are small and low-cost: remembering to ask a grieving friend how they are doing, inviting someone who is always left out, speaking up in a conversation when it would be easier not to. These acts do not seem to require great sacrifice, and yet they also do not seem to be simply obligatory. I suspect the standard cost-based criterion is doing too much work, and that we need to distinguish between sacrificial supererogation and something like attentive supererogation.
