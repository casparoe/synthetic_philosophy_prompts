I've come around to thinking that anthropic/multiverse reasoning is a genuine form of empirical confirmation, not just a way of shielding a theory from disconfirmation. Which ensemble you posit — string landscape, eternal inflation, whatever — matters less to me than the structure: a distribution over the fundamental paramaters plus a selection effect, which together let you say something about our own patch of the world, and which can come out wrong. So what would count against it? Give me the actual defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, including anything that has arguably already happened or conspicuously failed to happen.

Two things I especially want you to press on. First: is there any observation the view assigns lower probability to than a rival single-universe story does, or does the missing measure mean every observation comes out roughly equiprobable, so we can't lose? If it's the latter, does the same complaint sink quantum mechanics, since amplitudes dont rule anything out either — or is there an asymmetry that saves one and not the other? Second: of the usual worries — the measure problem, Boltzmann brains, the charge that fine tuning was never well-defined to begin with — which would the view's own defenders already concede as a real cost rather than waving off as a misunderstanding? And if the core survives all that, is it still risking enough to count as science, or has it been qualified into something that cant be wrong?
