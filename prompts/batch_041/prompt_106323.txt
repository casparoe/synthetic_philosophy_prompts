I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy, and my prospectus is on causation by omission: whether absences, failures to act, and other non-events can stand in causal relations at all, and what the answer implies for the act/omission distinction as it is used in ethics and in law. I have about two years and no empirical resources, so I need to commit to the question that most of the rest of the field depends on rather than the one that is currently most discussed.

The candidate questions I have collected, in roughly the form I have put them to myself, are these. First, what the causal relatum of an omission is -- an absence, a negative event, or a fact -- and whether any available answer forces a commitment to negative entities. Second, whether an omission counts as a cause only relative to a norm or expectation, so that "she did not water the plants" is causal only against a background of what was expected of her, and if so whether the resulting causal claims are objective or interest-relative. Third, whether prevention and the prevention of a preventer (the double-prevention cases) can be handled by a single account of causation or require two distinct notions. Fourth, whether difference-making accounts can admit omissions without making every absence in the universe a cause, and whether they need a contrast class in order to stop that slide. Fifth, diffuse and collective omissions, where no individual's omission makes a difference but the aggregate plainly does -- climate-scale cases are the obvious ones. Sixth, omissions by artifacts and automated systems, for instance a system that fails to flag something it should have, and whether the norms that license omission-causation apply to anything other than an agent. Seventh, whether the act/omission asymmetry as actually administered in law and medicine is underwritten by whatever the metaphysics of omission-causation turns out to be, or is quietly undermined by it.

Please rank these by what deserves my two years, and defend the ranking. I am not asking for a survey of positions or for a reading list; I want the reasoning. Structure the response as follows.

Ordering: an ordered list of all seven, one line of rationale each, about 100 words total.

The top three: one subsection for each, roughly 200 words apiece, covering why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for a philosopher working alone with conceptual tools, and what specific result would confirm that it belonged at the top.

Dependencies: about 150 words on which questions presuppose answers to which, and where my ordering would be fragile if a presupposition failed.

Deprioritized: about 100 words on what I should drop or defer, and what I lose by doing so.

What would change the ordering: about 100 words on the arguments or developments over the next year that should move any of these up or down.
