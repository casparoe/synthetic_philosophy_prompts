I host a monthly discussion evening at my local library — about a dozen adults, none of them with any philosophical training, ninety minutes, a whiteboard, no handouts — and next month I want to spend the session on the wasp. The story I have in mind is the one about the solitary wasp that provisions a burrow: she paralyses an insect, drags it to the entrance, goes inside to check the burrow, comes back out for the insect, and in the version I remember will run that loop over and over if you keep moving the insect a few inches away while she is inside. I have seen the word sphexishness used for it, and I have never checked where the story comes from, so please do not build anything on its provenance. What I want the group to leave with is a grip on the difference between behaviour that is rigid and behaviour that is flexible, and on why that difference is not the same as the difference between caused and uncaused — which is where I expect the group, and me, to slide straight into talking about free will.

Plan the session rather than explaining the idea to me. Tell me what has to be in place before the wasp can do any work at all, and in what order the remaining pieces have to come; which example should carry the whole evening — I would rather it were not the wasp, since the wasp is too easy to laugh at, but tell me plainly if that instinct is wrong, and what the carrying example needs to have and to lack; the misconceptions you expect this particular group to form, with the point in the sequence at which each should be headed off; one question to end on that would show me whether they have got it; and what I should refuse to go into at this level, and say out loud that I am refusing.

Two things of mine to take seriously. First, when I am interrupted halfway through locking up the house at night I go back and start the whole sequence again from the front door, and I have been telling myself that this is sphexish; I would like to know whether that is right, and what would have to be true of me for it to be. Second, I have been carrying around a distinction of my own — being rigid about a goal versus being rigid about the route to it — and I want to be told whether that is a real distinction or something I invented. Make the calls rather than laying the options side by side; if some other example would serve the group better than any I have named, choose it and defend the choice. No reading list and no citations — I cannot check them and neither can the group.
