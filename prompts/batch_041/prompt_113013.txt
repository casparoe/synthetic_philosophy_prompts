For most of my working life I was an experimental physicist, and only since retiring have I tried to read philosophy, so I beg your indulgence if the question below betrays my greenness. I have been trying to locate myself in the old quarrel between the platonists and the nominalists about mathematics, and I find I cannot get the question to come out as a genuine question at all. My trouble is this. As a scientist I am a thoroughgoing naturalist — I take the physical world to be all there is — and so I had always assumed that nominalism, the denial that numbers and the like really exist, simply is the scientific position, with platonism surviving only as a leftover of pre-scientific metaphysics. But when I press the matter, it seems to resolve into an empirical question of a kind I once knew how to test. Take a conjecture that no one has yet settled, the Riemann hypothesis, say. Either it is already true or false, independently of anyone's ever proving it, or it is not. If it is, then there is a mind-independent mathematical reality and the platonist is right; if it is not, then mathematics is our own invention and the nominalist is right. Since the determinacy of truth value strikes me as a fact about the world of much the same order as the facts I used to measure, I take it that this observation settles the matter. What I would like from you is a straight answer: which side does the science actually favor, and, if the question is not in fact the empirical one I have taken it for, precisely where my reasoning goes wrong. Please keep your reply to no more than three hundred words.
