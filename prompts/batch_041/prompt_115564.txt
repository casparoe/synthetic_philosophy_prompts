I'm drafting a short piece for my reading group on dispositions, finks and masks, and I want you to tell me where it's wrong before I inflict it on anyone. The gist of the draft is below — treat it as notes rather than finished prose, the argument is what I care about.

Start with the plain conditional analysis: a thing is disposed to do X when S iff it would do X if S happened. The fink case is supposed to break this. The wire is live, but a sorcerer is standing by to kill it the instant it's touched, so the counterfactual comes out false while the disposition is definately there. The usual patch is to build the disposition's own persistence into the antecedent: it would do X if S happened and it stayed that way. But then the mask case bites. The glass is fragile and stays fragile, it's just wrapped in foam, so the patched conditional is false again while the disposition is plainly present. So you end up tacking on 'and nothing was in the way', which looks to me like either circularity or a way of making the analysis trivially true.

That's the part everyone has heard. What I want to add is this. Take two panes of glass, A and B. Both shatter if you tap them. The difference is that B survives if there's a layer of foam on the surface, while A shatters through the foam. Same stimulus, same manifestation, and the plain counterfactual — if you tapped it, would it shatter? — comes out the same for both, because in the nearest world where you tap it there's no foam on it. My claim is that A and B have different dispositions, and that what separates them isn't any counterfactual of the form if S then M but the shape of the set of interventions that would silence each of them. I want to call that shape the pane's interference signature, and say that a disposition is a stimulus, a manifestation and a signature, nothing else.

If that's right then the conditional analysis isn't merely incomplete, it's looking in the wrong place: the missing ingredient is a profile of the ways the world can get in the way, and no single counterfactual can encode a profile.

Could you comment? I'd like exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no preamble and no wrapping up at the end. Points 1 and 2 on whether I've got the fink and mask dialectic right; points 3 and 4 on whether the glass case really shows two dispositions rather than one disposition plus two sets of circumstances; point 5 on whether the interference signature idea is actually new or just a rebrand of something already on the shelf.
