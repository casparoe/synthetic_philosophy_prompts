I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I should like to practise defending the view that all evidence is certain—that so-called uncertain evidence is really just ordinary evidence about probabilities—against a committed Jeffrey conditionalizer. Please play that opponent: someone who holds that experience often leaves us uncertain about the evidence itself, and that the correct response is to condition on a new probability distribution over an evidence partition rather than on a proposition. Be sharp, concede that the formalism has costs, but never concede that ordinary conditioning can handle soft evidence. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a single paragraph, and write in British English. Here is my opening move: in my anomaly detector, the only evidence I ever receive is a calibrated posterior over the hypothesis from a classifier trained on the same data I am trying to update. If I treat that posterior as the new distribution over the evidence partition, the rule tells me to condition on the very thing I wanted to compute. If I use the classifier’s likelihood instead, I am back to ordinary conditioning on a definite observation. So either Jeffrey conditionalization collapses into ordinary conditioning or it requires evidence I never have. Which is it?
