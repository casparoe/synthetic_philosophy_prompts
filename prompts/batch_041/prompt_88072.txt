I'm the critic. Here's my opening, and it's an accounting problem, not a moral one.

I'll grant you all three axioms, the chain of suspicion, and the explosion worry. My claim is that your equilibrium needs a first strike to be cheap, fast and decisive, and at interstellar scale it's none of those. Anything that can sterilize a planet has to be enormous, and enormous things radiate -- anyone with a decent telescope sees it coming for centuries -- and any civ that can build a detector and a dispersal program can also build a weapon, so the target has time to scatter, and the launch itself tells the whole neighborhood where you live. Call the whole bill the kinetic tax. Once that tax is high, shooting first stops being a cheap move and becomes an expensive commitment you have to finance and advertise. And if it's high enough, hiding stops being obviously right: the civ that broadcasts, trades and compounds its tech ends up stronger faster than the civ sitting in the dark burning resources on paranoia. So: is the dark forest an equilibrium of your own model, or is it just the special case where the kinetic tax happens to be low? And if it's a special case, what makes you so sure we're in it?
