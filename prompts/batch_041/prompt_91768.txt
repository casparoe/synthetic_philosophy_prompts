In my political theory class we spent a week on whether the division between left and right is a real division or just a historical accident, and my teacher closed the discussion with an image I have not been able to shake: left and right are the two wings of a single bird. His point was that a bird cannot fly on one wing, so a society needs both camps beating in their different directions; he took this to show that the distinction is not a defect of politics but part of its design, and that the sensible position is therefore to refuse to be a wing and to sit in the middle instead. A classmate objected that the image proves nothing of the kind, since a wing is only intelligible as a wing relative to the body it is attached to, which would make each side a mere organ of the whole rather than a movement with aims of its own.

I would like a careful audit of the wing image as an argument rather than as a decoration. Please set out the correspondence explicitly — what is meant to correspond to the bird, to the body, to each wing, to flight, to the air, to the beating — and then say where the correspondence breaks down. I am especially interested in which of the failures matter for the conclusion my teacher drew (that the distinction is real, that the two sides are complementary, and that centrism follows) and which are incidental. Part of what I want to know is what the image quietly imports: whether it assumes that the two sides share a single goal and a single direction of travel, whether it treats them as mirror images of one another, whether the cooperation of two wings is a model of disagreement at all given that wings never disagree about where the bird should go, and whether it could ever establish that the distinction is real rather than presupposing it. I would also like to know what, if anything, the image implies about what holds each side together — whether a wing is unified by a shared function or by shared aims. Finally, tell me whether the conclusion survives once the failures are on the table, and if it does not, what comparison would do better: one that preserves whatever is genuinely illuminating in the wing image without carrying the rest along with it.

Because I intend to use this in an essay, please state how confident you are in each of the claims you make, and single out the one point in your answer you are least sure of.
