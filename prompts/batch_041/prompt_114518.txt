I've spent much of the past year on a long essay about Malthus — not the caricature he is usually reduced to, but the actual mechanism he proposed — and as part of the work I forced myself to write down, mostly for my own clarity, where I actually stand. The difficulty is that the list now reads to me less like a single position than like several positions wearing one coat, and I'd like a disinterested audit before I commit any of it to print. Here it is. (1) I am a Popperian about science: a theory earns its keep by staking itself on a risky prediction, and when that prediction fails, the rational response is to treat the theory as refuted rather than to reach for a patch. (2) I think Malthus was right but early — that the tendency of population to press against the means of subsistence is a real and recurrent feature of human economies, and that what is commonly taken for his refutation is really a matter of timing. (3) I have almost no patience for ad hoc rescue: bolting on auxiliary assumptions after the fact, in order to save a theory the world has already contradicted, strikes me as the beginning of intellectual dishonesty. (4) All the same, I think the demographic transition is a genuine and decisive refutation of the strongest Malthusian forecast, and that any surviving version of the doctrine has been thinned out to the point where it predicts very little. (5) And I think the horizon over which a theory is judged ought to be proportionate to the horizon over which it makes its claims, so that a doctrine speaking in centuries cannot be killed off by a bad decade.

I suspect I cannot hold all five, but I genuinely do not know which pair is doing the damage; some of these seem to me to sit together quite comfortably, and I would rather be told which of those apparent agreements is an illusion than be handed a tidy list of five contradictions. So please treat the set as the object of the exercise: tell me which commitments are actually in tension and how severely, which merely look so and why, and, where something must give, what the cheapest revision would be and precisely what it would cost me — in explanatory power, in honesty, or in both. I would also like your view on which single revision, if I made it, would clean up the most at the least expense; and if you conclude that the package is perfectly consistent and I have merely been spooked by the word "refutation," I would be glad to hear that too, provided you show your working.
