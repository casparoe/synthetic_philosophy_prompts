For six years I worked as a back-end engineer, and three months ago I moved onto a small team that builds models of neural circuits. My new colleagues speak as though the claim that the brain computes were settled background rather than a live thesis, and my own work has begun to lean on it; I would rather know what I am standing on. I want a number, not a survey.

Give me your credence that computationalism is true in a substantive sense: that there is a level of description at which the brain literally implements computations over representations, and that this level is the correct one for explaining cognition — as opposed, on the one side, to its being false, and on the other, to its being true only in a sense so thin that no one who denies it need disagree. Decompose that credence into the sub-questions it actually rests on, and give each a rough probability with a line of justification:

- whether any physical system whatsoever implements some computation under some description, and if so whether that fact trivializes the thesis rather than supporting it;
- whether there is a fact of the matter about which computation a system implements, or whether what a system computes is relative to an observer or to our explanatory interests;
- whether the thesis requires something like classical symbol manipulation over language-like representations, and whether a purely connectionist or dynamical-systems description of the same system would count as computational or would displace the claim;
- whether computation can carry the explanatory weight it is asked to carry, or whether it presupposes the very representation and intentionality it is supposed to explain;
- whether the thesis is empirical — to be settled, in principle, by neuroscience — or conceptual, in which case what would even count as settling it.

Then combine the terms, state the resulting number, and tell me which sub-question the number is most sensitive to and what single argument or experimental result would move it most, in which direction. Finally, and most usefully for me: if a large part of the disagreement between people who answer yes and people who answer no turns out to be about what the word computer is being made to mean rather than about the world, say so plainly, and give me the credence that survives once the merely verbal part of the dispute is set aside.
