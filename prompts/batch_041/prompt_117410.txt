Right, so my epistemology reading group has been grinding through the Gettier literature for what feels like forever and we've basically stopped moving. The shape of it: someone proposes a fix to the justified-true-belief analysis — a no-false-lemmas condition, a defeasibility condition, a causal or reliabilist story, a safety condition, whatever — and within about ten minutes somebody else has a case where the fix either lets a Gettier case through or rules out something that's obviously real knowledge. The fake barn stuff does a lot of work here, and so do the cases where the person's belief is true and well supported but the support runs through a false premise. Then the defender patches it, the patch throws up a new counterexample, round and round. Two people in the group have now decided the whole project is doomed and that knowledge just isn't the sort of thing you can analyse into necessary and sufficent conditions. One of them keeps making the point that if you build knowledge out of a truth-entailing component and a non-truth-entailing component, you can always manufacture a case where the non-truth-entailing bit is satisfied in a lucky way — so the trouble isn't any particular condition, it's the shape of the analysis. The others say that's just pessimism and we haven't hit on the right condition yet.

What I want from you isn't another proposed analysis and definitely isn't a survey of who said what. I want a diagnosis of the standoff. Concretely: is this a substantive disagreement about which cases are knowledge, or is it a disagreement about the data itself — do we actually agree on the Gettier intuitions, or are the two camps quietly disagreeing about whether the word justified in the analysis is meant to be the internalist notion or the externalist one, so that they aren't even arguing about the same condition? Or are they answering different questions under one heading: one lot doing conceptual analysis of the ordinary concept, the other trying to carve out a theoretically useful epistemic kind — the sort of thing you'd want for a science of knowledge — where counterexamples to the folk concept simply don't bite? Or is the impasse a sign that the question is ill-posed from the start?

Give me reasons for whichever diagnosis you land on, engaged with what each side actually says, and then tell me what would count as progress given it: a distinction, a change of question, or an honest arguement that no progress should be expected and why.
