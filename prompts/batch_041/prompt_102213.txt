Ok so I'm not an academic, I just read too much political stuff online and this one problem keeps bugging me. Everyone says democracy is rule by the people, but you have to know who "the people" are before you can ask them anything, and deciding who belongs seems like exactly the kind of thing the people should get to decide. Chicken and egg. I want to make a flowchart poster for my wall that sorts where someone actually lands on this — so act like a political philosopher and walk me from the starting commitment to named positions.

Please don't just list famous names. Build the tree from the logical space first: the branch points should be questions a normal person can answer without already knowing the labels — like, does having a say come from being affected by a decision, or from being bound by it, or from living somewhere, or from something you don't choose at all? Then put the actual positions at the leaves, and when a combination is empty say so and guess why (incoherent, just unattractive, or nobody's bothered?). Route these cases through it so I can watch where they fall: a long-term resident who's fully subject to the laws but can't vote; someone abroad whose crops get wrecked by this country's emissions; people who aren't born yet; and a random lottery-picked mini-public that gets to make binding rules for everyone else. Think it through carefully first and sketch the branch questions before the tree, flag any two leaves you can't really seperate, and keep it to something that fits on one poster — six to eight branch points max.
