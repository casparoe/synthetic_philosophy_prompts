I never studied philosophy formally, but for the past two years or so I have been reading popular books about markets, arguing about them with friends, and keeping notes, and I have arrived at a set of convictions that I suspect cannot all be true at once. Before I defend them in an argument I keep losing, I would like an audit of how they fit together.

Here is the set.

(1) Adults own themselves and their labour, and it is no business of the state's to stop them trading what is theirs. This is why I think a market in kidneys should be legal.
(2) Prices carry information, and markets usually distribute scarce goods better than rules do. This is why I think caps on rents, and on prices during shortages and emergencies, mostly make the shortages worse.
(3) Some things should not be bought and sold at all. Votes belong on that list, and I am inclined to put certain friendships and honours there too.
(4) What makes those sales wrong is not chiefly what happens afterwards but what the sale does to the thing itself. Putting a price on a vote or a friendship does not simply add a transaction to it; it changes what the thing means for everyone, including people who never take part in the market.
(5) I do not accept that the state should protect people from their own bad bargains. Any argument that begins from the thought that we cannot let people sell because they would regret it strikes me as presumptuous.
(6) All the same, I think a price can be genuinely unjust — that a deal can be exploitative even when both sides freely agree to it — and that this is a wrong done to someone, not merely their bad luck.

Tell me which of these pull against each other, which only look as though they do, and how serious each real conflict is, ordered from the gravest to the mildest. For each genuine conflict, say what would have to give and what that would cost me. Then tell me which single change would repair the most at once, and which one of the six is quietly doing the most work in holding the rest together. I am not especially attached to any one of them, and I have no idea where the difficulty lies, so please do not tell me that they are all in tension to some degree. If two of them sit together perfectly well, say so and say why.
