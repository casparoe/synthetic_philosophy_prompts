I am a retired population ecologist. My working life was spent running breeding-bird censuses, which means I spent forty years refusing to infer absence from a null result: a transect that records nothing tells you about your detection probability as much as about the birds. For the last several years, as an amateur, I have been reading in the philosophy of science and in astrobiology, and I am aware that I am probably misusing the vocabulary, so please correct me where I do.

At our monthly discussion group, a retired radio astronomer has been leaning very hard on a single picture, and I would like to know how much weight it can actually bear. He calls it the dark forest, after Liu Cixin's 2008 novel of that name: the galaxy as a wood at night, every civilization a hunter stalking through it in silence, every sound a beacon to something that will shoot. He compresses the reasoning into two axioms — survival is the first need of any civilization, and the matter of the universe is finite while the expansion of a civilization is not — together with two further assumptions, that another party's intentions can never be known, and that a civilization's technology may leap ahead unpredictably. From this he draws two conclusions, which I suspect need to be separated: first, that the silence we observe is explained, because anyone sensible is hiding; second, that we ought to abandon any deliberate transmission of our existence into space. He sometimes slides into what he takes to be an equivalent image, in which we are birds chirping in a tree and wondering why nothing answers while the skies fill with hawks — though chirping is what birds do rather than a decision, and I am not certain the two images license the same conclusion.

What I want is an audit, neither a defence nor a demolition.

Set out the correspondence explicitly: what plays the part of the wood, the trees, the gun, the hunter, the prey, the sound, the silence, and the fact that a hunter must eat. Then tell me where the mapping fails, and which failures are load-bearing for his two conclusions rather than mere colour. I am particularly interested in what the source domain supplies that the target may not have: a shared evolutionary history among the parties, a standing selection pressure that makes concealment adaptive, hunters who are finite and hungry individuals with unified purposes, and a medium in which seeing and being seen occur at the same instant. Which of those does the argument actually need?

Two further lines of pressure. From my own trade: in a wood, silence is partly a fact about the observer, and a null result is not an absence. Does that discipline apply to the sky, and if so, what does it do to his first conclusion? And does a hunter's shot give him away? If striking reveals the striker, the equilibrium he needs may not exist at all.

Finish with a verdict on each conclusion separately, and if there is a better analogy for what a galaxy full of civilizations would actually be like — one that survives contact with both the physics and the biology — I would rather have that than a scorecard.
