A mate of mine works in the claims team at an insurance firm, and they've just launched a policy that pays out for hair restoration, but only if you're 'bald' — that's the word in the policy itself. Cue endless arguements about the borderline cases, so the legal people have come up with a rule: 120 hairs or fewer on the crown counts as bald, 121 or more doesn't. My mate thinks this settles something quite deep. His argument is that the sorites paradox shows a vague word like 'bald' either has a hidden cutoff that nobody could ever know — which he says is just silly, no one is going to discover the true number of hairs — or it doesn't really mean anything at all, so there's no fact of the matter about who's bald. Since the first option is a non-starter, he reckons its got to be the second, and therefore any precise definition is a straight upgrade on a vague one. He's now saying we should do this everywhere: define 'tall' in centimetres, 'heap' in grains, the lot, and the world would be better for it. Is he right about any of this? I've got a feeling the sorites thing is being made to do work it can't do, but I can't quite put my finger on where the reasoning goes wrong. Write your answer in British English please.
