Me and a friend got into an argument about whether a perfect forgery is really as good as the original, and we ended up constructing this thing, which I think proves too much, but I honestly can't find the bad step. Here it is:

(1) The aesthetic value of a painting is the value of the experience it gives to someone who looks at it properly.
(2) Two paintings that look identical, down to the last brushstroke and crack in the varnish, give the same experience to anyone looking at them properly.
(3) So they have the same aesthetic value.
(4) A perfect forgery looks identical to the original it copies.
(5) So a perfect forgery has the same aesthetic value as the original.
(6) If that's right, then discovering the painting you love is a forgery should change nothing about your aesthetic evaluation of it — only your non-aesthetic interests change (what it's worth, whether the museum got swindled).
(7) But when a treasured old master turns out to be a 20th-century forgery, the experience really does deflate, and that doesn't look like mere snobbery or a mistake about money — the painting seems to have less in it, not just less on its price tag.
(8) So a work's aesthetic value is not exhausted by the experience it affords; the object's history — who made it, how, when — is itself aesthetically relevant.
(9) But if history is aesthetically relevant, then every fact about a work's history is aesthetically relevant: that the canvas was once owned by a duke, that it hung in a particular chapel, that it was painted on a rainy Tuesday. Those clearly aren't aesthetic merits.
(10) So history isn't aesthetically relevant after all, and (8) is false.

But (8) seemed forced on us by (7), and (1)-(3) seemed forced on us by the plain fact that we evaluate paintings by looking at them. So where does it go wrong? I'd rather keep (7) than (1) — I really do think the forgery is worse — but I want to know what that costs me. I tried the fix where "the experience" already includes knowing the history, but then it seems like aesthetic value becomes hostage to beliefs, so a viewer who wrongly thinks the fake is real is getting the better experience, which seems backwards. Tell me which step to give up, why it looked fine, and what I'm paying for giving it up.
