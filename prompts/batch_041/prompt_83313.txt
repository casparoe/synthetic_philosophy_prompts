Be specific about what the meta-problem's explanandum is, and about how it relates to illusionism. Here's what the student wrote:

The meta-problem of consciousness is the problem of explaining why we have the problem intuitions we do — why it seems to us that experience is something over and above the physical facts, that no amount of brain science could explain what it's like to see red, that a zombie duplicate of me is conceivable. Chalmers introduces it as a companion to the hard problem: the hard problem asks why physical processes are accompanied by experience at all, while the meta-problem asks why we think there's a hard problem in the first place. It's tractable in a way the hard problem isn't, because the explanandum is a set of judgments and reports, and judgments and reports are the kind of thing cognitive science can get a grip on. Solving it would mean giving a mechanism — probably some mix of introspective opacity, attention, and the way we model our own minds — that produces the intuitions on cue.

As for whether it settles the hard problem: Chalmers is pretty clear that it does. If we can explain why we have the intuition that consciousness is nonphysical without ever invoking nonphysical consciousness, then we've shown the intuition is a quirk of our cognitive architecture rather than a window onto a real explanatory gap. That's essentially Frankish's illusionism, and Chalmers endorses it — the meta-problem is the easy problems wearing a trenchcoat, and once you've solved it the hard problem just dissolves. The only holdouts are people like Dennett, who thinks the meta-problem is trivial, and the panpsychists, who need the problem intuitions to be tracking something real.

One more thing worth adding: the meta-problem is really only a problem for physicalists. Dualists don't have to explain the intuitions, because on their view the intuitions are correct — we really do have nonphysical experiences, so there's nothing surprising about us reporting them.
