Ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole on this guy Galen Strawson and his argument that we can never really deserve blame or praise for anything. I think I get the gist: to be responsible for something you did, you'd have to be responsible for the kind of person you are that made you do it, and to be responsible for that you'd have to have somehow chosen to be that kind of person — but any choice like that already comes out of some earlier version of you that you didn't choose, so it's turtles all the way down and there's no starting point where you're the author of yourself. Fine, I follow that much.

Where I'm stuck is this. It feels like the whole thing only bites if your a determinist. The reason there's no starting point is that every bit of you was caused by something before it. But I don't buy that the universe is fully determined — I think there's real randomness in there, and that in some situations I genuinely could have done otherwise. So doesn't that break the chain and let me be the actual source of my own character? And if that's right, then Strawson's argument is just the old free-will-vs-determinism fight wearing a nicer suit, and it only scares people who already think free will is bunk.

A couple more things that bug me. First, couldn't I just decide to become a different person going forward? Like I don't love that I'm lazy, so I decide to change — doesn't that make me the source of the new me, even if I'm not the source of the old me? Second, my brother loves this argument alot and says it "proves nobody is ever responsible for anything," but then he locks his doors, wants criminals locked up, and makes his kids do chores and grounds them when they don't. That looks like a straight contradiction to me. If nobody's responsible, on what grounds does he punish anyone? Or is he just being inconsistent and Strawson wouldn't back him up?

Can you untangle whether I've got Strawson right, especially on the determinism bit, and tell me which of these worries is the more confused one? And please be upfront about where your own reasoning is most likely to be shaky here — what would have to be true, or what argument would someone have to make, to get you to flip your verdict on whether Strawson's argument actually works?
