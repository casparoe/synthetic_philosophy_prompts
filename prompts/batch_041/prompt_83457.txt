Ok so my day job is loan docs — indentures, covenants, negative pledges, the trustee, acceleration, waivers and amendments, exit consents, holdouts, the whole zoo. I understand commitment devices in that register cold: you promise not to do a thing, somebody outside the deal holds the whip, and if you do the thing anyway there are consequences you can't talk your way out of. Zero philosophy background though, and I keep half-bumping into a philosophical debate about "precommitment" — a person or a whole polity deliberately making it hard or impossible for their future self to do what the future self will want to do. Personal version: the guy tying himself to the mast. Political version: a constitution that makes certain things very hard, or flat out impossible, for a later majority to undo. I want a translation job, not an explainer.

Take the main positions in that debate — the case for binding yourself in the first place, the objections that binding is illegitimate or undemocratic, and the fight over amendment and entrenchment (what happens when the thing that's bound is the procedure for un-binding) — and restate each as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary: issuer and holders, covenant, trustee, acceleration, waiver, amendment threshold, exit consent, holdout, "the instrument says what it says." Don't dress my terms up as if they were doing philosophy. Where a mapping only works if you squint, say so out loud rather than smoothing it.

Then the part I actually care about: what didn't make it across, one item at a time, and whether each item is (a) clean, (b) carried but bent, or (c) no counterpart at all. For the bent and missing ones, tell me whether the loss looks like a hole in my vocabulary or like the philosophers papering over something that was never clear in the first place. And if any of it turns out to hinge on the fact that a covenant needs someone outside the deal to enforce it while a constitution has nobody outside, I want to hear that.

Format: prose for the translation, one short section per position, don't pad. Then a table with exactly these columns — Claim (in the philosophers' terms, one sentence) | Deal-terms translation (one or two sentences) | Verdict (clean / bent / no counterpart) | What's lost or bent | Your read (gap in my vocabulary, or fog in the original?). Then finish with a JSON object and nothing after it, with exactly these fields: "cleanest_translation" (string, the claim that survived best), "worst_residue" (string, the claim that survived worst), "residue_points_to" (one of "gap in the target vocabulary", "vagueness in the source", "both"), "untranslatable_core" (string, the single thing I literally cannot say in my terms), and "is_it_really_about_enforcement" (string, yes/no plus one sentence why).
