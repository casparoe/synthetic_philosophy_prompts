I keep running into a pattern and I don't know if it has a name. Here's the shape of it. Someone compares what they've got against the alternative, except the alternative they compare against isnt the actual alternative -- it's a version of it redrawn from a range that happens to include whatever they've already got. And the comparison always comes out saying the other one is better.

Two examples. My brother and me work at the same firm. He's convinced he's the badly paid one, and his reason is that whatever he's on, it's as likely I'm on double as on half. I ran the identical argument back at him about my own pay and it came out exactly as convincing, so now we both think we're the underpaid one, which can't be right.

Second one, different area. A friend says you should assume any scientific theory you currently accept is probably false, because for any theory there are more ways for it to be subtly wrong than to be exactly right. Maybe, but he'd have said the same about the theory that replaced the previous one, and he'll say it about whatever replaces this one. The argument never changes its verdict, no matter what anyone finds out.

What bugs me is the shape rather than either example. The argument always lands on the alternative, it doesn't care what you learn about your own side, and if you did move to the other side it would just start pointing back the way you came. Is there a name for this move? And whats actually wrong with it -- is it one error, or a family of them wearing the same coat? I ask because there's a version that seems perfectly fine to me: if the other thing really is a random draw from a set whose size I know, then 'the other one is probably better' can be flatly true. So I want the difference between that and the broken ones. Not after a reading list, just the concept and where its edges are.
