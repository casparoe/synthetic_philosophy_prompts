I'm drafting a short symposium piece arguing that the familiar three-way taxonomy of liberty -- negative, positive, republican -- is organized around the wrong question, and I'd like your comments before I revise. My line is this. The taxonomy is usually presented as three competing answers to "what obstructs freedom?": interference, internal impediment, domination. But that framing conflates two separate questions -- what counts as an obstacle, and how robustly the absence of obstacles must be secured -- and once you separate them, the republican contribution turns out to be almost entirely a claim about the second. What matters is not whether anyone interferes with you but whether your non-interference is contingent on another party's goodwill; I call this permission-dependence. To give the claim some teeth I propose a reversal test: hold the powerful party's costs fixed and ask whether their behaviour toward you would change if their preferences changed. If it would, you are dominated even while you are left alone. I run two cases through it: a gated community where the developer retains a veto over every rule change but has never once exercised it, and a platform whose moderation is currently permissive while its policy remains unpublished. Residents and users alike are unrestricted, and I claim they are unfree. The conclusion is deliberately deflationary: republican liberty is not a third kind of thing but the top end of a single continuum, because the negative conception was always implicitly a robustness claim -- Berlin's insistence on non-interference was never meant to be satisfied by a lucky accident.

I expect this to be contentious on three fronts, and those are the parts I most want commented on. First, is my reconstruction of positive liberty a strawman? I treat it as the self-mastery view and dispatch it in a paragraph, and I suspect a defender would say I have missed the point that the real division is between an inner and an outer criterion of obstruction, not between degrees of one thing. Second, does the reversal test do independent work, or does it collapse into an ordinary counterfactual about causation that any negative libertarian would cheerfully accept -- in which case the deflationary conclusion comes too cheap? Third, does turning freedom into a matter of degree rob it of the critical edge the republican is after, since a continuum cannot tell us when domination has actually occurred rather than merely loomed? Please be direct about which of these is fatal and which is a matter of exposition, and propose a fix for whichever you think is fatal; I would rather cut a section than patch it.
