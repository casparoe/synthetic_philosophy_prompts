ok I need a sanity check on my bioethics views before I commit them to a paper. I suspect some of these don't hang together and I genuinely can't tell which ones, so I want an audit rather than encouragement. Here's where I've landed:

1. When you're picking which child to bring into the world — which embryo to implant, whether to go ahead with this pregnancy — I think you're not merely permitted but required to choose the one that's likely to have the best life, or at the very least not to choose a worse one when a better one is sitting right there. Not "it would be nice"; required.
2. At the same time, it's the parents' call, full stop. No state eugenics, no insurers or clinics leaning on people, no law about which embryo gets used.
3. I'm pretty deep in disability rights stuff and I think the social model is basically right: most of what makes a disability a bad deal is how the world is built and how people treat you, not the condition itself. The impairment is real, but the disadvantage is mostly social.
4. That said, I do think it's wrong to deliberately select a deaf child, or to pick an embryo because it'll be deaf. Deafness is a disadvantage, other things equal, and choosing it for a kid isn't ok. I'd say the same about a few other conditions.
5. I also think the non-identity point is right: if you'd picked the other embryo, that kid wouldn't have existed, so you can't say the deaf kid is harmed by being the one you picked. Nobody ends up worse off than they would otherwise have been.
6. And I don't think anyone has a duty to have the best child they could possibly have — no duty to wait for a better moment, no duty to have kids at all. Loving your kid isn't a matter of trait-optimisation. (Two deaf parents who'd prefer a deaf kid, or a couple who can only conceive a deaf child and go ahead — I don't think either of those is wrong either, though maybe that complicates 4? Not sure.)

The one that's bugging me is 1 vs 2 — how can something be required and also entirely the parents' call? — but I have a feeling that's not where the real problem is, and I don't trust my own sense of where the fault lines run.

What I want back is a table, one row per pair or small cluster of commitments that are actually in tension. Don't do every pair mechanically — if two of them only look like they clash, include the row anyway but mark it as apparent so I can see the difference. Columns, in this order:

- commitments: which numbered ones, e.g. "3 + 4"
- kind: genuine or apparent
- severity: 1 to 5, 5 being worst
- mechanism: what exactly pulls against what, a couple of sentences, no more
- cheapest fix: the smallest change that restores consistency
- cost: what that fix makes me give up, or what extra commitment I'd have to take on to make it work

Sort the rows from most severe to least.

Then under the table, three sentences max: which single revision buys me the most consistency, and which one of the six is doing the most damage.
