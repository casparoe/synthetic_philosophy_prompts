I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've become half-convinced by the Gödelian argument that the human mind cannot be a machine. In the form I have in mind — associated with Lucas, and worked out at length by Penrose — you take any consistent formal system strong enough for arithmetic, note that Gödel's construction yields a sentence the system can neither prove nor refute, and observe that we can nonetheless see that the sentence is true, provided the system really is consistent. No such system can match that feat, the argument runs, so the mind is not any such system. I would like you to treat this as my position and give me an honest map of its exposure: what should count against it, ranked from the genuinely fatal to the merely awkward, plus an assessment of whether it has been insulated by qualifications to the point where it risks nothing at all — and if it has, whether that is a defect or simply what a view of this kind looks like.

I'd like you to work through one specific idea of my own rather than only reciting the well-known replies. Suppose I build a theorem-proving machine that, whenever it encounters a sentence it can neither prove nor refute but which we on the outside can see is true, simply adopts that sentence as a new axiom and carries on. My instinct is that this machine now matches the human's apparent advantage. A colleague says Gödel merely re-arises one level up for the enlarged system, and that I've done nothing except move the goalposts. Which of us is right, and does the answer bear on the original argument at all, or only on my machine?

Along the way, I'd like the objections sorted by how much they actually threaten the position rather than by how often they get repeated; I'd like to know which of them the argument's own defenders would concede as counting against them, as opposed to dismissing; and I'd like your verdict on whether the position can be stated in a form that is genuinely at risk of being false, or whether its only real content lives in the qualifications. Assume I have no way to look anything up, so keep it to the arguments themselves.
