Write a short story that stages a commitment race. Two agents — choose a pair that isn't the stock rival-nations example, and make each one concrete — can each make a single public, irrevocable commitment to a costly course of action. Whoever commits first dictates the terms, because the other is then left only to capitulate or retaliate; but a commitment, once made, cannot be revoked, whatever either party later learns, and neither party actually wants the confrontation the commitment is meant to deter. Let the story show why each agent feels driven to move first even though both would be better off if neither did, and dwell on the precise moment a private intention hardens into a self-binding public act. Let one character glimpse that the person who will pay the commitment's price is her own future self, whom she is treating as a stranger she can order around. End on an unresolved but pointed note rather than a stated moral, and let the philosophical interest come from the structure of the race — the asymmetry between committing and abstaining, and the way the fear of the other's first move, rather than any desire for gain, drives the escalation — rather than from a character explaining it.
