My friend and I spent a long evening arguing about bootstrapping and easy knowledge, and neither of us moved an inch, which surprised me, because we agree about nearly all the cases. We both find the standard ones compelling in the same way: the person who reads a colour meter's output, forms a belief about the ball's colour, and then concludes the meter is reliable; the driver who forms a belief about a car in the dark and then concludes the lighting isn't deceptive; the ordinary inductive self-calibration of a clock against the sun. We agree the reasoning in all of them feels bad. What we can't agree on is what is bad about it, and I've come to suspect that we are running two or three different disagreements under a single label.

He has offered a principle to settle it — roughly, that no belief-forming process may be used to certify its own general reliability in the very conditions in which it is being used — and a new case to motivate it: a wristwatch with a button that, when pressed, displays the message that this watch is accurate. He finds the watch absurd and the person's bootstrapping legitimate; I can't see the asymmetry, since the person is doing to her own vision precisely what the watch does to itself. My objection to the principle is that it also forbids the mundane inductive self-calibration we both accept, so it is either too strong or a restriction on closure in disguise. He says the latter is a feature rather than a bug, and we went around that loop twice.

I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement about which epistemic principles to give up, a standoff between case intuitions that no further argument will touch, a dispute about what the concept of knowledge is for, or two people answering different questions — one about the structure of warrant, one about the norms of assertion or inquiry — under one heading? Given the diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or nothing at all? And please separate the parts of this that are settled from the parts that are genuinely contested among people who work on it; don't hand me a contested reading as the consensus.
