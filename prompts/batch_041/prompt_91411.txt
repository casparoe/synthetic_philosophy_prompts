For a formal social choice seminar I am taking, I have been asked to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two parties who have spent a term arguing past each other about the discursive dilemma. The first holds that a group can literally hold a belief, and that the dilemma is therefore a genuine constraint on group rationality; the second holds that ascriptions of belief to a group are at best shorthand for a profile of individual judgments plus a decision rule, so that the dilemma constrains aggregation rules and reveals nothing about group minds. Write the document both of them could sign: a numbered section of agreed claims, including precisely which conditions on an aggregation rule generate the inconsistency and what becomes of the dilemma once the collective judgment set is permitted to be incomplete, so that the group may abstain on some propositions; then each party's position in wording its own adherents would endorse; then the cruxes, of which I suspect one is whether the group's judgment has a single referent or divides into an aggregative sense and a deliberative one. For each crux, set out the argument or case both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. If you conclude that part of their dispute is merely verbal, identify that part and explain why.
