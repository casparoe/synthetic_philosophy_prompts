I'm in a reading group that has spent the term on cultural evolution, and we keep stalling at the same place: someone reaches for the meme's-eye view — cultural items that spread because they are good at spreading, whether or not they do their bearers any good — and someone else objects that this is just Darwinian vocabulary pasted onto a metaphor, and we never get past the standoff. Before we read more contenders I want the specification instead. Set out the desiderata for a theory of culture that genuinely deserves to be called Darwinian: what would such a theory have to deliver to count as adequate?

Be concrete about what each requirement is doing. I want the phenomena it has to account for — why practices that are costly or outright ruinous to the people who carry them nonetheless persist and spread; why stories and tunes drift in transmission yet keep snapping back toward a small set of recognizable forms rather than wandering freely; why culture can turn over within a few generations when genetic change takes far longer; why so many cultural items seem to have a single point of origin and then fan out. I want the distinctions it must not collapse: an item's success at spreading versus its bearer's success at living; the winnowing of variants versus their systematic reshaping toward particular forms; faithful replication versus mere repetition; spread by transmission versus independent reinvention. And I want the neighbouring results it cannot contradict — in particular that human copying is noisy and reconstructive rather than mechanical, and that cultural inheritance is not particulate the way genetic inheritance is, since variants blend and contaminate.

Throughout, separate the requirements that are genuine from the ones that are just habits of thought carried over from biology, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the most popular pictures of how culture evolves; if some pair of them cannot be met together — say, if demanding a faithful replicator sits badly with explaining rapid cumulative change — that would be the most interesting result, so put it on the table. Give the whole thing as exactly five numbered items, one requirement each, a sentence or two apiece, with no introduction and no closing summary, folding the verdict on genuineness, on the most-excluding requirement, and on any unsatisfiable pair into whichever item each bears on.
