I'm in a reading group that's been going round the same argument about authorial intention for about a year and a half now — six of us, and nobody has budged an inch. Roughly the lines are: two people think a text means whatever its author meant by it, so a reading that contradicts the author's intention is simply wrong; two think that's a fallacy and that the meaning is whatever the words and conventions support, what the author privately meant being neither here nor there; and one keeps saying it's really all about the reader anyway. The frustrating bit is that all the arguments are old, everyone knows them, and they just bounce off. My hypothesis, tell me if it's rubbish, is that we're not really disagreeing about texts at all but about two different questions sharing one word — when someone asks 'what does this mean', one camp hears 'what was this person doing when they wrote it' and the other hears 'what would a competent reader take from this thing' — and that we're also sliding between 'which reading is correct' and 'which reading is worth having', which are seperate questions. And I keep raising a wrinkle nobody likes: even granting that intention matters, which one — the plan the author had going in, or whatever they were actually doing sentence by sentence, which might only be visible in the finished thing?

So: is this a verbal disagreement, two different questions under one heading, a real substantive split about what a text even is (an act, which has a doer, versus an object, which doesn't), or just a genuinely hard problem that we're mistaking for a muddle? Diagnose it against what each side actually says rather than giving me a survey of the positions, and say what would count as progress — some distinction that dissolves it, or an honest 'nothing will, and here's why'. Does the diagnosis change for a text whose author we know nothing about, an anonymous poem say? And please write it in British English, I'm trying to keep my spelling consistant.
