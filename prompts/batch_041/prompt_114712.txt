ok so me and my mate just spent two hours argueing about pain and i need to settle it before we meet up again. his whole case is pain asymbolia. you know the thing where someone has a brain lesion and they still feel pain — they can tell you its there, point to where it is, rate how intense it is — but it doesnt hurt. it doesnt bother them, no distress, and i think some of them will even keep their hand on something hot? anyway. his conclusion is that this proves the badness of pain isnt in the sensation at all. the sensation is neutral, its the aversion, the not-wanting-it, that makes pain bad, and once thats gone theres nothing bad left. so a person in that state isnt actually being harmed by their pain.

i dont think thats right but i couldnt articulate why, so what i want from you is the actual spec. forget picking a side for now — give me the desiderata: what does any decent theory of why pain is bad have to get right. all of it — every case it has to handle, every distinction it cant just flatten, with the case or consideration that motivates each requirement, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just the pet commitments of one camp.

things i definitely want on the list:
- the asymbolia patients themselves. is their pain still bad for them even though it doesnt feel bad? are they harmed? the theory has to say something and cant just dodge it.
- creatures that cant have the relevant attitudes at all — babies, animals, maybe people deep in dementia. if the badness is all in the aversion then pain is only bad for things sophisticated enough to mind it, which feels exactly backwards.
- why pain is bad in a way that nausea or an itch or a bad smell arent — or if they are, then say so and put them on the same scale.
- and heres my own addition, because i think his picture is missing something: he treats the sensation as pure neutral feeling, but the sensation also does work — it tells you where the damage is, it makes you protect the limb, it interrupts whatever you were doing. so i want a requirement that the theory account for pain being bad partly because of what it makes you do and know, not only because of how it feels. if that requirement is real it should bite against his view.

then tell me which single requirement does the most damage — the one that, once you accept it, knocks out most of the obvious positions — and whether any of them cant all be true at once. if two pull against each other say which two and dont smooth it over. last thing, one short paragraph at the end: does his asymbolia argument actually go through, or is he sneaking something in?
