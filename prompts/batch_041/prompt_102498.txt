I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy, mostly philosophy of language, and I've been working my way through the indeterminacy of translation stuff for a seminar paper. What's bugging me is this. The gavagai scenario itself seems totally uncontroversial as a story -- nobody denies you could cook up two translation manuals that fit every disposition the native has and still diverge on whether "gavagai" means rabbit, or rabbit-stage, or undetached rabbit-part, or whatever. Fine. But then why, after all this time, has the debate about what the scenario is suposed to show not converged even a little? One camp reads it as a heavyweight thesis about meaning: there's just no fact of the matter about what the native means, meaning is indeterminate all the way down. Another camp says it's really a modest point about the underdetermination of theory by all possible evidence, and that it only looks dramatic if you smuggle in some behaviorist or verificationist premise about what meaning-talk is even allowed to be. And then the Chomskian crowd seems to think the whole thing rests on a picture of language nobody serious holds anymore, so the thesis is either false or about nothing at all.

My suspicion -- tell me if it's right -- is that this isn't one disagreement but three or four stacked on each other: a fight about what the thesis even claims, a fight about whether its premises are independently motivated or just artifacts of an abandoned theory of meaning, and underneath both a deeper fight about whether there's a fact of the matter about meaning at all, which sounds suspiciously like the old realism-about-meaning / analytic-synthetic brawl wearing a new hat. Is that the right diagnosis, or am I flattening something? And more to the point: given whatever the real disagreement turns out to be, is there anything that would actually count as progress here -- a distinction somebody could draw, a reformulation that would finally get the camps addressing the same question, or is this the kind of impasse where the sides are talking past each other more or less by design and I should stop waiting for a resolution? I don't want a survey of who said what, I want a diagnosis with reasons and a straight answer about whether it can be moved.
