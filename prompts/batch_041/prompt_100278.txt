I chair the deployment review board at a mid-sized warehouse-robotics company, and on Friday we vote on whether to ship a picking policy that was trained with an "impact penalty": at every step the robot gets penalized for how far the world drifts from what our learned world model predicts the world would have looked like if the robot had done nothing that step. The teams pitch is that this is how you bake "avoid side effects" into a system without anyone having to write down what we actually want, which none of us can do anyway. Two of my reviewers say it's incoherent — the "did nothing" baseline is itself a value judgement (a jammed conveyor wrecks the pallet whether or not the robot intervenes, so drift-from-status-quo isn't neutral), and worse, what the robot would have done otherwise depends on the very policy we're training, so the baseline is circular. The team's answer is that they just subtract the intended effects and penalize the remainder. Draft me the short written recommendation I'll circulate before the vote: ship, ship with conditions, or hold, plus the criteria reviewers should apply to future proposals of this kind. Take the two dissenters seriously enough that if part of their objection is right, the memo says which part — and say plainly what the team would have to nail down for "avoid side effects" to be a real instruction rather than a vibe.
