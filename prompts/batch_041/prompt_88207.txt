Ok, I've been mainlining Hacking again (looping effects, interactive vs indifferent kinds, all of that) and I want a short story out of it instead of yet another blog post reheating the same three examples. Every version of this I've seen does the same thing: new diagnosis appears, patients start performing it, everyone nods sagely about social construction, roll credits. I want the version where the loop is actually load-bearing.

So: make up a condition. Not a real one, not a thinly veiled real one, and it goes in a fictional manual that you also invent. Give it this shape — the central diagnostic criterion is that the patient has to endorse a particular first-person sentence about their own mental state, some specific flavour of exhaustion or numbness or detachment that you can basically only report once you've got the word for it. Everything else about it is up to you.

Then I want to see the loop turn, at least twice, in different directions, over maybe fifteen or twenty years of in-story time. The people the label picks out in the first cohort shouldn't be the same kind of people it picks out by the third, and the manual's criteria should get rewritten in response, which changes who walks in the door next. Bonus points if one of the turns makes the category partly empty out instead of filling up — I think thats underrated as a dynamic, everyone always writes the self-reinforcing direction.

Two things I actually care about: (1) someone gets told they no longer meet criteria, and it lands as a loss rather than a relief, or as both at once; (2) there's a clinician or researcher who thinks the category is tracking something real and isn't just an artefact of the label, and they should get a real argument, not a strawman. Don't resolve it either way. And noone in the story gets to explain any of this out loud — no lectures, no epiphany about social construction, nobody saying the word "loop". The reader should be able to piece it together from the scenes alone.

Format: start in scene, no title, no epigraph, no philosophical narration, no "in a world where" setup. Hard cap 800 words. One sentence at the very end saying which bit of Hacking you were stressing is fine, but optional.
