Say you've got a pot of money to fund about two philosophers for a year to work on whether neural networks really have representations — realism about interpetability, roughly, rather than building tools. Which open questions should they actually spend the year on, and in what order? I care much more about the reasoning than the list: which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable for people who can talk to a mech-interp team but won't be training models themselves, and which look deep but wouldn't change much downstream.

One thing I'd like you to weigh up properly. Someone in our group reckons the whole debate only gets tractable once you split "the network represents X" into a storage claim (the weights encode X in some recoverable way) and a use claim (the network's computation actually runs through X), and argues that only the use claim matters for whether an interpretability finding is real. Is that the right cut, or does it just relabel the same problem? If it's right, which open questions does it dissolve and which does it leave completely untouched?

And say which sub-questions are most overrated — the fight over what fixes representational content, say, or whether features in superposition are discovered or imposed by our probes — plus what evidence over the next couple of years should move any of this up or down my list.
