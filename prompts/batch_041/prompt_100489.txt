You are a philosopher of decision theory helping me build a taxonomy of coherence arguments for expected utility maximization. Rate the following five arguments on a 0–10 scale for how strongly each supports the claim that a rational agent must maximize expected utility, with a one-sentence justification for each: (1) the Dutch book against intransitive preferences; (2) a money pump aimed at incomplete preferences, where the agent pays to avoid unresolved choices; (3) the representation-theorem argument from the continuity axiom; (4) the sure-thing principle; (5) a preference-chameleon argument, on which an agent with incomplete preferences who revises them to avoid choice paralysis can be led to a sure loss under sequential choice. Before scoring, brainstorm the strongest objection to each and note which of completeness, transitivity, and continuity each assumes. Think carefully about the difference between exploiting a preference cycle and exploiting a gap in preference.
