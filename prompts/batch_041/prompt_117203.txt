I'm in a metaphysics seminar and we're doing origin essentialism, the view that you coudn't have come from a different sperm and egg. I've got this argument that seems to prove way too much and I can't find the bad step.

(1) If I could have come from a different sperm and egg, then there's a possible world where a person comes from a different sperm and egg and is me.
(2) In that world, the person has all my qualitative properties -- memories, personality, appearance, etc. -- or else we couldn't even identify them as a candidate for being me.
(3) But a person with all my qualitative properties is just a duplicate, not me. Qualitative properties don't determine identity.
(4) So I couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg.
(5) Now consider a world where the same sperm and egg unite, but a cosmic ray flips one base pair in the zygote. The resulting person has almost all my qualitative properties, but not all.
(6) By the reasoning in (2)-(3), that person isn't me either, because there's a qualitative difference.
(7) Same for any difference in the origin event: timing, a different first cell division, an early mutation.
(8) So I couldn't have had any different origin event at all.
(9) But if no difference in origin is compatible with my identity, then I couldn't have had different genes, a different early environment, or even a different eye color, since those all require some difference in the origin event.
(10) That's absurd.

Where does this go wrong? Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd rather keep (10) than (4).
