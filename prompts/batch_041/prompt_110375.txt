For a work-in-progress session on the ethics of suffering, I have to circulate a one-page statement of my axiology, and I would like it audited before it goes round. This is where I have landed.

1. Suffering above a certain intensity threshold is lexically bad: an outcome containing such suffering is worse than any outcome that does not contain it, however much pleasure the first outcome also contains.
2. Pleasure and pain are commensurable: they lie on a single scale of welfare, so a question such as "how much pleasure would it take to make this life go as well as that one?" always has a determinate answer.
3. I am a negative utilitarian: pleasure has no positive intrinsic weight, and the reduction of suffering is the only thing that gives us reason to act.
4. I accept expected-value maximization under risk, including when the probabilities involved are extremely small.
5. To locate the threshold I use what I call the swap test: an episode of suffering lies below the threshold if I would judge a life containing it to be no worse than an otherwise identical life without it, once enough pleasure is added elsewhere in the first life; if no quantity of pleasure would bring me to that judgment, the episode lies above the threshold.
6. I am confident that a one-in-a-billion chance that one person endures an hour of torture is too small to outweigh a certain mild inconvenience for a billion people, so I would take the risk rather than the inconvenience.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to, and how severe each real conflict is. For each genuine conflict, give the cheapest way to restore consistency and say what that repair costs me. Then name the single revision that would clean up the most, and say whether what is left is still a view one could hold. I am asking for the audit, not the literature, so there is no need to cite anything.
