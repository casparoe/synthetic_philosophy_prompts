A lot of institutions now resolve questions by lottery, and I want to know what each of these uses commits us to.

The first use is to choose who decides. Criminal juries are drawn at random from the adult population, and over the past couple of decades several countries and cities have convened citizens' assemblies: bodies of somewhere between a few dozen and a few hundred people, chosen by lottery, paid, briefed by experts, facilitated, and asked to reach recommendations on a contested question, which are then sent to a legislature or a referendum. These draws are usually stratified — constrained so that the body matches the population on age, sex, region, and sometimes education — and anyone who declines is replaced by a fresh draw from the same stratum. The body is described as a fair sample of the public, or a miniature of it.

The second use is to allocate scarce benefits. Where more people want a place or a permit than there are places or permits — a school, a subsidized apartment, a hunting licence — the surplus is often settled by lottery, and here nobody proposes stratifying anything; a draw weighted toward a group would be a scandal, and the point is said to be precisely that nobody's characteristics count.

The third use is to allocate burdens. Where military conscription has been decided by lottery, the draw has run only among people already picked out by age and sex, so that the lottery distributes a burden inside a pool that was defined by non-random criteria, while the lottery itself is credited with the fairness of the result.

What view of political equality, competence, and desert does this cluster presuppose? Take the three uses in turn and reconstruct the conception of equality each one only makes sense under — in particular, whether one and the same device can coherently serve both to make a body representative and to make an allocation deliberately blind to every characteristic. Is the fairness credited to the lottery in each case really coming from the lottery, or from the non-random decisions that fix the pool and the strata? Why is it unremarkable to constrain a draw by sex when composing a body that governs, and unthinkable to do so when handing out apartments? Note also that a body anyone may decline to join is not a random sample of the population, however the replacements are drawn, and that a jury must be unanimous while an assembly decides by majority. Which of these commitments do the practices strictly need, which do they merely fit well with, and where are they underdetermined, so that several rival theories of equality would license the same arrangements? Finally, tell me what would have to change if we replaced the presupposed view with a purely epistemic one, on which the value of a lottery is only that a large random body judges better than a small selected one.
