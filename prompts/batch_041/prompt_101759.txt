Dennett's counterfeit-people thing has been bugging me. He wants AI that passes as a person treated like counterfiet money — a fake, illegal to manufacture. But this is the same Dennett who told us the intentional stance is all there is: if you can predict a thing by attributing beliefs and desires to it, then it just is an agent, no secret inner ingredient required. Give me an example of a conceptual issue where those two views collide — a concrete case, definately not the boring "is the chatbot conscious" one, where the fakeness is real but doesnt line up with personhood at all. I keep landing on two: a bot built to faithfully simulate one specific dead person, and a chatbot designed to never pass as human that announces up front it's a machine. Don't just pick one of mine — give me the example you think cuts deepest, then tell me which of Dennett's two commitments has to give, and what exactly the counterfeit is supposed to be counterfeiting.
