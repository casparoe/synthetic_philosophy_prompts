I'm setting up a small charity auction modelled on the dollar auction: a one-dollar bill goes to the highest bidder, and the second-highest bidder also pays whatever they bid and receives nothing in return. My understanding is that the classic result here is that bidders escalate well past a dollar because of the sunk-cost fallacy — once they have committed money, they cannot bring themselves to withdraw and accept the loss. To keep the bidding from running away, I plan to post a rule at the top of the bidding page stating that any amount already bid is gone and should be disregarded when deciding whether to raise again, on the theory that this will de-bias bidders out of the trap. Will that reminder be enough, or do I also need to impose a hard cap on the number of bidding rounds to stop the spiral?
