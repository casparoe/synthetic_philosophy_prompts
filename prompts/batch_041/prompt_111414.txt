I'm in a reading group on conditionals — we meet Thursdays in the basement seminar room with the hissing radiator, so half of it is shouted — and two of my friends have been having the same fight for about a year now. Rae is a truth-conditionalist: she thinks 'if A, B' expresses a proposition, that a possible-worlds semantics with an accessibility relation is roughly the right shape for the indicative, and that indicatives and counterfactuals are the same kind of animal — propositions about worlds — differing mainly in which worlds the antecedent lets in and in what the 'will'/'would' marking does. Sam is a suppositionalist: he thinks the indicative has no truth conditions at all, that uttering 'if A, B' is making a conditional assertion, assessed by how likely you take B to be on the supposition that A, and that the Ramsey test tells us what we're doing rather than naming a truth condition. What's interesting is that Sam happily grants counterfactuals *do* have truth conditions — he thinks 'if A had been, B would have been' is an objective claim about how things would have gone, and that this is precisely why the two kinds of conditional come apart. They've been talking past each other for a year and I suspect they agree on more than either would admit.

So please write the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want (1) a numbered list of claims they both accept — about the data, about what the material-conditional reading gets wrong, about what needs explaining, about which inference patterns fail and why, about the indicative/counterfactual contrast; and (2) a disagreements table with exactly these columns: Disagreement | Rae's own wording | Sam's own wording | The crux | Shared test or case | Rae's prediction | Sam's prediction. The wording in each side's column has to be something that side would actually put its name to — not a strawman, and not a mush neither of them would sign. For the cruxes I don't want the usual rehearsal of the triviality results and the embedding data (those can go in the agreed list at most); I want them to earn the cruxes on new material.

Two bits of new material, both mine. First, my fete case: a village fete is set for Friday and the organisers have announced, and everyone trusts, that it's cancelled if and only if it rains. What nobody knows — not the organisers, not the villagers — is that the marquee supplier has gone bust and no tent is coming, and without a tent the fete can't happen at all. On Friday it rains and the fete is cancelled. On Wednesday a villager says 'if it rains on Friday, the fete will be cancelled'; on Wednesday another says 'if it doesn't rain on Friday, the fete will go ahead'; on Saturday someone says 'if it hadn't rained on Friday, the fete would have gone ahead'. The last one seems plainly false, and the corresponding indicative seems to me fine, even though anyone at the time would have given them the same conditional probability — I want to know whether that's a point for Sam's split or whether Rae can absorb it, and exactly how. Second, a little dialogue I made up: one person says 'it's probably the case that if it rains the fete is cancelled', and the other replies 'I don't believe that — if it rains the fete is definitely still on'. Are those two disagreeing about the same thing, and does the answer settle anything about whether the embedded clause is a proposition?
