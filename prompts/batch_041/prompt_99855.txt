Ok so this might be a weird question and I'm not sure it even has an answer, but I keep running into this thing and I don't have a word for it. Some background: I'm a software engineer, never took a philosophy class in my life, but I end up in these long arguments with friends about "generations." The pattern is always the same. Someone says something like millennials killed the napkin industry, or Gen Z can't make phone calls, or whatever the take of the week is -- and the move is always that a bunch of people born in some window are treated as one thing that *does* stuff. What bugs me is that when I push on it, the exact window turns out to be totally up for grabs; I've seen sources draw the millennial/Gen Z line a few years apart from each other, and when I point out you could shift the cut by three years and get a different cast of characters with the exact same story, nobody thinks that's a problem. Worse, I can never tell whether the behavior is about the cohort at all -- maybe it's just what any 22-year-old does (you get less scared of phone calls as you get older?), or maybe it's what everyone did in a particular year regardless of age (everyone hated phone calls in 2020). And inside the group it's a total grab bag; the 40-year-old and the 25-year-old supposedly share a soul because a demographer's spreadsheet put them in the same column. So the label seems to be doing all the work, and it's doing it as if it were a cause rather than a bin someone drew.

I asked a friend who did some stats and they said "oh that's just the age-period-cohort identification problem," and I looked it up and yeah, I get that you can't separate the three from one snapshot. But that doesn't feel like the whole thing I'm chewing on. The demography point is about *measurement*; what annoys me is the *thing-ness* -- people treating the cohort as a real kind with a personality and causal powers, when the boundaries are arbitrary and the members are all over the place. I've also heard "socially constructed" thrown around but that seems too broad, since lots of stuff is socially constructed and this feels like a more specific error. Is there an established name for exactly this -- treating an arbitrarily-bounded, internally-variegated cohort as a unified causal entity? And if there isn't one, can you actually name and define it for me, and tell me how it's different from the age-period-cohort thing, from just saying "socially constructed," and from the idea of a self-fulfilling label (where the category changes the people in it)? I don't just want a term slapped on it -- I want to know where the edges are: what this is, what it isn't, and which of the nearby ideas is the closest fit.

Also please don't hedge and hand me a menu of five possible concepts to pick from. Take a side -- tell me whether this is a real distinct phenomenon worth its own name or whether I'm just re-describing something that already exists under a different label, and commit to that, with reasons.
