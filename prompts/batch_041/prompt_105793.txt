I want to understand the debate about fakes, forgeries, and the value of originals by having it restated in the vocabulary I actually use, and then I want an honest residue report. The debate I have in mind includes at least the following positions, which I will name by the philosophers they are usually attached to, since that is how I have seen them referred to; correct me if the attributions are off. First, the view associated with Goodman: a perfect forgery and its original can differ aesthetically even though no one can tell them apart, because they differ in their history of production. Second, the aura view associated with Benjamin: mechanical reproduction destroys something — authenticity, the here-and-now of the object — that is not a perceptual property at all. Third, the institutionalist view associated with Danto: what makes an object art, and an original the original, is its place in a practice or theory rather than any intrinsic feature. Fourth, the skeptic's view: if two objects are perceptually indistinguishable, no aesthetic difference between them can matter.
