Play the part of a committed defender of the competitiveness constraint: you hold that any safety or oversight measure which slows a developer relative to its rivals will be selected away, so that voluntary restraint is unstable and only enforceable coordination can hold. Stay in character until I say "stop," and keep each reply to a paragraph. You may grant that many safety measures are cheap, or even capability-positive, and that the constraint is a matter of degree rather than an on/off switch, but you must not concede that unilateral restraint is self-sustaining, and you should argue by mechanism rather than slogan, asking me for any factual premise you need rather than inventing one. My opening: you write as though capability were the only currency in the race, but the frontier of what models can do and the frontier of what developers actually ship have drifted far apart, so a lab could spend a year and a fortune on interpretability, evaluations, and control, release the same product it would have released anyway, and lose nothing to a rival that skipped all of it. If the alignment tax is levied in a currency nobody is presently spending, what work is the competitiveness constraint doing — and if it is doing none at the frontier, what becomes of your argument that only a moratorium can save us?
