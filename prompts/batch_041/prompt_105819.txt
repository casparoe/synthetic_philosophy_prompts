Someone I argue with keeps insisting a decision was "freely made" because nobody lied and nobody threatened, and something about that bothers me but I can't put my finger on it. Two cases. First, a person spends years being gradually talked into something — a job, a relationship, a course of treatment — until they genuinely want it, and today they'd choose it again; but the wanting is plainly the product of the other person's patient effort. Second, the person is told the truth about every option, except that the one they'd actually have taken has quietly been taken off the table, so they're left choosing between two things they'd never have picked. In both cases: no false statement, no threat, and a sincere endorsement afterward. Is there a name for what's wrong here — I keep reaching for "manipulation," but that seems to sprawl over everything, and I don't think it captures the bit about the want itself being manufactured, or the bit about the menu being rigged rather than the items being misdescribed. Is there already a distinction between messing with someone's options and messing with their wants, and how does either relate to coercion and deception? If there's no single name, coin one and tell me exactly where the line falls: does what matters turn on the person's endorsement afterward, on where the want came from, or on the shape of the option set at the time?
