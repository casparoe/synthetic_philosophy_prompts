My problem is that this debate looks completley stalled to me. As far as I can tell everyone on both sides gives the same verdict in every standard case — the preemption cases, trumping, double prevention, omissions, all of it. Same cause, same non-cause. And yet the two camps have been writing at each other for decades.

So my half-baked hypothesis: they're answering two different questions under one word. One is doing the metaphysics of a relation between events; the other is doing the methodology of inference over variables. If thats right, then the only real residue of a disagreement is (a) the relata — events vs variables, which sounds like bookkeeping until you try to make it work — (b) what the 'would' in the counterfactual is actually doing, similarity of possible worlds vs an idealized experiment, and (c) the circularity worry, that interventions are themselves defined causally so the whole thing eats its tail — though I can't tell if that objection is a real formal problem or just a demand for something no theory of causation could ever provide.

What I want from you: a diagnosis, with reasons, of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Verbal? A fight over which notion gets to be primitive?
