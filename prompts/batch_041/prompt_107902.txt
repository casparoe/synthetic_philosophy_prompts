Write a satirical ruling from a fictional body — call it the Cohort Assignment Appeals Tribunal of an invented statistical agency — hearing the petition of a person born on 31 December of the final year that the agency's schedule assigns to one generation, who asks to be reclassified into the next. Keep the register deadpan and bureaucratic: case number, findings of fact, evidence, reasoning, disposition. The comedy should come from the ontology rather than from wackiness. The tribunal must take seriously the question of what kind of thing a generation is, and its reasoning should quietly expose the real difficulties: that the boundaries are conventions drawn by whoever has an interest in drawing them; that birth year, current age, and historical period are confounded in any cohort claim, so that the same survey cannot tell whether people differ because of when they were born or because of when they were asked; that the reference class behind any generational generalization is chosen rather than found; that self-identification both measures membership and helps produce it; and that each cohort is defined largely by contrast with its neighbour, which is itself defined by contrast with it. Include a dissent from one member who argues the tribunal lacks jurisdiction because there is nothing here to be right or wrong about, and a reply to the dissent that tries to save the practice without conceding that anything goes. Invent the tribunal, the case number, and the litigant; do not name or quote real researchers or institutions. Roughly 700 words. End with a final line beginning "Verdict:" that states the tribunal's holding in a single sentence.
