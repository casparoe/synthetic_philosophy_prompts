My flatmate is writing a chapter on conspiracy theories and he's cornered me with this argument twice now. I cant find the exit.

(1) Take the moon-hoax theory. Everything you'd point to as evidence for the landings -- the photos, the rock samples, the telemetry, the astronauts' own testimony, the fact that the Soviets never once called it out -- the theory says was staged, doctored, or bought. (2) So it doesn't just survive the evidence against it, it eats that evidence and gets stronger. He calls theories like that self-feeding: the refutations are the fuel.

(3) Any theory that can digest every possible observation, so that no conceivable evidence would count against it, is not rationally acceptable. Thats the whole charge against conspiracy theories.

(4) Then he does this. Uranus wasn't sitting where Newton's law said it should, so they posited a planet nobody had ever seen, and the anomaly became a triumph. Beta decay looked like it was losing energy, so they posited a particle nobody had ever seen, and the missing energy became a triumph. The stars refused to show the parallax a moving Earth should produce, so the Copernicans said the stars are simply unthinkably far away, and the silence became a triumph. (5) Every one of those moves turned evidence that, by (3), should have counted against the theory into something the theory had already anticipated.

(6) So if (3) is right, a lot of the standard scientific record was irrational. (7) It wasn't. So (3) is wrong -- and then calling something a conspiracy theory is just an insult.

I want to keep (7), and I'll give up (3) if that's the price. He says giving up (3) is the whole game, and that whatever I put in its place will either be toothless or will quietly assume the conclusion. I tried "it isn't unfalsifiability, it's that the cover-up would need thousands of people to keep quiet for decades, which is wildly improbable," and he said I was just asserting a prior and calling it a virtue.

So: which step actually breaks, why did it look fine, and if I do have to give up (3), what am I buying in its place and what does it cost me?
