I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy, mostly metaphysics and phil of science, and I'm trying to write a seminar paper on laws of nature where I actually take a side instead of doing the usual both-sides thing. The controversy I keep circling is Humeanism vs anti-Humeanism, but the textbook version of it bores me a bit: Humeans say laws are just the axioms of the best systematization of the total mosaic (regularities, no necessary connections), anti-Humeans say laws do something -- they govern, necessitate, constrain what's possible -- and that the Humean can't get any of that out of a mosaic of local facts. Fine, I know that back and forth. What I'm actually stuck on is a narrower thing that came up in a reading group and that I can't shake. Suppose you're a Humean and you say a law is whatever earns its place in the best system by balancing simplicity and strength (and informativeness, or whatever else you want in the mix). Where do the standards of simplicity and strength themselves come from? If they're just facts about us -- our cognitive wiring, our interests, what happens to compress nicely for creatures like us -- then it starts to look like "law" is relative to inquirers, and there could be different best systems for different inquirers, with different laws, which feels like it slides into anti-realism rather than Humeanism. But if the standards are objective, sitting out there in the world, then the Humean looks like they're smuggling in exactly the kind of extra modal structure they said they didn't need. I want an essay that takes this seriously and lands somewhere: is the objectivity of simplicity and strength a genuine problem for Humeanism, or is there a way to have standards that are objective-enough without positing anything that governs?

Please also bring in the anti-Humean side here, because I suspect they'd say this dilemma just shows you need real governing laws -- and I want to know whether that move actually works or whether it just relabels the problem, since now you have to explain why *these* laws govern and not others, which sounds like the same standards problem wearing a fancier outfit. I'd like you to actually adjudicate between the Humean, the relativist-compression view, and the anti-Humean governing view, and say which one comes out best and why, rather than just laying out the options.

Don't bother telling me what to read -- I've got the literature, I want the argument. And if I've set this up wrong -- like if the "relative to inquirers" version isn't really seperate from standard Humeanism, or if the whole dilemma is just the old objectivity-of-modality worry with new paint -- say so directly instead of humoring me. Reach a conclusion and defend it; I'd rather you stake out a position I can argue with than hedge, and if it turns out the dilemma is a false one, that's a fine conclusion too as long as you show your work. One more thing, so you can push on it: I lean toward thinking the standards are objective, but I honestly can't say why, and I have a suspicion that lean is doing illicit work somewhere, so tell me if it is.
