I have come to accept that omissions are causes, and I want you to tell me what would count against the view. As I hold it: an omission causes an outcome just in case the outcome counterfactually depends on it — had the agent done the thing, the outcome would not have occurred — and where many omissions pass that test, the one we single out as the cause is the one that departs from a norm or expectation bearing on the agent. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: the cases the view must get right and does not, the results that would show it collapsing into a rival account, and the consequences its own defenders would concede are costs rather than things they would simply deny. I am most interested in whether the two halves can be held together — if counterfactual dependence alone is promiscuous and the normative half is doing all the selecting, does the view end up being about how we talk rather than about what causes what? Say also what it predicts that its rivals do not, and whether everything you raise can be absorbed by qualification, and whether that insulation is a defect.
