As a software engineer, I keep needing to put a probability distribution on a continuous quantity when all I know is its range or a symmetry, and I have been warned that this is a minefield. Before adopting any rule, I want a full specification of what such a rule must deliver. Write the desiderata that any adequate principle of indifference must satisfy, giving the motivating case behind each. Please include at least: consistency across the competing parametrisations in the classical chord problem; invariance under a change of variable, or else an explicit account of which parametrisation is privileged and why; agreement between indifference applied to a quantity and indifference applied to a derived quantity of it (say, a rate known only to lie between 1 and 2, its reciprocal, and the ratio of two such rates); and stability under conditioning. I am also uneasy that such a rule can be turned on itself, so add a requirement that it must not yield contradictory advice when applied to the choice of rule. Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of a particular camp, and flag any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied — I suspect the invariance and the additivity requirements pull against each other, and that would be the most useful part of the answer. Finally, identify the single requirement that rules out the greatest number of otherwise plausible principles. I want the reasoning only, so no reading list and no references, and please write in British English.
