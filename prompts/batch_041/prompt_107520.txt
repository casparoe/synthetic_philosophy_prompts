I am trying to fix my own position on performance legitimacy before it hardens into something I would have to defend in print, and what I want from you is an audit rather than a defence. Here is what I hold. (1) Legitimacy is a relation between a regime and each person it governs: the exercise of power is legitimate only if it can be justified to that person, taken one by one, and not merely because it produces good results in aggregate. (2) The sustained provision of order, security, and rising living standards can itself justify a government's rule, even where competitive multiparty elections are absent; this is what people mean by performance legitimacy, and I take it to be a genuine ground and not a piece of rhetoric. (3) Accountability is constitutive of legitimacy rather than an optional extra: no one should hold power without being answerable to the governed through mechanisms those governed can actually use. (4) On that basis I regard Singapore's PAP government as legitimate and the government of the PRC as illegitimate. (5) The justification invoked in (1) must be one the governed can actually see and grasp; hypothetical consent, or a justification visible only to the theorist, does not count.

Tell me which of these pull against one another, which only appear to, and — for each real tension, ranked by severity — what would have to give and what giving it up would cost. My suspicion is that one or two of them are doing incompatible work and that I have not located which; I would rather be told that a pair I assumed was harmonious is not, than have everything listed as equally fraught. Please also say how confident you are in each of your claims and single out the one point you are least sure about.
