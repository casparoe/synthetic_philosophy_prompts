I'm a second-year PhD student working on the frame problem (cog sci side, though my supervisor sits in philosophy) and I need a poem to open our reading group next week. I keep trying to explain to them why its not just "the robot has too many facts to list" and honestly a poem might land it better than another slide.

Let me just say the thing in my own words so you don't have to go hunting for the literature. There are really three bits. One: to describe a situation you have to decide what to write down and what to leave as understood — and whatever you leave out, someone can always ask why it stays that way. Two: to act you have to say what your action doesn't change, and there's no non-circular way to list everything it doesn't change, because the list itself needs a background. Three, the one that actually bites in real time: you can't notice everything, so attention does the framing for you, silently, and you never see the bill. And there's the nasty little twist that adding a true fact can make you lose a conclusion you already had — the background shifts when you add to the foreground.

So: a poem of about 18-24 lines, no fixed form, but with one line repeated — word for word identical — three or four times. That repeated line has to mean something different each time it comes round, purely because the frame around it has moved, and by the end the reader should see that the sentence itself never changed. Let the last stanza say plainly what the poem couldn't say, the stuff it had to leave unstated, and don't resolve it — the point isn't that there's a fix, it's that any description carries a background that can't be described without a further background, and so on down. I'd like it spoken by the background itself, if that works at all. And please don't just gesture at the technical term; use it once at most, maybe not at all. Someone in the group who's never heard of the frame problem should come away with the shape of it, and someone who works on it shouldn't wince.

British English please (I'm in the UK and I'll be reading it aloud). Cheers.
