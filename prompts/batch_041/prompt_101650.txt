Right, here's one I keep going back and forth on. When American psychiatry took homosexuality out of its diagnostic manual in 1973, was that a Kuhnian paradigm shift, or is it the kind of episode Kuhn's model can't actually handle? I know the usual Kuhn material — anomaly, crisis, revolution, incommensurability — so skip the recap. What I want is the mapping done properly: what was the paradigm that got overturned, which anomalies had piled up against it, was there a genuine crisis inside the community, and who exactly was the community whose consensus flipped? Then the awkward part. Kuhn says revolutions get driven by accumulated empirical failure and adjudicated by a fairly stable set of scientific values — accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity, fruitfulness. But this one looks like it was carried by activism and a shift in moral attitudes, with the evidence trailing behind as more of an afterthought. So: does that sink Kuhn's picture, or just force the externalist / social-constructivist reading of him, or does it mean the episode isn't a paradigm shift at all? And on incommensurability — did 'disorder' and 'pathology' really change meaning across the change, or did the words stay put while the values moved underneath them? Tell me honestly where Kuhn goes quiet on a case like this, rather than stretching him to cover it.
