I teach a graduate epistemology seminar and want a one-page decision tree for the dogmatism paradox: if I know that P, then I know that any evidence against P is misleading, and so I seem entitled to ignore it. Build the tree out of the logical space rather than out of a list of positions. Begin at the commitment that one knows P and knows that P entails that contrary evidence is misleading, and let each interior node be a question a student can answer without already knowing which resolution it commits them to. Each leaf should be a resolution, named where the literature has a name (closure-denial, denial that knowledge licenses disregarding evidence, the credal or Bayesian dissolution, contextualism, hinge-proposition and entitlement views, the anti-skeptical and the skeptical readings, and any others you think belong), with one sentence on that view's most awkward commitment.

Four choices must be forced somewhere in the tree. First, whether the incoming counter-evidence bears on P itself or on the claim to know P; I want the rebutting/undercutting defeater distinction doing real work at the first node rather than sitting in a footnote. Second, whether knowledge is treated as binary or as graded credence. Third, whether the puzzle is about what one may believe or about what one may do in deliberation. Fourth, whether the argument is being run together with the perceptual doctrine that experience can justify a belief without independent evidence that perception is reliable — my students conflate the two, and the tree should make the conflation visible rather than assume it away.

Eight to twelve nodes, six to ten leaves. Where a leaf stands for a combination of answers nobody occupies, say so and diagnose why: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Where two leaves cannot be separated by any question you can pose, flag them rather than paper over the difference. No citations; the tree should stand on its own.
