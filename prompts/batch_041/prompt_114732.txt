I've just moved back to my home city after twelve years in the States to run our regional office, and legal has pushed out the new global anti-bribery FAQ for high-risk markets — a deadpan numbered Q&A about gifts, meals, and relationship building, which flagged my cousin's wedding present and my uncle's standing arrangement with the customs broker. What bugs me alot about it is that it treats every gift relationship as a bribe that hasn't happened yet, that it assumes the public/private line I grew up with is the only one there is, and that it never asks who gets to write the definition in the first place.

So write me a parody of that document: same form, same flat in-house voice, same numbered questions, roughly 650 words, the kind of thing a multinational hands to local staff and never revisits.

Keep it deadpan — no winking, no asides telling me it's absurd. The jokes should come from getting the real material right, so that someone who knows this literature laughs instead of wincing: the grease-the-wheels argument, patron-client ties and what they actually deliver, the anthropology of the gift as something that creates an obligation rather than closing a transaction, the exception anti-bribery laws carve out for small facilitation payments to low-level officials, and the way the standard definition of corruption quietly presupposes a public/private split that not every society draws the same way.

The objections have to land without ever being stated: that the standard is somebody's local norm dressed up as universal, that enforcement is selective, that the compliance regime is itself a status marker. Don't let it slide into a defense of bribery either — one Q&A should be one a real compliance officer would sign their name to, and one should be a case where the local practice really is extortionate and the policy is right, which is the part I'd rather not think about. Give it a seperate traffic-light table and a glossary of terms the policy has invented. And don't let the punchline be that it's all relative, that's too easy.
