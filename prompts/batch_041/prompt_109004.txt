Right — two of my mates had a proper barney down the pub on Friday and I still can't work out who came off worse, so I'm handing it to you. Set-up: our other mate Dan got a strange offer off this loaded old boy he does odd jobs for. Dan can either take a grand in cash, today, no strings — or he can take a punt the old boy's cooked up: a one-in-a-trillion chance at ten quadrillion pounds. (Ten quadrillion. I had to look up what that even is. And no, the old boy couldn't possibly pay it — that comes up later.) Dan, who definately fancies himself a bit of a thinker, says the punt's the obvious pick: a trillionth of ten quadrillion is ten grand, ten times the grand, so by the numbers the punt's the better bet, done.

Rob says he's off his rocker, and roughly makes these points:

(1) a one-in-a-trillion chance is a polite way of saying never — you won't win, so taking the punt is just handing back a grand you already had;
(2) ten quadrillion doesn't exist, there isn't that much money on Earth, so the chance of actually getting it is zero, which makes the whole sum zero and Dan's 'ten grand' made up;
(3) and the very maths Dan loves would have you flog a guaranteed million for a one-in-a-quadrillion chance at some even sillier pile, then flog that for an even tinier chance at an even sillier pile, forever — and a rule that always swaps a sure thing for a smaller chance of a bigger thing ends with you giving up everything for nothing;
(4) and the number comes from some old bloke who fancies himself; you've no way to check a one-in-a-trillion claim, so you shouldn't hang your whole decision on it;
(5) and even if it were right just this once, it's a rotten rule to live by — anyone who took punts like that all his life would die broke.

Dan's comebacks: to (1) he says a trillionth is small, not zero, and somebody wins; 'never' is just what small numbers feel like to a brain built for counting apples. To (2), that's an objection to the old boy's example, not to the idea — shrink the prize to something he could actually pay and the same shape's still there, and you'd still take a sliver of a chance at a fortune over a poxy sure thing whenever the sums favour it. To (3), if the EV really is bigger then taking the sure thing is just leaving value on the table, and being spooked by big numbers isn't an arguement. To (4), you can't demand certainty about a probability before you act or you'd never act on anything; you go with your best guess. To (5), that's about doing it a million times over a lifetime — he's choosing once, and once, the sums say take the punt.

Neither of them budged and it got a bit heated. Can you go through it and say who's right on each of those points, one at a time, and then say what the pair of them are both missing? I don't need an overall winner if there isn't one — just a fair-minded referee's call, point by point.
