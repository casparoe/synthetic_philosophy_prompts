I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am working on an essay about the fashion for treating germs, climate and volcanoes as historical actors. My argument turns on a distinction I have been calling causal casting: the move of promoting a non-human cause from a background condition to a character in a historical narrative. Please describe the most important implications of that distinction—between a cause's causal weight and its narrative casting—for (a) the charge of environmental determinism, (b) the attribution of responsibility to human actors, and (c) the question of whether non-human causes can meaningfully be said to have agency. I am not asking for a literature review or citations; I want the conceptual consequences spelt out, including what follows for how historians should write if the distinction holds and what follows if it collapses.
