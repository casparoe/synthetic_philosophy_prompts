I design tabletop games as a hobby — board games, and occasionally a rules-heavy role-playing system — so the vocabulary I actually think in is mechanics: hard constraints that the components or the rules physically enforce, soft constraints that hold only because everyone at the table agrees to them, incentives and degenerate strategies, affordances, house rules, rules-as-written versus rules-as-intended, and the metagame. I have no academic background. But I keep running into a claim I can only half follow: that technical artifacts — a bridge, a door, an assembly line, a software default — can themselves embody or enforce a political arrangement, so that some of them are not neutral tools a society happens to use one way or another, but objects that keep producing a particular distribution of power whoever owns them and whatever anyone intends. The much-retold story about parkway bridges built too low for buses is the example I half-remember, and I suspect I've been nodding along to a slogan.

Please restate that claim, as faithfully as you can, in my vocabulary, one step at a time: which parts translate cleanly into hard constraints, incentives, and degenerate strategies; which parts have to be distorted to fit; and which parts have no counterpart at all in how games work. Then the part I care most about — the residue. Where the translation fails, tell me whether the failure is a defect in my way of talking, something about power, legitimacy, or coercion that mechanics cannot express, or whether it shows the original claim was doing less work than it sounds like it does, and my vocabulary merely made the vagueness visible. Do not soften the second possibility to spare my feelings.

Throughout, say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
