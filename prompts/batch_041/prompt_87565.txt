You are a philosopher of mind and action, and what I want from you is an audit of a position of mine rather than a survey of what has been written. Think it through carefully, and start by spelling out the several ways the load-bearing words here — routine, noticing, stepping out — can be read, since I suspect the verdict will turn on that; then give me the audit itself.

Last month I read about the digger wasp that drags a paralyzed cricket to its burrow, leaves it on the threshold, goes inside to inspect the burrow, and, if the cricket has been moved a few inches while it is inside, drags it back to the threshold and inspects again — on some accounts, endlessly; I have seen the word sphexishness used for this. I am told the story is contested and that the wasp does eventually adapt, and I don't know what to make of that. What made it stick is that I have caught myself doing something like it: I make a fixed round of the house at night to check the locks, and when I notice I am in the middle of it I break out by going to make tea — which is what I always do at that point. I would like to know whether the picture of myself I have been assuming survives contact with the wasp.

Here is what I believe.

1. What separates me from the wasp is not that my behavior is uncaused — it isn't — but that I can notice that I am in a routine and step out of it.
2. There is no sharp line between the wasp's loop and mine, only a difference in how deeply the routines are nested.
3. When I step out of a loop, the stepping-out is something I do; it is not itself another routine that something triggered.
4. What makes my routines mine rather than symptoms is that I could, on reflection, have done otherwise.
5. Nothing about me escapes causation.

Which of these can I keep? Sort the pairs into those that are genuinely in tension and those that only appear to be, order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say exactly what would have to give and what giving it up costs me. If any of the five is idle — pulling no weight in the set — say so. Finish with the single revision that would clean up the most, and what is left over after it. I don't want a reading list; I want the argument.
