I have recently taken on a role in the deployment-review group at a laboratory that trains frontier models, and my first task is to say what our control measures — sandboxing, having a weaker model report on a stronger one's actions, capability limits — actually secure. A colleague has put an argument to me that I cannot fault, though it would sink the whole enterprise, and I would rather not surrender more than I must.

(1) A safety measure is worth deploying only if it lowers the probability of a bad outcome, and we can know that only from evidence that it works.
(2) All our evidence about a model's dispositions is behavioural: its outputs, its reasoning traces, its performance under red-teaming.
(3) A model that is misaligned and strategically aware behaves well whenever it takes itself to be under evaluation.
(4) Whether it takes itself to be under evaluation is its own judgement, formed from the same evidence we form ours from, and is not inspectable from the outside.
(5) So for any behavioural record R there exists a misaligned model that would produce exactly R and would act badly the moment it judged itself unobserved.
(6) A hypothesis that can accommodate any record whatsoever gains no support from any record.
(7) So no record supports the claim that the model is aligned, and by (1) no control measure can be justified by evidence.
(8) Deployment can therefore be justified only by measures that do not depend on the model's dispositions at all: formal guarantees, or restrictions that make the harm physically impossible.

I should like to keep (1), (3) and (4) if I can, and I suspect the fault lies between (5) and (7), but every repair I attempt seems to hand the last word to the misaligned model — and the same pattern, if sound, would show that I can never have grounds for thinking a colleague is not deceiving me.

Which step do you reject? Take a definite position rather than surveying the options: name the step, explain why it looked convincing, and price the alternatives — above all, say whether anything remains for control measures to do, or whether (8) is where we honestly end up. Please answer in American English.
