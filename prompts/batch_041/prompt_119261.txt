ok this is gonna sound like a stupid question but I've been having the same argument about promises with basically everyone I know for like two years and we never get anywhere, so I want to know whats actually going on rather than get an answer.

Heres the setup. Someone breaks a promise and we start arguing about why it was wrong, and people give totally different answers. My one friend says it's wrong because of the social practice — we all grow up in a world where saying "I promise" is understood to commit you, and you're just playing by rules you were raised into, so if there were no such custom it wouldn't mean anything. Another friend says no, it's about the other person relying on you; you've given them something to lean on and then pulled it away, and thats the wrong, convention or not. A third friend says it's about respecting the other person's will — they've got a claim on you because you put yourself under their claim, and thats a kind of authority you have over yourself that you exercised. And then theres the guy who says its all just consequences, broken promises make people worse off and erode trust, full stop. None of these people will budge and honestly I dont think theyre even disagreeing about the same thing.

Heres what bugs me. Take the promise I made to my grandfather on his deathbed that I'd finish his workshop. Nobody knows about it, nobody can enforce it, he's gone, theres no reputation hit, no reliance after the fact, no future trust to erode. Does it bind me? I feel like it does and I cant say why, and every one of the four views above seems to either miss it or make it weird. Or take the promise I made once fully intending to break it — I lied, I never meant it — and yet I still felt awful when I did. Was I bound, or just guilty? Or take two people stranded somewhere whove never heard of promising and one of them says "I give you my word" — did that create anything, or did it only start working later once theyd built the custom up? And I keep coming back to promises to myself, which some people say arent promises at all.

My pet theory — could be wrong — is that the real split underneath all this is between people who think a promise is something you do (you exercise some kind of power and the obligation is just the shadow of that act) and people who think it's something that happens to you (a constraint you fall under, from outside). And maybe thats not really a disagreement about promises at all but about where reasons come from in the first place.

So what kind of disagreement is this? A verbal one about the word, four people answering four different questions under one heading, a hidden factual fight about how reliance and trust actually work, or just bedrock intuitions nobody can argue for? Diagnose it using the actual positions above, dont just tell me promises matter. And tell me honestly whether theres anything that could move this — a distinction, a case, a different question to ask instead — or whether it's the kind of thing that just stays stuck forever and I should stop bringing it up at dinner.
