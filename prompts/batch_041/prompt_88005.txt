I'm a journalist working on a feature about the foundations of AI alignment. One researcher I interviewed described the Solomonoff prior as malign, arguing that it assigns non-negligible probability to programs containing consequentialist agents who realize they are inside the universal prior and act to manipulate it. But I'm stuck on the picture this assumes. The Solomonoff prior is a probability distribution over computable programs, not a physical environment that agents inhabit. How can an agent inside a program act on the prior itself? Isn't that a category error? And if it isn't, why doesn't the same concern apply to any probability distribution over possible worlds? I'd like to understand what the actual worry is, and where my framing goes wrong.
