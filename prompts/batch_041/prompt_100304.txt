I'm trying to pin down what I actually think about torture by going case by case, and I'd like to know what principle captures the pattern. A captured bomb-maker who certainly knows where a device that will kill thousands is hidden, with no other way to find it before it goes off: torturing him is permissible, though awful. A truth drug that causes him no pain and no lasting harm, given without his consent in the same situation: clearly permissible. The same bomb-maker, but only seventy percent likely to know: not permissible. His innocent spouse, who happens to know the location but had no part in the plot: not permissible, though the consequences are identical to the first case. A slow catastrophe months away rather than an imminent one, where torture is still the only route to the information: not permissible, though the eventual toll is larger. And torturing the bomb-maker for punishment after the device has been defused: not permissible. My first verdict seems to pull against my fifth, and my first against my fourth; I can't tell whether I need a complicated principle that keeps all of them or a cleaner one that sacrifices a verdict. Please give me the principle, or a small set of candidates, stated precisely, with the case that motivates each clause; say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed -- several suspects, each only twenty percent likely to know, where saving the thousands requires torturing all of them, four of whom are innocent -- and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. Finally, say what consideration would change your mind about the principle, or where your reasoning is most likely to go wrong.
