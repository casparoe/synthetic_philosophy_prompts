ok so i've been argueing with my brother about whether generations are actually real or just astrology for people who read the Atlantic, and I want to settle it with numbers instead of vibes. here's the setup I want you to use. treat a cohort as a real kind only if it does well on all five of these tests (I made the last one up, so just take it as given):

1. Property cluster test: do the people in it share a bundle of traits that reliably hang together, and is there an actual causal mechanism holding the bundle together, or is it just a shared birth window?

2. Projectibility test: hand me a random person from the cohort and nothing else about them. Does the label let you predict anything better than you could from their birth year alone? Like, does calling someone a Millennial buy you anything over knowing they were born in 1989?

3. Looping test: does the label feed back on the people it labels, so they start performing the generation and the kind partly makes itself true? This one cuts both ways — a kind can be real and still be self-fulfilling, or it can be a sticker people just got stuck with.

4. Border test: are the edges principled or arbitrary? If the boundary is just a decade rollover, does anything about the people actually change when you cross it?

5. The one I made up, the borrow test: pick any two other social divides — class, region, gender, urban/rural, religion. Does the cohort still predict something interesting inside each slice, or does it dissolve into a rich-kid/poor-kid or coastal/heartland story once you look within? A real kind should survive being cut.

One wrinkle before you start. Sociologists have this age-period-cohort identification problem: age, the year you're measuring, and birth cohort are linearly dependent on each other, so you can't estimate all three at once from one survey — you have to constrain one of them, and whichever you constrain basically pre-decides how much of the effect gets credited to cohort. So keep in mind that a lot of what we call a generation effect could just be an age effect or a period effect wearing a coat.
