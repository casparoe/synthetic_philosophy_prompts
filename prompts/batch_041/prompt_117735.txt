my friend and i got in an arguement for like two hours last night about whether 'art' has a definition. his move, which he kept making, was: art is a family resemblance concept, like wittgenstien's games — chess, ring around the rosie, solitaire, professional wrestling — there's no one thing they all share, just overlapping similarities. so there's no essence, so everyone fighting about whether something 'is art' is just confused, and the definitional question is a psuedo-question.

i want to push back but not with the boring 'oh so anything is art then' move, because i can see that's not fair to what the analogy is actually doing. so: treat 'a concept is like a family' as an analogy that's being used to license a specific conclusion (no essence, no fact of the matter, stop asking) and audit it for me. what maps onto what — the parents, the shared genes, the resemblance, the family name, the boundary of who counts as family, the in-laws, the fact that you don't have to look like your relatives to be one — and where does the mapping break? i'm especially suspicious that the source domain is doing work behind his back: actual families are held together by descent, not by looking alike, so does 'family' smuggle in a hidden essence (the bloodline) that he's officially denying? how much of his conclusion actually follows from the analogy? does 'no single defining feature' really get you 'no fact of the matter'? and which of the disanalogies matter for the job he's using it for (settling whether some particular contested thing is art) versus which are just nitpicks i should let go of?

then the meta thing that's bugging me: 'family resemblance' seems like it might itself be a family resemblance term, and more to the point i keep feeling like the games example was meant as an antidote to a demand for a definition rather than as a positive theory of what concepts are. if he says 'art is a family resemblance concept,' has he already turned it into exactly the kind of theory it was supposed to dissolve? and if the analogy is bad, what's the better one — is there a comparison that keeps what's right about it (no tidy necessary-and-sufficient definition, ragged boundaries, new cases settled by judgment rather than a rule) without the descent baggage and without handing him 'no fact of the matter'?
