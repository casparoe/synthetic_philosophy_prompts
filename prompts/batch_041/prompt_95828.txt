My mum got diagnosed with early-onset Alzheimers last year and shes already stopped recognising me. Years ago she wrote an advance directive saying she wouldn't want to be kept going if she got like this — but the woman sitting in front of me now seems perfectly content, and I'm the one who has to decide whether to honour what she wrote. My brother says the person who wrote that isn't here anymore, so it shouldn't count; I'm not so sure, and I've realised the whole thing turns on whether a person stays the same person through time or is really just a series of stages. Could you write a dialogue between an endurantist and a perdurantist arguing about exactly this — whether a past self can bind a future self, what grounds the unity of a life, all of it? Keep both of them sharp and let them actually push back on each other's points; I don't want the perdurantist made to sound cold and lose, or the endurantist winning by default just for sounding warmer.
