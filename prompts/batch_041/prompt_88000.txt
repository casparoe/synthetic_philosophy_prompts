I keep circling back to the fall of Rome and keep landing in the same stalemate, so what I want is a diagnosis, not another tour of the theories. On one side: the catastrophe people — Gibbon's heirs, the lead-in-the-water-pipes crowd, the ones pointing at volcanic dust, climate anomalies in the tree rings, plague, debased coinage — who think something real and bad happened and want its cause. On the other: the late-antiquity 'transformation' people, who say nothing much fell at all — a political structure dissolved, the society under it reconfigured, and calling that a 'fall' just smuggles an evaluation in under a description. Oddly, they mostly agree on the evidence. So is this a verbal fight about the word 'fall', a real empirical dispute that some dataset ought to settle, or two camps quietly answering different questions ('why did this state end' versus 'how did this world change')? My hunch is it turns on what you take 'Rome' to name — a state, a civilization, a fiscal-military machine, or just the largest city on the Mediterranean — and that once you fix the referent most of the heat drains out. Is that right, or does a substantive disagreement survive the cleanup? And whats actually at stake here: could a new distinction or a new dataset move these arguements, or is the thing undecidable by construction?
