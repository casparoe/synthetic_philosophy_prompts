In philosophy of biology, some behaviors are said to have a free-floating rationale: they are tuned to reasons — the cuckoo chick evicts its foster siblings because doing so secures the food — yet the reasons are represented in no mind, and no one weighs them. Such a rationale is not merely as if; it can be correct or defective, so that a trait may pursue the right end by a reason that no longer holds, or the wrong end for a reason that once did.

Write a poem whose speaker is a reason that belongs to no one. Compose roughly twenty-four lines, in any meter or none, under this structural constraint: the poem may use the full grammar of deliberation and justification — because, since, so that, in order to, lest, otherwise — but no clause may take a person, animal, or mind as its grammatical subject. Let the connectives themselves carry the argument, so that a rationale unfolds, and is answered, with no one to hold it. Choose a single such pattern and follow it until the rationale holds or fails: a digger wasp provisioning a burrow it will never see filled, a moth steering by a light that breaks its steering, a fig and the wasps that pollinate and consume it. End by exposing the gap the concept turns on — something is being reasoned, and no one is doing the reasoning. A reader who knows the debate should find the position recognizable; a reader who does not should find the poem strange and exact.
