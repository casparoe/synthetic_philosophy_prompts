I've been trying to get clear on something about Sidgwick and I think it's a real philosophical puzzle and not just a biographical oddity. The standard picture: in the Methods he ends with the dualism of practical reason -- egoism and universal benevolence both apparently rational, no demonstration that they always coincide -- and he says the only reconciliation would come from a moral order, i.e. God and a future life, which ethics itself can't establish, so he treats them as postulates rather than knowledge. Meanwhile the man spends the last couple decades of his life as first president of the Society for Psychical Research, sitting through seances, organizing investigations, hoping to get empirical evidence for survival of death. I know the broad outline; what I want is the analysis.

Treat the psychical research program as an attempt to give empirical content to a postulate. What exactly is the structure of that move? If the postulate is something practical reason is entitled to assume because it's needed for coherent action, then what work is left for evidence -- and conversely, if you go looking for evidence, have you quietly stopped treating it as a postulate and started treating it as a hypothesis, with all the epistemic risk that brings? Then run it the other way. Suppose the evidence had come in, or suppose we decide now that the whole program was a failure. Does either outcome touch the dualism? My suspicion is that disconfirmation wouldn't hurt the postulate but confirmation would have been beside the point, and I want to know whether thats right or whether it just shows the postulates doctrine was doing something else all along.

Also say something about whether a person can honestly hold both that the postulate is practically necessary and that it would be good to have evidence, without that being incoherent, and what the episode shows about the boundary Sidgwick kept trying to draw between an ethics he wanted scientific and the supernatural assumptions he kept needing. I dont care about the parapsychology itself, and don't turn this into a history of the SPR.

End with a last line in exactly this form: Verdict: <one of: insulated / collapses into hypothesis / orthogonal> -- <one sentence saying why>.
