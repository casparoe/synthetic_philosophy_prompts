I've been going back and forth on the unity of the proposition for a few months now, and I think I've finally landed on a position I actually believe, so I want you to try to talk me out of it by telling me what would count against it. Here's the view: propositions -- or whatever you want to call the things that are true or false and that sentences express -- are genuinely unified, they're not mere lists or aggregates of their constituents, and that unity can't be explained by adding anything to the constituents: not a copula, not an instantiation relation, not some special "combining" relation. The reason is Bradley's regress: any such added item is itself just another constituent, so you need a further item to tie it to the others, and then another for that, and so on forever. I take the regress to be vicious rather than just an annoying infinite series, and so I conclude that the unity is primitive -- a brute, non-compositional fact about the proposition, not somthing built out of its parts.

What I want from you is a map of the exposure of that view. Which objections or results would genuinely count against it, and which are just the price of admission? In particular: is there any account that actually explains unity compositionally without tripping the regress -- a non-relational "mode" of combination, or a relation that somehow isn't a further constituent -- and if so, how much does that hurt me? Does the regress prove too much? If it's vicious here, doesn't it equally threaten any complex whole -- facts, states of affairs, even ordinary objects -- and if so is that a reductio of my argument or a bullet I should happily bite? And what should I say to the friend in my seminar who keeps insisting the whole thing is a pseudo-problem because each step is just the same fact under a new description? Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which ones a defender of my view would themselves concede really count against it versus which they'd dismiss as question-begging, and be honest about whether "unity is primitive" has quietly insulated itself from any possible counterexample -- and if it has, whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind or just what any view at this level of abstraction ends up looking like. I'd especially like to know which of these defeaters I should find most persuasive if I'm being honest with myself, and please keep separate what's genuinely settled among people who work on this from what's contested, rather than presenting one side's line as if it were the consensus.
