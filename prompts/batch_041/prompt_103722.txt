I have been reading LessWrong, the rationalist forums, and adjacent blogs for years, and I keep encountering the futarchy slogan that values are to be settled by voting and beliefs by betting. I met it most recently in a campus governance argument: someone proposed that the student union replace its line-item budgeting with a futarchy-lite scheme in which students first vote on a welfare function—say, a weighted sum of affordability, extracurricular access, and some measure of long-run institutional health—and then conditional prediction markets are run on which allocation maximizes that function. When another student objected that the welfare function would be gamed and that the markets could be manipulated by a well-funded faction, the reply was simply that futarchy is designed to vote on values and bet on beliefs, as if that settled the matter. I realized I could not say precisely what that sentence asserts, nor which of its possible readings would actually answer the objection.

Please lay out the distinct claims that the one-liner could be making, from the nearly trivial to the radical. I want a menu, not a single interpretation. For each reading, say what it commits its holder to, whether it is a descriptive claim about how futarchy would work, a normative claim about how political decisions ought to be divided, or an epistemic claim about markets; whether it is plausible on its own; and whether it would actually support the use my interlocutor made of it—that is, whether it addresses manipulation, welfare-function specification, or neither. Include readings on which the slogan is almost tautological, readings on which it is a substantive institutional bet, and readings on which it is false or incoherent. I am especially interested in whether the slogan presupposes a clean separation between values and beliefs, what work the word bet is doing (real money, scoring rules, conditional commitments, or something else), and whether any reading makes the markets merely advisory rather than decision-making. I do not need citations, reading lists, or a defense of futarchy; I need the conceptual disambiguation.
