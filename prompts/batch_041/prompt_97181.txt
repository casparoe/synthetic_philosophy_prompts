I'm a high-school junior and the captain of my school's ethics bowl team, and for the past month we've been arguing about human enhancement — gene editing, nootropics, growth hormone for kids who are short but healthy, cochlear implants for a Deaf child whose parents don't want them, mood-brighteners for people whose moods fall inside the normal range, and, in one memorably heated meeting, so-called moral enhancement. What I've noticed is that my teammates almost never disagree about the individual cases; they disagree about whether the therapy/enhancement line is doing any real work at all, and then read the cases off from there. So instead of another essay, I want to build a one-page classification instrument for our next meeting: a decision tree (a flowchart of questions) that takes someone from their verdicts on concrete cases to a named position on the therapy/enhancement distinction. Please make every branch node a question or a claim that a person can answer without already knowing which position it leads to — phrased, for example, as a disagreement about whether a given case counts as treatment, or about what makes the distinction matter — and never as a question like 'are you a bioliberal or a bioconservative?'. I'd like at least eight and no more than twelve leaves, and each leaf should carry the name of the position (or the word 'unnamed' if nobody seems to defend it) plus one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment.

The positions I'd like the tree to be able to reach, whether or not they end up as separate leaves, are roughly these: that the distinction is empty or merely rhetorical; that it is real and morally central, because medicine has a proper goal that enhancement exceeds; that it is real but morally inert, so that what matters is only the expected welfare produced; that the line should be drawn relative to each person's own baseline rather than to the species; that it should be drawn by reference to statistically normal species-typical functioning (there's an account in the philosophy of medicine that defines health this way, and I want the tree to reach whatever follows from accepting it); that the line is a legal and regulatory artifact rather than a metaphysical one, so the interesting question is who decides and on what authority; that in competitive contexts the line is fixed by the rules of the contest rather than by biology; that the morally relevant line runs through consent and coercion rather than through treatment and enhancement; and that what actually matters is an expressive or virtue-theoretic concern about mastery, humility, and what parents owe children. Please use these cases as the diagnostic material wherever they help: growth hormone for a healthy short child, cochlear implantation refused by Deaf parents, a drug that restores normal memory function versus one that produces above-normal memory, preimplantation selection for a non-disease trait, pharmacological mood elevation within the normal range, and gene editing that confers resistance to an infectious disease. Two more things: flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate — where two positions give the same answer to every question you've written — rather than pretending the questions distinguish them; and if any combination of answers corresponds to no position anyone holds, say so and diagnose it briefly, distinguishing a combination that is incoherent from one that is merely unattractive from one that is simply unexplored.
