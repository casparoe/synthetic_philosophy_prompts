I'm drafting an internal policy doc for a lab, and the hard part is defining honesty and deception for our AI systems in a way our evals and auditors can actually apply -- not a slogan on a slide. Before we pick a definition, write me the desiderata: what any adequte account of honesty/deception for an AI system has to deliver. Which cases must it get right (a literally true statement arranged to mislead, a misleading omission, sandbagging on an eval, sycophantic agreement, a confident falsehood the model "thinks" is true because of its training data, staying in character as a deceptive persona, a refusal that leaks a secret, deception engineered by the developer rather than the model), which distinctions it can't collapse (honesty vs sincerity vs accuracy, lying vs bullshitting vs simply being wrong, deception at the model level vs the deployed-system level), and which facts about these systems it has to stay consistent with -- for instance that we don't have a clean notion of what a model believes, and that its behavior is stochastic and prompt-sensitive. Give the case or consideration behind each requirement. Then flag any subset of requirements you think can't be jointly satisfied -- I especially expect friction between a belief-based definition and one that's measurable from behavior -- and tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out candidate definitions. Skip the scorecard on existing proposals, I mostly want the list.
