im working up a feature on deterrence for a general-audience mag and I want to open it with a poem instead of the usual throat-clearing about MAD. the thing I want it to catch is the Schelling inversion everyone half-knows but nobody feels: a threat is only credible to the degree the threatener has handed away the decision, so the strongest position is the one where you can no longer choose to back down — you become dangerous exactly by becoming unable. the risk itself is the leverage. can you write me 14 to 18 lines, in the voice of someone who has just done this to himself, where the form enacts the slide? start it tight — regular meter, clean rhyme, a man who thinks he's steering — then let it come apart as it goes, rhyme going off, sentences running past their stop, until the poem is about as uncontrolled as the situation he's describing. and let one line keep coming back but flip on itself, something like "I can hold it" turning into "I cant hold it," because thats the whole trick — it only gets real the moment he stops being the one who decides. two-sided would be nice if it fits: the other guy running the same math about him. should feel right to someone who knows Schelling and still land for someone who doesnt.
