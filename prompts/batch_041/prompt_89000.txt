So I've been reading a lot of Mill for a seminar on liberalism and empire — the On Liberty stuff side by side with what he was actually doing all those years at India House — and the thing thats bugging me is how cleanly the two halves sit together if you're Mill, and how completely deranged the join looks from the outside. He's the guy who says the only warrant for coercing a civilized person is preventing harm to others, and he's also the guy who spent his working life in a London office drafting despatches about how to run a subcontinent, on the theory that despotism is legitimate for people who aren't yet fit for anything better, as long as it aims at their improvement. And the improvement never has a deadline, because whoever is doing the ruling is also the one grading the exam. I'd rather make this land as satire than as another essay, so here's what I want.

Write me a mock internal FAQ — a new-hire onboarding document, deadpan corporate register — issued by a fictional "Office of Territorial Improvement" that is obviously standing in for the Company's London office, addressed to incoming junior clerks. Numbered Q&A, ten or twelve questions, 500-650 words, with a fictional signatory at the bottom (invented official, no real names). The questions should be the ones a slightly nervous new clerk would actually ask: what are we doing here exactly, when will they be ready to govern themselves, what counts as evidence that they're improving, what if they'd rather not be improved, does the liberty principle apply to them or only to us, what happens if we've misjudged their character. The answers have to be perfectly faithful to the real argument — that's where the humor comes from: the harm principle with a civilizational exception bolted on, improvement as the declared end, the tutor as sole examiner, the clerk in London who has never been there pronouncing on the character of a place he knows only from files, the courteous refusal to name a date. Nobody inside the document should notice anything is wrong and you shouldn't wink at the reader. A specialist should laugh because you got the position right, and someone who's never heard of any of this should still feel the squeeze. One warning: don't let it collapse into generic colonialism-is-bad. The satire only bites if the document reads like it was written by someone sincerely trying, in good faith, to be humane about it.
