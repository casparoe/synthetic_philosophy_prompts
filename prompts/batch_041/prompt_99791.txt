I am applying for a six-month, part-time independent-research grant and need a short proposal, roughly two pages, on a question in the history and philosophy of machine learning. I have a half-formed idea and would like you to turn it into a proper proposal. Please do not include citations, a bibliography, or a literature review; I will supply the references myself, and I would rather you reason about the positions than name texts. I have spent a long time in the rationalist and AI-safety blogosphere, where talk of raising, educating, or teaching a system is everywhere, and I want to know whether that talk earns its keep or is just a flattering metaphor.

The idea. Turing, in the 1950 paper that introduced the imitation game, closes by suggesting that rather than trying to programme an adult mind directly, we might build a machine that begins like a child and then educate it. In an earlier report on machine intelligence he had sketched a different route: networks of simple logical units wired up at random, to be organised by having their connections altered rather than hand-programmed. The usual reading treats all this as charming foreshadowing — the child machine as a quaint ancestor of training a large model by gradient descent. I think that reading flattens three separable claims that the education metaphor is carrying, and that only the third is philosophically live. First, an innateness claim: the child machine must already come with the right architecture and priors, because you cannot educate a blank slate into an adult. Second, a curriculum claim: learning should proceed through graded, staged experience rather than raw exposure to everything at once. Third, a relational claim: education, as opposed to mere training, involves authority, discipline, and the cultivation of initiative — a relation of the kind a parent bears to a child, not a reward channel — and part of what is transmitted is not information but a stance.

The third has no obvious counterpart in current practice, and it is the one I want to press on. My suspicion is that the education metaphor either does real work — it picks out design constraints, about the teacher-learner relation, about what the two must share for teaching to be possible, about why approval is not the same as reward, that training obscures — or it evaporates the moment the mechanics are specified, in which case calling a system educated rather than trained is anthropomorphic framing with no content and the habit of saying we will raise an AI should be retired.

What I want from the proposal:

If the honest version is really two or three questions, say so, and say which is load-bearing.
- Why the standard answers leave it open: what the it-is-just-engineering reading of the child machine gets right, what the it-is-all-anthropomorphism reading gets right, and why neither settles the matter. This is the part I most need help with, since I keep going back and forth.
- The method, month by month, over six months, working alone and part-time with no lab and no experiments: this is conceptual and historical work, so the method is argument and close reading rather than data, and I need you to be concrete about what a month of it looks like.
- What a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing — including the honest possibility that the conclusion is simply that it is only a metaphor, and whether that is worth a paper.
- Scope: whether six months part-time is enough, and if not, what to cut.
