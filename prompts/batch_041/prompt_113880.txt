I'm building up to choosing a theory of pleasure and I keep geting stuck, because pleasures don't seem to share anything: the cold soda fizzing on your tongue, the headache finally lifting, the click of a proof going through, hearing your friend got the job, a hot bath after a freezing walk. So before I read another one, write me the desiderata -- what any adequate theory of pleasure actually has to deliver. Which cases it has to get right (the nasty ones count too: schadenfreude, masochism, the sad song you keep replaying). Which distinctions it can't just flatten -- pleasure vs. wanting, since the reward-system work shows liking and wanting double-dissociate; pleasure as a felt sensation vs. pleasure as a way of taking some object; how much vs. how long; and whether the theory owes us a single common currency at all or is allowed to be a disjunction of quite different states. Which facts it can't contradict: people can want something they don't enjoy, and maybe can be wrong about what pleases them but not about whether they're pleased right now. Here's a case I made up to stress it -- someone with every behavioral and neural marker of enjoyment who sincerely says he feels nothing pleasant; does the theory follow the report or the mechanism? Give me the consideration behind each requirement, flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement does the most to kill off the contenders.
