I am a high school student writing an independent-study essay on indicative conditionals. I have been reading about the triviality results, and I reconstructed the following argument, which seems to show that all sentences are probabilistically independent. I cannot identify the illegitimate step. Please do not give me a reading list or citations; I want the argument assessed on its own terms.

Let P be a probability function. For sentences A and B, let A→B be the indicative conditional. I begin with three assumptions:

(1) Conditional-probability hypothesis: for any A and B with P(A)>0, P(A→B) = P(B|A).
(2) Law of total probability: for any sentence S and any sentence C with 0<P(C)<1, P(S) = P(S|C)P(C) + P(S|¬C)P(¬C).
(3) The hypothesis in (1) is not restricted to unembedded conditionals. If A→B is a sentence, then it can itself be conditioned on, and (1) applies under any condition: for any C such that the relevant probabilities are defined, P(A→B|C) = P(B|A and C).

Now let A and B be arbitrary sentences with P(A)>0 and 0<P(B)<1. Apply (2) to S = A→B and C = B:

P(A→B) = P(A→B|B)P(B) + P(A→B|¬B)P(¬B).

By (3), P(A→B|B) = P(B|A and B) = 1, and P(A→B|¬B) = P(B|A and ¬B) = 0. Therefore

P(A→B) = P(B).

By (1), P(B|A) = P(B). Since A and B were arbitrary, every pair of sentences is probabilistically independent. That is plainly false: the probability that a fair die shows an even number given that it shows a six is 1, while the probability that it shows an even number is 1/2. So at least one of (1), (2), or (3) must go.

I would rather keep (1) than give up the idea that conditionals have probabilities. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If the culprit is (3), how exactly should the scope of (1) be restricted, and what does that restriction imply about the semantics of indicative conditionals, especially when they are embedded under conditionals or probabilities? If the culprit is (1), what remains of the idea that asserting a conditional is warranted to the extent that its consequent is probable given its antecedent?
