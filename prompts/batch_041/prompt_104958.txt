I need to write down my commitments about indicative conditionals for a seminar and have someone check whether they hang together. Here they are. (1) Indicative conditionals express propositions: "if A then C" is true or false, and its truth depends on the world. (2) The probability of "if A then C" is the conditional probability P(C | A), and that is what governs when it is reasonable to accept one. (3) A Stalnaker-style semantics is correct: "if A then C" is true at a world just in case C holds at the nearest world where A holds. (4) Import-export is valid: "if A, then if B then C" and "if A and B, then C" are equivalent. (5) The Ramsey test is the right account of how we actually evaluate conditionals: accept "if A then C" to the extent that adding A to your beliefs would lead you to C. (6) Modus ponens is valid for indicatives.
