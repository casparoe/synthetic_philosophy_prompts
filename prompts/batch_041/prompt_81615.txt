ok so ive been lurking on rationalist blogs and forums for like fifteen years and this is the one thing I still cant get straight, so I want to write down my actual gut calls and see what principle they add up to.

The setup, which I assume you know: some guy walks up and says give me $5 or I'll torture 10^100 people in a distant galaxy. Classic Pascal's mugging.

My verdicts:

1. He obviously can't do it, there's no evidence he can do anything, it's just words. I don't pay, not even a little tempted.
2. Same guy, but he gives me a demo that convinces me he has the tech, and I put the odds he actually follows through at maybe 1 in a million. I pay, obviously. It's five bucks.
3. Same guy as in 1, same total lack of evidence, but the threat is only "I'll torture one person for a day." I pay anyway.
4. Pascal's wager. Infinite payoff, tiny probability, believe or burn. I think it's garbage and I lose no sleep over it. This one I'm confident about.
5. Now flip the mugging around: a charity says $5 buys a 1-in-a-googol chance of saving 10^100 lives. I donate. But notice this is the exact mirror of a mugging where the guy credibly has a 1-in-a-googol chance of torturing 10^100 people -- and in that one I don't pay. Same numbers, same expected value, opposite call.
6. A googol muggers, each independently making the threat from 1. Now the chance at least one of them is real is basically a coin flip or better. I still don't pay any of them, and paying all of them is impossible anyway.

So which of these do I have to give up, and what's the principle that saves the most of them? Is there something principled separating 4 from 5 (both "huge good, tiny odds") or am I just doing motivated reasoning because God feels dumb and the charity feels nice?
