ok so this is a thing i've been chewing on for a while and i don't know if it has a name. i'm not a lawyer or a philosopher, i just work in a creative-ish field and read too much.

here's what i keep noticing. somebody has an idea. maybe they say it out loud in a meeting, maybe they mention it to a friend over drinks, maybe they write it in a notebook and never do anything with it. then later someone else -- who either never heard it, or heard it and forgot -- actually does something with that idea, and it becomes "theirs." and the first person feels robbed. not legally robbed, they usually know there's no case. just... robbed. and the weird part is the feeling seems strongest exactly when there's the least to lose: when the idea was never going to make money, when the second person never even knew about the first, when the idea was honestly the kind of thing lots of people were already circling around anyway.

a couple concrete flavors of it:

- a group of people kick an approach around for months in conversation, nobody writes anything down, one person eventually writes it up properly and now its "her framework." everyone else is genuinely happy for her and also quietly stung, and none of them can state a grievance, because she did do the work of articulating it.
- a word or phrase that everyone was using loosely for years gets claimed by whoever first writes an essay about it, and from then on you're supposed to cite that person as if they invented the thing, which they sort of did and sort of didn't.

and here's the part that really bugs me. we have a sharp, common word for taking someone's actual sentences -- plagiarism. we have basically no word for taking someone's idea and just saying it in your own words, even though i'm pretty sure most people feel that one more keenly. and separately: you can give an idea away, publish it, explain it to everyone, hand it over -- but you can't give away the credit for it. the credit is the one part you don't control, and turns out to be the only part that mattered.

so: is there an established name for this whole cluster -- the felt ownership of an idea that exists with or without any law, that attaches to whoever first put it into words, that survives you giving the idea away, and that gets stronger the less actual harm there is? if there's a name, great, tell me it, and then tell me precisely how it differs from the things its not: the labor-mixing story about property, the legal "moral rights" you hear about in europe, the priority-and-credit norms people describe in science, the matthew effect, and the ordinary idea/expression line. i have a feeling it's none of those exactly. and if there's no name, coin one and draw the edges -- what counts, what doesn't, and whether this is one thing or three things wearing a trenchcoat. i don't need a bibliography, i need the carving.
