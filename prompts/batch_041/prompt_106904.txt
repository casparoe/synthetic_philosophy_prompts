So I've been refereeing this thing between two friends for like three months now and I need it to stop. One of them is a hard retributivist — desert is the whole story, hard treatment is what the guilty are owed, and if it happens to deter that's a bonus and not the point. The other is a card-carrying consequentialist — the only thing that can justify deliberately making someone suffer is some good that comes of it (deterrence, reform, incapacitation, norm-stabilization), full stop. They keep re-litigating the same five examples and talking past each other and I'm loosing my mind.

What I want is the joint statement they could both sign. Not a transcript of the argument — the document that says what they actually agree on, where they actually split, and what would move each of them. Specifically:

A numbered list of claims both of them will sign. I want these to be non-vacuous, so push on them: do they both accept that the innocent must never be punished, and is it for the same reason or different ones? Do they both accept that hard treatment is a pro tanto harm that stands in need of justification? Do they both think a literal eye-for-an-eye is a dead end? Do they agree that current sentencing practice is a mess, and if so for the same reasons?

Then a disagreements table. For each row: the crux stated in wording neither side would object to; then the same crux restated in each side's own preferred framing, phrased so that side would sign off on it; then a concrete case both of them agree bears on the crux; then each side's prediction about that case, written down in advance.

I want at least these cruxes in there, plus whatever else you think is load-bearing:

- The desert ledger. Suppose we could keep a perfectly accurate record of what each offender deserves — call it a ledger — while the actual penal system is run on purely consequentialist lines. Would the retributivist be satisfied by the ledger alone? Would the consequentialist care what's in it? I suspect this is where they'll find out whether "desert" is a claim about intrinsic value or a claim about what the state is licensed to do.
- The conscience implant. Cheap, safe, universally administered, eliminates recidivism entirely and leaves offenders genuinely remorseful. Nobody gets harmed again. Does any hard treatment remain warranted? Make each of them commit to a yes or no and say what that does to the other side's view.
- The unwitnessed crime, committed by someone who will never reoffend and dies tomorrow. Nothing to deter, no one to protect, nobody will ever know. What's the point of punishing, on each view?
- Proportionality. Ask each side where the ceiling on punishment comes from and whether they can state it without borrowing from the other side.
- Moral luck: does how bad it turned out change what's deserved, and does it change what's justified?

Last thing: if you think part of this fight is verbal — e.g. one of them is really talking about what's deserved while the other is talking about what the state may deliberately do — say which part and why, and give the disambiguated version of each side's claim so they can see it's two questions, not one.

Keep it as a document both of them could put their names on.
