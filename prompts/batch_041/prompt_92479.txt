I'm a novelist, halfway through a book set in a prehistoric-ish village, and I keep getting stuck on the same thing: how much am I allowed to put inside my characters' heads when all I've got is the stuff they left in the ground? So I've been going through cases and gut-checking them, and I want to know if there's a principle behind my gut.

A hoard of forty bronze axe heads, never sharpened, buried in a bog with no settlement for miles: I say votive, they were giving them to something. Same forty, but iron nails, unused, under a house floor: I say a smith's stash, purely economic. Those two feel different to me even though they're structurally identical, and I can't fully say why.

A little clay figurine under every threshold in a village: I'll say we can infer a practice -- something people did at doorways -- but not what they thought it was for. A grave with a wagon, a horse, and four cups: I'll happily say they thought the dead would travel and host. A big ring of stones lined up on the solstice sunrise: they tracked the sun, sure, but I won't say they worshipped it. And a temple with a fat altar and zero images: I genuinely can't tell if they thought their god had no shape or just didn't bother carving one, so I say we learn nothing.

The half-baked principle I've been carrying around is: if an object was made but never used, and the effort was lavish enough that it would be nuts to just store it, it points at belief; if it could plausibly be somebody's savings, it points at economics, and I shut up about belief. Does that cover all my verdicts or do I have to toss one? The axe/nail pair is where I feel it wobbling. And tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't thought about: a pit with thousands of tiny deliberately-broken pots, or a hoard of coins mixed with jewelry and one human skull. I don't want a reading list, I just want to know what I'm allowed to write. (Sorry for the long one, I've had alot of coffee.)
