I have been working on the Judy Benjamin problem for some time, and I am struck by how little convergence there is in the literature. The setup is familiar: Judy, a paratrooper, is dropped into a field divided into Blue and Red territories, each of which is divided into Headquarters and Second Company areas. She has no information about where she is, so she assigns equal probability to each of the four cells. She then receives a message from someone who claims to know something but will not tell her which territory she is in: she is told that if she is in Red territory, then she is in the Headquarters company area. She treats the message as reliable. The question is how she should update her credences.

What puzzles me is that the candidate answers diverge sharply. Conditionalizing on the material conditional that the message expresses — treating it as equivalent to the proposition that she is not in the Red Second Company area — yields a posterior on which her probability of being in Red drops to one third. Minimizing relative entropy subject to the constraint that the conditional probability of Headquarters given Red is one yields, as far as I can tell, the same result. But many of us have the intuition that the message tells her nothing about whether she is in Red or Blue, so her probability of being in Red should remain one half, with the conditional probability of Headquarters given Red going to one. Others argue that the intuition is unreliable, or that the message should not be modeled as a proposition at all, or that the problem is underdescribed because the reliability of the message and the mechanism by which it was selected matter.

The debate has not resolved, and I want to understand why. Is this a substantive disagreement about which formal rule is correct, or is it a verbal dispute about what we mean by the evidence? Are the parties answering different questions — one about what an ideally rational agent would do, another about what a fallible human should do, another about how to represent the learning event in a probabilistic model? Is the impasse due to a clash of intuitions that neither side can argue for, or is there a hidden formal or empirical question that would settle it? My own hypothesis is that the disagreement is really about whether the message should be represented as a proposition to be conditionalized on or as a constraint on a conditional probability, and that once that is settled the rest follows — but I am not confident.

I would like a diagnosis, not a survey. Tell me what kind of disagreement this is, using what each side actually says, and say what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or an argument that no progress should be expected. If the problem is ill-posed, say so and explain why.
