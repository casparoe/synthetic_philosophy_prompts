For some months I have been trying to find out whether there is a received name for a pattern that keeps arising in my own small decisions, and I should say at once that a bare label would not satisfy me. I read a little philosophy in the evenings but have no training in it, so what I want is the thing properly pinned down: an account of what it is, what it is not, and where its edges lie, together with an honest comparison against the nearest ideas already in circulation.

The pattern is easiest to see in an ordinary case. I am deciding whether to confide in a friend. Nothing external compels either course. But I notice that my impulse to confide is itself a clue about how things will go: being the sort of person who leans towards confiding in this particular friend is correlated with being let down, and the correlation is a plain fact about people like me rather than anything my choosing brings about. So I reason that my leaning towards confiding is evidence against confiding. I then turn to the opposite course and find the identical structure — my reluctance to confide is evidence that I am the sort who bottles things up and is later resented for it, so the reluctance is evidence against silence. Each branch, examined honestly, looks like the worse one; the deliberation that was supposed to settle the matter keeps returning evidence that neither branch is good; and the deliberation itself is one of the things the regularity is tracking. What unsettles me is that there is no person doing the predicting here — no oracle, no adversary with designs on me — only a statistical tendency, and yet the tendency seems to bind me just as tightly.

Is there an established name for this configuration? I am particularly concerned to know whether it is the very same thing as the old puzzle in which a man tries to outwit a prophecy of his own death and, in fleeing, brings it about — or whether the absence of an intentional predictor changes the analysis, and whether the two cases come apart further once I allow that I might set about becoming a different sort of person, so that the regularity simply ceases to apply to me. If nothing in the literature matches it exactly, please say so plainly and then coin a term, defining it so that it can be told apart from its nearest neighbours — the situation in which the correlation is deliberately engineered by another agent, and the ordinary business of an outcome being merely self-fulfilling.
