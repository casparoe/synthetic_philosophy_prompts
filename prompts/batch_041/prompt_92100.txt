My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about how to read a statute — he is a committed textualist, I drift purposivist — and we eventually noticed that neither of us had an actual case in mind; we were trading intuitions about which approach is more predictable. So I want to settle it by constructing a case rather than finding one, and I want the construction to be unkind to both of us.

Build me a statute and a fact pattern in which the plain meaning of the operative words reaches further than the statute's discernible purpose, so that the textualist is pushed to the broader reading and the purposivist to the narrower one — the reverse of the lazy assumption that textualism is the stingy theory and purposivism the generous one. Constraints: a mundane setting (a licence, a fee, a filing deadline, a duty to report), nothing constitutional; the purpose has to be recoverable from the statute's own structure and from the mischief it addresses, not from legislative history, because I refuse to stipulate what a legislature secretly had in mind; and it must not be a gotcha — the operative words must be genuinely plain and genuinely over-inclusive, not sloppily drafted. Then take one further step, which is the part I actually care about. Define *purpose in continuance* as the view that a statute's purpose is whatever would best serve its underlying aim as the world now stands, rather than the aim the enacting legislature had. Say what purpose in continuance does with your case and why it separates from both textualism and purposivism — or, if it collapses into one of them, say which and identify the feature of the case that made it collapse.

Give me four sections. "The case," a paragraph or two. "What each reading says," walking through textualism, purposivism, and purpose in continuance at a few sentences each. "What is doing the work," naming the single feature the whole result hangs on, what happens if it is removed, and how a defender of the theory that comes out worst would attack the construction — about a paragraph. And "How my friend fights back," the strongest textualist reply, in a few sentences. No case citations or real statutes; I am not asking you to find law, I am asking you to build a hypothetical.
