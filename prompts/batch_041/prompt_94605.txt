I'm in a cognitive science reading group and we keep getting stuck on Dennett's three stances, the physical, design, and intentional ones, and on what it means for a pattern to be real rather than just a useful fiction. But the reason I'm asking is that my lab has an actual written policy about how we talk about the AI systems we build, and I want to know what it's secretly committed to. The policy says every claim about a system's behavior has to be tagged with the stance it's made from. If we say the model emitted a token because of a gradient update, that's physical. If we say it summarizes documents because that's what it was designed to do, that's design. If we say it's trying to be helpful, or that it wants to avoid a bad rating, that's intentional. You cant mix tags in one sentence, and intentional claims are allowed in product meetings but banned from the safety audit, where only physical and design claims count. At the same time the policy says the system's personality is a real pattern, not just a useful fiction, because it stays stable across prompts and predicts user retention better than any lower-level description we've tried.

So: what philosophical committments does this practice presuppose about real patterns and the three stances? Does the tagging only make sense if the intentional stance is a predictive strategy we can adopt and drop at will, or does it presuppose something stronger, like intentional patterns being real in the same way physical patterns are? Which rival views does it quietly rule out? And is there a real tension between calling the personality pattern real and banning intentional talk from the safety audit, or can both be true under some account of what makes a pattern real? I'd like the implicit theory reconstructed, with a line between what the policy strictly needs and what it merely fits well with, and a note on what would have to change if we decided only physical-stance claims get to be called real. If the policy is underdetermined, tell me which extra clause would settle it.
