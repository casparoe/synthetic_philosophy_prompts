"Fitch's paradox is the proof that not everything can be known. Take the anti-realist principle that every truth is knowable. Since we plainly don't know everything, the knowability principle must be false, and with it the anti-realism of Dummett and his followers. The proof turns on the law of excluded middle together with the assumption that knowledge is closed under conjunction, which is why intuitionists and other friends of non-classical logic have tried to wriggle out of it. So realism is vindicated: there are truths that nobody can ever know, and the only move left to the anti-realist is to water down 'knowable' to 'knowable in principle under ideal conditions', which turns the thesis into something no realist would ever deny."
