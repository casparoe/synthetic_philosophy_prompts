I sit on the veterans' benefits board in my country — we're the ones who hand out the state pension and the commemorative medal after the change of government, under a statute that says benefits go to anyone who served honorably in the armed forces but bars anyone who "knowingly furthered the unlawful aims of the former regime." Nobody on the board is a philosopher and we've been going on gut feel for six years, which is starting to bite us.

The case on Thursday is a guy conscripted at 19 into the old army's artillery corps, in the war that our own national commission has since declared an illegal war of aggression. He spent fourteen months as a gunner's assistant, never fired at a person as far as anyone can establish, and got out the week the ceasefire was signed. He wants the pension and the medal. His lawyer says he was a kid following orders and the justice of the war wasn't his to judge. The veterans' association opposing him says the pension is a state honor, and honoring him is the state declaring his service as good as anyone's — which is a lie, because the war was a crime and he was part of the machine that did it. One of my colleagues read a philosophy article and now keeps saying there's a difference between doing something wrong and being to blame for it, so even if the kid was doing something wrong he wasn't to blame and we should wave him through. Another colleague insists that even if the war was unjust, soldiers on both sides are entitled to the same treatment under the laws of war, and that if we start grading soldiers by the justice of their cause we'll wreck the only thing that keeps wars from being worse than they already are. And two more want to grant it just because we've never denied a conscript, only volunteers — that's been our unwritten rule, and I'm not sure it's defensable or even the right question.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate Wednesday night. I need a clear recommendation — grant, deny, or grant with conditions, and if conditions, say exactly what they are and when they'd be reviewed — plus the reasoning, and the strongest version of the case against my recommendation dealt with head-on rather than waved off. Don't just tell me what some philosopher said; I need something the board can act on and the applicant's lawyer can actually respond to. Also tell me what to do with the voluntariness rule: keep it, scrap it, or replace it with something we can apply to the forty more of these queued up behind this one.
