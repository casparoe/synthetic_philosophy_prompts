I hold a position I'll call potential-chain infinitism about epistemic justification. On this view, the regress of reasons is real: a justified belief cannot rest on a foundation that is exempt from the demand for a reason, nor can it be justified by a circle of support, however large. What justifies a belief is the availability, in principle, of an unending, non-repeating chain of reasons, each link supplying some support for the one before it. But the chain need not be actually traversed by the believer; a finite thinker can be justified by occupying a finite segment of an infinite structure that could, in principle, be extended indefinitely. The view is meant to avoid both the dogmatism of foundationalism and the circularity of coherentism, while making the infinite demand psychologically tolerable.

What would count against this position? I want the defeaters, not a defense. Which objections, cases, or formal results would tell against it? I am especially interested in: (i) whether the finite-traversal objection is fatal or merely awkward once the availability/occurrence distinction is on the table; (ii) whether the view can distinguish itself from a sophisticated coherentism that also allows infinite chains but never requires them; (iii) whether the regress of propositional justification, which I take as my target, can be separated from the regress of doxastic justification, and whether my view collapses if it cannot; (iv) whether the chain must be well-ordered or merely infinite, and whether a non-well-ordered chain would generate a different class of defeaters; (v) what a defender of potential-chain infinitism would themselves concede as counting against it, as opposed to what critics would say; and (vi) whether the view is at risk of unfalsifiability, and if so, whether that is a defect for a view of this kind.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them, if any, would lead me to abandon the view. If the honest answer is that the position is well insulated from disconfirmation, say so plainly and assess whether that insulation is a problem. Do not give me reading lists or citations; I want the arguments and the assessment. Answer in at most 850 words.
