"Rand's central ethical claim is that altruism is a vice and selfishness a virtue. On her view the only rational thing a person can do is pursue his own interests, so any act that benefits someone else at some cost to oneself — giving to charity, helping a friend move, staying late for a colleague — is a betrayal of one's own life. She holds that human beings are selfish by nature and that morality should stop pretending otherwise, and she argues that a society organized around self-interest will maximize total well-being, which is why she defended unregulated capitalism. Her case against altruism is, at bottom, that it makes everyone worse off. The obvious difficulty is that nobody could actually live this way, since a world of pure self-interest would be a war of all against all; her ethics therefore stands refuted, and a reader who feels the pull of her prose should remember that the doctrine it dresses up is simply greed."
