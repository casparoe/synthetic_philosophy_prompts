I'm the lay member on the therapeutic use exemption panel of our national anti-doping body — not the endocrinologist, the person they put on so it isn't all doctors — and I have to circulate my written opinion before we vote next Thursday. Can you draft it for me? I'm not telling you which way I lean, because honestly I don't know yet.

The case: an elite 800m runner, late twenties, male, has applied for a TUE for a hormone on the prohibited list. Everybody on the panel agrees the stuff is performance-enhancing in healthy people; thats not the fight. The fight is this. His natural levels sit at the very bottom of the population reference range, but they're well below his own documented baseline from when he was 19 to 21 — we have the old labs, he was tested constantly as a junior. He has symptoms: poor recovery, low bone density, the usual list. No clinician will give him a disease diagnosis. The endocrinologist we paid to look at it says the drop may be partly caused by his own training load, and nobody can tell us it's reversible by anything except the drug itself. Two of my colleagues want to make this the test case for a general rule — grant a TUE when the treatment restores an athlete to their individual baseline, deny when it pushes them above it. The other two think that's mad: the population range is the only line that isn't just a way of saying 'give me my career best back in a syringe', and they point out we granted something similar two years ago and the sport is still arguing about whether that was right.

So: the memo. It should land on grant, deny, or grant with conditions (and if conditions, spell out exactly what they are, who monitors them, and how we square it with the two-years-ago case). I need it to state plainly the strongest case for whichever side it rules against — half the panel holds that view and I'm not going to circulate something that pretends they're being silly. And I'd like it to actually wrestle with the therapy/enhancement distinction instead of just declaring where the line falls, because I've noticed we all use those two words to mean totally different things and nobody's called it out yet.

Two last things. Tell me what would change your mind — the specific evidence or argument that would flip your recommendation — and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to fail, since whatever we decide here becomes precedent and I'd rather hear it now than at the appeal. And start with the actual recommendation in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning after; no preamble, and don't restate the case back to me. Keep it to about a page and a half, alot of us will be reading it on our phones.
