I'm putting together a handout for a small reading group thats doing a session on Berkeley's Querist, the book of questions he published in the 1730s about Ireland's economy, where he keeps pressing the idea that money isnt really a commodity in its own right but more like a ticket or a counter for moving real wealth around, and that a country's real wealth is its land and the labor of its people rather than its stock of coin. What I want is a decision tree that takes a reader from a few basic commitments about money to a named position on what money is and what makes it valuable.

Please build it so the branch nodes are diagnostic questions, not labels -- each node a concrete case or yes/no question that someone can answer without already knowing the name of the view it leads to. Cases I want used along the way: the government announces tomorrow that it will no longer accept its own currency in payment of taxes; a bank fails and a small town starts issuing its own scrip that local shops agree to take; an island with plenty of gold but no food or tools; a country where everyone has lots of money but nothing to buy with it; a system with perfect public record-keeping of who owes what to whom, so no tokens ever change hands; and a case where the money supply is cut in half overnight and all prices and wages adjust proportionally.

Leaves I need it to reach: the commodity/metallist view, the state-and-taxes view, the credit/debt view, Berkeley's ticket-or-counter view (with a note on which modern positions it most resembles and where it actually differs from them), the convention or status-function view, the neutral-money/quantity view, and the money-as-public-record view. Where two leaves can't be seperated by any of the questions, say so instead of pretending the tree distinguishes them. Where a combination of answers doesn't match any real position, flag it and say in one sentence whether thats because the combo is incoherent or just nobody has defended it. Also give me one sentence per leaf on that position's most awkward commitment.

American English please.
