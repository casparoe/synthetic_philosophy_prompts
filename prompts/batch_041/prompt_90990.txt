I've been volunteering on an oral history project that collects testimony from people who lived through a couple of different conflicts, and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for.

Here's the thing I keep seeing. The whole setup — the form we fill in, the way the interviews get edited, the way the funders talk about the project — assumes there are two kinds of people: the ones something was done to, and the ones who did it. And a lot of our narrators are both. A woman whose older son was disappeared by the security forces and whose younger son was killed by neighbours a few years later for talking to them. A man who was picked up and held for months, and who after he got out was part of a group that went after suspected informers. From what I've read about Rwanda, the same thing comes up with the gacaca courts — people who lost relatives to one side's killings sitting in the same room being asked to account for what they did, and the paperwork really does want you to be one or the other, because that's what decides whether you're there for reparation or for confession. When someone is both, the interview goes badly: they keep correcting the questions, the story comes out tangled, and the editor cuts it because it doesnt fit the shape.

What I want to know is whether there's an established term for this, and I don't mean just the obvious point that victim and perpetrator can be the same person. I half-remember something from Holocaust testimony — something about a grey zone where the lines get muddied — but as I understand it that's about complicity and degrees of collaboration, not about someone standing on both ends of the violence in the same conflict. I'm after the specific thing where the categories the institution needs are the wrong shape for the people it's processing.

So: is there a name? If there are several nearby ones, tell me which is closest, how each differs from what I've described, and which of my examples don't belong. If nothing quite fits, coin something, define it, and tell me where the edges are — does it need one person, or does a whole group count? Does it matter whether the two roles were in the same conflict, or whether the first caused the second? Is there a separate name for the institutional failure as opposed to the personal situation?

And please tell me what would change your mind — what would have to be true for you to say there's no established concept here — and where you're most likely to be wrong, especially if you're half-remembering a term yourself.
