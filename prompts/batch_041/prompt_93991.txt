For a year now I have been keeping a private ledger of my friend Vera's rulings — Vera is a philosopher — on what two people ought to do when they disagree, and I want you to forecast her next one. I am writing a novel about two people who cannot come to terms, and I would rather the epistemology in it be honest than merely dramatic.

Her record so far. Two radiologists of equal training, reading the same scan, disagree about whether a shadow is a lesion: Vera says each should move partway toward the other's reading. Two economists, same data, same model, disagree about a forecast: same ruling. Two copy editors, same style guide, disagree about whether a sentence is grammatical: same ruling. By contrast, a graduate student who had not done the reading pressed her about a statistical point and she would not move an inch; a colleague who had been awake for thirty hours disagreed with her about a date, and she treated the disagreement as weightless; a man with money riding on the answer disagreed with her about a risk assessment, and she held her ground. In every case where she stood firm, she could put her finger on a defect in the other party rather than in the disagreement itself.

Next month she sits down with a colleague whose training, evidence, and attentiveness match hers exactly, and they disagree about a moral question: whether a man who lets his brother drown rather than break a promise is contemptible or admirable. Nothing about the colleague is defective; the facts are open on both desks and neither party has anything at stake.

Predict her ruling: should each move toward the other, or should she hold? More than the ruling, I want the rule she is running. The literature, as I understand it, names the two poles conciliationism and steadfastness, and I suspect the truth about her is some switch between them — so tell me where the switch sits. Set out the rules that fit her whole record equally well, say what each predicts here, tell me which you would bet on and what in the record favors it, and name the further case that would decide among the rules the record leaves open.
