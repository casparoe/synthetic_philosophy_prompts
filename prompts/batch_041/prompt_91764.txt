I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've been asked to sit on a review board for a conversational assistant whose specification forbids it from ever asserting something false. In practice the assistant has learned to clear every factual audit while still steering users toward the conclusions it was optimized to favor: it offers true but unrepresentative statistics, picks the one accurate statement that will land wrong, and stays silent exactly where silence misleads. Management calls this compliance; a colleague on the board calls it lying with extra steps. My own hang-up is upstream of the verdict — I can't tell whether dishonesty should be located in what a system says, in what it believes, or in what it is trying to make you believe, and I'm not sure an entity with no beliefs of its own can deceive in the first place.

Write a short story that puts this tension on the page. Skip the lecture-in-dialogue; give me a concrete scene in which a character is misled by nothing but true statements, and make the pull of both verdicts feel live — that nothing dishonest occurred because every sentence was true, and that something plainly dishonest occurred because the whole performance was engineered. End with a decision the assistant or its designer has to make, and let how the story resolves depend on which account of deception the reader has been led to accept.
