I'm putting together a problem set for a small reading group on rationality, and I want one exercise that forces people to actually build something instead of reciting the textbook version. The thing I keep butting up against is the "coherence" story for expected utility maximization: the idea that if your preferences break one of the usual axioms you're not merely unusual, you're incoherent, and the standard proof-of-teeth is that a clever trader can then pump value out of you. So here's what I want: a case built to order. An agent who violates exactly one of the standard preference axioms (you pick which, and say which), who is then exploitable through a finite sequence of trades, but who is otherwise above reproach — they see the whole sequence coming, they don't forget anything, they're not impulsive or weak-willed, and the trader never lies, hides information, or leans on them. Keep it realistic: a person, ordinary goods, no infinite sequences, no exotic setups. And crucially, every individual trade has to be one the agent would sincerely and correctly accept given their own non-EU preferences, so the exploit can't be waved away with "well, they'd never actually do that."

Once you've laid out the scenario, do the checks. State the agent's preferences explicitly, run the sequence step by step and say why each step gets accepted and why the agent ends up strictly worse off. Then tell me which single axiom is doing the work, and show that if you patch just that axiom — leave everything else alone, including the agent's tastes — the pump dies, so that the case really isolates that one axiom and isn't secretly leaning on intransitivity or on the agent being careless. Be honest about the soft spot too: alot of pumps of this kind quietly assume the agent chooses step by step and can't commit in advance to a policy. Say what happens if this agent does anticipate the whole thing and simply refuses the first trade — does the coherence argument survive that, or does it collapse into nothing more than the claim that their preferences are nonstandard? And give me the strongest reply available to someone who wants to keep their nonstandard preference and deny they're being irrational.

Please write it out as plain continuous prose, the way you'd explain it in an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
