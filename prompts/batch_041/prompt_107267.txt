Ok so software engineer here, no formal philosophy background, and theres a pattern I keep noticing at work that I'm pretty sure has a name somewhere but I dont know it. Take any long-lived service. Over hundreds of deploys it gets patched, restarted, most of its code swapped out, and we all talk about it as the same service that just changed — one thing that persists and happens to have different properties at different times. But the second something breaks, we flip and talk about it as a sequence of versions, each one a separate snapshot, where 'the service' is really just the stack of those snapshots. Same thing with a company: it's the same company that just replaced half its staff, or it's a different company every few years. Nobody acts like the two ways of talking contradict each other, they're just for different jobs. But I've gathered philosophers think there's a real question underneath all this — whether a thing that changes is genuinely one thing that stays itself, or is really a bunch of time-slices bolted together, and that exactly one of those has to be right. Is there a name for the position that says neither description is more fundamental, both are legit, and which one is 'real' just depends on what you're trying to do? And if thats not an established view, what would you call it — and how is it actually different from just dismissing the whole thing as a word dispute? I'm not after a one-word answer, I want to know what the thing really is, what the nearest named ideas are, and where this one stops applying. Answer in American English please, thanks.
