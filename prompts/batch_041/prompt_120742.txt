Mencius and Xunzi are usually introduced as the optimist and the pessimist about human nature, and the standard illustration runs one way: Mencius expects the moral tendencies to grow so long as they are not starved or cut down, while Xunzi expects goodness to be manufactured by ritual and deliberate effort working against the grain of our native dispositions. I want a case engineered to run the other way, with the reversal falling out of the two accounts of how character is formed and lost rather than out of the labels.

Build one realistic scenario, no longer than a paragraph: a single ordinary person over one continuous stretch of life, with no rulers, wars, famines, or anything else either thinker already uses as a stock illustration, in which Mencius's own account yields the prediction that this person does not become good, while Xunzi's own account yields the prediction that he or she does. Neither verdict may be written into the description; each has to follow from what the thinker says about the material we start with, what shapes it, and what counts as success. The outcome should turn on a single feature of the case, with everything else held fixed.

Then do the work: give each verdict with the chain of reasoning that produces it; identify the feature doing the work; say what each thinker would predict if that feature were removed or reversed, and whether the case then collapses into the familiar direction; and say how a defender of whichever thinker comes out worse would attack the case, including whether the defender can claim that the two are simply using different standards for what counts as becoming good, and whether that concession would leave the disagreement substantive or merely verbal. This is for a seminar whose point is that the optimism/pessimism contrast is a bad summary of their disagreement, so the case has to survive the charge of being rigged.
