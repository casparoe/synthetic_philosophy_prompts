For some time now I have been collecting small cases in which I am confident that a wrong has been done to me, and unable to say what the wrong consists in, and I would like to know whether the thing I keep noticing has a name. If it has one, I want the name and, much more, the definition and its edges; if it has none, I would like you to coin one and tell me what falls outside it. A bare label would leave me exactly where I am.

Here are the cases. A friend reads a letter I had left on the table; the letter tells her nothing she did not already know, and I would cheerfully have read it to her had she asked, yet something was done to me, and it cannot be the increase in her knowledge, since there was none. A doctor consults my file out of curiosity, finds nothing remarkable and treats me no differently; I am wronged, and it seems to matter that she did not ask, though I would have said yes. A fact about me reaches two acquaintances by different routes, one from my own telling and one inferred from a pattern in my purchases and movements; I resent the second and not the first, even though the second has the less accurate picture of me. And in the other direction, I am sometimes glad that something about me is known and would be sorry to have it forgotten, while still objecting to the file it sits in and to the fact that the file can be opened without my knowing.

What strikes me about all four is that the wrong does not vary with how much is learned, how sensitive the fact is, or what is done with it afterwards. It seems to lie in the route rather than the destination: in whether I was asked, in whether what is known about me is held as a representation of me rather than received from me, in whether the knower can act on it without me. But I cannot state this without making either a claim too thin to be a wrong at all (that I was not consulted) or a claim that is plainly false (that I lost control of something I wanted to keep, when in one case I would have handed it over and in another the fact was never mine to withhold).

I am aware of the usual answers: that privacy is control over information about oneself, that it is being inaccessible to others, that it is a matter of the norms belonging to a context, that it is a condition of intimacy and of the self. Each fits part of what I notice and misdescribes the rest, and none of them tells me what the object of the wrong is — a state of affairs, an act, a relation, or a standing power.

So tell me what this is called, if it is called anything, and if it is not, name it. Either way I want the boundaries: whether my four cases are one phenomenon or several that I have wrongly bundled together, which of them the concept covers badly, and whether someone who learns nothing, remembers nothing and does nothing with what they did not learn can be wronged at all — and if so, by what. Please write it as continuous prose, without headings, lists, bold text or tables, the way one would write a letter; the label is the least of what I am after, the carving is the point.
