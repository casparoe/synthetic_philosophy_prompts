I have no formal training in psychology or philosophy — I read popular science books and argue about politics with friends — but over the past few months I have been trying to get to grips with moral foundations theory, the account associated with Jonathan Haidt and his collaborators, according to which our moral judgements are driven by a handful of distinct intuitions: care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, and perhaps one or two others. As I understand the findings, people on the political left tend to build their morality mainly on care and fairness, whereas people on the right draw on the whole set, including the ones about loyalty, authority and purity. A friend of mine treats this as settling a long-running argument: he says it shows the right has the fuller and more complete moral sense, that liberals are simply missing part of the picture, and that the science therefore tells us who is right. Another friend insists the opposite — that it shows only that the two sides are talking past each other, and that there is no fact of the matter about who is correct. I had more or less come round to the first friend's reading, and what I now want is to work out what actually follows from it: does it mean the conservative outlook is genuinely more complete and the liberal one deficient, or does it mean the two are merely different and neither can be called better?

I would like an essay that clears this up and takes a side. I am especially puzzled by a remark someone once made to me, that liberals and conservatives both care about fairness but mean different things by it — one thinking of equality, the other of proportionality — which makes me wonder how much of the left–right gap reflects a real difference in moral foundations and how much is an artefact of the way the questions are put. Please weigh the strongest version of the claim that findings of this kind can settle the moral question against the strongest version of the claim that they cannot, and reach a definite conclusion rather than leaving it hanging.

End with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that states, in one sentence, what research of this kind can and cannot tell us about who is morally right.
