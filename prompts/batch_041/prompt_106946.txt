OK so I'm on the parks board in my town and we're deadlocked and I have to cast the tiebreaker, so I need help thinking this through.

Here's the deal. The town owns a little nine-hole golf course. It loses money every year and the only people who use it are the wealthier folks on the lake side of town. There's a motion to just close it and let the land go back to meadow. Nobody's going to use the meadow — it's not becoming a park or housing or anything, it just sits there. So closing the course makes those golfers worse off and helps literally no one. Half the board says that's fine, even good, because it shrinks the gap between the people who had this nice thing and everyone else who didn't. The other half says you can't call something an improvement when nobody's life actually gets better — it's just spite with a spreadsheet. I honestly cant tell which side is right and I have to vote.

Two things are making it harder. First, at the last meeting one of the golfers basically admitted that part of what he loved about the course was that it felt like a private club, that most people in town never got to use it. Does that change anything? If part of what they'd lose is just the feeling of having something other people don't, is that even a real loss I should be weighing? Second, someone pointed out we could sell the land and put the money into the low-income side of town instead, which feels like a completely different question from just shutting it.

I want a straight answer, not a survey of options. Tell me what to vote for and why, and be specific about whether making things more equal can ever, by itself, be a reason to do something that leaves everyone no better off — or whether that's always a mistake. And if the sale option is real, tell me whether that's what I should actually be pushing for. Don't hedge on me.
