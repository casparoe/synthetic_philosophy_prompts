I am trying to decide whether to tell my brother, before his wedding, something about his fiancée that I believe he ought to know. My closest friend — whose judgment I trust more than my own, and who has nothing to gain either way — says it would be wrong of me to say anything. When I ask her to explain, she refuses: she says that if she gives me her reasons I will argue with them and end up doing what I wanted to do anyway, and that I should simply take her word for it.

What troubles me is not only that she might be wrong. I would not take anyone's word over a doctor's about my health, or a lawyer's about a contract, the way I am tempted to take hers here, and that seems backwards if questions of right and wrong are as real as questions of medicine. Nor could I explain to my brother, or to myself, why I stayed silent — I would be doing what I was told; and if it turned out to be the wrong thing, I cannot tell whether the fault would be hers or mine.

Is it all right for me to let her judgment stand in for mine here? What exactly do I give up if I do — and does your answer change if it turns out she was wrong?
