I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy background, and my company has voluntold me to run a 45-minute session at our monthly engineering lunch-and-learn. We've got sales and support people in four countries now and there's been grumbling — someone expensed a very nice dinner for a client's procurement manager, someone else got asked for a consulting fee by a local official, and HR's only answer is check the compliance PDF. I want the session to be about the actual question underneath: what makes a gift corrupt, and is that judgment the same everywhere or are we just exporting a Western standard? I need the plan for explaining it, not the explanation itself.

So: what order do the pieces have to come in? I have a hunch you can't get anywhere near the question is this corrupt until they've got the idea of an office or a role and what its terms are, and that everything downstream — gifts, nepotism, everyone does it, relativism — hangs off that. If that's the wrong spine, give me the right one and tell me why mine breaks.

What's the one example that carries the whole thing? I was going to use a customs officer and a fifty dollar note, but that's the version everyone's already seen a hundred times and they'll have an opinion locked in before I finish the sentence. Maybe something where money isn't even the point — a cousin who gets the contract and happens to be the cheapest bid, or a nurse who expects something for jumping the queue.

Then the misconceptions, in the order they'll actually show up in the room, and where in the sequence I should head each one off. My guesses: that corrupt just means illegal; that it requires cash; that it's about the size of the amount; that it's just their culture, so case closed; that corruption is obviously inefficient; and that we in the West are the clean ones. Plus two or three people who think the whole topic is HR nonsense and want to get back to their tickets.

Also, my own half-baked theory — tell me if it's wrong or just a bad name for something that already exists. I think a gift and a bribe differ less in size or in intent than in what happens to the relationship afterwards: a gift keeps a relationship alive and puts both sides in each other's debt indefinitely, a bribe ends the relationship, it's a transaction that closes. If that's the useful thread, build on it. If it collapses on the hard case where the office itself is a family obligation, say so and pick something else to carry it.

Finish with the one question I can ask at the end that tells me whether they got the structure of the thing rather than just came away feeling bad about bribery, and what I should openly say I'm not going to settle in 45 minutes.
