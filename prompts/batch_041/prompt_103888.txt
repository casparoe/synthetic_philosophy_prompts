Bit of a weird one, and I'm not sure I've framed it right, but here goes.

I've been reading alot of mechanistic interpretability work — the sort where people train a little classifier (a "probe") on a network's internal activations to see whether some concept is decodable from layer 17, or train a sparse autoencoder to break the residual stream into a big dictionary of features, or do activation patching to find which components matter for a task. The field constantly says things like "the model represents X here" or "we found the feature for Y". But the same people will turn round and say a probe only shows that information is decodable, not that the model uses it, and that you need a causal intervention before you can call it a real representation. And someone else will say features aren't the right unit at all, because of superposition — everything is smeared across many directions and the dictionary is an artefact of your sparsity penalty.

What I want is this: what does that practice actually presuppose about representation? Treat it as a given that I'm pointing at a real, existing research practice and asking what it commits you to — not asking you to defend it. So reconstruct the implicit theory. Does saying "the model represents X" commit you to X being a thing in the network, to an internal state with content, to something with a causal role, or just to a decodable correlation? Which rival pictures of representation does the practice quietly rule out — does it presuppose realism over instrumentalism, a features-first ontology over a thoroughly distributed one, genuine content-ascription over a deflationary "it's only weights and activations" line? Where do the parts pull against each other — the "a probe isn't enough" line and the "we found the feature" line look like they need different theories, so tell me whether that's a real tension or whether there's a reading that reconciles them.

Be careful to separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits nicely with; I suspect a lot of it is underdetermined and several theories would license the same behaviour. And then the counterfactual: if you swapped the presupposed view for a rival — say a flatly instrumentalist one, where "representation" is just a useful redescription with no claim about what's inside — what would actually have to change? What would stop counting as evidence, what would still go through unchanged, and would anything about how the work is done have to be abandoned?

Please push back if I've set this up badly. If "what does the practice presuppose" is the wrong question for this case, or if the inconsistency I'm seeing is just me misreading what people mean, say so plainly rather than humouring me — I'd rather have the objection than a tidy answer that ignores it. British English, please.
