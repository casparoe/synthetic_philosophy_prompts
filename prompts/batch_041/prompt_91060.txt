I sit on the ballot-initiative review board for my state, which is one of the ones with a single-subject rule, and we have to sign off on the text before anything goes to the printers. Our track record so far: we certified one that raised a tax and earmarked the revenue for schools; we struck one that changed how the governor is elected and also lowered the threshold for recalling him, because when we split it for polling the recall half only pulled about 30% on its own; we certified one that was drafted as a constitutional amendment but would in practice operate like an ordinary statute; we certified one with a sloppy, arguably misleading title, on the theory that titles get cleaned up downstream and aren't really our business; and we struck one that handed the actual rate-setting to a commission the initiative would create.

Next month's item: an initiative that caps executive pay at state contractors (polls around 70% on its own) and in the same text bans a class of pesticide on farmland (polls around 65% on its own), and it directs a new state agency to work out the exact cap and the phase-out schedule.

What should we do if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which past decision is the closest analog, and if our precedents dont settle it, what principle are we buying into with each way of going? And tell me what future initiative would actually show which rule we've been running all along.
