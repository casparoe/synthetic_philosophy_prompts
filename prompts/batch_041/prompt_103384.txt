I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I'm preparing an application for a one-year research fellowship at my company's responsible-AI group. The deliverable is a short proposal of roughly two pages, and I have only a half-formed idea that I would like you to turn into something rigorous.

My starting point is that the guidance we write for AI assistants — when to decline a request, when to do something other than what the user asked — tends to collapse two distinctions. The first is between requests whose compliance would harm someone other than the user and requests whose compliance would harm the user themselves. The second is between declining to help and actively overriding the user's stated preference. I suspect that much of the debate over AI paternalism is really a dispute in which the second distinction gets mistaken for the first, and that the question worth asking is not whether an assistant should ever be paternalistic but what kind of relationship it stands in to the person it assists.

I would like the proposal to (a) state the central question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; (b) explain why the usual framings — safety guidelines, the standard objectives of helpfulness, honesty, and harmlessness, and simple consent models — leave that question open; (c) describe the method and why it is the right one, with a month-by-month plan; (d) say what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and (e) give an honest assessment of what might make the project too large for a single year.

As part of the framing, please take seriously the thought that the assistant might stand to the user as a tool, as an advisor, or as something closer to a fiduciary, and say whether that difference changes when overriding is permissible. I am also uneasy about the assumption that there is a single user whose wishes settle the matter: when an enterprise deploys the assistant on a user's behalf, or when the person making a request has a stake that their own future self may not share, whose preference is the one being overridden?

No bibliography or citations, please — I will add those myself. What I need is a proposal that makes the project look both genuinely open and actually doable.
