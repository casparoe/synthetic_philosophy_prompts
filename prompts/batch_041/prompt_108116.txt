Write me a joke about moral foundations theory — the one where morality comes in taste buds: care and fairness on the "individualizing" side, loyalty, authority and sanctity on the binding side, plus that sixth one, liberty/oppression, that got bolted on later and that libertarians and activists both claim for themselves, which is kind of the problem with it. What I find funniest about the theory is structural: it's built to map the moral palate rather than rank it, so the moment someone asks it "fine, but who's actually right?" it can only answer by telling you who tastes what — and it keeps its options open by adding a new foundation whenever a group turns up complaining they're not on the list. Build the joke on that, or on the two clusters, or both. It has to be funny to someone who has actually read the theory, so the punchline should turn on the real content and not on "liberals are snowflakes / conservatives are bigots" — if my flatmate who's never heard of Haidt laughs and a psych grad student doesn't, it's probablly a bad joke.

Then one section, headed "Where the joke cheats", roughly 150 words: which parts of what I've said are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who know this literature — whether the theory is really purely descriptive or quietly smuggles in a normative claim, and whether liberty/oppression is a real foundation or a patch for the groups it didn't predict. Don't hand me a contested reading as the consensus.
