I cover economics and decision theory for a living, and after too many years of writing about forecasters and macro models I've basically become a Keynesian about probablity — not the fiscal stuff, the Treatise of Probability stuff. The picture I've landed on: probability is a logical relation between a body of evidence and a proposition, so it's objective, but it's often not numerical and not even comparable across cases; more evidence makes the same probability better grounded, because the weight of the argument is a separate dimension from the probability itself; and in a lot of the situations that actually matter — investment, politics, the distant future — there's no probability to be had at all, so we fall back on convention, on what everyone else seems to be doing, on animal spirits. I find this both true and weirdly liberating, which is probably a warning sign.

So: what would count against it? I mean the whole package, not just the easy target. Which observations, formal results, thought experiments, or episodes would actually tell against the logical-relation claim, against weight being a genuinely separate dimension, against the non-numerical/non-comparable part, and against the flat claim that in some real situations there's simply no probability to be had? Rank them by how decisive they'd be, and tell me which ones a Keynesian would themselves concede count against them. I keep hearing the old objection that partial belief is just degree of belief, that a logical relation nobody can inspect isn't doing any work, and that everything interesting in the view survives perfectly well inside a subjectivist framework — is that a defeater, or a takeover? Also be honest about whether this has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if any surprise can be absorbed after the fact by saying there was no probability there, does that sink it, and does it sink the formal theory too or only the rhetoric? And do the two halves even hang together — the technical theory of probability and the later, looser talk about genuine uncertainty — or is one doing all the work while the other is decoration? If you think they come apart, say which one I should keep.
