I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for something like twelve years now, and the Meno problem keeps coming back to bite me in arguments — you know the one, why is knowledge supposed to be worth more than a plain true beleif? Last week I lost two hours in a group chat over it with a friend who's doing a PhD in philosophy, and the annoying part is that neither of us could say what the extra value was supposed to *be*. Stability? Resistance to counterevidence? Being buildable-on? Those all sounded like true beliefs with better insurance, not a different kind of thing.

So here's what I want from you: argue for my position, and argue it properly, not the polite version.

The position has two halves. First, split the value of a belief into two piles. Snapshot value is what it's worth to have this true belief right now — the content, its being true, its being usable. Pedigree value is what it's worth that you came by it the way you did. Claim one: everything knowledge has over mere true belief is pedigree value. Claim two: pedigree value isn't epistemic value at all, it's credit. It's about what you can be praised or blamed for, what you're entitled to lean on in front of other people, what you get to assert without someone raising an eyebrow and asking "how do you know?". Strip out the social practice of asking people to show their work, and the gap between the lucky guesser and the knower is a gap nobody has any reason to care about.

Two things I want included, because they're where I get stuck:

1. If knowledge is basically a license, why doesn't it feel like one? When I know something it feels like I'm in touch with the world in a way the guesser isn't, and that feeling is doing real work in my life that a purely social story seems to leave unexplained. Either the feeling is a trick, or the social story is missing something.
2. The hermit case. Someone who never asserts anything, never has to justify himself, never gets graded or sued or disbelieved — does knowledge buy him anything over true belief? If your answer is yes, say exactly what, and don't let it turn out to be stability with a new name.

Also give me the honest meta-read: am I doing anything here other than reinventing a well-worn position with fresh labels? If the two-pile split is just the credit view of knowledge in a hoodie, say so, and say what if anything I've actually added. And if the right move is to taboo the word "knowledge" and re-ask the question in terms of the underlying states, do that instead of humoring my vocabulary.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in each of the main claims you end up making, and single out the one point you're least sure about — I'd definately rather have a flagged weak spot than a clean-looking answer.
