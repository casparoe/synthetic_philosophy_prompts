Longtime rationalist-blog lurker here (SSC, LessWrong, the ACX comments). There's a pattern I keep seeing and I dont know if it has a name, and its bothering me more than it should.

Someone posts a take — a diet, a forecasting trick, a bit of software, a read on some news event. First commenter is lukewarm, next two agree because agreeing is cheap, and by the tenth comment everyone is saying the same thing. The part I care about is what happens to the ones who started out privately unconvinced: they read the pile-on as evidence, and the doubt stops being a doubt and turns into an actual belief. Six months later the whole thing falls over and everyone swears they saw it coming.

I know about information cascades, and about the preference-falsification / pluralistic-ignorance family where people just hide what they really think. But in those the private belief stays put. Here the public agreement actually eats the private doubt, and then the memory gets rewritten. Is there a name for that? If it's only a cascade plus hindsight bias, fine, but I want the carving: what exactly the thing is, where it stops being an information cascade, how it differs from preference falsification, pluralistic ignorance and groupthink, and where the edges are. If there's no name, coin one, and tell me whether it needs the later collapse, or the memory rewrite, to count at all.

No reading list please, I just want the concept. British spelling throughout.
