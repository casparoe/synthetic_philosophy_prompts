I am putting together a short position paper for a graduate seminar on the ethics of food, and I want a consistency check before I circulate it, because I have a nagging sense that I have smuggled in more than one incompatible standard without noticing. A colleague made a remark after my last presentation that I cannot stop chewing on, and rather than defend the whole thing piecemeal I would rather have someone trace the internal structure of the package and tell me where it cracks. Here is the position as I would state it. (1) The badness of suffering does not depend on species: a mouse's pain is as bad, in itself, as a human's, and the same goes for a chicken's. (2) What we ought to minimize in the food system is the total amount of suffering we cause, not merely the number of deaths and not merely human interests. (3) Plant agriculture is not harm-free; I take it as settled that harvesting a field of grain or soy kills very large numbers of small animals, so a purely plant-based diet carries a substantial death toll of its own. (4) Partly for that reason I have come to think that, calorie for calorie, eating chicken is worse than eating beef: a single steer yields an enormous number of edible calories from one death, whereas supplying the same calories from poultry requires the deaths of a great many birds. (5) At the same time I am confident that grass-fed beef is better than grain-fed beef, since grain-fed cattle consume feed crops and so multiply precisely the field deaths I invoked in (3). (6) And I hold that the worst of the common options is factory-farmed chicken, on account of the intensity and duration of the suffering inflicted on each bird. (7) Finally, I think the most important animal-welfare problem is not farmed animals at all but wild animal suffering, simply because the number of animals living in the wild dwarfs the number we raise.

I would like you to audit this set: which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and, for the genuine ones, how severe each is and what the cheapest repair would be. I am not asking you to tell me which view is true, but to trace what commits me to what and where the joint pressure actually lies. Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and be explicit whenever a proposed fix would force me to give up something I plainly want to keep. I am especially interested in whether the package is even internally consistent about what it is trying to minimize, or whether it quietly switches between counting deaths and counting suffering. Please state how confident you are in each of your main claims, and single out the one judgment you are least sure of.

Then, on the final line, give me the single commitment whose abandonment would resolve the most tension at the lowest cost, in the form \answer{...} containing a short label for that commitment.
