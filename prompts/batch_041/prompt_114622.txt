Here's what it actually says. Then there's bonding leave: ten weeks job-protected for any new parent, by birth or adoption or surrogacy, but paid at 60%. And here's the part that blew up our last meeting — if both parents work for the system, the ten weeks is one pot they have to divide between them, it isn't ten weeks each. There's also a primary caregiver tag that unlocks four more paid weeks, but only one parent can hold it, and you just declare it yourself on an honor system, nobody checks. Adoptive and intended parents get bonding but not recovery, obviously. And the per diem and part-time staff, who are mostly women and mostly working the overnight shifts, don't get any of it.

What I want to know is what this thing believes about families, because it feels like it's pulling in two directions. The medical part is paid in full and the relationship part is paid at 60% and has to be shared, which makes it look like the policy thinks recovering from childbirth is a real need and raising a baby is a nice-to-have you divvy up with your spouse like a timeshare. But then why the primary caregiver bonus, which only makes sense if the place thinks those early months really matter?
