Every time my neighborhood fights about a new apartment building, somebody says 'housing is a human right' like that ends the argument — the people against the building use it to mean we can't let a developer put up anything that isn't affordable, and the people for it use it to mean we can't keep saying no to everything. Both sides can't be getting the same sentence right. Can you lay out the different precise things that one line could actually be claiming — a legal right versus a moral one, a right to a roof over your head versus a right to a spot in some particular neighborhood, and who exactly is supposed to be on the hook for providing it — and then say which of those readings are just nice-sounding, which are real but arguable, which are actually false, and which one each side is probably leaning on when they say it? I want a menu I can bring to the next meeting, not a lecture. But dont just hand me the menu and walk away — tell me which reading you'd actually defend as the right one, and why, even if thats picking a fight.
