In a discussion of the kalam cosmological argument, a friend kept invoking the principle that nothing comes from nothing, as though it were at once obviously true and by itself sufficient to establish that the universe must have had a cause. I suspect the phrase is doing several different jobs at once, and that at least one of those jobs is in tension with the very conclusion it was being made to support. Please treat "nothing comes from nothing" as a slogan and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making: from the near-tautological, through the various causal principles it might stand for (about events, about things, about the coming-to-be of being as such), to the strong metaphysical thesis that no being whatsoever can originate from nothing even given an omnipotent agent. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and say whether it would actually support the cosmological arguer's conclusion that the universe had a cause. Give particular attention to the reading on which the slogan is a blanket prohibition on creation from nothing, and ask what follows for the argument if that is the intended sense. Lay the readings out as a menu rather than settling on one, and say which of them the people who repeat the slogan in cosmological debates most plausibly mean. Throughout, state how confident you are in each verdict and identify the single point about which you are least sure.
