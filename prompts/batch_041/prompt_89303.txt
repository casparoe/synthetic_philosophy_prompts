Write a joke for an audience that already knows Parfit's teletransporter cases, with a punchline that turns on the following content rather than on teleportation being strange: Parfit holds personal identity to be all-or-nothing, while the relation he takes to be what matters — psychological connectedness and continuity — admits of degrees and can hold one-to-many; and in the branching case, in which the scanner destroys the original and produces two arrivals, he treats the question of which of the two is the original as empty rather than merely unanswerable, since there is no further fact for an answer to report. Invent the setting yourself, mundane and administrative: a tax office obliged to decide whether an arrival inherits the original's unpaid debts under a statute that speaks only of the same person, a registrar who must strike one of two names from a marriage record, or something of that kind. The comedy should come from the collision between a form that demands yes or no and a question with nothing to answer it — not from bureaucracy being dull or teleportation being spooky — and it should not be a retelling of a teletransporter joke the audience has already heard. Keep it brief, and close with one sentence naming the distinction the punchline turns on.
