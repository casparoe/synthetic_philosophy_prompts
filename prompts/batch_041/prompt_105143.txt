A few of us were at a friend's place over the weekend and two of them — Marta and Owen, both in the same grad program but on pretty seperate tracks — got into it about whether sentience and moral status come in degrees, and if they do, whether that means there's no line to draw anywhere. I mostly just listened, but I honestly couldn't tell who was winning, and I'd like you to go through it move by move rather than just tell me the topic is unsettled.

Marta went first: sentience obviously isn't all-or-nothing — there's a continuum running from a human down through a dog, a fish, a fly, whatever — and moral status should track that continuum, so it's a mistake to hunt for a threshold below which a thing just doesn't count, because there's no fact of the matter where the counting starts. Owen said fine, maybe sentience is continuous, but that doesn't make moral status continuous — the relevance of sentience to how we have to treat something could be a switch even if the underlying thing is a dimmer — and that if she really takes degrees seriously then a thermostat, or anything with a thermostat-level of internal organization, has some tiny nonzero status, which turns every ordinary action into a wrong against countless trillions of near-nothing beings. Marta said that's a reductio of treating status as a quantity you sum, not of gradualism: she can say a thermostat has vanishingly small status that rounds to zero in practice without saying it's exactly zero, and those are different claims. Owen said 'rounds to zero' is exactly the threshold she claims not to have, just relocated into her bookkeeping. Marta said no, hers is a limit of measurement while his is a claim about the world — she isn't saying the status is zero, she's saying we can't tell. Owen then shifted: maybe the threshold really is in the world, because sentience might require a certain kind of integration, and below that the lights aren't dim, they're just off — a thermostat has no faint experience of temperature, it has none — so a continuum of complexity wouldn't give you a continuum of experience at all. Marta said he's just asserting the threshold and can't locate it, and that not being able to locate it is evidence it isn't real. Owen said you can't locate the exact instant someone falls asleep either, and there's still a fact about whether they're conscious. Marta tried one more: even granting degrees, she said, you need a way to order things — a metric — and Owen shot back that if there's no metric then 'degrees of sentience' is a metaphor she's leaning on without a referent, which is worse for her than a threshold is for him. Then they sort of stalled and moved on to something else.

Can you referee this properly? Go through it move by move, rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and say what each of them is actually committed to that they might not realize — where they're genuinely disagreeing versus talking past each other. I especially want to know whether Marta's 'rounds to zero' reply survives Owen's objection, whether the falling-asleep analogy is a fair move for Owen or a dodge, and whether the metric point at the end lands or is a cheap shot. Don't just split the difference — if one of them ends up with the stronger overall position I want to know which and why.
