I have been having a long argument with a friend about moral particularism, and I want to work through a case with you. She thinks the villagers of Le Chambon-sur-Lignon -- the Protestant village in France that sheltered thousands of Jews during the Holocaust -- are a perfect illustration of particularism. Her story is that they did not consult a rule or a principle; they simply responded to the particular needs of the people in front of them. I think that is too quick. I want you to analyze the episode through the lens of the particularism/generalism debate, but I am not interested in the empirical history for its own sake. I am interested in the moral anatomy. What kind of reasons did the villagers act from, as far as we can tell? Were they applying a principle like hospitality or the sanctity of life, or were their reasons genuinely particular? Does the fact that they were Huguenots, a persecuted minority with their own history of refuge, matter to the analysis, and if so, does it cut for particularism or for generalism? And what does the episode show about the debate itself: does it support the particularist claim that moral judgment does not need principles, or does it reveal that the generalist can always reconstruct a principle after the fact?

I am especially interested in a distinction that my friend and I kept circling: the difference between acting from a principle and being able to formulate a principle that codifies what one did. I want you to say whether the debate turns on that distinction, or whether particularism requires something stronger -- that the agent's reasons are themselves particular and not subsumable under any principle at all. Then tell me what Le Chambon reveals about the limits of the debate: does it show that the two sides are talking past each other, or does it give one side a genuine advantage? I want the analysis to run in both directions, with the debate illuminating the villagers and the villagers testing the debate, and I want you to be explicit about where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak. A disciplined case analysis, not a survey of positions.
