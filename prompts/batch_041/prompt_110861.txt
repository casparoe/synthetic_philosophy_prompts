I run a reading group at the local rationalist meetup - mostly people who've mainlined the Sequences, a couple of engineers, one actual statistician, and my flatmate who comes for the biscuits. Next month I want to do a session on the principle of indifference and Bertrand's paradox. I dont want you to explain it to me, I want a plan for how to run the explanation for this crowd, about an hour, whiteboard, no slides.

First: what has to be in place before the paradox can even land? My hunch is the whole thing hinges on a move the audience won't see coming - that a probability only exists once you've fixed a sample space, and that "picking the sample space" is doing real work rather than reading something off the world. If that's the load-bearing idea, what's the order the pieces have to go in, and why does each depend on the last?

Second: the carrying example. I keep going back and forth between the chord-in-a-circle thing and the cube-factory setup, where a factory makes cubes up to some size and "pick one at random" can mean uniform in edge length or uniform in volume, and you get two different answers. Which one actually carries the session, and what does it need to have and lack so it doesn't just collapse into the other? If I use one, what do I do with the other - save it, or drop it?

Third: misconceptions, and where to head them off. I'm expecting at least (a) "so this shows Bayesian priors are just made up and it's all subjective," (b) "the fix is a maximum-entropy prior" from the half of the room that's read too much Jaynes, (c) "Bertrand's paradox means probability theory is inconsistent." For each, where in the sequence does it come up, and what do I say? And be straight with me on (b): is the invariance / well-posed-problem rescue actually a fix, or just relocating the choice of sample space one level up? I don't want to bluff when someone asks.

Fourth: what do I leave out. What can I honestly say "we're not doing that today" about at this level, and say it out loud so nobody feels cheated?

Last, one question to ask at the end that would actually tell me whether they got the dependency - that the sample space is a choice, not a discovery - rather than just nodding along.
