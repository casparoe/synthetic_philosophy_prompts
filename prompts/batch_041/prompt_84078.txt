I am a high school senior and the student representative on our honor council. Next week the council votes on a proposed sanction policy for academic dishonesty, and I have to give the opening remarks to a room that will include students, teachers, and a few parents. The policy's preamble says the goal is for the student to own the self that produced the act; the operative clause requires a character remediation plan and a public affirmation that the student has chosen to become a person of integrity. I want to use Galen Strawson's Basic Argument to show why that demand is incoherent: you do what you do because of the way you are, and you cannot be ultimately responsible for the way you are without a self-creating act that would itself have to issue from how you already are, so the regress never bottoms out in a self-made self. But I do not want the speech to become an excuse for cheating or to leave the room thinking that no one is ever accountable. I want to distinguish Strawson's denial of ultimate responsibility from the ordinary accountability the council can still enforce, and I want to close by proposing that we replace the character-remediation clause with a repair-and-restitution clause. Please write the speech, about five minutes long, in continuous prose, the way I would actually deliver it, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. No citations or reading list.
