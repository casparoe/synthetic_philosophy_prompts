So I'm a novelist, which means I spend my days making things up, and now I've got something in my actual life I cant think straight about, and I'd rather have a real argument than hear that it's complicated.

There's an ant colony living in the wall of my study. They come out through a crack behind the baseboard. Since April I've been putting a dab of honey on a saucer in the same spot every evening, and they show up within a minute or two. My partner calls it my pet, and I laughed, but I don't know why I laughed. (I'm also midway through a novel about a hive of something or other, so I don't trust my own eye here.)

The problem: my landlord wants to fumigate the building next month and I keep stalling. I've started feeling like it would be killing something, not like clearing out a nuisance. My brother in law teaches biology and says I'm being silly: it isn't one animal, it's twenty thousand ants, each with whatever an ant has instead of a brain, and wiping them out is more like weeding than like killing a pet. Except that's the part that bugs me, because the colony really does act like one thing. When I taped over the entrance on the far side of the wall, the whole traffic pattern rerouted in about a day and a half. It decides things. It has something like a mood — hot afternoons and they're frantic, cold mornings and almost nobody's out. When a forager dies on the saucer the others carry the body off, and I can't help reading that as something. So I can't tell which is the illusion: that there's a creature in my wall, or that there's just a lot of bugs.

What I want from you: is there a fact of the matter here, or am I only choosing how to talk? If there's a fact, what kind of evidence would bear on it, and how would anyone get that evidence when the whole thing is sealed up inside a wall? Second, suppose you convinced me the colony is one mind — does that change what I owe it, compared with twenty thousand tiny minds? Does the suffering add up the same either way, or is one big mind worse than a heap of small ones, or somehow less bad? Third, does it matter that I've been feeding them since April and they now depend on a saucer I control? If that adds to what I owe them, is it a real obligation or just sentimentality about my own habit?

Take a position. Tell me what you'd do about the fumigation and why, and tell me what would change your mind. Don't hand me a reading list, I won't read it.
