Friend A (poker) says: for a repeated bet with a positive edge, bet the fraction of your bankroll that maximizes the expected log of your wealth. That's the growth-optimal fraction, and he thinks it isn't just a decent heuristic but the rational thing to do with money, because over a long sequence of bets that strategy beats any other essentially different strategy almost surely — so betting more is taking on ruin risk for nothing, and betting less is throwing away growth. Friend B (econ) says sure, all true, but maximizing expected log wealth is just maximizing expected utility with one particular utility function — log, i.e. risk aversion of one — and you can't get 'rational' out of that unless you've already decided that long-run growth is the thing to want. On his view an expected-utility maximizer with a different utility function isn't making a mistake when she bets a different fraction, and he thinks A's almost-sure-dominance argument quietly needs the number of bets to go to infinity, which it doesn't, because we die. My own suspicion, which I'd like you to test rather than just accept: that they're answering different questions under one heading — A is asking which strategy grows fastest along a single path that runs forever, B is asking what a person with given preferences should do in one decision now — so alot of the heat is about which question is 'the' question, not about the answer to either one.
