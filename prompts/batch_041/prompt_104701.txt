I'm assembling a handout for a graduate seminar that straddles the philosophy of action and behavioural economics, and I want a decision tree that sorts the positions one can hold on the relation between weakness of will and time-inconsistent (hyperbolic) preference. Root it at the commitments every participant will grant — that people sometimes act contrary to what they judge best, and that people sometimes reverse their preferences over time — and make every branch node a question answerable without knowing which position it leads to. Build the tree from the logical space first (the questions and the combinations of answers they permit) and only then locate positions in it, rather than listing schools and attaching questions to them.

The tree must place all of the following as leaves: the Socratic denial that strict akrasia is possible; the Aristotelian diagnosis that the akratic possesses the universal but fails to apply the particular; the Davidsonian requirement that akrasia involve an all-things-considered judgement at the time of action; the hierarchical account of the will built on the unwilling addict; the multiple-selves or intrapersonal-bargaining account; the view that the phenomenon is nothing but time-inconsistent preference, with the sophisticated, naive and partially naive variants kept apart; the view that precommitment cases and clear-eyed cases are different phenomena, only one of which is weakness of will; and the deflationary view that there is no single phenomenon to explain.

Three cases must do work at the diagnostic nodes: (i) an agent who judges at 9pm that she should run at 6am, sets an alarm, and at 6am judges she would rather stay in bed and switches it off; (ii) an agent who, at the moment of acting and with no change of judgement, eats the cake she judges all things considered that she should not; (iii) an agent who continues to dwell on a grievance she judges she should let go, the act in question being mental rather than bodily. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and any combination of answers that nobody defends, with a one-line diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Please give me, in this order:
1. The tree — labelled branches and leaves, each leaf carrying the position's name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. (About 600 words.)
2. The single branch node that does the most work, and why. (About 150 words.)
3. The leaves the tree cannot separate, and the empty cells with their diagnoses. (About 200 words.)
