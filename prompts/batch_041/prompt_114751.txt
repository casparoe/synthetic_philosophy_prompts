I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, writing a paper on the implicit philosophy of rationality in contemporary machine learning systems. I want to analyze a concrete design pattern that has become common in large-scale recommendation and search systems, and I would like you to reconstruct the philosophical commitments it presupposes.

The pattern is a two-stage pipeline. First, a candidate generator uses an approximate nearest-neighbor index to retrieve a fixed number k of items from a corpus of millions, optimizing for the probability that the relevant items are somewhere in that set. Second, a more expensive ranker scores only those k items and orders them by a weighted objective that combines predicted clicks, dwell time, and diversity. The system also has a fallback rule: if the ranker’s top score falls below a threshold, it serves a default item rather than the top-ranked one. The retrieval stage is tuned to a target recall at k, not to the final objective; the ranker is tuned to the final objective; the fallback threshold is set by a product manager to limit embarrassing failures, not by any formal optimization.

What conception of rationality does this architecture presuppose? Does it presuppose that rationality is satisficing at the retrieval stage and optimizing at the ranking stage, and if so, is that division principled or merely a concession to computational cost? Which parts of the practice strictly require a satisficing view, and which parts are compatible with a fully optimizing view? Is the fallback threshold a satisficing criterion, a deontological constraint, or something else? What would a consistently optimizing system look like, and what would a consistently satisficing system look like? What would change if we treated the thresholds and the choice of k as themselves objects of optimization, that is, if we moved to a meta-optimizing architecture? Finally, does the practice presuppose a descriptive or a normative reading of bounded rationality: is the boundedness of the system a fact about its resources that we regret, or is it an ideal that the system approximates? I am not asking for citations or a literature review; I will supply those. I want the conceptual reconstruction, the distinctions between what is presupposed and what is merely convenient, and an assessment of where the practice is underdetermined between rival theories of rationality.
