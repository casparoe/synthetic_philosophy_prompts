The second is my control case: a dimmer whose brightness setting takes the values 1/2 + 1/2, 1/2 + 1/3, 1/2 + 1/4, and so on at successive steps before noon, each step a finite turn of the dial and the dial moving continuously in between, so that the setting converges to exactly half brightness — a value the dial never occupies before noon. Because the dial's position is a continuous function of time on the interval up to and including noon, its value at noon looks forced.

What I want is (a) your credence that the dimmer has the determinate setting of half brightness at noon, and (b) your credence that the standard lamp has no determinate state at noon, each decomposed into the sub-questions it depends on, with a number, one line of justification per term, and how the terms combine. I would expect those terms to include: whether completed supertasks are metaphysically possible at all; whether continuity of a physical quantity in time is enough to fix its value at the limit; whether the lamp's indeterminacy comes from the discontinuity of its state space rather than from the completion of an infinite sequence; whether the case is settled by physics or is a verbal matter about which descriptions happen to pick out a fact; and whatever else you think belongs. Then tell me which term the two numbers are most sensitive to, what would move each of them most, and whether the contrast between the cases really isolates the source of the indeterminacy — or whether I am assuming what I want to prove by treating the dimmer as the better-behaved case.
