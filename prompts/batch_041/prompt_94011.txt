I am a survey methodologist by training: I design panel studies for a living and I think habitually in terms of target populations and study populations, sampling frames, coverage, unit and item non-response, weighting and post-stratification, and the total survey error way of splitting error into sampling error and its non-sampling cousins — coverage, non-response, measurement, processing — together with the rougher distinction between descriptive inference, where the goal is a quantity defined on a well-defined finite population, and analytic inference, where the goal is a relationship that is supposed to travel beyond the sample. Last night I spent three hours arguing with a close friend, a political philosopher, about the franchise: whether voting ought to be compulsory, whether the voting age should come down, and whether resident non-citizens should have a vote. We talked past each other almost continuously. His parting shot was that I was treating the demos as though it were a sample and the election as though it were an estimate, and that this was a category error rather than a technical disagreement. I suspect he was partly right, but also that the translation does more work than he is willing to allow.

Your task is to act as a translator between the two vocabularies. Take the positions my friend and I were defending — the case for compulsory voting as a remedy for unequal participation, the case for a lower voting age, and the case for extending the franchise to non-citizens, together with the standard objections to each — and restate them as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary of survey design and measurement. Be scrupulous about not importing my framework's assumptions and then presenting the result as a translation: where a political claim is about authority, standing or legitimacy rather than about accuracy, say so, rather than silently redescribing it as a bias to be corrected. Then give me the residue report, and think carefully about the difference between a gap in my vocabulary and a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked — that difference is the whole point of the exercise. You may plan privately, but the answer itself should contain only the list below.

Give me your answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion, ordered as follows: (1) the core translation, with the franchise question recast as a question of sampling design in my terms; (2) what happens to compulsory voting when it is recast as the elimination of non-response rather than its modelling; (3) what happens to a lower voting age when it is recast as an extension of the frame and of coverage; (4) what happens to votes for non-citizens when it is recast as a redefinition of the target population, and therefore of the estimand; and (5) the residue — what does not survive the translation at all, and whether that points to a gap in my vocabulary or to something in the political argument that was never a claim about measurement in the first place.
