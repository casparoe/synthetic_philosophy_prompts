I am taking a graduate seminar on evolutionary theory, and we have spent the last several weeks with Gould and Lewontin's critique of adaptationism — the argument that a great many features we are tempted to call adaptations are better understood as byproducts forced by other constraints, in the way that the triangular spaces beneath a dome are said to be forced by the decision to mount the dome on arches rather than chosen for their own sake. Our seminar has been quarrelling about how much this picture can actually establish, and I would like you to audit the analogy itself rather than simply rehearse the surrounding debate. One of my classmates has been pressing what she takes to be a fatal objection. She says the image is chosen badly, because the spaces in that Venetian church are not free leftover gaps that merely happened to get decorated — they are part of how the dome is held up, and the mosaics set into them were placed with as much deliberation as anything else in the building. On her telling, the analogy therefore cuts against the very conclusion it was introduced to support: it shows not that apparent design can be an accident, but that a byproduct of structure can be taken up and used for a purpose, which is precisely what an adaptationist would have predicted. I honestly cannot tell how much of her objection is architecture and how much is logic, which is part of why I am asking.

What I want from you is an adjudication, and I care less about who wins than about what the comparison can and cannot carry. Please lay out the correspondence explicitly — a table is welcome — saying what in the church plays the role of the constraint, of the byproduct, of the decoration, and of the designer, and then tell me where the mapping breaks. I am especially interested in which features of the architectural source are quietly imported into the biological target and whether they survive the trip: an architect works with foresight toward a finished building, a dome is raised once and not rebuilt, and the decoration is a further deliberate act laid on top of the structure, whereas the evolutionary process has, on the standard view, none of these features. Having done that, tell me which of the disanalogies genuinely matter for the use to which the analogy is being put and which are idle, and whether the anti-adaptationist conclusion — that we cannot read current utility off a current trait and infer that it was selected for — survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If you think a different image would do the same work more honestly, propose one and say what it buys and what it costs.
