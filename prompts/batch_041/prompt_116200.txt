A friend ran a silly contest on his blog a while back and its been nagging me ever since. You could submit as many entries as you liked, the pot was a fixed amount, and at the end one entry got drawn and whoever sent it took the pot divided by the total number of entries. So the more entries people send, the less the prize is worth, and I can't fix that by holding back. What got me is that the usual advice — work out what everyone else will probably do, then pick your best reply to it — tells me to send about as many entries as everybody else put together, which is exactly the move that wrecks it for the lot of us. But if I ask a different question, "what should I send if everyone in my position sends the same thing?", the answer is plainly one, and that outcome is better for every single person than the one we actually got. I keep bumping into the same shape elsewhere: two people who know each other well, stuck in a one-shot version of "if we both keep quiet we both come out ahead", where the textbook says sell the other one out and the "we'd both reason the same way" answer says don't. And the friend I argue about this with says the second question is just wishful thinking wearing a symmetry costume.

So: is there a name for this — for the reasoning rule, or for the family of situations where it bites? I dont just want the label though, I want the edges. How is it different from ordinary cooperation that's propped up by the threat of a next time, from a coordination problem with two equally good answers, and from just doing my bit for the group? And when is the "everyone in my position does the same thing" premise actually allowed — does it need the others to be in genuinely identical situations, same information and same preferences as me, and what happens when they're only nearly identical, or when I suspect they'll reason differently than I do? Also tell me which parts of this are settled and which are live disagreements among people who study it, rather than handing me one camp's line as if it were the consensus.
