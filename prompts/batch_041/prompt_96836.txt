Bit of a rambling one, sorry in advance. I've been reading up on memes and the Darwinian view of culture — the idea that beliefs and catchphrases and rituals stick around not because they're true or useful to the people holding them but because they're good at getting themselves copied and passed on — and I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a word for.

The thing I mean: there are phrases that take over a group completely and last for years, and the striking part is that nobody can actually pin them down. Like the way "synergy" colonised every meeting I've sat in, or a political chant, or one of those soft-focus lines that go around social media, something like "everything happens for a reason." Everyone repeats them constantly. But if you ask someone what it actually means, they give you a loose gloss, and if you press — do you mean literally that, because it seems plainly false — they back off, no, not exactly that, its more the spirit of it, and then carry on using it the exact same way. And here's the bit that nags at me: when someone in the room tries to nail down a sharp definition, the phrase basically stops spreading among the people who heard the definition, even though everyone's nodding. The vagueness doesn't look like a defect that gets sanded off over time. It looks like the thing that lets it travel — it can mean something slightly different to every person who hands it on, so no one ever has to commit to a claim that could actually be knocked down.

I know "memes" and "selfish memes" are the obvious ports of call, and there's all that cultural-evolution stuff about catchy or emotionally gripping ideas spreading, and about good replicators needing high fidelity. But those seem to be about things spreading by being memorable or useful or hard to forget, whereas this seems to spread by being un-pinnable, which feels like the opposite of fidelity. So: is there an established name for this specific pattern — a cultural item whose transmission advantage is precisely its indeterminacy? And more than a name, I want the boundaries drawn: how is it different from a vague political slogan that's deliberately vague so it can hold a coalition together, or a cliché that just shuts down argument, or the ordinary drift and distortion any idea undergoes as it's passed person to person? Give me the carving, not just the label — what exactly the thing is, what it isn't, and where the edges are. (One case I think is NOT it, to save you the trouble: something like "drink eight glasses of water a day" spreads great and is basically false, but it's perfectly determinate, so it feels like a different animal.)
