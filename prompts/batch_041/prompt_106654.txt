Right, so I'm putting together a long-form interview for the ideas desk — proper sit-down, hour and a half, with a bloke who's just brought out a book arguing that culture is a Darwinian process all the way down: variation, selection, inheritance, the full apparatus, not just a loose metaphor. I'm sympathetic but I don't want to hand him the TED-talk version of his own argument. Can you draft me nine or ten questions, in an order I could actually follow live, and give each one a follow-up aimed at the answer he's most likely to give? I want the follow-up to be the bit that makes him squirm, not a soft "could you say more".

Things I want to get at, roughly — you'll know better than me how to sequence them:

- The replication problem. Selection needs copying with decent fidelity, and the standard objection is that cultural transmission is reconstructive, not replicative — we don't copy the tune, we rebuild it, and the rebuilding keeps getting pulled back towards the same handful of attractors. If that's right, has he got anything left for selection to do, or is this really an epidemiology of representations rather than a genealogy of copies? Give me his best defence and then the follow-up that prises it open.

- Blending. Genes are particulate, which is what stopped the whole thing collapsing; culture looks much more like paint — mix two ideas and you get a blend, and if variance halves every generation you can't accumulate anything. Ask whether cultural inheritance is particulate enough to carry a Darwinian story, and what happens when descent is mostly horizontal and the family tree is a thicket rather than a tree.

- The grain problem. How many memes in a symphony, or a poem, or the concept of Tuesday? If there's no principled way to count them, is "meme" doing any explanatory work, or is it a bookkeeping device we could do without?

- The meme's-eye view and its ugly consequences. If memes are selected for their own spread and not for our benefit, we should expect cultural items that harm their hosts — celibacy, suicide cults, chain letters, the stuff that spreads precisely because it's sticky. Does he bite that bullet, and how does he square it with the intuition that most culture is useful to us?

- Cumulative culture. The thing that actually looks special about human culture is the ratchet — improvements that don't slip back, which seems to need high-fidelity imitation. Is the ratchet the real explanandum, and does his Darwinian machinery explain it or just redescribe it?

- The demarcation question. There's a live debate about what makes something a Darwinian population at all rather than a marginal case — how much heredity you need, how continuous it has to be, whether the population has to be a population in any interesting sense. Where does culture sit: paradigm case, marginal case, or not a case at all?

- The unfalsifiability worry. Memetics has a reputation for explaining everything after the fact. Ask him, flatly, what observation would count against his view — and then don't let him answer with "well, it's early days".

- Whether memetics is a rival to gene–culture coevolution or just a fancy way of describing the same data. If it's the latter, what's the extra content?

Don't give me a reading list or citations — I can't use them on air and he'll just wave them away. Keep the jargon to a minimum, and where you can't, gloss it in a clause. British English throughout, please — I'm filing for a UK outlet and the subs will have my guts for spelling it the American way.
