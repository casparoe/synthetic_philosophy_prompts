Two mates of mine work at the same hospital and they had a proper row in the pub last week about a change the trust has made to its ICU software. I was there and honestly couldn't tell who was in the right.

The change is this. Up to this year the software defaulted to 'comfort care only' for any patient whose notes didn't say otherwise, and a doctor had to actively order escalation to keep someone on life support. Now it's the reverse: the machine keeps everyone on life support unless a doctor actively ticks a box for comfort care. Managment say the new default saves lives, because under the old one people died when nobody got round to ordering escalation in time.

Ravi thinks the whole fuss is about nothing. A doctor who doesn't tick the box has still decided something, he says -- she's decided the patient stays on the ventilator, and that decision is exactly what it always was. Whether you reach it by writing an order or by not writing one is like the difference between saying yes and just never saying no. And if you insist there's a real difference, he says, you end up with a doctor who never got round to it being innocent while a doctor who switched a ventilator off is a killer -- same outcome, same reason, so same responsibility, and any view that says otherwise has gone wrong somewhere.

Priya says thats backwards and the new default makes the doing/allowing line matter more, not less. Under the old system, she says, letting someone die was just the background -- nobody was doing anything, the illness was doing the killing. Under the new one the hospital has arranged for a machine to keep the patient alive, so a family who wanted comfort care is now up against the institution rather than the disease. That means the person who set the default is the one doing something, and the doctor who fails to tick the box might never have looked at the patient at all; you can't hold someone responsible for what a system they didn't build does on their shift.

Ravi came back with: fine, then the act has just moved one step up the chain. Whoever chose the default has done something, but it's the same something that doctors did under the old system when they ordered escalation, so nothing has changed except who signs the form. Priya said no, what's changed is what 'nothing was done' means -- the line that used to separate killing from letting die now separates 'somebody chose this for you' from 'this just happened to you', and that's a line worth having even if the labels have moved. Ravi said that if choosing a default counts as an act then every software setting is an ethical act and you could never build anything. Priya said that's a reductio of his position, not hers, since she's perfectly happy to say that software design is an ethical act.

They've asked me to referee and I can't. Can you go through it move by move -- rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say what each of them is missing, and then if you have to call it, a line on who's closer to right overall. Please write your answer in British English.
