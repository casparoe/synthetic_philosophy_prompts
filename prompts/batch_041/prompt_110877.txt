I'm a novelist who teaches a graduate seminar on desire, narrative, and the good life. For the final exam, I want one essay question on tranquilism — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters — that cannot be answered by reciting a summary of Epicurus or Buddhist accounts of craving. The question should force students to decide whether tranquilism can assign any non-instrumental value to love, curiosity, or the completion of a long project, and whether the view is self-undermining if the desire to be free of craving is itself a craving. Write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, each worth four marks, with a one-line note on the mistake that typically costs students each point. The model answer should argue from the view itself rather than cite sources.
