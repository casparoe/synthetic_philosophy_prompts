You are a philosopher working on animal welfare, moral status, and the ethics of food production. I need you to reconstruct the implicit theory behind a pattern of real-world practice — not to defend a position of your own. Please think carefully before you write: begin by brainstorming the candidate theories that could lie behind the pattern I describe, then choose the one that fits best and argue for it against the others.

Here is the pattern, from finfish-and-shrimp aquaculture. On the finfish side, welfare codes and company standards are detailed: fish must be stunned or otherwise rendered insensible before slaughter, and stocking density, water quality, handling, and transport are regulated. On the crustacean side, the same facilities, often under the same corporate "responsibly sourced" banner, are largely silent. Shrimp and crabs are, in most places, killed by chilling or by being boiled alive; there are no stunning requirements; stocking densities are not capped; and in many hatcheries broodstock females undergo eyestalk ablation — the eyestalks are cut or cauterized to induce spawning — routinely and without anaesthetic. At the same time, the industry is beginning to advertise crustacean welfare, and some jurisdictions have recently extended legal sentience recognition to decapods. So the pattern is not simply "fish count, crustaceans don't": the line is drawn and redrawn, and the crustacean side is now being nudged.

Reconstruct the theory this pattern presupposes. What view of moral status, of welfare, and of the sentience threshold does it commit to — that crustaceans lack the relevant capacity, that their pains matter less, that we cannot yet tell, that sentience is a matter of degree and they fall below a cutoff, or that tractability and cost, not principle, govern? Which rival theories does the pattern quietly rule out, and which would license it just as well? Be careful to separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably, and to say where it is philosophically underdetermined.

Then answer the forward-looking question: if the presupposed view were replaced by the best-supported rival, what specifically would have to change — in the codes, in the slaughter and hatchery practices, in what counts as a welfare-relevant variable — and what would stay the same? Close with a candid note on which parts of the disagreement turn on empirical questions about crustacean nociception and pain that philosophy cannot settle, and how the theory should be held in light of that uncertainty.

Write no more than 1000 words. Lead with a single-sentence statement of the implicit theory, then the reconstruction, then the presupposition/fit distinction, then the underdetermination, then the replacement scenario, then the empirical caveat. Do not give me citations, a bibliography, or a reading list — refer to positions by name or description and I will add references myself.
