I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar in normative ethics and I am drafting the final exam. I want one essay question on the utility monster that cannot be answered by reciting the standard setup and the standard replies. To that end, build the question around a strengthened variant: the monster's advantage lies not in greater intensity of pleasure but in a convex conversion of resources into utility, so that each additional unit yields a larger increment than the last and no threshold of diminishing returns ever arrives. The question should force students to say which commitment of utilitarianism the objection actually exploits, whether the objection can be run without importing a premise the utilitarian is not committed to, and what distinguishes an objection that is a genuine reductio from one that merely pumps an intuition. Give the students sixty minutes and one essay, and instruct them not to spend more than a short paragraph restating the scenario.

Please write (a) the essay question itself, (b) a model answer of about 700 words, and (c) a marking scheme totaling 20 marks, built around five must-hit moves, with the marks for each and a one-line note on the characteristic error that costs a candidate that move. A candidate should not be able to earn full marks merely by naming the standard examples and rehearsing the usual replies.
