Here is a paraphrase of the passage, since I do not want to rely on a remembered wording: Reid suggests that the Author of nature has implanted in us a disposition to believe human testimony, that this disposition is initially unlimited, and that it is only through experience of deceit that we learn to distrust. He contrasts this with the view that our trust in testimony is founded on induction from observed correlations between testimony and truth. He seems to think that the anti-reductionist position is the correct one: our reliance on testimony is not derived from other sources of knowledge but is itself a basic principle of our nature.

I am unsure about several things. First, is Reid claiming that the principle of credulity is a source of justified belief, or only a psychological tendency that explains why we believe? Second, if it is a source of justification, how can it be defeasible? Third, how does the principle of credulity relate to the principle of veracity? Does Reid think that the two principles together explain the normativity of testimony, or does he think that the principle of credulity is sufficient? Could you help me understand what Reid is actually committed to here, and how an anti-reductionist reading of this passage might be defended against the objection that it encourages gullibility?
