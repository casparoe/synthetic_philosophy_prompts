ok so im a junior and im the student rep on our school's assembly committee, which sounds way more official than it is — its me, two teachers, and the vice principal, and we plan the assemblies and approve what goes in them.

heres my problem. the last assembly of the year always has a student comedy bit. dev (sophmore, wrote the whole thing himself) wrote a roast of the new phone-pouch rule that also does a whole bit about Mr. R by name. its honestly funny and dev is not wrong about the rule. but the vice principal already told us that whoever performs it gets a saturday detention, and dev is on a conduct agreement from last fall, so one more thing and he gets sent to the alt school. maya volunteered to do it instead — shes a senior, already got into her college, editor of the paper, and Mr. R actually likes her.

so the committee has to pick one of exactly three things by friday, because there's only one slot: no bit at all (we fill the slot with something else), maya performs it, or dev performs it.

and the two of them make a loop out of it:
- maya would rather there be no bit at all, but if its going to happen she'd way rather do it herself than let dev get thrown out of school over a joke.
- dev wants the bit to happen. he'd rather maya do it than him (hes not an idiot, he knows about the conduct agreement) but he'd rather do it himself than have nobody do it.
- so both of them think maya doing it is better than dev doing it.

meanwhile our committee bylaws say two things that each seem fine on their own:
1. whether a student takes part in an assembly bit is that students own call — we don't put anyone in a bit they don't want, and we don't keep anyone out of one they want.
2. when everyone agrees that one option is better than another, we go with the better one.

rule 1 plus maya's own judgement gives me: no bit beats maya doing it. rule 1 plus dev's own judgement gives me: dev doing it beats no bit. rule 2 gives me: maya doing it beats dev doing it. so maya beats dev beats no bit beats maya, which is a loop, and we can't actually pick anything at all. my brother, who is an econ major, says this is literally sen's liberal paradox and that its definately a known unsolvable thing.

but we have to walk into that room friday and pick something, so please dont just tell me its unsolvable. can you write the memo im going to pass around before fridays meeting? it should say what we should actually do about the assembly — one of the three, or something we havent thought of, as long as we can actually pull it off by friday — plus the reasons for it, and if theres anything we need to do to make it happen (who talks to who, by when) put that in too. i really want it to take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously instead of steamrolling it: whichever way we go someones going to be furious, and i'd rather the memo say why theyre wrong than pretend they arent. and tack a couple of sentences on at the end about what we should change in the bylaws so this doesnt happen again next year, since we can amend them in the fall.

about a page please, no sources or citations or anything, and dont make it sound like a legal brief — the vice principal is going to read it and i dont want him thinking im showing off.
