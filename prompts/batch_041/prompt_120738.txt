So I have two friends who have been arguing about punishment for about two years now and I want to force them to produce one document together instead of another round of shouting. Friend A thinks desert is a side-constraint: a sentence has to fall inside a range fixed by what the offender actually deserves, and only inside that range do deterrence, incapacitation, and rehab get to pick. Friend B is a rule consequentialist who thinks proportionality is real and genuinely binding — binding because of what adopting it does to a legal system over time (predictability, perceived legitimacy, stopping officials from cooking the numbers) rather than because desert has any independent authority. The thing is they agree on maybe 95% of actual sentences, which is exactly why their fight is interesting and why reciting Kant vs. Bentham at them is useless. Neither is a strawman, neither is dumb.

Write the joint statement they could both put their names to.

I want:
- a numbered list of claims they both sign, including the ones that would look strange to outsiders (do both of them reject 'the sentence that maximizes compliance' as a decision procedure? do both take punishing someone we know to be factually innocent to calm a riot off the table? if so, say whether it comes off for different reasons, and whether that difference does any work)
- the precise points where they still part, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording as fair
- the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move one of them
- for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence they both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two cases I want them to chew on, though invent better ones if you can: (a) the tech case — electronic monitoring plus curfew is now available, and on the best evidence we have it deters just as well and produces less reoffending than prison, but it is a smaller deprivation than the offender deserves; (b) the publicity case — a sentence well below what is deserved ends up deterring more, because of how the story gets told. You can stipulate the empirical facts, just flag when you're stipulating rather than claiming.

Also make them separate out what exactly desert attaches to — the condemnatory message, the amount of hard treatment, the loss of liberty/time — because I suspect case (a) actually bites there and nowhere else. And have each of them write down what they would count as evidence against their own side. If it turns out part of their disagreament is about the meaning of 'deserved' rather than about the world, say which part and why.

Format: numbered agreed claims first, then a table for the disagreements. Somewhere around a thousand words. Don't let either of them hide behind 'in principle' or 'all else equal' — if a caveat is needed, make them say what its doing.
