My friend and I argued for two hours last night about eternal recurrence and I think he got me. He doesn't buy it as a cosmology, he treats it as a test -- how would you feel if you had to live this exact life again, forever -- and then he says the test eats itself. His argument:

(1) If the recurrence is true, everything I do is done again, identically, infinitely many times.
(2) The value of a life is just the total value of what you do in it.
(3) So any act I do now gets added into an infinite total: do good and I contribute infinite good, do bad and I contribute infinite bad.
(4) Infinite good and infinite bad are both infinite, and no infinity is larger than another, so a life of infinite good and a life of infinite bad come out equal on this measure.
(5) So if the recurrence is true, nothing I do makes my life better or worse -- the exact opposite of the weight it's supposed to put on every choice.
(6) So either the recurrence is false, or the whole live-so-you-could-will-it-all-again business is empty.

I really dont want to give up (6), or the weight it puts on my choices. I'd sooner give up something in the middle, but I cant see which step is doing the damage, and he just says it's arithmetic and I should accept that nothing matters. Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what do I pay for rejecting it? If there's more than one candidate, price them against each other.
