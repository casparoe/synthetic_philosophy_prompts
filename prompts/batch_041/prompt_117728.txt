I have been running small open-weight language models on my own hardware for a couple of years, mostly to check documents against a rulebook for a side business, and I keep hitting a problem I cannot solve cleanly. A model will state a fact, a rule, or a judgment with complete confidence, and then contradict itself when I reword the question without changing what it asks; my working theory is that there is a real difference between the model having something available inside it and the model merely producing a sentence that sounds like the thing, and that these come apart because the model is partly answering the question of what would please the person asking rather than the question I actually asked. I would like you to turn that distinction into a procedure I can run. Give me an ordered set of checks to apply to a single claim a model has made, to decide whether it is drawing on something stable and internal or just generating a plausible-sounding sentence: for each check, what I do, what result counts as the model genuinely having it, what result counts as the model merely saying it, and what result is inconclusive. The checks have to be runnable at a laptop by someone with no research training, using only what the model says back to me, with no access to its weights or internal states and no outside source of truth, since the whole point is that I often do not know the right answer and am trying to find out what the model has. One idea I have been toying with, and want you either built in or argued against, is to stop asking the model directly and instead ask it what someone careless, or someone eager to please me, would say, on the theory that the honest answer leaks out when the model is not the one on the hook. Mark clearly the points where the procedure cannot decide and I have to make a judgment call myself. Then run it on two cases: a model that insists across several phrasings that a contract clause means one thing but flips when I first ask it to restate the clause in plain English, and a model that gives the same answer across many phrasings, in several languages, and when asked to explain itself, but whose answer I strongly suspect is wrong. Tell me which parts of the procedure survive these cases and where it will mislead me.
