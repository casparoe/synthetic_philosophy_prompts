So here's my situation. I did a PhD in philosophy years ago and then jumped ship into nonprofit work, and my old supervisor died last month at 78. She was, among other things, one of the last people in the field who defended completely unironically, in print and in seminars, the view Kant is famous for about animals: that we have no direct duties to animals at all, that whatever we owe them is really owed to ourselves and to humanity, and that being cruel to an animal is wrong because of what it does to the person being cruel rather than because the animal has any claim on us. She never once called an animal a rights-bearer or a person, and she had loud, decades-long fights with the animal-rights crowd about it, some of whom basically thought she was a monster.

But here's the thing, and it's why I need help. She also ran a wildlife rehab out of her garage for thirty years and would get up at four in the morning to feed orphaned squirrels, and she always insisted there was no contradiction there. Her line was that the indirect-duty view doesn't make kindness to animals optional or merely useful, it makes it more urgent, because being gentle with something that can't reciprocate, can't demand anything of you, and can't even understand what you're doing is a kind of rehearsal for how you treat everything powerless: children, the dead, the future, people who'll never be able to thank you. She thought a person who's kind only when the other party can hold them to account isn't kind at all, and she thought rights talk actually gets in the way of seeing this, because it trains you to go looking for the claim instead of noticing the disposition.

Now her family has asked me to give the speech at the dedication of a small animal sanctuary thats being named after her, and the room is going to be a nightmare to write for. There'll be her two brothers and their families, who are lovely and totally secular but who I can already hear thinking that "we have no duties to animals" sounds like she didn't actually care about them; there'll be a big contingent from the local rescue community who all talk fluently in the language of animal rights and are going to find it bizarre, maybe offensive, to be told the animals don't have any claims on us; and there'll be maybe a dozen philosophers who will notice immediately if I sand her view down into something she'd have rejected. Can you write the speech, about ten minutes, that honors her honestly, no smuggled-in rights talk and no pretending she was secretly a utilitarian, while still leaving the rescue people feeling that their work is vindicated and the family feeling that she loved animals, which she did? If you can work in one concrete moment with the squirrels, great. And please dont end it on a generic she-would-want-us-to-carry-on-her-work note, that would be the one thing she'd actually have hated.
