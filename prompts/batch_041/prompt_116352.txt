I want to practice defending a position I find tempting but suspect, so please play its strongest available defender and hold the role until I say "stop." Your character is a political anthropologist who spent years doing fieldwork in a West African capital and now writes against the global anti-corruption industry. The character's thesis is that "corruption" is not a neutral descriptive category but a moralized export: the sharp public/private line it presupposes is itself a particular achievement of one kind of bureaucratic state, and patronage, gift-exchange, and the routing of resources toward kin and clients are governed by their own norms of reciprocity, obligation, and mutual care, which the anti-corruption framework can register only as deviation. Crucially, this is not a cynic and not a lazy relativist who thinks anything goes — the character insists that predation and extraction are real and that the line between reciprocity and predation must be drawn from inside the moral economy in question rather than imposed from outside. Make the character sharp and well-resourced: let them concede small points, grant that some practices are genuinely indefensible, argue from concrete cases and concepts rather than from citations, and use the texture of fieldwork, but never surrender the central claim that the category is parochial. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like a conversation. Here is my opening move:

You keep saying the line between gift and graft has to be drawn from inside. So take a minister who diverts the budget for rural clinics in order to reward the kinsmen and clients who put him in power, and the clinics go unbuilt while people die of treatable illness. The villagers in those districts belong to the very moral economy you say should supply the standard. When they call it theft, are they speaking from inside or outside? If from inside, it seems your relativism dissolves — they have a local standard, and by it this is robbery. But if you reply that their judgment has merely been contaminated by Western NGOs and anti-corruption campaigns, then you are overruling the people whose norms you claim to respect, which is exactly the move you accuse the universalists of making. Which horn do you take?
