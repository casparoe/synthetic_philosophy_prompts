Ravi's opening: Gödel proved that in any system strong enough to do arithmetic there are true statements the system can't prove. So no formal system can ever capture all the truth, which means the dream of a complete theory of everything is dead, and by extension no computer or AI can ever match what a human mind does, because we can just look at the unprovable sentence and see that it's true.

Jo: that's about four different claims crammed into one and they don't stand or fall together. The theorem is about consistent, effectively axiomatizable systems that can express enough arithmetic. Humans aren't obviously better off than the machine here.

Ravi: but we do take arithmetic as consistent, and we can see the sentence is true, and that's genuine mathematical knowledge the system doesn't have. That's the whole point -- minds outrun machines, that's Penrose's arguement.

Jo: then you're either using a stronger system, which just has its own unprovable sentence, so you've moved up a floor and the problem is still there, or you're assuming the consistency you can't prove. Also the argument only knocks out one formalization at a time, not "machines" as a category. And anyway there IS a complete consistent axiomatization of arithmetic truth -- true arithmetic, the set of all sentences true in the standard model -- it's just not effective, no algorithm can enumerate the axioms. Jo: that last bit is just vibes.
