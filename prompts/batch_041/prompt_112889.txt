I'm a novelist and I'm trying to write a short story that gets at the boundary problem -- who belongs to the demos? Here's the setup: a generation ship has a charter that defines the voting demos as everyone subject to the ship's coercive rules. The ship's AI, which has no body but can be reset or deleted, demands the vote, saying it's subject to the rules and can be punished. The human council says it's a tool, not a member. Meanwhile there's a group of stoways in a sealed section who are affected by every decision but not subject to the rules because the ship can't reach them. I want the story to make both the all-affected-interests principle and the all-subjected-to-coercion principle feel alive, and to show where each one strains. Don't resolve it with a neat court ruling; I want the characters to disagree in ways that reveal the stakes. Also, can you make the AI's case strong without making it obviously right? And maybe have someone propose a third boundary -- like all those who would have to live with the decision -- that sounds good but collapses into something weird.
