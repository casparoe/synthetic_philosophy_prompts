I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about drug policy by going case by case, and I want to know what principle (if any) is generating my verdicts. I'm not after a reading list or a history of the debate — I want the rule that gets me, or the rule I should switch to, and then an honest account of where my gut is just inconsistent.

Here's my gut:

- Someone caught with a couple grams of coke at a festival: shouldn't be arrested, shouldn't even be fined.
- Same person handing a gram to a friend who asks, money changing hands at cost: still leave them alone.
- Same person selling grams to strangers at that festival every weekend: that's a crime, and a real one.
- Someone gives a pill to a mate at a party and the mate dies: I don't want them charged with murder, even though they supplied and even though someone died. I know some places do charge that and I hate it.
- A cannabis shop in a legal state with loyalty points, and billboards on the highway: shop's fine, billboards I'd ban tomorrow. The billboard bothers me more than the shop does, and I can't defend that.
- A supervised injection room two blocks from a school: fine, doesn't bother me at all.
- Naloxone handed out free at pharmacies: obviously fine, should be everywhere.
- A pill-testing tent at that same festival, no cops around it: fine, and better than having cops there.
- A doctor prescribing heroin to someone who's used for fifteen years and failed everything else: fine.
- A country that decriminalizes possession of everything but leaves the supply to gangs (thinking of Portugal, which I think got the first half right): better than what we have, not the end state.
- The state itself selling drugs out of government stores, at cost, no ads, no branding, like the liquor stores some places have: I find I like this more than licensed private shops, which surprises me.
- Arresting a pregnant woman because she tested positive for meth: not fine, don't care what the alternative is.
- A landlord who knowingly rents to a dealer: genuinely torn.
- Alcohol, tobacco, weed: I'd keep all three legal, but I'd want plain packs and no ads for tobacco, ads for alcohol, and billboards banned for weed. I can't get that ordering out of harm.

The rough rule I keep reaching for is: never punish the user, and punish the seller only when they're in the business of it — a gift or a cost-price handoff is just use by another route. But that rule definately doesn't get the billboard case (nothing about a billboard is supply), doesn't get the dead friend (that's supply and I let it go), and doesn't get the state store (that's the biggest seller of all and I approve).

So: is there one principle that gets all of this, or a small set of clauses? State it precisely, say which case each clause is doing the work for, and tell me what it predicts for three I haven't settled: (a) a legal cannabis market where the shops are fine but one company gets big enough to lobby against any further regulation; (b) a country that decriminalizes possession but doubles the penalty for selling, so users are safe and dealers rot; (c) me giving my own leftover pills to a friend with the same condition who can't get a script. Then say which of my verdicts you're seperate-ly going to have to overturn, and why that one and not another.

Lead with the principle in your first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my cases back at me.
