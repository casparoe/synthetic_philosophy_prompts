I build recommender systems, so I've ended up reading a fair amount of social epistemology, and two factions in my reading group have been talking past each other for months. Call them the exposure camp and the trust camp. The exposure camp says the pathology is informational: people are sealed off from contrary evidence by curation, homophily, and self-selection, so the fix is structural — change what gets served. The trust camp says the pathology is about credibility: people do meet the other side and dismiss it as illegitimate on contact, so more exposure does nothing or backfires, and the fix has to repair who counts as a legitimate source. I want the document these two camps should have produced together rather than another round of argument.

Please draft a joint statement both could sign. It should contain: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, beginning with stipulated definitions (an epistemic bubble excludes by omission — members never encounter the contrary view; an echo chamber excludes by discrediting — members encounter it and reject it as illegitimate), then what both accept about the empirical record and about which interventions remain live options; (2) the residual disagreements, each phrased so the side that holds it would accept the wording as its own; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling something would actually move a side — and for each crux a case, argument, or study design both camps agree would bear on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance. One crux worth working out: whether a trusted in-group figure publicly vouching for an out-group source is necessary for correction to take, or merely convenient. (4) A short note on any part of the dispute you judge merely verbal, and why.

Make the disagreement exact rather than hedging it into agreement. Assume nobody can look anything up: no citations, no author names, no reading lists — state any empirical claim in your own words and flag it as contested if it is.

End with a single line of the form \answer{...} giving the number of genuine cruxes and the number you judge merely verbal, e.g. \answer{4 genuine, 1 verbal}.
