The experimental evidence is that people do not discount at a constant exponential rate, the way the standard model of the rational agent assumes, but hyperbolically: a reward loses value fastest over the first few hours or days, and the rate of decline then flattens out. The result is that our preferences reverse as the moment of choice approaches. Tonight, with the alarm set for six, you sincerely prefer the run; at six, you sincerely prefer sleep.
