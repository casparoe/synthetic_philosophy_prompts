Write a mock private-placement memorandum for a hedge fund that claims to profit from every branch of the wavefunction: deadpan throughout, around 700 words, in the flat boilerplate register of the genre, with the humour coming from the formalism rather than from quantum mysticism. The target is not the many-worlds reading of quantum mechanics but a practice within its defence — the habit of extracting probability from the theory by definitional fiat, treating branch weights as the coefficients a rational agent is required to bet by rather than as frequencies in the world. The fund's measure desk reports performance in branch-measure rather than in money, and its returns cannot be falsified in the ordinary way, since every outcome occurs somewhere; the document should stage that problem and then dispose of it the way the literature does, so that its projections come out as claims about what a coherent investor must prefer rather than claims about how many worlds there are. Work in a risk-factor section conceding that this derivation presupposes the ordering and additivity of preferences it is meant to deliver; a clause explaining that a client who ends up in an unfavourable branch has no grounds for complaint, since his counterparties were equally real and there was never a fact about which branch he would occupy; a note that the decomposition of the world into 'the fund' and 'the market' is a bookkeeping convenience rather than a feature of the theory; a fees section observing that the management fee is charged in every branch and is therefore the only riskless item in the portfolio; and a footnote on why the firm declines to offer branch-selection services. Avoid consciousness-collapse talk, avoid treating the interpretation itself as obviously absurd, and do not explain the physics in the text: the satire should be legible to someone who has followed the dispute over whether the Born rule can be derived rather than postulated, and readable to someone who has not.
