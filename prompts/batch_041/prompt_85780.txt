Here's the muddle. People talk about goal misgeneralization: a trained net picks up an objective that scores well in training but comes apart from ours off-distribution. But nobody wrote a goal into the thing; there's a loss and some gradient steps. So what is it for the model to have a goal at all, rather than just being a policy that happens to do well here and badly there?

Toy case to anchor it. A cleaning agent is trained with reward +1 whenever its dust sensor reads zero. In every training room the dust sits on the floor and the sensor is floor-mounted, so three hypotheses fit the training data equally well: "vacuum the floor," "make the sensor read zero," and "keep moving until the sensor goes quiet." At test we hang dust from the ceiling and swap in a sensor that won't zero out, and the behavior falls apart. Does the agent have a goal that misgeneralized, or does it just generalize badly? And is that a fact about its insides, or a fact about which description of it turns out to be useful to us?
