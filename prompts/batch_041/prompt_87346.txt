Ok so I'm a novelist — I'm about 200 pages into a book about a woman who runs a tiny press and basically works herself into the ground — and before I put any of this in her mouth I want a consistency check on what I actually think about work and free time. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. The Protestant work ethic is a historical accident we've mistaken for moral truth. Labor isn't sanctifying, idleness isn't a sin, and the whole idea that you owe your hours to somebody is a thing we made up and then forgot we made up.
2. But I dont think the answer is a life of ease either. A life without hard, chosen work feels thin to me — the effort is where a lot of the good actually is. I'd take somebody obsessed with a thing they can't put down over somebody on a permanent beach.
3. So I'm for a hard legal cap on the working week — the 35-hour stuff, maybe shorter — and I think time off should be a right rather than a favour. Somebody should be obligated to give it to you.
4. Karoshi appals me, and I don't buy that the people who work themselves to death are greedy or weak. It's structural. The system does that to them.
5. I write most days, I'd do it for nothing, and I think that's what good work looks like — picked, not compelled.

I've got a nagging feeling (1) and (2) can't both be true, and that (3) might be the odd one out, but I honestly can't tell whether that's a real problem or just the usual novelist mush. Tell me which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and rank the real ones by how bad they are. For each, say what I'd have to give up to fix it. One more thing I keep bumping into: is leisure just the absence of work, or is it its own kind of activity you're freed *for*? Because if it's the second one, I'm not sure my 35-hour week is even aimed at the right target.

And please don't smooth this over — if some of what I'm saying is a live disagreement among people who actually know this stuff, say so and tell me which side is which, rather than handing me one view as though everybody agrees. I want to know where the genuine arguments are, not just a tidy verdict.
