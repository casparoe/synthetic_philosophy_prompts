So I joined this after-school philosophy club thing and my friend Maya has gone all-in on Leibniz, specifically the identity of indiscernibles — the idea that if two things have every single property in common then they're really just the same thing, so there can't be two genuinely distinct but exactly alike objects. I tried to bust it with the classic move: picture two spheres, same size, same color, same material, same temperature, perfectly alike, floating two miles apart in an otherwise totally empty universe. She says that's not a counterexample at all and wrote out her reasoning on the whiteboard like this:

(1) If a and b have all the same properties, then a and b are the same object. (2) In the two-sphere world the two spheres match in every intrinsic property — size, shape, color, composition, temperature, you name it. (3) So if they really are two different objects, they have to differ in some non-intrinsic, relational property. (4) And they do: the one on the left has the property of being to the left of the one on the right, and the one on the right has the property of being to the right of the one on the left, and those are two different properties. (5) So the spheres are discernible after all, the principle survives, and my counterexample is dead.

Here's my problem and I can't put my finger on it. This feels like it proves way too much. If just being related to another object counts as a property that makes you different, then dont literally any two objects whatsoever end up "different," for free, just in virtue of being two? Like she could run this on any pair of things no matter how alike, so the principle never actually rules out anything and it ends up being a really fancy way of saying every thing is itself. And I swear if there were only one sphere in that empty universe she could still "discern" it from itself using some relation it has to itself. I want to keep the principle as a real, meaty claim and not some triviality, but I also cant see how to block her step without giving up something I might want. I'm also not sure if she's allowed to help herself to the fact that it's *that* particular sphere the other one is related to, or if that's just smuggling the difference in through the back door. So which numbered step should I reject, and what does rejecting it actually cost me?
