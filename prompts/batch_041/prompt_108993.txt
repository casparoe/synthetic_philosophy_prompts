I chair the undergraduate appeals panel in my department, and this week I have to write our opinion on a grade appeal that has turned into an actual argument about set theory. In our intermediate logic course the students were asked what Skolem's paradox shows. One student wrote, roughly, that if set theory has any model at all then it has a countable one; that inside that model there are sets the model calls uncountable, including the set it takes to be the reals; so the reals are countable after all, the power set axiom isn't really about anything, and there's no fact of the matter about the size of the continuum. The TA gave him a zero with the note "confuses a model with reality." The student's advisor, who works in philosophy of math but not logic, says the zero is indefensible, since relativity of cardinality is exactly what the paradox shows. Now the department chair wants our ruling and none of us are confident we can tell who's right.

Write the panel's opinion for me to circulate. Start with the recommendation itself, then the reasons: I need a concrete bottom line -- a specific grade for that answer, or a specific remedy such as regrading that question for the whole class, with a date by which we notify the student -- and an honest treatment of the strongest thing that can be said for whichever side loses. Also say whether the panel should be ruling at all on the larger claim about the continuum, or only on the grade. Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold, or tables, the way you'd write a letter, and remember the department chair isnt a logician, so don't assume he knows the terminology.
