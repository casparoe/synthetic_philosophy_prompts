For a graduate seminar I teach on the philosophy of psychiatry, I want to hand out a decision tree that sorts the positions one can hold about the reality of psychiatric categories once one has taken looping effects seriously. Please construct it.

My students already know the standard naturalism-versus-social-construction dispute and find it stale, so the instrument should carry them past it. The starting commitment, from which every branch must hang, is therefore not the crude question of whether psychiatric categories are real, but the more specific concession that the classified sometimes move under classification: the population, its self-descriptions, and even the criteria can shift in response to the fact of being classified. The tree's job is to show what a position must add to that concession in order to earn a claim about what the category tracks. Do not let it bottom out in a single naturalism/construction fork; the most interesting positions both reject a pre-social essence, and the tree must separate them from one another.

To make the positions come apart at the joints, build the branch nodes around the following proposal of mine, which I would like you to use but also to correct if it is not exhaustive. I distinguish three modes in which looping can occur: (i) performative looping, where the label induces the very symptoms it names; (ii) semantic drift, where the classified community revises the criteria from inside, redefining what the kind is; and (iii) apparatus-driven drift, where the extension of the category moves because the institutions that record, fund, and diagnose it have changed. Each branch node should be a question a reader can answer about these modes — which of them they regard as genuine, and whether each is a threat to the category's reality or merely part of what such a kind is — without already knowing which position their answer commits them to.

The following positions must appear as leaves, each named (or marked unnamed if no one occupies it) with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment:

- the category tracks a dysfunction fixed independently of the classification (essentialist realism);
- the category is a real kind but a distinctively interactive one, whose members' self-understanding is part of what the kind is (dynamic nominalism);
- looping is a defect, and the moving target shows there is no kind there to track (constructivism or eliminativism about the category);
- looping is a mechanism like any other, and a self-sustaining one, so the feedback entrenches a real causal structure (feedback-stabilized realism);
- the category is real without any essence, because the symptoms cause one another (symptom-network realism);
- the category is an administrative instrument that need not track anything (pragmatic instrumentalism);
- the category is authorized in part by the community it classifies, so the classified have standing to say what the kind is (first-person realism).

Use these cases at the nodes, and at least one of them at every node that needs an example: the declassification of homosexuality; the rise and fall of multiple personality; the reframing of autism by autistic self-advocates; anorexia as a culture-bound syndrome; the cross-cultural variability of depression. Include one indifferent-kind control, a category that does not loop, so that readers can see what the absence of looping is supposed to look like.

Keep the instrument to six to eight branch nodes and seven to nine leaves. Where two leaves cannot be separated by any question in the tree, say so explicitly rather than papering over it. Where a combination of answers is left unoccupied, name it and diagnose briefly whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The handout is standalone: no citations, no reading list, no attributions.
