A friend and I run a small reading group on moral psychology and we've spent most of this year circling one argument: honor cultures, dignity cultures, victimhood cultures — is any of them actually morally better than the others? We're not confused about the descriptive side, more or less: honor is reputation and retaliation, dignity is not owing anyone an answer for a slight, victimhood is status claimed from harm suffered. The fight is that she thinks the honor ethic is at least coherent and honest about what it demands, I think dignity is the only one that treats persons as persons, and we both suspect the third is just dignity culture having a bad day. A year in, nothing has moved an inch. My hunch is that the three words are doing double duty — descriptive labels for social patterns and normative ideals — and every time we seem to make progress we've quietly switched which job the word is doing. But I want a real diagnosis, not my hunch handed back to me. Is this a verbal dispute, a disagreement about which cases even count (is dueling really honor, is a callout really victimhood), or a bedrock disagreement about what makes a culture good — one side ranking by what it demands of you, the other by what you're owed? And given that diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a reframing, some actual empirical finding, or is the question ill-posed and we should stop meeting about it? Also tell me straight if my hunch is the wrong diagnosis.
