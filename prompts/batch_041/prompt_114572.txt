I've been thinking on my own, without any training in philosophy, about something that keeps tripping me up, and I'd like a straight answer rather than a survey of what different people have said.

The thing itself is small. My wife and I are trying to decide whether to have a third child, and a friend of mine — who reads a lot of popular science and likes to argue — has been pressing me with an idea that I can't shake. His claim is that if you really believe that adding a happy person to the world is a good thing, then you should be willing to keep going: a world with a great many people whose lives are good but modest is better than a world with fewer people whose lives are wonderful, as long as the total amount of happiness comes out higher. He says the only way to resist this is to smuggle in a rule that some lives don't count, or to say that once people exist you care about them but that you don't care at all about people who might exist, which he calls a magic trick. When I say I'd rather have two children and give them everything than six and give each of them a decent but unremarkable life, he says I'm just privileging the children I happen to be able to picture.

Here is the part I actually want help with, because it isn't about my friend's slogans. I can see that a life can be worth living without being wonderful — my own is like that — and I can see that more of those lives is, in some sense, more good. What I can't accept is where this leads: that the best world might be an enormous one full of lives that are, on average, barely better than mine, and that choosing the smaller, richer world is a kind of moral failure on my part. And yet every escape I try seems to cost me something I believe. If I say quality matters more than quantity, I seem to be saying the many modest lives matter less than the few rich ones, which sounds like the snobbery I hate. If I say we should maximize the average, I seem to be saying it would be better if the miserable people simply hadn't been born, which sounds monstrous. If I say that once someone exists we owe them everything but that we owe nothing to people who don't yet exist, I can't tell whether that's a real distinction or just a way of not thinking about it. And if I put a floor under what counts as a life worth adding — below which a life shouldn't be created at all — I have no idea where the floor goes or why, and my friend immediately asks whether I'm just drawing it wherever it lets me keep my current plans.

I want you to take a position. Tell me which of these moves, if any, you think is actually defensible, and tell me what I should do about the third child — not as a dodge into "it's a personal decision," but as a judgment about what the right answer is, with the reasoning laid out. If you think the honest conclusion is the one I find repulsive, say so and defend it. If you think there's a way out that doesn't quietly cheat, name the cheat you're avoiding and show that you're avoiding it. And tell me plainly where your confidence comes from and where it runs out, since I'd rather have a real answer with its seams showing than a balanced summary that leaves me where I started.
