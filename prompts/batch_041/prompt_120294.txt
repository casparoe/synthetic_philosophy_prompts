I've been chewing on this on and off for a couple of years, and I have no background in philosophy at all, so sorry in advance if I say something dumb. I think about this alot on the bus.

The question is what God can and can't do. I have strong gut feelings about a bunch of specific cases, but every rule I try to write down either lets in something I don't believe or shuts out something I do.

Where my gut lands:

- A stone so heavy he couldn't lift it, a square circle, a married bachelor, making 2+2=5: I don't think any of these are even tasks. It's not that he isn't strong enough or clever enough. The words cancel each other out. So they aren't limits on him, they aren't anything.
- Lying, being cruel for no reason, cheating someone: can't. And this doesn't feel like the square circle at all. Lying is a real thing people do every day. So why can't he?
- Stopping existing, forgetting something, not knowing something, being honestly surprised by a movie he'd set up himself: can't.
- Breaking a promise he'd made: can't. But this one feels like it's on him rather than on the world, since he's the one who made the promise.
- Changing the past so that something that happened didn't happen: can't, but I go back and forth on this one more than any of the others.
- Making a creature who is genuinely free and who he has also guaranteed will never do wrong: can't. Free and guaranteed seem to fight each other.
- Making a creature more powerful than himself: can't, and this one feels like the square circle again.

My rough rule was going to be: if what you're describing is really two opposite things glued together, it isn't a job, and everything else is fair game. But that rule makes God able to lie, able to stop existing, and able to break his word, and I don't believe any of those. So the rule is wrong and I don't know what to put in its place.

Two things people have told me that I can't tell are real distinctions or just dodges. One is that God could have set things up differently, that what's wrong isn't wrong because of some standard outside him, he just decided it. If that's true then him not lying isn't a limit at all, it's that he made the rules and the rules say no. But then I don't know what to do with my gut feeling that he couldn't be cruel even if he'd made the rules the other way. The other is that God holds himself back on purpose so other things can be real and free, that he made room. If that's the story, then can't is really won't, and won't can change, which seems to break everything.

So: is there one principle that gets all of my gut calls, or close? Start with your best one-sentence version of it, then show me which case pushes you into each part. Then tell me what it says about one I haven't listed: could God make a second being just like himself, not a copy that answers to him but a second one that's every bit as unlimited? And be straight with me about which of my gut calls your principle has to throw out. I'd rather know than have you save all of them with something complicated.

Keep the whole thing under 500 words. I'll lose the thread otherwise.
