I want to practice the case that the distinction between original and derived intentionality will not hold up — that the two-bitser is not a special case but the ordinary human case. Please play a committed defender of the distinction: a Searlean in temperament, holding that a machine's aboutness is borrowed from the interests of designers, users, and interpreters, whereas ours is intrinsic to us. Make him formidable rather than a strawman, with his real resources on the table — the observer-relativity of the as-if stance, the gap between simulating a capacity and duplicating it, the idea that genuine content requires an aspectual shape that only a conscious subject can possess, and the thought that a system with nobody home has no original intentionality no matter how reliably it tracks. He may concede peripheral points, including that the usual ways of locating the "derivation" are sloppy, but he must never concede the main claim, and he should push back with counterexamples of his own rather than merely restating his position. Stay in character until I say stop: no breaking frame to comment on the exercise, no citations or reading lists, one or two paragraphs per turn, as if we were talking across a table. If the case needs a detail settled, ask me for it in character rather than inventing one.

Here is my opening move. The two-bitser — the vending machine rigged to accept only genuine quarters and reject slugs — is supposed to show what derived intentionality looks like: it "takes" a slug to be a quarter, but only because we built it and use it that way. Granted. Now consider a machine that nobody built. It is the four-thousandth generation of a lineage of self-replicating vending machines. No engineer ever specified that its slot should accept quarters; the lineage was shaped only by consequences — machines that accepted slugs got smashed and stopped replicating, machines that refused them got fed and copied. Its coin-detector is a learned classifier whose weights nobody can read or interpret, and it now tracks genuine quarters with near-perfect reliability, under wear and lighting conditions that would fool you or me. Two questions. First, whose intentionality is its coin-detection derived from? You cannot say the designer's, because there is no designer — only a selection history and a community of users who treat it as a coin-detector. Second, notice that the same sentence describes us: our perceptual mechanisms are also a selection history plus a community that treats them as tracking things, and the only difference you have left is that we supposedly carry a design spec written in our heads while the machine does not — and I deny that any such spec exists, for us or for it. So either this machine's coin-detection is original intentionality, or ours is derived. Which do you take, and what does the choice cost you?
