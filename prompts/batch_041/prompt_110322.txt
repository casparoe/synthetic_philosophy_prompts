Here's the pattern I keep running into.

Instance 1: a city public works crew gets measured on how many potholes they fix per quarter. Year one, numbers go up. But what actually changed is the unit. A single long trench that would have been one repair gets logged as eleven repairs, because the work order system just has a field for it and filling it in that way is not false. Nobody lied. Two years later the old figures are useless, because 1994's 4,000 repairs and this year's 12,000 repairs are not the same kind of thing and there's no way to convert.

Instance 2: personal. I track deep work hours in a spreadsheet. Early on I only counted time when I was genuinely locked in. At some point answering Slack while half-reading a paper started counting. I could not tell you when, and I don't think I ever decided to switch. If I go back to the strict reading now my numbers crater, which feels like discovering I've been slacking, even though nothing about the actual work changed.

Instance 3: a friend in college administration says the same thing happened with their retention rate. The words of the metric never changed. What changed is which students count as having been retained — transfers, people who left and came back, people who got a GED. Again, nobody is cooking the books; each judgment call is individually defensible. Again, the old series can't be compared to the new one.

The shape, as best I can state it: nobody is lying or being cynical, the metric keeps its name and its wording, but its extension quietly drifts. The drift is in the definition rather than in anyone's effort. It ratchets — after the drift, going back to the old reading registers as failure, so there's pressure never to go back. And the tell is that you can't even measure how far it's drifted, because the unit moved along with the thing being measured, so there's no fixed baseline to compare against.
