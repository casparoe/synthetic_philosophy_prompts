In February I was given a diagnosis that has made a question I used to enjoy at dinner parties into something I need an actual answer to. The medical particulars are irrelevant here; what matters is that I have spent the months since trying to work out what, if anything, is true in the consolation a physicist friend offered me. He is not a sentimental man, and what he said was this: on the four-dimensional picture, the future is already as real as the past, and so the things I am going to lose are, in the only sense that matters, still there — not remembered, not anticipated, just there. He then sketched the argument behind it, which I gather philosophers call the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. Special relativity leaves no frame-independent simultaneity, so any event spacelike separated from my here-and-now is simultaneous with it in some frame; if what is simultaneous with something real is itself real, then everything in my elsewhere is real; and from there the future is admitted too, so that there is no frame-independent fact about which events have already happened and which have not.

I am a distributed-systems engineer. I have spent eleven years on replication and consensus, so I am fluent in the vocabulary of events, happens-before, logical and vector clocks, causal pasts, local views, and whole-execution traces, and I am not fluent in physics beyond a good undergraduate course. I want the argument restated in the vocabulary I actually think in, and I want to know what that vocabulary cannot carry.

Please do two things, in this order.

First, the translation. Take the argument and its conclusion and map them, step by step, onto what I already reason with — events, the partial order induced by happens-before, concurrency, the distinction between a process's local view and the trace of the whole execution — as faithfully as you can manage, and tell me what each mapping costs. Do not let distributed-systems assumptions in through the back door and then present them as translation: if a step only goes through because I have already granted that the whole trace exists, or that the trace is complete, flag that at the step rather than quietly relying on it.

Second, the residue report, which is the part I care about. Which claims come through cleanly, which come through only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in what I do? I am especially interested in the pieces that would have to carry the consolation: tense and becoming, the openness of the future, the difference between an event being in the trace and the trace being finished, the fact that a log is written by appending to it and that appending is itself an event. For each residue, say whether it looks like a defect of my vocabulary — something real that events and happens-before simply cannot express — or like evidence that the original claim was less clear than it sounded. I would rather have an exact account of my ignorance than a reassuring one, and I would rather you say that a step is a stipulation than pretend it is an inference.

No reading list, please; I cannot check it and it is not what I am after. End with a final line of exactly the form \answer{...}, containing one of the following labels and nothing else: SURVIVES, if the consolation is still standing after the translation; SURVIVES-WITH-LOSS, if something survives but not the thing my friend meant; or DISSOLVES, if the consolation turns out to have been an artifact of a vocabulary rather than a fact about time.
