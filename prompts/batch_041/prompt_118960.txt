You know the Popper-ish idea of verisimilitude (truthlikeness) — that even though our theories are all false, some are closer to the truth than others, and that's basically what scientific progress amounts to. My friend says that whole idea is a counting trick that eats its own tail, and here's his argument, which I've cleaned up and numbered for you:

(1) For "closer to the truth" to mean anything, one false theory has to be able to be more verisimilar than another false theory — otherwise progress-talk is empty.
(2) The natural way to cash that out: a theory's verisimilitude is its truth ratio — the fraction of the propositions it entails (its content) that are actually true.
(3) Take any consistent theory T. For any sentence c it entails and any sentence s at all, T also entails the disjunction c ∨ s (if c holds, so does c ∨ s).
(4) So for every false consequence c of T, and for every true sentence s you like, T entails the true disjunction c ∨ s. That's a lot of true consequences.
(5) The true consequences of any consistent theory therefore vastly outnumber its false ones, so its truth ratio comes out essentially 1.
(6) But that holds for every consistent theory, good or bad alike, so they all end up equally verisimilar.
(7) So no theory is ever closer to the truth than any other, and "progress" is an illusion.
(8) Which is absurd, so something above has to be broken.
