Write a short story of roughly 1,500 words built around a phenomenon I will call nested sphexishness, defined as follows. A system is sphexish to the extent that its apparent flexibility — its responsiveness to circumstance — is produced entirely by a fixed repertoire of rigid routines, so that a perturbation it was not built for does not yield a new response but merely re-triggers an existing routine from the top. (The name comes from the standard telling of the digger wasp Sphex, which places a paralyzed cricket at the mouth of its burrow, descends to inspect the burrow, and, if the cricket is nudged away during the inspection, retrieves it, replaces it at the mouth, and inspects again — indefinitely.) Nested sphexishness is the special case in which the routine that is supposed to detect and correct sphexishness is itself sphexish, so that the corrective procedure merely re-runs in a loop.

Set the story in the Bureau of Deliberative Certification, a small regulatory body that licenses machines as deliberative rather than merely reactive. The distinction is legal and financial: a licensed deliberative system is treated as the locus of its own responsibility, while the operators of an unlicensed reactive system bear liability for whatever it does. Certification is decided by a perturbation battery — a series of probes that displace some element of a system's environment mid-task and watch whether it adapts or loops.

Your protagonist is a junior certification engineer who, while auditing the Bureau's own review procedure, begins to suspect that the procedure is sphexish in exactly the sense above: when a submission is anomalous, the process returns to a fixed checkpoint and re-runs, and the engineer can predict the re-run before it happens. Give the engineer one concrete discovery — a specific probe or observation — that makes the suspicion nearly undeniable, and then force a decision about whether to act on it.

Let the story dramatize, without lecturing, the following questions: whether the difference between deliberation and sphexishness is a difference in kind or only in the size and interleaving of the repertoire; whether free will requires flexibility that is not itself decomposable into rigid parts, and whether such a requirement is even coherent; and whether a system flexible enough to notice its own rigidity has thereby escaped it or only added another loop. Do not resolve these questions neatly; let the characters disagree and let the ending stay genuinely open.

Begin the story with the story itself — no preface, no framing remarks, no commentary before or after. Do not use section headings or epigraphs.
