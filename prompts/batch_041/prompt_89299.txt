ok so this happened in my bio class and i honestly cant stop thinking about it. we were doing a unit on genetics and my teacher put up this made-up scenario: theres a gene tweak that makes a person sleep about 3 hours a night, wake up feeling completely rested, no health problems, totally normal life otherwise. the slide just said "is this a disease?" and two kids in my class went at it for like 15 minutes while the teacher just sat there and let it happen.

heres the back and forth as best i can remember it.

Maya went first. she said yeah, its a disease, or at least a disorder. a disease is when some part of your body cant do the job its supposed to do, and the system that controls sleep isnt doing what its supposed to do here. she said whether the person is happy about it doesnt matter at all, thats like saying a broken bone isnt broken if you enjoy the attention. biology decides, not vibes.

Devon said no, thats backwards. he said "the job its supposed to do" just means "what most peoples bodies do," which is a statistic, not a medical fact. if the person isnt harmed and isnt suffering, calling it a disease is us saying we dont like it, not us discovering something about them. he said the whole word is a value judgment wearing a lab coat.

Maya came back with: fine, but medicine treats stuff that doesnt bother you all the time. high blood pressure feels like nothing and we still call it a disease, cause its a malfunction that raises your risk of dying. so the real standard is malfunction, not harm, and the sleep thing is a malfunction of the sleep system.

Devon said that just sneaks the harm back in, since the only reason the blood pressure matters is the dying part. and he said something i thought was actually pretty good: "the sleep system" isnt a thing sitting in your body with a label on it, we decided where that system starts and ends. carve the body up a different way and theres nothing there to malfunction. he also said if it turned out half of everybody had this gene, maya would probaly call it normal, which shows the answer depends on how many people have it, not on biology.

Maya tried one more. she said ok, different example: someone born who cant feel pain at all. looks healthy, says theyre fine, isnt complaining. but they bite their tongue and break bones without noticing. youd call that a disorder and youd be right, even though theres no suffering going on. so harm isnt doing the work.

Devon said the only reason that counts as a disorder is that the rest of us are imagining their life going badly, which is a value judgment about what a good life looks like, not a fact about the nerves. describe the same person with zero mention of "disorder" -- just "this person cant feel pain, heres what happens to them" -- and nothing in the description says anything is wrong.

then the bell rang and they never resolved it, and the teacher wouldnt tell us who was right, which is definately driving me nuts.

can you go through their moves one at a time and tell me whos right on each one, not just whos right overall? like rate each move and say why. and tell me what both of them are missing, cause i feel like theyre each onto something real and also each leaving something out. please dont just name some famous theory at me and stop there -- i want to know what actually goes wrong or right in each step of the argument, and whether the made-up gene case is even the right kind of example to settle it.
