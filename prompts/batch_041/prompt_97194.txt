Me and a friend spent like three hours arguing last night about designer babies and I want to see the thing actually done properly, as a dialogue.

Two characters. First is a procreative beneficence type: if you're having a kid and you can choose between embryos, you've got a real reason — maybe even an obligation — to pick the one who'll have the best life available to you, so selecting for higher intelligence is the same kind of move as selecting against a disease. Second is a critic from the disability-rights side, but make her sharp and not a slogan machine — her complaint isn't that difference is beautiful, it's that the whole setup turns a future child into a product to be optimized, and that selecting against certain traits quietly expresses a verdict about which lives are worth living.

The thing I actually want them to fight about, because it's where me and my friend kept getting stuck, is that intelligence is a positional good in a way that eyesight isn't. If everyone selects for it, the advantage mostly cancels out — nobody's life actually goes better, everyone just pays for a treadmill. So the beneficence guy has to explain why selecting for a positional trait is even in the kid's interest at all, and the critic should press him on whether that collapses the whole case into pure competition that has nothing to do with the child's welfare.

Real back and forth — they should answer each other's actual points, neither should be a strawman, and don't let them just recite the non-identity problem at each other and call it a day.
