I'm in a fourth-year seminar on collective action and social structure, and the prof has spent his whole career thinking about the Moloch / multipolar-trap thing — he grades us on whether we can tell a real trap from a fake one. For Monday I have to predict his verdict on a new case using only his verdicts on the old ones, so what I actually need is for you to work out the rule he's running. No reading list, no cites, just the logic.

His calls so far, as best I can reconstruct them:

1. Two rival dictionary publishers both racing to add entries faster than the other, each knowing that more entries means sloppier entries, and each would stop tomorrow if the other did: trap.
2. Two ad agencies in an escalating spend war that cancels out: trap.
3. Two states in an arms race, each arming because the other is: trap.
4. Me at 2am scrolling instead of sleeping: not a trap, and he was definately emphatic about it. One person losing to her own impulses is weakness of will; a trap needs at least two parties in the ring.
5. Two neighbors who each wait for the other to shovel the shared walk, and it doesn't get shoveled: not a trap. He calls these Babel cases — everyone wants the same thing, they still can't get it, but nobody is being pushed to escalate, they just each guessed wrong about the other. Still bad! Just not a trap.
6. Two labs racing to finish the same open dataset, both releasing everything as they go, each saying it would have been fine with them if the other had won, and nobody ending up worse off in anything they care about: not a trap.

The new case I have to call:

Nobody's competing for money, and every team sincerely says it would be thrilled if one of the others got there first. But the field tracks who published first, and that number is excellent — so each team ships a half-working version as early as it can, and then everyone builds on the half-working version, because that's what exists. The tool that actually gets used ends up worse than any one team's would have been, effort is duplicated to hell, and the people relying on the tool to test their wells are the ones who eat it. No team has any interest that conflicts with any other team's.

What I want: his verdict on that, the candidate rules that fit everything in 1–6, what each one predicts here, which you'd actually bet on and why, and what case I should put to him next that would genuinely tell those rules apart.
