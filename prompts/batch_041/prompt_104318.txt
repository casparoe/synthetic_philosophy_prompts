I'm a fourth-year PhD student working on social ontology, and for the past month I have been arguing over dinner with my flatmate, who is a demographer, about whether the generational labels we all use pick out anything real. I have come to suspect that my verdicts on particular cases are more defensible than any of the arguments I have been giving for them, and I would like someone to tell me what principle, if any, they add up to. Here is where I land, case by case. The Baby Boomers are a real kind: there is a genuine bulge in the birth series, the people in it were formed by the same postwar expansion and the same civil-rights and antiwar upheavals at roughly the same point in the life course, and they organised self-consciously around the label. Generation X is not a real kind; it is a label that got attached to a stretch of birth years after the fact, and it mostly records what advertisers thought of them. Millennials are a real kind. Generation Z is not a real kind, even though its members use the label and are constantly described in terms of it. 'Digital natives' is not a real kind, and the empirical work I know of suggests the shared trait was never really there. The so-called Greatest Generation is not a real kind either, even though its members lived through the Depression and the Second World War, because the label was retrofitted decades later by people with something to sell. The Silent Generation is not a real kind; it is a birth dearth with a mood attached. On the other hand, the people who came of age into the 2008 financial crisis are a real kind, and so are immigrants who arrived before adolescence and grew up between two languages: both cut across the birth-year labels and neither is a proper birth cohort. Teenagers are a real kind, but not a generational one, since they are an age grade and everyone leaves it on schedule. My grandmother's 'generation' in the family-tree sense is not a kind at all. And when my flatmate speaks of the F2 and F3 generations of a laboratory population, I think the word is doing something entirely different and entirely unproblematic.

My tentative principle is that a cohort is a real kind just when a historical event or process struck its members at the same point in the life course and left a durable common mark, with the label being a real kind only insofar as it latches onto that event. But that seems to make Generation Z real, which I deny, and it gives me no explanation of why Millennials survive while Generation X does not, when both labels issued from the same demographic-marketing industry with equally arbitrary boundaries and both were taken up by the people they name. My second attempt has been to say that a label marks a real kind when the boundary is fixed by the mechanism rather than by the label itself -- I have been calling these 'mechanism-bounded' and 'label-bounded' cohorts -- but that appears to put Millennials on the wrong side of the line, so I assume the distinction needs repair rather than mere application. What I want, then, is this: state, as precisely as you can, the principle or small set of principles that gets all of my verdicts, or nearly all of them; tell me which clause of it each verdict is doing the work for; and be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon. If you think 'generation' is really several concepts wearing one word and that my cases are being sorted by different criteria in a way I have not noticed, say so and separate them rather than forcing a single formula. I would also like its predictions on three cases I have not made up my mind about: the children of China's one-child policy; the people who came of age during the 2020-22 pandemic, who I suspect will end up with a label nobody agrees on; and a hypothetical country whose birth rate collapses for a decade and then recovers. Finally, tell me what would have to be true for Generation Z to count as real after all -- I would like to know whether my verdict there is a finding or a prejudice. I do not need a reading list; I know the classic sociological treatments of generations well enough and will supply references myself.
