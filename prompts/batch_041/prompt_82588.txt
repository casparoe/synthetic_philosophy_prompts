my reading group is doing a week on the inefficacy objection and everyone keeps reaching for the same analogy: individual emissions, or eating meat, or casting a ballot, are like a single vote in a national election. no one vote decides anything, the margin is huge, subtract yours and the outcome is bit-for-bit identical -- yet we don't say voting is pointless, we say participation matters and you should still do it. the move is supposed to rescue individual climate action (or at least show that the "it makes no difference" objection proves too much, since it would also condemn voting).

can you audit it properly? give me the mapping first, a table is fine -- what plays the role of the candidate, the electorate, the threshold, the ballot, the campaign, the norm of participation -- then tell me where it actually breaks. a few things I'm already suspicious of: elections are discrete, they have a determinate decision rule and an artificial equality of inputs, whereas emissions are continuous and the harm runs through nonlinear physical processes with tipping points; the vote analogy makes you a contributor to a *decision*, while the climate case is supposedly about contributing to a *harm*, and those look like different relations; and aggregation is rigged in the election case (one person one vote) but not in the climate case, where marginal contributions vary wildly by individual. does that last one sink it, or is it just a complication?

but also tell me what the analogy gets right that I might be too quick to dismiss -- the proves-too-much move seems to have genuine force and I want to know exactly which disanalogies matter for whether it keeps that force and which are noise. then: does the conclusion people draw from it -- that if you care about the outcome you should still act -- survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the conclusion the whole time? and if voting is the wrong analogue, is there a better one? I've seen people offer the firing-squad case (every shooter can truly say his own bullet wasn't necessary) and the harmless-torturers case (a large number of people each turn a dial by an imperceptible amount, and one person ends up in agony), but I can't tell whether either is an improvement. say what each gets right and wrong as an analogue and, if you think one is better, why.

lastly, does any of this change for meat? there the harm is legible and localized to a particular animal in a way it isn't for climate, so does "one more purchase" map onto "one more vote" any better or worse than "one more flight" does? I suspect the answer splits by case and I want to know where the split falls.
