I am working on a paper about the ethics of assertion and I want your credence rather than a tour of the literature. The proposition is this: there is a non-derivative moral difference between lying and merely misleading. To fix the comparison, take a case in which a hearer comes to hold a false belief because the speaker asserted something she believed to be false, and an otherwise identical case in which the hearer comes to hold the very same false belief, equally foreseen, equally intended, and by the same means of communication, except that every statement the speaker made was one she believed to be true. Is the first case worse in itself, or only in its typical consequences and its bearing on the speaker's character?

Give me a number for the proposition and the decomposition that generates it: your probability that there is a distinct norm governing assertion as such, rather than a single norm against bringing about false beliefs in others; your probability that violating such a norm is wrong independently of its consequences; the weight you assign to the fact that a hearer who is misled has nothing to complain of regarding the truth of what was said to her; and any other terms that belong in the sum, each with a number and a line of justification, followed by the combination. My own credence is about 0.3, and I would like to know which term we most likely diverge on, plus what evidence or argument would move you most.

One wrinkle I want you to absorb rather than set aside. I have been calling laundering the case in which the hearer assembles the false belief for herself out of a sequence of true statements, none of which the speaker disbelieves, and in which the speaker would have lied outright had the sequence not been available to her. If the lying/misleading difference is real, does it survive laundering, or does laundering let an agent capture the wrong of lying while incurring none of its costs?

Please answer in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write a letter.
