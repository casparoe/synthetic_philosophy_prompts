I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have been reading about decision theory in my spare time. I would like a careful conceptual explanation, written in British English, of the absent-minded driver problem and of a variant of it that I have been trying, without much success, to think through.

Begin with the original problem: a driver travels along a road with several exits, knows the overall layout, but cannot tell, on reaching an exit, which one it is or how many she has already passed, and must decide at each whether to leave the road or carry on. The first exit is worthless, the second is the good one, and driving past both yields only a small consolation. Please explain why reasoning that is locally correct — at the point of decision, take the option with the greater expected value given the information available — is held to give the wrong answer here; what the standard remedy is; and why the problem is thought to mark a distinction between choosing an action and choosing a policy.

Then consider my variant. There are three exits and the driver again cannot tell them apart. Leaving at the first pays 0, at the second pays 2, at the third pays 6, and continuing past all three pays 1. I now add a dashboard device that, at each exit, emits a signal. With probability q the signal names the driver's true position; with probability 1 − q it is a random one of the three labels. Before setting off, the driver may choose whether to switch the device on, at a running cost c.

What I would like is the conceptual machinery rather than a trudge through the algebra:

- how the device alters the driver's information state, and why information about one's own position in a decision tree is not simply information about an external state of the world;
- whether a rational driver would ever pay a positive price for such a signal, and what fixes the most she should be willing to pay;
- whether it matters that the decision to buy is taken before setting off rather than at an exit, and how this connects to commitment;
- whether the standard policy-based analysis still goes through unchanged, or whether the variant exposes a gap in it.

Please do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I have no way to check them and do not need them. What I want is the reasoning laid out plainly, including an honest account of where the analysis becomes contested or the intuitions pull apart.
