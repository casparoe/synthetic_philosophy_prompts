I've been reading about al-Ghazālī's occasionalism — the bit where he says fire doesn't really cause cotton to burn, that God just creates the burning each time the fire is present, and that God could equally have made the cotton burn without any fire or not burn with the fire right there. Isn't this basically just Hume a few centuries early? Like, he's noticing that we only ever observe one thing following another and never the "necessary connection" itself, so really we can't know what'll happen next. If that's the view, how did he keep it from collapsing into skepticism — how could anyone still trust that water boils or that a remedy works twice, if at any moment God might just do something else? Did he basically have to say every regular event is a little miracle? And honestly, is there any real difference between his position and Hume's, or is that just a label thing?
