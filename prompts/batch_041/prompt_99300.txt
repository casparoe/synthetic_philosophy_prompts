My sister works at a big teaching hospital and she showed me the intake note template they all use now. It's the usual SOAP note, except someone has bolted a box onto the top called a "narrative coherence score" — the triage nurse rates, 1 to 5, how smoothly and consistently the patient tells their story, and that number gets dumped into the chart next to the vitals. In a couple of services they actually use it to decide who gets the attending and who gets parked with the resident. Meanwhile the patient's own account of what's wrong still goes in the "subjective" field and the doctor's exam still goes in "objective." And the same hospital has posters everywhere about "listening to the patient" and a mandatory online module on "patient voice."

I can't stop tripping over the mismatch. What view of testimony and knowledge does this setup presuppose — that a patient's report is evidence to be calibrated rather than a claim to be believed, and that what makes a report credible is how cleanly it fits a script rather than whether the person has any reason to lie? Which rival pictures of how we know things from other people does it quietly rule out? And is the thing internally consistent, or does the coherence score contradict the "subjective/objective" split in some way — since one treats the patient as a source and the other treats them as a symptom.

Also be honest about how much of this the practice actually needs. Could several different theories of testimony license the exact same template? If we swapped in a view where testimony is a basic source of knowledge and credibility should track a hearer's background assumptions instead of the speaker's fluency, what concretely would have to change about how the note is built and how the score is used? I don't need names and reading lists, I just want the implicit theory dug out and the underdetermined parts flagged.
