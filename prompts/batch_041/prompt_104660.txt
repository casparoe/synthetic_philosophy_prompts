My friend does philosophy of mind and she has very firm opinions about which things have real intentionality — she says original — and which only have it on loan from us, derived. She keeps handing down verdicts and now I want to know what she'll say about a new case.

Her rulings so far: adult human, original. Dog, original. Octopus, original. A parrot that picked up words by mimicry, derived. Thermostat, derived. A chess engine that plays at grandmaster strength, derived. A wild bacterium swimming up a sugar gradient, original. Her two-bitser — the coin detector in a vending machine — derived. A corporation that "decides" to recall a product, derived.

The new case: a robot whose neural net was never hand-coded. Its weights were shaped by an evolutionary simulation that ran for tens of thousands of generations, with mutation and selection inside the sim, and the thing it got selected for was tracking obstacles and food in its world. A grad student wrote the fitness function, but no human ever specified what any of the robot's internal states mean, and yet they reliably covary with stuff in the environment and do real causal work in producing its behavior — it'll even swerve at a hologram of an obstacle, as if it took the hologram for the real thing.

So: original or derived? I care less about the verdict then about the rule she's actually running. Give me at least two rules that fit every one of her earlier verdicts, say what each one predicts for the robot, tell me which you'd bet on and why, and name the one further case that would best force those rules apart. And if you think she's leaning on a distinction that doesnt survive contact with the robot, say that too.
