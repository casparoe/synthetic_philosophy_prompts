I have been trying to work out what I think makes something a disease, going case by case, and my verdicts will not sit still together. Nearsightedness: a disease. Male-pattern baldness: not a disease, though it is a departure from the species norm and often a genuine grief. A woman born with a uterus that cannot function, who has never wanted children and is untroubled by it: not a disease. An eighty-year-old man with a narrowed heart valve that has never caused a symptom and probably never will: a disease. A body that cannot digest milk sugar: a real condition in a society where milk is a staple, and nothing at all — not even a defect — in a society where nobody drinks it. Grief after a death: not a disease, however devastating and however long it lasts. A person whose internal organs are mirrored left to right, discovered by accident, everything working perfectly: not a disease. A man who is distressed by having two healthy legs and wants one amputated: I do not think his leg is diseased, but I think something is wrong with him, and I cannot say what.

The uterus and the heart valve look like the same case to me — a part that does not do its job and causes no trouble — and I give opposite verdicts. So either my principle is more complicated than I want, or one of those verdicts has to go.

I know there are long-running camps here, one defining disease by biological malfunction and one by disvalue, and I do not want a survey of them. I want the principle that fits my verdicts, even if it belongs to no camp.

Please give me, in this order: "The principle, stated" — your best formulation, with its key terms defined (about 150 words); "Clause by clause" — each clause with the verdict that forces it and the verdict that nearly kills it; "Verdicts on new cases" — its verdicts on at least three cases I have not given, one of them designed to break the principle; and "The verdicts I have to give up" — which of mine it cannot keep, what a simpler rival would preserve at the cost of overturning something, and which you would choose.
