I am an undergraduate research assistant in a mechanistic interpretability lab and am concurrently taking a seminar on explanation in the cognitive sciences. Last month I trained a sparse autoencoder — a tool that decomposes a layer's activations into a large number of sparsely active directions — on the middle layers of a small language model, and one of the directions it recovered fires on dates written day-first but not month-first; in my draft I describe this by saying the model represents the format of a date. My postdoc objects that this is a redescription of a direction in activation space rather than a claim about anything inside the model, and that the finding earns its keep only insofar as ablating that direction changes the model's outputs. My advisor counters that if the postdoc were right, it would make no sense to say the model gets the date format right or wrong, or that it is confused about European dates. A labmate insists the question is empirical and proposes a control: train the same kind of probe on a randomly initialized model of the same architecture and see whether it also decodes the format; I cannot see why she thinks that would settle anything. I have to write a short position paragraph for the seminar, and I would rather not end at "it depends what you mean by represents." Can you tell me what this disagreement is actually about, whether there is a distinction or a test that would let me state a more precise claim than either of them is making, and what I would be committing myself to if I sided with one of them?
