I'm a backend engineer who spends most of his time on replicated state machines -- consensus protocols, leader election, the works. Which is why the philosophical stuff about the actual split-brain patients keeps catching my eye: I think I could restate that whole debate in my own vocabulary, and I want you to do it properly and then tell me what doesn't survive the trip.

Here's the shape of it as I understand it, and correct my understanding of the philosophy side as you go. There seem to be three main things people say about a patient whose hemispheres have been surgically disconnected. One: there are two subjects in there, two streams of experience running in parallel, each with its own perceptions and preferences -- hence the hand that buttons the shirt fighting the hand that unbuttons it. Two: there's still just one subject, but access is divided; some information reaches the part that can talk and some doesn't. Three: there's no fact of the matter about how many subjects there are, it depends on where you draw the boundary, and the cases feel uncanny because we're hunting for a fact where there's only a decision about bookkeeping.

Now translate each of those three into my vocabulary -- replicas, quorum, partitions, eventual consistency, per-replica state, and the question of whether a cluster counts as one system, which in my world gets settled by admin boundaries and the SLO, not by anything inside the machines. For each, say what carries over cleanly, what only survives with distortion, and what has no counterpart at all. Related: I've read that even in the classic cases the cut isn't total, that some subcortical wiring stays intact, so it's more like a partial partition than a clean split. Do the philosophers care about that or do they idealize it into a clean cut? Because in my world a partial partition is exactly where "one system or two" gets genuinely murky, and I'd want to know whether they're getting that for free from the biology or ignoring it.

The residue report is the part I care about most.
