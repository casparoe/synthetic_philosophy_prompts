I spent about 35 years as a stratigrapher before I retired, and since then my daughter has been feeding me philosophy books, so I've ended up with a view about history that I'd like you to try to break. Here it is. For the big, coarse-grained outcomes of human history — which alphabet, which side of the road, which railway gauge, which legal code, which file format — I've come to think contingency beats convergence. The outcome we actually got is mostly a frozen accident: one of several equally workable options that got selected for reasons having nothing much to do with it being the best one, and which now persists mainly because the cost of switching is higher than any benefit you'd get. On this picture there's no attractor dragging the system toward a single solution; there's just a metastable state sitting in a deep well. Set that against what I'll call the attractor view, where the same underlying problem, started from many different initial conditions, flows to the same answer anyway, so that rerunning history with a different early nudge would land you in more or less the same place.

So what I want from you is a map of how exposed my view actually is. What would count against the frozen-accident picture — which observations, which arguments, which cases, which formal results from adjacent fields? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and be clear about which ones I would myself have to concede rather than ones only a critic would press. Tell me what my view predicts that the attractor view doesn't, and the reverse, and then be honest about whether either prediction can be checked at all, or whether this is the sort of claim that can be narrated after the fact no matter how things turned out — and if it has that problem, say whether it's a genuine defect or just something you have to live with in historical explanation. A friend of mine who works in innovation policy keeps hammering me with the fact that the same invention seems to show up independently in several places at once, and the biologists keep pointing at the camera eye turning up again and again in unrelated lineages. I can't tell if that's convergence cutting against me or convergent evolution being a different phenomenon entirely. And I'd like the counterfactual problem handled without hand-waving: I obviously can't rerun history, so which proxies are legitimate — natural experiments, near-misses, places that were one system and then got split and run under different rules on either side of a line, or the reverse where two systems merged? Which of those do real evidential work and which are just nice stories?

One more thing I care about: whether my position has quietly turned into something that nothing could touch. If every outcome can be retroactively described as contingent, then what would it even look like for the frozen-accident thesis to be wrong? Give me that failure condition if it exists, and if it doesn't, tell me straight that the view is insulated and whether that should bother me. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my question back at me.
