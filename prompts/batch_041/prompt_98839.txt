I am a retired experimental physicist. Some years ago I traded the laboratory for a reading chair and have since become an increasingly serious amateur of philosophy — serious enough that I have been invited to contribute one question to the final examination of a small upper-level undergraduate seminar on practical rationality and intention, which I now help to run. I would be grateful if you would draft that question, together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

Let me explain what I am after. Every student in the room will have met the toxin puzzle, and I am determined that the question should not be answerable by reciting the standard setup and the standard gestures — the ones that say, in effect, that the agent cannot intend because she knows she will not follow through. What I want instead is the thing I spent a career trying to get undergraduates to do at the bench: isolate the variable. So I have devised a small family of variants, each of which alters exactly one feature of the original, and I want the question organised around them, closing with a forced choice about what the puzzle ultimately shows.

Please use the following scenario and variants exactly as stated, and make the question fully self-contained — answerable by reasoning about the scenarios as given, without appeal to any published treatment, and without requiring students to name or cite authors.

The original scenario: at midnight tonight a device will scan you and, if and only if it detects that you now intend to swallow a vial of toxin at noon tomorrow, will transfer one million dollars to your account. The scan is perfectly reliable. Nothing compels you to act after midnight; you remain free to change your mind. The toxin will make you violently ill for a day — ill enough that you would not undergo it merely to keep a million dollars already in hand — and the money depends only on the midnight intention, not on what you do at noon. The familiar tension is that you appear to have a reason to intend (the money) while being unable to intend, since you know that at noon you will have no reason to drink and therefore that any state you manufactured at midnight would not be a genuine intention.

Variant R (the Reimburser): everything is as in the original, except that a second party will pay you a further million if and only if you actually swallow the toxin at noon.

Variant S (the Surety): everything is as in the original, except that before midnight you may sign a costless, irrevocable contract under which a third party will, at noon tomorrow, physically compel you to swallow the toxin should you not do so voluntarily.

Variant P (the Craving): everything is as in the original, except that you know that at eleven tomorrow morning you will be given a drug that will fill you with an overwhelming craving for the toxin, so that at noon you will want nothing more than to drink it.

I would like the question to require, in some order: a precise statement of where the tension in the original actually lies, stated as a conflict among premises rather than retold as a story; an analysis of each variant that says whether the tension survives and, crucially, which single feature of the original the variant removes or restores; and a closing forced choice among three readings of the puzzle — (i) that it shows intentions are not under our direct voluntary control, (ii) that the reasons governing the formation of an intention differ from those governing the performance of the intended act, or (iii) that the scenario is somehow incoherent and no genuine intention could exist in it — with a defence of one reading and an account of how the variant the student found most revealing bears on the choice.

Alongside the question, please supply a model answer of roughly seven hundred words, written at the standard I would hope the best students reach, and a marking scheme. The marking scheme should name the specific moves a top answer must make, assign marks to each summing to twenty-five (the question should be answerable in ninety minutes), and, for each required move, state in one line the characteristic error that forfeits it. I am impatient with answers that are eloquent but never say which premise is doing the work; the scheme should reward the isolation of premises and penalise hand-waving, and should make explicit what separates an A answer from a B answer.
