I'm a novelist — literary fiction mostly, though the book I'm drafting has a speculative streak — and I've spent the last few months arguing with two friends (one a philosopher, one a civil-liberties lawyer, of course) about what privacy is actually *for*. I've ended up defending a position I want to stress-test. Roughly: privacy has no value in itself; whatever value it has is entirely downstream of the relationships and the self-presentations it makes possible. I've been calling it the scaffolding view — privacy is the scaffolding around a house of many rooms, and once the rooms are built the scaffolding can come down without any real loss. The strong version, which is the one I actually want to hold, is this: if you could wave a wand and make everyone permanently and perfectly transparent to everyone else — no secrets, but also no gossip, no judgment, no consequences, everyone simply indifferent to what they see — nothing of value would be lost, because there was never anything there except the relationships. (Though typing that out I wonder if I'm quietly conflating two different claims, and I'd like you to tell me if I am.)

What I want from you is the other side of it: what would count against the scaffolding view? Not just the reflexive "but people feel violated" stuff — I want the observations, intuitions, cases, and results from adjacent areas that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be. Which of these would a defender of the view (me, I suppose) have to concede counts against them, and which could I wave away as question-begging or as smuggling in the very intrinsic value we're arguing about? Does the view predict anything its rivals don't, or is it just retrodicting? And be honest with me: has a view like this been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability — every apparent counterexample absorbed as "well, that's still scaffolding for *something*" — and if so, is that a fatal problem or just the price of a view this general? I'd especially like to know which single counterexample you'd bet on, and what would have to be true of my imagined transparent society for it to be a genuine loss rather than merely a change. If it helps, I'm definately most moved by cases where the harm seems to attach to the exposure itself rather than to any downstream relationship.
