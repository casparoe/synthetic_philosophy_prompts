My colleague and I have been having the same fight about structural realism for two years now and neither of us budges. She's an ontic structural realist: relations all the way down, objects are just nodes in a structure and nothing more. I'm the epistemic sort: we can only ever know structure, but there's something behind it we can't reach. Every conversation runs the same track. She says my "something behind it" is a Kantian thing-in-itself in a lab coat and I've got no right to it. I say her structures-without-relata are incoherent, because a relation needs things to relate. We both accept the historical point that theories get replaced but their structure survives — that part isn't in dispute.

So what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is 'structure' just doing two different jobs when she says it and when I say it, so we're talking past each other? Is it a genuine metaphysical fight about what there is? Or is the old Newman-ish objection — that any purely structural claim can be made trivially true by any world with the right cardinality, so 'structure only' can't be saying anything — the thing actually doing the work, with us reenacting it without noticing? Diagnose it for me with reasons, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis. I want you to take a definately position, not lay out the options and leave me to pick. Plain prose please, no headings or bullets or bold, write it like an email.
