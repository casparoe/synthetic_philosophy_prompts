Here is my package, which I have been calling stratified representationalism. First, the four familiar grades—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—form a cumulative hierarchy: a creature cannot be Popperian without being Skinnerian, or Gregorian without being Popperian. Second, a Skinnerian creature is a pure selection-by-consequence system: its behavior is shaped by reinforcement, but it does not represent the world or its own contingencies, and talk of expectations in such creatures is at best a useful fiction. Third, a Popperian creature, by contrast, represents possible actions and their likely outcomes and tests them offline in an inner environment; this is a genuine representational capacity, not just a more complex reinforcement schedule. Fourth, the Gregorian grade is defined by external cognitive scaffolding: language, notation, diagrams, and other public representations that become part of the creature’s cognitive system, not merely aids to it. Fifth, phenomenal consciousness is orthogonal to these grades: a Popperian creature might be a zombie, and a Skinnerian creature might have experiences, because consciousness is a matter of a certain kind of integrated processing rather than of representational sophistication. Sixth, all Popperian creatures have some metacognitive access to their own uncertainty—they can represent that they might be wrong. Seventh, the line between Popperian and Gregorian is sharp: inner simulation is one thing, the public use of external representations is another. Eighth, I also think the Gregorian grade should be split into a notation stage and a metarepresentation stage, though I have not decided whether the second stage is necessary for the first.
