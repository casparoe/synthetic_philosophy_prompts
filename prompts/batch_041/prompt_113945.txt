I chair the ethics committee at a research institute that runs both an invertebrate neuroscience lab and a synthetic biology group. A new project proposes to build 'homeostatic agents' — software or soft-bodied devices with nociceptive-like sensors, a pain-avoidance learning rule, and the capacity to trade off present damage against future function — and to put them through aversive conditioning protocols. Our current welfare policy covers only vertebrates and cephalopods and was written on a binary assumption: an entity either has a welfare interest or it does not. The committee is split. Some members say these agents fall somewhere between an insect and a thermostat, and that the binary policy cannot handle the case; others say extending welfare review to artefacts is category confusion and will make the policy unworkable. The deeper question we keep circling is whether sentience comes in degrees and, if so, what threshold should trigger which protections. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Friday's vote. It should recommend a concrete decision on the project — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a time frame and a defensible procedure for future borderline cases. It should engage seriously the strongest argument on the side you decide against, and it should say what, if anything, would change the committee's mind.
