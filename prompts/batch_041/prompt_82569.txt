ok so I'm running a seminar session on philosophy of mind and I want to do Dennett's heterophenomenology through Blade Runner, and I want the reading to actually take the method seriously instead of just using the film as a dunk on it.

Heres my rough take, tell me where I'm wrong. Heterophenomenology says: collect what the subject says about their experience, treat it as sincere report of what they believe, build up a picture of their world the way a critic builds up the world of a novel, and then just never ask whether theres really anybody home behind it. The whole point is that the "anybody home" question isnt a question the science can answer and maybe isnt even a question. Fine. Now the Voight-Kampff test is an empathy test -- it catches replicants by watching their bodies while theyre asked about little stories, cruelty to animals, that kind of thing. So on the one hand its a third-person instrument reading reports and reactions, which is exactly Dennett's method, and the film treats it as the definitive test of humanness. On the other hand Rachel's childhood is implanted, so her reports are perfectly sincere and completely false at once, and under Dennett's rules that isnt a problem at all -- which is either the methods greatest strength or the moment it stops being able to say anything about her. I genuinely cant decide which.

And I think the film cant either, or rather the film wants the opposite answer and cheats to get it. We get the tears in rain speech with the rain and the music swelling and were obviously supposed to be inside Roy's head at the end, which is precisely the first-person access Dennett says the science never gets. And depending on which cut your watching theres the unicorn business with Deckard, which seems built to make the audience panic about a fact that on Dennett's view wouldnt matter even if it were true. So the film stages the heterophenomenological method and then smuggles in the very thing the method brackets.

What I want out of this: where does the film put a real objection to heterophenomenology rather than a sentimental one? I have a hunch theres something in the fact that the test measures empathy specifically -- empathy is a strangely third-personal thing to call the mark of the human, and Dennett would probably say thats all you ever had anyway -- but I dont know how to make that into an argument. And where is the film just being a film, granting us access a heterophenomenologist would say nobody gets? Also, why does the films own procedure (interrogate, observe, record, model the subjects beliefs) look so much like Dennett's, and yet land as horror instead of as science? If you know the novel it came from, the empathy boxes and Mercerism strike me as an even more direct version of the same problem, so say something about that if its relevant.
