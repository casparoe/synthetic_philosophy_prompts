I have spent the past year doing interpretability work on large language models, and I have developed a habit that I suspect is a philosophical error but cannot quite diagnose. When a model produces something ingenious, I catch myself saying that it had a reason for what it did, even while knowing that nothing in the system is doing any reasoning. A colleague recently pointed me toward the older idea of a free-floating rationale: the proposal that natural selection can be said to have a reason for a trait — the reason a bird feigns a broken wing, say, is that doing so draws predators away from its nest — even though no mind ever entertained that reason. As I understand it, the notion is normally cashed out by appeal to a selection history: the rationale floats free because nothing holds it, yet it remains a rationale because selection is answerable to it.

What I cannot find is anyone who has thought carefully about what becomes of the idea when the selecting process is gradient descent on a loss function rather than differential reproduction. Does the rationale stay free-floating, does it become something else, or does the analogy quietly break? I am not after a verdict so much as a research agenda.

Suppose I have three years of a postdoc and a small budget, and I intend to spend them on the conceptual side of this — theory and careful argument, no experiments, and no assumption that I will become an ML researcher. Which open questions about reasons without reasoners should I prioritize, and in what order? I am less interested in the list than in the reasoning: for each question, why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for someone with a philosophy background, and what argument or evidence over the next few years would move it up or down the list. I would also like you to sort the questions into those that are really relabelings of older disputes about function ascription and teleosemantics, and those that are genuinely new once the selector is a gradient rather than a genome. Where one question depends on another, say so explicitly, and tell me which ones I should be willing to drop.
