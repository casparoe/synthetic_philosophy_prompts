I'm in a reading group that's going through Rawls and the utilitarians side by side, and we keep circling back to the separateness-of-persons objection -- the complaint that utilitarianism doesn't take the distinction between persons seriously, becuase it treats everyone's good as one big pool to be totalled up, so that a loss to one person can always be outweighed by a big enough gain to somebody else. My friend Priya got fed up with it last week and wrote this out on the whiteboard, and I haven't been able to shake it:

(1) The objection is that utilitarianism treats different people's good as a single pool to be summed, so that a loss to one person can be outweighed by a larger gain to others.
(2) So taking the distinction between persons seriously means that no person's loss can ever be justified by gains to other people -- a person's claim just can't be traded off against anyone else's.
(3) But every moral view we actually hold does that kind of trading. The difference principle makes the talented accept smaller rewards than they could get in a free market so the worst off are better off. Progressive taxation takes from some people to pay for other people's healthcare. In the trolley case we steer the trolley into the one to save the five, which is a loss imposed on one person for the benefit of others.
(4) So if (2) follows from the objection, the objection condemns the difference principle, public healthcare, and redirecting the trolley.
(5) None of us are willing to give those up, so (2) is false.
(6) And if the objection doesn't get us to (2), then it doesn't forbid anything the utilitarian actually does -- it just comes to saying that utilitarianism is utilitarianism. So the objection is either false or empty.

Priya thinks that's a clean dilemma and I half-believe her. I'd rather keep (1) than (5), but I don't want to keep it by cheating. Where does the argument go wrong, if it does? Name the step you'd give up, explain why that step looked fine until now, and tell me what rejecting it actually costs -- including whether the objection still has any teeth against utilitarianism once I've paid up. If you think there's no bad step and I really am stuck choosing a horn, say which horn and why.
