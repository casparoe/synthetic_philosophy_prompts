I am a high school student and the secretary of my school's physics club, and we are preparing for a debate on the motion that space and time are not infinitely divisible but consist of smallest units. I have been given the opening speech for the affirmative, and I would like you to set out the most important implications of Zeno's paradoxes for that debate. I already know the textbook answer — that the dichotomy and Achilles are dissolved by the mathematics of convergent series — so please do not spend the response on that. What I want is what follows from the paradoxes for the question of whether the world is continuous or granular.

Three claims made by other members of the club pull in different directions, and I would like the implications organised around them. The first is that Zeno's arguments are arguments against motion, and therefore against a continuous world, which would make him an ally of the discrete view. The second is that the arrow is not even a point in favour of continuity: if time is composed of instants — as it is on either view — then at each instant the arrow is simply where it is and is therefore at rest, and a stretch of rest cannot add up to a motion. The third is that the paradoxes reach beyond motion: if a line is composed of points, then a line with length would have to be assembled out of parts with no length, which cannot be right.

Please give me the implications that genuinely bear on the debate, ordered by importance, and for each say what it rules out, what it leaves open, and whether it holds whichever way the debate is settled. If some of what I have been told is a confusion dressed up as a result, say so plainly rather than politely. I am not asking whether Zeno himself would have accepted the discrete view; I am asking what the arguments imply. I do not need references or a reading list, since I have no way to check them and I am working from what I can reason out myself. Finally, please tell me how confident you are in each implication, and single out the one you are least sure of.
