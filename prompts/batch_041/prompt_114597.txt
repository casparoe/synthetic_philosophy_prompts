The question:

"Personal identity unit. In class we labeled three positions. (A) The same-matter view: you persist as long as your original physical parts do, and no amount of functional equivalence makes up for losing your original stuff. (B) The P-continuity view: what makes you the same person over time is overlapping chains of direct psychological connections — memories, intentions, plans, character traits — and it doesn't matter what carries them, meat or silicon. (C) The view that identity is not what matters: what matters is psychological connectedness and continuity with the right kind of cause, and these can hold without identity, or branch.

Now consider two cases. Case Alpha: over a year, a surgeon removes your neurons a few at a time and replaces each with a silicon chip that does exactly what that neuron did — same inputs, same outputs, same role in the network. Between procedures you're awake and talking to your friends, and you say you feel exactly the same. At the end, every original neuron of yours is gone, and what's sitting in the chair is a silicon-based person, S, who remembers your childhood, has your sense of humor, and is in the middle of your college applications. Case Beta: everything is the same, except that each removed neuron is kept alive in a nutrient bath, and at the end the surgeon reassembles all of those original neurons into a working biological brain. The person with that brain, B, also remembers your childhood, has your sense of humor, and is in the middle of your applications. S is still there too, in the next room.

(a) For each of A, B, and C: does the view say S is you? Does it say B is you? (b) Alpha and Beta differ only in what happens to the neurons after they come out. Does that difference matter to any of the three views, and if so which and why? (c) Do these cases support the claim that identity is not what matters, or do they leave it untouched?"

Jordan's answer:

"Okay so this is basically the Ship of Theseus but for people, and we did that one too, so I'll use it.

View A is the one where your stuff is you. So in Alpha, every time a neuron gets swapped out you lose a little more of yourself, and by the end S isn't me at all. There's no exact moment where I die, which is weird and kind of a problem for the view, but it's not my problem. In Beta the surgeon takes all my original neurons and puts them back into a working brain, so B is me, obviously, because B is literally made out of my brain. So view A has no problem with Beta at all, it gives the answer you'd expect.

View B is the psychological one. S remembers my whole life — my third grade teacher, my dog, all of it — and memory is the thing that makes a person the same person (that's the theory we did with Locke), so having all my memories is enough to make S me. Same with B: B has all my memories, so B is me too. But two different people can't both be the same person as me, that's just logic. So in Beta view B gives us a contradiction, and the contradiction means view B is wrong and neither of them is me.

View C is Parfit. Parfit's whole point is that you should only care about your future self if that future self is literally the same person as you, so the important question in Alpha is whether S is identical to me, and he says yes, because the psychological connections are all there and he doesn't care that the chips are silicon instead of neurons. In Beta he's stuck though, because both S and B have the connections, so neither one is identical to me, which means Parfit has to say that in Beta I dont survive at all. So I guess the cases do end up supporting the idea that identity isn't what matters, since in Beta there's no good answer to which one is me.

For (b): the only difference between Alpha and Beta is where the old neurons end up after they come out, and where a pile of matter ends up can't change who someone is, so that difference makes no difference to view B or view C. It makes a huge difference to view A, because view A is the one that cares about matter, and in Beta the matter gets put back together, which is why view A is happy in Beta and unhappy in Alpha."
