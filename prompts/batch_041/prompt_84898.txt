I'm a third-year undergraduate currently taking a course on the foundations of quantum mechanics, and I've run into something in our unit on the reality of the quantum state that I can't reason my way out of. The instructor framed the debate as a choice between psi-ontic views, on which the wavefunction is a genuine element of physical reality, and psi-epistemic views, on which it is a mere representation of knowledge — which I took to mean that a psi-epistemicist is at bottom an instrumentalist about quantum theory rather than a realist. In our discussion section, though, the TA remarked in passing that plenty of psi-epistemicists consider themselves full scientific realists about the quantum world, and that epistemic is being used here in a technical sense; he moved on before I could press him, and I suspect he was speaking loosely. I'd like you to act as an expert in the philosophy of physics and help me get this straight. Please reason carefully rather than reciting a taxonomy — if it helps, start by setting out in your own words what each side is actually committed to — and be blunt wherever I've set the question up badly.

Here is the knot, which I take to be a single question about what epistemic can possibly mean here. Epistemic has to do with knowledge, and knowledge is somebody's: it needs a knower. So if the quantum state is epistemic, whose knowledge is it? It can't be the experimenter's in any straightforward way, since the experimenter may know the preparation perfectly and still not know which underlying physical state the system is in. And if it is nobody's knowledge in particular, then I don't see what work the word epistemic is doing beyond the unremarkable fact that the theory doesn't fix everything. The reply I keep getting is that the quantum state is epistemic in the way a probability distribution in statistical mechanics is — it summarizes incomplete information about an underlying state that is fully objective, fixed by the physical facts and not by anyone's opinion. But that looks to me like a concession rather than a defense: if the distribution is objective and determined by how the world actually is, then it is as real as anything else in the theory, and we seem to be back to psi-onticism under a different name. So, concretely: (a) what exactly is the psi-epistemicist committed to, and does the view owe us a bearer of the knowledge, or is that a demand it can legitimately refuse? And (b) is there a principled difference between a state that is objective but incomplete and a state that is ontic, or does objectivity simply collapse the distinction? I'd also like this folded in: I have a vague memory of a no-go theorem from around 2012 that is supposed to have finished off the epistemic view altogether — please tell me what it actually rules out, under what assumptions, and whether it reaches the versions of the view that people still defend. I would much rather have my framing corrected than answered politely, so please flag any place where the way I've posed this is doing damage.
