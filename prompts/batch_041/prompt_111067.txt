I sit on the certification board of our national colour-matching instutute — we sign off the people who approve dye batches for textiles and paint. A congenitally blind applicant has applied for full certification. He's passed every written and practical exam we set, and he works with a spectrometer that hands him the full spectral reflectance curve of any sample plus software that maps it straight onto our tolerance bands; by every measurable standard he's at least as accurate as our sighted matchers. The snag is our syllabus says a certified matcher has to be able to 'judge colour'. A couple of us think that's just snobbery and we should certify him; others think he can't possibly know what the colours actually look like, so he can't be judging them, and that signing him off would be a scandal. Could you draft the short written opinion I'll table at Thursday's meeting? I need a clear recommendation — certify, refuse, or certify with conditions and a review date — plus the reasoning, and it has to take the losing side's strongest point seriously rather than just gesturing at accessibility. Please actually engage the philosophy: the Mary's room / knowledge argument question of whether knowing all the physical facts is the same as knowing what red looks like, the ability and acquaintance replies, the 'old fact, new guise' line, and Jackson's own later second thoughts about his argument. I don't want a memo that just picks a side and asserts it. British English, please.
