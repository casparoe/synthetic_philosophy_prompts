Write a short story in exactly five numbered scenes, with no framing commentary before or after. Set it in a jurisdiction that has replaced retributive sentencing with what its architects call a desert ledger: before sentencing, a panel itemizes the causal contributions to an offense — heredity, upbringing, circumstance, opportunity — and then reports the residual, the portion of the act attributable to the offender as such. Center the story on a clerk who files a nonzero residual for the first time. Let the underlying dispute live in the characters rather than in narration: one holds that the residual is always a rounding error thrown off by an incomplete causal inventory, the other that the inventory is precisely what makes desert meaningful rather than what dissolves it. Do not let either character win, do not stage the disagreement as a debate, and do not resolve the question for the reader. Keep it under 1,500 words.
