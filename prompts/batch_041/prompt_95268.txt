I'm doing an MSc thing on common-sense reasoning and I've finally hit the frame problem properly, and honestly the more I read the less I feel like I know what a solution would even look like. Everyone from the situation-calculus crowd to the non-monotonic-logic people to the embodied-robotics lot claims to have dissolved it, but they're clearly not all answering the same question, and I've started to suspect half the disagreement is that nobody ever wrote down what the theory is actually supposed to do. So before I go any further: give me the desiderata. What does any adequate account of reasoning about what changes and what stays the same have to deliver? I mean the real list — the cases it has to get right (the one where you shoot the turkey, the gun jams, and somehow the thing is still alive afterwards; the one where nudging a domino topples a whole row; the one where a robot with a bomb and a ticking clock has to work out which of its beliefs even matter right now), the distinctions it can't collapse (between the agent's beliefs and what's actually true, between the narrow technical problem and the broader one about relevance and search control, between an action having no effect and an action having an unknown effect), and the formal constraints it can't dodge (defeasibility, no exhaustive list of exceptions or of frame axioms, tractability, open worlds where you can't just assume the only things that change are the ones you happened to name). For each requirement say what's behind it — which case or which failure forces it — and tell me honestly which of these are non-negotiable and which are just house rules of one camp. If some of them can't be had together, that's the bit I most want: name the incompatibility and say which side gives. Then, briefly, how do the main families do against the list — situation calculus with non-monotonic fix-ups, the planning/STRIPS tradition, probabilistic or decision-theoretic takes, and the relevance-first embodied line? And don't give me a reading list, I can find the papers meself; I want the spec and the diagnosis, not a bibliography.
