I am a novelist at work on a book whose protagonist has adopted a position I have come to call asymmetric tranquilism, and I need to understand what would count against it before I can decide how the book should end. As I define it, asymmetric tranquilism holds that the only thing intrinsically bad is suffering; that craving is bad only insofar as it produces suffering; that the only thing intrinsically good is the absence of craving; and that the absence of suffering is a subordinate good, valuable because it removes an obstacle to tranquility. Positive pleasure, on this view, has no intrinsic value; it matters only as a means of reducing craving or as evidence that craving has subsided. The view also assigns a priority: when relieving suffering and eliminating craving conflict, eliminating craving takes precedence, because suffering is often a symptom of craving and because a life without craving cannot be made worse by the absence of pleasure.

Tell me what would count against this position. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or a survey of the literature; I want the arguments, cases, intuitions, and empirical possibilities that should move someone who holds it. Please rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and explain what makes each one decisive or not. Distinguish what a defender of asymmetric tranquilism would themselves concede as counting against the view from what only an opponent would press. Say what the view predicts that its rivals do not, and whether it risks anything at all or has been insulated by its qualifications. If it can be saved by retreating to a narrower claim, say what that narrower claim would be and what it would cost. I am especially interested in cases where the absence of craving and the absence of suffering come apart—for instance, a creature incapable of craving but in constant, unmodulated pain, or a creature that craves intensely but never suffers because its cravings are always satisfied. What do those cases show, and do they refute the view or only refine it? Finally, tell me whether the position is falsifiable in any meaningful sense, and if it is not, whether that is a defect or simply a feature of a view about what matters. Please write your answer in American English, in continuous prose with clear headings, and keep the tone analytical rather than inspirational.
