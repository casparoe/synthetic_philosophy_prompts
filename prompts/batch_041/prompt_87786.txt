OK so context: I'm a philosophy professor (ethics and political philosophy, mostly) but before I defected I did an MSc in stats, and honestly that's the only technical training I have. I've been roped into running a session on the Principal Principle and deference to objective chance for our grad seminar, and I want to teach it in the dialect I actually think in rather than pretending to be a philosopher of probability. So I want a translation job, and then the interesting part, which is what doesn't survive the translation.

Here's the debate as I (mis)understand it, so you can see what needs restating and can tell me if I've mangled it. The principle itself says roughly: a reasonable initial credence function, conditional on the proposition that the objective chance of A is x and on any evidence that's "admissible", should equal x. Chances are supposed to supervene on the total Humean mosaic — the whole arrangement of local matters of fact, past and future both — and that's what generates the nasty case. Some futures are such that, if they came about, the present chance facts would have been different; call those undermining futures. A Humean chance function looks like it can give them positive probability, but you can't have positive credence in something that would make the proposition you've conditioned on false, so the principle looks inconsistent. I think one response is that the undermining futures have to come out with chance zero, and another (I've seen it called the New Principle) is that the principle is just mis-stated and you should defer to the complete theory of chance rather than to the chance of the particular proposition. Correct me if I've mangled any of that.

What I want: restate the whole thing — the principle, the admissibility clause, the undermining problem, and both repairs — in the vocabulary I actually have. Priors and posteriors, parameters versus data, de Finetti exchangeability, sufficient statistics, regular conditional distributions, calibration, martingales if you need them. I'm fluent in that stuff up to a master's level but I'm not a probabilist by trade, so dont assume measure theory past conditional expectations. Treat chance as a parameter, treat credence as a prior, and see how far it goes before it breaks.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which bits translate cleanly? My guess is that admissibility is doing something like sufficiency or exogeneity, and that the de Finetti picture already hands you a version of deference for free, since conditioning on the limiting frequency makes the sequence iid. Which bits only translate with distortion? And which bits have no counterpart at all — in particular, does the statistical toolkit have anything at all that expresses a norm binding every rational agent, as opposed to a constraint that's relative to whatever prior the agent happens to have? If there's real residue left over, tell me wether that's my vocabulary being impoverished or the original claim being less clear than it looks.

Is that the undermining future in my dialect, or a different animal? And does "condition on the whole theory of chance" become "condition on the parameter"?
