My job is to run the analytical desk at a small policy shop that advises foundations on long-horizon grants, and once a month I have to sign off on one-page assessments of whether some current regime, empire, or hegemonic order will still be standing in ten, twenty-five, and fifty years. My analysts write these as narratives -- leadership, fiscal position, restive periphery -- and then attach a number they had effectively chosen before they started. I have three days of their time, they have no statistical training beyond arithmetic, and the foundations do act on what we send them. I want to replace the narrative with a procedure.

Write the procedure. In order, it should tell them how to fix what entity's survival is in question, how to assemble the comparison set of past cases, how to turn that set into a survival base rate and what to do when the cases in it disagree by orders of magnitude, how to judge whether the case in front of them belongs in that set at all, and how to phrase a final answer that keeps separate what the base rate supports from what the inside view adds. Two difficulties have to be handled rather than waved away, because my analysts will hit them within an hour. First, any entity belongs to indefinitely many comparison sets -- all states, all land empires, all federal unions, all commodity exporters -- and the choice of set largely determines the answer, so the procedure needs either a rule for choosing or an honest way of reporting across choices. Second, the historical record is stocked with entities that were still standing when their records stopped and with entities that are in it only because someone saw fit to name them, so a plain average of past durations is not a survival base rate.

Then run it on two cases: a large multi-ethnic federation that has held together for seventy years and is now under fiscal strain, and a small city-state that has outlived four empires that conquered its region. At least one of the two must come out in a way the foundation officer who commissioned it will not want to hear. Finally, mark the points where the procedure must stop and hand the decision back to me -- the cases where there is no usable reference class, and the cases where any number I publish would be rhetoric wearing the costume of a base rate. Do not send me a reading list; where the procedure turns on a distinction, state the distinction.
