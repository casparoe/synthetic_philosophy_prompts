My brother-in-law is a doctor and he's dragged me into an argument I can't climb out of. He thinks it's just obvious that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg. The particular sperm that won its race and the particular egg it happened to hit are essential to you; in any world where a different one got there first, you were never born. Okay, I basically buy that. But then he runs it out into something I can't swallow, and I'm sure something slips somewhere, I just can't put my finger on it. Here's his chain:

(1) You could not have come from a different sperm and egg. If a different sperm had fertilised that egg, or that sperm had hit a different egg, the resulting person is not you.

(2) Your genome was fixed the instant that sperm and egg fused — nothing afterward changes it. So if a different sperm or egg had fused, you would have had a different genome.

(3) From (1) and (2): your genome is part of what you are. You could not have had a different genome.

(4) Your genome, together with the environment you grew up in, made you what you are — your height, your health, your temperament, all of it. So every one of those things came from your origin.

(5) If a trait came from your origin, and your origin couldn't have been different, then the trait couldn't have been different either. That's just the same reasoning that made (1) true in the first place.

(6) So you couldn't have been taller, or healthier, or of a different temperament. And the world you grew up in is equally essential, since the same genome raised in a different world gives you a different person.

(7) Generalise: every fact about you traces back to your origin one way or another, so every fact about you is essential. There is no respect in which you could have been otherwise.

(8) But that's plainly wrong. I could've gone to med school instead of law school, I could've been an inch taller if I'd eaten better as a kid, I could've been a kinder person. So (1) has to be false after all — you could have come from a different sperm and egg.

(His favourite extra detail, which I admit is what makes it feel airtight: your mother's egg had been sitting in her ovary since before she was born, and the sperm that won was one of a few hundred million and it wasn't even the fastest, just the lucky one. So "your origin" bottoms out in a coin flip.)

I'd much rather keep (1) than (8). (1) feels true to me, and (8) feels like giving up on my being anyone in particular. So if the mistake is upstream, tell me which step it's on, why that step looked so fine, and what it costs me to keep (1). And if the answer is that some earlier premise has to go instead, say which and price that too.
