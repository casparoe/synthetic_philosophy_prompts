Two months ago I became director of programmes at a small foundation that funds public-health work in a dozen districts, and on Thursday I chair my first allocation meeting. Our practice is to choose between two competing designs for the same pot of money, and the previous director, who made those calls for eleven years, left minutes recording her preference in each case; the projections are given as healthy life-years gained per ten thousand residents in each district a proposal would touch, and within any pair the two designs always cover the same districts. Three of her decisions are still argued about in the office. She chose (10, 10) over (20, 4). She chose (12, 12, 12) over (40, 8, 4). And she chose (10, 6) over (5, 5). Everyone agrees she was not simply maximising the total; beyond that the board splits, and one trustee who used to teach philosophy says her rule was prioritarian while another says she was always willing to level down.

The decision in front of me is between two designs for the same money: Design A projects (10, 10, 10) and Design B projects (10, 10, 34). B leaves the first two districts exactly where A leaves them and does far better in the third, so no district is worse off under B; most of the board treats that as decisive, and I want to know whether my predecessor would have.

So: predict her choice between A and B, tell me which rule or family of rules actually fits her three recorded decisions, what each of those candidates predicts here, and which single further case would best separate the survivors. If her record genuinely leaves the matter open, say so, and tell me what she would be committing herself to on each reading, including the reading that makes her look worst. I have to defend my decision to a board that remembers her reasoning, and I would rather argue against the strongest version of the view I reject than against a caricature. In particular I would like your honest verdict on whether "prioritarian" or "willing to level down" is the better description of the record, and what in the record forces the issue.
