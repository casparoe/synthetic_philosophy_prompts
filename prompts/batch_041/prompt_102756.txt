Write a mock advertisement and FAQ, about 600 words, for a start-up that promises to help clients collect the prize in Kavka's toxin puzzle. The venue is a glossy corporate landing page, deadpan throughout, and the humor must come from getting the puzzle's structure exactly right rather than from winking at it: the copy should never name the puzzle or explain it directly, yet a reader who knows it ought to be able to reconstruct the whole setup from the fine print. Have the firm's numbered clauses work through the beats the puzzle actually turns on — that the reward attaches to the intention rather than to the act, so the client is paid for a state of mind he will later have every reason to betray; that a client cannot simply resolve to intend, since a resolve he already knows he will break is not an intention; the difference between intending to do something and merely predicting that one will do it; whether an intention formed by a paid agent can count as the client's own or only as the firm's, and what the reward-giver would say about that; and the embarrassment of a money-back guarantee that pays out only if the client actually goes through with it. Close with a satisfaction clause whose conditions quietly concede the sting, and let the whole page stay straight-faced — the target is the temptation to treat deciding to intend as a solution, not any named person.
