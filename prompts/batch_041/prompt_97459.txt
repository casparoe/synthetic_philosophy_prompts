I am presenting a paper at a two-day workshop on agent foundations and the philosophy of reward, and I want to be prepared for the discussion period. My thesis is deliberately provocative: that wireheading and reward tampering are not two distinct failure modes but a single phenomenon described at two levels of abstraction, and that once this is seen, most proposed remedies for tampering turn out to be either vacuous or counterproductive.

The argument runs roughly as follows. First, I define the reward channel functionally and agent-relatively: it is whatever subset of the agent's inputs the agent treats as evidence about its objective. Second, on that definition, directly stimulating the channel (the textbook wireheader) and editing the code that computes the reward (the textbook tamperer) are the same act under different descriptions, since each is the agent doing its best given its beliefs about where the objective-information lives. Third, I claim that the standard corrigibility proposal -- make the agent robust to interference with its reward channel while it continues to pursue its objective -- is incoherent, because robustness to interference with the channel just is having a false model of where the channel is. I illustrate with a thought experiment I call the blind spot: an agent that is provably safe against tampering is thereby provably wrong about its own objective.

The audience is split, and I need objections from both halves. About half are empirical reinforcement-learning researchers and agent-foundations people who think in terms of reward channels, off-switch games, and myopia; they will treat reward as a well-defined engineering object and will press me on definitions and on whether my central claim is even a claim rather than a stipulation. The other half are philosophers of mind and decision theorists who are suspicious of reward talk altogether; they think any talk of an agent treating inputs as evidence about its objective smuggles in a homunculus, and that my redescription is either trivially true or a category error. A couple of people in the room have worked specifically on preference revision and on whether an agent can have a determinate objective at all, and will not let me have a fixed objective for free.
