I've been going round in circles about the cogito for weeks and I'd like to actually pin down what it proves, becuase I keep flip-flopping. Rather than ask you what Descartes meant, let me just give you my gut verdicts on a pile of cases and ask what principle, if any, they add up to.

First, the little test I use on myself, which might help you see where I'm coming from. Whenever I'm tempted to say the cogito proves some X, I ask: could there be thinking going on with nothing for X to be true of? If yes, then it doesn't prove X. I'm fairly sure I've pinched this from someone.

My verdicts:

- Demon scenario, everything about the world a lie. That something is being thought right now: rock solid, can't be shaken. That I existed an hour ago: not proved in the slightest. That there's a body somewhere: not proved.
- Waking from a dream: I want to say there was thinking going on in the dream, but no one it happened to — the dream had no owner. So bare thinking with no thinker seems possible to me.
- Split-brain patient: it seems to me the cogito could run twice over, once per hemisphere, so it can't prove the 'I' is one thing. If anything it's evidence against.
- Thought insertion: a patient reports that thoughts are being put into his head, and I'm inclined to believe him. So there's thinking, and it isn't his in the sense of being his doing. But it still seems to be present to someone.
- Under general anaesthetic: no thinking, so nothing proved. Fine, no drama.
- A woman who has lost the ability to say or think the word 'I' but is plainly still thinking away: I think the cogito still works for her, which is odd, since the argument's whole first-person grammar is missing.
- Two people whose streams of thought get wired together so that each has the other's thoughts as immediately as their own: I genuinely don't know whether that's two cogitos or one.

My hunch, which I'd like you to test: the cogito proves only that thinking is occurring — a bare occurrence, no owner — and the 'I' is smuggled in by grammar. That gets my dream and insertion verdicts. But it doesn't get the thing I'm most stubborn about: in the demon scenario I feel I know that there's something it is like to be me right now, not merely that thoughts are occurring but that they're had. I can't tell whether that's an extra thing the cogito proves or just my inability to imagine thinking that belongs to no one.

So: is there a principle that gets all of this, or close, and which of my verdicts does it make me give up?
