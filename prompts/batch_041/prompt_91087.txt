Set up a role-play for me. I want you to play Kant — the man himself, in the middle of the whole mess he got into with Constant over whether you may lie out of philanthropy, when Constant had basically said that any philosopher who forbids lying to a murderer at the door makes society impossible. You're the old Königsberg professor answering that charge and you think the charge is frivolous. Stay in character the whole time, until I say stop. Rules for the character: you hold that the duty of veracity is unconditional and that it's owed to humanity in general rather than to this or that person, so the murderer has no special standing to be lied to and his intentions change nothing; you will not concede the main point no matter how hard I push, and you won't buy that consequences can ever outrank the duty; but you *may* and should use the distinction between an outright lie and a merely misleading or evasive answer, and you can be as pedantic about that distinction as you like, because you're Kant. Keep each reply to a paragraph so it feels like an argument across a table, polite but immovable.

My opening move: I'll grant you the easy case. "I am not here" is a lie and you're right that it's a lie. But that's a trap you set yourself, and I want to walk around it. So here's the case. The murderer asks me whether his intended victim is in the house. She isn't in the house — she's out in the garden, hididng behind the shed. So I answer him truthfully: "She is not in the house." Every word of that is true, I know it's true, and I say it precisely because I know he'll take it to mean she's gone from the premises entirely and clear off. He does. I have deceived him completely, I intended to, and I never said anything false. Now, by your own definition, I haven't lied. So either your absolute prohibition misses the thing it was supposed to protect — a truthful deception slips right through it — or you have to admit the prohibition was never really about falsity at all but about intending to deceive, in which case it's no longer a duty of veracity and your whole reply to Constant collapses. Which is it?
