Bit of an odd one, this. I've got no philosophy background whatsoever, I just argue about this stuff in the pub, and I've started writing my answers down so I can see whether I'm actually consistent. Me and my mates keep going round in circles on one question: when is it ok for a bunch of people to overrule one person about that person's own business? My mate Dan did an economics degree and swears there's some old proof that you cant have both 'everyone decides their own private business' and 'if everyone prefers one option to the other, the one everyone prefers should win'. I never know what to say to that, and it definately feels like its at the bottom of why we never get anywhere.

So here's my gut on a load of cases. I'd like to know what principle actually fits, if any.

- My neighbour paints her front door bright orange. The whole street hates it, everyone preferred it white. I think she should be allowed. It's her door.
- Same neighbour wants to chop down the huge oak in her back garden, the one you can see from the whole lane that half the village is fond of. I think the village should be able to stop her. I'm aware this looks like it contradicts the door one.
- My friend wants a big tattoo across his face. All of us think it's a terrible idea and he'll regret it. I think he should get it.
- Another friend wants to blow his entire savings on a boat. I think he's an adult and it's his money, but if his wife is dead against it I also think she should get a say, and I can't tell you why that's different from the tattoo.
- Two friends want to get married. Both families think it's a disaster and say so to anyone who'll listen. I think the couple should get married and the families should shut up.
- Parents want to name their kid something that will get him bullied his whole life. Everyone who hears it winces. Half of me says the parents decide, half of me says someone should be able to stop them.
- An old man wants to refuse a blood transfusion that would save his life. His whole family is begging him. I think it's his call, and I feel bad about that.
- A bloke in our building is a nudist in his own flat, curtains shut, nobody ever sees anything. Some neighbours know and are uncomfortable about it. I don't think that should count for anything at all.
- Two elderly sisters want to sell the family farm to a developer. Nobody's harmed, the village just doesn't like it. I think the sisters should do what they want.
- Everyone at work wants the office at 20 degrees, one woman keeps turning it up to 24 because she's always cold. I think she should put a jumper on, even though it's about her own comfort.

I keep landing on 'only count the opinions of people it actually affects' and then the oak tree and the kid's name break it. I tried 'the group can only veto something if it can't be undone' - the door can be repainted so she wins, the tree can't come back so the village wins - but then the face tattoo and the boat and the old man's transfusion should all get vetoed too and I don't believe that. I've started calling the door kind 'spectator cases', where people are only upset because they have to look at it, but I don't know if that line holds up either.

Can you tell me what I actually believe? I want a principle, or two or three of them, stated as sharply as you can, with the case that forces each clause in, and what it says about cases I haven't listed. And tell me straight which of my answers has to go, because I don't think they can all survive. If it comes down to giving up either 'everyone rules their own life' or 'the majority rules', say which one the rest of my answers say I should give up, and if that's what that old proof is about then explain it to me without the maths.
