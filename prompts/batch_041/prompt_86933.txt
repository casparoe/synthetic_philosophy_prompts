I want to know what the Catholic practice of canonization is philosophically committed to. To be declared a saint, a person must be shown to have practiced the theological virtues — faith, hope and charity — to a heroic degree; for a candidate who did not die as a martyr, one miracle must be confirmed as having occurred through their intercession before beatification, and a further miracle before canonization; and the case is argued by a postulator who promotes the cause and, until its role was reduced in 1983, was contested by a promotor fidei, the 'devil's advocate', whose task was to look for flaws in the evidence. The candidate is always dead before any of this is settled, and what is conferred is public veneration and the right to invoke their intercession, not any benefit to the person. There is also a shortcut: by 'equivalent canonization' the pope may dispense with the judicial process and the miracles altogether, on the strength of a long-standing cult. Meanwhile the Church teaches that holiness is the vocation of everyone, not of a select few; beatification is treated as a permission and a fallible judgment, while canonization is widely held to be an infallible act. I find it hard to tell, from all this, whether sanctity is being treated as an achievement or as a gift.

What theory of maximal moral goodness does this practice presuppose, and does it treat that goodness as desirable in itself or only for what it secures? Please reconstruct the theory; separate what the practice genuinely requires from what it merely sits comfortably with; say which rival views of sanctity it quietly rules out — that sanctity is continuous with ordinary virtue, that it is the person's own doing rather than a gift, that it is a purely ethical rather than a theological property, that it is the kind of thing that cannot be known with certainty; say where the practice is underdetermined, so that several different theories would license the same institution; and say what would have to change in the practice if the presupposed theory were replaced. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
