I'm presenting at a small workshop on decision-making under uncertainty, and I want a Q&A prep sheet for the discussion afterward. My thesis: transformative choices — the ones where you cannot know in advance what the outcome will be like, and where the very standards by which you would judge it will have shifted by the time you are in a position to judge — actually split into two kinds of ignorance that need separate treatment. The first, experiential ignorance, is a matter of missing information, and testimony from people who have already undergone the change can partly close it. The second, which I call evaluative drift, is not like that: no testimony helps, because the person receiving the testimony is not the person who will be doing the judging. My proposal is that choices dominated by evaluative drift should be made by what I call narrative fit — selecting the option that best coheres with the life you are willing to author — rather than by maximizing anticipated welfare.

The audience is a mix. Two decision theorists will treat any such proposal as expected-utility-with-exotic-utilities until proven otherwise. A welfare economist holds that preference change makes the whole question incoherent. And a clinician in the room will keep asking what any of this actually tells a patient who has to decide by Friday.

Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I will actually get, phrased in each of their vocabularies, ordered by how much damage they do, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft; what I should concede up front; and the one question I am hoping nobody asks.
