I've been going around in circles about whether democracy is actually the best way to make decisions, or whether it'd be better if the people who actually know what they're talking about had more say. Everyone I bring it up with either says thats just elitism, next, or says well obviously, would you let the public vote on your own surgery, and neither reply gets me anywhere. So I want to actually line up the concrete setups people propose instead of arguing about the vibe. Here's my list, and what I want is for you to sort them into tiers (S/A/B/C or just best to worst is fine) by how much you'd trust each one to produce better decisions than a normal elected parliament, one sentence per item on why it lands where it does. The thing that worries me most is capture — the setup quietly ending up controlled by rich people, or by the already-educated, or by whoever runs the scoring — so please flag for each one how exposed it is to that, and say which of the six you'd actually be willing to try first if you had to pick one.

The list: (1) keep the elected parliament, but add a second chamber of randomly picked citizens — they get paid, get a month of briefings from experts, no parties and no campaigning, and a bill needs both chambers. (2) Extra votes for anyone who passes a general knowledge and civics test — free, no education or wealth requirement, retakeable as many times as you want. (3) Elected people still pass the laws, but a panel of relevant experts can block a law if they judge it factually wrong, with their reasons published — economists for the budget, doctors for health stuff, and so on. (4) A lottery everyone can enter, and the winners get a two week crash course and then are the legislature for a term. (5) Your votes weight depends on your track record — a nonpartisan body scores how well you called past outcomes (inflation, crime, whether a policy worked), and people who keep getting it right get more weight. (6) A council of forecasters that publishes predictions about what each policy will do, and parliament needs a supermajority to go against them.

Also curious whether you think any of these are worse than what we've already got, cause I keep suspecting a couple of them would be, and I dont want to be talked into something just because it sounds smarter than voting.
