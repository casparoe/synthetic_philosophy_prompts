I'm a novelist, and through a series of bad decisions I'm now on the board of my kid's school -- small, progressive, endlessly proud of its "culture of accountability." The school runs a points system: kids earn marks for kindness, punctuality, participation, lose them for whatever, and the total quietly determines privileges -- field trips, elective picks, who gets to be a house captain. It's all logged in an app the teachers and parents can scroll. Last month a kid got bumped down a level and lost her robotics slot over an incident in the hallway that nobody will specify, because the system records "pattern flags" rather than events. Family's furious, half the faculty loves the system, and I have to draft teh board's opinion before Thursday.

So: draft that opinion. It should actually recommend something -- keep it, kill it, or keep it with named changes and a timeline -- and it has to take the strongest case for the system seriously, not just wave the word surveillance around. What I keep circling is Foucault on discipline: how a system like this doesn't merely punish kids, it manufactures a certain kind of kid and a certain kind of official knowledge about them, and the two feed each other. But the other trustees won't sit through a seminar, so the theory has to earn its keep in concrete recommendations.

Also, please be straight with me about what's actually settled versus what's genuinely contested among people who know this material -- don't hand me a line as consensus if serious people disagree, and flag where I'm just pattern-matching onto Foucault because it sounds good.
