I have spent an unreasonable amount of time in rationalist decision-theory threads, and I keep watching the same move. Someone proposes a non-expected-utility rule — most recently risk-weighted expected utility, in which the agent's attitude toward risk is represented by a transformation of cumulative probabilities rather than being absorbed into the curvature of the utility function — and a commenter replies, in effect, that rationality just is maximizing expected utility, as though that settles the matter. I want to know what that sentence could actually be claiming. Lay out the distinct precise theses it might express, ordered from the nearly trivial to the radical, and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or simply false. In particular, distinguish readings on which expected utility maximization is a definition of rationality, a representation theorem conditional on axioms, a normative requirement, a descriptive generalization, a claim about the proper objects of utility, and a claim about where risk attitudes must be located in the representation. Note which readings are equivalent, which entail which, and which are compatible with risk-weighted expected utility. Then tell me which of those readings would actually support dismissing risk-weighted expected utility, and which would leave it untouched or even presuppose it. I am not looking for a general defense of expected utility theory, nor for a reading list; I want the menu of readings, with verdicts, so I can see which one the commenter must have in mind and whether that reading earns the dismissal.
