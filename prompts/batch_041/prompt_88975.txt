I'm a PhD student splitting time between stats and philosophy, and I'm in a reading group on imprecise probabilities that has turned into a weekly argument. I've been trying to write down my own view before I present next week, and I keep worrying that I've stitched together a Frankenstein. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Rational credal states are sets of probability functions, not single functions. The set can be very wide when evidence is sparse, and that's not a defect.
(2) When you learn E, you update by conditionalizing every member of the set on E (we can set aside the zero-probability case).
(3) In decisions, I use maximin: pick the act whose worst-case expected utility over the set is highest.
(4) I accept the sure-thing principle: if A is at least as good as B conditional on E, and also at least as good as B conditional on not-E, then A is at least as good as B unconditionally.
(5) I accept the value of information: if you can get a costless, truthful piece of evidence before you decide, you should weakly prefer to have it, or at least never prefer to avoid it.
(6) I think dilation is real and rational. The case we've been stuck on: a coin with unknown bias, a witness whose reliability is unknown, the witness says 'heads', and your interval for heads can widen, even to [0,1]. I think that's correct, not a bug.
(7) I have a strong intuition that if E is independent of H given the true value of some parameter, then learning E shouldn't make me less confident in H. Dilation seems to violate that, though I suspect the 'given the true value' part is doing a lot of work.
(8) I think a fair coin should get a precise credence of 0.5, even though I'm an imprecise probabilist.

I suspect some of these are pulling against each other, but I can't tell which. Some might only look inconsistent and actually be fine. Can you audit the package? I'd like the tensions ranked by severity rather than a flat list, with an honest note on which pairs are only apparently in tension and why. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would be, and what you'd lose by making it. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I definately don't have a strong prior about which one is the culprit, so please don't assume I'd rather keep any particular item.
