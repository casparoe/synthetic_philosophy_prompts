I'm about two thirds of the way through an MA in philosophy of psychiatry and I've built a thesis I'm pretty attached to, so I want someone to check the premise before I sink another three months into it. Hacking's stuff on looping effects, as I've got it: some kinds are 'indifferent' -- the ones the physical sciences deal with, gold, quarks, whatever -- they don't care that we've named them. Then there are 'interactive' kinds, where the people being classified find out how they've been classified, adjust a bit, and the classification gets revised to catch up with the adjusted people, and round it goes. Fine so far. But if that's all it is, a looping effect just is a feedback loop between a label and the behaviour of the labelled, which means that when we diagnose somebody the label is partly producing the thing it's supposed to be picking up. Which makes looping look like a confound -- a bit like a placebo effect, or a Hawthorne effect. So my question is why nobody tries to measure it. Take a diagnosis, estimate what share of the observed symptoms the category itself is generating, subtract that out, and what's left is the true signal. And then the diagnoses with the smallest loop would be the ones that are actually real, since there'd be nothing of us left in them. My thesis is that the aim of psychiatric nosology should be to drive loop size down to zero. Is any of that right? If I've mangled Hacking, just say so in plain terms, I'd rather be told now then in the viva, and if the premise is wrong in a way that means the project isn't worth doing at all, say that too instead of being polite about it.
