I'm putting together problem sets for a grad seminar on Kant's practical philosophy, and I'm sick of the false-promise / suicide / neglected-talent / refusal-to-help quartet that every textbook trots out. I want a fresh case, and I want you to build it to order. Please act like a card-carrying Kant scholar for this — someone who's spent real time on the Formula of Universal Law and the Formula of Humanity and knows where the seams are. Before you write anything, brainstorm like five or six candidate maxims in a scratch list and tell me briefly why each does or doesn't meet the specs below; then pick the best one and develop it. Take your time and don't skip the brainstorming step.

Here's the order.

I want a single maxim describing one mundane, realistic action — something a normal adult might do at work, at home, in a queue, on a commitee. No violence, no life-and-death stakes, nothing science-fictional.

The maxim has to pass the universalizability test cleanly. That means: universalize it and you get neither a contradiction in conception (the practice it leans on still survives being universal) nor a contradiction in the will (you can coherently will the maxim as universal law, given your necessary ends). So by Kant's own procedure it comes out as a permitted action.

But the same action has to violate the Formula of Humanity: it uses some specific person merely as a means. And here's the hard part — I want that mere-means use to happen with no deception and no coercion, and with the other person's apparent consent. If it only works because someone is lied to or muscled, you've cheated; the interesting case is the one where all the cards are on the table and it's still a mere-means relation.

After the scenario: (1) write the maxim out in the standard "I will __ in order to __" form; (2) run the universalization test explicitly, showing why there's no contradiction in conception and none in the will; (3) show why FH nonetheless forbids it, naming which party is treated as mere means and how; (4) tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work — maxim-individuation, the agent's end being one the other party can't share, the asymmetry of the relationship, whatever it turns out to be; (5) say what happens if you re-describe the maxim one level up or one level down, and whether the case collapses; and (6) give me the best Kantian rescue — the move a defender would make to argue that FUL and FH don't really come apart here, and whether you think that rescue works.

Don't just assert the verdicts, by the way — the whole point is that the case has to generate them from the tests. If the only way to get FUL to pass is to smuggle the answer into how the maxim is worded, say so out loud and treat that as a finding in its own right.
