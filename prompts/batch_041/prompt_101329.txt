I've got two friends who've been going at this for months and I want to force them to write something together instead. One of them is a cohort realist — she thinks "Gen Z" and "Boomers" pick out real kinds in the world, in the way that species or social class are supposed to, that there's a fact of the matter about which one you're in, and that they do genuine explanatory work in sociology and politics rather than just labelling a chart. The other is a skeptic — he thinks their just marketing categories, gerrymandered on arbitrary birth-year cutoffs, and that anything that looks like a "generational" difference is really an age effect or a period effect wearing a costume.

Write the joint statement the two of them could both put their names to after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want a numbered section of agreed claims — about what a cohort even is, about what would make one a real kind rather than a label of convenience, about what the demographic data can and can't settle on its own, and about where each of them concedes the other has a point. Then the precise residual disagreements, each one phrased the way that side itself would want to word its own view. Then the cruxes, and for each crux the argument or piece of evidence both of them agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about it written down in advance.

Two things I want you to build in rather than skip past. First, the demographic version of the identification problem — that how old you are, what year you were born, and what year it is now are tied together in a way that makes them hard to pull apart, so any "cohort effect" you report is partly a modelling choice. Second, the difference between a whole birth cohort and the smaller slice inside it that actually articulates a shared style or sensibility and ends up speaking for the rest — I suspect one of them will want that distinction to rescue the realist view and the other will want it to sink it. Work out which.

If it turns out that part of what they've been arguing about is just two different senses of "real", say so, and say which part. And finish by telling me where your drafting is most likely to be wrong: which agreed claim or crux you'd word differently on a second pass, or which crux you'd drop entirely.
