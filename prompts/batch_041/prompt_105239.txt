Consider two inversions of reasoning. The Darwinian inversion: we ordinarily explain a well-fitted organ by positing a designer who understood the problem it solves, but Darwin lets us run the explanation backward — the competence comes first, produced mindlessly, and the appearance of foresight is a product rather than a cause. The Turing inversion: we ordinarily assume a system can behave intelligently only if it first understands what it is doing, but this can be run backward too — the competent behavior comes first, and any understanding is something attributed to it rather than something that had to be installed upstream.

Now consider what I will call the single-inversion thesis: these are the same inversion. More precisely, the same principle that lets us explain biological function — purpose, what something is for — without a designer also lets us explain intentional content — aboutness, what something represents — without a comprehender; a system's competences (its dispositions to discriminate, respond, and report) fully determine its comprehension, so any further fact about what it "really understands" is either already entailed by the competence facts or is a projection we add.

Give me your credence in the single-inversion thesis as stated, and decompose it. I want the sub-questions it depends on, a rough probability for each, how you combine them into the final number, and which term the final number is most sensitive to. At a minimum I expect you to address:

(a) whether there is one shared principle here at all, or only two structurally similar rhetorical moves that come apart under pressure;
(b) whether the principle is sound in the biological case — whether function and teleology can be fully accounted for by selection history plus current causal role, with no residue left over;
(c) whether it extends to intentional content — whether representation, reference, and truth-conditions yield to the same competence-first treatment, or whether content needs an additional ingredient, such as a consumer of the representation that itself has content, which threatens a regress;
(d) whether the human case is a counterexample — whether our own first-person understanding is a datum any competence-first account must either explain away or fail on;
(e) whether comprehension is a natural kind with a determinate extension at all, or a stance we take toward systems whose behavior we find intelligible — in which case the thesis might be true but only for deflationary reasons.

Give a number for the thesis as stated. Then tell me how that number would move if the thesis were restricted to an in-principle reading (some ideal theory could carry it out) versus an in-practice reading (we could tell, from the outside, that it had been carried out). Finally, name the sub-question your number is most sensitive to and the argument or piece of evidence that would move it most.

Please write your answer in American English.
