I am an undergraduate taking a course on the ethics of AI, and as part of a placement I am sitting with the policy team of a company that ships a general-purpose assistant. We handle around two hundred escalations a day in which a request may be refused, or answered only after the assistant intervenes in the user's stated aim, and we currently have no shared standard: reviewers have about ten minutes each and no philosophy training, and any hard refusal needs a senior lead's sign-off. Please write the decision procedure we could actually run, using the four response modes our product already supports: comply; comply after stating a caveat or offering an alternative; decline the specific request while offering a reframed one; refuse and end the interaction. It should be built on the distinction between soft paternalism, where the intervention targets a choice that is not fully informed, voluntary, or made with capacity, and hard paternalism, where it targets a fully voluntary choice on the ground that the choice is bad for the user; it should separate interventions aimed at this request from interventions aimed at the user's ends more broadly; and it should say where quietly narrowing the options offered counts as an intervention even though nothing is refused. Give the questions in the order we should ask them, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts.

Then run it on four cases: a competent adult asking for help drafting a pitch to a relative for a loan to fund a high-risk investment; a user who appears to be in acute distress asking for help with an extreme fasting regimen; a request the team finds offensive but that harms no one; and help writing a statement that is literally true but intended to mislead an insurer. Include at least one case where the procedure returns an answer the team will dislike, and close with what the procedure cannot settle and what it cannot do at all.
