Ok so I've been lurking on LessWrong and adjacent forums for the better part of a decade, and there's one move I keep seeing that's slowly driving me up the wall.

Somebody starts a thread about the simulation argument, or Boltzmann brains, or just plain Cartesian doubt about the external world. And somebody else eventually drops the butterfly thing — you know the one, where you supposedly can't tell whether you're a person who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly who's now dreaming he's a person — and then acts like that settles the whole thread. Sometimes it's deployed to mean 'so we can't know anything about reality,' sometimes 'so all experiences are equally real, who cares,' sometimes 'so stop asking, the sage doesn't bother with that question,' and once, memorably, as a proof that the self isn't even continuous across a single night's sleep.

My suspicion is that these are not the same claim wearing different hats, and that at least a couple of them aren't even claims the story supports.

I want you to be a philosopher of mind who also knows the Chinese/Buddhist side of this literature cold, and who's good at pulling apart positions that look identical to a layperson. Go slow. Also please don't just reach for the textbook Descartes thing, I've read the textbook Descartes thing, that's the regurgitated version and it's exactly what I'm trying to get past.

Before you write anything, spend a paragraph brainstorming every distinct precise reading of the one-liner you can generate, even the dumb ones, even the ones that make it sound trivial. Then give me the menu.

For each reading I want:
- the exact thesis, stated so a pedant couldn't object
- what it quantifies over (this moment? all moments? the reliability of experience in general? the reality of the distinction rather than the reality of any particular world?)
- a verdict: trivial / plausible-but-contentious / defensible-if-you-pay-a-price / flatly false
- and whether it would actually get you to the conclusion the person in the thread wanted. This is the part I care about most. A reading that's true but too weak to support 'we can't be justified in our beliefs about reality' is useless as a thread-ender, even if it's the philosophically respectable one.

Then, seperately: which reading do people *usually* mean when they repeat it, and which reading (if any) actually licenses the skeptical conclusion? And finally, which of these readings the original story can plausibly bear vs which are just bolted on by people who've only ever seen the meme. If the ending of the story matters here — the part about things transforming — say so, because I have a hunch a lot of people are quoting a version that's had the frame narrative stripped out, and I want to know if that changes which reading is available.

No bibliography, no reading list, just the analysis. And feel free to tell me bluntly which readings are cope.
