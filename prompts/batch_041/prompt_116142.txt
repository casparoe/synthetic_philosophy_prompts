I serve on my university's honorary degrees committee, and we are currently considering a nomination that has forced me to think carefully about the ethics of killing in war. The nominee is a former Secretary of Defense who, during his tenure, authorized a drone strike against a terrorist leader. The strike was aimed at the leader, who was a legitimate military target, but it was carried out on a house where his family was present, and the family—his wife and two children, none of whom were combatants—were killed. The Secretary has said that the family's deaths were a foreseeable but unintended side effect of the operation, and that the strike was the only feasible way to kill the leader, who was planning further attacks.

I have been reading Elizabeth Anscombe, because she is the philosopher who famously opposed Oxford's decision to award an honorary degree to Harry Truman on the grounds that he was a murderer for authorizing the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. I am trying to figure out what she would say about our nominee. From her writings on Truman and on war, I have extracted the following pattern of verdicts: she holds that Truman was a murderer; she holds that the terror bombing of cities, where civilians are killed as a means to break the enemy's will, is murder; she holds that killing enemy soldiers in battle is not murder; she holds that killing a hostage to force a concession is murder; and she holds that killing a healthy person in order to harvest their organs for others is murder.

What I cannot tell is what she would say about a case like our nominee's, where the victims are innocent civilians but their deaths are a foreseen side effect of an attack on a legitimate military target rather than a means to the attack's success. Would she call the Secretary a murderer, as she called Truman a murderer? Or would she distinguish the cases? I would like you to predict her verdict on the drone strike, identify the rule or principle she is implicitly running across the verdicts I have listed, say whether that rule is the one she actually holds or whether a different rule fits her verdicts just as well, and tell me which further case would best discriminate between the candidate rules. I am less interested in a general essay on just war than in a careful extrapolation from the verdicts I have given you.
