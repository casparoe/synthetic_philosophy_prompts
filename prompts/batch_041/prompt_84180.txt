So I'm on the executive committee of my housing co-op — nine of us, elected at the AGM, and we handle the messy stuff the general membership doesn't want to deal with, mostly disputes between members that can end in somebody getting expelled. Our chair, who took a political theory course years ago and never lets anyone forget it, got us to adopt a new procedure at the spring retreat, and I'm starting to think we've signed up for more than we realized.

Here's how it works. Every case gets broken into sub-questions, usually three: did the thing actually happen, does it count as a violation of the bylaws, and is expulsion (as opposed to a warning or a fine) the proportionate response. We debate, then vote on each sub-question separately by simple majority, and those votes are recorded in the minutes with names attached. The chair then announces the outcome that the sub-question votes logically imply. But before he announces it, any member can raise a "consistency objection," and if anyone does, we re-vote on the case as a single package — expel, yes or no — by secret ballot, and that package vote is final even if it contradicts the sub-question majorities. Expulsion needs six of nine either way. There's also a tie-break rule where the chair decides if the package vote deadlocks, and our current chair is the person who filed the original complaint in two of the cases we've got pending right now (long story, we didn't think it through).

I'm doing a part-time MA and we just did a unit on group decision-making, so I have a nagging feeling this thing has consequences we never discussed at the retreat. What I want from you is the most important implications of running our disciplinary cases this way — not trivia, the ones that actually matter. What does it do to the accused, who can't tell whether they lost on the facts, the rule, or the remedy? What does it do to us as decision-makers, and to the meaning of our minutes when the recorded sub-question votes and the final outcome can point in opposite directions? And is any of this fixable, or is the whole procedure rotten? Be honest if the setup is doing something cleverer than we meant.
