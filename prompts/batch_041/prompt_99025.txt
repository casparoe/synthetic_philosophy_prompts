I chair the historical advisory board of a state museum. Our charter says an exhibit may be approved only if it explains, rather than merely recounts, the event it treats; the board's standing reading is that this requires at least one general regularity covering the event. The curator's new exhibit on a nineteenth-century famine instead rests its explanatory claim on three counterfactual hinges: moments at which, she argues, a different decision by a named official would have averted the worst of the crisis. A majority of the board wants to reject the exhibit as storytelling; a minority says the hinges do explanatory work precisely by showing what general regularities cannot. Draft the memo I will circulate before Friday's vote, recommending a concrete disposition of the exhibit and of the charter's requirement, including a deadline for any revisions, with a fair statement of the strongest consideration on the losing side.

Present the memo as exactly five numbered paragraphs, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
