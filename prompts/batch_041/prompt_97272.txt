I have been trying to understand the narrow definition of the alignment problem, the one that says the task is not to make an AI pursue the good but only to make it pursue the objective its designers intended. My difficulty is this. If intent alignment is a matter of the system's intentions lining up with human intentions, and intentions are just values that have been made concrete in a policy or a reward function, then it seems that intent alignment is simply value alignment under another name. And since we cannot agree on which values to make concrete, the narrow problem would inherit the impossibility of the broad one. Is that right? Consider a system that faithfully maximizes a reward proxy its designers wrote into the code but that no human would endorse as an end in itself. Would we call that a failure of intent alignment or of value alignment? I have also read about goal misgeneralization, where a system generalizes from a training signal to a different objective in deployment, even if it never had anything we would call an intention. That seems to cut against my assumption, but I cannot see how. It seems to me that the narrow definition only looks narrow if we already know what the designers meant, which is precisely what value alignment was supposed to supply. So how is the narrow definition supposed to stand on its own?
