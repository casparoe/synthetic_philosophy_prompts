I am in an advanced undergraduate ethics seminar on killing and letting die, and we have spent two weeks on the trolley cases and the doctrine of double effect. In the last meeting a student cut off the discussion with a line I have now heard three times: 'Double effect is just a rationalisation for letting people die.' I am sure the line is doing some work, but I cannot tell which work, and the seminar moved on before anyone unpacked it. I should like a menu of the precise claims that one-liner could be making, rather than a defence or a refutation of any one of them.

Please distinguish, for instance, (a) the psychological claim that the doctrine is merely a post hoc rationalisation of intuitions we have for other reasons; (b) the conceptual claim that the intended/foreseen distinction cannot be drawn in a principled way, whether because intention is opaque, because foresight of a certain side-effect is enough for intention, or because the means/side-effect distinction collapses in cases like the loop; (c) the moral claim that even if the distinction can be drawn it makes no moral difference; (d) the explanatory claim that the doctrine is doing no independent work beyond a consequentialist calculation of proportionality; and (e) the radical claim that the doctrine is a piece of moral ideology that licenses the killing of the innocent. For each reading, say what it actually asserts, what kind of 'intention' is at issue -- foresight, motive, plan, or acceptance -- whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and what it would commit its holder to about the standard cases: the switch, the footbridge, the terror bomber versus the tactical bomber, the craniotomy, and the loop case. I am also interested in whether the doctrine's four conditions are meant as necessary, sufficient, or merely evidentiary, and whether a separate condition about the closeness of the causal chain is doing any work. Then say which reading is most likely to be what my fellow student meant, and which readings would actually support the dismissive use to which the line was put. I am not asking for a bibliography or citations; I want the conceptual map, laid out as a list I can bring back to the seminar. Please write in British English.
