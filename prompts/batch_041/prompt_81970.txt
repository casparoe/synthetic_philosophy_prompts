Alright, I'm drafting a short essay on Kripke's rule-following stuff and I want your comments before I show it to anyone. Here's the gist of what I'm arguing — be brutal, it's early.

My claim is that the real target of the skeptic isn't our grip on arithmetic, it's our picture of understanding as an inner state that carries representational content. The plus/quus trick gets all the attention, but I think it's a mistake to file it under the same heading as grue. Grue is Goodman's predicate and it messes with *induction* — which generalization the evidence supports. Quus messes with *composition* — what rule you were following when you did the sums. People treat both as "the skeptic makes your past behaviour fit infinitely many rules," but the grue case is about projection and the quus case is about the algorithm, and if you conflate them you lose what's actually new in Kripke's setup. So that's my first move: seperate them.

Second move: I read the "skeptical solution" (the community-agreement thing) as unstable, and I think this is the interesting part. The solution wants to say meaning-attributions are warranted by how the community corrects and endorses each other — no inner fact required. But to do that work, the community has to be going on in the *right* way, and "right" is exactly the normative notion we were trying to naturalize away. So it either collapses back into primitivism (meaning is just a primitive normative fact we can't reduce) or it becomes a social externalism that just relocates the problem one level up — now it's the *community's* rule-following that needs grounding. I'm arguing the skeptical solution buys quietism at the price of incoherence.

Third, the punchline: I think the paradox is best read as showing that meaning-talk is *assessment* rather than *report* — when we say someone means plus by "+", we're assessing them as a rational agent, not describing an inner state. This saves normativity but I admit it costs us the idea that meaning is a fact about the person. I'm not sure yet whether that's a feature or a bug.

I recieve that this is a fairly deflationary reading, and maybe a bit revisionary about what Kripke himself was up to.

3. Where am I sliding between things? Concretely: which of my claims are settled among people who actually work on this, and which are live disputes where I'm presenting one side as if it were the consensus? I especially want that separation on the primitivism-vs-dispositionalism-vs-community question, because I have a bad habit of stating contested readings as fact.
