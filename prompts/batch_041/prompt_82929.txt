So, practical question. I do model review for a consultancy — clients bring us their simulations (supply chain, wildfire spread, epidemic curves, prices) and we have to say whether the output can actually be used for the decision they want to make, usually on about a day's notice, usually with a handful of runs and no access to the source code. The fight I keep having is that someone says "our model is fully deterministic, there's no randomness in it, so this is a fact about the future," and I have to explain that deterministic and predictable are two seperate things and the forecast is dead long before it reaches anything they care about. I already know chaos doesn't buy you indeterminism and I don't care about the free will angle, so skip that part.

What I want is an actual procedure I can run: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops and the answer is just no, and where it has to go back to the client or to whoever signs off on the risk because the question is one of values and not one the analysis can settle. It has to tell me what to do when I can't get the number, because I definately never get the number — no Lyapunov exponents, no rerunning history, no clean ensembles, and for half these systems the dominant error is the model itself rather than the initial condition, which is exactly the error an ensemble spread will hide from me. Use whatever the real material on this says — sensitive dependence, attractors, trajectory versus distribution, the fact that you can sometimes predict the statistics of a system whose individual runs are hopeless.

Then run it on three: a deterministic-looking supply-chain model a client wants to base a three-year plan on, a wildfire spread model where they want the trajectory to pre-position crews, and an epidemic model where they want a point forecast to justify a policy. At least one should come out as a no, and I'd rather it be one that hurts. Finish with what the procedure can't do, i.e. where I'd be fooling myself that I'd actually checked something.
