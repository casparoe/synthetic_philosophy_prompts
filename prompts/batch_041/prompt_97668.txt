Suppose Marx could be brought forward to study a contemporary platform firm. Its drivers own their cars and phones and are legally classed as independent contractors; a reinforcement-learning system assigns jobs and silently revises piece rates as supply and demand shift; and the firm's chief assets are a matching algorithm and a large store of trip and location data. I don't want a list of things he would denounce. I want to know what he would conclude, reasoning from his own analytical commitments, on three questions: whether this arrangement is exploitation in his technical sense — the extraction of surplus value — or something his framework would classify differently, such as expropriation or a levy on rent; whether the driver is alienated, and from precisely what; and whether orders issued by a system that owns no machinery fit his account of accumulated past labor, embodied in capital, coming to dominate the living worker. Where his framework is genuinely indeterminate, say so, and say which way he would probably lean and why.
