Been rereading the typogenetics stuff in GEB — Hofstadter's toy genetics, strands over A/C/G/T, enzymes that are just chains of amino acids where each amino acid stands for one typographical operation (cut the strand here, delete this, switch that, copy that), and the enzyme gets translated off a strand and then goes to work on strands, possibly the very one it came from. The case he's really after is the self-rep: a strand that makes an enzyme that copies the strand, so the thing just cranks out more of itself. And the reason it matters is the Gödel thing — once a code can describe the machine that rewrites the code, you've got the same loop as a sentence that talks about its own provability, which is honestly the wierd part.

Can you write me a pantoum about it? Pantoum rules in case you need them: quatrains, lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the final stanza loops back to the first stanza's lines so the poem could in principle start over forever. Five stanzas. I want the form doing the work — the repetition should feel like a strand copying itself, and that closing loop should land as the strange loop, not just as a neat trick.

Two things I care about. One, at least one of the returning lines should mean something different the second time around: the first time it's describing the strand, later it's the instruction the enzyme is actually executing, since in this system the description and the program are the same letters. Two, it should be accurate enough that someone who's read Hofstadter doesn't wince, and clear enough that a friend who's never heard of typogenetics gets the machinery from the poem alone. No tidy uplift at the end please — the interesting bit is that self-replication doesn't need a self, only a fixed point.
