I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy who came to the field from a pre-med track, and the phrase 'socially constructed' has recently stopped being a seminar toy for me: my younger sibling came out as trans last year, and my partner spent four years being told her condition was in her head before a third doctor named it. Between those two things and a running argument with my roommate, a physicist who thinks the phrase is either trivial or meaningless, I've been keeping a list of kinds and my verdicts on whether each is socially constructed. I can feel a pattern in the list but I cannot state it, and I would like you to try: what principle, or small set of principles, captures these verdicts, what does it say about cases I have not considered, and which of my verdicts, if any, does it force me to abandon?

Here are the verdicts. Money, specifically the euro: constructed all the way down; if the practice of accepting it evaporated, nothing would remain. Race as it is used in the United States: constructed, but not all the way down; the practice creates the categories and their meanings, while the ancestral and phenotypic facts it latches onto would survive its disappearance. Gender: constructed, and more deeply than race, because the practice does not merely sort pre-existing dispositions but partly constitutes the self-understandings and preferences that a base would consist in. Peanut allergy: not constructed at all; the diagnostic threshold is a social artifact, but the immune response it tracks is discovered, not conferred. Being left-handed: not constructed, even in a society that stigmatizes it; the stigma is social, the handedness is not. Being a genius: constructed, because there is no non-social fact of the matter about who counts, only the practice of recognition. Being tall: not constructed, even though the threshold is conventional and comparative. Being a witch in Salem: I want to say not constructed, which surprises people, because there was no kind there to construct, though this is the verdict I would give up most readily. Being in love: not constructed. Being married: constructed.

My own first stab is this: a kind is socially constructed just in case you cannot specify its extension without mentioning social practices. That seems to get money and peanut allergy right, but it embarrasses me on two fronts. First, it makes race constructed only if you specify it socially, whereas I want to say it is constructed even though you can point to ancestry; and it makes peanut allergy constructed after all, since its diagnostic threshold is a social practice. Second, it says nothing about the difference between a kind like genius, where a practice confers a status on real people, and a kind like witch, where the practice conferred nothing. Please give me the principle, or a small family of them, that actually fits, stating each clause precisely and naming the case that forces it; tell me what the principle says about cases I have not listed, including whether it makes 'being chronically ill' constructed (my partner's diagnosis was contested for years and is now conferred by a medical practice that also tracks something real in her body); and if no single principle fits without strain, tell me which verdict to drop and what the repaired principle then implies.
