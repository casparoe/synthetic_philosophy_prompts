Quick sanity check for a metaphysics reading group. We've been going in circles on the special composition question — when do some things add up to a further thing? — so I just want the live options ranked, best to worst, a line or two each on what each buys and what it costs. Here's the list on the table: (1) universalism, where any plurality whatsoever composes something, so there's a fusion of your left shoe and the moon; (2) nihilism, where nothing ever composes, so no tables, just particles arranged tablewise; (3) a life criterion, where things compose exactly when they make up a living organism; (4) contact or fastening, where they compose when they're stuck together the right way; (5) brutalism, where it's a primitive, unanalyzable fact which pluralities compose; (6) a proposal a friend keeps pushing, that composition happens just in case the plurality has a single well-defined persistence condition — roughly, there's a fact of the matter about which later things it's identical to; (7) deflationism, where the whole thing is a verbal dispute and each side is right in its own language. Rank them, flag any that are really the same view in different clothes, and say which one you'd actually pick if you had to defend it against the statue-and-lump case and the problem of the many.
