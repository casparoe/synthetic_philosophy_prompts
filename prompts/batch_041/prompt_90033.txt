Here is where I have landed: (1) Rational updating on new information is always a matter of conditionalizing on that information, or Jeffrey conditionalizing when the information is uncertain. (2) The probability of an indicative conditional is the conditional probability of its consequent given its antecedent (the Ramsey test). (3) The Judy Benjamin problem shows that learning an indicative conditional is not like learning a proposition, so neither conditionalization nor Jeffrey conditionalization tells you how to update. (4) The correct rule for updating on an indicative conditional is to minimize the relative entropy between your old and new credence functions subject to the constraint that the new conditional probability of the consequent given the antecedent is 1. (5) In the Judy Benjamin case, this rule gives the right answer: Judy's probability of being in Red goes up, her probability of being in Blue goes to zero, and her probability of being in neither goes up. (6) The order in which you learn conditionals should not affect your final credences: if you learn A then B, or B then A, you should end up in the same state.
