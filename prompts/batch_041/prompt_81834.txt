I am a retired experimental physicist who has spent the last few years reading philosophy, and I have become stuck on Wittgenstein's private language argument. In my old field, a dispute that survived this long would usually be suspected of conflating separate questions or of lacking an agreed criterion of success, and I want to know whether that is what is happening here. Some commentators seem to read the argument as a reductio of any sign meant to refer to a sensation knowable only to its user; others take it as a general sceptical challenge about meaning; others treat it as a therapeutic dissolution of a pseudo-problem. Are these substantive disagreements, verbal ones, or different questions being answered under one heading? My tentative hypothesis is that the impasse turns on an unexamined disagreement about what can count as a norm of correctness for a sensation term—whether it must be publicly checkable or whether a first-person practice could be normative in some other way. I would like a diagnosis of the disagreement's structure, engaging the actual positions rather than surveying them, and an account of what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or an argument that no resolution should be expected.
