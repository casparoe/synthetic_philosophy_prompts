I'm writing a feature for a general-audience science magazine on whether it makes sense to say that science gets closer to the truth, and I've hit a snag I can't resolve on my own. As I understand it, Popper's formal definition of verisimilitude was refuted in 1974 by Tichý, Harris, and Miller, who showed that on Popper's own definition no false theory can be closer to the truth than any other — so the flat-Earth cosmology and Newton's theory of gravitation come out exactly equally close to the truth. If the one rigorous account anyone ever produced turned out to be empty, and nothing comparable has replaced it, doesn't it follow that "closer to the truth" is not a respectable scientific notion at all, and that realists who describe successful theories as approximately true are dressing up a metaphor? Please explain precisely what the 1974 result destroyed, and tell me plainly whether my editor can print that the idea of approaching the truth has been quietly abandoned by philosophers of science.
