Write a dialogue between two philosophers going at each other about original vs. derived intentionality. A is in the Searle corner: real (intrinsic, original) intentionality is produced by biological brains and nothing else, so thermostats, computers, Dennett's two-bitser and the rest only have meaning borrowed from us. B is in the Dennett corner: intentionality is ascriptive, comes in degrees, there's no brain-magic, and if the intentional stance lets you predict and explain a system, thats all you ever needed. Please dont let them just rehash the Chinese Room. Instead make them fight over a case I'm inventing: a neural-interface prosthetic arm that, after a year of training, starts anticipating the user's reach before the user is consciously aware of deciding, and occasionally reaches for things the user says they never meant to reach for. Is that the arm's own intentionality, or still derived from the patient's brain, and if it's derived, derived through what exactly? Also get them onto whether "derived" intentionality has to be derived from something that's already intentional (so the chain has to bottom out in a real mind somewhere) or whether it can be manufactured out of plain non-intentional causal processes, like selection or learning. Have each of them press the other on where their line actually falls, whether it's principled or just gerrymandered around the cases they care about, and what would count as evidence either way. Both should be sharp and neither should be a strawman, and they should actually answer each other's moves instead of talking past each other.
