I am a novelist, and I am writing a scene in which a retired philosopher, cornered at a dinner party, tries to settle a metaphysical argument by telling a joke. The argument concerns Hofstadter's careenium: a vast table of tiny magnetic balls — the simms — bouncing and clumping, whose clumps, seen from far enough away, form stable higher-level structures, the simmballs, which appear to chase, recognize, and shove one another. What is at issue is whether the simmballs push the simms around, or the simms push the simmballs around, or whether the question itself is the trap — and the philosopher's joke is meant to turn on the real structure of that puzzle rather than on generic brain-in-a-vat humor. I know the analogy well enough that you need not restate it; what I want is the joke itself, something someone could plausibly deliver in one breath and that a listener who already knows the careenium would find funny because the punchline lands on the content of the puzzle. No puns on careenium and cranium, or on simm and symbol — the humor has to come from the puzzle, not the spelling. Give me the joke in a single paragraph of no more than 150 words, with no preface and no gloss; if it needs a gloss, it is not the joke I am looking for.
