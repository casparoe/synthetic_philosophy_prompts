Every time healthcare comes up my friend trots out the same line: medicine should be like the fire department. Nobody asks for your credit card while your kitchen's burning, nobody calls that tyranny, so why should a burst appendix be any different? I mostly buy it, but I want to know how far the analogy actually goes. Lay out the correspondence first -- what plays the role of the fire, the house, the neighbours' houses, the firefighters, the taxes, the coverage decision -- then tell me where it breaks, which of the breaks actually matter for the conclusion he's using it to get (universal coverage, single payer, that kind of thing), and whether the conclusion survives or whether the analogy only gets him as far as "nobody dies in the street." A table of correspondences is fine, I'm not fussy.

Two things I want you to work in. He swears that firefighting used to be a private insurance business -- companies kept their own brigades and only put out fires at buildings carrying their company's plaque -- is that even true, and does the real story help him or hurt him? And second, does it matter that a fire jumps to the next house in a way that, say, my bad knee doesn't? Also if there's a better analogy for what he's after, say so. Don't bother with sources or reading lists, just the argument.
