OK so this is a slightly odd request. I'm a retired physicist — spent 35 years on statistical mechanics and dynamical systems, hung it up a few years ago, and have spent the time since reading philosophy for basically the first time in my life. Nietzsche's eternal recurrence has kind of taken over my brain and I want a poem about it. Here's my angle, because I think the interesting part is usually glossed over.

There seem to be two completely seperate things going on under that one name. One is the cosmic claim: time is infinite, the world's total force is finite, there are only finitely many ways to arrange a finite system, so every possible arrangement has already happened and will happen again, infinitely often — meaning this exact life, down to the last detail, is coming back around. As someone who spent a career around statistical mechanics I have my doubts. Why should the number of relevant distinct states be finite at all? And even granting the recurrence of states, "the same life" seems to smuggle in something the physics doesn't hand you. The other thing is the existential one: the demon who tells you you'll have to live this life over and over, nothing new in it, ever, and asks whether you'd curse him or call it divine. Those two readings don't obviously have anything to do with each other, and I suspect Nietzsche wants the second to survive even if the first falls apart.

So: give me a pantoum. The reason I want a pantoum specifically is that its lines keep coming back — the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza reappear as the 1st and 3rd of the next — and I want that coming-back to do the actual work: each time a line returns it should mean something a little different, the way the same life heard once as a curse and later as something you might affirm. Let the repeated lines carry the physics-skeptic's objection and the demon's question trading places, so that by the end it's genuinely unclear which one is the real claim. Have the last stanza close the loop by bringing the first stanza's opening line back, so the poem ends where it started and you can't tell if it's a fact about the world or a test you administer to yourself. Five or six stanzas. I want it accurate enough that someone who's actually worked through Nietzsche's own attempt to prove the cosmic version would nod, but I'm not after name-dropping — I want the poem to be about the tension, not to gesture at it.

Lead with the poem itself, no preface or commentary, just the poem.
