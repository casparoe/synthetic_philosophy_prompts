Been reading LessWrong and the Sequences for over a decade, and I've hit a wall on belief attribution that I can't think my way past, so I'm outsourcing it. Dennett's intentional-stance thing used to settle this for me — you're allowed to attribute beliefs whenever it's useful for prediction and there's no deeper fact of the matter — but ever since everyone started fighting about whether LLMs have beliefs I've noticed my gut and my stated position have totally diverged, and I want to know which verdicts have to go.

Here's how I actually call it, gut not theory:

Thermostat: no beliefs. "The thermostat thinks it's too cold" is a joke, I'd never actually feed that into a prediction.

Chess engine: shaky. I'll say Stockfish "thinks it's lost here" and half mean it, but I wont say it *believes* anything about chess the way I believe things about chess.

My dog: yes, obviously — she believes there's a squirrel out there, she's wrong sometimes, she updates, she acts on it.

Ant colony: no, and I feel strongly about this. Nobody's home. "The colony decides to send foragers left" is shorthand and nothing more, even though the colony's behavior is genuinely adaptive.

A corporation: yes, and I'll defend it. "The board believes demand will fall" is a real belief held by a real system, can be true or false, and it guides what the company does.

ChatGPT or whatever the current model is: no. And this is the embarrassing one because I can't say why. It answers, it generalizes, it's way more flexible than the dog and the corporation put together.

The pair I can't square is the corporation and the model. Both are made of parts that dont individually hold the belief. Both can be interrogated in language. Both let the state guide behavior. But one's a believer in my book and the other isn't. Meanwhile I deny beliefs to the ant colony, which probably "represents" where the food is as well as anything does.

I keep reaching for a principle like: a system believes P when it has an internal state that tracks P, in the sense that the state would've differed if P had differed and its behavior would've differed accordingly. But that seems to hand beliefs to the thermostat and maybe the colony, which I don't want.

So: is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? Test it against stuff I haven't listed — a protein, a market, a river that "finds" the fastest way downhill, an ensemble of five models that vote, a person who'd sincerely answer "I believe P" but whose behavior never reflects it. And tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and whether I should just drop that one. If the real answer is "belief comes in degrees" or "it's about how useful the stance is," say what that does to the corporation-vs-model pair, because that move currently feels like letting me have my cake and eat it too.
