I chair the historical redress comittee at a mid-sized state university, and our board meets Thursday, so I need you to draft the recommendation memo I'll circulate.

Background: from the 1930s into the 60s, the university's real-estate arm helped enforce restrictive covenants and pushed a condemnation that cleared a Black neighborhood to build what's now the north dorm complex and a parking deck. The land is still ours and still in use. The board has finally set money aside for a redress fund, but it wrote into the resolution that the fund should achieve "reparative parity" -- a phrase lifted from a consultant report that nobody on the comittee can agree on. Does parity mean matching what the displaced families would plausibly have today had they never been pushed out, or closing the current gap between their descendants and the county average? Those are wildly different numbers, and the board has capped the fund at $12 million anyway, so whatever we recommend has to fit inside that and be executable by next fall.

The descendants aren't one voice either: one organization wants the land back as a community land trust, one wants a scholarship pipeline, one wants direct cash to verified descendants. And one faculty member says we owe nothing at all, since the victims are dead and nobody alive did it or could have stopped it.

Give me the memo: a concrete recommendation (form of redress, dollar range, timeline), the reasoning for it, and an honest statement of the strongest case against us doing anything. Dont hand me a history lesson.
