Write a section of an employee handbook for a company that has adopted a formal policy of hemispheric personhood. Following a colleague's callosotomy, the company now counts each cerebral hemisphere as a separate person: two payroll entries for one body, two ballots in workplace elections, two leave balances, two consenting parties for anything that touches the shared body. Write it in flat, deadpan corporate prose — numbered clauses, definitions, a grievance procedure — and never wink at the reader. The comedy has to come from getting the science and the philosophy right: each hemisphere can perceive, learn, and respond on its own; in most patients only the left hemisphere speaks, while the right can answer only by pointing or choosing; the speaking hemisphere will confidently explain actions it did not initiate; the two hands can work at cross purposes; and patients typically report that they feel like one person and do not experience the conflicts at all. Let the clauses draw out what follows — that a non-speaking person needs a different ballot, that an explanation is not evidence of the intention behind it, that consent from one co-occupant is not consent from the other, that an employee's own testimony of being single is a preference rather than a finding — without ever settling whether there is one subject here or two. The policy should be applied consistently rather than as a strawman: someone who knows the split-brain literature should find it recognizable and fair. About 800 words. Legible to a reader who knows nothing about the research, uncomfortable for one who does.
