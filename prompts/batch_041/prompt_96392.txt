Berkeley's master argument is standardly put as: you cannot conceive of an unperceived object, therefore nothing exists unperceived. Read that way, "unperceived object" is a contradiction in terms, the tree outside my window goes out of existence the moment I stop looking, and God is essentially an extra pair of eyes Berkeley needs to keep the world from winking out — which makes the whole system look like a verbal trick propped up by theology. Is that a fair statement of the view? I'd like a definite interpretive line rather than a survey of options: what the master argument is actually meant to establish (idealism outright, or merely that the materialist's "matter" is incoherent), what work God is doing in the system, and whether the perceiving mind is exempt from "to be is to be perceived" on some principled ground or only by stipulation. Finally, tell me plainly where my framing goes wrong.
