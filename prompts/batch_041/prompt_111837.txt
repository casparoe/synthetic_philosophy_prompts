I have no academic background in philosophy; I have just been reading about this on my own for a while, and for the past several months a friend and I have been arguing in circles about the lamp puzzle, the one usually called Thomson's lamp. The setup we use: a lamp is switched on and off at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8 of a minute, and so on, halving the gap each time, and we ask what state the lamp is in when the minute is up. My friend is convinced the puzzle proves that supertasks, that is, any setup in which infinitely many operations are completed in a finite time, are impossible: the lamp, she says, cannot be on and cannot be off, so the whole idea ends in contradiction. I think the puzzle is not a contradiction but an underdetermined case: nothing in the setup ever fixes what happens at the minute mark, and we go wrong when we assume that the state at that instant has to be the limit of the states just before it. Call her position the impossibility view and mine the underdetermination view. We have each become better at defending our own side and no better at hearing the other's.

I want the document we should have written together instead: a joint statement that both of us could put our names to. I am not asking you to decide who is right. Please draft it with the following parts.

First, a numbered list of agreed claims: what the setup does and does not specify; what the mathematics does and does not license; what would count as a contradiction as opposed to a mere gap; and anything each side ought to concede about its own weaker points.

Second, a disagreements table. For each row: the point at issue, in wording neither of us would object to; the precise version of each side's position, written so that the side in question would accept the wording as its own; the crux underneath it; and the case, argument, or thought experiment that both sides would accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction about it written down in advance.

Four cases of my own should be taken seriously, because they are where my friend and I actually get stuck. (a) A dial whose needle turns half of the remaining way to vertical at each stage, so that it seems to settle at vertical even though infinitely many turns occur. (b) A printer that records the lamp's state after every flip, so that at the minute mark the record is an endless alternating string with no last entry. (c) A lamp whose switch takes a nonzero time to throw, or a sensor with a finite response time, so that the mechanism cannot keep up with the schedule. (d) The truncated version, in which the switch is welded shut after some finite number of flips and the answer is obvious, and the question of exactly what changes when it is not welded shut.

Please also say explicitly whether any part of our disagreement is only verbal, in particular about what the words possible and determined are being asked to do, and if so, which part.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested position as if it were the consensus; where you cannot tell which side a point favours, say so rather than assigning it. End the document with a single final line of the form \answer{...} enclosing, in a few words, the one sub-question that both sides would agree is decisive.
