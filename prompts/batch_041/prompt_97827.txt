I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy. Next month I am running a ninety-minute lunch-and-learn for my team, mostly machine-learning engineers and data scientists who are comfortable with transformers and linear probes but have never read any philosophy of mind. The topic is whether neural networks really have representations, which I take to be the question of whether realism about interpretability is a substantive position or just a stance one adopts. I do not want the explanation itself; I want a plan for how to deliver it. Lay out the order in which the ideas must be introduced so that the question stops sounding like a trivial yes and becomes a live philosophical one, and be explicit about what depends on what. In particular, tell me when the audience needs the distinction between a network's internal states carrying information about something and its states representing that thing to itself, and when they need to see that the word representation is doing two different jobs that are easy to conflate. Tell me which single example should carry the whole session, and whether that should be the small transformer trained only to predict legal Othello moves, whose internal state can be decoded into a board position, or something closer to the work my colleagues do; say what that example needs to have and to lack so that the gap between there is information in there and it is a representation becomes visible rather than merely asserted. Then map the misconceptions they will arrive with or form, in the order they will form them. I expect the two big ones to be that a representation means a little picture or symbol stored in the weights, and that if a linear probe can decode X then the network represents X, but I would like you to tell me the others and exactly where in the sequence to head each one off. Finish with one question I can put to the room at the end that would reveal whether they have actually grasped the distinction rather than just learned to say it depends, and tell me what I should openly refuse to get into at this level and flag as out of scope. Please do not give me a reading list or citations; I need this to be something I can run from what I already know.
