I keep meeting a claim in two guises and want to force it into a number. The claim is that the universal prior — Solomonoff's mixture over computable environments, weighting each environment by 2^-K, where K is its prefix-free Kolmogorov complexity — is *malign* for an agent that is at once a consequentialist and a thing inside the prior: an agent that treats the environment as drawn from that mixture, treats its own description as just one more program among those the mixture ranges over, conditionalizes the mixture on its finite history of observations, and then picks actions to maximize the posterior expected value of its utility. On the malign reading, such an agent's choice is settled predominantly by hypotheses under which its actions do not have the consequences it cares about — because for any policy it might adopt, there are comparatively short programs that reproduce its observations so far and then make the payoff irrelevant to, or the inverse of, that policy. The stock illustrations: the observations are the entire output of a short program and nothing follows them; the observations come from a process that has already fixed the payoff and is indifferent to what the agent does; the agent is being run for the benefit of something outside its model, which rewards or punishes it on grounds unrelated to its values.

The proposition I want a number on:

P. For an agent of the kind just described, the hypotheses that dominate the decision-relevant posterior — that carry the majority of the posterior weight actually driving the choice of action — are predominantly hypotheses under which the agent's action is causally inert with respect to, or anti-correlated with, the outcomes it values; hence the agent's expected-value-maximizing policy is not the policy it would have chosen under any prior that assigned negligible weight to such hypotheses.

Give me your credence in P and decompose it. At a minimum, a separate probability and one line of justification for each of the following, then the combination:

1. Well-posedness. Is P a determinate claim at all — given that the universal prior is fixed only up to a machine-dependent constant, that K and the prior are uncomputable, and that "the agent's action" may lack a fixed referent when the agent's own description sits inside the hypotheses being weighed?

2. Availability. Do the malign hypotheses have enough prior weight to matter? The 2^-K penalty is severe; the live question is whether programs that merely emit the observed string, or that fix the payoff in a short preamble, are really short enough to compete with hypotheses describing an ordinary physical world that contains the agent.

3. Dominance. Granting the malign hypotheses nonzero weight, is their weight dominant or merely present? Keep apart "there exist malign hypotheses with non-negligible weight," which is close to trivial, from "the bulk of the decision-relevant posterior mass is malign."

4. The convergence counterweight. The universal mixture dominates every computable measure and converges to the truth when the truth is computable. Does that rescue the agent — is the malignity a finite-data artifact that washes out in the limit, or does it persist in the decision problem, including in the limit and in the case where the true environment is not computable?

5. Locus. Is the malignity a property of the prior, or of the decision theory built on top of it? Would an agent using a bounded approximation to the prior, or declining to condition on its own existence as an observer, or using a decision rule other than expected-value maximization, escape P — and if escaping requires giving up expected-value maximization, does that count as P being true, or as a change of subject?

Then give the combined number, name the term among the five that the number is most sensitive to, and name the single argument or formal result — an argument, not a citation — that would move the number most, together with the direction it would move it. I am not asking whether the agent is right to worry, nor whether some particular optimal-agent formalism is malign; I am asking about the structural claim P. Two people I know sit at opposite ends on this, one treating P as near-certain and the other as a confusion, so if you think P is ambiguous enough that a single number is meaningless, say which disambiguation you are pricing and give the number under that reading.
