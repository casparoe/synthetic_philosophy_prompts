Here's my view. I say vagueness isn't one phenomenon and people keep trying to give one theory for all of it. I split predicates into two sources. For predicates whose borderline cases come from a continuous magnitude we can't discriminate -- tall, bald, heap, red-ish -- I'm an epistemicist: there's a fact of the matter about where the cutoff sits, we could never know it, and some sorites premise is just false. For predicates whose borderline cases come from us making the rules up as we go -- vegan, a sandwich, a sport, grown up -- I'm a supervaluationist: there's no fact of the matter, the predicate is true on some admissible precisifications and false on others, which is why the sorites reasoning feels compelling without being sound. So: epistemicism in one region, supervaluationism in the other, sorted by whether the vagueness comes from the world's grain or from our stipulations. I've been calling it the two-source view, which is a boring name, I know.
