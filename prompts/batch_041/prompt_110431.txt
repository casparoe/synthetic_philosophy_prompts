"4. Aristotle distinguishes the person who is a slave by nature from the person who is a slave by law or convention, and he holds that the first kind of slavery is not only just but beneficial — beneficial to the slave, not merely to the master. (a) Reconstruct his argument for that claim as fully as you can. (b) Explain what he takes the difference between the natural slave and the legal slave to be. (c) Say whether he thinks the two categories coincide in practice. 20 points, roughly 600 words, no notes."

The script, transcribed as written:

"Aristotle's defence of slavery in Book I runs on a principle he applies all the way through: wherever two things are related as ruler and ruled, the relation is natural and it is good for both of them. The soul rules the body, reason rules appetite, and in the same way some people are by nature able to foresee what ought to be done while others are only able to carry out with their bodies what somebody else has foreseen. The second type is the natural slave. He can take in an instruction but cannot deliberate about what is to be done, which is why Aristotle says he has no deliberative faculty at all — no share in reason whatsoever, so that he is not a candidate for virtue in the way a free man is, and is in that respect nearer to a domestic animal than to his master. And because he cannot plan a life for himself, being directed by someone who can is actually good for him: that is why Aristotle is willing to say slavery is advantageous to the slave and not merely to the master. The natural slave is also, on this account, simply property — a tool, and a living one, an instrument belonging to another person whose entire function is use.

Legal slavery is a different matter. The prisoner taken in war, or the person bought at market, is a slave only by convention, and Aristotle concedes that such a person is not automatically a natural slave. But I don't think this weakens his position, because in practice the two categories do line up. The test of who is a natural slave is whether he was enslaved in a just war, and being defeated by a better people is itself the evidence that you are the sort of person fit to be ruled; that is what Aristotle means when he says that among the barbarians there is no natural ruler, and so they are a community of slaves, male and female."
