I have been deriving a corollary from the handicap principle that I suspect is a misreading, and I would like you to locate the error rather than defend the theory in general.

As I understand it, the principle explains the reliability of a signal by its cost: a signal is credible because producing it is expensive, and it is expensive so that senders lacking the underlying quality cannot afford it. I have been treating this as entitling me to two further claims. The first is that reliability is a monotone function of cost, so that within any domain the most expensive signals are the most trustworthy and the cheapest the least. The second is that whenever a signal is cheap to produce, its honesty must be either accidental or secured by some mechanism outside the handicap account altogether.

Two cases make me doubt the derivation. The first is a class of signals that appear perfectly reliable while costing the sender almost nothing, because the signal is not chosen at all but instead falls out of the sender's physical state: it cannot be faked not because faking is prohibitively expensive but because there is nothing separable to fake — the signal and the quality it indicates are the same underlying fact, or the signal is mechanically fixed by the quality. The second is a case I constructed rather than observed: a signal whose production cost is identical for high- and low-quality senders, so that the cost is a pure deadweight loss carrying no information, yet the signal is honest because a low-quality sender who sends it is penalized afterward by the receiver.

What I want is the precise misstatement. Which of my two claims is a genuine implication of the principle and which is not? And what does the principle actually require for honesty to be an equilibrium — I have been assuming it requires that the signal be costly enough that a low-quality sender simply cannot afford to produce it; if that is not the requirement, what is? Finally, are my two cases best read as counterexamples to the principle, or as cases it was never meant to cover?
