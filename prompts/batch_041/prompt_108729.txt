I've ended up as one of the ~20 stewards of a mutual aid fund my neighbourhood started last spring. We pooled about £9k from a couple of fundraisers, and the rule we settled on is that any single steward can approve a request up to £400 without checking with anyone else, because we didn't want someone waiting a week for a committee meeting when they need a deposit for a flat or a new boiler or whatever. It was fine when there were six of us and barely any requests came in. Now there's twenty-something of us, we get far more requests than money, and I've noticed something that's been bugging me: the stewards who approve things fastest are exactly the ones most confident that they can tell a real crisis from a good story, and those are not the people I'd pick to make the call. Someone at a party said this is just the unilateralist's curse and that there's a whole literature on it, and I did look it up but everything I found was about AI labs and nukes and space, not about a WhatsApp group with a spreadsheet. I'm supposed to bring something to our next meeting, so I want to actually understand it before I open my mouth.

So: what do you think are the most important implications of the unilateralist's curse for how a group like ours should be run? And I'd especially like you to think about two wrinkles that I suspect the standard version doesn't have. First, our judgments aren't independent at all — we all get the same forwarded screenshots, half of us are in the same church group, and if one person posts that a request looks legit the rest of us are primed. Second, whoever ends up acting is not a random steward but presumably the one with the loosest threshold or the most optimistic read, which seems like it should matter for what the group as a whole ends up doing. Does the usual argument for some kind of rule or norm still hold up under those two twists, or does it get weaker or stronger? And the obvious fix is to make everyone log their intention in the spreadsheet before approving anything — is that actually enough, or does it just relocate the problem? Last thing, and this might be a dumb question: is there a real argument that we should be *more* permissive, i.e. that there's a mirror-image failure where good requests get missed because each steward assumes someone else will pick them up? I can't tell if that's the same phenomenon or the opposite one.
