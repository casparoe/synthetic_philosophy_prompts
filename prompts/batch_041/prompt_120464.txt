I'm a novelist, which means I spend an embarrassing amount of time having strong opinions about what is and isn't art, and I finally tried to write my rules down on paper and the whole thing fell apart. I've been going back and forth for weeks and it's driveing me nuts. Can you help me figure out what I actually believe?

Here are my gut calls. No theory, just the gut, in the order they came to me:

- The old woman at my aunt's funeral who sang some unaccompanied thing in a cracked voice, badly, and made the entire church cry. Art. Maybe the most art thing I saw all year.
- My neighbor's kid playing a Bach partita note-perfect at a recital, everyone clapping, and I felt absolutely nothing. I still want to say it's art. This is where I get stuck, because my gut says art and my gut also says the kid might as well have been a player piano.
- A charity video that made me sob on a train, and then I noticed the donate button and felt like I'd been mugged. It made me feel more than anything at the funeral. Not art. A machine.
- A novel I admire enormously, the architecture of it is breathtaking, and it left me cold. Art, obviously art, and yet.
- A poem a friend says rewired her whole life and which does absolutley nothing for me, I can't even parse it. I want to say it's still art. But if the whole point is feeling, whose feeling, and how would I ever know?
- The little book I made for my daughter when she was small, badly stapled, badly drawn, sentimental beyond reason. Art, to me, though I'd never defend it in public.
- A great joke, told well. Half of me says art.
- My own last novel, which I labored over for three years and which I now think is a lie from the first page. Not art, even though I made it, even though I meant it at the time. Though — did I mean it? That's the problem, isn't it.

The rule I keep reaching for is something like: art is when a person genuinely feels something, and builds a thing that makes you feel it too. That gets the funeral and the charity ad exactly right, and I think it's what my gut is secretly doing with the joke and with my daughter's book. But it throws out the cold partita and the poem, and my gut flatly refuses to throw those out.

So: is there a principle that keeps all of these, or do I have to give one up? And if I have to give one up, which one, and what does the repaired version say about a case I haven't listed — say, a forger, someone who paints exactly like some great painter, sincerely, feeling everything that painter felt, but it's all in the style of someone else? And a friend tells me I'm just reinventing some Russian count's theory from a hundred years ago, but couldn't remember which one — is he right, and what did the count actually say that I'm missing? Not looking for a reading list, just the shape of it.
