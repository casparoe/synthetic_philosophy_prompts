I run a small wildlife trust and next month I have to give the opening remarks at our annual donor dinner — mostly birders and people who've symbolically adopted an owl, plus a handful of our field ecologists. The problem is that over the past year I've read a lot about wild animal suffering and I've basically been convinced by it: if most wild animals die young and badly, and if keeping a habitat from being bulldozed just means fewer animals get born (and not being born isn't a harm to anyone), then some of what we fund may be adding to the total suffering in the world rather than reducing it. I can't open with that, and I also can't stand up and give the usual every-creature-is-precious speech I don't believe. Write the remarks, maybe 600-700 words, that use this tension to structure the whole thing — the donors' love of nature set against the welfare of the individual animals that make it up — and that leave them feeling they've been told something true rather than sold something. Dont resolve it with a tidy both-matter ending.
