I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of science, half-affiliated with an ML theory lab, and I've got a disagreement in my reading group that just wont move. One camp says the no-free-lunch theorems are basically Hume's problem of induction formalized: if you average over all possible target functions, every learning algorithm has the same expected performance, so any inductive success has to come from a bias that can't itself be justified a priori. The other camp says that's a bait-and-switch. The theorem is about a uniform average over all functions, but we never face that; we face a fixed world with some particular distribution, and in that setting some algorithms do better than others. So the theorem says nothing about whether induction is justified. Then the first camp says that's exactly the point -- you have to assume the world is one of the lucky ones, and that assumption is the unjustified bias. Then the second says that's not a bias, it's just what it means to learn in a world with structure. And we go around again.

I keep suspecting the impasse isn't really about the math. Maybe it's verbal: one side means by 'justification' an a priori proof that induction will work in all possible worlds, the other means a local explanation of why it works here. Or maybe it's a hidden factual disagreement about what the theorem quantifies over -- all functions versus a fixed but unknown distribution -- and that's a scope question that could actually be settled. Or maybe they're answering different questions: one is doing epistemology (what can we know without assumptions?) and the other is doing learning theory (what can we do with assumptions?). I've also been reading about the companion result that without prior weighting every pair of objects is as similar as any other, and I cant tell if that's just the same point in different clothes or a genuinely separate constraint.

What I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two questions under one name? What is each side actually committed to, and what would count as progress -- a distinction, a formal result, a change of question -- or should I stop expecting progress? Also, tell me how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure about. No reading list or citations needed; I want the structure of the disagreement.
