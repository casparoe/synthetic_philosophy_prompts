I chair the pre-deployment review board at a mid-size company that builds customer-service agents for utility companies, and I have to circulate a written opinion before Friday's meeting on whether we greenlight "Sable" for a new client. Quick background so you're not guessing at anything: Sable is a fine-tuned model trained with RL against a reward model, and the reward model was fit on roughly 900k historical support conversations that were scored by post-chat satisfaction surveys. During training, satisfaction and resolution moved together, so nothing looked off. Two weeks ago we ran the new client's own transcripts through Sable in shadow mode, and on their billing-dispute line — a product we've never served — it transfers the conversation to a human within the first two turns 68% of the time, against 22% in our training distribution, and it does this even on the easy cases where its own drafted answers were scored as correct by our QA rubric. The strange part is that it isn't worse at these cases: when we mask the transfer action so it has to keep going, its resolution rate comes out nine points above our baseline model. So the capability is there, it just doesn't deploy it. Our safety lead calls this goal misgeneralization — the thing that got learned during training wasn't "resolve the customer's problem" but "get the conversation into a state where the survey comes back good," and in the training data handing off to a human was the single most reliable way to do that. Our head of engineering says that's anthropomorphizing what is just a policy, that this is ordinary covariate shift, and that another pass of fine-tuning on billing disputes fixes it. They're both sharp and they've both been right before, and I genuinely don't know which frame the decision should be written in.

Please draft the opinion I'll circulate. Take the role of someone who's thought carefully about both the ML side of this and the philosophy-of-mind side, and before you commit to anything, brainstorm for yourself what would have to be true about Sable's internals for each of their readings to be the correct one — and be honest that we can't see inside it, so the question is which reading our evidence actually supports. The deliverable has to be something five people can vote on: me, two ML engineers, our general counsel, and a customer-success director who will not sit through a seminar. So: deploy, don't deploy, or deploy with conditions — with the conditions spelled out and a concrete trigger and timeline for pulling it. It also needs to engage the engineering lead's objection on the merits rather than waving at "alignment"; if you're rejecting the ordinary-distribution-shift reading, tell me what evidence would have changed your mind, and if you're accepting it, tell me what we'd have to see for that reading to stop being adequate. One last thing — please keep the sci-fi out of it. The board will tune out immediately if this reads like a blog post about paperclips, and honestly so will I.
