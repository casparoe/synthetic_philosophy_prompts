I'm a science journalist, I've been sitting in on a workshop about convention and norms for a piece I'm writing, and it's basically two camps talking past each other. Camp one is the David Lewis type: a convention is a regularity that everyone follows, expects everyone else to follow, and prefers to keep following given that the others do; it's arbitrary in the sense that some other regularity could of done the same job; and it's held up by common knowledge, because it's a solution to a coordination problem. Camp two is the cultural-evolution crowd: conventions and norms are just whatever behaviors got copied and stabilized in a population, held in place by imitation, conformity bias and the threat of punishment, and you don't need common knowledge or a shared preference for any of it.

What I want is the document these two should have written together instead of doing another panel. A joint statement of an adversarial collaboration: the claims both can sign about what a convention is, what actually needs explaining, and what their two accounts are really competing over; then exactly where they part ways, with each position written in wording its own side would accept; then the cruxes, and for each crux a case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table.

Three things I'd like them to hash out, because I think thats where the action is. First: a village where everyone drives on the same side of the road, everyone expects everyone else to, but nobody has any belief at all about what the others expect third parties to do. Is that a convention yet, and what happens to it if one person's expectation shifts? Second: a regularity everyone privately dislikes and would rather swap for the other option, but keeps following because deviation gets you fined or shamed. Does that count, and does it matter that the preference to conform is manufactured by the penalty rather than coming out of the coordination problem itself? Third: whether the ought adds anything over and above everyone will do it and you'll be punished if you don't, and if it does, who gets to explain it.

If part of their fight turns out to be about words, say which part and why. Skip the lit review, I don't need citations or reading lists, I need the formulations.
