I am a novelist at work on a book set in a small town owned, in effect, by one employer: a decent man who has never interfered with anyone's choices and who would be sincerely pained to learn that any of his workers felt unfree. My protagonist is certain that if she crossed him her life would come apart, though nothing has been forbidden to her and she is free to leave. I have been reading about the distinction, in the republican tradition, between freedom as non-interference and freedom as non-domination, and I want the book to be right about her case. What are the most important implications of that distinction for it? Is she unfree, and if so, in what does her unfreedom consist? Does it matter whether the employer's power over her counts as arbitrary — and if arbitrary power here need not mean capricious power, what does it mean? Finally, what would have to change, in the world of the book rather than in her attitude toward it, for her to be free? Please answer in no more than 250 words.
