I have no formal training in philosophy or cognitive science, but I have been thinking about consciousness on my own for some time, and I have reached a position I would like you to test. I hold that qualia, understood as intrinsic, non-relational, introspectively accessible properties that are supposed to constitute the raw feel of an experience, are an incoherent posit. On this view, the term does not name anything over and above the fact that experiences have a subjective character; it reifies a feature of first-person reports into a metaphysical entity, and every attempt to specify what qualia are either collapses into a functional or representational description or retreats into an assertion of ineffability. I am not asking whether the word is useful in ordinary conversation. I am asking whether the theoretical posit is coherent.

What I want from you is a map of what would count against this position. Specifically:

1. What observations, arguments, intuitions, or formal results should move me? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and explain what each would show.

2. What would a defender of qualia concede as counting against their view, as opposed to what only a critic would say? I want the strongest internal concessions, not a list of external objections.

3. What does my position predict that its rivals do not? If it predicts nothing, is that a problem, or is it a sign that the dispute is verbal?

4. Is there a version of the qualia posit that survives the incoherence charge, for example one that treats qualia as determinable rather than determinate, as relational rather than intrinsic, or as the intrinsic properties of a representational state rather than of an experience? If so, does that version still deserve the name, or has the incoherence charge simply forced a retreat that leaves the original disagreement untouched?

5. I am especially interested in cases where two experiences seem to have the same functional and representational profile but are supposed to differ qualitatively, and in the reverse case. What would such cases actually establish? Would they show that qualia are needed, or only that our descriptions are incomplete? Please do not simply recite the standard zombie or inverted-spectrum arguments unless you can say precisely what they add beyond the functional and representational facts.

6. Finally, tell me whether my position risks unfalsifiability. If every proposed counterexample can be absorbed by saying that the alleged quale is really just a functional or representational feature, what could possibly count against me? If nothing could, is that a defect in the position or a sign that the position is a conceptual clarification rather than an empirical claim?

I would personally find most persuasive a case where denying qualia forces me to deny something I already accept about experience, or a formal argument that the incoherence charge equivocates between senses of intrinsic or introspectively accessible. But I want the full map, not just the parts that fit my priors.
