I keep encountering the paradox of tolerance used as a conversation-ender. Someone asserts, in effect, that a tolerant society has no obligation to tolerate the intolerant, and treats that as a licence to exclude, deplatform, or silence whoever they have labelled intolerant — as though the paradox itself had settled the question. I want to know what precise claims that one-liner could actually be making, so that I can tell which of them, if any, really does license the move and which are just rhetorical work done under the cover of a paradox.

Please lay the possible readings out as a menu rather than defending a single one. For each reading, give the precise thesis: what exactly is being tolerated or withheld, who is doing the tolerating, whether the claim concerns an individual's attitudes, a community's norms, or a state's coercive powers, and whether the claim is descriptive, conceptual, or normative. Then say, for each, whether it is a trivial truth, a substantive and defensible position, or simply false.

Two concrete cases should be used to test every reading. The first is a member of a small discussion group who argues in obvious bad faith, but only with words and only within the group's own rules. The second is a member who advocates a policy that, if adopted, would dissolve the group's norm of open deliberation altogether. For each reading, state what it would and would not license in each case — whether exclusion, continued inclusion, or something in between.

I also want the boundaries between the readings made explicit: which ones collapse into each other on inspection; which require the additional premise that tolerance is itself a value worth protecting rather than merely a procedural rule; and which quietly smuggle in an empirical prediction — that tolerating the intolerant will cause tolerance to disappear — that is contestable and would need evidence. Distinguish, in particular, the reading on which the paradox is a conceptual or logical point about the self-undermining structure of an unlimited tolerance norm from the reading on which it is a practical slippery-slope prediction about what happens if you let a particular group operate.

Close by saying which reading the person who deploys the slogan in an argument most plausibly intends, and whether that reading actually delivers the conclusion they draw from it.

Begin your answer with a single sentence stating the bottom line — which reading genuinely licenses the exclusion and whether it succeeds — and then give the menu and the reasoning behind it, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
