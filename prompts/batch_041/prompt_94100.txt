Write a poem in terza rima — interlocking tercets, aba then bcb then cdc, and so on — spoken by a maximal element of a partially ordered set: something that has nothing strictly above it, though it need not be above everything. Let the form do the mathematics. The interlocking rhyme should stand for the chain that Zorn's lemma asks about, each tercet passing its sound to the next the way each element in a chain is topped by a later one; the closing stanza, where the dangling rhyme is finally taken up and the ascent is brought to rest, should feel like the step at which the hypothesis supplies an upper bound and the climb stops because it cannot go higher. Make the speaker exact about the distinction a specialist would insist on — maximal is not greatest; there may be neighbours neither above nor below it, and it is not above them either — and let it concede that its existence was promised without ever being located, a conclusion reached by a choice it cannot show you. Give a line, too, to the fact that the lemma is stated only for an order that is nonempty, so the poem has to begin with something rather than nothing. It should be a poem first and an illustration second: someone who has used the lemma should find the situation right, and someone who has not should still find the ending earned.
