And it gets perverse the more sure I am: the stronger my private view that they're wrong, the more I should wonder what they know that I dont, and if they know nothing I don't, why are they on the other side at all? Same shape when my uncle sells me his car suspiciously cheap (why me? why cheap?), and in reverse when a poker player suddenly bets big — his willingness to put money in tells me about his hand, not just mine — and I swear I've felt it in non-market settings too, like a journal editor who's unusually eager to publish my paper, or a landlord who's desperate to have me sign. What I can't figure out is whether the thing I'm pointing at is one concept or three. I know 'efficient markets' is nearby, and 'adverse selection' and 'the market for lemons' are nearby, and I have a hazy memory of some theorem to the effect that in a fully rational market where everyone starts from the same priors, private information alone can't generate any trade at all — but I don't trust that memory, I don't know what it's called or what it actually assumes, and I can't tell if it's the thing I'm after or a cousin of it.
