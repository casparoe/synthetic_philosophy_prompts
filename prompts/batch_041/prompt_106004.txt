I sit on the research review panel at my lab (I'm a 4th-year PhD student; the panel is three of us, and our PI chairs when he bothers to show up). We're supposed to sign off on any project that uses what the lab handbook calls an "aversive reward channel" — negative signals used to shape behaviour rather than just to define the task. Nobody has ever submitted one, so we've never had to decide what that phrase actually means. Now we do.

The case: a group two floors down wants to train a robot-arm policy in sim to never make contact with a human model in the workspace. They could just give a big positive reward for keeping clear, but they say that's too slow to learn, so they want a penalty channel: −1 for every timestep in contact, −10 on a hard collision, policy trained to maximise the sum. Their line is that this is "just a shaping term", purely instrumental, so it doesn't count as aversive under the handbook and they dont need us at all — they're only here because our chair saw the compute request. They also say the penalty isn't a state of the agent in any meaningful sense, it's a scalar in the loss, and the agent has no access to it except through gradients.

What makes it hard, and why I'm writing to you instead of just voting: the sim has a stochastic fault mode where the contact state is entered with some probability no matter what the policy does — there is no action sequence that avoids it. Their prelim runs show the policy basically stops moving under those conditions, which looks like the thing everyone gets worked up about. But they also have a control agent trained only on the positive reward, no penalty, and it also stops moving. So the freeze doesn't cleanly separate the two cases.

And I should be upfront that this is personal: the reward-function library they're using is mine. I wrote the penalty term for an unrelated project two years ago and I've been feeling weird about it ever since. My partner thinks I'm being silly, but I've not been sleeping great, and one of my panel colleagues has already said that if we take this seriously we have to take every negative reward in every RL paper seriously, which is obviously unworkable.

Draft me the opinion I can circulate before Thursday's panel meeting. I want a real recommendation — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — and conditions concrete enough to actually enforce. I'm thinking a cap on penalty magnitude, a requirement that they run the no-escape condition as its own arm with the freeze behaviour logged, and some kind of stopping rule, but you tell me. Give me a time frame, since their compute block starts Monday. And take the "it's just a number in a loss function, there's no fact of the matter about whether a value head is miserable" line as seriously as it deserves rather than waving it away, because half the panel will say exactly that and I need to be able to answer it in the room. No reading list, we're not doing citations, I just need the memo.
