In my graduate seminar on truth, a colleague tried to dissolve a question about truth’s role as a norm of inquiry with the one-liner that truth is not a property but only a device for disquotation. I want a disambiguation rather than a defense: lay out the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making, from the nearly trivial to the metaphysically radical. Distinguish, for example, the claim that ‘true’ is not a predicate, that it is a predicate but does not pick out a property, that it picks out a merely expressive or logical property, that it functions only to endorse or generalize, and that it is a norm of assertion or belief. For each reading, say whether it is plausible, whether it is substantive, and whether it would actually support my colleague’s conclusion that truth cannot be the aim or norm of inquiry. End by saying which reading a deflationist is most likely to have meant and which reading the argument in the seminar actually needed.
