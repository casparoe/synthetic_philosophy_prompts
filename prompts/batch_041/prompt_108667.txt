Since last winter I have been volunteering at a drop-in centre for asylum seekers, and the question of who counts and who must take them in has stopped being an abstract one for me. I have now been asked to give evidence to a committee reviewing how our region handles arrivals, and to file a short statement of my own position beforehand. Before I put my name to it I want a consistency check, because cross-examination is precisely where a loose joint shows. Here, as plainly as I can put it, is what I actually believe.

(1) A state has the right to control its own borders, and refusing someone entry at the frontier does not require a justification owed to that person. (2) There is a stringent duty to rescue someone in mortal danger when one can do so at modest cost, and a border does not cancel that duty, so a state that is able to admit people fleeing mass violence is obliged to admit them. (3) Refugee status should require an individualised, well-founded fear of persecution for who one is or what one believes; general conditions of violence or deprivation in a country do not, by themselves, make anyone a refugee. (4) Displacement by climate change — sudden disasters as well as slow-onset sea-level rise and drought — ought to ground that same status, even where there is no persecutor and the harm is spread across millions. (5) Since no state is uniquely placed to help any particular claimant, the duty to admit is one that states hold collectively rather than one any state owes to any individual; what matters is that each takes a fair share. (6) Whether someone is owed protection turns on what they are fleeing, not on how they travelled, so irregular entry or a destroyed passport cannot count against a claim.

Tell me where this does not hang together. Give me exactly five numbered items and nothing else: no preamble, no closing summary, nothing beyond what I ask for. Items one through four, ordered by severity with the most serious first, each a sentence or two naming the commitments that pull against each other, how badly they pull, and the cheapest way to make them consistent; if a pair only looks like a conflict, say so in that item rather than treating it as real. Item five should name the single revision that would clear up the most across the whole set, and what it would cost me elsewhere.
