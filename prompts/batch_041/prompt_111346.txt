I recently found myself in a long argument with a friend who insists that conscious experience is epiphenomenal—a byproduct that never causes anything. I want a constructed thought experiment that isolates the issue. Build a case involving a creature whose pain states are epiphenomenal, yet who reliably reports that it is in pain whenever and only when it is in pain. The case must have two versions that differ in exactly one respect: in Version A, the pain state and the verbal report share a common neural cause, but the report is not caused by the pain; in Version B, the report is caused by the pain state itself. No God, no pre-established harmony, no telepathy; keep the neuroscience plausible, and keep each version to a paragraph. Then show what an epiphenomenalist must say about the creature's knowledge of its own pain in each version, why the paradox of phenomenal judgment arises in one but not the other, which feature of the case does the work, and what happens if that feature is removed. Finally, say how an epiphenomenalist would try to resist the version that makes trouble for her.
