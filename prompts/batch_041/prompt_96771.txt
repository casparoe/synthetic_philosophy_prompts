Bit of an odd request, but here goes. I've been reading rationalist blogs for about fifteen years — LessWrong, Slate Star Codex before it became the other thing, and the forums that spun off them — and for most of that time I've been a moderator on a discussion board that's finally shutting down. Forty-odd people are coming to the back room of a pub on Saturday for the last meetup, and I've been asked to say a few words before we pull the plug. That's the speech I want you to write.

Here's what's making it hard. Kierkegaard, in the little book he wrote just after a satirical paper had spent a while making him a figure of fun — the one where he goes after the newspapers — argued that the press doesn't merely report on the public, it conjures one up. The public, on his account, is a phantom: an abstraction nobody is actually a member of, which can never assemble anywhere or be held to anything, and which gives everyone the lovely feeling of taking part while nothing ever gets decided or done. He thought the characteristic movement of his age wasn't revolution but levelling — an abstract, envious flattening that converts everything into chatter, including the things you care about most. And he was pretty blunt elsewhere that the crowd is untruth, that only the single individual can be in the truth. (I'm paraphrasing from memory, and I'd rather you didn't put any of his actual sentences in quote marks — I can't check them, and misquoting him at his own funeral would be a bit too on the nose.)

So the awkward bit: on that view, the thing I gave fifteen years to was structurally a mistake. It was chatter, it was a phantom, and alot of the worst days were exactly the levelling he describes — we hounded a long-time member off the board in 2019 over something I still wince about, and I was the one holding the ban hammer. But I also met my two closest friends there, and those were one-to-one things rather than crowd things, and I suspect that's precisely the bit he'd say was real. I want the speech to hold both of those at once without going soft on the first one. If it collapses into we-were-a-family-and-the-internet-was-better-then, I've failed, and everyone in the room will know it.

The room: a dozen people who'll get the reference instantly, another dozen who'll glaze over the moment I sound like I'm giving a lecture, a handful of partners who never posted and are mainly there for the chips, and one guy who did his dissertation on Kierkegaard and will absolutely pull me up if I get him wrong. So keep it under five minutes, written to be spoken rather than read, and if the idea can land without me having to name him, better. One more thing — three of us are planning to start again on a new platform, and I want to say something honest about that, because I dont think the phantom is a property of the software.

British English please. No quotes from him, no reading list, just the speech.
