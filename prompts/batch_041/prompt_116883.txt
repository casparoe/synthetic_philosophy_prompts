For a feature I'm writing on Buddhist philosophy's strange second life in Western logic, I want a little flowchart sidebar — the "which reading of Nagarjuna are you actually committed to?" kind — that takes a reader from gut-level commitments to a labeled position on his four-cornered move (you know, the P / not-P / both / neither thing). Don't start with the labels; start with stuff a smart reader who's never heard of Madhyamaka can still have an opinion on: whether the four corners are meant to exhaust the options, whether when he tears down all four about something like arising he's still asserting anything positive, and whether the "not" in the second corner cancels a claim or secretly presupposes one (I've seen this called the prasajya vs. paryudasa distinction — non-implicative vs. implicative negation — but don't make the reader learn the Sanskrit). From there branch out to teh actual positions: the flat-footed classical reading where the fourth corner is just a contradiction so tossing it is free, the paraconsistent reading where the third corner is a genuine true contradiction, the truth-value-gap reading where the fourth is a hole rather than a rejection, the "it's a therapeutic ladder you kick away" reading, and anything else that deserves a leaf. Give each leaf its name and one sentence on the ugliest thing it commits you to, and call out any two leaves the tree can't really pull apart. Maybe 8-10 nodes, American English, and please no reading list — I can't cite anything I can't check.
