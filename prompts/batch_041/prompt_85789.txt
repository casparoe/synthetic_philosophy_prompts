I've somehow ended up with a seat on the medical-necessity board of our public health insurer — I spent three years fighting them over my own claim, they finally caved, and then they put me on the committee, which I'm still not sure was a reward. We vote on which conditions count as diseases, which in practice means which ones get paid for, and next week's case has been eating at me.

Our track record, roughly, over the last couple of years:

- Stroke with a visible lesion, left her badly impaired — yes, disease, nobody blinked.
- Progressive nerve degeneration, obvious and getting worse — yes.
- A harmless anatomical oddity in a guy's gut, no symptoms at all, he feels completely fine — no.
- A woman who's miserable because her job is unbearable and her marriage is falling apart — no. The line we took was basically: that's life, not a disease.
- Chronic pain, nothing detectable on any scan, but it got a lot better on a drug that's normally used for something completely different — yes.
- A guy who's never had the slightest interest in sex, no distress about it, perfectly content — no. Nothing to treat, and he doesn't want anything.

I know there's a long-running fight about whether disease is a biological fact or a social verdict, and honestly our six decisions feel describable either way, which is exactly my problem.

Next week: a man in his 40s who had a brutal viral illness two years ago. Since then his emotional life is flat — no joy, no grief, no anger. He works, he reasons fine, but his scans changed from before the illness and his doctors think some of the emotional machinery isn't doing its job anymore. He says he's fine, doesn't feel sick, doesn't want to be sitting there. His wife says the man she married is gone and she's leaving him. Nothing we know of treats it.

Do we call it a disease? Tell me what rules our six decisions are actually consistent with, what each one predicts about him, which one you'd bet we're really running and why, which of the six is the closest analog, and if the record genuinely doesn't settle it, what further case would tell the remaining rules apart.
