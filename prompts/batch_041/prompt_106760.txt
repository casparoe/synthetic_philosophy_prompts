My flatmate and I got into a stupid argument last night about whether you can even write a money pump joke — his position being that the money pump is a reductio and reductios aren't funny, which I think is rubbish — so I'm turning it over to you. Background you'll need, because I want the thing to actually be about the real content: in teh standard synchronic version, someone with a cycle of preferences — A over B, B over C, C over A — gets walked around the loop by a trader offering swaps, paying a little at each step, until she's back where she started and out of pocket; the usual moral is that intransitive preferences are self-undermining, i.e. she can be made worse off by her own lights without anyone lying to her. The diachronic version is a different beast: her preferences at any given moment can be perfectly ordinary and transitive, they just change as time passes, and the pump only bites because she's naive about that — she pays to get the tempting thing out of the house and then pays to get it back. If she's sophisticated she sees it coming, and that's where it gets good: she either ties herself down in advance (and half the literature insists that isn't really choosing) or she pays the pumper upfront to leave her alone, and I'm fairly sure there's a version where the sophisticated agent ends up worse off than the naive one, which is the funniest result in the whole area and nobody ever writes jokes about it. There's also the self-torturer, the guy who keeps dialling the voltage up because each notch is imperceptible and each notch pays, and ends up at a setting he'd have refused at the outset — if you can use him, use him.

What I want is a joke, or ideally two, one per pump, that would actually land for someone who knows this material. So the punchline has to turn on real content: that the pump needs a pumper with deep pockets and a perfect read on your preferences; that the naive agent's failing is her beliefs about her future self rather than her preferences right now; that the sophisticated agent's fix is to buy her own freedom, which is either the height of rationality or a confession that she was never one agent to begin with. And here's my own contribution, which I'd like you to work in if it doesn't wreck the joke: the whole thing is best seen as arbitrage — the pumper isn't selling you anything, he's buying your future self's regret at a discount and selling it straight back to you — which means the genuinely scary case is the pump with no pumper, just you and time and nobody else in the room. I don't care about names or who said what, I care that the joke works off the ideas, so don't build it around some paper only three people have read. Thanks!
