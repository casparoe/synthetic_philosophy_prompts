Someone on my team keeps saying translation is just shipping. Meaning is the parcel, the words are the box, and translating is repacking the same parcel into another language's boxes. His point is that the box doesn't need to know whats inside it — the post office moves parcels just fine — so Google Translate doesn't need to "understand" anything either, it just needs the shipping rules, and "understanding" is a myth philosophers made up to keep themselves employed. When the model gets a translation wrong, in his view, it's because it hasn't seen enough parcels, not because it doesn't understand. (I'd been primed to be suspicious by an old Hofstadter essay on how shallow Google Translate is, so I don't know if im being fair to him.)

So: where does the parcel picture actually hold and where does it come apart? Lay out the mapping for me — what's supposed to be the parcel, the box, the post office, the shipping rules, the sender, the recipient — and a table is fine — then tell me where it breaks. The thing I keep tripping on is cases where the target language makes you commit to something the source never said: a language where you can't just say "she's gone" but have to say whether you saw it yourself or only heard about it, or one where "my friend" forces you to pick a gender, or where you have to pick a level of formality for who you're talking to. A parcel never has to make those calls. Does that matter, or is it just more data? And what does the picture quietly assume about whether there's even a determinate parcel before anyone boxes it?

Then tell me if his conclusion survives — that we can drop understanding from the requirements and just scale the data. If the picture is broken, whats a better one? Someone here suggested porting code to another platform. Keep the whole thing under 500 words.
