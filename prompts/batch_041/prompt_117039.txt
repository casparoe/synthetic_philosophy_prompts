I teach a 300-level philosophy of biology course and I want to spend one 90-minute session on free-floating rationales — Dennett's idea that there can be true purpose-stories, of the form this trait is there in order to do X, that no one entertains and no one intended. The students have read some Dennett and some Millikan, so they know the words, but I want them to actually feel the creepiness of the thing rather than nod along. Here's the rough shape I'm after, but I want you to design the thing properly, including all the details I'm leaving out. Round 1: everyone gets a private rule card from a deck I've prepared — things like take the nearest token you can see, avoid a token if someone else is within two steps of it, follow the trail of string left by the last person, prefer the more common color, and so on. Round 2: I take the rule cards of the top third of scorers, copy them, mutate a couple of them slightly, and hand those out; the bottom third's cards are discarded. Round 3: same again.
