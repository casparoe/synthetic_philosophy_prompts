I've got three years and basically my own head plus a small travel budget to work on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness -- the Frankish/Dennett line that there's no inner glow, and that "phenomenal properties" are really just the brain misdescribing its own introspective states back to itself. I'm a computational cognitive scientist by training, not a metaphysician, so I care a lot about which questions are actually tractable for someone like me versus which ones will quietly eat the whole three years. Here's the twist I want you to take seriously rather than wave away: I think the usual framing collapses two different illusions into one, and that a lot of the arguing-past-each-other in this literature comes from people not being clear about which one they're denying. The ground-floor illusion is that our experiences seem to have some intrinsic, ineffable character. The upper-floor illusion is that we're *acquainted* with that character -- that it's directly present to us in a way no third-person description could ever capture. Treat those as seperate and see what falls out.
