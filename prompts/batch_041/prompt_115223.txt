Write an essay adjudicating the controversy over evidential cooperation in large worlds. Set it up as follows. Two agents are causally isolated: nothing either does can affect the other. But the world is large, so each has reason to think there are many agents who share its decision algorithm and face a similar choice. Evidential decision theory says an act is rational if it is the act that, conditional on your doing it, gives the best news about the world. Does that make it rational to sacrifice for such an agent, on the grounds that your sacrifice is evidence that they will also sacrifice? Or is this a confusion -- a kind of magical thinking that treats correlation as if it were causation? Complicate the case: each agent is uncertain whether the other is a duplicate, a simulation, or merely a distant reasoner who will reach the same conclusion, and neither knows whether the other has the same evidence. Reach a verdict: is evidential cooperation rationally required, rationally permitted, or irrational? Justify it by considering at least one case where the correlation is perfect and one where it is merely probable, and by addressing the strongest objection to your view. Do not cite sources or give a reading list. Structure the essay as: The dispute (~150 words), The case for evidential cooperation (~500 words), The case against (~500 words), My verdict (~400 words), Objections and replies (~300 words).
