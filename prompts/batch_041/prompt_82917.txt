ok, so I'm building a comaprative scale of historical atrocities. Not for anything official, it's for a seminar I'm teaching and for settling arguments with friends, but I want it to actually work, and every attempt I've looked at seems to have been reverse-engineered from a conclusion the author already liked. So before I read anyone else's version, I want the spec. What does a theory of comparative atrocity have to deliver before it's worth anything?

Give me the requirements list with the case behind each one -- the pairs of cases it absolutely has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the facts on the ground it can't contradict. Actual hard cases, not "genocide is bad". Some that made me want to do this in the first place:

- a case with a much lower death toll but where the intent was to erase a people entirely, set against one with a far higher toll that was the byproduct of a policy nobody set out to maximize -- assuming that distinction survives contact with the cases at all
- atrocities that erase their own record: archives burned, everyone who could testify killed, no graves left to count. Any scale that runs on evidence is going to systematically underrate exactly the worst ones. Is that a bug in the scale, or a fact about the world the scale has to live with?
- cases where the victims are made into participants, forced to do part of the killing, so the clean victim/perpetrator split a ranking wants just isn't there
- and the one I keep circling: are we ranking events, or what was done to each person? If it's per-person, a mass atrocity is a huge pile of small ones, and then the theory starts licensing sentences like "one Rwanda is worth a lot of small massacres", which I'm pretty sure I don't want but can't see how to block without giving up something else.

Then tell me which requirements you think can't all hold at once. My hunch is the knot is around completeness plus refusing to trade one atrocity against many smaller ones plus being sensitive to numbers at all, but you tell me. And mark which requirements are doing real work versus which are just taste -- I don't need this to be usable for reparations or sentencing, it's for a seminar, so if a requirement is only there because of some legal or bureaucratic use, say so and I'll drop it. Skip the scorecard against existing theories, I'll do that myself once I have the list.
