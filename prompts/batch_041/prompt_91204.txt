Ok so I've been reading about corporate criminal liability and its bugging me. When a company gets charged with a crime, the law basically treats it like it has a mind — it can "know" things and "intend" things and be guilty. And the strangest part is the collective knowledge thing: courts have apparently said a corporation can count as knowing something when the pieces of that knowledge are scattered across different employees, even if no single employee ever put the pieces together. So the company "knew" a fact that literally nobody in the company knew. Meanwhile we obviously can't put a company in jail, only fine it, and we also go after individual executives separately, as if they were the real agents all along and the company was just the scene of the crime.

So here's what I want to know. What does this whole practice presuppose about group intention? Does it only make sense if you think the corporation is a genuine agent with its own mind over and above the people inside it — a real "we" that believes and intends? Or can you run the exact same practice on a deflationary picture where "corporate intent" is just shorthand for facts about individual people, no extra entity needed? And is the practice even consistent with itself, because it kind of seems to want it both ways: a real group mind when it's time to assign liability, but individual minds when it's time to punish. Reconstruct the implicit theory for me, say which rival views of collective intentionality it actually rules out and which ones it turns out to be perfectly compatible with (I suspect it's underdetermined and I want to know where), and then tell me what would have to change in the practice if we switched to a flatly individualist picture where only people can intend anything and the company is just a name for a set of arrangements.

And please be straight with me about which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely contested by people who actually work on collective intentionality — dont hand me one camp's view dressed up as the consensus.
