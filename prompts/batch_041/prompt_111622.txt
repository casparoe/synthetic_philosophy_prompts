I work as an analyst in the transport planning office of a mid-sized city, and I've been handed the 'options appraisal' chapter of our new 20-year mobility plan -- the part where we're supposed to show that we compared the shortlisted options fairly and picked the best one. The trouble is that the office is split over what 'fairly' and 'best' even mean here. My supervisor wants every option scored inside one monetised cost-benefit model (travel time savings, emissions priced in, accidents priced in), because he says otherwise the chapter is just alot of opinions and the council will end up going with whatever the loudest group at the consultation wants. Priya, our economist, says the opposite: that the choice of framework -- monetised CBA versus a multi-criteria grid with accessibility, equity and 'quality of place' columns -- isn't a technical question at all, and that calling it technical just smuggles a set of values in under a technical label. She says the framework has to be chosen by the elected members, in public, and then we do the maths inside whatever framework they choose. My supervisor says that if politicians pick the framework then the technical work downstream is just decoration, and he keeps going on about some Vienna Circle philosopher he read in grad school who said the choice of a framework is a practical decision and not a theoretical one, though I'm fairly sure he's using that to mean the opposite of what Priya means by it. He also says the same crowd were socialists who thought rational planning was the future, and that you can't get values out of science, and that both of those can't be true at the same time. Priya's line on him is that saying 'quality of place' is meaningless is itself a bit of metaphysics, which he takes as an insult.

I have about six weeks, a 4,000-word limit, and a committee meeting before the public consultation where a residents' group has already accused us of hiding value judgments inside the numbers. What I actually want is advice on how to write this chapter: do I build the single metric and defend the framework choice in an appendix, do I do the multi-criteria version and put the framework choice to the committee as a seperate decision, or is there a third option I'm not seeing? And if Priya is right that the framework choice isn't a factual matter, does that mean my supervisor is wrong when he says some frameworks are simply worse than others -- is there really no way to argue that a metric which can't represent anything not traded in markets is a bad metric, or is that definately just a preference dressed up? What I'd like is something I can hand to both of them, since right now the whole thing is going to be decided by whoever writes the memo.
