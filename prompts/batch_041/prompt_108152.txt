My friend and I spent close to three hours last night arguing about relational personhood — ubuntu in its Southern African formulation, and the Akan and Igbo versions of the idea that Menkiti and Gyekye argue over — and we ended in exactly the standoff you would predict. Her position was that any account on which the community confers or completes personhood has to be paternalistic: if the community is the arbiter of who counts as a person, then it can always override a person's own judgment about her life, and she holds her standing at the pleasure of others. I told her that gets the logic backwards, that a view on which personhood is constituted by relations can protect a person against things a liberal would let her do to herself, but I could not produce a case on the spot and I want one now. So please construct a thought experiment to order. I need a scenario, realistic and contemporary, in which the radical reading associated with Menkiti — personhood is not something one is born with but something one attains over a lifetime of morally proper participation in communal life, with the community as the arbiter of who has attained it — reaches the verdict that protects the individual, while a standard liberal individualist account — personhood is a status the individual holds, and it grounds claims against the community — reaches the permissive verdict and allows precisely what the radical reading forbids. The case exists for one purpose, and it should govern everything else: to show that the communitarian/individualist axis does not line up with the permissive/protective axis. A case whose interest depends on the community simply being the stronger party is worthless here, so the inversion has to fall out of what the theories independently say about personhood, not out of a stipulation that the community wins.

Two constraints while you build it. First, the individual must consent freely, lucidly, and without pressure of any kind — no coercion, deception, manipulation, or economic duress, and no failure on her part to understand what she is agreeing to. If the case works only because her choice was not fully her own, it does not work at all, since both theories would then forbid the arrangement and there would be nothing to see. Second, keep the operative facts social rather than metaphysical: someone who rejects every supernatural commitment in the story must still be able to assess it, so nothing may turn on whether a ritual actually accomplishes anything or whether ancestors really are watching. Otherwise, ordinary realistic detail is welcome and better than drama — a village or an ordinary city neighborhood, a family, a debt or an obligation, a role only one person can fill, and a thing she agrees to that costs her standing and her relations rather than her body, since I do not want this to become a case about self-harm. If you can also arrange it so that both accounts grant that she is a person and disagree only about what her personhood consists in, that would be a bonus, but the inversion is the requirement.

After the scenario, do the check, in this order. State the verdict each of the three positions gives — the radical reading, the liberal individualist, and Gyekye's moderate communitarianism, on which the community is genuinely primary and no one flourishes outside it, but the individual's value and rights are not a grant from the community because the person is intrinsically valuable in herself — and give the reasoning that gets each one to its verdict. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if I remove or reverse it; I expect the case to collapse back into the ordinary alignment, but show that rather than assert it. Then give the strongest line of attack open to a defender of whichever position fares worst, and say whether the objection succeeds or merely relocates the disagreement. If one of the three positions cannot be made to deliver the verdict I am asking for without a premise the theory does not independently supply, say so plainly and tell me what the case would have to look like instead. Keep the scenario to about a page, put the analysis after it, and write the whole thing in American English; no citations are needed.
