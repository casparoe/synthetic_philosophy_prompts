Okay so I've been chewing on this one for a week and I can't tell if I have a real objection or if I'm just confused about levels.

Background, for anyone who hasn't read the book: Hofstadter's move with strange loops is that self-reference never really closes on itself, because below every tangled hierarchy there's an inviolate level. The two hands in Drawing Hands can't be drawing each other all the way down, somebody is outside the picture doing the drawing, and that's the level where the paradox evaporates. Same with Gödel, the sentence is undecideable from inside the system but from outside you can see it's true. Same with the self: the I is a tangled loop, but the neurons underneath are the inviolate level where the loop is actually running.

Now here's an argument my friend and I worked out and it seems to eat the entire idea:

(1) A tangled hierarchy is a hierarchy where moving in one direction through the levels brings you back to where you started.
(2) Every tangled hierarchy has an inviolate level: a level that isn't itself part of the tangle, at which the loop gets instantated and the apparent paradox dissolves.
(3) That inviolate level is a level too. So either it's itself tangled or it isn't.
(4) If it's tangled, it needs its own inviolate level, which needs one too, and so on forever.
(5) If it isn't tangled, then it's just an ordinary untangled level, which means the tangled hierarchy above it was never doing any self-grounding work on its own. All the work was being done from outside the loop.
(6) Either way, nothing is ever genuinely self-grounding. Self-grounding is always a story told from one level up.
(7) Consciousness, the self, and language are supposed to be exactly the cases where self-grounding actually happens, the self that refers to itself, the language that defines its own terms.
(8) So there is no self-grounding self. There's a description of what the inviolate level (the brain) is doing, and we've mistaken the description for the thing described.
(9) Therefore the strange-loop story doesn't rescue the self, it just relocates it, and once you see that the self goes the way of phlogiston.

I can't accept (9). And it feels like the argument is too strong, it looks like it would eat any level of description whatsoever, including the inviolate level itself, which the argument needs in order to get off the ground in the first place. But I can't find the slide. Is the problem at (3), or at (5)-(6), or is (2) the one to give up?

And if it's (2), what does the strange-loop view even look like without it? Because (2) is the thing that's supposed to keep the loop from being a flat contradiction. I'd rather keep the loop and drop the inviolate level, but I want to know what I'm paying.
