Alright, I've been mainlining rationalist blogs and comment sections for a couple years now and I've ended up with a take on moral responsibility that I'm pretty sure is roughly Strawson's, and I want a consistency check before I inflict it on my reading group. Here's the pile:

(1) I don't think being responsible is some extra fact about a person that we have to go dig out from under the practice. I think it just is the practice of holding each other to account — all that resentment and gratitude and guilt and indignation and forgiveness. There's no deeper fact about whether someone "really" deserves it sitting underneath that.

(2) I think determinism is probably true and that it doesn't threaten any of this, because the reactive attitudes aren't the kind of thing that gets vindicated or sunk by a thesis about causation. Asking whether we're justified in resenting people given determinism is close to a category mistake.

(3) But I do think there's a real difference between the personal attitudes and the impersonal ones. My resentment is about how someone treated me; indignation and moral disapproval are about what someone did, full stop. I think it's the impersonal ones that make the responsibility moral rather than merely personal.

(4) I think the objective attitude is the right one for some people — little kids, people deep in severe mental illness, people you're managing rather than relating to — and the wrong one for ordinary adults. So there is a fact of the matter about when it's appropriate to treat someone as a responsible agent and when it isn't.

(5) And yet I really do think there's no external standpoint from which to grade the reactive attitudes as a whole, and that we can't just decide to drop them — they aren't up to us like that.

Then one case verdict that definately bugs me. A guy raised in a cult from birth who later does something genuinely awful — I think he's still a proper target of my indignation, even though I'd grant he never had a real shot at becoming someone who could take part in normal relationships. But a teenager with severe reactive-attitude deficits who does something comparably bad, I don't think he's a proper target.

Something's off here and I can't tell what, and it's occured to me a few times now that I might be talking out of both sides of my mouth. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, how bad is each one, and what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? If you had to name the one that has to go, name it. Write it out as plain prose please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just like a long email. Thanks.
