I help run a monthly philosophy open mic at a bar near campus: eight minutes a performer, and the house rule is that the material has to land for someone who actually knows the material. Not name-dropping a philosopher and hoping for a laugh, but jokes whose punchline only works if the audience can follow the technical content. This month's theme is collective decision-making, and I've drawn the doctrinal paradox, also called the discursive dilemma: a group votes by simple majority on each of two propositions and separately on their conjunction, and can end up endorsing both propositions while rejecting the conjunction, even though every individual voter's own set of answers is perfectly consistent. I find this genuinely funny, in the way that a well-built trap is funny, and I want a joke that exploits it.

So please write me one. The humour has to come from the structure itself: each voter scrupulous and coherent, the group incoherent, and the audience doing the arithmetic in their heads as the punchline arrives. Avoid anything that could be salvaged as a generic joke about committees being useless or about academic politics. Invent your own concrete setting instead of the tired three-judges-and-a-contract case — a small hiring panel, a housing co-op, three siblings and an estate, a band — and give it a real package-deal question that is simply the conjunction of the other two. The ballots must actually check out; if the arithmetic fails, the joke dies, and I would rather have a plainer joke that works than an elegant one that doesn't. You may let it glance at the fact that the same group would decide the other way under a conclusion-first procedure, but the punchline should land on the basic structure.

Keep it to 180 words or fewer, joke only: no title, no explanation of the paradox, no closing line telling me what it illustrates. A final line of dialogue as the punchline is fine.
