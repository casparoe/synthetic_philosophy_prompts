Quick context, sorry it's a bit long: I run a tiny interview podcast out of my garage. No philosophy degree, I just talk to people about ideas I've been chewing on for months and half of what I say is me thinking out loud. Next month's guest is a philosopher who's spent about a decade arguing something I find kind of wild, and I don't want to waste the hour letting her just restate it.

Her line, as best I can put it: care ethics went wrong by putting care *inside the carer's head* -- the warm feeling, being totally absorbed in the other person's situation, wanting to help. She thinks real care actually lives in arrangements: who is set up to notice whom. So the moral question isn't 'do I feel care,' it's 'what makes some needs visible and other needs invisible.' And she thinks the whole care-versus-justice debate is a sideshow, because justice is just care that got built into a system and generalized. Her slogan is that the worst failures of care aren't failures of feeling, they're failures of plumbing -- the pipes that are supposed to carry a need to somebody who could actually do something about it. She also has a habit, whenever she's cornered, of saying 'that's an engineering problem, not a moral one,' and I'm definately going to be annoyed if she gets to use that five times.

Can you write me the interview questions? I need them to actually dig into the position, not softballs, and no stuff like 'what drew you to care ethics.' Please hit these, and at least one question each:

1. Whether the carer's feeling drops out entirely. If care is in the arrangement, could a cold, indifferent bureaucracy that happens to meet everyone's needs count as caring? I want to see if her view eats itself here.
2. If justice is just institutionalized care, how does she stop 'caring' institutions from being paternalistic, or from just being power wearing a nice outfit?
3. The visibility thing. Somebody decides what's legible and what isn't. So hasn't she just moved the whole moral question back to whoever designs the pipes -- which sounds like the justice question she said was a sideshow?
4. Far-off strangers. If care needs a mediator, then whether I owe anything to people in a famine now depends on whether the news covers it. Is that a bug or a feature, and which way does she actually want it?
5. People who can't be in any arrangement at all -- the dead, or people not born yet, maybe animals. Does her account just leave them outside care?
6. A test case I keep turning over: a society that 'cares' for disabled people by building places to put them out of sight, and calls that care. How does she tell good plumbing from bad plumbing without sneaking the inner feeling back in as the standard?

For each of the six, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give -- and since she'll probably reach for her 'engineering problem, not a moral one' line, make each follow-up a little harder to escape with it, so by the end that move doesn't work anymore.

Format it like this, please:
- "Warm-up": two questions, one line each, just to get her talking.
- "The squeeze": the six questions above, each one or two sentences, in the order I listed them.
- "If she says...": one follow-up for each of the six, one sentence each, written as 'If she says X, ask Y.'
- "The closer": one question, two or three sentences, that she can't dodge with the engineering line.
