I'm a backend engineer with no real philosphy background, and I keep running into something I think is Bradley's regress. A sentence (or a JSON object) isn't just its parts; something has to tie the parts into one content. But if you say a relation ties them, then you need another relation to tie that relation to the parts, and so on. I've heard people propose these escapes: (1) the relation is special/non-relational, like a copula that isn't another item; (2) the whole is primitive and the parts are only abstractions; (3) propositions aren't structured at all, they're just sets of possible worlds; (4) unity comes from the act of judging/asserting, not from the content; (5) type theory/functional application, where one part slots into another and that's the end of it; (6) deny that 'proposition' is the right unit and talk about sentences-in-use. Rank these into tiers by how much they actually stop the regress versus just moving it one step back, and say which one you'd trust if you had to build a knowledge graph that isn't just a pile of triples. Give me a tier list with a sentence each, and one line on whether the regress is a real problem or a trick of how we talk.
