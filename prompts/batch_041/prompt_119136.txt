Examination question:

Consider the following toy model, adapted from a well-known popular treatment of consciousness. The careenium is a bounded arena containing a vast number of tiny magnetic spheres, the simmballs. The simmballs move frictionlessly and collide elastically. On collision, their magnetic orientations may flip according to a fixed local rule. The walls of the arena are sensitive to states of the external world: their magnetic permeability changes with those states, so external information is injected into the simmball dynamics. At the micro level, the complete state of the system is given by the positions, velocities, and magnetic orientations of all the simmballs, and the dynamics are deterministic. At the macro level, however, one can identify stable, repeating patterns of simmball configurations. These patterns can be mapped onto external states and can interact with one another in regular ways; call them symbols. The symbols are multiply realizable: the same symbol can be carried by different micro-level simmball arrangements.

Question: In this model, is it correct to say that the symbols push the simmballs around? That is, do the symbols have causal efficacy with respect to the simmball substrate, or is the causal story exhausted by the micro-level dynamics? In your answer, distinguish clearly between (a) the micro-level dynamics, (b) the macro-level patterns, and (c) the sense in which the latter might be said to cause the former. Conclude with a defensible position.

Student's answer:

The careenium model gives us a clear two-level picture. At the bottom are the simmballs, whose motions are governed by deterministic local rules. At the top are the symbols, stable macro-level patterns that can be mapped onto external states and that interact in regular ways. The question is whether the symbols push the simmballs around. I will argue that they do not, but that they are nonetheless real and explanatory.

First, the micro-level dynamics are causally complete. Every change in a simmball's position, velocity, or magnetic orientation is determined by its collisions with other simmballs and with the walls. The walls may change their permeability in response to the external world, but that just means the boundary conditions vary; it does not introduce any new kind of causal power. Once we have specified the complete micro-state and the local rules, we have specified everything that makes a difference to what happens next. So there is no causal work left for the symbols to do.

Second, the symbols are macro-level patterns. They supervene on the simmball configurations: no difference in symbols without a difference in simmballs. They are also multiply realizable, since the same symbol can be carried by different simmball arrangements. This makes them useful for prediction and explanation. If we see a certain symbol form, we can often predict that another symbol will form next, without tracking the individual simmballs. But this predictive success does not show that the symbols are causes. It shows only that the macro-level patterns are reliable indicators of what the micro-level will do. The symbol is a description, not a cause.

Someone might object that the symbols are causes because they figure in true counterfactuals. If the symbol had not formed, the subsequent simmball motions would have been different. But this counterfactual is true only in virtue of the micro-level realizer. The symbol itself adds nothing to the causal story; it is the simmball configuration that does the work. So the counterfactual does not establish downward causation.

Thus, the correct position is a non-reductive physicalism about the symbolic level combined with a denial of downward causation. The symbols are real patterns, but they are epiphenomenal with respect to the simmball substrate. The simmballs push each other around; the symbols are along for the ride. This preserves the autonomy of higher-level explanation without positing mysterious downward causes.
