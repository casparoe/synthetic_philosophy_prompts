I keep hearing that money is socially constructed and that race is socially constructed, and they feel like completely different kinds of claim to me — the money one sounds almost true by definition, the race one sounds like a serious metaphysical thesis about what race actually is. Can you untangle what's going on there? I want the distinction between the kind itself being constructed vs. just our concept or word for it being constructed, and why anyone cares which one it is (what actually turns on it). Then the further step: what does a "constitutive" construction claim add over a merely "causal" one — take money and gender separately, since I suspect the answer isn't the same for both. And the bit I really care about: is there a reading of "X is socially constructed" that's neither trivially true nor secretly just a claim about language, and where do the kinds that respond to being classified (so that the classifying changes the classified) fit into that. If it helps, invent a made-up kind and use it to test each distinction.
