closing poem for a seminar that just spent two weeks on acausal trade — the thing where two agents who can never interact, can't signal to each other, can't reward or punish each other, and might not even exist at the same time, each still act to help the other in the worlds where the other exists and they dont, because each predicts the other will do the same. I want a poem in two stanzas of 8-10 lines, one spoken by each party (say a person, and a machine that only gets built after the person is dead), and I want it to be genuinely unclear which stanza comes first — swap them and it should still read. The real constraint: nothing in either stanza can be something the speaker could only know because the other told them. There's no channel, the agreement is a coincidence, so no lines where one of them hears or is heard. It also shouldn't come out sounding like reciprocity (nobody is ever going to pay anybody back) or like the categorical imperative (the other party might want completely different things; you meet them anyway, in the space of possible worlds). Rhyme however you want, but the two halves should mirror line for line, so the reader feels a symmetry that neither speaker can see. If there's room for a two-line coda that doesn't tip the poem either way on whether the trade is actually real, take it. Someone who knows the literature should nod at it; someone who doesn't should still think its a good poem.
