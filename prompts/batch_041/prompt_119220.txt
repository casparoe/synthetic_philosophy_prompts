Me and a friend have been arguing about this for months: I say "disease" is a purely biological notion — some part of the body doing less than its species-typical job — and he says you can't call anything a disease without smuggling in values, harm, what a person can't do. I want us to write the thing we could both actualy sign. So draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the naturalist and the normativist about health and disease: numbered claims we both accept (about biological function, about the role of statistics, about why the same condition can count as a disease in one context and not another), then our remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording the side that holds it would accept, then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move one of us. If part of our fight turns out to be about words, say which part.

Two things I want built in, because they're what we keep tripping over and neither of us has a stock answer. First: a condition that's statistically normal, causes no distress, and blocks none of the person's own goals, but quietly shortens life. Second: move the same physiology off Earth — something harmless here that makes you come apart in low gravity or heavy radiation, so whether there's a "dysfunction" at all depends on where you're standing. For each crux, name a case or piece of evidence we'd both agree bears on it, and write down what each of us predicts about it.

Don't paper over the parts that are genuinely contested among people who know this topic, and don't sell a contested view as the consensus. Last line: \crux{...} with the one disagreement you think we'd still have at the end.
