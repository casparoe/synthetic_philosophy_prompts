I'm writing a profile of a cognitive scientist and I've been sitting in on her lab meetings for a couple months, taking notes, so I've got alot of her rulings on which grade of creature different things belong to. She sorts minds into four rungs — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — and this is how she lays it out for new students: a Darwinian creature is wired from birth and does what selection built it to do; a Skinnerian creature can be reshaped inside its own lifetime by reward and punishment; a Popperian creature can run a model of the world in its head and let the wrong move die in the simulation instead of dying itself; and a Gregorian creature can take up tools for thinking — words, numerals, maps, other people's procedures — and wear them like prosthetics, so its intelligence isn't all locked in the skull.

From my notes, her calls so far: a moth spiralling into a candle flame — Darwinian, just a fixed steering reflex. A beaver building a dam — Darwinian as well, even though the dam is a marvel; nothing in that beaver's life taught it the shape of a dam. A rat learning to press a bar for pellets — Skinnerian. A dog shaped to sit and roll over for treats — Skinnerian. A crow that, on its very first attempt, drops stones into a tube to float a snack up to the top — Popperian, on her account, since the trick was never reinforced before it happened, the bird worked it out. A kid who can't do 47 times 12 in her head but nails it on paper with a written method — Gregorian. A navigator who reasons about a route using a chart and a set of numbers rather than dead reckoning — Gregorian.

Now the one that's coming up at the next meeting and that I'd like your read on: a wild chimpanzee that cracks nuts with a stone hammer and anvil. She didn't invent it — she picked it up watching her mother, and neighbouring chimp communities do it differently, or not at all. Which of the four rungs will our scientist put her on? And the part I actually care about: her verdicts so far seem consistent with more than one rule, and I think they split right here, so lay out the competing rules, say what each one predicts for the chimp, give me your best guess and why, and then name the single further case that would tell the two rules apart.
