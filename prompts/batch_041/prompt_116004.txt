I'm a journalist who covers philosophy and the sciences, and I've been asked to give the keynote at a benefit dinner for a foundation that funds both libertarian legal scholarship and animal welfare reform. The room is split: half are libertarians who revere Nozick's minimal state and his doctrine of self-ownership, and half are animal advocates who think libertarianism is cold to suffering. I want to give a fifteen-minute speech that takes Nozick's own arguments seriously — especially the Kantian idea that persons must be treated as ends and never merely as means, and his insistence on following an argument where it leads — and uses them to press the question of animals. Nozick himself was tentative here; he raised the issue of whether animals fall under the same moral protections, and he worried that any boundary we draw to exclude them will also exclude some humans. I don't want a speech that simply claims he was secretly an animal rights theorist, nor one that dismisses the tension as a quirk. I want the tension to do the structural work: the libertarians should feel the pull of consistency, and the animal advocates should see that the framework they often dismiss might contain resources for them. Please write the speech. No headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — continuous prose only, the way one would write a letter.
