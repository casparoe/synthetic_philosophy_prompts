Random one, but this has been bugging me. I think I've got a weird version of the usual "won't bet on unknown odds" thing, and I want to know if it has a name of its own or if I'm just misdescribing the normal one.

What I do: offer me a bet on a coin you tell me is fair and I'll take it. Offer me a bet on a coin whose bias you know and I don't, and I'll pass. Fine, that's the standard thing. But the standard thing doesn't actually seem to be about the missing information. Hand me the biased-coin bet by itself, with no fair coin anywhere in sight, and I'll happily take it. And I'm still cool on it relative to the fair coin even after you tell me the bias — even when it comes out to exactly 50/50, so there's literally nothing left that I don't know. What seems to move me is the comparison: I only get cold feet when a bet I feel I understand is sitting next to one I don't. When everyone at the table is equally in the dark, or when I'm the only one who knows, I'm fine.

Is there a name for that? And is the honest answer that I'm averse to the unknown, or averse to being the person who knows less? I don't just want "thats ambiguity aversion" — I want the line between those two drawn sharply, and some sense of which one I'm actually on.
