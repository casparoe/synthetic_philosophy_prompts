I am a retired geologist who has recently become absorbed in the philosophy of science, and I am preparing to interview a historian-philosopher who defends a provocative thesis about simultaneous discovery -- the kind familiar from Newton and Leibniz, or Darwin and Wallace: that the phenomenon is not evidence for the inevitability of discoveries in any strong sense, but rather for the systemic and path-dependent character of scientific knowledge, and that the real puzzle is why we care about priority at all. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press this thesis rather than allowing the guest to restate it. Include at least one question on how the view distinguishes conditional from unconditional inevitability, one on what the plate tectonics revolution -- which I lived through -- reveals about the thesis, and one on whether the view can account for the normative force of priority disputes; for each of those, add a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give. Avoid generic questions about the guest's career.
