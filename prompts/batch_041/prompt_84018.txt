hey so for philosophy club each of us has to bring in a thought experiment and then defend it to the group, and mine landed on Nozick's entitlement theory — the historical one where a distribution counts as just if everybody actually got what they have through a legit first acquisition plus legit voluntary transfers after that, and where you're only allowed to take an unowned thing in the first place if doing it doesn't leave anybody else worse off (thats the proviso bit). what i want is a case where the theory gives an answer that even Nozick fans would squirm at, but not because of some obviously dumb rule — it has to fall out of the theory working exactly the way its supposed to.

specs:

- every single step of the history has to be clean. no theft, no fraud, no lying, no coercion, no state action, nobody tricked, nobody under duress, no broken promises. if i can point at any injustice anywhere in the chain then the whole thing is ruined, so this is the hard part and i know it.
- setting is small and boring and real — a village, a neighborhood, a co-op, a family business, a group of friends. not a desert island and not a whole country. i want to be able to list every transfer that happened.
- the resource at the center of it should be the kind that runs down or gets crowded when people use it (water, fish, grazing, a road, bandwidth) so that the harm, if there is any, comes from everybody doing their own legit thing rather than from one bad actor.
- no basketball, no Wilt Chamberlain, and no imagine ten people with an acre each. i've seen those. the whole point is a new case.
- at the end, applying the proviso deal by deal, entitlement theory says nobody has been wronged and the holdings are just, while a patterned view (pick one and tell me which: strict equality, or the difference principle) says somebody has been wronged. and i want them to split ONLY over the history vs pattern thing plus the proviso. i dont want them splitting over some side issue.
