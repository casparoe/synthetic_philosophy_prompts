The occasion: the last ever dinner of the Welbury Stone Circle Society — forty years of volunteers who were convinced the stones on the ridge encoded an ancient eclipse calendar. It didnt pan out. There are a few professional archaeologists in the room (invited out of affection, plus one who has been politely rude about the whole thing in print), and the rest are the old members, several in their eighties, who gave every Saturday for decades to surveying and measuring. Ive been asked to give the toast, and Im a historian of science, so I know exactly how easy it would be to do the whig thing: they were ahead of their time, their patient fieldwork anticipated modern archaeoastronomy, the questions they asked eventually led somewhere. None of that. Write me a five-minute toast that honors forty years of somebodys Saturdays on its own terms — no progress narrative, no consoling at least they asked the right questions — and that the old members can hear as praise rather than as a eulogy for a mistake. The archaeologists have to be able to sit through it too. Warm, concrete, and give me at least one detail of what they actually did out there in the rain.
