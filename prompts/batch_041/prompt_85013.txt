Alright, I'm trying to write down my Wittgenstein-ish commitments for a seminar and I want a consistency check before I say any of this out loud. Here's roughly where I've landed.

Meaning is use — if you know what a word means then you can use it, and you dont have a definition or a picture in your head that you consult when you do. I think alot of our concepts work by family resemblance anyway; there's no one thing all games have in common, and asking for necessary and sufficient conditions for "art" or "religion" or "democracy" is a confused question about how those words work. I think "species" is like that too, which is why the hunt for the One True Species Concept looks hopeless to me — though I don't take that to mean species aren't real.

I also think there's a real difference between using a word right and using it wrong, and that it isn't up to me, and it isn't up to the majority either. The norm is internal to the practice.

At the same time I do linguistics-adjacent work and I'm pretty committed to understanding a sentence being a computational process over structured representations — a compositional semantics is the right model of linguistic competence, and I don't mean that as a loose metaphor.

I think the rule-following remarks are basically right: no interpretation of a rule determines its own application, so what pins down correct use is a shared custom, training, a form of life, and not a mental act of grasping a rule. Which I take to mean meaning is social all the way down. Relatedly, I've always thought the private language argument is really about the impossibility of a rule that only one person could ever be in a position to follow, not about whether sensations are private.

But I also think a single speaker can be right about what their word means while the whole community has it wrong — someone introduces a technical term, the rest of us misuse it for a decade, and they're the one who's correct. And "meaning is use" obviously can't mean "meaning is whatever most people happen to do," or the whole notion of correctness collapses.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and which do I have to give up? Order the conflicts by how bad they are, give me the cheapest fix for each, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most. Write it as continuous prose please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just an email.
