ok so im a 3rd year PhD working on ML safety and I've been going in circles with a labmate about whether debate can actually do what people hope. Assume the protocol is interactive: two agents argue, a judge with bounded compute can ask questions and ask for clarification, and the judge picks a winner. My labmate says the whole thing is dead on arrival because of obfuscated arguments. Here's the argument they gave me, cleaned up a bit:

(1) For any hard question Q with true answer A, there is a false answer B and a proof-like argument for B that is valid on its face but contains a flaw buried deep enough that checking it requires at least as much compute as solving Q from scratch.
(2) The judge has bounded compute, way less than what's needed to solve Q.
(3) So for any Q, a dishonest debater can produce an argument for B that the judge cannot verify as flawed.
(4) The honest debater can only win by pointing out the flaw.
(5) If the judge can't verify the flaw, the honest debater's objection is just another claim the judge can't check.
(6) So the judge is stuck comparing two unverifiable stories and will pick based on style, confidence, or who sounds more authoritative.
(7) A dishonest debater who is better at rhetoric than the honest one will win.
(8) Therefore debate provides no safety guarantee against a superhuman liar, and the program should be abandoned.

I think (1) is doing something sneaky but I cant articulate it. Also I dont see how (5) follows if the honest debater can do things like ask the dishonest one to commit to a smaller subclaim, or point out that the obfuscated argument proves too much (e.g. it would also prove B' which the dishonest debater denies), or use the judge's questions to force a checkable commitment. But maybe those are just more unverifiable moves. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd like to keep (2) and (8) if possible, but if I have to give one up tell me which is cheaper. And if the argument is actually valid, what's the real limit it establishes -- is it "debate fails" or just "debate needs a judge that can do X"? I want the diagnosis, not a reading list.
