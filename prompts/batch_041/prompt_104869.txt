I've been arguing with a friend about whether taxation is theft, and rather than pick a theory first, I've been going by my gut on a pile of cases. Here are my verdicts. A mugger takes my wallet: theft. My school's fundraiser collects for a trip that gets cancelled and keeps the money: theft. My dad pays income tax and would obviously rather keep it: not theft. Our neighborhood association bills us for a shared fence we voted against: basically theft, though I'm less sure. The city raises water rates to build a reservoir I'll never use: not theft. A charity takes my donation and spends it on a cause I despise: theft. The government takes the same amount and spends it on a war I think is unjust: not theft -- and this is the pair I can't square, since the last two look alike. The case that really trips me up: a private security firm starts patrolling our street without asking, then bills every house, and everyone pays because the firm says it "can't guarantee coverage" otherwise. If the city did exactly that, I'd call it taxation and not theft; when the firm does it, I want to call it theft. My friend says the whole thing is easy: it's theft when it's illegal and not theft when it's legal, but that would make mugging stop being theft the moment the law changed, which can't be right. Is there a principle that gets all of my verdicts, or close? If I have to give one up, which one, and what does the surviving principle say about the private security firm and about a government that protects only the people who pay it?
