I teach a graduate seminar on explanation in economic history and I am writing the take-home final. Draft a single essay question that asks students to decide whether the question of why Britain, rather than China, was the first to break into sustained intensive growth is well posed, and that cannot be answered by reciting the standard positions or by listing causes on either side. Build it around an apparatus I will hand out with the exam: call a contrastive why-question well posed only if (a) both terms of the contrast belong to a single contrast class of cases that could have occupied the outcome position, (b) the contrast is not merely two independent singular why-questions run together, and (c) facts about the foil case bear on the answer in some way other than being the negation of facts about the target case. The question should require students to say which condition the Britain-and-not-China formulation most plainly violates, whether that failure is fatal or repairable, and what the best reformulation would be, including whether recasting it as a question about why sustained growth began at all, or why it began in northwestern Europe rather than the Yangzi Delta, changes what counts as a good answer. A strong answer will say what any well-posed contrastive question about the divergence would have to specify, such as the unit of analysis, the time window, and the contrast class, and will explain how hindsight and selection on the outcome can make some formulations immune to evidence. Give me the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme naming five moves an A answer must make, five marks each, together with the specific errors that should cost marks, for instance reading the question as a request for a list of British advantages or supposing that the mere existence of a contrast class settles well-posedness. The model answer must not rest on naming particular historians or their books; it should argue from the apparatus and from considerations students can reconstruct. Write it all in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just the question, then the model answer, then the marking scheme, the way you would set it out in an email.
