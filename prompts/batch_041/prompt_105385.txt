I am trying to find out whether there is an established name for a pattern I have noticed in group decision-making, and, more importantly, how precisely that pattern is understood, since a bare label would not help me. The pattern is this: a group has to settle several questions that are logically tied together, so that the answer to one constrains the answers to the others; each member of the group is perfectly consistent in his or her own answers; the group then decides each question separately by majority vote; and the resulting set of collective answers is one that no member holds and that is internally inconsistent. Everyone has kept his or her position, no one is being unreasonable, and yet the group as a whole is committed to a set of claims that cannot all be true.

A concrete instance: a panel has to decide whether a drug is safe, whether it is effective, and whether to approve it, where approval is supposed to require both safety and efficacy. Imagine the members split in such a way that a majority judges the drug safe, a majority judges it effective, and a majority votes against approval. The panel has thereby endorsed both premises of an argument and rejected its conclusion.

What puzzles me is not simply that a group can be inconsistent while its members are not. Two further features seem important. First, the result seems to depend on which question is treated as the one to be settled last, or as the conclusion to be read off from the others: the same individual opinions can yield a coherent collective answer under one way of ordering the questions and an incoherent one under another, with no change in anyone's views. Second, the trouble is often hidden, because the group rarely puts the whole connected package to a single vote; each question is decided on its own, and the conflict becomes visible only when someone tries to act on all the answers together.

I suspect this is discussed somewhere, perhaps under more than one label, and I cannot tell whether I am describing one recognized phenomenon or two, whether the order-dependence belongs to the same phenomenon or is treated as a separate matter, and whether there is a term for the way the problem stays out of sight when decisions are taken one at a time. I would also like to know how this differs from things it might be confused with: a group that is merely divided, an individual who is himself inconsistent, and the familiar practice of trading votes across issues so that each side gets what it wants. Please do not simply supply a name. Explain what the phenomenon is taken to be, what it is not, how the several nearby concepts relate to the exact pattern I have described, and, if no single established term fits it, propose one with a definition and clear boundary cases. Write your answer in American English.
