I'm a science and ideas journalist — weekly column, a newsletter, mostly the mind and behaviour beat, plus whatever the labs are up to. For reasons too dull to go into I've ended up as one of two trustees of a small youth project in my city: fifteen or so kids a year who've been thrown out of school, and we do mentoring, workshops, and mostly just somewhere to be. My co-trustee is a retired primary school head and we've been argueing about what we're actually for since about week two.

Her line: the kids are fine underneath. She says watch anyone — her, me, a kid who's just put his fist through a door — and when a toddler steps off the kerb into traffic, the hand goes out before the thought arrives. Nobody taught us that. That reflex is the raw material, and our job is to stop it getting crushed. She keeps pointing at the bald hill behind our building, which she says was woodland a hundred years ago and would be again if we stopped grazing it — stripped, she says, not barren. And she insists she isn't saying you just leave them to it; you obviously still have to water the field. Her point is whether you're watering seedlings or fighting weeds.

Mine: lovely, and I think its backwards. Everything we'd actually want from these kids — turn up on time, don't nick stuff, apologise like you mean it, sit still while someone else talks — has to be installed, painfully, and it only sticks with repetition and ritual. Nobody's born polite. If the raw material were already good we wouldn't need any of it, we'd just get out of the way. The reason we've got teachers and rules and ceremonies and all that scaffolding is that we start crooked and need straightening, the way you steam a plank to bend it round into a wheel. The instincts aren't the seed of decency, they're the problem decency exists to solve.

Funder meeting in a month and we have to pick one model. So: who's closer to right? And does it actually matter — would the two programmes really end up looking different, or is this one of those arguments that only lives in the pub? If there's a third thing neither of us is seeing, I'd like to hear it.
