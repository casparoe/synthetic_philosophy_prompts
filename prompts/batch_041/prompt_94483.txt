I am a fifth-year PhD student in philosophy, working at the seam between metaphysics and the philosophy of the social sciences, and I am trying to find out how much exposure a thesis I have been defending in my dissertation actually has. The thesis is that claims that a kind is socially constructed carry content only insofar as they unpack into two separable components: first, historical-causal contingency — the kind's present extension is explained by contingent social processes rather than by a natural essence, and it could have been otherwise; second, representational dependence — the kind persists because people take it to exist and keep applying the category, so that it would lapse if the relevant representations lapsed. I call the further claim that these two components exhaust the content of such claims the exhaustion thesis: once you have said, for a given kind, what makes it contingent and what representations sustain it, there is nothing left over for the metaphysics of social construction to contribute, and whatever disputes remain are verbal, normative, or straightforwardly political rather than metaphysical. I am content to grant that race and gender are what I have been calling feedback kinds — classifying someone as a woman or as Black reshapes the classified person's self-understanding, behavior, and even embodiment, and those changes feed back into the classification, whereas my treating a bill as money alters my relations to others and not the bill. I take that mechanistic difference to be real but harmless to the exhaustion thesis, since a feedback loop is simply one causal route by which a collective representation reproduces a kind. I also suspect that money's reputation as the clean, purely conventional case is something of a myth: it is merely the kind in which we are most heavily invested and least able to opt out. A colleague in sociology told me last week that this is a fancy way of saying that everything is constructed; I found the remark irritating chiefly because I could not immediately say what would show her wrong.

What I would like from you is a map of the thesis's exposure. Which observations, cases, arguments, or results count against it, sorted by how decisive they would be, from the ones that should make me abandon or substantially revise it down to the ones that are merely awkward? I am most interested in the places where the two components come apart: kinds that are plainly contingent but sustained by no one's representations, and kinds whose persistence appears to depend on more than collective acceptance — material constraints on what can serve as money, the machinery of institutional enforcement, the biological anchoring of sex, the way racialized groups can acquire measurable and heritable differences through the very social processes that racialize them. The most serious counterexample would be a case in which two kinds share a complete contingency-and-representation profile yet still differ in some respect that does explanatory work; I want to know whether such a case already exists, or whether one can be constructed, and if it can be constructed, whether it is a real kind or a philosopher's artifact. Please also say what the thesis predicts that its rivals do not, since a position that predicts nothing is one I should stop defending.

Finally, I want the concessions that come from inside the position rather than from its critics: what would a committed defender of the exhaustion thesis grant as counting against it, and how does that differ from what critics merely allege? Be honest, too, about whether I have qualified the thesis into unfalsifiability — if every apparent counterexample can be redescribed as just another causal route by which a representation reproduces a kind, say so plainly, and then tell me whether that is a fatal defect for a thesis of this kind or simply what a thesis pitched at this level of abstraction looks like when it is doing its job. The defeaters I would personally find most persuasive are those that arise from within the phenomena — a kind that stops being sustained by representation and persists anyway, or one that is fully represented and yet does not exist — so give those special weight, but do not spare the thesis. Please write your answer in American English.
