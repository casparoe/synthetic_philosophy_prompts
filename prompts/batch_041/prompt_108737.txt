I'm trying to nail down my view on how credences should shift when the incoming evidence is a conditional rather than a plain proposition, and I want an audit of the package before I defend it in a seminar. Here is where I've landed. (1) When I learn a proposition for certain, I conditionalize: I discard the possibilities in which it is false and renormalize. (2) When what I learn is a conditional or a claim about a conditional probability, I do not conditionalize on the material conditional and I do not treat the conditional itself as certain. Instead I update by minimizing relative entropy subject to the constraint the evidence imposes, on the grounds that this takes the evidence seriously while disturbing my prior as little as possible. My test case is the Judy Benjamin problem: a paratrooper is dropped into a region divided into Red and Blue territory, each with two sub-areas, and she gets a radio message to the effect that if she is in Red territory the odds are three to one that she is in the headquarters sub-area rather than the second-company sub-area. (3) Learning a conditional like that is learning a constraint on a conditional probability — not learning a proposition about the world, and not learning that some conditional has probability one. (4) There is a single correct rule for updating, and it should not matter whether the evidence arrives as a proposition or as a conditional; nor should it matter in what order the pieces of evidence arrive, so that updating on E and then F must yield the same credences as updating on F and then E. (5) The radio message tells the paratrooper something only about the Red territory, so her total credence that she is in Blue territory should be exactly where it started, at one half.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look that way, and how severe each real tension is. Where a conflict is real, say which commitment has to give, what the cheapest repair is, and what that repair costs. I would especially like to know whether the verdict in (5) is something the rule in (2) can actually deliver, and whether the order-independence demanded in (4) is something that rule can honor; if it cannot, tell me whether that is a defect in the rule or a reason to drop the demand. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and finish by naming the single revision that would clean up the most.
