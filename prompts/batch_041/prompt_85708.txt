To stop us talking past each other, here's the distinction I actually care about, since I think it's doing all the work. Three grades of realism about a feature:

1. Causal realism: the direction/feature is causally efficacious — steer along it and behavior changes in the predicted way, ablate it and the behavior degrades.
2. Predictive realism: the feature is a stable, non-arbitrary unit of the network's computation — not an artifact of the basis we happened to choose, and it survives when you look with a different tool.
3. Intentional realism: the feature is *about* something, with correctness conditions — the network can get it wrong.
