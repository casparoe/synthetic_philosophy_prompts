Something I've noticed for a while and can't find a word for. I got into painting during lockdown and I go to a lot of small gallery shows now, and I've realised I basically cannot be argued into liking a picture. If a friend tells me the composition is balanced, or the palette is daring, I can agree with every word and my actual reaction doesn't move an inch — it's like being told a joke is funny. But something else works, and it works almost every time: they say 'look at the way the light comes off the table on the left' and I look, and now I see it, and I like the painting more, and this is the bit that puzzles me — the new liking feels completely mine. It doesn't feel like I'm humouring them or faking it. I'd defend it in an argument as a judgement about the painting.

Then there's a wrinkle. Sometimes no reason gets given at all: a painter I admire says, flatly, 'that one's the best thing in the room', and I go back and suddenly it is. And sometimes I find out later that the person was being sarcastic, or had gotten confused about which painting we were even looking at, and the liking collapses — but sometimes it survives, and I can't tell you in advance which way it'll go.

I keep wanting a name for the whole thing, not just the first half. 'Bias' and 'hype' are way too crude. I have a feeling there's a literature about whether somebody else's say-so can give you a reason to think a thing is beautiful, but that seems to be about whether it's *justified* to take the person's word, whereas what I'm describing is the machinery of actually coming to see it — plus the odd fact that the end result feels like first-hand judgement even when it plainly came from outside, and that it can be revoked retroactively when the source turns out to be a dud.

Is there a term for this configuration, or for the individual pieces of it? And I mean the carving, not just the label — if the nearest established concepts are close but not quite it, tell me which ones and exactly where they miss, and if there's nothing, coin something, define it, and give me some boundary cases: what's the same phenomenon and what only looks like it. I could be wrong but I'm definately not just after a word.
