I have a semester of leave and a collaborator who builds computational models, and we intend to spend it on the knob-twiddling picture of creativity: the idea, associated with Hofstadter, that creative work is largely a matter of making variations on an existing theme — of noticing which features of a pattern are variable, how far each can be turned, and which settings leave the theme recognizably itself. The version of the picture I want you to work with is my own, so please treat it as a hypothesis under assessment rather than as something you will find stated in the literature. On my version there are two sorts of knob. Given knobs are the dimensions a representation already exposes as variable, the ones an agent can deliberately turn. Discovered knobs are dimensions that become visible only after some variation has been made and the result strikes the agent as wrong or interesting; they could not have been listed in advance. Think of a mathematician loosening a definition to see what survives, or a musician reworking a standard until one chord stops sounding like a mistake and starts sounding like the point. My suspicion is that the interesting cases of creativity live almost entirely in discovered knobs, and that this distinction is where the picture is either vindicated or collapses. I also assume that a variation counts as a variation of the theme only if it preserves some invariant the agent treats as constitutive of the theme, which raises at once the question of what fixes that invariant and whether the picture has anything to say about which variations are worth making rather than merely possible.

What I want is an agenda, not a survey. Rank the open questions this picture faces by how much else depends on the answer and how much a year of work by a philosopher and a modeler could realistically get with it, and say what would move each question up or down the ranking. Set aside the familiar framing questions — whether creativity is domain-general, whether machines can have it — unless one of them is genuinely upstream of the questions I have raised. No citations and no reading list; I am after your reasoning.

Give exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, each naming a single question, the reason for its rank, and what would move it. No introduction and no conclusion.
