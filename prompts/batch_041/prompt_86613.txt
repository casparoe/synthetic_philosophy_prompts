I spent thirty years as an experimental chemist before retiring, and I have lately been reading metaphysics with more enthusiasm than judgment. I have built an argument that I can neither refute nor believe, and I would like you to tell me where it fails and what the repair costs.

(1) The bronze bust on my shelf is not identical with the bronze that constitutes it. That bronze was an ingot on Monday, when no bust existed; I have since melted the bust down and cast a second, identical bust from the same mold, so the bronze has outlived one statue and now constitutes another. By Leibniz's law, things that differ in their histories and in what would befall them are not one thing.

(2) All the same, the bust and the bronze occupy exactly the same region of space and are composed of exactly the same atoms, bonded in exactly the same way.

(3) Two objects that occupy the same region and share all their atoms in the same arrangement are physically indistinguishable: no measurement, experiment, or causal interaction could ever tell them apart.

(4) A difference that no possible physical investigation could reveal is not a difference in the material world.

(5) So there are not two material objects here. The bust is not a material object at all, but at most a manner of speaking about the bronze.

(6) Nothing turns on the bust being art rather than nature. The same steps apply to the ingot, to my desk, to the dog, and to me: each either is identical with the matter that composes it -- which (1) rules out, since I survive the continual replacement of my atoms while that collection of atoms does not -- or is not a material object.

(7) Therefore there are no composite material objects; there are only elementary particles arranged one way or another, and our talk of tables, dogs, and persons is convenient fiction.

I am a material object if anything is, so something above must give. I am attached to (1) and would rather not surrender it, though I will if that is the honest price. What I want is a verdict on which step to reject, an explanation of why it looked so plausible, and an account of what the remaining positions cost. Write it as continuous prose, please -- no headings, numbered lists, bullet points, bold, or tables, just a letter.
