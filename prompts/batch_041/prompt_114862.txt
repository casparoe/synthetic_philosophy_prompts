Choice one. Option P: you just get $2,000, no draw, nothing. Option Q: they draw a ball — 90 of the balls pay $2,000, 9 pay $12,000, 1 pays nothing.

Choice two, fresh start, unrelated to the first. Option R: they draw — 10 balls pay $2,000, the other 90 pay nothing. Option S: they draw — 9 balls pay $12,000, the other 91 pay nothing.

In choice two, R and S share ninety balls that pay nothing — identical in both, you get nothing either way on those. Strip out the shared balls and both choices come down to exactly the same thing: ten balls that pay $2,000 versus nine balls that pay $12,000 and one that pays nothing. It's the same ten-ball decision twice. If you take the ten in choice one, you take the ten in choice two. You're picking the same bundle both times and answering differently because of the color of the balls that pay you the same regardless. That's not a preference, that's a wobble.

2. Rob: The "strip out the shared balls" move is the whole trick and it isn't allowed. In P there is no draw. Choice two: if one of the shared ninety comes up, R and S both pay nothing and it's over, your choice can't affect anything, and the only live case is again the other ten — the same pair, $2,000 certain versus nine-in-ten at $12,000.
