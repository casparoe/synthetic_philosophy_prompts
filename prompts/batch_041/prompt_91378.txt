Im sketching a short piece on Adam Smith and the colonies and I want your reaction before I write it up properly.

The usual line is that Smith was against the British empire. My claim is that this gets his argument wrong in an interesting way: Smith doesn't object to empire as such, he objects to *this* empire's accounts. His case in the colonies chapter of the Wealth of Nations is basically three moves -- the monopoly of the colonial trade raises prices at home and drags capital into a channel it wouldn't otherwise take; the colonies cost Britain far more in defence and administration than they ever return; and the constitutional point that the colonists aren't represented, so taxing them is illegitimate, along with his aside that if you gave them seats in Parliament in proportion to what they contribute they might actually consent, though nobody's going to offer it and they probably wouldn't take it. His conclusion -- that if the colonies can't be made to pay for their own defence, Britain should just let them go -- falls straight out of that ledger. Take away the monopoly, add the representation, and I think most of his objections evaporate. So his anti-imperialism is contingent, not principled: he has no objection to ruling other peoples as such, only to a badly-structured arrangement. And where he does sound a moral note about the treatment of the natives, it never gets wired into the conclusion -- it's decoration.

What I want from you: is that a fair reading of Smith, or am I flattening something -- does the anti-mercantilist argument do more normative work than I'm letting it, or is there a premise about consent or utility I'm ignoring that would make him more principled than I say? Second, is the thesis actually interesting, or does it collapse into the obvious "Smith was a consequentialist about empire"? Third, give me the best version of the opposite reading -- someone who thinks Smith *does* have a principled objection to empire -- and tell me where my sketch is weakest. No need for citations or a reading list, I know the terrain, I want the argument stress-tested.
