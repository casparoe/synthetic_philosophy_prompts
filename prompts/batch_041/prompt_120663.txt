I'm teaching a unit on Schopenhauer's philosophy of music and I want to use Jane Campion's The Piano as the main text, but I keep getting stuck on a tension I cant resolve. Schopenhauer says music is the one art that doesn't copy the Ideas but objectifies the Will itself, that melody is the highest grade of that objectification, and that music expresses the inner essence of the world rather than any particular emotion. The Piano, though, is a film where the piano is literally property: it gets sold, traded, used as collateral for sex, and finally mutilated along with its player. Ada's muteness makes the piano her voice, but the film keeps showing that voice as something men own and bargain over. So is Ada a Schopenhauerian artist whose music is pure Will, or does the film show that Schopenhauer's metaphysics can't handle the material, gendered, colonial conditions that make music possible? I want a reading that takes both the film and Schopenhauer seriously, not one that just uses the film to illustrate the theory. Where does the film vindicate him, maybe in the beach scenes or in the way the score works on us, and where does it resist him? And does the film add something to the philosophy of music that Schopenhauer's own account misses? I'm not looking for citations or a bibliography. Just start with the reading itself, no preface or restating what I asked.
