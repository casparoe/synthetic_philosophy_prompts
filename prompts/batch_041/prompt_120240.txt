My writing group hands out a prize every year with one rule I've never liked: novels only. We've had the same outside judge for four years, a poet friend of mine, and the judges record is odd enough that I want a prediction before we sit down. Yes: a novel in verse. Yes: 600 pages of letters, invoices and a bus timetable. Yes: a graphic novel. Yes: a translation of a book whose original nobody has ever managed to produce. No: twelve stories that share a town and three characters. No: a sequence of prose poems with the same narrator running all the way through. No: a memoir with the names changed. No: an unedited diary a woman kept in the 1930s, which two of us genuinely read as a novel and she wouldn't hear of it.

This year's entry is a 400-page first-person narrative, invented start to finish, one voice, one continuous story, except that the publisher is bringing it out as a memoir -- real name on the cover, subtitle saying it's a true story, and the author won't say the word novel out loud. Does she take it?

I care less about the yes than about the rule. More than one rule fits everything above, so give me the ones that fit, what each predicts here, which you'd bet on and why, and what entry would best separate them. Also, one of us keeps muttering about family resemblance and I've never known what to do with it -- is that what she's running, and if so does she have any boundary at all, or is it just taste with better manners?
