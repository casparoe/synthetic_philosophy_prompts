I'm a second-year econ PhD who keeps getting dragged to the philosophy department's reading group, and they've now asked me to write down my actual position on preferences and welfare so everyone can take shots at it. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I honestly don't know if it's a coherent package or just a pile of things that each sound fine on their own: (1) a person's utility function is just a compact way of writing down their choices — saying they prefer A to B is just saying they'd take A over B in the relevant situations, and there's no further fact about how much they want it that the choices don't already encode; (2) the thing a policy should maximize is preference satisfaction, so once you've got everyone's preferences in the right form, you've got welfare; (3) but real choices are distorted alot of the time — framing, addiction, being exhausted, being advertised at — so what someone picks can come apart from what they actually want, and welfare economics needs some notion of the person's real or corrected preferences to even make sense of that; (4) we can make at least rough interpersonal comparisons, i.e. say that some people are worse off than others and that some policy leaves the worst off better off; (5) economics should stay behaviourist — if it isn't in the choice data it isn't in the theory, so don't posit hidden mental states; and (6) sometimes it's fine to override what someone picks for their own good, seatbelt laws and that sort of thing, and not just because of externalities.

People keep telling me I'm trying to have it both ways somewhere in here, but I can never get a straight answer about where. Can you audit it? Which of these pairs are genuinely in tension and how bad it is, which ones only look like they conflict, and for the real ones what the cheapest repair is and what that repair costs me elsewhere. Rank them by how much they threaten the whole thing instead of flagging everything as equally bad — I'd rather know the one or two that actually matter and can live with the rest. Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, ordered by severity, each a sentence or two, with no intro or conclusion.
