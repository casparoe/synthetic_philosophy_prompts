Draft the joint statement that a committed dark-forest theorist and a committed contact optimist could both sign at the close of a good-faith adversarial collaboration on interstellar signaling. The dark-forest theorist holds that any civilization which reveals its position invites a first strike, so concealment is the uniquely rational policy and broadcasting is close to suicidal; the contact optimist holds that the dark-forest argument leans on contestable game-theoretic assumptions and that the expected value of signaling, cooperating, or at least listening openly justifies it. I want, first, the claims both sides can sign: about the structure of the situation as a game of incomplete information with detection risk; about which features are load-bearing, such as whether a first strike can be both effective and clean, whether the chain of suspicion is robust, whether signaling a peaceful disposition is possible and credible, and whether the encounter is one-shot or indefinitely repeated; and about what the argument does not depend on. Then the precise residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. Then the cruxes, each with the argument or case both sides agree would bear on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. If you judge part of the dispute to be merely verbal, say, about what counts as rational under deep uncertainty, name that part and explain why. Write it in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
