Book club's doing Julius Caesar this month and I've been on an Aquinas kick, so: read the play through his scattered remarks on tyrannicide. My rough sense of the terrain (going from memory, so correct me) is that in his Sentences commentary he actually defends the guy who kills a tyrant to liberate his country, but then in the Summa he's much more worried about sedition, and in the kingship treatise he warns that going after a tyrant can leave you with a worse one and that the remedy belongs to public authority rather than private zeal. So where does the play land?

Brutus is the obvious test case. Does he have anything like the standing Aquinas wants, or is he exactly the private man taking the law into his own hands because he's read too much Greek history? And is Caesar even a tyrant in the relevant sense — the play keeps him slippery, a man who three times refuses the crown and a man who just crossed the Rubicon and is being offered a kingship by his own creatures. Then there's the aftermath: the mob, the proscriptions, Antony playing the corpse like an instrument. Is that Shakespeare dramatizing Aquinas's worry that killing the tyrant hands you a worse master, or just showing that this particular conspiracy was badly run? And is there a reading where the conspiracy itself is what makes Caesar the thing they feared, so that the play is a counterexample to the whole framework rather than an illustration of it?

I want both directions taken seriously — where the play confirms something Aquinas saw that the treatise-writing version of him underplays, and where it resists the reading and you have to admit the play isn't doing Thomistic moral theology at all.

One ask on the epistemics: separate what's genuinely settled about Aquinas's position here from what's a live interpretive fight among people who know the corpus (I gather even whether he permits tyrannicide at all is disputed, and what exactly the public-authority condition amounts to is worse). Don't hand me a contested reading as the consensus. No citations needed, I'm not going to check them, and skip the plot summary.
