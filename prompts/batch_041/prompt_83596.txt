Imagine Kant got to see two things from our century: farm animals genetically engineered so they can't feel pain at all, and cultured meat grown from cells with no animal suffering involved. His line, as I understand it, was that our duties regarding animals aren't really owed to the animals — being cruel to them matters mainly because it deadens our sympathy and makes us worse toward people. So what I want is a prediction: what would he actually say about the painless-animal farm and the lab-grown meat? Does killing a painless animal bother him at all, once the cruelty worry is off the table? Or does his system still have something at stake — the animal's nature, what it's for, our own character, that dignity he only handed to rational beings? And be straight with me about whether he'd have to admit the animal's own good never counted for its own sake. Work it out from his actual machinery, don't just hand me the lazy "Kant didnt care about animals" answer.
