Science journalist here, so I read philosophy the way I read physics papers — for the story. A philosopher friend walked me through the Kripke machinery (footnote 56 of Naming and Necessity, rigid designators, all that) and then built this on top of it, and I've been carrying it around for a month because I can't find the exit.

(1) Names and natural-kind terms are rigid: they latch onto the same thing in every possible world in which they latch onto anything.
(2) So a true identity statement between rigid designators is true in every world — Hesperus is Phosphorus could not have failed to be so.
(3) And we only learn which thing a term latches onto by looking, so some of these necessary truths are a posteriori. Fine, I'm on board.
(4) Add the origin bit: the table in the seminar room could not have been made from a completely different block of wood, and the Queen could not have come from a different sperm and egg. A thing's origin is part of what it is.
(5) But an origin has an origin. The block of wood came from one particular tree, the tree from one particular seed, the Queen's parents from particular grandparents — apply the same reasoning at every link and the whole actual causal history leading up to a thing is essential to it.
(6) And that history is what made it the thing it is. Given the wood, the glue, the woodworker, the workshop, you get this table; given that sperm and that egg and the world they grew up in, you get this woman. So her scar, her taste in music, the fact that she became queen — all of it is fixed by a history that could not have gone differently.
(7) Nothing about anything could have been otherwise. Every counterfactual is really about a different object wearing the same name.

A friend of mine who does systems biology thinks (7) is just correct and that Kripke discovered what any determinist already knew, which is definately not helping. I'd rather not give up (1) or (4) — those are the parts I actually understand and find convincing. Where does this go wrong, which step do I cut, and what does cutting it cost me?
