In the seminar I am taking on the historical Jesus, we have been keeping a running ledger of verdicts on whether particular traditions go back to Jesus himself, and next week I have to predict the entry for a new case. So far the ledger reads: the account of Jesus being baptized by John, authentic, because the early church would hardly have invented a story in which its founder submits to another man's baptism of repentance; the parables of the kingdom, authentic, because they turn up in more than one source; the long first-person speeches about his own divine status in the Gospel of John, inauthentic, because they appear in only one source and sound like later theology; and the raising of Lazarus, inauthentic for those same two reasons.

The new case is the cry of abandonment from the cross, in which Jesus asks why he has been forsaken. It appears in only one independent source, since the other Gospel that contains it is generally thought to be copying the first; the words are preserved in Aramaic alongside a Greek translation; it is deeply awkward for a movement that wanted to call him the Messiah; and it also echoes a psalm of lament that a later writer could have used to shape the scene.

What verdict should the ledger record? Set out the rules our earlier verdicts are jointly consistent with, say what each one predicts about this case, tell me which you would actually go with and why, and name the further case that would force us to choose between them. Finally, tell me whether the fact that the answer turns on which criterion we privilege has any bearing on whether criteria of authenticity can ever settle who Jesus was.
