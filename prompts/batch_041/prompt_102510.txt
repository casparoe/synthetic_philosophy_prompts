I'm closing out a seminar on cyclical vs linear views of history and I'd rather end on a poem than a summary, so: write me a pantoum — the form where lines two and four of each stanza come back as lines one and three of the next, and the last stanza folds its repeats back into the opening. That return is the whole point of using it. The repeated lines should carry the wheel reading (same events, same mistakes coming round again), but the lines you wrap around them should drift enough that by the final stanza the returning line means something different than it did at the start — so the poem also reads as a spiral, not a closed circle. I want it to be about someone living through what feels like a repeat of an earlier crisis and unable to tell whether they're on a wheel or a spiral: could be a ruler watching a familiar dynastic decay play out (group solidarity thinning out over a few generations the way the classic cycles describe, or a constitution curdling into its own corrupt double), could just be a person noticing the same argument arrive back on schedule. Please don't let it land on either "it's all a loop" or "no, we're always moving forward" — I want the reader stuck in between, which is honestly where our discussion ended up. After the poem, add a short note on where it takes liberties with the actual cyclical theories and where the reading it leans on is contested by people who study them, instead of passing off one interpretation as the settled consensus.
