I'm putting together a session for a reading group that's half econ PhDs, a couple of people from the b-school, and one sociologist, and the recuring annoyance is that everybody in that room says 'herding', 'cascade', and 'everyone was just copying everyone' as though those are all the same thing. So: build me a decision tree / diagnostic that takes a described case and lands on exactly one named mechanism. It should be built from the logical space — questions first, names only at the leaves — not from a list of theories, so that it sorts people by what they actually think is going on in the case rather than by the label they already like.

Constraints:

- No node may be a question that names a mechanism ('is this a cascade?'), and no node should require the person to already know the literature. Nodes have to be answerable from the described case: what you'd have to observe, what someone would do under a counterfactual, that kind of thing.
- 9-11 leaves. They must include: the sequential-Bayesian informational cascade; the reverse (self-correcting) cascade once a private signal goes public; reputational or career-concerns herding, where the action is picked to look good rather than to be right; pluralistic ignorance, where private beliefs and public behavior come apart; threshold / bandwagon dynamics where a critical mass flips the aggregate with no new information; pure coordination with self-fulfilling expectations and multiple equilibria; plain non-Bayesian imitation; and — I care alot about this one — the null branch where nobody is inferring anything from anybody and it's just correlated private signals, i.e. a common shock. Everyone forgets the null; I want it reachable by an explicit path.
- The node I think is doing the most work is "what would make this reverse?": a public announcement of one person's private signal, a revelation of private beliefs, a payoff change, a change in who's watching, or nothing at all. Build at least one branch around that, and make the different answers genuinely split the leaves rather than funneling back to the same place.
- Here are the cases the tree has to sort. Give me the path and the leaf for each:
  (1) a hiring committee where every member privately has doubts, reads the others' silence as confidence, and hires someone nobody wanted;
  (2) a med-school cohort where two students announce that a chapter is required reading and everyone after them studies it without ever checking whether that's true;
  (3) a VC partner who thinks a deal is a coin flip but votes yes because she doesn't want to be the one who killed it if it turns out to be huge — she'd vote yes even if she thought the odds were worse than even;
  (4) a neighborhood where generators sell out, not because anyone knows anything, but because once enough neighbors have one it becomes worth having one;
  (5) a market that drifts up on no news and then snaps back the day a well-known fund publicly discloses it has been selling;
  (6) everyone in a town buys umbrellas the same week, because it's about to rain.
- Also flag any pair of leaves the tree can't separate from choice data alone, and say what extra assumption would separate them. Note any combination of branch answers that's empty, and tell me whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
- And tell me if any two of my six cases are actually the same mechanism wearing different clothes.
