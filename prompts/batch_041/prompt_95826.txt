Consider the following arrangement. A prison service replaces fixed sentences with a points-based "rehabilitation ledger": detainees earn points by attending therapy, disclosing their histories, and demonstrating insight into their offences, and they are released once a review board judges that they have become the kind of person capable of governing themselves. The service presents the change as a Foucauldian advance — a shift away from the discipline of docile bodies toward what it calls "self-authorship" — and appeals to Foucault's later writings on the care of the self as its warrant.

Give an example of a conceptual issue that this arrangement exposes within Foucault's own framework: a difficulty internal to his account of power, knowledge, and the subject, rather than an objection to the policy. Your example should show why the difficulty survives both of the obvious replies — that the ledger is discipline under a new name, and that it genuinely realizes Foucault's later thinking — and should make clear what it reveals about the relation between his analysis of discipline and his analysis of the self.
