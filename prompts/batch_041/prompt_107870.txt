I teach an upper-level seminar in moral psychology and am setting the final exam. I want a single essay question on guilt and shame that cannot be answered by reciting the familiar act/agent contrast — guilt is about what one has done, shame about who one is — or by repeating the anthropological gloss that shame cultures orient to the gaze of others while guilt cultures orient to an internalized standard. The question should press on whether the two emotions differ in evaluative content at all (can shame take a single act as its object without generalizing to the self? can guilt take the self as such?), and then on whether the second half of the topic — whether a culture can choose its moral emotion — is well posed once that is settled: what would have to be true of these emotions for a culture to select between them, and would what gets selected be what is felt or what it is fitting to feel? Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 700 words that takes a definite position rather than surveying the literature, and a marking scheme naming the five moves a top answer must make with the marks for each (total 20), plus a one-line note on how candidates typically get each move wrong.
