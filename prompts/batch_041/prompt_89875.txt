ok, I keep getting into this argument and I want a real answer instead of vibes. Whenever space settlement comes up, someone says it's basically a backup drive for civilization: you'd never keep your only copy of anything on one machine, you back it up and you keep the backup somewhere else, and that's what Mars, or a generation ship, or whatever, is for. Then it gets used to argue that settling space should be near the top of the priority list for anyone who cares about the long term, ahead of things like global health or even AI safety work, because those all die with the original and the backup doesn't.

I want you to audit that properly rather than just agreeing with it. Start with the mapping: in the analogy, what plays the role of the original data, what's the drive, what's the backup, what's the offsite location, who does the restore, and what's the failure event being insured against? Then tell me where the comparison breaks, and seperate the breaks that matter for the argument being made from the ones that are just trivia. Also be explicit about what exactly is supposed to be backed up — the people, the information, the values, or just the capacity to keep going — because I think people slide between those without noticing and the analogy gets stronger or weaker depending on which one they mean.

Four things I'm already suspicious of, so deal with each: a backup is only worth what the restore path is worth, and I don't see who runs the restore if the original is gone; backups inherit whatever's wrong with the source, so if the thing that kills us is something we built, the copy probably has it too; offsite backups work because the disaster is site-specific, but most of the disasters people actually cite here aren't; and a backup is a copy of a thing rather than a continuation of it — I don't know if that's just me being sentimental or if it does real work.

Then the payoff. If the analogy only half holds, does the conclusion it's carrying — space settlement as top-tier x-risk insurance — still go through, or was the analogy doing the whole job? And if it doesn't hold, what's a better comparison for what settling space actually buys you? I'm not going to win these arguements with "it's cool," so I want something that maps better even if it's less flattering.
