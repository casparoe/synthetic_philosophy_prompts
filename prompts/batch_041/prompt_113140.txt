Spent like two hours last night arguing with a mate about whether there can be laws of history at all — the Popper / poverty-of-historicism thing. He thinks Popper was mostly shadowboxing, and I couldn't quite put my finger on why he's wrong, so I'm outsourcing it to you.

His move goes roughly like this. Popper's whole case only really lands on laws that are secretly predictions — societies must pass through stage A, then B, then C, and we'll know when we get there. He says thats a strawman, because the generalizations anybody actually cares about in history never predict a timeline. What they do is constrain the space of possible social orders. His favourite example: you can't run a big centrally planned economy without either a price system or a surveillance apparatus. He calls claims like that "invariance claims," and he says two things in their favour. First, they're falsifiable, because you can point to a society that pulled it off, so they're not the unfalsifiable mush Popper was worried about. Second, the unpredictability-of-future-knowledge point Popper leans on just doesn't touch them, because they don't say what will happen, only what can hold together and persist. So there are laws of history after all, they're just about what's stable rather than what's next.

I think he's wrong but I need the actual argument, not just me going "nah." Please argue against him. And don't just recite that trends aren't laws, or re-run Popper's own hits — assume all of that is already on the table and go past it, because his whole framing is built to shrug that off. What I want is the case that these invariance claims are either engineering constraints or definitions wearing a lab coat, and that whatever they're laws of, it isn't history; and separately, that the space-of-possible-societies picture has a hole in it, namely that what's possible depends on knowledge nobody has yet, so the space isn't a fixed thing you can survey from outside. If some stripped-down version of his view survives all that, tell me which one and what it costs him.
