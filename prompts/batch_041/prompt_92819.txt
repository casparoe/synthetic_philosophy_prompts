So I've been reading about the historical Jesus and I keep hiting the same wall. New Testament scholars have this toolkit of criteria of authenticity — embarrassment, multiple attestation, dissimilarity, coherence — and my understanding is that's how you sort the sayings in the gospels that actually go back to Jesus from the ones the early church made up. Fine. But then why is there still no agreement about who he was? If the criteria work, why can't you just run every saying through them and see the real Jesus sitting at the bottom of the sieve? Two of them even seem to pull against each other: dissimilarity wants a saying to be unlike the church, coherence wants it to fit with the other stuff you've already accepted, and you can't run the second one until you've run the first. And doesn't dissimilarity, taken seriously, hand you a Jesus who belongs neither to Judaism nor to the movement that formed around him? My friend who did classics says people working on Alexander or Socrates have nothing like these criteria and get along fine, which makes me think either the criteria are broken or NT people are doing something strange.

The explanation I keep getting online is that scholars just have different religious and political agendas and won't follow the evidence were it leads. That feels way too easy, but I can't say why. Also, someone told me there's a newer approach that says drop the criteria altogether and instead study how memories of Jesus got shaped and passed around in the early communities — is that a real alternative or just vibes?

And please tell me how confident you actually are in what you're saying, and flag the point you're least sure about. I don't want a smooth confident summary that's really just the standard line recited back.
