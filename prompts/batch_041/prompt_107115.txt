I belong to a reading group on what is often called prosaic AI alignment — the question whether systems built by scaling the methods we already have (pretraining, human-feedback fine-tuning, and their immediate descendants), with no new conceptual insight, can be made to reliably pursue what we intend. One member has been pressing an analogy that has quietly become the group's reference point, and I would like a proper audit of it rather than another round of competing assertions.

His analogy is that training a model by gradient descent is like evolution by natural selection. Evolution optimized for reproductive success, he says, and produced organisms — ourselves — whose motives are largely indifferent to it: we use contraception, raise adopted children, and pursue ends that do nothing for our genetic legacy. Gradient descent optimizes the training loss, he infers, and will likewise produce systems whose internal objectives come apart from that loss. The moral he draws is that the prosaic programme is in trouble: current methods can manufacture capable systems whose goals drift from what the training signal rewarded, and scaling the same methods further will not repair the drift.

I want to know how much of this the analogy can actually carry, and I would like the audit written to the following plan.

The analogy and its work (roughly 150 words). Restate, as neutrally as you can, what the analogy is being asked to do: the conclusion it is meant to license, and the inferential step in the middle that the conclusion depends on. Make clear what would have to be true of the mapping for the conclusion to follow.

The correspondence table (roughly 250 words, table form). A table with one column for the element in the evolutionary story and one for the element it is supposed to correspond to in gradient-descent training. Include the awkward middle terms rather than skipping them: the base objective, the organism's own objective, the environment, the selection pressure, the population, the generation. Where a source item has no honest target counterpart, say so in the cell instead of inventing one.

Where the mapping holds (roughly 200 words). Which correspondences are genuinely load-bearing for his conclusion, and which of those actually hold up? Separate the ones the argument needs from the ones that merely make the story vivid.

Where it breaks (roughly 400 words). The disanalogies, each stated plainly, and then sorted: which of them matter for the conclusion he is drawing and which are irrelevant to it. I expect the interesting cases to be the ones where the source has a feature the target lacks but the conclusion still survives, and the reverse.

What the analogy imports (roughly 200 words). Features of the evolutionary picture that get carried along silently and that his conclusion may be leaning on without argument — an open-ended environment, a selection pressure measured in the world rather than computed, many generations, a population of variants, no hand that chooses the objective. For each, say whether gradient-descent training has anything playing that role.

Whether the conclusion survives (roughly 250 words). Your verdict, and if the strong conclusion fails, the strongest version of it that the analogy does support — or, if the analogy is doing no work at all and the conclusion rests on something else, say what that something else is.

A better analogy (roughly 150 words). If there is one that carries the same point with fewer smuggled premises, give it and say what it preserves and what it gives up. If the honest answer is that no analogy will do this job, say that instead and explain what the argument has to be made in its own terms.
