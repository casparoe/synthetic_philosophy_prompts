"Two-dimensional semantics is the idea that every expression has two meanings. The first meaning is the a priori one: it's basically the definition you'd give if you knew nothing about the world, the thing you're picking out by description. The second meaning is the a posteriori one: the actual object out there in the world that the word refers to. So really it's Frege's sense/reference distinction dressed up with a Kripkean twist — the sense is the first dimension, the reference is the second. Its big payoff is that it explains how a statement like 'water is H2O' can be necessary and still be something we only find out empirically, which vindicates the analytic/synthetic distinction after Quine tried to kill it. And it's what lets Chalmers argue that zombies are conceivable, which is supposed to show that physicalism is false."
