I'm a novelist, three books in, and I'm deep into a fourth — historical fiction set among American abolitionists in the 1840s and 50s, mostly around the split between the Garrison crowd and the people who followed Frederick Douglass once he decided the Constitution was on their side after all. The trouble is my two main characters have been having this arguement for two hundred pages now and I've started to suspect they aren't even having the same one. I want to write the document they should have written together instead.

So put on two hats for me: a constitutional historian of antebellum America, and someone who's genuinely good at the philosophy of interpretation and of language — what a text means, whose meaning counts, when a dispute is really just two people using one word differently. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming before you draft anything. I want to see you work out where the real fault lines are before you commit to any wording.

Then draft the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between the two camps would produce. On one side: the abolitionist who holds that the Constitution is a pro-slavery compact, a bargain struck with the slaveholders, and that the only honest move is to renounce the Union. On the other: the abolitionist who holds that the Constitution, read properly, is an antislavery document, and that the way forward is to hold the country to its own text. Both of these people are serious, neither is a fool, and neither will sign anything that sells out their view.

I want: a numbered list of claims both could actually sign — about what the text does and doesn't say (the clauses on apportionment, on the slave trade, on fugitives), about what the framers wanted versus what the words mean, about what follows for strategy if you win or lose the interpretive argument. Then the disagreements, each written in wording its own side would accept as fair to them. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move somebody. For each crux, give me the case, clause, or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts it shows. And if you decide part of this fight is verbal — if the two of them mean different things by 'the Constitution,' the text versus the compact versus the way the courts have actually run it — say so plainly and tell me which part.

Don't give me citations or a reading list, I can't use them, and please don't invent quotations and stick them in real dead people's mouths — paraphrase the positions, no quotation marks around things nobody said in those words. I care about the drafting being exact, not the sourcing. Numbered agreed claims first, then a disagreements table.
