I have no training in philosophy or in computer science — I work in IT support and I have spent the better part of a year reading about machine alignment in my spare time, mostly forum threads, a couple of popular books, and long arguments on a Discord server where nobody agrees about anything — and I have arrived at a package of views about keeping powerful artificial agents from wrecking things that I would like stress-tested before I inflict it on anyone else. Here is where I have landed. First, the single most useful safety rule for an agent of that kind is that it should stay inside the lines: do the thing it was asked to do and nothing else, and keep its footprint as small as it can, since I have seen this described as low-impact or minimal-impact design and it strikes me as the most promising idea in the area. Second, a side effect, as I want to use the term, is any difference between the world as it ends up and the world as it would have been had the agent done nothing at all; that comparison, and not the agent's intentions, is what should be scored. Third, the score ought to be as value-free as possible — a raw measure of how much is different, rather than a judgement about whether the change was for better or worse — because the whole appeal of the rule, for me, is that I do not have to hand the machine my ethics in order to get it to behave. Fourth, at the same time, I think an agent ought to be able to see that some outcomes are bad regardless of anyone's opinions: leaving a city in ruins and never rebuilding it is worse than rebuilding it, and that is not merely my taste. Fifth, and finally, I do not think the machine needs any theory of the good at all, because when it does not know what to do it should ask a person and then do what that person says.

What I want back is an audit rather than a defence or a general list of every objection under the sun. Which of these five actually pull against each other, and how badly — ranked by severity, not dumped in a heap? Which pairings only look inconsistent because of some confusion I ought to be able to see through, and what is the confusion? For each real conflict, tell me what the cheapest repair would be and what I lose by making it, bearing in mind that I would rather keep the spirit of the footprint rule than any particular formulation of it. Then tell me which single commitment, if I gave it up, would buy me the most consistency, and whether the remainder would still be worth holding afterwards. I would also like to know which of the five is doing no work — the one I could keep or drop without any of the others noticing — if there is one.
