I am trying to work out which of the available repairs to the doctrine of middle knowledge is actually worth defending, and I would like your ranking of them. The doctrine, as I understand it, is this: prior to any decree of his will, God knows what each possible free creature would freely do in each set of circumstances in which it might be placed, and it is this pre-volitional knowledge that allows him to arrange a world in which his purposes are achieved without determining any creaturely choice. The standing difficulty is that no truth-maker seems available for such counterfactuals of creaturely freedom. They cannot be true in virtue of God's will, since the knowledge is supposed to precede it; and they cannot be true in virtue of the creature's act, since no creature exists and none has yet chosen.

A number of replies are on offer. I have stated them roughly, and some are more standard than others:

1. The counterfactual is grounded in God's exhaustive grasp of the creature's nature, that is, in his knowing what a nature of that kind would do.
2. It is grounded in God's ideas of possible individuals, the creature figuring there as an object of divine understanding rather than as an existing agent.
3. It is grounded in God's decree, so that the counterfactuals hold only in virtue of the divine will, at the cost of making the knowledge natural or free rather than middle.
4. It is true but ungrounded: the counterfactual is a primitive truth, a brute fact about the possible creature or about the actual world.
5. It is not strictly true at all. What God knows in advance is that the creature might do this and might do that in such circumstances, and providence is planned over a distribution of mights rather than over a determinate would.
6. The counterfactual's real subject is not an individual creature but a world or an essence, and since worlds and essences do exist pre-volitionally, the truth-maker is available after all.
7. The framework should be relocated so that the counterfactual has God rather than the creature as its subject: what God knows is what he himself would do in response to each creaturely choice, with creaturely freedom preserved by conditional decrees.
8. The objection should be dissolved rather than answered: it rests on an over-general truth-maker principle, and once truth is distinguished from truth-grounding, the demand lapses.

Please rank these, adding any reply I have missed and noting any that are not really distinct from one another. The dimension I care about is how much of the doctrine's original theoretical work a reply preserves, and I want your weighting to be visible. There are four components: (a) pre-volitionality, so that the knowledge can explain how God's purposes are secured without determining the will; (b) the counterfactuals remaining genuinely about the creature's free act, so that the free-will defence and the manoeuvres about feasible worlds still run; (c) support for the claim that grace can be infallibly efficacious without being causally determinative, which is what middle knowledge was devised to secure in the first place, since a grace that is effective because it is congruent with the creature's free character is doing something a determining decree cannot do; and (d) whether the reply answers the grounding objection or merely relocates it, so that a position scoring well on the first three but supplying a truth-maker that itself stands in need of one should be marked down accordingly.

Give each reply a score out of ten, or arrange them into tiers if that makes the comparison cleaner, and defend each placement in a sentence or two. Then tell me which one you would bet on, which one is most popular among defenders and whether popularity tracks merit in this case, and what would have to be true for the reply you rank last to be the correct one. Finally, add a conditional verdict: if we set the grounding objection aside entirely and cared only about pre-volitionality and the efficacy of grace, which reply would come first, and does that reordering tell us anything about what the grounding objection is really doing. There is no need to attribute any of these positions to particular authors or to point me towards literature; I want them assessed on their merits.
