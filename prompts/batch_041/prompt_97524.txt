Last weekend my roommate and I were at a charity casino night, and one of the stalls has left me genuinely unsettled. The game is played with two opaque bags of marbles. The volunteer explains that bag A contains exactly fifty red marbles and fifty black ones, while bag B contains a hundred marbles that are red or black in some proportion she does not disclose — only that each marble is one of the two colours. You play two separate rounds; in each, you name a colour, draw a single marble from the bag of your choosing without looking, and win if it matches the colour you named. The stakes are a couple of tokens, so I doubt this is merely squeamishness about losing. When I played, I found that I wanted bag A in the round where I called red, and I also wanted bag A in the round where I called black; in both cases I would rather draw from the bag whose composition is known to me. My roommate says this is plainly incoherent: to prefer A for red is to think it likelier than B to yield red, to prefer A for black is to think it likelier than B to yield black, and no hundred marbles can be likelier than another hundred to yield both. I cannot muster a good reply, yet the preference does not feel like a blunder. It feels as though I am avoiding something rather than miscalculating, and I would like to know what.

Two details make me suspect the matter is subtler than my roommate allows. First, the volunteer who assembled the bags knows the composition of bag B and may well have chosen it herself, so drawing from B feels a little like accepting a wager whose terms were fixed by someone who knows more than I do. Second, I noticed that if she were to draw a marble from bag B first and set it aside face-down without showing anyone, I would suddenly be perfectly content to draw from B — which strikes me as odd, since a marble I cannot see should tell me nothing about the rest. I cannot tell whether one of these is a real consideration and the other a rationalisation, or whether both are beside the point.

Could you help me work out whether my roommate is right that I am simply being irrational, and if he is not, what it is that I am actually responding to and whether it survives scrutiny? I do not want to be told, flatly, to maximise expected value; I want to understand whether the discomfort I feel is tracking something genuine about the choice or is merely an artefact of how the situation is presented, and what would have to be true for my preference to be defensible. Where the two of us differ, please be explicit about which assumption is doing the work.
