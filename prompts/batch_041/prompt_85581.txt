I'm a second-year MA student and there's something I keep noticing in arguments that I don't have a word for, and at this point its distracting me. The shape is always the same. Someone says something, I ask why, they give a reason, I ask why that, and we climb — and at some point they say a version of "that's just what the practice is" or "at some point you have to accept something" and we stop. What gets me is that where we stop doesn't seem to have anything to do with the claim. With my officemate, who's read all the same stuff I have, the same claim gets two steps and we're both satisfied. With my brother-in-law, who's a lawyer, it takes six, and then he tells me the further question I'm asking doesn't really make sense — and he says it in the same tone he uses when he's actually right about something. In a seminar last term I watched the same thing happen and the chain ended on "that's what the framework says". Nobody in any of these cases thinks they're cheating, and honestly I don't think they are: the stop doesn't feel like "I'm bored of you," it feels like "the demand for one more reason isn't in order here." But that "in order" seems to be governed by something — there's a rule about when it's fair to keep asking — except the rule is different in different rooms, even when the evidence, the claim, and what the words mean are all held fixed. I know the three usual moves when someone asks whether the chain of reasons can run forever (you stop somewhere, you loop, or you never stop), and I don't think what I'm describing is any of them, because all three are about the shape of the chain and this is about the shape of the conversation: the chain gets cut at a different length depending on who's holding the other end, and both cuts look legitimate from where the person cutting is standing.

So: does this have a name? I'm not fishing for a reading list, and I'll be annoyed if the answer is just a label — what I actually want to know is how the thing I'm describing differs from the nearby things I'd probably get pointed at, whether it's one phenomenon or two (the "we share enough premises so the chain is short" case and the "the question has stopped being appropriate" case feel different to me and I can't tell if that's a real seam), and if there's no established name, coin one, define it, and tell me where the edges are — what's an instance, what looks like one but isn't, and what happens to it when the person asking has nothing at stake in the conversation at all.

Answer as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no closing summary.
