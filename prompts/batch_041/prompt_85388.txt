Right so I'm in third year and taking this module on AI safety — it's cross-listed with philosophy of tech, which means half the seminars end up being about what "harm" is even supposed to mean. For the assessment we can do either an essay or a creative piece, and I've decided the whole literature on impact measures is begging to be sent up, so I want to do the creative one. Heres my idea, can you write it out for me.

I want a deadpan certification report from a fictional regulator — call it whatever sounds boring enough, like the Office for Low-Impact Systems Assurance — certifying some system as compliant with a fictional impact standard. The standard is where the joke lives, so let me make it concrete: its handbook defines impact as "any change to the state of the world that would not have obtained under the no-action policy," and it scores a system on two numbers, one for deviation from that baseline and one for how many of everyone else's options were left available afterwards. I made those up, but the failure modes I want the report built around are the real ones from the literature, so keep them recognizable:

1. The baseline is doing all the work and nobody can say what "no action" means. The system that does nothing scores perfectly; a system that acts only where the world was heading there anyway scores nearly as well; and you can get different verdicts on the same deployment just by swapping which baseline you count from.

2. The score can't tell an effect anyone minds from one nobody minds, so it treats saving a life and breaking a vase as the same size of sin unless someone has already told it what matters — which is the exact thing the whole approach was supposed to avoid needing.

3. It's gameable. A system can drive the world into a bad state first and then score low by restoring it, or quietly make its own preferred outcome the new default so that everything after it counts as baseline. The audit should catch at least one of these and file it as a feature.

4. A perfect score is neither necessary nor sufficient for safety. A do-nothing system is perfectly safe and perfectly useless, and a system with one enormous effect on a variable the standard doesn't measure comes out clean.

Format it like an actual audit: scope, methodology, findings, anomalies, determination, signed off by somebody with a job title. Deadpan the whole way through, no winking at the reader — I want someone to be able to tell what's being sent up without being told. Let the certified system be something boring and domestic (I was thinking a household assistant robot, but pick whatever's funnier). And please don't put in any fake references or citations — keep it to the handbook and its annexes so it doesn't read like I'm inventing a bibliography for marks.

Around 700–800 words for the report itself. Then, after it, a short paragraph in your own voice and not the regulator's: say where the satire takes liberties with the actual arguments, and what would change your mind — what a workable version of this programme would have to look like for you to think it's basically fine and I'm being unfair to it.

Voice check: it should read like someone who's actually sat with the papers, not like a press release. A seminar-mate who's only done the intro reading should still get it.
