I am currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on the philosophy of history, and we have spent the past few weeks on the problem of contingency. Our instructor introduced a distinction that I have not encountered elsewhere: between load-bearing contingencies and decorative contingencies. A load-bearing contingency is a small, low-probability event whose occurrence changes which large-scale outcome obtains; a decorative contingency is a small event that alters only the local details of an outcome that would have obtained anyway. She then asked us to adjudicate a controversy: when historians describe history as chaotic, or say that it turns on a nail, are they committed to the claim that most consequential historical outcomes are load-bearing, or can a broadly chaotic causal structure still leave macrolevel outcomes robust? Write an essay that takes a position on this controversy. In doing so, distinguish between the claim that historical processes are deterministic but sensitive and the claim that they are genuinely indeterministic, and explain how each claim bears on the load-bearing/decorative distinction. Reach a clear conclusion about whether the idea that history turns on a nail is best understood as a thesis about causation, a thesis about explanation, or a rhetorical trope. Do not merely survey the literature. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
