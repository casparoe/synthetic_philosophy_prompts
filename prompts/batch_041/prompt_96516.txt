A colleague of mine who writes popular science has asked me to vet the draft of an article he is about to file on memes and cultural evolution, on the strength of my being the one person in our department who reads the philosophy of biology. This is the heart of it: "Memes are to culture what genes are to biology: discrete units of imitation that copy themselves from brain to brain, and the ones that survive are the ones that are best at copying, not the ones that do us any good. Dawkins coined the term in the 1970s, and the idea has since grown into memetics, a thriving science of cultural evolution. Because memes are replicators, culture evolves by natural selection in exactly the way genes do; only the substrate differs. The old objection that cultural transmission is too lossy to sustain Darwinian selection has been settled in memetics' favor, and the upshot is that any widespread cultural trait can be assumed to be an adaptation." I suspect the gene analogy is being asked to carry more weight than it can bear, but I cannot say precisely where he goes wrong, and he would be embarrassed to have a reviewer catch it instead of me. Go claim by claim: tell me what someone who works on cultural evolution would accept, what they would wince at, and what is flatly mistaken; say whether his closing inference survives once the errors are corrected; and tell me whether my own reservation is a real objection or merely aesthetic discomfort. Please write your answer in American English.
