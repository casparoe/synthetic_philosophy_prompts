I cover science for a living, and I have been stuck for a week on an argument I cannot referee. A friend of mine is deciding whether to have her dog's front dewclaws removed. Her vet says the claws are leftovers from an ancestor that needed them and that they do nothing now; her breeder insists the dog uses them to grip things and to brace when it turns, and that a structure with a job cannot be called functionless. Each of them thinks the other is simply wrong about a fact. My suspicion is that they are answering different questions without noticing, but I cannot work out whether that is true, and if it is, which of them is using the word the way biologists do. If a leftover structure is still used for something, does that make the use its function? If a structure was selected for a job in some ancestor and no longer contributes to survival or reproduction, does it still have a function, and can it meaningfully be said to malfunction? And is there any fact of the matter here at all, or is this a verbal dispute that a decent definition would dissolve — and if it is, does the definitional question bear on what she should do about the surgery, or should she decide that on grounds that leave the word out of it entirely?
