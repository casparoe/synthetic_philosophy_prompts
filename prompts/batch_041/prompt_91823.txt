I am preparing a session for my graduate seminar in epistemology, and I would like you to plan how I should teach it rather than explain the material to me. The students have already had Bayes' rule as a norm of update, the dynamic Dutch book argument for conditionalization, and enough probability to manipulate a partition; what they have not seen is any treatment of uncertain evidence. My aim for the ninety minutes is that Jeffrey conditionalization should arrive as the forced response to a problem they can feel, not as a formula handed down, and that by the end they should be able to say precisely what the rule does and does not settle. Give me the order in which the components must be introduced and the dependencies that dictate it, the single example that should carry the whole session and why it must have the features it has (and, relatedly, why I should not let the standard diagnostic-test example carry it), the misconceptions they will predictably form and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off, the check I should run at the end to see whether the distinction between certain and uncertain evidence has actually landed, and what I should openly refuse to cover at this level and say so.

The sequence I have in mind, which you should feel free to reject or reorder if the dependencies demand it, runs roughly as follows: the inadequacy of conditionalization when the agent's response to a perception or a report is a shift in the probabilities over a partition rather than certainty in a proposition; the rule itself, with the old conditional probabilities held fixed and only the weights over the partition moving; the temptation to collapse the rule into ordinary conditionalization by treating a proposition about the agent's own posterior credence as the certain evidence, and what is wrong with that; the status of the rigidity condition as a substantive constraint rather than a bookkeeping convenience; and the fact that successive updates of this kind do not generally commute, in contrast with conditionalization on propositions. I want the session to end at the edge of the rule's competence rather than at a tidy summary, so please build toward the following case and place it deliberately. An agent's prior is spread over four cells formed by two binary divisions — two regions, each with two kinds of position within it — and the information she receives bears on the relation between region and position rather than on which region she is in: she is told, in effect, that she is in the first region or else at the base of the second, so that what is constrained is a conditional probability rather than the new probability of any cell. The rule as stated simply does not deliver an answer here, and I want the plan to make that silence vivid and to resist supplying a numerical resolution as though it were forced; if some further principle is needed to get an answer, I want the students to see that a further principle is being invoked and that it is not the rule.

Among the misconceptions I expect, and would like mapped: that Jeffrey conditionalization is just conditionalization applied to a proposition about one's own credence; that rigidity is automatic or vacuous; that the order of successive uncertain updates is immaterial; that the rule is uniquely forced by Dutch book considerations rather than being one sufficient rule among several; that the rule can absorb any uncertain evidence whatever, including constraints on likelihoods or on conditional probabilities; and a simple confusion between the partition and the evidence proposition, with students treating the new cell probabilities as outputs of the rule when they are in fact its inputs. For the final check I would like something that distinguishes a student who can compute a posterior from one who has understood what the rule is a rule about — perhaps a fresh case in which the agent's observation is described only in likelihood terms, so that the right answer is to say what further information would be needed before the rule could be applied at all. I am content for the session to leave the measure-theoretic generalities, the general theory of minimizing relative entropy, and the wider literature on conditional constraints untouched, provided the plan says explicitly that these are being deferred and why.
