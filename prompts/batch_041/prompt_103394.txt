I'm giving the toast at my old supervisor's retirement dinner in a few weeks — sorry, retirement do; she'd correct me. She spent her whole career in a weird little corner of psychology: anomalistic psychology, but really just one question, namely what Turing's imitation game would even be measuring if telepathy turned out to be real. You know the bit in the paper where Turing introduces the imitation game, where he suddenly starts taking extrasensory perception seriously — not as a joke, but as a possible confound: if the interrogator could read the machine's mind, or the machine could read the interrogator's, then a pass wouldn't tell you anything about intelligence. She definately thought most people treat that passage as an embarrassment, but that it's actually the most honest moment in the paper. Her line was that a test is only as good as its background conditions; she called it the background-conditions problem, and she thought it applied to IQ tests, peer review, dating apps, everything. I want the toast to actually turn on that idea, not just mention it.

The room will be about half academic psychologists who know the Turing passage and half her family and old friends from her amateur radio club, who mostly don't. So it needs to land for the experts without losing everyone else. Please write a five-minute toast that's warm and affectionate, not a lecture. Maybe one gentle joke at her expense, but nothing mean. She's a vegetarian and a truly terrible driver, if that gives you any colour. British English, please. And don't make it too pious — she'd hate that.
