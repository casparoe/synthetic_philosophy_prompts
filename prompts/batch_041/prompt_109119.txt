My friend and I burned most of saturday arguing about whether the Holocaust is unique, and by the end neither of us could tell whether we disagreed about history or about what 'unique' is even supposed to mean. She's the comparability person: if you can't compare it to other mass killings you can't learn anything from it, and she thinks uniqueness talk quietly turns a crime into a sacred exception, which is very convenient for anyone who'd rather not ask why it happened. I'm on the other side, roughly: I think what matters isn't scale but that it wasn't a pogrom that got out of hand — it came out of distinctly modern things, the bureaucratic habit of treating a population as material to be reorganized, and the intent was total in a way that's different in kind. We kept sliding between 'unprecedented', 'incomparable', 'worse', and 'can't happen again', and I suspect half the fight is that we never pinned down which one we were defending.
