I spent thiry-odd years as a geologist and since I retired I've been trying to teach myself philosophy, mostly by running a small monthly discussion group — about twenty other retirees, a mix of scientists, engineers and a couple of doctors — at our local U3A. I've got the next session to lead and I want to do the paradox of voting, the thing where it seems like it can't be rational to bother voting at all. But I don't want to just talk at them about it. I want them to actually feel the squeeze.

Here's what I've got so far, and I'd like you to turn it into a proper runnable session.

The setup: the group has to pick one of three things — I'll use three options for what we do at the following month's meeting, call them A, B and C, because it's something they genuinely care about a little, which matters. I'll hand out preference slips so the room divides exactly into three equal blocks:

- a third rank them A, then B, then C
- a third rank them B, then C, then A
- a third rank rank them C, then A, then B

(I'll make the headcount divisible by three, so 18 or 21 people.)

If you take those blocks and run pairwise majority votes, you get A beating B, B beating C, and C beating A — rock, paper, scissors, no winner. And if you just ask everyone for their single favourite you get a dead three-way tie. So the first thing I want them to bang into is that there's no "will of the people" sitting there waiting to be discovered; the answer depends on how you ask the question.

Then the second half. Once they've felt that, I want to add the cost. Everyone starts with, say, three tokens (redeemable for something at the end — biscuits, whatever). To cast a vote you have to drop one token in the box. And whatever wins gets delivered to everyone, voters and abstainers alike. And here's the twist I want you to build the mechanics around: I'm going to announce up front that the outcome will be decided by drawing a handful of ballots (say five) at random out of the box, not by counting them all. So your one vote only ever counts if it happens to get drawn and happens to be the one that breaks a tie. Voting costs you a token for certain; abstaining is free.

I want the whole thing to run about 75 minutes and I'd like it to mostly run itself while I keep my mouth shut and just note down what happens.

What I need from you: the actual rules and a script for me (what I say, when, what I hand out), the materials, the payoff scheme spelled out so its fair, a rough timeline for the 75 minutes, and — the part I care about most — a debrief that takes what just happened in that room and shows them the pieces of the real problem: why the instrumental case for voting collapses once the electorate gets big, why the aggregation itself can be incoherent, why people vote anyway, and where the strategic temptation shows up (voting for your second choice to block your third). Tell me honestly what will go wrong the first time I run it and how I should handle it, and tell me which part of the paradox this setup simply can't reproduce, so I know what I'll have to say myself instead of letting them discover it.

Oh, and no reading lists or citations please — half of them will nod along and never look anything up, and I can't have the session hinge on names I'd only get wrong anyway.
