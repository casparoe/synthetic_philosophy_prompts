I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have agreed to lead a session of my company's reading group — a dozen engineers and two product managers, all comfortable with recursion and system design, none with any background in the area — on the idea of HCH together with the associated treatment of deliberation as factored cognition. As I understand the picture: there is an idealized deliberator that, whenever it gets stuck on a problem, may hand a smaller sub-question to a fresh copy of itself, where that copy begins with only the sub-question and returns only an answer that the caller is expected to be able to check. Please plan how I should teach this rather than explain it to me. Specifically: what has to be established first, and in what order, so that the later moves feel necessary rather than arbitrary; which single concrete case should carry the whole session, and what that case must include — and must conspicuously not include — for it to do the work (I am inclined to build it out of an on-call escalation chain, but tell me if something else carries it better and why); which misconceptions my audience is likely to bring or form — my guesses are "this is just recursion" and "if the copies are independent, then it is just parallelism" — and at exactly which point in the sequence each should be headed off; one question I can put to the room at the end that would reveal whether they have understood what the idealization is for, as opposed to merely following the mechanics; and what I should deliberately omit at this level, saying openly that I am omitting it.
