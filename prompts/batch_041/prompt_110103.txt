I'm a novelist about a year out from drafting, and I've got three families to write: one that answers a slight by handling it personally, one that files the complaint and trusts the institution to sort it out, one that goes public with the injury and works the audience until someone with authority has to respond. I've been chewing on the three-way split people make here — honor cultures, dignity cultures, victimhood cultures, i.e. self-help vs. impersonal rules vs. appeal to third parties — and it's been generative, but now I'm stuck, because there are maybe fifteen open questions in there and I've only got room in my head for two or three before I start writing.

So which of those open questions actually matter most, and why? I want them ranked, with the reasoning shown: which are upstream of which, which are secretly the same question wearing different hats, which look deep but are realy just taxonomy fights that won't change a single scene, and which would change how I write an argument between two characters. Is the victimhood type genuinely a new thing, or is it martyrdom and romantic suffering with a fresh vocabulary? Is dignity culture actually stable on its own, or does it quietly depend on the honor logic it claims to have replaced? Do the three categories survive contact with the non-Western cases I keep bumping into, or are they ideal types nobody instantiates? Tell me what would move your #1 down the list — what kind of case or evidence would do it.

And then the thing I keep circling: everyone insists one of these cultures is morally superior to the others, usually the one they were raised in. Which of those claims actually have teeth and which are overrated or don't even follow? Spell out the criteria — better at what, for whom, measured how — and tell me whether the obvious points (the body counts in honor cultures, say, or the status competition for victimhood, or the way dignity culture maybe only works because the state has a monopoly on violence) settle anything or are malformed from the start.

I'd rather have a short defended list than a survey. And if the three-way split itself is the overrated thing, definately say so — I can take it.
