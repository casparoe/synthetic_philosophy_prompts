I'm a second-year PhD in philosophy of mind / cog sci and I need a two-page proposal for an internal fellowship — one year, my own time, no lab, so it has to be doable by one person thinking and maybe coding a little. Here's my half-formed idea, please give it a spine.

Everyone who uses "sphexishness" as a foil — the wasp that keeps re-dragging the cricket to the burrow every time you shift it, the loop it can't climb out of — treats the rigidity as a fact about the creature. I think the predicate is doing two jobs that come apart. Local sphexishness: one specific loop, triggered by one specific perturbation, which is all the wasp case actually shows and which plenty of agents have, us included, in particular circumstances. Global sphexishness: an entire repertoire that's finite, trigger-indexed, and cannot re-describe its own situation to itself. The free-will argument needs the global notion, and the wasp only delivers the local one. And there's a second problem: rigidity looks observer-relative. A good enough predictor of any system will find its responses "merely mechanical," so if sphexishness just means "predictable from the outside," it's a fact about our grip, not about the agent — and the flexibility criterion for free will collapses into an epistemic one.

Turn this into a proposal: state the question so it has a determinate answer, say why the going positions leave it open (positions and arguments, no citations — I'll add those), then the method month by month. I want the method to include actually constructing a minimal agent where the two notions come apart — ideally both directions: locally looped but not globally rigid, and globally rigid yet never perturbed so it never fails a loop test. Say what a result looks like, what would count as the project failing, and be honest about whether a year is enough or whether this is secretly a dissertation.
