Write a deadpan product review, in the house style of a serious consumer-technology magazine whose readership is mostly philosophers and philosophically minded scientists, of an invented consumer device marketed as a way to "turn conceivability into possibility." Invent the product and the company; do not name any real manufacturer, and do not attribute claims to any living person. The review should be roughly 800 words and should carry the following named sections, in this order and at approximately these lengths: "The pitch" (~120 words), "What arrives" (~120 words), "In use" (~300 words), "The fine print" (~150 words), and "Verdict" (~110 words).

The humour must come from getting the modal epistemology right, so that a philosopher working on modality laughs rather than winces. The device should have plausible-sounding features that quietly presuppose or beg the very question the inference from conceivability to possibility is supposed to answer: for example, a dial distinguishing prima facie from ideal conceivability that the manual says should be left on its default; a "negation" button; a mode that re-runs an input through a second, two-dimensional reading; a filter that flags a posteriori necessities and warns that an output is at most epistemically possible; a preset that returns a confident verdict on philosophical zombies while refusing to output anything at all for the corresponding physicalist worlds; and a coherence alarm that, the reviewer notes, never once sounds. Let the reviewer test the device on ordinary cases and on a few contested ones, and let the reader come away seeing that the product either rebuilds possibility into its definition of conceivability or is simply unreliable — without the review ever stating that directly. Keep the voice straight-faced throughout: no winking, no editorialising about the satire, and no breaking character to explain the joke. Avoid quoting or citing any real text, and avoid relying on titles, dates, or attributions.
