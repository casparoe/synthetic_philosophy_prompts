I'm on the board of a housing co-op, about 60 units, and I keep hitting the same wall whenever we have a big decision, and I've started to think it's not just us. Two fights last year: whether to sell the empty lot behind the building to a developer, and whether to absorb the six-unit building next door that asked to join us. Both times we spent the whole meeting not on the question but on who was even allowed to vote on it, and it always comes back to the same sentence — everyone who'll be affected should get a say. But you can't say who's affected until you know what the decision is, and you can't decide until you know who's voting. The people who'd live in the new building aren't here yet; the neighbour whose light gets blocked isn't a member; the guy on the third floor swears the merger will raise his fees. Everyone's affected by everything, so 'affected' either means everyone, which can't be right, or it's doing no work at all. Then somone says, fine, it's the people already bound by our rules, the current members — clean answer, but it feels like taking the current answer as the answer. Is there a name for this? I don't just want the label. The two replies people give — 'everyone touched by it' versus 'everyone already under our rules' — aren't the same answer dressed up, they're genuinely different, and I want to know which is supposed to be the real one and whether either can avoid going round in a circle. And here's the thing that really nags me: our co-op's own edge isn't ours to set — the city and the province decide what a co-op can be and who can be pushed in or out. So even if we sorted it for us, whoever's above us has the identical problem, all the way up until someone doesn't. Is that the same problem one level down, or a different one that only looks the same?
