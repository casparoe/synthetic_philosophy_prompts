I've been in the FIRE world for about six years now — decent savings rate, accounts maxed, and I spend way too much time in a Discord where people argue about whether 3.5% is the new 4% and whether Coast FIRE is cheating. Lately I've been reading Keynes — that 1930 essay where he predicts his grandchildren (us) will be on fifteen-hour weeks within a hundred years, so basically around now, and says the real problem won't be scarcity anymore but figuring out how to live well with all the freedom, plus some of a memoir he wrote near the end of his life about what he believed as an undergrad, where he says the whole thing was about states of mind rather than actions, that he and his friends had no use for general rules or duty, and then sort of confesses later that this left them blind to how much civilization depends on conventions and rules being held up by a very small number of people. It's the second part that got me, because everyone in my Discord treats the good life as a design problem — pick your number, hit your number, then build a life you actually want instead of optimizing for a finish line — and I have a nagging feeling Keynes is asking something that doesn't quite fit into that.

So what I want from you is a translation exercise, not a summary. Restate Keynes's view of the good life in the vocabulary I actually use — savings rate, FI number, withdrawal rate, sequence-of-returns risk, one-more-year syndrome, hedonic treadmill, purpose-after-FI, all of it — as faithfully as you can and piece by piece: first the 1930 prediction and his picture of life after the economic problem is solved, then the states-of-mind ethics and the rejection of general rules, then the later admission about conventions and the fragility of the whole arrangement. And then the part I actually care about, the residue report. Which pieces map cleanly (my suspicion is the 1930 essay maps almost too well onto the post-FI aimlessness everyone complains about — tell me if that's cheap), which pieces only survive if you fudge them, and which have no counterpart at all in how FIRE people talk? And when something doesn't translate, say whether that's because the FIRE framework is genuinely missing a dimension of value it can't represent, or because Keynes was being vague and my vocabulary is just exposing it.
