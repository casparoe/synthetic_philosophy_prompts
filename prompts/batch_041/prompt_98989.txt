I'm a PhD student in musicology writing on Schopenhauer's aesthetics, and I want a poem to open a seminar on his account of music. The claim I most want the poem to get right is the one that makes the view so strange: music, alone among the arts, is not a copy of the Ideas but a direct copy of the Will itself, so that it bypasses the ladder of objectification that painting, sculpture and poetry must climb. Could you write it as a chaconne, with a ground bass of one fixed line repeated unchanged at the foot of every stanza while the voices above it move through the grades of the Will's objectification, from the blind forces of nature up to the human? The ground must stay exactly the same each time and the upper voices must change, so that the form itself says what the doctrine says; then, at the point where music enters, let the pattern break, so that music reaches the ground directly, without having climbed through the intervening voices at all. A musician should be able to hear the form and a philosopher should recognise the doctrine, though the poem need not name Schopenhauer.
