Been reading a lot of Greek history this year (Thucydides, Xenophon, the Socrates trial stuff) and there's a shape of thing I keep bumping into that I dont have a word for, and I'm not sure anyone does. The pattern: an assembly votes something drastic, and then the same men, with essentially the same facts in front of them, vote the opposite not long after. Mytilene -- kill every adult male, then next day spare them, with the first ship already out of the harbor carrying the order. Sicily -- the expedition voted, then a commander recalled once the fleet is already sailing. Socrates -- the jury splits almost evenly on guilt, and then once he's been allowed to propose his own punishment they vote him dead by a landslide. Arginusae -- the generals condemned all together in one vote on a show of hands, and then, if I've got this right, the same city later turns on the men who pushed that vote. The part that won't let me go is that there's no moment where the assembly is wrong. Both votes are the assembly. The second isn't an appeal to anything the first lacked -- no new evidence, no higher body, no rule about not deciding while furious. So "they fixed a mistake" and "they changed their minds" pick out the same event, and there's no fact of the matter about what the body actually thought, only about what it thought at 4 p.m. on Tuesday. I also read that Athens had a procedure where a decree the assembly had already passed could still be attacked afterward as illegal -- the thinking being that the assembly, being accountable to nobody, could by a kind of structural fiction do no wrong, so the only way to undo a law was to go after whoever had proposed it. Is there a name for the underlying thing? I don't mean demagoguery or mob fickleness, those are about causes; I mean the structural part, where the body is its own only court of review so its output is a function of when you ask it. I know there's probably something in social choice about votes that don't stay put, and something about tying your own hands in advance, but I want the carving, not the label. So: what is this exactly, what isn't it (vs. a body that was simply misinformed the first time, vs. one whose membership changed, vs. plain preference reversal in one person), and where are the edges (does it need the same people? the same facts? a short interval? does precommitment dissolve it or just move it somewhere else?). If nothing established fits, coin a term and define it so I can tell when it applies.
