I hold a practice-dependent account of moral considerability for video-game characters: a character matters morally when the game's rules and affordances position it as a locus of agency whose interests can be frustrated or satisfied within the game's normative economy, and this status is not reducible either to player sentiment or to the character's merely fictional existence. What would count against this position? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: which cases, arguments, or results in the metaphysics of fiction, moral status, or game design would a defender have to concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss? In particular, tell me what would have to be true for the view to be false, what it predicts that rival accounts do not, and whether it risks anything at all or has been insulated by qualifications. I would be most persuaded by a case in which the game's affordances and the character's fictional status come apart.
