I am building a diagnostic quiz for an advanced seminar on political guilt, and I want it to sort respondents by the view they actually hold rather than by the label they would pick for themselves; it should read as an instrument, not an essay. The question is whether a nation can be guilty for crimes committed by earlier generations.

The scoring key must have a leaf for each of the following: Jaspers' own four-fold scheme of criminal, political, moral and metaphysical guilt, in which political guilt falls on every member of the defeated nation; the liberal view that only individuals can be guilty, so talk of national guilt is at best a metaphor for collective liability; the view that descendants do inherit guilt; the view that they inherit liability or responsibility but not guilt; and the view that the nation is a fiction and the question therefore ill-formed. Add whatever further leaf the logical space requires, labelling it unnamed if nobody you can identify defends it.

The axis I care most about is one I have introduced myself and want the quiz to test: guilt, which requires the bearer to have done the deed; accountability, which requires only a standing relation to it, such as having benefited from it, having authorised it, or continuing to identify with the body that acted; and standing, which is neither of these but a special authority to speak and act in the name of the past. My suspicion is that the third cell is empty and that most respondents who call themselves guilty mean the second, and I want the instrument to show whether that is so.

Use concrete cases only, eight to twelve of them, each with two or three answer options written so that a respondent cannot tell which leaf an answer leads to. Cases I want included: a citizen whose own family was persecuted by the same state; a citizen whose family arrived after the crimes; someone who actively resisted at the time; a state that has since changed its constitution and lost territory; and a state paying reparations, where the question is the ground of the payment rather than its size.

Close with the key: the answer patterns that yield each leaf, any leaves the instrument cannot separate and why, and, for each empty or thinly populated combination, whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
