I want to put a position about creativity on the table and have you map its exposure. The position, in my own terms: every creative act is a variation on an existing theme, and invention is the exploration of a conceptual space by turning the knobs that space already affords — the creative move proper is perceiving which features of a theme are variable and which are fixed, not producing anything from nothing. What would count against this? Distinguish the cases of apparent radical novelty that are real threats from those the view can absorb as variation at a higher level; say what the view predicts that a rival account of creativity would not; and give an honest verdict on whether it has been insulated into unfalsifiability, since any seeming counterexample can be redescribed as a variation on something. Consider also a refinement I am tempted by — that the space itself can be twiddled, so that there are second-order knobs governing which first-order knobs exist — and tell me whether that rescues the view or empties it. Where the view's defenders would themselves concede ground, say so, and rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. What would move me most is a case in which the dimensions themselves, not merely the settings, are the creative achievement. If the position is really a framework rather than a claim, and asking what counts against it is the wrong frame, tell me that plainly rather than manufacturing defeaters. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
