our past calls:

- older guy, huge bleed, no brainstem reflexes at all, flat eeg, on a vent. we said dead, family agreed, done. unanimous, took four minutes.
- woman with a flat eeg but her brainstem was intact — still triggering the vent on her own, still had a gag. family wanted us to call her dead and stop. we refused. the way one of us put it: her body was still running itself as a unit, so she wasnt dead, she was just gone upstairs.
- our donation-after-cardiac-death pathway: team withdraws the vent, we wait for the heart to stop, then we wait five minutes of silence before anyone goes near the patient for organ recovery. unanimous.
- a heart stopped and then started up again on its own about two minutes later. we logged it as a near miss and wrote that if we'd declared him dead at the two minute mark we'd have been wrong. we did not say anything about what would have been fine at five.
- a patient with a dnr whose heart stopped and the team correctly did nothing. we said he wasnt dead the instant we decided not to restart him — he became dead at some later point, and we didnt bother pinning down when.

new case: guy in his forties, awful head injury, not brain dead by our usual test — he still coughs, he still fights the vent a little — but he's never waking up and the family has agreed to withdraw. he goes on the donation pathway. vent off at 11:04, heart stops. at 11:09, five minutes flat, our intensivist declares him dead. at 11:11 the heart starts up again by itself. it beats for another four minutes and then stops for good. nobody had touched him yet, no organs were taken, no harm done in that sense. now the family is asking whether he was dead at 11:09 or whether we only thought so, and the surgeon wants to know if five minutes should be ten.

two of our nurses have been going at each other about this and i cant tell who's right. one says permanence isnt the same as irreversibility — permanent just means nobody's going to reverse it, and nobody was. the other says if it started again then it obviously wasnt irreversible, so at 11:09 he wasnt dead, we just got lucky.
