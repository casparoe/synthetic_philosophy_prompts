Hi — I'm making a handout for the first meeting of a grad seminar on pain, and I'd rather hand them a decision tree than a reading list. The entry point is the asymbolia cases: people who report that a stimulus hurts, sometimes badly, but who are completely untroubled by it — no flinching, no pulling away, no wanting it to stop. I want the tree to take a student from "fine, I accept that such patients exist and that this is what they say" to a named position on what makes pain bad.

Each internal node should be a question a student can answer without already knowing which view it commits them to, and each leaf a position on the badness of pain. Put the usual suspects on the leaves — pain is bad in virtue of its intrinsic feel; the badness lives in a separate unpleasantness/affective component; pain is bad because of a desire it creates; because of an imperative it issues; because of what it represents, e.g. bodily damage; and the deflationary view that the badness just is the aversion — but build the tree out of the questions that split them, not out of a list of names.

Things I specifically want it to get at. (1) The fork between "so asymbolic pain is still bad, the patient just doesn't register the badness" and "so asymbolic pain isn't bad at all" — that seems like the real divide and I want it visible. (2) Whether a view is allowed to say there's badness that nobody is bothered by, or whether being bothered is constitutive of badness. (3) Whether the asymbolic case is evidence about badness at all, or only about motivation and action — a view could hold that badness is untouched and asymbolia just knocks out the urge to escape. (4) If your tree leans on the sensory vs. affective distinction, say whether any leaf survives without it, since I've got students who think its a mess.

Flag any leaves your tree can't separate, and note branch combinations nobody seems to occupy, saying whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. About a page of actual tree is plenty. American English, please.
