I've been stuck in the same arguement about the arrow of time for about three years and it never moves, and I'd rather have a diagnosis than another round. One side says the direction of time just is the thermodynamic arrow — entropy increases, that's what "later" means, and there's nothing left to explain. The other side says that's backwards: the second law is already asymmetric and the low-entropy past (the Past Hypothesis) is something you put in by hand, so thermodynamics presupposes the arrow instead of producing it, and the arrow has to be primitive or grounded somewhere else. A third person in the group insists the whole thing is a word game about what "the direction of time" is supposed to name. My hunch is that most of the stall comes from people sliding between the arrow as a feature of the world and the arrow as a feature of our descriptions, and that the real question underneath is what an "explanation" of temporal direction could even be — does it have to reduce direction to something non-temporal, or is taking it as given allowed? Is that the right diagnosis, or is the hidden issue something else, like whether the low-entropy past is a law or a contingent boundary condition, and whether that fight is itself just about the word "law"? And does it matter that the second law is a statement about ensembles while we only get one universe — does that make "the arrow is statistical" incoherent, or is that a red herring? Tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is, and what would count as progress.
