I'm in a term-long reading group on two-dimensional semantics — primary and secondary intensions, the epistemic versus the subjunctive dimension, and what any of it is supposed to buy us — and we keep colliding with a one-liner that one member treats as a settled principle: "if it's conceivable, it's possible." He deploys it in both directions. When someone presses him on the zombie argument, he says the objection begs the question, because the conceivability of the scenario just is the evidence of its possibility; and when someone asks what the two-dimensional apparatus is contributing, he says it is exactly what licenses the move, since the whole point of splitting meaning into an epistemic and a subjunctive dimension is to let conceivability, which lives on the epistemic side, count as a guide to possibility, which lives on the other. The rest of us are less sure. One member says the slogan is empty, since it is conceivable by any ordinary standard that water is not H2O, and yet that could not have been true; a second says the slogan is true but far too weak to do any work in the zombie argument; a third suspects that the four of us are simply disagreeing about what "conceivable" means.

What I want from you is not a verdict on the zombie argument but a map of the principle itself. Lay out the distinct precise claims that "if it's conceivable, it's possible" could be making: what kind of conceivability is in play (prima facie or idealized, positive or negative, imaginability or mere absence of contradiction), what kind of possibility is meant (epistemic, logical, metaphysical), whether the principle is being offered as an entailment or as defeasible evidence, and whether it is a claim made inside a two-dimensional framework or the very thing the framework was built to explain. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and say which of the four of us it would actually support.

Finally, tell me which reading the zombie argument actually needs, whether my friend is entitled to that reading, and where precisely the water case bites — I want a menu I can take back to the group rather than another round of us talking past one another.
