A friend of mine in a cognitive science masters summarized Dennett's view of the self for me like this: Dennett says the self is just a story we tell about ourselves, a useful fiction with no basis in the brain, like a character in a novel. There's no real self underneath, just narratives, and since it's all made up we can rewrite who we are more or less at will. The upshot, he says, is that any robust sense of personal identity or moral responsibility is a leftover illusion that a scientific picture should dissolve. He said Dennett calls this the self as a center of narrative gravity. I buy that Dennett is a deflationist about the self, but the "no real self, just a story you can rewrite" part sounds like it might be importing something Dennett wouldn't accept. Can you go claim by claim: what would Dennett himself sign off on, what's a common simplification or a conflation of two things he keeps apart, and what's just wrong? And if the parts that survive are right, does the conclusion about moral responsibility follow, or does Dennett have resources, say from his compatibilism about free will, to block it?
