I am giving the opening remarks at a two-day workshop called Myth and the Archive, and I need the speech. The room will be about half professional historians and archaeologists and half members of the public, including a local community group that treats the Exodus as its founding story. The workshop's three cases are the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus. I want the remarks to be built around a distinction I have been calling the boredom criterion: a legend begins to turn into history not when it is confirmed by something dramatic, but when it starts preserving details too dull to have been invented—the name of a minor official, a pointless detour, a logistical annoyance. Please write the five-minute opening speech. It should use that criterion to frame the three cases without pretending to settle any of them, and it should be candid with the experts while remaining accessible and respectful to the community members. The speech should not quote sources or appeal to authorities; the idea itself has to do the work.
