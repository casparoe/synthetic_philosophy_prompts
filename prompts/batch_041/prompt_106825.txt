For a graduate seminar on Kant's practical philosophy, I'm writing the unit exam on the supposed right to lie. I want a single essay question built around the case Kant takes up from Constant — the murderer at the door asking whether your friend is hiding inside — but it must not be answerable by reciting that Kant forbids lying even to save a life and then rehearsing the standard objections. The question should force students onto the interpretive crux: whether the argument in that essay actually requires a truthful declaration to the murderer or only forbids a false one, and what it therefore implies about evasion, silence, and misleading without asserting. It should also press them on whom the duty of veracity is owed to — the murderer, the agent himself, or humanity — and on whether Kant's claim that the truth-teller escapes responsibility for the consequences is a juridical or an ethical claim. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific interpretive moves each must contain.
