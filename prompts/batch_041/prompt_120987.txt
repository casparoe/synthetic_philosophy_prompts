ok, I need to write a short paper arguing for a position on Hempel's raven paradox, and I want the argument to be sharper than the standard line that the white shoe does confirm the hypothesis, just a tiny bit. here's the thesis I want to defend: the paradox isn't really a problem about degrees of confirmation at all. it's a reductio of the assumption that confirmation is preserved under logical equivalence.

the setup is the usual one. H: all ravens are black. H*: all non-black things are non-ravens. H and H* are logically equivalent — same truth conditions, same models, no difference you could ever detect. a white shoe is an instance of H*. if instances confirm their generalizations, the shoe confirms H*. if confirmation is preserved under equivalence, the shoe confirms H. then everyone either bites the bullet or tries to quarentine the shoe with background knowledge or an irrelevant-evidence clause.

I want you to argue that the quarantine move is a dodge and the real lesson is the failure of equivalence-preservation. the argument I want you to develop: confirmation is a relation between evidence and a hypothesis as formulated, because when we ask whether e confirms h we're asking whether e raises the probability of h given the questions we're actually putting to the world, and those questions are individuated by the predicates we use to carve up the domain. ravens and non-ravens are not two ways of saying the same thing when you're actually out there sampling; they pick out different procedures, different reference classes, different ways of being wrong.

then handle the three replies I expect a referee to make:

1. that this just relabels the problem — if confirmation isn't preserved under equivalence then it isn't a logical relation at all, and we've lost the whole project.
2. that my view makes confirmation depend on psychology or salience, which is worse than just biting the bullet.
3. that the raven case is only weird because ravens are rare and shoes are abundant, so my view is just a dressed-up version of the sampling-rate explanation.

for each one, give the reply and then the counter-reply to the reply. and end by saying what my view predicts in a case where the two formulations have the same reference class and the same sampling rate — whether there is such a case, and if there isn't, why not.
