Write a villanelle in the voice of A. J. Ayer, addressed to the two figures he reported encountering during the near-death experience he had not long before he died — after his heart stopped and he was revived, he described a red light and two beings he took to be charged with running the universe, and he conceded that the episode had slightly weakened his confidence that death is the end, while maintaining that he remained an atheist and that no one should treat it as evidence for survival. The two refrains should be the two claims he could never hold together: the verificationist verdict that a statement about surviving death is not false but meaningless, since nothing could count for or against it; and the first-person insistence that something happened, and that it left him less sure. Let the refrains keep returning because neither can absorb the other, and in the final two lines let them meet so that the deadlock shows rather than resolves — the poem should end with him still putting his case to the two of them, since for him the question was never settled by the experience, only made harder to dismiss. Somewhere in the poem, register his own later concession that survival might at least be a coherent hypothesis if a person is construed as a construction out of memories and experiences rather than a soul — a concession that gives the claim empirical sense in principle and cuts both ways, since it makes the question answerable to evidence he never had. The voice, the refrains, and the argument are what matter; no citations or essay titles needed.
