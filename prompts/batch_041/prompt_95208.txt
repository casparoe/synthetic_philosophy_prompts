I have spent the past two years as the note-taker for the release-review committee at a mid-sized nonprofit AI lab, and next week I am the one drafting the recommendation memo for a decision that the committee keeps deferring. The difficulty is that our own track record, which I have summarized below, seems to license two or three incompatible rules, and each member of the committee is quoting a different precedent at me. In reverse chronological order:

- Three months ago we released the weights of a model whose known jailbreak let it work through a set of hazardous chemistry questions, on the grounds that the questions appear in undergraduate textbooks and the jailbreak was circulating on forums within a day of the model's release.
- Seven months ago we released the weights of a 34B dense model that scored, on our own evaluations, at or slightly above the level at which we had earlier withheld a 70B model. The minutes record that a rival lab was weeks away from publishing something comparable, and that withholding would have cost us standing in the field without changing what anyone could download.
- Eleven months ago we withheld a 13B model trained from scratch on a new data pipeline; it was the strongest model we had seen on agentic computer-use tasks outside the frontier labs. The minutes say only that it was not reproducible from public artifacts.
- Fourteen months ago we released the weights of a 13B fine-tune of another lab's publicly released base model, even though our domain fine-tuning raised its score on a bio-relevant multiple-choice evaluation appreciably. The reasoning was that the base was already public and that anyone with a few GPU-weeks could have done what we did.
- Eighteen months ago we withheld a roughly 70B chat model, our strongest at the time, and shipped only an API behind a usage policy. The minutes describe it as the first open-weights model at that level, though nothing about its evaluation numbers was unprecedented.
- Two years ago we released a 7B base model with no safety tuning at all, because nothing on any evaluation looked concerning.
- And one case that fits none of the others: a 3B model that scored unusually high on a narrow persuasion evaluation, withheld despite being weak on everything else, because we were uncertain and the cost of waiting looked low.

The case before us is a 30B mixture-of-experts model with roughly 4B active parameters, so it runs on a single consumer GPU once quantized. It is a fine-tune of a base model whose weights a different lab released publicly about eighteen months ago, under a license that plainly permits derivatives. What is new is our post-training: we built an environment of 2,400 containerized vulnerable services with automated exploit verification, and used it for reinforcement learning on verifiable tasks. On a held-out set of 200 such services, measured as the fraction fully exploited within a fixed budget, the best open-weights model available today scores 11%, our withheld 70B scored 22%, our withheld 13B scored 19%, and this model scores 46%. The environment took four of us nine months to build, is not public, and our best guess is that a well-resourced team could rebuild something equivalent in about six months for a couple of million dollars; the base model and the general RL technique are both public. We have heard that a larger lab is working on something comparable, but we have no date and no evidence that they have a comparable environment. I would like you to tell me what we are going to decide and why. Specifically: set out the two or three rules that fit the precedents above equally well, say which precedents are actually load-bearing for each and which are decoration, and say what each rule predicts about this model; then give me your prediction, naming the precedent you take to be the closest analog and the reason you prefer it to the others; then name the one further case that would do the most to separate the surviving rules, and say what verdict each rule would give on it. If you think the precedents genuinely underdetermine this one, say so, and tell me what principle we would be adopting by releasing and what principle we would be adopting by withholding.
