so I host a small philosophy of mind podcast, and my next guest defends a higher-order thought theory of consciousness -- the actualist kind, where a mental state counts as conscious just when there's a suitable higher-order thought about it. he's been at this for years and has heard all the lazy objections, so I need questions that actually put him under pressure. draft me eight to ten, and for each one give a follow-up that's keyed to the answer he's most likely to give (not a generic "can you say more").

I want at least one question each on:
- what the theory says when the higher-order thought has no first-order state to be about -- the targetless / empty HOT case -- and whether he's then stuck saying that's being conscious of nothing;
- the misrepresentation problem, i.e. the HOT gets the first-order state's content wrong, so what is the subject actually conscious of;
- whether the HOTs have to be occurrent, and whether a dispositional version is really the same theory or a different one wearing the same name;
- the charge that the view only ever delivers access / reportability and never phenomenal consciousness propper;
- the regress worry -- a HOT is itself a mental state, so does it need to be conscious, and if not why doesn't the whole thing collapse;
- the generality problem, where the HOT has to be about the right first-order state and not just some state or other.

then two more: one that pins him down on whether HOTs are meant to be sufficient for a state's being conscious or merely necessary, and what actually turns on that. and make the final one a genuinely open question about what could count against the view -- something he can't wriggle out of by just re-describing the mechanism. keep the questions tight, no long wind-ups, I'll be reading them live off a sheet.
