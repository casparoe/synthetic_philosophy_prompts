I have been reading Kepler's astrological writings and have run into a passage I cannot make sense of. I am working from a translation I cannot check at the moment, so what follows is my paraphrase rather than a quotation: Kepler argues that the aspects — the angular relations between planets, such as the sextile, square, trine, and opposition — are not efficacious because of any light or physical ray the planets emit. They are efficacious because they are the same ratios that govern musical consonance, and the human soul, and perhaps the souls of animals too, possesses an innate geometrical faculty that is stirred when it encounters those ratios. The configuration is like a seal or a signature; the soul is like wax, or like a string tuned to a pitch that sounds when another string of the same pitch is struck. The planets themselves intend nothing by their positions and need not know what they are doing; the harmony lies in the configuration, not in any planetary mind. The soul, however, is not affected by the planets the way a body is affected by a push. It is moved by the ratio itself, which it recognizes as congruent with its own constitution. Hence the stars incline rather than compel: the soul can resist or fail to respond, and what happens depends on what the soul already is.

What I cannot work out is what the causal story is supposed to be. Is the configuration supposed to cause the soul's response, or merely occasion it? If the soul already has an innate faculty for geometry, why does it need the planets at all — why is the mere existence of the ratio not enough, and why could the soul not be stirred by any harmonic source? And if the response depends on the soul's own nature, in what sense is this an astrological influence rather than a fact about human psychology? Please help me understand the passage. I would like (1) the strongest reading on which the account is coherent, (2) the weakest link in that reading, (3) whether Kepler is committed to something like a pre-established harmony or to occasionalism, and (4) whether the account is falsifiable in the way his polemic against ordinary astrology seems to require. I would also like to know how the denial of planetary souls sits with the need for a soul on the receiving end: is the asymmetry principled, and does it do any work, or is it an ad hoc concession to piety? Please do not give me the standard summary that Kepler was a reluctant astrologer who kept a reformed astrology and discarded the rest. I want the mechanism, and I want to know where it breaks.
