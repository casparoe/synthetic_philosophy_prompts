My seminar keeps circling one line my professor loves: folk psychology is our phlogiston. We posited a hidden stuff to explain the visible stuff -- phlogiston for burning, caloric for heat, the ether for light -- and then the real science showed up and the hidden stuff turned out to be nothing at all. So beliefs and desires are headed the same way, and "belief" will be a word we use about ourselves the way we still say the sun comes up.

My friend thinks the analogy cuts the other way, and that my professor is using it to smuggle in the conclusion. Can you audit it? Give me the correspondence table first -- what's supposed to line up with what -- then the places the mapping actually breaks, and seperate the breaks that matter for the argument the analogy is doing from the ones that are just decoration. Two things I keep getting stuck on: the phlogiston people were actually pretty good at predicting things in their own domain, so is that even the right feature to compare to how well we predict each other with belief-talk? And the people doing the eliminating have to use beleifs-talk to make their case -- does that matter? Then the real question: once the disanalogies are on the table, does "belief is our phlogiston" still support throwing beliefs out entirely, or only something weaker like reducing them or replacing them? And if this analogy is carrying more than it can, what would a better one be?
