ok I want a short story that dramatizes the socialist calculation problem, but not the boring "planner is dumb" version. The thing I keep circling back to (I hang around LessWrong alot) is that the knowledge the planners need isn't lying around waiting to be gathered — what someone would give up for a good, what a machine is worth in its next-best use, that stuff only exists once there's exchange to pull it out.

Hook I want you to use: a small colony runs everything through one big central computer. It's genuinely brilliant, models every colonist down to their quirks. But when it has to place a strange machine that could serve three different uses, its model won't cough up a number. So it does the only thing that works: it runs an internal auction among simulated colonists. The "plan" turns out to be a market in a lab coat. I want the reader to feel the trap closing — the only way the machine can plan is to secretly stop planning.

Under 1200 words. Don't have a character explain the moral out loud, and no "and so Hayek was right" ending — end on whatever is actually unsettling about it.
