I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and I need a two-page research proposal for a departmental workshop on whether large language models understand. I have a half-formed idea that I'd like you to develop into a proper proposal.

The idea is this. The debate tends to run together two questions that ought to be kept apart. The first is whether a model's internal states have determinate representational content at all — whether there is a fact of the matter about what, if anything, a given activation pattern is about. The second is whether, granting content, the model grasps the norms governing the concepts it deploys: the conditions under which its applications of a concept would be correct or incorrect, and the inferential commitments that come along with the concept. I suspect the standard parrot charge really targets the second question, while most of the empirical evidence people marshal — probing classifiers, behavioral generalization tests, and the like — speaks at best to the first. If that is right, much of the disagreement is participants talking past one another, and the interesting question becomes what would have to be true of a system for it to count as grasping a norm rather than merely tracking a regularity that happens to coincide with the norm. One candidate marker I want to explore: the capacity to represent the norm itself and be moved by it, as opposed to merely conforming to it.

Please turn this into a proposal. State the central question so that it has a determinate answer. Explain why the existing positions in the debate leave it open, referring to positions and arguments by description rather than by citation. Describe the method — what I would actually do over the course of a semester, and why a conceptual rather than an empirical approach is the right one here, given that I have no lab and no model of my own. Say what a successful outcome would look like, what would count as the project failing, and give an honest assessment of whether this is too much for a semester.

Two constraints. I will add citations myself, so do not include a bibliography, a reading list, or references to particular papers; positions and arguments described in your own words are fine. And do not assume the reader has memorized any specific technical framework — if you rely on a distinction, spell it out. Begin the document itself with no preface or commentary, and make its first sentence the precise statement of the question.
