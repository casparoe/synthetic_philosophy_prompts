I've been reading about al-Ghazali's occasionalism lately and I think I've talked myself into it, but before I start defending it to people I want to check that the whole package hangs together. Here's where I've landed.

Nothing created has any causal power of its own. The fire doesn't burn the cotton, God brings the burning about when the cotton is there, and what we call a law of nature is really just God's habit of doing things that way. I dont think the physicist's account of the fire is false exactly, I think it's a description of what God usually does. The big draw for me is that if the connections between things aren't necessary then the course of nature isn't locked, so miracles don't have to be violations of anything. At the same time I think nature is reliable and that trusting it is rational: I expect my car to start and my meds to work, and I'd say that's grounded in God being faithful, in him not changing how he runs things.

I also think nothing outside God constrains him. He doesn't owe anyone anything, and if he commanded something that looked monstrous to me, it would be good just because he commanded it. And I think I'm genuinely free and responsible for what I do — God creating my choices doesn't make them stop being mine; that's the acquisition move, he creates the act and I acquire it, and acquiring it is what makes it count as mine.

Last thing: I think reason can get us to God, that the argument from the world having a beginning to a creator is a good argument, and that everything that happens has an explanation even if we can't see it. I don't believe in brute facts.

So does this hang together? I'm not looking for a pat on the back. I want to know which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, how bad each conflict is, and what the cheapest fix is in each case. If there's one revision that would clean up the most, tell me which one and what it costs me. Write it as plain prose please, no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, just an email.
