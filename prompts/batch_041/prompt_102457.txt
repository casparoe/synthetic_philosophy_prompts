I'm a PhD student in political philosophy and I've been stuck in the same argument with a friend in my cohort for months. She thinks guilt is strictly personal — you can only be guilty for what you yourself did — so "a nation is guilty for what it did generations ago" is either a category error or just a metaphor for something else (liability, forward-looking responsibility, membership duties). I think the nation is a persisting agent, still the same "we" and still living off what it took, so the guilt is real and can outlive everyone who was actually there. We keep talking past each other and I want to force us to produce the thing an adverserial collaboration would actually yield: a joint statement we could both put our names to.

Draft it for us. I want (1) the agreed claims — what guilt requires, what responsibility requires, where the line between them falls, what the Jaspers-style criminal/political/moral/metaphysical split does and doesn't settle, and any concrete cases we'd both agree are guilty or not guilty; (2) the live disagreements, each written in wording the side that holds it would accept; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one of us; and (4) for each crux, a case or argument we'd both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I especially want it pinned down whether the bearer is the state, the nation, or the citizenry, and whether persistence means institutional continuity, population continuity, or just continuing to hold the gains — my hunch is that a lot of the fight lives in there.

One request: no reading lists or citations, it should stand on its own. And if part of what we're doing turns out to be just disagreeing about words, say which part and why.
