I serve on the clinical coverage committee of an invented regional insurer, Meridian Health Assurance, and for our staff retreat I have been asked to draft a satirical edition of the determination bulletin we actually circulate after each review cycle. The target is the therapy/enhancement distinction as a payer's operational tool rather than as a philosopher's puzzle: I want a deadpan parody that is impeccably bureaucratic and internally consistent, applies the same baseline it has always applied (function restored to the normal level for the member's age and sex, no higher), and becomes absurd only because that baseline is. It should be funny to someone who has thought about the normal-function baseline, the moving frontier of medicalization, and why reconstructive surgery, growth hormone for idiopathic short stature, running blades for amputees, and stimulants for vague attentional complaints all land on different sides of the line for reasons that have nothing to do with the metaphysics, and it should still be legible to a colleague who has not. Please do not quote or cite anyone real, and do not name living scholars; invented committee members, case numbers, and a fictional appellant are all welcome, and the bulletin should adjudicate at least a couple of concrete requests — say, a member seeking coverage for a memory implant that would take him above his own youthful baseline, and another seeking coverage for a procedure she insists is reconstructive because the feature it corrects embarrasses her at work.

Keep the prose flat and institutional throughout, with no winking at the reader and no editorial asides; the comedy has to come from the content — the appeal to a normal level that no one can locate, the cost logic that quietly determines which side of the line a request falls on, the way each determination treats its own category as obvious. Since we want to be able to hand this out alongside the genuine bulletin, do not use any section headings or letterhead, and let the fifth determination be the one that quietly eats its own tail. Around 600 words in total.

Please give me the bulletin as exactly five numbered determinations, each a short paragraph of a few sentences, with no introduction, no preamble, and no closing note.
