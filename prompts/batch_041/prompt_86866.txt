For the philosophy of mind reading group I run, I'm making a handout and I want it to be a decision tree, not another list of isms. Start from the one thing everyone in the room already grants -- that we find consciousness puzzling and keep producing these reports about an explanatory gap -- and branch down to a named position. Every node has to be a question someone can answer without already knowing which label it leads to, so no "are you an illusionist?" nodes.

I want the tree built around a three-way distinction I keep harpping on that I think most publised taxonomies fudge: whether a view treats the puzzling reports as (a) data any theory has to predict, (b) an illusion to be explained away, or (c) partly constitutive of the phenomenon itself, so that accounting for the reports already is (part of) accounting for consciousness. And I want at least one node turned by a case of my own invention: the "silent gapper" -- a creature with every functional mark of finding its own experience mysterious (self-directed attention, surprise, the works) but no language and no reports -- since that ought to split views that are secretly about reports from views that are really about seemings.

Leaves I need to land on: illusionism; plain deflationism (no gap to explain in the first place); the line that the meta-problem is only ever as hard as the hard problem; a higher-order / self-representational story; and the mysterian "we just can't do this" option. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really separate, and mark any answer-combinations nobody occupies, saying for each whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Give it back as JSON with these fields: nodes (array of {id, question, branches:[{answer, next}]}, where next is either a node id or a leaf id), leaves (array of {id, name, awkward_commitment}, where awkward_commitment is one sentence on the most embarrassing thing the view has to own up to), unoccupied (array of {combination, verdict, why}), and inseparable (array of {leaf_a, leaf_b, why}). Aim for maybe a dozen nodes, and keep the node questions in plain language a first-year could answer.
