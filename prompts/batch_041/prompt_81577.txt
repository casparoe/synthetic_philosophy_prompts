I'm in a reading group thats been working through decision theory paradoxes, and our last session is on the Pasadena game. I want to close with a poem, and I want the form to do some of the philosophical work. Heres the setup: a fair coin gets flipped until it comes up heads. If the first head is on flip n, the payoff is (-1)^n times 2^n divided by n. So you lose $2 on the first flip, win $2 on the second, lose $8/3 on the third, win $4 on the fourth, and so on forever. The expected value is the alternating harmonic series. If you sum it in the natural order it converges to negative ln 2, but because it only converges conditionally you can rearrange the terms to make it sum to any real number you want. So the games expected value isnt well-defined in the way standard decision theory needs it to be. I want a poem that captures that -- maybe a poem whose lines can be reordered to give two different overall readings, one that feels like a win and one that feels like a loss, using the exact same words. Or a poem with a refrain that flips sign each time it returns. The form itself should be conditionally convergent. 14-20 lines, no fixed rhyme scheme needed, but the structure should track the rearrangement idea. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the paradox will nod, and it should work as a poem even for someone who doesnt. Dont just name-drop the game -- embody the problem. And please dont resolve it into a tidy moral; the whole point is that the sum depends on the order and theres no canonical order.
