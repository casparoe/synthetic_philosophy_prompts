I've been reporting on the religion-and-morality beat for a few years now, and I'm struck by how completly stalled the argument is. The religious-prosociality people point to charity, volunteering, and self-reports; the secular side points to in-group bias, out-group hostility, and the high-trust Nordic societies; and both sides seem to have their favorite studies and neither moves. It's not that anyone disputes the other side's data exactly -- it's more that they keep talking past each other. I want you to diagnose the impasse rather than adjudicate it. Is this a substantive empirical disagreement (about causation, selection effects, or what the measures actually track), a verbal one about what 'more moral' means, two different questions under one heading (individual-level religiosity vs. society-level secularity and trust), or a clash of bedrock intuitions about whether naturalness or tradition has value? My working theory -- treat it as a hypothesis, not a conclusion -- is that it's mostly the second and third: the two sides are using 'moral' in different ways, and they're also answering different questions about different units of analysis, so the empirical literature can't settle it because the dependent variable keeps shifting. I want you to test that against what each side actually says.

To make this concrete, take 'more moral' to have at least three possible readings: (a) more prosocial behavior in the aggregate, (b) more conformity to a shared moral code, and (c) more willingness to extend moral concern beyond one's own group. Tell me which reading each side is implicitly using, where the readings conflict, and whether any of them is the 'real' one or whether that's just a further verbal dispute. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a new measure, a distinction, a different question, a causal design, or an argument that no progress should be expected. Keep it under 700 words, lead with your bottom-line diagnosis in two or three sentences, and don't give me a reading list or citations; I want the structure of the disagreement, not a survey.
