I am a secondary-school student on my school's debate team, and the motion at our regional tournament is that our country should reintroduce compulsory military service. I would rather write my position paper from a settled view than assemble one while writing, so I am setting out below what I actually believe and asking you to audit the package for internal consistency. Please do not argue for or against the motion, and do not tell me what is currently fashionable; I want to know where the joints are.

I hold the following. (1) The state may not compel a person to risk their life, or to kill, without that person's consent, and I regard this as a genuine constraint on policy rather than a consideration that may be set aside whenever the stakes are high. (2) Even so, people who receive the protection a state provides owe something in return, and the state may enforce that debt, so a general obligation to serve is not automatically unjust. (3) A force drawn from the general population is preferable to a professional one, because it is less likely to be used in wars of choice and because it keeps the armed forces tied to the society they defend. (4) Effectiveness matters: a defence that cannot make invasion costly is a gesture, and I suspect that modern deterrence depends more on expensive equipment and long-trained specialists than on large numbers of short-service conscripts. (5) The model I admire is the small-state one — general service, a militia structure, and a foreign policy of staying out of military alliances — and I would like my country to look more like that. (6) Conscientious objection must be honoured, and those who refuse should be able to perform alternative civilian service. (7) As a matter of design, I would have the obligation fall by lottery on the eligible cohort rather than on everyone, and I am drawn to a proposal a classmate made, that a person who is drawn should be permitted to pay a volunteer to take their place.

Please tell me which of these items genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to; rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad; for each, say what would have to give and what that concession would cost me elsewhere in the package; and finish by naming the single revision that would clean up the most. I would much rather have three or four well-argued findings than an exhaustive inventory in which every pair is declared inconsistent.
