I have talked myself into a strong form of Skolemite relativism about cardinality. The thought is this: because the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem supplies any consistent first-order theory with an infinite model with a countable model, a countable model of set theory can satisfy "there exist uncountable sets" without error, since "uncountable" is interpreted through the model's own relation of bijection, and no external standard can show the model to be simply wrong. I want to go further and hold that this is not a paradox or a defect but the correct picture of set-theoretic discourse: the question whether the real continuum is uncountable, absolutely and not merely relative to some model, has no determinate answer.

Tell me what would count against this. I am especially interested in the following, and in anything of the same kind that I have not thought of:

- the second-order escape and what it actually costs — whether insisting on a standard or full semantics for the set-theoretic axioms buys absoluteness only by helping itself to a notion of consequence that is itself model-relative or unexplained, and whether that escape is even available to someone who regards first-order logic as the logic;
- whether the relativity, once granted, spreads further than its friends usually admit — to finiteness, to well-foundedness, to the notions of a well-formed formula, a proof, or a model — and whether that spread is a reductio or simply the honest consequence;
- whether the position is self-undermining, given that the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem, and the very notion of a model, are themselves set-theoretic;
- how the position differs from the set-theoretic multiverse view, and whether the difference is more than verbal — what Skolemite relativism forbids or predicts that the multiverse view does not;
- and whether the position has been insulated from defeat by qualification, for instance by retreating to "no absolute first-order-expressible cardinality" or to a deflationary reading of "absolute," and if so whether that insulation is a legitimate refinement or a way of risking nothing.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which of them the position's own defenders would concede rather than merely what critics allege, and tell me whether it can be stated at all without covertly invoking an absolute notion of cardinality or of a model. That last point would move me most, so I would rather be told that the demonstration cannot be given than be given a reassuring one.
