I'm in a phil of action reading group that got badly derailed — we were supposed to be doing intention and intention-formation, but someone brought in the behavioral-econ literature on present bias and now we meet Thursdays and argue about whether weakness of will is even a thing. So I tried to build an argument stitching the two together, and it bothers me because it proves way too much. Background so you don't need the literature: hyperbolic discounting is the idea that people discount future rewards at a rate that's steeper for near-term than far-term payoffs, so as a payoff gets closer your ranking can flip — at 3pm you genuinely prefer not to have a second glass of wine at 9pm, and at 9pm you genuinely prefer to have it. Some people ('sophisticated' discounters) see the flip coming and try to precommit, e.g. by not buying the wine; others ('naive' ones) don't. A friend coined a distinction I've gotten attached to and want to keep: 'thin akrasia' for a gap between judgment and action inside a single moment, and 'thick akrasia' for the cross-temporal kind where your later self reverses your earlier self's verdict. OK, here's the argument. (1) Akrasia is acting against your better judgment about what to do here and now. (2) Your better judgment at a moment is the ranking of options you'd endorse from that moment, given full information and reflection. (3) At 9pm the agent's ranking puts another glass above stopping; at 3pm it put stopping above another glass. (4) Within any single moment the ranking is complete and consistent — the discounting only produces reversals across moments, never inside one. (5) So at the moment of action the agent always does what her better judgment, so defined, tells her to do. (6) So nobody ever acts against her better judgment. (7) So akrasia never happens. (8) But akrasia obviously happens — procrastination, addiction, my entire relationship with my phone. So one of (1)–(5) has to go.

Tell me which step to give up and what it costs. I'd like to keep both the behavioral-econ story (hyperbolic discounting is a real pattern and explains something) and the thought that akrasia is a real phenomenon and not just a fancy name for changing your mind, so my hunch is the damage is in the philosophical premises — but I genuinely can't tell where. Is the thing actually valid and I just have to pick a premise to abandon, or does something slide somewhere (a redefinition, a scope shift, a time-indexing trick, an equivocation between occurrent and standing judgment)? And if the fix is that my friend's thin/thick distinction is doing the real work, fine — but then say whether 'thin akrasia' survives step (4) or turns out to be empty, with everything real being thick. Name the step, tell me why it looked innocent, and price the alternatives I'm choosing between. No citations needed, I'll chase those down myself.
