I am a doctoral student in philosophy preparing a six-month research proposal for a small grant on decision theory and existential risk, and I would like help turning a half-formed idea into a rigorous prospectus. The idea concerns acausal threats and surrogate goals. In the acausal setting, an agent A may try to deter another agent B from some action by threatening that, if B defects, A will adopt or pursue a surrogate goal—a goal that is not A's terminal goal and that would be costly to B. The usual rationale is that this makes the threat credible: A can precommit by changing its own preferences, so B cannot dismiss the threat as a bluff. My suspicion is that the device is less stable than it looks. If A genuinely adopts the surrogate goal, then A's behavior may be distorted even in worlds where B cooperates, and A may lose the ability to pursue its original ends; if A adopts it only conditionally, then the condition must be legible to B, which raises the question of how B can distinguish a conditional commitment from a mere announcement. I want to know whether there is a coherent version of the surrogate-goal strategy, and what assumptions about agency, logical omniscience, and payoff structure it requires.

Please write a proposal of about 1500 to 2000 words. It should state the central question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer—something like: under what conditions, if any, does a surrogate goal constitute a credible acausal threat rather than a costly self-manipulation? It should explain why the existing answers leave the question open, without a literature review by citation; you can refer to positions and arguments, and I will add citations myself, so do not include a bibliography or invent titles, dates, or authors. It should set out an approach that makes progress rather than restating the paradox. I would like the method to include at least three tasks: first, a taxonomy that distinguishes (i) unconditional surrogate goals, (ii) conditional adoption triggered by B's observed or inferred policy, and (iii) merely announced surrogate goals that A does not actually adopt but that B may believe; second, a formal model, perhaps a two-agent decision problem with acausal correlation, in which each variant is represented and the credibility conditions are derived; third, an analysis of failure modes, including a commitment regress, surrogate goal laundering (where A's surrogate goal is really just a disguised ordinary preference), and cases where the strategy collapses into standard deterrence or into a self-fulfilling threat. The proposal should say what a positive result would look like, what would count as failure, and how the project could be completed in six months at part-time intensity.

I am also interested in the ethical and strategic upshot, but I do not want the proposal to become a treatise on AI safety. A useful deliverable would be a clear verdict on whether surrogate goals add anything to acausal bargaining that ordinary precommitment and reputation cannot, plus a short list of open problems that a larger project could pursue. If the topic turns out to be too large, say so honestly and propose a narrower version, for example focusing only on the conditional-adoption variant. The proposal should be written for a funding panel of philosophers and decision theorists, not for a specialist in acausal trade, so define terms as you go.
