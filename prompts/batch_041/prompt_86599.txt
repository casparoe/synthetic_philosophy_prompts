Ok so I need a joke for the end of our reading group session on self-deception — the whole 'is it even possible' mess. I'd like you to take this on as a professional philosopher who also happens to be funny, so think carefully, start by brainstorming a handful of angles, and only then write the thing.

The bit I want the puchline to turn on is the structure of the problem, not a generic 'oh, those philosophers' gag. Roughly: to deceive someone you need to know the truth and they mustn't, and when the two parties are the same person you've got somebody who both knows and doesn't know. It gets worse — the deceiver also has to keep the deception hidden from the deceived, so the intention to deceive has to be hidden as well. The deception has to cover it's own tracks, and the person laying the tracks is also the person who mustn't find them.

Use whatever scaffolding helps. There's the picture where the self splits into deceiver and deceived (which means they could, in principle, meet — and that would be awkward), or the deflationary picture where there's no paradox at all, just belief formation skewed by what you'd like to be true, or the idea that the self-deceiver never quite believes the flattering thing but carries on as if they do. Sartre's bad faith is fair game if it earns its place. The setting is up to you — a hotline, a support group, a performance review, two flatmates, whatever you can make work.

It should be funny to people who actually know this literature and can survive hearing it read out at the end of a seminar. A few lines is plenty. British English please. And tell me at the end which account of self-deception the joke quietly assumes, so I know who in the room is going to kick off.
