my dad had a cardiac arrest a few weeks ago and spent nine days in the ICU before we withdrew care, and ever since I've been trying to write down what I actually think about death, because I noticed I was telling different people different things. here's where I've landed — I'd like to know if it hangs together:

1. death is when the body stops working as an integrated whole — the brain-death idea, no brainstem, nothing coordinating the rest of it.
2. but when I think about what I actually lost, it's the mind — the memories, the personality, the lights being on. a body breathing on a vent with nobody home isn't him.
3. I'm a registered organ donor and I think donation is close to an unambiguous good, and that we never take vital organs from someone who isn't already dead. that rule is the whole game; without it you've just got doctors killing people for parts.
4. when I say death is irreversible I mean it literally — if the function could in principle come back, they aren't dead yet, no matter what anyone has decided.
5. I dont think the lights just go out. not religious exactly, but I don't think this is all there is.
6. whether someone is dead should be a medical fact, not something a family or a legislature gets to decide.

(I also gather that a lot of organ donation nowadays happens after the heart stops rather than after brain death — they wait a few minutes with no pulse and then that's the declaration.)

so: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and if something has to move, what's the cheapest thing to give up? rank the tensions by how bad they are rather than telling me they're all somehow in tension. and no reading list — I want to know which of my six has to give.
