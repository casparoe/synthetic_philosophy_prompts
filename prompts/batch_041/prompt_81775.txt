I chair the commemoration advisory committee for a mid-sized city, and we owe the council a written recommendation before its next meeting. In 1954 the city named a street after a locally prominent attorney who had led the legal campaign to keep a section of the city racially restricted. The street runs through what was then the excluded neighborhood and is now home to many of the descendants of the families kept out. A coalition of those residents has formally asked us to keep the name, arguing that it no longer honors the man but marks their own history of living under, and outlasting, the restriction — and that renaming the street would let the university and the newcomers at its edge edit their neighborhood's past. The honoree's surviving family has asked for the opposite, on the ground that any continued official use of the name is a continuing insult. Our commemoration policy permits removal only where continued use would amount to the city's own endorsement of the conduct commemorated, and it says nothing about who counts as the relevant community. Please draft the recommendation I will circulate: a concrete decision (retain, remove, or retain with a commissioned counter-marker), the date by which the council should act, and the reasoning, including a fair statement of the strongest argument for the outcome you reject. State how confident you are in the recommendation and identify the single point you are least sure of.
