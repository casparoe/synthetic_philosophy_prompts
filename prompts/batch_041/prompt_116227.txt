I work in applied mechanism design and behavioural economics; my working vocabulary is feasible sets, objective functions, incentive compatibility, commitment devices, self-signaling, and preference endogeneity, and I know Sartre only well enough to be dangerous — one read of Being and Nothingness and the popular lecture. Restate his account of radical freedom and bad faith in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, keeping four claims distinct and treating them separately: that freedom is not a capacity one has more or less of but the structure of consciousness itself, so that no option set is ever simply given to an agent; that facticity and transcendence are not two components of a person but two aspects of a single project, so that the constraints one faces are constraints only in the light of what one has chosen; that the resistance of the world, what Sartre calls the coefficient of adversity of things, is not a project-independent property of the world; and that bad faith is not a false belief but a self-undermining relation to one's own agency, which is why the demand for sincerity is itself suspect. For each, say what carries over without loss, what carries over only with distortion, and what has no counterpart at all — in particular, whether bad faith can be handled as self-signaling or distorted updating or whether its self-undermining structure is something those models cannot state, and whether the coefficient-of-adversity claim is one decision theory must reject as a category error or can absorb by making the feasible set project-relative. Close with the residue report and a verdict: is what fails to carry over a defect of my vocabulary or evidence that the Sartrean claims are less determinate than they look? Keep the translation honest — do not read feasibility constraints and expected utility back into him and call that translation.
