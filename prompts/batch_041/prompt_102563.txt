Write a sestina (six stanzas of six lines and a three-line envoi) about incommensurability between scientific paradigms, in American English. The six end-words must be drawn from a real episode in the history of science and must be words that appear on both sides of the rupture while changing sense; the strict repetition of the same six words, with no stanza contradicting any other, should itself make the case that the signifiers hold still while the meanings drift. Take Kuhn's later position that incommensurability is partial untranslatability rather than total incomparability: give the poem one moment in which a speaker who has learned both vocabularies manages to carry a claim across the gap, and one moment in which the same effort fails and only pointing is left. Voice: a historian of science for whom the older vocabulary has become a second language, addressing a practitioner of the newer one. Do not let the poem state its thesis, and do not use the words "paradigm," "incommensurable," "gestalt," or "translation" before the envoi. End the envoi with all six end-words in a final line that reads like the fragment of a glossary no one could have finished.
