i'm putting together a case study for a grad seminar on the politics of history education and i want to do the 1619 Project vs the 1776 Commission fight, but not as yet another "who's right about the founders" debate. what i actually want is to run the whole thing through a distinction i keep circling back to: school history as civil religion (the story a polity tells itself to authorize its own order -- sacred founding, chosen people, sins and redemptions) versus school history as an apprenticeship in a truth-seeking discipline (evidence, revision, the standing obligation to be publicly wrong). so map the episode onto those two functions: who is actually doing which, what each function explains about why the two camps talked past each other so completely, and what each one can't explain about the fight. then the part i really care about -- does the episode show that the usual liberal fix ("just teach the controversy, present both narratives, let students judge") is incoherent, or does it just show that one side was confused about which game it was playing? run it in both directions, use the theory to read the episode and then say what the episode does to the theory, and be honest about where neither function fits instead of forcing it.
