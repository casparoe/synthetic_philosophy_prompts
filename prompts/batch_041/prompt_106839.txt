Here's a pile of my gut verdicts about what counts as a disease, and I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to. Male pattern baldness: not a disease, even though it ruins some peoples lives and is a clear departure from how hair is supposed to work. Adult lactose intolerance: not a disease, even though the enzyme is literally not doing its job -- most of the world's adults are like this. Sickle-cell trait: not a disease, though sickle-cell anemia is. Nearsightedness: a disease, even though you could tell me it's just eyes doing what eyes do when a kid spends his childhood indoors reading. ADHD in a kid who's flunking out and miserable: a disorder, even if the same wiring was great for hunting. Grief that lasts a year after a spouse dies: not a disorder, no matter how much it hurts or how many DSM-style boxes it ticks. A harmless septate uterus found on a scan, no symptoms ever: not a disease. I keep wanting to say disease = biological dysfunction plus real harm, but baldness has the harm, grief has the harm, and neither is a disease, while myopia is one and maybe isn't even a dysfunction. So is there one account that gets all of these, or which verdict do I have to give up? And on whatever principle you land on, what does it say about aging -- disease or not?
