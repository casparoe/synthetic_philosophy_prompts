I'm a second-year undergraduate in a small seminar on formal systems and computability — eighteen of us, one ninety-minute meeting a week — and I've been asked to run next week's session as a hands-on activity rather than a discussion. The topic I've drawn is the MIU system our instructor used to open the unit on unprovability. You begin with the string MI, and at each step you may do exactly one of four things: add a U to the end of any string that ends in I; double everything following the initial M; replace any run of three I's with a single U; or delete any adjacent pair of U's. The puzzle is to get from MI to MU, and the reason our instructor dwells on it is that no cleverness exercised inside the system will ever produce MU — the only way to see that is to jump out of the system altogether and notice something the rules can never disturb, namely that the number of I's in a string is never a multiple of three, whereas MU has none. I already tried the lazy version, writing the four rules on the board and turning the room loose, and the result was exactly what you'd expect: within ten minutes half the group was certain they were one move from MU, and nobody paused to ask whether the target was reachable at all. So what I want is an activity that makes the group hit that wall themselves and then makes the escape to the meta-level feel like their own discovery.

What I'm after is a runnable plan: roles or teams, a scoring scheme that doesn't require real money, a minute-by-minute timeline for the ninety minutes, and a debrief that ties specific things that actually happened in the room back to the distinction between reasoning within a system and reasoning about it. I'd like the structure to embody the two levels rather than merely describe them — so that some participants are visibly stuck doing the work of the system while others are doing something that only makes sense from above it — and I'd like the payoffs arranged so that the move outside the system looks like a waste of time at first and then turns out to be the only thing that pays. Please don't let me announce the invariant, or even the name for what they're doing (jumping out of the system, which the course abbreviates JOOTSing), until after they've felt the difference; the failure has to come before the label. If it helps, you're welcome to fold in a second, slightly altered rule set under which the analogous target is actually reachable, so that the group can see the meta-level verdict is a fact about the particular system and not a trick they can always pull.

Finally, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it, and be candid about which part of the idea the activity can't reproduce — my worry is that any game I stage will make stepping outside look like a tidy manoeuvre that is always available, when the real lesson may be that it isn't, and that finding the thing the rules leave untouched is itself a creative act rather than a guaranteed next step.
