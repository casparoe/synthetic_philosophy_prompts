Ok, so this is the kind of thing that gets stuck in my teeth. I was in a thread about hedonism (the SSC subreddit, where else) and this one guy kept ending every exchange with the same move — essentially that pushpin is as good as poetry, prejudice aside, quantity of pleasure being equal and all that. He was clearly deploying it as a reductio: look how absurd the view gets when you actually take it seriously. But two other people read the same sentence as a plain statement of hedonism, and one of them said yeah, obviously, thats what the view says, whats the problem. And I realized I can't actually tell what the sentence is claiming, which means I can't tell who's right.

So: can you lay out the distinct precise theses that one line could be expressing, as a menu? I want the whole spread, from the trivially-true readings up to the radical ones. Is it a claim about how pleasures feel — that there's a single common felt quality and the apparent differences are only in the causes? A claim about commensurability — that any two pleasures sit on one scale and can be traded off against each other? A claim about what we ought to do — that when two options give equal pleasure, nothing else can break the tie? A claim about what pleasure is — that pleasure doesn't name a natural kind at all, just a grab-bag of unrelated states we happen to lump together? For each reading I want to know whether it's a platitude, a real substantive claim, or just false, and — this is the part I actually care about — which reading the anti-hedonist needs for his reductio to land, and which reading the hedonist has to accept if he wants to keep calling himself one.

And underneath all of it there's the thing people call the heterogeneity problem, which I think is the real question: what would it even be for two pleasures to have something in common? Same felt quality? Same functional role — the same thing they get you to do? Same bit of brain? Same disposition to seek them? Because my suspicion is the answer differs per reading, and that "nothing" only kills some flavors of hedonism — the ones that need one commensurable currency — and not others, like the ones where a pleasure is just whatever you happen to be into. Is that right, or is there a version that survives all of it and I'm missing it?

Last thing, because I keep running into it: "it's all just dopamine anyway" sounds like the same move wearing a lab coat. Is that the same thesis or a different one, and does it actually help the hedonist or the anti-hedonist? My gut says it's a descriptive claim being smuggled in to do normative work, but I can't articulate why thats fishy.
