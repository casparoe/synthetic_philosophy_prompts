Ok so I'm a software engineer, never studied philosophy formally, and I've been thinking about this a lot lately because I've been logging my own procrastination in a spreadsheet for like eight months now, which is maybe a weird hobby but whatever. The view I've landed on, and I'm honestly not sure if it's a real position or just something I made up, is that weakness of will / akrasia isn't a separate thing at all — it's just ordinary hyperbolic discounting wearing a costume. On this picture there's no special faculty of 'will' that can fail. There's one ranking of outcomes, and the value you assign to a future outcome decays steeply and non-exponentially the further away it is, so the same option gets ranked high when it's close and low when it's far. What we call acting against our better judgment is just what it looks like from the inside when the curve swings: the agent isn't contradicting themselves, they're a person with time-inconsistent preferences, and the guilt story is something they tell afterwards to make the sequence look like a single self who failed. I like this because it's parsimonious, it explains why the failure always seems to happen at the moment of choice rather than at the moment of judging, and it explains why the fix (when there is one) comes from the environment — deleting apps, timers, making the bad option physically annoying — rather than from insight.

What I want from you is the other side: what should count against this? And I mean real defeaters, not vibes. I'm especially interested in the ones that come from the position's own commitments rather than from critics. For instance, does the account even describe akrasia, or does it dissolve it — if a sophisticated discounter knowingly prefers the nearer thing and structures their life around that, is that weakness of will or just a person with unusual preferences, and if it's the latter, has my view quietly stopped being about akrasia at all? I'd also like to know what it predicts that a rival wouldn't. I'd expect it to predict that akratics should be eager to use commitment devices and happy to have someone else bind them, since that's the obvious fix for a time-inconsistent preference, but my impression is that alot of real akratics refuse exactly that kind of help, resent it, and would rather fail on their own terms — is that a genuine problem or can it be absorbed? And is there a class of cases with no temporal structure at all, where someone judges one thing right, does another, and nothing about distance or delay has changed? Because if those exist and aren't rare, I think I'm in trouble in a way that no amount of fiddling with the discount curve fixes.

Please be honest about the last part too. I've noticed that every time I get a counterexample I add another clause (the discount rate isn't stable, it depends on how much you identify with your future self, it depends on mood, on blood sugar, whatever) and I have a feeling I could keep doing that forever. If the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, say so, and then tell me whether that's actually bad for a view of this kind or just the normal price of doing business. Rank the defeaters for me, fatal down to merely awkward, and tell me which ones a committed defender of the discounting picture would themselves admit are hits.
