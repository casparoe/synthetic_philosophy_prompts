I am working through the allegory of the cave in Book VII of the Republic and have been stopped by a stretch that is usually passed over in silence — the exchange about naming and the echo. The translation I am reading renders it as follows:

"And if they were able to converse with one another, would they not suppose that they were naming what was actually before them? ... And suppose further that the prison had an echo which came from the other side, would they not be sure to fancy when one of the passers-by spoke that the voice which they heard came from the passing shadow? ... To them, I said, the truth would be literally nothing but the shadows of the images."

I would like help understanding what work this exchange is doing. My suspicion is that it is not merely scene-painting: that Plato needs it in order to tie the content of the prisoners' beliefs to the causal history of what has acted on them, and that the echo detail in particular is there to foreclose the thought that they could be thinking about the passers-by at all. If that reading is defensible, I want to know what it commits Plato to — in particular, whether the prisoners' words refer to the shadows, refer to the artifacts but under false beliefs about them, or fail to refer at all, and which of these the passage actually supports. And I want to know whether the later scene, in which the freed man is made to name the objects as they pass and the ascent is described as a re-education of sight, presupposes that commitment or sits loose of it. If my reading is an over-reading, tell me what the exchange is doing instead and which feature of the text tells against me.

Please separate what is settled about this passage from what is genuinely contested among people who know the text, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. I am not asking for a survey of who has said what; I want to know which claims about the passage are safe to build on and which are up for grabs, and how confident I should be in each.
