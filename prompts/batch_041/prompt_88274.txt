A friend and I argued for most of an evening about safe Pareto improvements, and we broke off without resolution, so I would like an adjudication that treats both of our positions as live options. His central claim was that a safe Pareto improvement is simply a Pareto improvement that one party can secure without the other's cooperation: because the move is unilateral, there is no acceptance problem, and that is the whole of the safety. He then added that the idea is really the veil of ignorance applied to bargaining, since choosing the arrangement that maximizes expected utility over the possible parties one might face is what makes the choice safe. I have largely come around to his first point, and my question is about what follows from it. If a safe Pareto improvement just is a unilateral Pareto improvement, what work is the veil-of-ignorance element doing, and does it ever change which moves qualify as safe? If it does not, is the term simply redundant with ordinary Pareto improvement, with safety amounting to nothing more than the absence of a veto? I would also like to know whether my concession is itself the mistake, and if so, what the safety condition is actually tracking, and why a change that is an improvement for every possible bargaining partner's preferences would be a different and stronger requirement than the one my friend described.
