My brother and I are fighting over my mum's house and I can't stop thinking about it, and since I'm doing a philosophy course with my first essay due in a few weeks I figured I'd write about it and kill two birds.

The setup: growing up he got everything — our parents paid his tuition, bailed out his business twice, took him back in after the divorce. I got a bus fare and a "good luck". Now we're both in our 40s, he's got a bad back, no savings and a decade of things going wrong, and he says he should get 60% of the house because he needs it more. Part of me agrees with him. But luck egalitarianism, which is what I've been reading, says an inequality is only ok if it traces back to choices rather than luck — so he should only have to eat the part of the mess he actually chose, and honestly alot of it he didn't choose.

The bit I can't get past is that the inheritance is itself a windfall, and the bad luck he wants compensated was partly caused by our parents favouring him in the first place. So if we weight the second windfall by how the first round of family luck went, are we correcting the unfairness or just running the same unfair lottery a second time? I want to know wether the luck egalitarian story can even answer that.

Write the essay: strongest version of both sides, then actually come down on one — should he get more, and can I get there from luck egalitarianism or do I have to abandon it? Don't end on "it depends".
