Hi, I'm taking a decision theory seminar and we've spent the last three weeks on anthropic reasoning, and I want to get my own view straight before the essay's due. Here's where I've landed, and I'm slightly worried it doesn't hang together:

(1) Sleeping Beauty: I'm a thirder — when she wakes up and doesn't know what day it is, heads should be 1/3, not 1/2.

(2) The doomsday argument basically works. The fact that I'm alive now, rather than among the vastly larger number of people who'll live later, is real evidence that the human story is on the short side.

(3) The 'presumptuous philosopher' case — the one where the self-indication assumption tells you to jump at a minuscule chance of an astronomically huge population over a guaranteed ordinary one — is absurd, and I take it as a refutation of the self-indication assumption (the idea that a world is more likely to be yours the more observers it contains).

(4) I think we're probably in a simulation. The counting argument there — if simulated minds vastly outnumber real ones, a random observer is probably simulated — seems right to me.

(5) I also think our universe looking fine-tuned is evidence for a multiverse, and I don't think that needs any fancy anthropic principle, just the obvious point that we couldn't have found ourselves in a dead universe.

(6) I think the universe is probably spatially infinite, with infinitely many people in it.

So — does this hang togther? I know self-sampling (SSA: you're a random observer among the observers in your world) and self-indication (SIA: worlds with more observers are more likely to be the one you're in) are supposed to be the two options, and I've got a feeling I'm quietly helping myself to one of them in some of these and the other in the rest. Which of the six are actually in tension, which pairs only look like they clash, and can you rank the real conflicts by how bad they are? If something has to give, what's the cheapest single revision that cleans up the most, and what does it cost me in the others?
