So I've been chewing on this for a while and I want you to help me build a made-up case about it, not just talk about it in the abstract.

The thing is what I've been calling an acausal threat. Rough version: you threaten someone with something bad, but you can't actually touch them. Nothing you do reaches them and nothing they do reaches you. The threat is supposed to work anyway, because the other person works out what you'd do by asking themselves what someone like them, in that spot, would do, and assuming you'd land on the same answer. And the twist that makes it bite is a surrogate goal: instead of just saying you'll punish them, you actually go and change yourself so that punishing them is something you genuinely want, so it isn't a bluff. I think I've got that right but I'm honestly not sure, and I'm not sure the whole thing isn't a trick of reasoning that falls apart when you look at it closely.

So: build me a case.

Requirements, and I care about all of them:

- Two ordinary adults. No AI, no time travel, no simulation stuff, no mind reading, no perfect predictor. If they can guess each other's moves it should be the way a close friend or a long-time rival guesses, right a lot of the time and wrong sometimes.
- They really are cut off. Nothing one of them does can physically reach the other, not through a third party either, no shared bank account, no mutual friend who could carry out the threat for you. That would be cheating and it's the exact thing I'm trying to avoid.
- The surrogate goal has to be real and has to cost the person something. Not a bluff, not a performance. Once they've done it they'd actually follow through.
- The stakes are normal. A few months pay, a job, custody of a dog, something like that. Nothing infinite, nothing astronomically huge.
- The person making the threat should come out ahead if the other side gives in, compared to a version of the story where nobody threatens anybody. And clearly worse off if they change themselves and the other side doesn't give in. I want it to be a real gamble, not a free win.
- One paragraph for the setup. Then the work.

After the story:

1. Say what plain causal reasoning says about both sides of it. Does the threat get believed, should the target give in, should the threatener bother changing themselves in the first place.
2. Say what you get if you treat the other person's decision as evidence about your own, or if you think of the two of them as running the same decision rule. I think that's the view where the threat starts to work, but tell me whether that's actually the same as evidential decision theory or a seperate thing, and be honest if it doesn't come apart from plain causal reasoning in the case you built.
3. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, then remove it and show me the case collapsing back into an ordinary empty threat.
4. Tell me how someone who thinks all acausal threats are hot air would go after your case, in the strongest version of that attack.
5. And if the requirements can't all be met at once, say so, tell me which ones are fighting each other, and tell me which one I should give up.

I'd rather have a case where the threat fails and I understand why than a case where you've quietly built in that the other person gives in. The whole point is whether the giving-in falls out of the setup or whether you had to smuggle it in.
