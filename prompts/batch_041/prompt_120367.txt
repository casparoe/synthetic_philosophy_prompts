I'm supposed to hand in a short position statement for my metaphysics seminar and then defend it in class, so I'd like a dry run first. Here's what I've got:

1. When a thing exists at a time, all of it is there. No temporal parts, no slices — the poker on Monday and the poker on Tuesday are the same poker, wholly present both times.
2. Time is another dimension like space. Past and future are as real as the present, and I don't think theres anything special about now.
3. The poker is straight, then it's bent, and that's a change in the poker, full stop. I don't want to say being-bent is really a relation between the poker and Tuesday — being bent isn't about Tuesday.
4. I also think a thing can go out of existence for a while and come back, and it's the same thing, not a copy. Not rebuilt out of the same parts — I mean nothing at all in between, and then it's back.
5. No junk objects. The poker and the moon don't add up to an object, however you bracket them, and neither do my left shoe and the Eiffel Tower.

Where does this creak? Rank the tensions from worst to mildest, tell me which ones are only apparent, and for each real one say what the cheapest thing to drop would be and what dropping it costs me.
