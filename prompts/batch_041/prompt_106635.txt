A friend of mine says the multiverse earns its scientific keep like any theory does: it retrodicted a small nonzero cosmological constant that nobody was expecting, and that counts as a confirmed prediction — and since string theory is supposed to give you the landscape, the same trick makes it testable. I think that's rigged, since a theory that says "every vacuum exists somewhere" gives probability 1 to anythin we could observe, so nothing can confirm it. Give me your credence that he's right that the observed value of Λ is genuine Bayesian evidence for a multiverse, and decompose it: prior on a multiverse, likelihood of a small Λ given the multiverse, likelihood given a single universe with Λ as a free fundamental parameter, the measure problem, and the observer-selection correction. Number each term, combine them, and say which one the final answer is most hostage to and what would move it. One more thing — if his logic is good, isn't he stuck saying that a rival theory, "there's exactly one universe and its Λ just happens to be tiny," is equally confirmed? Under 250 words.
