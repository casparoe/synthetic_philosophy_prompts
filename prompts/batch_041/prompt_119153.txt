I am writing a novel whose central character is a game theorist — a woman in her sixties who has spent her career on the iterated elimination of dominated strategies and who has lately taken to applying the method, half in earnest, to her own life. In one scene, at a dinner table, she is asked to tell a joke. I want the joke to be one that only someone who really knows the subject could tell: funny to a table of game theorists, puzzling but not nonsensical to everyone else, with a punchline that turns on a genuine feature of the theory rather than on the bare fact that the theory exists. I would like it to turn on one of the more technical features — the contrast between eliminating strictly dominated strategies and eliminating merely weakly dominated ones, for instance, or the fact that a strategy can be beaten by a mixture of the other options even when no single one of them beats it — rather than on anything as well-worn as the prisoner's dilemma or the tragedy of the commons. Please give me the joke as a short stretch of dialogue she could deliver at the table, and then, in a sentence or two, name the piece of theory the punchline rests on, so that I can be confident I have not misrepresented her field.
