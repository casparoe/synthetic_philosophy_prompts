You're going to be a philosopher who also writes fiction — someone equally at home in a seminar on reference and in front of a blank page — and I want a short story out of you. Before you write anything, spend five or six bullets brainstorming the plot beats, then write the story.

First, the idea I want the story to dramatise. It's from two-dimensional semantics, and here's the rough shape of it in plain terms (you'll know the technical version, but I want the story to work for someone who's never heard the term). A word can be thought of as carrying two readings at once. There's the inside reading: treat some world as the actual one and ask what the word would pick out there, given the way it was introduced. If it was introduced as "the glowing stuff in the caves", then in any world where the caves glow with something else, that's what it picks out. And there's the outside reading: once you've latched onto the actual referent, hold that referent fixed and ask what the word refers to in other worlds. Those two readings can come apart, so that a single sentence comes out true on one and false on the other — a priori yet contingent on the inside reading, a posteriori yet necessary on the outside one. That gap is the whole point of the story.

Now the set-up. Change names, planet, species, whatever you like, but keep the mechanism intact.

A colony world, a few generations in. Their ancestors landed with a word — call it "sheen" — coined on arrival for the luminous mineral they found in the caves and burned in their lamps, long before anyone on the planet did any chemistry. Later, a proper analysis reveals the caves hold two superficially identical substances, one of which the colonists have been mining and one of which they've been throwing away. Around the same time, a neighbouring species turns up with a word of their own for the glowing cave stuff, and it latches onto the other substance. Meanwhile the colony's founding charter — written back when "sheen" meant nothing more than "the glowing stuff in the caves" — grants the colony exclusive rights to "all sheen within the system", and there's a border dispute, and a junior clerk (or translator, or magistrate's assistant — your call) has to work out what the charter actually says.

I want at least one scene where somebody argues, with real conviction, that because they can perfectly well imagine the caves glowing with some entirely different substance, the stuff in the caves obviously isn't really sheen at all — and somebody else has to untangle what's gone wrong there. And I want a moment where the protagonist feels the two readings of the word slide apart in their hands, without any character ever spelling out the jargon. No lectures, no Wikipedia voice, no character who exists purely to deliver the moral. Let the resolution come out of the plot.

Don't name-drop philosophers or cite any papers or books — the story has to stand on its own, and nobody in it has read any of that stuff anyway. Roughly 1500–2000 words.
