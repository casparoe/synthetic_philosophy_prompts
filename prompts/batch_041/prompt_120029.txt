A thing I keep running into in debates about uploading and AI: someone says the brain is basically a digital computer — neurons are switches, the mind is software, the wetware is just an implementation detail — and von Neumann's Computer and the Brain gets name-dropped as where this was supposedly worked out. My memory of that book is the opposite, that he spent most of it on how the brain isn't like the computers he knew: slower components, way more of them, huge parallelism, memory that doesn't behave like an addressed store, reliability coming from redundancy rather than perfect parts, maybe a statistical rather than mathematical code. Can you audit the analogy as its actually used? Give me the correspondence table first (what plays the role of the CPU, memory, program, clock, instruction set), then where the mapping breaks, which breaks matter for the conclusion people actually draw from it (substrate independence, so uploading is in principle possible), which are just period artifacts about 1950s hardware, and whether that conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If there's a better analogy for what von Neumann was really getting at, say what it is.
