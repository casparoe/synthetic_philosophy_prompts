I've been reading Bernard Williams's essays on opera and I'm pretty much convinced by his line: that the "opera as drama" approach — judging an opera by whether the music serves the drama, treating the singing as a problem to be managed — gets things backwards. On his view, the distinctive value of opera is in what music can do that spoken drama can't, and demanding dramatic realism or coherence is largely a category mistake. What would count against this? I want the cases and arguments that should actually move someone who holds it: operas where the drama really does seem to be the point, or where the music does very little work; the strongest case for the "opera as drama" side; and whether Williams's defenders would concede any of it or just say the objection misses the point. Rank them from "this forces a real revision" to "he already has an answer to that."
