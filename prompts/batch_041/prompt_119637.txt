Last night a friend of mine and I argued for two hours about the common prior assumption, and we got nowhere, so I want to settle it on paper. His position is that rational agents who share the same evidence cannot rationally disagree: given a common prior, if two people's posterior beliefs about some event are common knowledge between them, those beliefs must coincide, so any persistent disagreement between equally informed people is itself evidence that someone has reasoned badly — which is why he thinks he and I should each drift toward the other's view merely because the other holds it. I objected that the theorem takes the common prior as a premise and so cannot be evidence for it, and that any disagreement can always be redescribed as differing information, namely information about one's own prior; the assumption is therefore either a substantive empirical claim about how human priors are distributed, which nobody has established, or an empty normalization that does no work. He answered that the assumption is not a psychological claim but a coherence condition of the same family as a no-arbitrage condition — on his telling, it amounts to denying that two agents who share information can both regard a bet against each other as profitable — and that abandoning it licenses free money. I said a framework's internal tidiness is not a standard any actual person meets, and that he is deriving the assumption from the very consequence he wants it to explain.

Write an essay on this controversy: is the common prior assumption a defensible idealization of rationality, or a question-begging premise dressed up as a coherence requirement? Present the strongest version of each side — including the case that the assumption earns its keep by making disagreement tractable and by ruling out profitable speculation among equally informed agents, and the case that it can always be reinterpreted as extra information, so that it says nothing about how anyone's credences actually are — and then reach a verdict. If the right answer depends on what the assumption is being used for, say which uses it survives and which it does not; but do not end on a non-committal note. Roughly 900 words.
