I am writing a paper on the "hinge" propositions that Wittgenstein discusses in the notes collected as *On Certainty*, and before I commit to a reading I want the specification laid out on paper rather than assembled ad hoc in the course of arguing. Set out the desiderata that any adequate account of hinge certainty must satisfy: the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the pressures it must be able to withstand. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it and state what it would take to fail it. The purpose that fixes adequacy, for my purposes, is this: an account that can license criticism of another person's or community's hinges without covertly relying on a hinge of its own, and that leaves room for hinge change to be something other than a bare change of practice.

The list should cover at least the following, though you should add whatever else the account is on the hook for:

- a hinge that an entire community later abandons, not because fresh evidence refuted it but because the practice of inquiry shifted (the abandonment of a foundational framework during a period of mathematical or physical crisis is the sort of thing I have in mind) — must the account call this rational revision or conversion, and does it have a third option?
- two communities with incompatible hinges and no agreed neutral court of appeal — can the account say that either is criticizable from outside, and if so, where does its own standard come from?
- hinges acquired in childhood by training rather than evidence — can the account distinguish having learned a hinge from having been indoctrinated into one, and does it need to?
- a person who holds fast to a hinge against apparent counterevidence in a way we want to call unreasonable — when does immunity from doubt become an intellectual vice, and what in the account marks the line?
- the first-person hinge, the general hinge about how things have always been, and the hinge about method or uniformity — does one account have to treat all three as the same kind of thing, or is disunity here acceptable?
- the account's own claims: are they hinges, ordinary assertions, or neither, and can the account say so without taking back with one hand what it gives with the other?

Flag any subset of the requirements you believe cannot be jointly satisfied. I am particularly suspicious of the pair consisting of the requirement that hinges be groundless — justified by nothing more basic — and the requirement that their abandonment be evaluable as reasonable or unreasonable rather than merely describable; if those two cannot both be had, say so plainly and say what each option costs. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive accounts, and which requirements are commitments of a particular camp rather than data any account must accommodate. You need not score the main contenders against the list; I will do that myself, and I do not want a reading list or citations.

Throughout, keep separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus — where a constraint is the commitment of one interpretation rather than a phenomenon the account must handle, label it as such.
