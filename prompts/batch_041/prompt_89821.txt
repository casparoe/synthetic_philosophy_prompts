Explain the conceptual issue at the bottom of the Judy Benjamin problem. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether the problem is best understood as a failure of conditionalization, a failure of relative information minimization, or an artifact of modeling the incoming message as a constraint on a posterior conditional probability — and then argue for that verdict. No preamble.

The setup, so that we are working from the same case: Judy is dropped with her platoon into a territory divided evenly into Red and Blue, and each of those halves is divided evenly into a Second Company area and a Headquarters Company area, so that her prior is uniform across the four cells. She then receives a radio message from someone who cannot locate her but who can report on how the Red half is divided: the message tells her that, conditional on her being in Red territory, she is three times as likely to be in the Headquarters Company area as in the Second Company area. The message is not a report that she is in Red, nor a report that she is in Headquarters, and it says nothing about how the Blue half is divided.

The standard complaint is that minimizing relative information subject to the constraint the message imposes does not yield a unique posterior: the constraint fixes the ratio of the two Red cells but leaves the rest of the distribution underdetermined, and the objective does not rank the different admissible ways of meeting it. I want an explanation of what this shows, not a survey of proposed repairs. Specifically: (a) why the constraint the message imposes bears on a conditional credence rather than on a proposition with a truth value, and what changes if we instead model the message as a likelihood — as evidence whose probability depends on the state of the world — rather than as a constraint on the posterior; (b) whether the non-uniqueness is a defect of relative information minimization in particular or of any rule that takes the message at face value as a constraint; (c) whether the case is really about conditionals at all, or whether the same structure would arise for any evidence that bears on a ratio of credences rather than on a single cell; and (d) what the case does and does not imply for the broader claim that Bayesian agents cannot update on conditionals.

Be concrete where you can. If you claim the likelihood approach pins down a unique posterior, give the shape of the calculation rather than just naming the approach, and state what the sender must be assumed to have observed for that approach to apply at all. Do not cite literature or offer reading suggestions; reason from the case itself.
