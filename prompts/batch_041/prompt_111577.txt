A funder has asked you to design the research agenda for a five-person, three-year project on the frame problem: two philosophers of mind, one logician, and two engineers who build tool-using language-model agents. The aim is work that will still matter in a decade, and the deliverable is a defended agenda, not a survey of the literature. You have no access to sources, so argue from the structure of the problem rather than citing anyone.

To fix vocabulary, the project's founding note proposes splitting the traditional difficulty into three questions. Treat this split as a proposal to be attacked, not as a received taxonomy.

(a) The discovery question: given an action and a description of a situation, how does a system determine which features of that situation bear on what follows?

(b) The maintenance question: once relevance has been settled, how does a system keep its representation of the world current without recomputing everything at every step, and what licenses the locality assumptions that make this possible?

(c) The recursion question: if a system must choose its own description before it can compute consequences, then framing is itself an action, selected under the very uncertainty it was meant to reduce. On this reading framing is not a preprocessing step but a control problem, and it recurses.

For a contemporary language-model agent — retrieval, tools, long context — the three questions reappear in unfamiliar clothes. Relevance is a learned salience rather than a set of axioms, so a relevance judgment cannot be audited in the way an axiom can. The frame is set implicitly by tokenization, prompting, and retrieval, so the agent may never represent the choice at all. And the cost of a bad frame is paid in attention rather than in search, which changes what counts as a failure and what a repair would even look like.

Answer the following. First, which open questions in this area most deserve the project's effort? Generate the candidate set yourself, then rank it. Second, for each ranked question, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for this particular mixed team, how neglected it is, and what argument or result in the next two years would move it up or down. Third, state your ranking criteria and their weights, the trade-offs among them, what you deliberately excluded, and what would change the ranking. Fourth, rule on two disputes: does the three-way split survive scrutiny, or does one of (a) through (c) absorb the others? And is the frame problem, so posed, still a live problem for language-model agents, or has scale dissolved it — and if it has, what residue remains?

Return a single JSON object and nothing else, with these fields.

`agenda_statement`: one paragraph stating the agenda you are recommending.
`criteria`: array of objects with `criterion`, `definition`, `weight` (0 to 1, summing to 1 across the array), and `why_this_weight`.
`candidates_considered`: array of objects with `id` (short slug), `question` (one sentence), `subproblem` (discovery, maintenance, recursion, or other), `subproblem_note` (one line), and `would_be_settled_by` (what kind of result or argument would count as progress).
`ranking`: array of objects with `rank` (integer), `id`, `defense` (two to four sentences justifying its position relative to the entries below it), `upstream_of` (array of ids), `tractability` (object with `for_philosophers` and `for_engineers`, each 0 to 1, plus `note`), `neglectedness` (0 to 1 plus a one-line note), and `confidence_in_placement` (0 to 1).
`dependency_map`: array of objects with `from_id`, `to_id`, `relation` (presupposes, constrains, partially_answers, or independent), and `note`.
`tradeoffs`: array of objects with `between` (two ids or two criterion names), `tension`, and `how_resolved`.
`excluded`: array of objects with `candidate` and `reason`.
`what_would_change_the_ranking`: array of objects with `trigger` (an argument, result, or capability shift) and `effect` (which ids move and in which direction).
`split_verdict`: object with `survives` (boolean), `collapses_into` (id or null), and `note`.
`dissolution_verdict`: object with `dissolved` (boolean), `residual_problem`, and `note`.
`least_confident_judgment`: object with `claim`, `confidence` (0 to 1), and `what_would_shift_it`.
`bottom_line`: one or two sentences.

Keep every string field compact and argumentative; no field should contain a literature review.
