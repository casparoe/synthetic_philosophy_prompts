I'm in a small phil of physics reading group and we've spent three weeks circling the question of whether the wavefunction is a real physical thing or just a bookkeeping device, and I volunteered to make a tier list for next week because I thought it would force us to be concrete. The group is mostly grad students plus two physicists who will roll their eyes if I just put Copenhagen versus many-worlds on the board, so I want to include a bunch of views that actually say something different about the wavefunction: (1) wavefunction realism, where the wavefunction is a concrete field living on 3N-dimensional configuration space; (2) pilot-wave mechanics, where the wavefunction is real but the particles are the fundamental beables and the wavefunction guides them; (3) many-worlds, where the universal wavefunction is all there is and branching is just decoherence; (4) spontaneous collapse models, where the wavefunction is real but randomly localizes; (5) the personalist Bayesian view, sometimes called QBism, where the wavefunction is an agent's credence and not a representation of anything out there; (6) relational quantum mechanics, where a state is only defined relative to another system; (7) the nomological view, where the wavefunction is more like a law than a thing; (8) a dispositional or causal-power view, where the wavefunction encodes dispositions of a system; and (9) an epistemic hidden-variable view, where the quantum state is like a probability distribution over deeper ontic states, which has to deal with the no-go result that under a preparation-independence assumption two distinct quantum states can't have overlapping distributions over the same underlying ontic states. I also want to include a structural-realist option, where the wavefunction isn't a thing but the structure it encodes is real, though I can't decide if that's a tenth view or just a modifier on view 1.

Could you rate each of these on three 0-10 scales: literal reality (does the view say the wavefunction is a concrete part of the furniture of the world?), completeness (does it treat the wavefunction as the whole story about the quantum state?), and extra structure (how much non-wavefunction machinery it adds, like particles, branches, collapse, primitive dispositions, or agents' beliefs)? Then sort them into tiers (S through D) by how defensible you think they are as answers to whether the wavefunction is real, not by how popular they are, and say in a sentence why each lands where it does. If two views tie on the overall tier but diverge on the sub-scores, tell me which sub-score is doing the work; if the structural-realist option belongs in its own tier, say so and explain why; and if you need a tie-breaker, imagine a future experiment that could weakly measure a single particle's wavefunction and reconstruct it without destroying the particle, and say which views that would help or hurt.
