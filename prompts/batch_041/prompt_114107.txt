Write a case-study page of about 600 words, in the deadpan promotional register of a management consultancy — headings, a client pull-quote in each entry, and a closing "Our metrics" section — for a firm that sells "collapse repositioning" to societies that have already collapsed. Three anonymized engagements: a Late Bronze Age palace economy, a Classic Maya polity, and Rapa Nui. Each entry needs a Challenge, an Intervention, and a Result, and the comedy must come from the genuine conceptual difficulties rather than invented ones: that "collapse" has no settled unit of analysis (state, elite, population, settlement, institution); that demographic continuity is compatible with political collapse, and demographic loss with institutional continuity; and that whether an outcome counts as failure depends on which observer's continuity we score. Let the client quotes come from parties who did well out of the transition — a survivor, a successor polity, a later excavator. The firm's proprietary metric, the Continuity-Adjusted Failure Index, is calibrated so that a client can be moved from "collapsed" to "transformed" by redefining the stakeholder whose continuity is measured; use it, but keep the move plausible enough that an archaeologist recognizes it rather than winces. The target is the collapse discourse on both sides — the moralizing fable of decline and the consultant's assurance that nothing ever really fails — but never state the objection outright; the irony should be legible without a wink. No names of scholars and no citations.
