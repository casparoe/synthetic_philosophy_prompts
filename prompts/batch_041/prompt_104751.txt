For a seminar on the foundations of spacetime I need to lay out my commitments and have them audited for consistency. Here is where I stand.

(1) Substantivalism. Spacetime is a genuine entity in its own right, not merely a system of relations among material bodies.
(2) The bucket. Newton's rotating bucket is a decisive consideration in favor of (1): the concave surface of the water is explained by rotation with respect to space itself, and no facts about the water's relations to other bodies can do that explanatory work.
(3) Leibniz equivalence. The hole argument shows that giving spacetime points primitive identities makes general relativity indeterministic, so I hold that diffeomorphic models represent the same physical situation and that points carry no identity over and above the metrical structure they bear.
(4) No surplus structure. I count something as real only if it can make a difference to some possible observation; structure that no possible observation could reveal is not part of the world.
(5) Dynamical spacetime. My substantivalism is about the metric field of general relativity, not about a fixed Newtonian stage: the inertial structure is itself a physical field governed by the field equations, and it varies from solution to solution.

Two things. First, audit the set: which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each tension? Rank the genuine ones by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each give the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs. Second, a test case: take a universe containing a single bucket of water and nothing else, with the water's surface concave. On my package, what is the water rotating with respect to, and is there any fact of the matter about it? Tell me which of (1)–(5) is doing the work in your answer, and whether my own principles leave me able to give that answer at all.
