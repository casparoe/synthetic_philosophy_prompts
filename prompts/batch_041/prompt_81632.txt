So I've somehow ended up as the judge for our local Nomic league — you know, the game where changing the rules is itself a legal move — and I have to rule on a dispute at next week's meeting, and I'm realising the whole thing hinges on the reading of one clause and I keep flip-flopping. The old judge moved away, so I've got two years of his rulings to be consistent with, and I'd rather not have my first ruling appealed and overturned.

Our rulebook, the relevant bits:

R1. Any rule may be changed by a two-thirds vote of the members present.
R2. R1 may only be changed by a unanimous vote.
R3. No rule change takes effect until the next meeting.
R4. The judge rules on disputed moves; a ruling binds for the meeting it's made in, and any member may appeal it to the full membership at the next meeting.
R5. Rules are numbered in adoption order and numbers are never reused.

Precedents I'm stuck with:

1. A proposal to drop R1's threshold from two-thirds to a simple majority, passed two-thirds to one-third. The old judge ruled it didn't pass — R2 demands unanimity for changes to R1, and two-thirds isn't unanimity.
2. A proposal to amend R1 so that changes have to be tabled in writing a week ahead, passed unanimously. Ruled valid. (So R1 has been amended exactly once, and only by unanimity.)
3. A proposal saying that a change passed earlier in the same meeting was void unless re-passed under the new rules. He struck the retroactive part: a rule change can't reach back and unmake the vote that created it.
4. A proposal amending R3 so changes take effect immediately, passed two-thirds. Ruled valid, and he then let the very next proposal at that meeting take effect immediately too.
5. Someone argued that redefining 'present' for quorum purposes was a back-door amendment to R1 and needed unanimity. He rejected that.

Now the fight. A bloc has put up a single proposal, passed two-thirds to one-third (not unanimous), that does two things at once: strikes R2 off the books entirely, and lowers R1's threshold to a simple majority. Their argument is that R2, read plainly, only ever says something about R1 — it says nothing about itself — so amending R2 is governed by R1, which needs two-thirds, which they got; and once R2 is gone there's nothing left protecting R1. The other side says that's obviously a trick: R2 is the thing that makes R1's protection mean anything, so a vote whose authority over R1 comes from R2 can't be used to dissolve R2 — otherwise 'entrenched' just means 'entrenched until two-thirds of us feel like it', which is to say not entrenched at all.

What I need:

(a) The candidate principles. Give me the two or three general rules such that precedents 1–5 all come out the same way under each of them, and show me that 1–5 definately don't settle this on their own. Also tell me which of 1–5 is the closest analog here.
(b) Which one I should run, and what verdict it gives. Does the whole proposal fail? Does all of it pass? Does striking R2 pass while lowering R1's threshold fails (or the reverse)? And does R3 matter — both halves would take effect at the same moment, so is the vote judged by the rulebook as it stood when the votes were cast, or by the rulebook the proposal itself produces?
(c) The next dispute that would actually distinguish those principles, so I know what I'm signing up for.

And if I'm wrong that this is open — if 1–5 already force an answer and I'm just not seeing it — say so, because that'd save me alot of agonizing.
