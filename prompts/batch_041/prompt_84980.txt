I've somehow ended up as the person writing the strategy note for a small foundation putting about £1.2m over three years into conceptual work on the foundations of welfare measurement — mostly philosophers, a couple of normative economists, and no money for new empirical studies. My job is to say which open questions we should push on and in what order, and I keep going round in circles.

Here's the shape of the area as I see it; tell me where I'm wrong. Everyone agrees we make interpersonal comparisons constantly — triage, cost-effectiveness analysis, benefit caps, sentencing — alot of it assumes that being X is better or worse for A than being Y is for B. The old positivist line was that such comparisons aren't factual claims at all, so the whole business is disguised valuation. That's mostly dead but nothing has really replaced it. Then there's the thing that keeps tripping me up: comparisons of level (who's worse off) versus comparisons of unit (whether a given improvement is worth more to one person than to another). Most policy work seems to need the second quietly while most of the philosophy is about the first. And a colleague keeps insisting the real question is which transformations of our scales we're willing to treat as admissible — a comparison only means something if it survives the rescaling we'd allow — and that for things like health-state indices nobody has done that properly. I also can't get past the disability business: if the scale says a year with a severe impairment is worth a lot less than a year without, and people living with it report being about as satisfied as anyone else, one of those has to give, and which one gives seems to settle a lot downstream. And I've no idea whether any of this carries over to comparisons between humans and animals, or between us and people who don't exist yet.

So: what are the main open questions here, and which two or three would you actually back with three years and four or five people? I want the reasoning more than the list — for each, why it's upstream of the others, whether it's tractable for philosophers rather than people who need fresh data, what a decent output would even look like, and what would move it up or down my ranking. Tell me what to deprioritise too, including anything that sounds weighty but that you'd bet won't change anything downstream, and say so if some of the questions I sketched are really the same question, or not questions at all.

Answer in British English, please.
