Ok so theres this puzzle I keep coming back to — the Judy Benjamin one, where you get dropped somewhere thats either Red or Blue territory, and inside whichever one it is youre either at HQ or at the secondary position, so four cells you start off treating as equally likely. Then you get a message that only tells you something conditional: that if youre in Red, its a coin flip between Red HQ and Red secondary. Nothing else, no info about which territory youre in. The weird part is that this conditional probability was already a half under your prior, so the message looks like it tells you nothing, and yet most people feel like you should now think Red HQ is more likely than Blue HQ. The standard relative-entropy answer says nothing changes at all, which a lot of people find wrong.

Heres the position ive landed on and want you to stress-test. I think the felt pull toward Red HQ comes entirely from treating the message as an act — somebody chose to say a Red-conditional thing to you, and that choice is itself evidence about where you are. So the right move isnt to hunt for a special rule for updating on conditionals. The right move is to model the sender: ask what the sender would have said in each of the four cells, then just condition on the fact that they said that. Do it that way and the puzzle evaporates. And notice whats falling out of this: minimizing relative entropy isnt the uniquely correct rule, its just what you get from one particular sender model, the maximally uninformative one. Change the sender, change the answer. So theres no single right answer to the puzzle and demanding one is a mistake — the thing is underdetermined and thats fine. Im definately open to being wrong about this.

So: what would count against it? I want the real defeaters, ranked by how decisive theyd be, not just the obvious ones. Things im already aware of and want you to weigh rather than merely list: whether this makes the whole thing unfalsifiable (if any sender model is allowed, havent I just rigged it so I can get whatever answer I want — and is that a bug, or is that actually the correct diagnosis); whether the sender-model story can reproduce plain conditionalization when the message is categorical rather than conditional and the sender is perfectly reliable; whether its even legitimate to posit a sender when the problem says you simply learn a conditional (maybe theres no messenger at all, maybe you read it off a legend or deduce it from something else) — does the view collapse if theres no act to model; and the objection that im changing the subject, since the original point was about the logic of learning from conditional information, not about testimony.

Then tell me which of these a defender of my view would themselves concede as counting against them versus wave away, and be honest about whether the view risks anything at all or has just been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether thats actually a problem here, given that I claim underdetermination is the point. The thing that would move me most: a demonstration that the sender-model story cant be made to match ordinary conditioning in the cases everyone agrees on, or a dynamic-coherence / Dutch-book style result showing these updates are diachronically incoherent, or a clean argument that relative-entropy minimization has an independent justification (minimal information, conservatism, whatever) that the sender model lacks and cant fake.
