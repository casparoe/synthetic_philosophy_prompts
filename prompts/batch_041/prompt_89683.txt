OK so I'm putting together a handout for a second-year seminar on animal ethics, and I want the whole thing to be one decision tree that sorts people from their gut reactions down to a named position on whether moral status comes in degrees. The running case is meant to be the full run from insects up to thermostats, thermostats obviously at the bottom, but I want the tree to have to earn that rather than just assume it.

The distinction I want you to keep seperate throughout, because everyone keeps mashing them together, is between (a) how much moral status a thing has, (b) how much weight its interests get when you're totting things up, and (c) its welfare range, meaning how good and how bad things can actually get for it. A tree that treats those three as one thing is no use to me.

Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then drop the actual positions into the space. Each branch node should be a question a student could answer without already knowing which label it leads to. So not are you a utilitarian about animals but things like: does a bee's pain count for a little, or does it count for nothing while we stay unsure whether there's any pain there at all? And: is there a level of consciousness below which nothing at all is owed, or does every extra increment of sentience buy a bit more consideration?

I definately want these leaves in there somewhere: a sharp-threshold view; a pure sliding-scale view where status just is proportional to sentience; a view where status is binary and equal but weight varies with welfare range; a view where what varies is our confidence rather than the fact of the matter, so the tiers are epistemic; a view where sentience isn't what comes in degrees at all, only the complexity of the interests does; a continuity view on which a thermostat gets a vanishingly small but nonzero something; and the flat view that plants and thermostats simply have no interests and the only live question is insects versus fish. Anything else the space throws up, add it. Where a position has no name in circulation, call it unnamed and say so.

Use these cases at the nodes: thermostat, houseplant, oyster, bee, octopus, salmon, mouse, dog, human infant, and a chatbot.

Two housekeeping things. First, flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually seperate, I'd much rather know than have it papered over. Second, for the combinations of answers nobody occupies, say so and diagnose it in a sentence: is it incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? Each leaf gets the position name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. No reading lists, no citations, I just want the instrument.

British English throughout please, behaviour, labelled, that sort of thing. And no more than about twelve leaves or I won't get it onto one side of A4.
