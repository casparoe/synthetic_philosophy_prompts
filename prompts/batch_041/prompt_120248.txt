I'm in a reading group that's been stuck on Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism for weeks, and I want a diagnosis rather than another round. The utilitarian side says: look, the man who saves his wife rather than the stranger doesn't have to be running a calculus — the theory tells us which act is right, not what he should be thinking while he does it; and if he does have the thought that partiality is permitted here, that's a thought he can have before or after, not instead of loving her. Rule-utilitarians add that the rule he acts on can just be save your wife. The Williams side says: that's exactly the dodge. If what makes the act right is that it maximizes welfare, then the agent's deepest commitments are being treated as one input among others in a general calculation, and that's the attack on integrity — not the occurrent thought but the structure of justification the theory is committed to. Same shape in the Jim case: the utilitarian can tell Jim to shoot or not, but the reasons the theory hands him are the wrong kind of reasons to build a life on. And the one-thought-too-many complaint is supposed to be about more than a stray occurrent thought. Neither side moves an inch. My hunch is that they're answering different questions under one word, justification — one side means the consideration that makes the act right, the other means the consideration the agent can act on — and that Williams's objection is really about the second, which is why it seems devastating to one side and easy to deflect to the other. But I'm not sure that's the right diagnosis, or that it isn't just verbal. Can you tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is — a substantive one about practical reason and the limits of moral theory, a verbal one, or two different questions under one heading — and, given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a counterexample, a formal result, or an argument that no progress should be expected? Don't give me a literature survey, I want the structure of the impasse.
