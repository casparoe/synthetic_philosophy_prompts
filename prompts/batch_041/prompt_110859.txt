Before I commit to a line on causation by omission I want the requirements list first — what any account of it has to get right, with the case behind each item, rather than another theory plus it's pet example.

I'm after things like: it has to make the gardener's failure to water a cause of the plant's death, but not the neighbour's failure, and not the stranger's in another country — so it needs something to stop every not-doing by everyone counting as a cause of everything. It has to say whether my not throwing the rope and my pushing him in are the same kind of thing or different, and whether it matters that I could have done otherwise. It has to cope with 'omissions' that are really the absence of a thing, like the missing rock that lets the river flood, without just positing negative events as causes. It has to square with whatever general theory of causation you hold — counterfactual, regularity, powers-based, interventionist — instead of being a special annex bolted on for omissions. And it should explain why people's omission judgements shift when some norm gets broken.

For each requirement say what forces it on us. Then flag the pair you think can't both be met, and which single requirement rules out the most otherwise plausible accounts. If the whole list turns out to be unsatisfiable, say so — that'd be the interesting result.
