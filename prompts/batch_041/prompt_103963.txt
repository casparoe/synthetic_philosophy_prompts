Analyze the post-reunification German prosecutions of East German border guards through the Hart-Fuller debate. The relevant facts are these: after 1989, guards who had shot people fleeing across the inner-German border were tried in unified Germany; they argued that the shootings were authorized by the positive law of the German Democratic Republic and that punishing them retroactively violated legality; some courts rejected that defense, reasoning that the law or its application was so unjust that it could not ground an obligation, while others emphasized that the guards could have refused. Using Hart's legal positivism and Fuller's natural-law position, say what each would make of the trials: was the border law valid law? Does the positivist have to accept retroactive punishment, or can Hart separate validity from obedience and still condemn the guards? Does Fuller's account of law's inner morality really condemn a regime that had courts, rules, and published statutes, or is the injustice too localized? Then tell me which features of the episode the debate illuminates and which it misses, in particular what the case does to the debate's own imagined denunciation case, and whether it shows that the positivist/natural-law line is stable or collapses when the unjust law is not a whole system but a border-shooting statute. Be disciplined about the mapping, and say where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
