I am in a graduate seminar on hedonism, and we keep returning to a question that I can state plainly but cannot find a satisfying name for. The question is what all pleasures have in common. Someone offers the taste of salt on fries, the relief when a headache lifts, the satisfaction of finishing a proof, the warmth of a bath, the pleasure of hearing that a rival has failed, and the absorption of a good conversation; each is supposed to be a pleasure, yet when we look for a single phenomenal quality—a distinctive feeling tone, a sensation, a quale—that all and only these share, the list resists unification. The sensory pleasures do not resemble the intellectual ones; relief seems defined by the removal of pain; malicious pleasure may not be felt as pleasant in the same way at all. The hedonist then often shifts to a functional or intentional characterization: a pleasure is whatever one desires, or takes pleasure in, or whatever plays a certain role in motivation and attention. That move, however, threatens to make the theory circular or to change the subject, since it no longer says what pleasure is, only what it does. I have heard the heterogeneity problem mentioned in this vicinity, but I cannot tell whether it names the specific objection to hedonism, the general shape of the dialectic, or something narrower in the philosophy of mind, and I suspect that the interesting issue is not the label but the carving.

What I am after is whether there is an established concept for this exact dialectical shape: a theory of a mental kind is threatened not by a counterexample but by the apparent absence of any common intrinsic property among the kind’s instances, and is rescued only by a functional or response-dependent account that then seems to lose the original phenomenon. How does this shape differ from family resemblance, from the determinable-determinate relation, from functional kinds, and from the idea of a cluster concept? Does the same pattern arise for emotions, for pain, for attention, or for the virtues, and if so, is there a general term that covers all of them, or does the pleasure case have a distinctive structure because pleasure is supposed to be a single phenomenal quality?

If no established term exists, please coin one and define it, with clear boundary cases and a note on what would count as an instance of the phenomenon rather than a mere case of vagueness or ordinary theoretical disagreement. I would also like to know which of the neighboring concepts comes closest and why it still falls short, since a bare name would not help me see where the phenomenon begins and ends.
