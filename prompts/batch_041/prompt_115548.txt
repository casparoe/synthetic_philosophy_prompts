It should be his own invention rather than one of the well-worn ones, and it should turn on the actual content of the falsifiability criterion and the demarcation problem — for instance, that the criterion is not itself falsifiable, that any failed prediction can be absorbed by adjusting an auxiliary assumption, or that astrology, the standard example of a pseudoscience, arguably makes testable predictions and is simply false, which makes it a poor illustration of unfalsifiability. It must not be a generic joke about scientists being nerds. Give me two or three candidates, each no longer than three sentences plus a punchline, and for each one tell me which feature of the criterion the joke turns on, whether it survives being explained to a reader who does not know the material, and whether it would embarrass my astronomer in front of colleagues who know the literature.
