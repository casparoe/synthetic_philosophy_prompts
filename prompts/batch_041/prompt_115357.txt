I read way too many rat-adjacent blogs, so I basically talk in terms of carving reality at its joints, and now I want to make a quiz for my local rationality meetup that sorts people by what they actually believe about natural kinds instead of by which philosopher they can name. Nine questions, each a concrete case with three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. No citations, no reading list — I dont care who said what, and half the meetup won't read a paper anyway.

Build it from the logical space rather than the standard menu. The axes I care about: (1) whether "is X a natural kind" is a claim about the world, about our concepts, or about what our theories let us predict; (2) what holds a kind together — an essence, a cluster of properties that causally hang together, or just our interests; (3) whether a classification's success at induction and explanation gives you any reason to think it's metaphysically privileged or whether that's just a leap; (4) whether the answer applies everywhere (chemistry, biology, psychiatry, race) or only in the hard sciences or nowhere at all. I want the key to be a grid over those axes so people land in cells, not in named camps — but then give me the names of the cells that have occupants.

Cases I want used, at least most of them: jade (nephrite and jadeite lumped by the folk category and later split), water vs heavy water, that element 118 situation where relativistic effects are supposed to make the heaviest noble gas not act like one, biological species with ring species and horizontal gene transfer, gene talk after alternative splicing, depression as a DSM category, race, and pandas counted as carnivores. Every question should be answerable by someone who's never heard of any of the labels, and none of them should just be "do you think kinds are real?"

Then: name the positions that fall out, say which cells are empty and whether that's because the view is incoherent or just nobody bothered, and flag any two positions the quiz can't actually separate on these cases. If two of them only come apart on cases I left out, say so. One thing I want the quiz to make visible: I keep catching myself saying a category isn't real when what I mean is that it isn't useful for my purposes, and I suspect a lot of people at the meetup are doing the same thing.
