Hey — I'm a novelist (speculative fiction, mostly) and I've got a story idea that keeps collapsing every time I try to draft it, so I'd love to just hand the whole thing to you and see what you make of it.

The setting: a country that abolished elections about thirty years ago and now fills its legislature by pure lottery. Everyone's in the pool, the draw is public, they publish a random seed each year so anybody can re-run the numbers and confirm it's fair. The pitch that sold it was that a random sample of the population beats an elected chamber — more representative, harder to buy, no campaigns, no career politicians, and ordinary people given decent briefings and a few weeks to deliberate make reasonable calls on the big stuff. Fine, I'm on board, that's the world.

The twist I want to build on, and the reason I think this is a story and not just a thought experiment: there's a rule the system was founded on that everybody has mostly stopped mentioning — anyone who *volunteers* for the pool gets quietly dropped from it. The old justification was that wanting power is itself disqualifying, so the only people fit to hold it are the ones who'd rather not. Thirty years on, the legislature is staffed by reluctant draftees counting the days, and there's a little cottage industry of consultants who coach people on how to get themselves bounced out of the pool — medical exemptions, care obligations, whatever sticks. And then a junior auditor at the lottery authority — whose entire job is proving the draw is random, which it verifiably is — notices something in the last few draws that shouldn't be there. Not in who gets picked. In *what they get asked to vote on*.

I'd like the story to follow the auditor, or one of the draftees, or both — your call. What I don't want is a lazy dystopia where the machine turns out to be evil; I want it to actually chew on the ideas. Is "doesn't want the job" a qualification or a disqualification for ruling? What does sortition actually buy you that voting doesn't, and does any of it survive contact with a rule like this one? What happens to accountability when nobody chose to be there and nobody can be voted out? And if the selection is provably fair but the agenda isn't, what exactly have you verified — the legitimacy, or just the fact that the illegitimacy is auditable?

3,000 words or so, any style you like. Give it real texture — a place, some people, weather, whatever — not just talking heads in a room. And if you want to move the country somewhere non-American, definately do that, it'll help me see it. Thanks.
