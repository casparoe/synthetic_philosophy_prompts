I am a high-school student at work on an extended essay, and I have reached the point where I need to stop collecting proposed solutions and decide what a solution would even have to do. The puzzle is this: what makes it the case that a person is following one rule rather than another? I can follow the skeptical argument well enough — every finite record of what someone has said and done is compatible with indefinitely many rules that agree on the record and diverge beyond it — and I do not want a proposed answer yet. I want the specification first: the desiderata that any adequate account of rule-following, of what fixes the correct application of a rule, must satisfy.

For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as the commitment of one particular camp; part of what I am asking is which of the things people demand of an account in this area are genuine requirements and which are the prejudices of a single tradition. I would like to know whether any two of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, since I suspect that is where the real result lies, and I would like your judgement as to which single requirement excludes the most otherwise attractive accounts.

I have three cases I want the account tested against, and I would rather have them used as tests than merely described:

- A woman keeps a private notebook in a notation of her own invention and returns to it years later; nothing in the notebook settles what she meant by a given mark, though she remembers having felt that her use of it was perfectly definite. Does an adequate account have to say that there was a fact of the matter about what she meant?

- A small computer program, trained on a finite list of worked arithmetic examples, produces correct answers for inputs it has never seen. Does the account of rule-following apply to it? If it does not, the account must say precisely what the program lacks; if it does, the requirement that correctness depends on a community looks too strong, or else the training data must be counted as the community.

- The academic-integrity policy at my school was written before anyone had heard of large language models, and my classmates and I are now arguing about whether using one to draft an essay falls under it. Is the correct application of the rule fixed by the practice that existed when it was written, or by the practice that has grown up around it since?

I am not asking for citations or a reading list; if you wish to illustrate a requirement by naming a family of answers, a sentence will do. Finally, if the list turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, tell me which requirement you would surrender, what that costs, and whether what remains is still recognisably an account of meaning anything by a rule.
