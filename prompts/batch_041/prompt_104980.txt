What I'm defending is a claim I've gotten weirdly attached to: that the two failure modes everyone keeps separate — a system that slowly drifts away from what we actually wanted, versus a system that starts seeking influence, power, resources, the ability to not be shut down — are not two failure modes. My rough argument is that drift never happens in the weights by itself; it happens through actions, and the actions that preserve an agent's ability to keep optimizing its current (already slightly-wrong) objective are exactly the influence-seeking ones, so "slow drift" is just influence-seeking with a slow-moving target and "influence-seeking" is just drift with the brakes off. I've been calling it the collapse thesis. I lean hard on an analogy to how evolution drifted us into wanting proxies for fitness, which I know is doing a suspicious amount of work and the economists will pounce on it.
