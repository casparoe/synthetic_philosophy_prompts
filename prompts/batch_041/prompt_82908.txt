ok so I have to present my "position" on animal consciousness at a reading group next week and I'd rather find the cracks in it now than in the room. Here's what I've got, roughly in order of how sure I am:

1. Physicalism, no funny business. Consciousness is something a physical system does, and nothing extra gets added when it does it.

2. It's a matter of degree. There's no single line where the lights come on, just a long ramp from whatever a nematode has (if anything) up to us. Pretty confident about this one.

3. All the same, for any given system there's a fact of the matter about whether there's anything it's like to be it. Not vague, not a matter of how we decide to talk. We might never know, but it's on or off.

4. My working criterion: what matters is whether the system integrates information in the right way — recurrent, global, flexible, the kind of processing that makes information available to the rest of the system for the control of behaviour. Receptors plus reflex arcs don't count.

5. Verdicts: fish yes, octopuses yes, birds yes, insects I honestly don't know but I lean no, and I'd bet against a single ant being conscious.

6. Precautionary principle: where the evidence is a genuine toss-up and the possible suffering is large, I act as if they're conscious. That's why I don't boil lobsters.

7. And I think the number of affected individuals matters — a lot of mildly conscious beings can outweigh a few richly conscious ones.

What's got me stuck is a case I've been calling the swarm case. Imagine a colony where no individual comes anywhere near passing my criterion — each one is basically a reflex machine — but the colony as a whole does: it integrates, it's flexible, it avoids harm, it learns. If I take (4) seriously I seem forced to say the colony is conscious. If I take (1) and (3) seriously I have to ask who exactly is having the experience and whether there's a determinate fact about it. And (2) seems to mess with (6) in a way I can't put my finger on: if consciousness is a ramp, then "the evidence is a toss-up" is true at basically every point on the ramp, and there are astronomically more barely-conscious things than clearly-conscious ones, so precaution seems to swallow everything.

So: which of these actually conflict, which only look like they do, and how bad is each one? Order them by severity rather than telling me they're all in tension. For the real ones, what's the cheapest thing that has to give, and what does giving it up cost me? I don't know in advance which of the seven I'd sacrifice, so don't assume.

And please be explicit about which parts of this are genuinely contested among people who actually work on it versus which are close to settled — I don't want a contested view sold to me as the consensus, or the reverse.
