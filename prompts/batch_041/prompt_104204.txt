ok so I've been chewing on this for like a year and I can't get it straight, and I don't have any philosophy training so maybe I'm just confused, but here's my deal.

I keep running into versions of the idea that your mind isn't only inside your skull, that stuff outside your body can actually be part of your thinking and not just a tool you use. I have gut feelings about which cases are which but they dont fit together.

My gut calls:

1. Contacts and calendar are all on my phone. Ask me my mom's number or whether I'm free Thursday and I honestly don't know until I look. If my phone dies I'm helpless. Gut: this is part of my memory. Not like a metaphor. It's where the info lives.

2. A calculator. I use it for anything past two digits. Gut: no, that's a tool, not part of my mind, even though I'm worse at math without it.

3. The cane my blind friend uses. She says she feels the sidewalk through it, not the handle pressing her palm. Gut: yeah, that's part of how she perceives. It's doing what my eyes do.

4. A guy I read about once with serious memory problems who keeps a notebook with everything in it, appointments, names, what he did yesterday. Without it he's lost, with it he functions. Gut: the notebook is part of his memory, obviously.

5. My husband. He remembers every birthday, every appointment, where we parked. I just ask him. Gut: he is not part of my mind. He's a person. But I can't say why that's different from the notebook except that he has his own life going on.

6. The shared grocery list my husband and I both add to. Gut: part of both our memories?? Which seems insane.

7. Autocomplete on my phone, the smart suggestions. Gut: no, that's the phone guessing, not me. Even when it guesses right and I just tap accept.

8. An AI assistant I chat with that remembers my projects and preferences. Gut: tool, not mind. But then #1 is a problem.

9. A forum/wiki where my hobby community keeps everything it knows. I'm on it constantly, I know the regulars by handle. Gut: no. But it's the same kind of thing as #1, just shared.

10. My glasses. Gut: part of my eyes, not my mind. Not sure that matters.

11. A notebook I keep but almost never open, where just knowing I wrote something down lets me stop thinking about it. Gut: still counts? or not?

So my half-baked rule is: if you rely on it without ever thinking about relying on it, and you'd be genuinely lost without it, it's part of your mind. But that makes the phone part of my mind, which I kind of believe, and makes my husband part of my mind, which I don't believe at all, and makes my car's GPS part of my sense of direction, which I also kind of believe and hate.

What I want: is there a principle that gets all 11, or close, spelled out precisely enough that I could apply it to cases I haven't thought of? And if nothing gets all of them, which of my gut calls has to go, and why that one and not the one right next to it? Also, what does your best version say about: someone with early dementia who starts using a notebook like #4 before they're as lost as he is, part of their mind yet or only once they're bad enough? A whole group relying on the same shared doc, does the group get one bigger mind or does each person? And can something be part of your mind when you don't understand what it's telling you (I use the tip calculator and have zero idea how it gets the number)? And what if someone else edits the notebook, so it's partly not even your own content?

Please answer in American English, and don't just give me the famous names for these positions, I want the actual principle and an honest note on where my gut calls break it.
