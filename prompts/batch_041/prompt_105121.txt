(1) Rational choice is just choosing whatever best serves your goals. (2) You can only compare options you've actually thought of. (3) There's always another option you could think of — another subject, another draft, one more apartment. (4) So the only way to be sure you've picked the best is to consider every option, and since the list never ends you'd never get around to painting. (7) Exept any stopping rule you use (take the first canvas you like, look at ten and take the best of those) can be rewriten as the rule that maximizes your expected payoff once you count the cost of looking. (8) So satisficing is really just maximizing with extra bookkeeping. (9) Which means the whole fight about "bounded rationality" vs. maximizing is a word game and there's nothing actually there.

I'd honestly like (9) to be false, since a genuinely non-maximizing chooser is more interesting for her. But I don't want to hand my character a bad argument and look dumb. So: which step do I give up, why did it look fine until now, and what does dropping it cost me? If you think it's actually all valid, say so and tell me which premise hurts least to lose.
