A former supervisor of mine is a panpsychist, and she has been unusually forthcoming about which composite systems she counts as unified subjects — that is, as having a single experience over and above whatever experience their parts may have. Her recorded verdicts run as follows. A single electron: there is something it is like to be it, but it is no more than a micro-subject. A hydrogen atom, a water molecule, a single neuron: the constituents may have their experience, but there is no subject at the level of the whole. An intact human brain: one unified subject. The universe considered as a whole: not a subject (she is no cosmopsychist). A colony of bees: no colony-level subject, even though the colony solves problems that no single bee could. A split-brain patient after callosotomy: two subjects, two streams of experience. A cerebral organoid grown in a dish and showing spontaneous, coordinated activity: no subject, so far. A large trained artificial network: no subject. A human under general anaesthesia: no subject, though reversibly so.

She holds that what converts a plurality of micro-subjects into a single subject is a relation she calls phenomenal fusion, and she is adamant that fusion is not the same thing as information being shared, nor as the parts' merely being causally coupled. But she has never said whether fusion is available only to biological tissue, and she has published nothing systematic on the point.

The case we are discussing next month is, I think, the one that will force her hand. A patient, some years after a complete callosotomy, has her two hemispheres reconnected by an artificial silicon bridge that restores precisely the high-bandwidth, recurrent traffic the corpus callosum once carried, so that the two halves once again constitute a single causally integrated system; the bridge, however, is not neural tissue.

Predict her verdict. Set out the candidate rules that fit her recorded verdicts equally well — I expect there to be at least two — and state what each predicts about the reconnected patient. Then give your best guess as to which rule she is actually running, with your reasons, and name the further case that would best settle the matter between whichever candidates remain tied once you have given your verdict. I am less interested in the verdict itself than in the rules and in where they come apart, so make the rules explicit. Since she has written nothing systematic, do not go hunting for her in the literature; reason from the verdicts above.

Please write your answer in British English.
