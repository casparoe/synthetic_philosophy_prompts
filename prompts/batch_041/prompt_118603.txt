I am trying to isolate my risk attitudes from my utility function, and I want a principle that captures the following verdicts, stipulating for the sake of the exercise that utility is linear in money. A 50% chance of $100 and a 50% chance of $0: I prefer a certain $50. A 1% chance of $1,000 and a 99% chance of $0: I prefer it to a certain $10. A 90% chance of $100 and a 10% chance of $0: I prefer a certain $80. A 50% chance of losing $100 and a 50% chance of losing nothing: I prefer the lottery to a certain loss of $50. A 1% chance of losing $1,000 and a 99% chance of losing nothing: I prefer a certain loss of $10 to the lottery. My tentative principle is that I evaluate a lottery by a rank-dependent weighted average of its outcomes, where the weight on an outcome depends on the probability of doing at least as well, through a single increasing sensitivity function g with g(0)=0 and g(1)=1, and utility is linear in money. Does any single g fit these verdicts? If not, what is the smallest repair -- separate sensitivity functions for gains and losses, a reference-dependent utility function, or dropping one of my verdicts -- and what does the repaired principle say about a 50% chance of $100 versus a certain $49, and about a 50% chance of losing $100 versus a certain loss of $51? Please be direct if the request rests on a confusion, for instance if isolating risk attitudes from utility curvature in this way is not a coherent thing to ask for.
