I've been arguing with a friend about whether history is made by individuals or by impersonal forces, and we keep trading examples instead of getting anywhere, so I want to try pinning it down with an actual number and an actual breakdown. Here is the question, stated as precisely as I can manage: conditional on Adolf Hitler having died of natural causes in 1936 — after the Nazi seizure of power but before the war and the industrialized mass murder — what is your credence that a recognizably similar catastrophe would still have befallen Germany and Europe within the following fifteen years? Let me fix "recognizably similar" as a regime that (a) was aggressively nationalist and revanchist, (b) carried out state-organized persecution ending in the mass killing of Jews on a comparable scale, and (c) pulled Europe into a general war. Please tell me how you are disambiguating that phrase, and whether a looser or tighter reading would move your number.

What I actually want is the decomposition rather than a bare figure. So please break the credence into the sub-questions it rests on — for instance: the chance that some authoritarian nationalist regime consolidates in Germany absent Hitler, given the unresolved grievances from the first war, the economic collapse, the weakness of the parliamentary institutions, the pre-existing antisemitism, and the conservative elites' willingness to ride the radical right; the chance that such a regime, once consolidated, escalates from persecution to genocide; the chance that it does so on roughly the same scale and timetable; the chance that the slide into a general European war survives the removal of Hitler's particular willingness to gamble and his specific decisions along the way; and whatever residual you want to assign to the man himself. Assign each a rough probability with one line of justification, show how they combine, and then tell me which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. I have been thinking about it as a question of redundancy in a causal chain — how many separate links would each have had to fail the same way for the catastrophe to be avoided — so I'd also like to know, on your account, how many independent points of failure there were and which one was most nearly load-bearing. Finally, please be explicit about which parts of this are settled among people who study the period and which are genuinely contested, rather than presenting a contested view as the consensus, and tell me what argument or piece of evidence would shift your number the most.
