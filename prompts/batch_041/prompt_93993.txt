Write an essay on the following controversy in hinge epistemology: whether the commitments Wittgenstein treats as hinges in On Certainty can themselves be assessed as rational or irrational, or whether they lie outside the space of reasons altogether.

Some background. On Certainty is usually read as offering a third way between foundationalism and coherentism. Its suggestion is that certain commitments — that one has hands, that the world is vastly older than one's own existence, that the words one uses mean what they seem to mean — do not function as justified beliefs at all. They are the scaffolding against which justification, doubt, and error become possible; they are what we stand on rather than what we argue to. One influential line of interpretation takes this to mean that hinges are non-propositional: animal certainties, enacted in practice rather than held as beliefs, and so not the sort of thing that can be true or false, rational or irrational. A second reading takes them to be genuine propositions that we hold, but hold groundlessly — without evidence and without the possibility of evidence, yet still within the domain of the epistemic. The controversy concerns which reading, if either, the text and the phenomenon support, and what each reading costs. If hinges lie outside the space of reasons, hinge epistemology appears to buy its immunity from scepticism by making fundamental commitments immune to criticism, and it threatens to license a relativism on which whole world-pictures are beyond rational appraisal. If hinges lie inside the space of reasons, the view looks like a familiar foundationalism or coherentism in disguise, and its distinctive claim lapses.

The idea I would like you to try out. Distinguish two ways in which a commitment might be insulated from rational appraisal. Call a commitment strategically insulated when a rational challenge to it is in principle formulable, but the community that holds it has arranged its practices so that no such challenge is ever live — the challenge is not impossible, merely never made. Call a commitment constitutively insulated when no rational challenge is even formulable without already presupposing the commitment, so that the challenge is not merely unused but unusable. The proposal to test is this: hinge epistemology's distinctive claim is that its hinges are constitutively, not merely strategically, insulated, and that this single feature is at once what saves the view from collapsing into ordinary foundationalism and what exposes it to the charge of relativism.

Write an essay that weighs the arguments on both sides and reaches a verdict. Address at least the following. Is the distinction between strategic and constitutive insulation stable, or can every allegedly constitutive case be redescribed as a strategic one sustained by social pressure or by the costs of abandoning it? Do the non-propositional and the groundless-propositional readings even need the distinction, or does one of them make it idle? If constitutive insulation exists, is it sufficient to deflect the relativism charge, or does it merely relocate it? And could a hinge epistemology that forgoes constitutive insulation still make good on its claim to be a third way? Do not end on a non-committal note: defend a verdict about whether hinge epistemology is best understood as resting on constitutive insulation, and about whether, so understood, it succeeds. Reason from the phenomenon of hinge commitments and from the costs of the rival readings, rather than from what any particular commentator is reported to have held.
