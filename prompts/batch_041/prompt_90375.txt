Im working through the old Francis Adams translation of Hippocrates' Airs, Waters, Places for a seminar on enviornmental determinism and non-human causes, and theres one stretch I keep going round in circles on. Here it is:

"And with regard to the pusillanimity and cowardice of the inhabitants, the principal reason the Asiatics are more unwarlike and of gentler disposition than the Europeans is, the nature of the seasons, which do not undergo any great changes either to heat or cold, or the like; for there is neither excitement of the understanding nor any strong change of the body whereby the temper might be ruffled and they be roused to inconsiderate emotion and passion ... It is changes of all kinds which arouse understanding of mankind, and do not allow them to get into a torpid condition. For these reasons, it appears to me, the Asiatic race is feeble, and further, owing to their laws; for monarchy prevails in the greater part of Asia ... Thus, then, if any one be naturally warlike and courageous, his disposition will be changed by the institutions."

What is he actually claiming about how the climate shapes people? On one pass it reads like the seasons are the master cause of the Asiatics being unwarlike, then two lines later its "owing to their laws" as well, and a naturally warlike man's disposition "will be changed by the institutions." So does the environment cause the character, or merely set a baseline that politics can move you off? The mechanism bothers me too: "changes of all kinds arouse understanding" sounds like a real causal process acting on the body, but I cant tell if it's meant that way or is just a flowery way of saying variety is stimulating. And one more thing I want to get straight, since it's the whole point of the seminar: does he treat the climate as a kind of actor that does things to people, or just as a standing condition they happen to live under? I keep flip-flopping between a hard determinist reading and a soft one where institutions override nature, and I'd like to know which the passage actually supports. Dont give me the secondary literature on this, just help me read it.
