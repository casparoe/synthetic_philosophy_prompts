I am a secondary-school biology teacher, and three weeks ago a genetic test confirmed that I carry a mutation that will almost certainly cause a neurodegenerative condition in middle age. Since then I have been trying to explain to my family what the result does and does not mean, and I have become uneasy about how casually I once said that a gene is a code and that a mutation carries information. A colleague in the philosophy department offered the following argument. He says it shows that the language of genes for traits is not literally true. I cannot accept that conclusion, because if it were right I could not say that the mutation I carry is the cause of my condition in any informative sense, but I cannot locate the mistake. Please tell me which premise or step I should reject, why it looked plausible, and what rejecting it costs.

(1) A code is a system in which one set of symbols stands for another set of items.
(2) For such a system to be a code, there must be a mapping from the symbols to the items.
(3) If a mapping is not intrinsic to the symbols and the items, then it must be fixed by something that represents the items to a user of the code.
(4) The mapping from codons to amino acids is not intrinsic: any codon could have mapped to any amino acid, and the same amino acid is encoded by several different codons.
(5) Therefore, the mapping must be fixed by something that represents amino acids to a user.
(6) But nothing in the cell represents amino acids to a user. There is no author, no convention, and no interpreter in the cell, only molecules, binding affinities, and catalytic reactions.
(7) Therefore, the mapping is not fixed at all.
(8) Therefore, DNA is not a code.
(9) If DNA is not a code, then it does not carry semantic information.
(10) Therefore, talk of a gene for a trait is at best a metaphor.

Which step do you reject? If I have to choose, I would rather keep (1) through (4) and (9) than (10), but I want to know what I am paying. Explain why the step I should reject looked fine, and what the alternatives cost.
