I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade, and I keep running into the same move in arguments about autonomous systems: someone insists that the "responsibility gap" is overblown because we already have a working precedent for holding an agent accountable when no single individual satisfies the traditional conditions of blame. Their example is always the corporation. A corporation has no mind, no single employee who intends the corporation's overall course of action, and yet we treat it as an agent, assign it duties, and punish it when it goes wrong -- and nobody thinks this is metaphysically scandalous. The claim, as I keep hearing it, is that an autonomous system is relevantly like a corporation: a locus of agency constituted by many sub-parts, none of which individually carries the whole, and that once we see the analogy the gap stops looking like a gap and starts looking like an accounting problem we already know how to solve.

I want an audit of that analogy rather than a verdict on autonomous systems in general. Lay out the correspondence explicitly -- a table is fine -- and tell me what plays the role of the board, the charter, the employees, the treasury, the legal person, and the sanction, and what in the autonomous-system case is supposed to map onto each. Then tell me where the mapping breaks. I care especially about the disanalogies that bear on the specific work the analogy is being asked to do: does the corporation case show that a genuine moral agent can be constituted out of non-agents, or does it show only that we can manufacture a legal fiction of liability for prudential and compensatory reasons -- and if it's the latter, does anything follow about blameworthiness for an autonomous system at all? I'd also like to know which features of the corporation -- the fact that its behavior is always in principle traceable to human decisions, that it can be deterred by sanctions, that it can be dissolved, that its "intent" is fixed by documents humans can read -- are load-bearing for the anti-gap conclusion and whether the autonomous-system case actually shares them. Finally, tell me whether the anti-gap conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion the whole time; and if it breaks, sketch what a better comparison would be.
