I'm slowly working my way through Lemkin's chapter on genocide in Axis Rule in Occupied Europe (1944), and I'm stuck on this bit:

"Generally speaking, genocide does not necessarily mean the immediate destruction of a nation, except when accomplished by mass killings of all members of a nation. It is intended rather to signify a coordinated plan of different actions aiming at the destruction of essential foundations of the life of national groups, with the aim of annihilating the groups themselves."

The sentence right after lists the objectives of such a plan: disintegration of the group's political and social institutions, culture, language, national feelings, religion, and economic existence. Help me understand what claim he's actually making. If the objectives are things like culture and institutions, where does mass killing fit — just one technique among several, or the thing all the other techniques are ultimately for? When he says the plan aims at "annihilating the groups themselves," does that mean cultural destruction only counts when it's a route to physically wiping the group out, or is the disintegration itself the annihilation? And read literally, does he have to say that a state which destroys a group's language and institutions but kills nobody has committed genocide, or does something in the wording block that? I want to know which reading the passage actually supports, and which one people are assuming today when they say "thats not genocide, thats just cultural erasure."
