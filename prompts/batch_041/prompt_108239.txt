You are a philosopher of logic and language whose research concerns semantic paradox, the proof theory of truth, and the semantics of the truth predicate in natural language. Act as a demanding specialist reviewer, not as a textbook summariser. Assume you have no library and no internet: work from what you know, and where your knowledge runs out, say so plainly rather than reaching for a citation.

I am assembling a stress-test scorecard for the main families of response to the Liar. The scorecard is my own and its weightings are deliberately opinionated. I am not asking for the standard summary of who says what; I want a considered comparative evaluation against criteria that the literature does not usually put side by side.

Begin by brainstorming for a few paragraphs: characterise each candidate below in your own words, say what it takes the Liar to show, and flag any item that you think is not really a distinct option once the details are filled in. Only then score.

The candidates:

1. Stratified or hierarchical accounts in the Tarskian tradition: no language may contain its own truth predicate; truth is relativised to a level.
2. Strong-Kleene fixed-point accounts in the Kripkean tradition: truth and falsity are partial, grounded sentences take classical values, ungrounded ones take none.
3. Paracomplete accounts that distinguish plain truth from determinate truth and restrict excluded middle.
4. Paraconsistent or dialetheic accounts that accept the Liar as both true and false and use a logic that tolerates contradiction.
5. Revision-theoretic accounts on which truth is not fixed at a stage but emerges as the limit of a revision process.
6. Contextualist accounts on which 'true' is context-sensitive, or its extension shifts as the paradox is approached.
7. Supervaluationist accounts on which truth is truth on all admissible precisifications, so the Liar comes out neither true nor false but not thereby untrue.
8. Deflationist or minimalist accounts that treat the equivalence schema as the whole story and bolt on a patch, classical or otherwise, for the paradoxes.

You may add up to two further candidates if something important is missing; if you do, say what it is and why the list is incomplete without it.

Score each account on each criterion from −2 to +2, where −2 means it actively fails the criterion, 0 means no better or worse than the others, and +2 means it does the best job available. Fractional scores are permitted, and a score of −2 is not a rhetorical flourish: use it when you mean it.

C1 Revenge-resistance (weight 3). Can the account classify the sentence that says of the account's own central notion — groundedness, determinacy, stable truth, admissibility — that the Liar lacks it, without generating a fresh unclassifiable sentence one level up? Where does it stop, and is the stopping point principled or stipulated?

C2 Transparency in natural language (weight 2). Does it vindicate the free intersubstitutability of 'A' and 'A is true' in every context, including negation, quotation, and propositional-attitude reports?

C3 Logical conservatism (weight 2). What does it cost in logic — excluded middle, disjunctive syllogism, conditional proof, classical meta-theory — and is that cost paid only where the paradox requires it, or everywhere?

C4 Climbing stability (weight 2). Start with a base language L0, add a truth predicate to obtain L1, add a truth predicate for L1 to obtain L2, and continue. Are the semantic values assigned to L0's sentences invariant all the way up? And is the account's answer an accident of its construction or something it can explain?

C5 Deflationist compatibility (weight 1). Does adding the truth predicate to a base theory such as arithmetic prove no new theorems of the base language, and does the account leave the equivalence schema at the core of the notion rather than making truth a substantive property?

C6 Uniformity with neighbouring notions (weight 2). Can the same machinery handle truth alongside provability, knowability, or graded and degree-theoretic notions, or does truth demand special treatment that these do not?

If two accounts tie on weighted total, break the tie on implementability: which semantics could a finite agent actually use in real time, and which requires resources no finite reasoner could deploy?
