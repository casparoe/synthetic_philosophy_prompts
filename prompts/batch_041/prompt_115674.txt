Hi — I'm a third-year philosophy PhD working on social ontology, and my dissertation is basically about whether "generations" (boomers, millennials, gen z) are real kinds or just a marketing category wearing a demographer's labcoat. What's bugging me is that the whole debate seems to run together three seperate things: generations as cohort kinds (just a birth-year band you can drop into a regression), generations as historical kinds (defined by living through some shared shock), and generations as narrative kinds (defined by being told, and telling yourself, that you're a generation). Everyone either does the deflationist move and says it's all astrology, or quietly treats "millennial" like it picks out a natural kind the way "gold" does, and I think both miss the actual question, which is which of those three strands is doing the causal work, in which contexts, and for whom. I've got about three years and one dissertation chapter to spend here, so I want to prioritize rather than survey. Rank me exactly five open questions in this area that are worth the years, as a numbered list, one or two sentences each on what the question is and why it ranks where it does, no intro and no wrap-up. American English, please.
