I am convening an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over logical omniscience: a defender of classical epistemic logic, for whom closure under logical consequence is a harmless idealization, and a critic who regards it as a distortion of the phenomenon of knowledge. Draft the joint statement they could both sign. It should contain a numbered section of agreed claims about knowledge, consequence, and the agent in a model; a disagreements table stating each side's position in wording that side would accept; the cruxes -- for example, whether the fault lies in the semantics or in its application, whether explicit and implicit knowledge are two kinds or one, and whether a resource-bounded consequence relation can preserve factivity, the principle that if one knows that p, p is true; and, for each crux, a test, case, or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use these shared cases: an agent who knows a long conjunction and each step of a proof but never assembles the proof, and a proof assistant that has verified a theorem while its user remains ignorant of it. Do not include a bibliography or citations.
