My friend is a comparative cognition person and she's got this habit of sorting every animal and gadget into one of Dennett's four grades -- Darwinian (the know-how is in the genes), Skinnerian (it's been reinforced into the individual), Popperian (the individual tests options inside its own head before acting), Gregorian (it leans on language and external mind-tools). Her calls so far: E. coli swimming up a sugar gradient, Darwinian. A day-old chick pecking in exactly the right spot with no prior experience, Darwinian. A lab rat learning to press a lever, Skinnerian. A dog salivating at a bell, Skinnerian. A crow that seems to run through a sequence in its head before committing, Popperian. A kid doing long division with pencil and paper, Gregorian. A toddler soaking up words, Gregorian. And the one that threw me: a songbird that learns its song by copying the adults around it, she calls Skinnerian rather than Gregorian, on the grounds that copying is still just reinforcement and nobody's handing the bird a tool.

Now she's about to grade two new things and I want to get ahead of her. First, a New Caledonian crow population where one bird works out a new hooked-tool technique and it then spreads through the group by imitation. Second, a current large language model that's been fine-tuned on human feedback.

So: what does she say about each, what rule is she actually running (I have a suspicion it isn't Dennett's own, whatever she tells herself), what other rules fit every one of her earlier calls just as well, and which single further case would best force her to choose between them? Dont worry about being right -- I care more about the rule shes using and what would break the tie.
