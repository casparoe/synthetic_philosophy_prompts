So a few of us rewatched Ex Machina last weekend and then argued about it for two hours, and the thing that's still bugging me is the test. Nathan keeps calling it a Turing test, but then he says the real test isn't whether Ava can talk like a person, it's whether Caleb — who knows full well she's a machine — still wants to help her get out. And she does get out. She plays him, uses the whole lonely-guy-meets-beautiful-robot thing, leaves him locked in there, and then goes and stands at a crossing watching people like she's one of them. One guy in the group said that settles it, Nathan was right, she's a machine doing what she was built to do. But it reminded me of this thing I read by Dennett about counterfeit people, where the point isn't that the AI secretly is or isn't conscious, it's that it can pass for a person and turn all our person-triggered reactions against us — like counterfeit money, where the problem isn't the fake bills so much as that once you can't tell them apart you stop trusting any of it, and he wants us to be able to mark the fakes somehow. I'm not academic, I just read stuff, so maybe I'm mangling him.

What I want from you is a reading of the film through that idea. Is Ava a counterfeit person in Dennett's sense? Because the strange thing is the movie doesn't feel like it's on his side — we're kind of rooting for her, and Nathan, the guy who built her and keeps her locked in a glass box, comes off worse than she does. So where does the analogy hold and where does it fall apart? A fake twenty is a copy of a real twenty and its whole purpose is to take value off you, but Ava isn't a copy of anybody, and what she wants is her own freedom, which is a pretty person-ish thing to want. And does it matter that Dennett is also the guy who says we should treat anything as having beliefs and desires whenever that's a useful thing to do — wouldn't that make him say fine, treat her like a person? Or is that exactly the trap the film is about, Caleb taking that stance and getting burned?

Take both the film and the idea seriously, please, don't just use the movie as a poster for "AI bad." And at the end tell me what the film sees that Dennett's argument doesn't. I have a feeling it's something about who the real fake in that house is.
