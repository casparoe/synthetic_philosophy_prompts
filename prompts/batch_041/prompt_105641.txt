Ok so my friend Dana runs this recurring thing on our forum — every Sunday she posts one verdict, "does this thing exist or not", and the rest of us try to reverse-engineer whatever rule shes implicitly running. Shes not being mystical about it, shes a boring physicalist, she just has strong opinions about when a bunch of stuff adds up to one object. Her rulings so far:

Exist: a human being, a dog, an oak tree, a gut bacterium, a water molecule, a salt crystal, a diamond, a rock you could pick up on the beach, a snowflake.

Don't exist: a pile of sand, a heap of gravel, a bag of groceries, a chess set laid out on a board, a cloud, a river, a queue at the airport, a corporation, a bee colony, a biological species, this morning's traffic jam.

Next week she's posting: a candle flame. She's also taking side bets on a hurricane, a lichen, and a cold virus.

What I want from you: what rule (or rules) is she actually running? I can think of at least two that fit every ruling above and I want them seperated. For each rule, predict her verdict on the flame, the hurricane, the lichen and the virus; then give me your best guess overall and why; then name the case that would actually tell the rules apart — the one where they make opposite calls, so whoever runs that test has to pick a side. If her rulings genuinely underdetermine it, say so instead of faking a clean answer, and tell me what extra principle she'd be buying with each choice.

Context: I've been reading this stuff on the rationalist blogs for years, so feel free to get into the weeds — intrinsic bonding vs. self-maintaining dissipative structures vs. the organicist line that only lives compose vs. straight nihilism. Just don't hand me a reading list, I want the reasoning.
