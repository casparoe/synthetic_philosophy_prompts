I teach the MIU system every year and every year we hit the same wall: we show MU is unreachable — the number of I's is never divisible by three, and MU has none — and one student says we had to jump out of the system to see that, and another says no, we just found a fact about the system. This year a colleague took the second student's side and pushed it much harder than I could answer. Her line: any meta-argument is itself a derivation in some formal system. Write down arithmetic on string lengths and call it M+; the invariant proof is then just a theorem of M+, and the invariant itself is only a claim about which strings are derivable, i.e. a formal claim. Whatever I do to justify M+ — why mod 3 and not mod 5, why these axioms and not others — happens inside another system M++, and so on forever. So nobody ever jumps out; you only ever swap one system for a bigger one, and the sensation of stepping outside is an artifact of changing notation. Her conclusion: JOOTSing isn't a thing, its just system-hopping, and Hofstadter dressed up a change of alphabet as transcendence.

I want to argue the other way, but it has to be a real argument and not hand-waving about creativity or intuition. So make the case against her. Where does the regress actually fail — is she equivocating on 'system', or is the real point about what licenses the move to M+ in the first place? What does the MU case show that a purely formal extension of the system doesn't? And tell me which parts of my side hold up and which I should just give up.
