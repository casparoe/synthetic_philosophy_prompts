Me and a friend do a film + philosophy night and next months pick is mine — The Prestige, mainly because of the hats. You know the bits: Tesla's machine in Colorado Springs, that shot of all the identical top hats, Angier drowning one of himself in the tank under the stage every night, and Borden turning out to have been two identical men sharing one life the whole time, one of them loving Sarah and the other Olivia. I want a proper reading of it through the identity of indiscernibles — Leibniz's principle that there can't be two objects differing only numerically — and through Max Black's thought experiment of two identical iron spheres at a distance from each other in an otherwise empty universe. My hunch, and you can back it up or wreck it: the film is running two different problems side by side without admitting it. Borden's double is two things at once, and the film quietly hands them different properties to keep them apart (different women, different grief), which is basically Black's own escape hatch — count relational properties and the spheres were never indiscernible, they're a mile apart. Angier's copies are the other problem, one thing splitting in two over time, the Parfit sort of case where you ask whether there's still a fact about which one is you; and the film is horrified by that one precisely because it can't find any property to pull the copies apart, so that looks like the one place it really denies the principle. Is that a fair split, or am I reading in a distinction that isn't there? And if the honest answer is that the film needs a haecceity, a bare thisness that isn't a property at all, I'd rather hear that said out loud. Second thing I can't shake: Borden's twindom is natural, Angier's duplication is machine-made, and I'm fairly sure the film treats the double as one of two real men but the copies as disposable — that smells like a prejudice about origins rather than a view about identity, so tell me wether it's a real distinction or just sentiment. No plot recap, no reading list. Last line should start Verdict: and say in one sentence whether the film comes out denying or accepting the identity of indiscernibles, or ducking it.
