I'm in a reading group thats working through the outbreak of the First World War, and every week the same analogy comes out of someones mouth: the July crisis was a chain reaction, or a row of dominoes, or a runaway train. Once the first domino goes, or the brakes fail, theres nothing anyone can do. It keeps getting used to wave off the Fischer argument — if the whole thing was a mechanism grinding forward, then pinning the war on Germany is like blaming the last domino, or the poor guy who happened to be at the wheel. I dont buy it but I cant articulate why, and I want a proper audit rather than a vibe.

So give me the correspondence table first. What in the July crisis is supposed to be the dominoes, whats the initial push, whose the hand, what plays the role of gravity and the table it all sits on, what corresponds to the little gap between one domino falling and the next. Then the places it breaks. I suspect the interesting ones are about agency — statesmen deliberating in real time, halt orders, the difference between a partial and a general mobilization, the blank check being a choice — and about asymmetry, whether all the dominoes were really the same size, so to speak. Then tell me which of those breaks actually matter for the use the analogy is being put to, i.e. the no-ones-to-blame conclusion, and which are just pedantry that a defender of the picture could shrug off.

And if it does break, what would a better analogy be — something that keeps the pressure toward responsibility on someones shoulders without pretending any single actor could have stopped the whole thing by an act of will. Fischer is in the background here, so say what the analogy would have to look like for a claim about German responsibility to even be statable, as opposed to looking like the last domino blaming itself.
