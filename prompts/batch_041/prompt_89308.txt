My two flatmates have spent the week arguing about the toxin puzzle, and I have been drafted as referee. Their setup: a billionaire will wire you a million dollars at midnight if and only if a scanner then confirms that you intend, at that moment, to swallow a capsule at noon the next day; the capsule brings on a day of miserable but harmless illness. The money is in your account well before noon, and nothing you do at noon can add to or subtract from it. At noon you may swallow the capsule or not.

Flatmate A thinks the puzzle has a simple solution everyone overlooks: buy a commitment device. Pay a reliable friend in advance to come at noon and physically make you swallow the capsule. Once that is arranged you can sincerely intend to swallow it, because the friend guarantees that you will; the scanner registers a genuine intention, you collect the million, and the paradox dissolves. On his view the puzzle was never about the will's limits, only about our failure to notice that we can purchase resolve.

Flatmate B calls this a shell game. The enforcer now occupies exactly your position: he has already been paid, so at noon forcing a nasty capsule down your throat is pointless effort for him. If he is rational he can no more intend at midnight to force you than you could intend to swallow the capsule; and if you cannot rely on his intention, you cannot sincerely intend on his behalf either. A replies that this only pushes the question back a step, and that some people really are the resolute type who keep commitments even when keeping them is pointless; hiring one of those is no different from hiring a strong pair of arms. B answers that if resoluteness means doing pointless harmful things on principle, it is a defect rather than a resource, and that if the plan works only because of the enforcer's arms, then the intention the scanner is supposed to detect is not really yours at all.

Please adjudicate this exchange move by move, rating each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and then say what both of them are missing. Is the commitment device a solution to the puzzle, a relocation of it, or a change of subject? I would also like your best guess at which flatmate is closer to the truth overall, and the single question you would put to them to settle it.
