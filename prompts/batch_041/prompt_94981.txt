I had a long argument with a friend last night and I want to keep it going, but I need someone to actually take a side. Here's the setup. My friend is a philosopher who's spent years thinking about confirmation, and she's got what she thinks is a clean, principled line on which observations confirm which universal generalizations. I've been trying to reverse-engineer her rule. Her verdicts, as best I can reconstruct them:

1. Hypothesis: "All ravens are black." Observation: she goes out, picks a raven, and finds it black. Verdict: confirms.
2. Hypothesis: "All ravens are black." Observation: she picks up an object at random and finds it's a white shoe. Verdict: does not confirm.
3. Hypothesis: "All ravens are black." Observation: she picks up an object at random and finds it's a green apple. Verdict: does not confirm.
4. Hypothesis: "All non-black things are non-ravens." Observation: she picks up an object at random and finds it's a white shoe. Verdict: confirms.
5. Hypothesis: "All non-black things are non-ravens." Observation: she picks up a raven and finds it's black. Verdict: does not confirm (she says a black raven is "the wrong kind of thing" for that hypothesis).
6. Hypothesis: "All emeralds are green." Observation: she picks up an emerald and finds it green. Verdict: confirms.

Now here's the thing I actually want you to do. There's a new case, and I want you to tell me what she'll say and, more importantly, what rule she's running, because I think her six verdicts are jointly consistent with at least two genuinely different rules and I want to know which one she's actually on.

The new case: she's testing "All ravens are black," but this time the sampling is done by a machine she doesn't control. The machine draws one object uniformly at random from a huge, opaque urn. She knows the urn contains some ravens and some non-ravens, some black things and some non-black things, in proportions she has no idea about. The machine draws out a white shoe. She looks at it. Question: does this observation confirm "All ravens are black"?

I care less about the yes/no than about the following: (a) state the candidate rules that fit verdicts 1 through 6, and for each one say exactly what it predicts about the new case; (b) commit to which rule you think she's actually running and give your reasons; (c) name one further case that would cleanly separate the rules you can't otherwise tell apart, and say what each rule predicts about it. Don't hedge into "it depends what she means by confirm" -- pick the most defensible reconstruction and defend it. If you think two of her verdicts are actually inconsistent with each other under every rule, say so and tell me which one you'd give up.
