I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of two decades, and one recurring frustration is how quickly discussions of unrealistic assumptions in formal models collapse into slogans — the ones about models being useful even when wrong, or about maps not being territories — that stop doing any work the moment you press on them. I want to force the issue with a distinction I am stipulating for this exercise rather than reporting from the literature. Call an assumption in a model a load-bearing falsehood when the model's success at prediction in its target domain depends on retaining that assumption in full awareness of its falsity: correct or relax it and the predictions degrade. Call it an idle falsehood when the assumption is false but the model would predict identically without it. My claim, which I would like you to argue for rather than merely assess, is that the view associated with Friedman — that the realism of a theory's assumptions is irrelevant to appraising it, and that only its predictive performance matters — is defensible with respect to idle falsehoods and indefensible with respect to load-bearing ones, and that nearly every philosophically interesting case is the latter.

Please build the strongest case you can for that position. In doing so: give a criterion for sorting a given false assumption into one category or the other, and explain how it resists the obvious rejoinder that a sufficiently stubborn modeler can declare any assumption load-bearing; say why it matters, epistemically, whether a science rests on idle or on load-bearing falsehoods, and whether the difference ought to change how models are reported, taught, and trusted; construct one concrete example of each kind from a domain of your choosing, avoiding any case you suspect I have already heard rehearsed; and confront the strongest objection you can find — for instance, that being load-bearing is not a property of an assumption at all but of the modeler's failure to search for a better model, so that every load-bearing falsehood is really just an unsolved research problem in disguise.

I care far less about a survey of positions than about a sustained argument with its joints visible, so where you are genuinely unsure, say so, and mark what evidence or argument would move you.
