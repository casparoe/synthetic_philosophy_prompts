Two members of my reading group, Mara and Dev, spent most of last week's session arguing about infinite sequences of tasks and have asked me to referee, since neither will concede. Here is their exchange as faithfully as I can reconstruct it.

They began with the old lamp puzzle: a lamp is switched on one minute before midnight, off half a minute before, on a quarter of a minute before, and so on, one flick per halving interval. Mara said this shows that an infinite sequence of tasks can never be completed. At midnight the lamp must be on, since it is on after every odd-numbered flick and nothing later turns it off; it must equally be off, by the same reasoning; that is impossible, so the story describes nothing, so finishing infinitely many acts is incoherent.

Dev said the contradiction shows only that the lamp's final state is unspecified. The flick sequence has no limit, so the description simply fails to settle what the lamp is doing at midnight. That is a defect in the story, not a proof about tasks. He compared Zeno's runner: the positions 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ... approach 1, and we say the runner arrives at 1, not that the run is impossible. Supertasks are fine when the sequence converges; the lamp just doesn't converge.

Mara then proposed a device built to converge: a dial marked from 0 to 1 whose pointer, at each halving interval, is set to 1 − 1/2^n, so that it reads 1/2, then 3/4, then 7/8, and so on. Where is the pointer at midnight? Dev said at 1, the limit. Mara said there is no moment at which it reaches 1 and no last step that puts it there; at every earlier moment it reads less than 1; so either it sits at 1 with no act having moved it there — a change with no time of change — or the description leaves the answer open, exactly as with the lamp. Dev replied that this is simply how limits work: the value at midnight is the limit of the values before midnight, and nothing needs to happen at midnight.

Mara's last move was a variant of the same dial, fitted with a catch that makes it impossible for the pointer ever to sit at 1: if a step would set it to 1, the catch snaps the pointer to 0 instead. No step ever sets it to 1, so the catch never fires. Yet the limit of the pointer positions is 1, which the catch forbids. So either the pointer is at 1, violating the device's own specification, or it is at 0, in which case it got there with no step to move it; and if the catch is instead built into the physics so that the pointer jumps at midnight, then the jump was stipulated rather than derived, and the limit rule was never a rule. Dev answered that a rule about what happens at steps has no application where there are no steps, so the catch is idle and the pointer is at 1; and that if Mara builds a discontinuity into the device, she has simply described a different device.

Please adjudicate claim by claim rather than side against side. Is Mara right that the lamp shows supertasks cannot be completed? Is Dev's Zeno comparison apt, and does it actually support his conclusion? Is the principle that the state at midnight is the limit of the states before it something Dev is entitled to, or a stipulation? Does the catch variant tell against it or not? Where one of them is right, say what the other should concede; where they are talking past each other, say what the real question is. Say also what both of them are missing about what would have to be true of a physical device for it to have a determinate state at the limit point, and end with an overall verdict.
