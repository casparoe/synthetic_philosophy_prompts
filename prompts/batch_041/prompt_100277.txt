I'm on the editorial board for our department's writing-standards handbook, and we have to vote Thursday on whether to run a student's satirical essay in the new edition. The essay is a flip-the-default piece: it takes our own style guide and rewrites it so every "the author ... he" becomes "she," every species-sense "man" becomes "woman," and so on, and then it appends a deadpan editorial note saying this is obviously absurd and unbecoming of a serious publication — which is exactly the note our original guide implied about the gender-neutralizing edits we made two editions back. Half the board thinks it's the sharpest thing we've seen in years. The other half thinks it's a cheap gotcha that makes us look like we can't take our own subject seriously. And one member keeps insisting the joke only lands because it borrows the sting of sexism to make a point about sexism, which she says is either the whole trick or a cheat, depending on whether you think that borrowing is legitimate.

There's also a wrinkle I want you to actually look at rather than skate over. One section swaps the default in two example passages — one about nurses, one about engineers — and claims the swapped versions read differently smooth in each case: smoother for the nurses, clunkier for the engineers. The student says that shows gendered defaults are just base rates doing their job, not ideology. Our style editor says thats a non sequitur: of course different nouns sit differently in different occupations, and the essay is confusing "this sentence sounds odd" with "this default is doing harm." The student fires back that a default reading isn't the same thing as the literal meaning, and that this distinction is the whole ballgame; the style editor says that's a distinction without a difference when your the one being defaulted out of the sentence.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. I want a concrete recommendation — run it, cut it, or run it with an editorial headnote (and if a headnote, what it should say) — with the reasoning, and I want the strongest version of the case against running it answered rather than waved away. Also tell me honestly whether the nurse/engineer bit helps the essay or hurts it, since I might send it back to the student for a revision.
