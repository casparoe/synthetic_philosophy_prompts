A friend of mine has been reading the rationalist blogs and tried to explain acausal threats and surrogate goals to me over coffee. Here's her version, roughly:

"Acausal threats are like blackmail, except the blackmailer and the victim never interact. The threatener announces his policy, that's the only message involved, and then it's just up to the victim. The reason it works is that in these setups the two agents are running the same decision procedure, so each one can predict the other by predicting what it would do itself -- if you'd really carry out the threat, the other guy knows it, and he pays up. Nothing has to travel between them. Surrogate goals are the fix people came up with for the other side of this: instead of just having a normal goal like 'make paperclips,' an agent adopts a second goal, say 'destroy anyone who threatens me,' and because it genuinely wants that it simply can't be blackmailed -- threatening it stops being worth anyone's while. It's basically Mutually Assured Destruction, except for agents who never meet and couldn't even in principle send each other signals. Which shows that rational agents will converge on being bullies."

The last line sounds off to me, but I dont trust my own read of the rest. Could you go through it claim by claim and tell me what someone who actually works on this stuff would nod at, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong -- and then say whether her conclusion about bullies follows from whatever's left standing?
