So I got roped onto the committee for my town's 300th anniversary, and we have to sign off on the script for the historical exhibit. The draft runs: settlers show up, mills boom, big fire, rebuild, hospital and college, "and so our town became what it is today." My neighbor taught history at the community college for years and says the whole thing is Whig history — telling it as if everything was leading up to us — and we should scrap it. The chair says that's just what historians say when they don't want ordinary people to have a story, and anyway every history is told from somewhere, so why not from here. I can see both of them being right and Ive got no idea how to referee.

Could you write a short dialogue between the two of them where they actually go at it? I want them both sharp, not one of them being a punching bag for the other. And I'd like them to dig into the stuff I keep tripping over: whether judging people in the past by our standards is the same mistake as explaining the past by how it turned out, whether my neighbor's little 1931 Butterfield book is even about town exhibits or about something much narrower, and whether the chair's "it's told from somewhere" is a real answer or a dodge. Don't let them shake hands at the end just to be polite.
