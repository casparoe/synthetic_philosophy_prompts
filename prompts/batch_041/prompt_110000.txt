I teach a mixed graduate and advanced-undergraduate seminar on philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence, and I am drafting this year's take-home final. Most of the questions I have used in the past end up rewarding students for reciting the familiar map of objections to the Turing test — Searle's Chinese room, the Lady Lovelace objection, the problem of other minds — and I want one question that goes after something students almost never think about: the argument from extra-sensory perception, which is the last objection Turing canvasses in his 1950 paper in Mind, together with his surprisingly sympathetic treatment of it. What I want is an essay question that forces students to confront the methodological oddity of that episode rather than merely summarize it. The question should press them on whether Turing's handling of telepathy sits comfortably with the operationalist, performance-based stance he adopts earlier, when he replaces the question of whether machines can think with the question of whether a machine can do well in the imitation game. On one hand, he seems willing to treat a contested parapsychological hypothesis as a live empirical possibility, and he proposes shielding the interrogator from telepathic influence so that the game stays fair; on the other hand, his entire strategy is supposed to sidestep metaphysics by fixing on observable conversational performance. I want the question to make students ask whether that is a genuine tension or instead evidence that his position is better described as a thoroughgoing empiricism than as behaviorism, and to make them think about what the shielding proposal itself presupposes — for instance, whether it assumes telepathy is a physical process that can be blocked, and what becomes of the imitation game if it cannot be. The question should also require them to say whether the episode strengthens or weakens the claim that the game tests intelligence rather than the interrogator's credulity, and to consider the possibility that an unblockable telepathic channel would turn the test into an experiment contaminated by the interrogator's own expectations.

Please write the essay question, a model answer of about 850 words, and a marking scheme worth 30 points. The marking scheme should be built around five or six must-hit moves, with the points for each, and it should also list the specific errors that should cost a student points — for example, treating the extra-sensory perception discussion as a mere curiosity or an embarrassment to be passed over, confusing Turing's operationalism with logical positivism, or asserting that he dismissed the evidence for telepathy when in fact he took it seriously. The question should be answerable in a 90-minute exam with no texts in hand, so it must not depend on students recalling exact page numbers or turns of phrase, and it should not require them to name particular parapsychologists or experiments. Please write everything — question, model answer, and marking scheme — in American English. The model answer should be self-contained, should not need citations, and should not pretend to quote Turing verbatim.
