Alright, so I'm reworking the grad seminar on the ontological argument and for the Godel session I want to finish with a poem instead of yet another reconstruction -- they've read enough reconstructions. What I want is a villanelle where the form is doing the proof's work, not just decorating it. The two refrains should be the two halves of the modal move: one line that only commits to the thing being possible, one that commits to it being necessary, and they should finally come together in the closing couplet the way the S5 step lets 'possibly necessary' just collapse into 'necessary'. The quatrains in between can carry the actual machinery -- God defined as the being that has all the positive properties, the axiom that necessary existence is itself a positive property, the move from 'a being like that is possible' to 'it exists in every world'. Then I want the last tercet to let the objection in, the one that says if this all goes through then nothing is contingent anymore, every truth comes out necessary, which is supposed to be a reductio on the whole thing. Dont resolve it -- I want the poem to end with the proof and the objection both winning, which is honestly where I think the argument leaves you. Someone who's actually worked through the modal version should be able to point at each line and say which step it is; someone who hasn't should still get a good poem about something being argued into existence by its own grammar. Dont put Godel's name in it, I dont want it to be a portrait of the man, just let the argument talk. Voice-wise I'd say first person, the proof itself speaking, if that works. 19 lines, obviously.
