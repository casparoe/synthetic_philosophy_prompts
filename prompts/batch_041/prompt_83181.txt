I'm a lapsed pianist who reads a fair amount of popular science, and I've been circling a pattern that I'm fairly sure has a name but that I can't find one for — or rather, every candidate I turn up seems to cover only part of it. Here is what I keep noticing. When I play, my wrong notes are nearly inaudible to me: I finish a passage convinced it was clean, and then the recording makes the errors leap out, even though the only thing that changed is who produced the sound. When my old teacher sits down and plays the identical wrong note, I hear it at once, and she is no less predictable to me than my own hands are. So "I stop noticing what I expect" can't be the whole story, since she is expected too — but neither can "I stop noticing what I do," because the same blindness shows up where no finger is involved: I can proofread a page of my own prose three times and drift straight past a typo that I would catch in a sentence of yours on the first pass.

What I want from you is the carving, not just the label. Is there an established concept for the way a signal goes quiet to me in proportion to how much it is mine — or in proportion to how much it is expected — and am I looking at one phenomenon or at two that merely resemble each other? Please lay out the nearest candidates and separate them: what each one actually claims, which of them covers the piano case, which covers the typo case, and whether any single one covers both. I'm equally interested in the boundaries. Does the same quieting apply to my own thoughts and intentions, so that I'm a poor witness to my own motives? Is it the same thing that keeps me from tickling myself? And if the usual explanation is that the brain somehow subtracts the sensations its own movements are about to produce, I want to know how that story is supposed to survive the typo case, where there is no movement and nothing to subtract.

Please lead with your verdict — the term, or the plain statement that no single term fits — and then do the comparisons. Keep the whole reply under 400 words.
