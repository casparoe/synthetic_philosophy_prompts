Write a villanelle about a multipolar trap: the structure in which each participant's locally rational move — arming, overbidding, cutting the standard, racing to the bottom — is the best response from where that participant stands, so that a destination no one chose is reached anyway, and the participants are not fools but merely responsive. Let the two refrains carry the two halves of the structure: one spoken in the first-person plural, the move that is always worth making from the part's position, and one impersonal, the aggregate condition that keeps arriving no matter who is playing. The form should be load-bearing — a villanelle cannot help returning to its refrains, and that compulsion, rather than any speaker's intention, should read as the trap itself, with the closing quatrain letting the two refrains meet in a way that sounds like resolution while remaining a lock. Make the selection logic legible: the trap holds as long as the relevant unit is the participant and would dissolve if the whole were the unit that counted. Keep the actors concrete and plural rather than gesturing at an abstraction, so that someone who knows what a multipolar trap is finds it exactly rendered and someone who does not finishes the poem feeling the ratchet close.
