An artist's assistant produces a painting that is physically indistinguishable from a known Vermeer — the same support, pigments, and brushwork — and it enters a museum as a Vermeer; independently, a freak natural process involving no human agency assembles a physically identical object in a warehouse. Setting aside market price, the moral taint of the deception, and art-historical interest, give me your credence, as a number between 0 and 1, that those two objects have exactly the same aesthetic value as the original. Then decompose that number. How much of it rests on whether aesthetic properties supervene on the perceptible surface, on whether a correct theory of art makes causal history partly constitutive of the work, on whether a naturally produced duplicate is a work of art at all, and on whether 'aesthetic value' is determinate enough here to have a truth value? Give each sub-question a rough probability with one line of justification, show how they combine into the final number, name the term you are least confident in, and say what evidence or argument would move it most. Reply in continuous prose — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
