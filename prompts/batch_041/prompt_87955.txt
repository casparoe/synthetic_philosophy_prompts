Our instructor has asked us to imagine that Bentham could be revived and briefed on controversies that postdate him, and to reconstruct what he would actually say. I want to avoid the lazy answer that he would simply be permissive about everything, so I have been trying to make the question more concrete.

Here is the specific test case. A classmate has proposed a rule she calls the externalized-disgust hedonic rule. On this rule, a sexual act is permissible just in case it maximizes the sum of experienced sexual pleasure minus pain among the participants, with no discount for disgust, offense, or moral disapproval felt by third parties; consent is treated not as a side constraint but as a reliable proxy for expected utility. She claims this is what Bentham's utilitarianism really implies once you strip away Victorian hypocrisy. I suspect she is half right, but I cannot tell where the half ends.

Could you reconstruct the position Bentham would likely take on the following posthumous issues: (1) polyamorous marriage and enforceable relationship contracts; (2) the decriminalization of commercial sex work and the regulation of online platforms; (3) AI-generated sexual content, sex robots, and virtual companions; (4) affirmative-consent standards and the relevance of power imbalances; and (5) the criminalization of nonconsensual deepfake pornography and so-called revenge porn? For each, tell me what his actual commitments would push him toward, where they would leave him genuinely undecided, and where my classmate's rule diverges from what Bentham himself would likely endorse. I am especially interested in whether he would treat third-party disgust as a legitimate input into the hedonic calculus, and how he would handle cases where the participants' pleasure is real but the practice is widely thought degrading. Please reason it out rather than just listing what he wrote; I do not need citations or a reading list. Write your answer in American English.
