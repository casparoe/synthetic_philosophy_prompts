I am writing a chapter on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the suspicion that the states we group under the heading of pleasure — the warmth of a bath on a cold morning, the relief at a clean biopsy result, the satisfaction of finishing a proof, the pleasure a masochist takes in pain, whatever a dog gets from a thrown ball — share no interesting common feature, so that a theory identifying welfare with pleasure is either empty or false. Before I commit to that verdict, I want the standard of adequacy written out. Tell me what a theory of pleasure would have to deliver to count as adequate for a hedonist's purposes: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the facts about creatures and about our own psychology it must be consistent with, and the puzzles it must dissolve rather than rename.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, explain why it is a genuine requirement rather than a prejudice of one camp, and mark whether you take it to be non-negotiable or merely a preference. Include at least the following pressure points, and add any I have missed: sensory pleasures that seem located in a body part versus pleasures that seem to have no location at all; pleasures that are taken in something, and so have an object, versus pleasures that are merely felt; pleasures that arrive unwanted, and desires that are satisfied without any pleasure; pleasures available to a creature with no propositional attitudes; states that are simultaneously pleasant and unpleasant; pleasure taken in something false or bad; and the demand that pleasures be comparable in magnitude, since any hedonism worth having must be able to weigh a fish's pleasure against a person's, or one evening against a life.

Finally, and this is the part I care most about: flag any subset of the requirements you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement excludes the most candidates — sensory-quality accounts, attitudinal accounts, desire-based accounts, and any pluralist or pragmatist retreat that treats pleasure as a family-resemblance term. If the incompatibility you land on turns out to be the heterogeneity problem itself, say so plainly. A table is fine for the requirement-by-requirement list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; I will do the author-by-author scoring myself.
