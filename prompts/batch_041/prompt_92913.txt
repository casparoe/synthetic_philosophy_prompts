I am preparing a long-form interview for a philosophy podcast with an unnamed decision theorist who defends a distinctive version of coherent extrapolated volition. Her central claim is that coherent extrapolated volition is best understood as the fixed point of a reflective process: begin with an agent's actual preferences, correct them for logical inconsistency and empirical error, and iterate until the resulting set is stable under further reflection. She distinguishes this from the ideal-advisor model, according to which we ask what an idealized version of the agent would choose under full information and unlimited deliberation. She also holds that extrapolation is neither a prediction of what the agent would in fact choose nor a decision procedure the agent could execute in real time, but a criterion of what the agent has reason to value. I want the interview to press her on the mechanics and commitments of this position rather than allow her to restate it. Please draft ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. At least one question should examine how her fixed-point account differs from ideal-advisor accounts and whether the difference is substantive or merely terminological. At least one should ask whether coherent extrapolation can be specified without covertly importing substantive values under the guise of coherence, extrapolation, or idealization. At least one should address adaptive preferences and the worry that the procedure may ratify preferences formed under oppression, manipulation, or false consciousness. At least one should ask whether the account is a criterion of correctness or a decision procedure, and what it implies for agents who cannot complete the extrapolation. At least one should press the aggregation problem: how individual coherent extrapolated volitions are to be combined, and whether the account is committed to a particular social-choice procedure. At least one should ask what would count as evidence against the account, or what would show that the fixed point is not unique. The remaining questions should probe other weak points, such as the status of higher-order versus first-order preferences, the possibility of self-destructive or self-effacing volitions, the role of convergence, and the objection that the view is unknowable or action-guiding only in principle. Please avoid generic biography questions, questions that merely ask her to define terms she has already defined, and questions that require her to cite literature or recommend readings.
