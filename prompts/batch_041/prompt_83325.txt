Bit of an odd one this. Our little reading group has been going round in circles on the doomsday arguemnt for about six months and I can't tell any more whether we're disagreeing about anything or just taking turns restating our starting points. The setup is simple enough: I know roughly how many humans have been born before me, so if I treat myself as a random draw from all the humans who'll ever live, I should expect to be somewhere near the middle of the pack; that means the total number of humans probably isn't astronomically larger than the number so far, which means we're probably in for something nasty fairly soon. One camp says fine, that's just how indexical evidence works, it's a modest shift in credence rather than a proof, and the fact that it feels mad is a fact about us, not about the argument. A second camp says the whole thing evaporates as soon as you let the fact that there are more people in a bigger world make you more likely to be one of them — the two effects cancel exactly and you learn nothing. A third lot say it proves too much, since the same reasoning should convince you that your own country is about to be wiped out for reasons nobody's ever noticed.

My hunch, and tell me if it's rubbish, is that none of this is really about the numbers or about population ethics at all. It's the sleeping beauty problem in a hat. Both sides already agree on the arithmetic and disagree about what a self-locating belief is even supposed to be; the doomsday argument just makes the disagreement vivid because the stakes sound apocalyptic. If that's right then the doomsday literature is stuck for the same reason the sleeping beauty literature is stuck, and neither will move until somebody produces a principle about self-locating credence that's independently motivated and settles both.

My flatmate also has a fix he's very proud of: he reckons the reference class shouldn't be 'all humans who will ever live' but 'all humans who've heard the doomsday argument', on the grounds that you can only be a random draw from a class you're actually in, and the class of people who know the argument is a strange, self-referential, shrinking thing. I think that's either trivially question-begging or secretly the right answer and I dont know which.

So — what kind of disagreement is this really? Is my sleeping beauty hunch right, or is there a hidden factual or formal question doing the work? And given your diagnosis, what would count as actual progress: a theorem, a distinction, a change of question, or should we just accept the whole thing is ill-posed and go to the pub?
