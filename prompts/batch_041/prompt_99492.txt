A guy in a thread about hyperbolic discounting waved the whole literature away the other day — "thats just a dressed-up version of a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush." I've been chewing on it since and I genuinely can't tell whether he's right, wrong, or right about something adjacent.

So lay out the distinct precise claims that proverb could be making, and for each say whether it's a platitude, a real claim that's probably true, or flat-out false. A few I can already see: it's a claim about risk (the bush birds are a gamble, so read as a general rule the proverb is saying your odds of getting the two are under 50%, which is obviously false if there's three birds and your odds are good); it's a claim about time preference (future birds are worth less even when certain, which implies a huge short-run discount rate); it's a claim about me (the two birds aren't mine until I do the follow-up work, so I'm discounting by my own probability of flaking); it's a claim about mortality or about the bush getting raided, i.e. that the future opportunity decays; or it might just be advice about when to stop searching, with no valuation claim in it at all. I'm sure there's more.

Then tell me which of those, if any, is actually the same claim as hyperbolic discounting, and which would license his dismissal. My suspicion is that the distinctive thing in hyperbolic discounting is the reversal — Monday me prefers the bush, Tuesday me prefers the hand, and no set of valuations at a single time can capture that — but I want the menu in front of me before I commit. And say which reading he's probably running, and whether any of them makes the research redundant rather than just paraphrased.

Last thing, and this is the part I actually care about: if the "I'm discounting by my own odds of flaking" reading is the right one, is that still weakness of will, or does it turn akrasia into a self-fulfilling belief — I discount because I predict I'll flake, and I flake because I discount? I can't tell if that's a real distinction or two descriptions of one thing, and the akrasia tag has never settled it for me.
