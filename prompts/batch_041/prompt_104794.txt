I chair a reading group that has now spent a year circling the levelling-down objection without moving, and I want a diagnosis rather than another round. The prioritarian insists that a world where everyone is equally badly off is not, merely for being equal, better than one where some do better and no one does worse; the egalitarian answers that this simply assumes what is at issue, namely that only individual levels matter. My hypothesis is that the two sides are not really disputing the value of equality but answering different questions — one about what makes an outcome good, the other about how a distributively minded agent ought to choose — and that once the question is fixed most of the impasse turns out to be verbal. Test that hypothesis. If it holds, say which claims on each side survive and which turn out to be about nothing. If it does not, tell me what the real disagreement is: whether comparisons between persons are themselves a source of value, whether a reason for action must be person-affecting to count at all, or whether a prioritarian weighting is just a concern for equality wearing different mathematical clothes. Then say what, if anything, would move this — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or why nothing should.
