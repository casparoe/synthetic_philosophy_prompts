I'd like a consistency check on my position in the foundations of quantum mechanics before I commit it to paper. The view I hold is this: (1) the wavefunction is a complete description of the physical state, so there is no collapse and no hidden variables; (2) the dynamics is unitary and therefore deterministic; (3) every branch the wavefunction contains is equally real, and which branch I find myself in is a matter of indexical self-location rather than of any branch being singled out as the one that occurs; (4) the Born rule supplies objective probabilities — it is a mind-independent fact about a preparation that a given outcome carries a given weight, not merely a fact about what it is reasonable to expect; and (5) the most defensible justification of those Born weights is decision-theoretic, in the sense that they are the weights a rational agent maximizes expected utility with, so what they articulate is a norm governing rational betting rather than a further physical ingredient of the theory. Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which single revision would resolve the most. Rank the real tensions by how costly they are to repair, and for each say explicitly what the repair would force me to give up.
