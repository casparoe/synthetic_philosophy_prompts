Consider a variant of the Judy Benjamin problem in which the prior over the four quadrants is not uniform and the radio report is uncertain. The quadrants are Red-Headquarters, Red-Second Company, Blue-Headquarters, and Blue-Second Company. Before receiving any report, Judy's credences are P(Red-Headquarters) = 0.1, P(Red-Second Company) = 0.4, P(Blue-Headquarters) = 0.3, and P(Blue-Second Company) = 0.2. She then receives a message from a source who reports that, if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that she is in Headquarters rather than Second Company; in other words, the source asserts that P(Headquarters | Red) = 0.75. Judy knows that this source is reliable with probability 0.8. If the source is reliable, the asserted conditional probability is correct. If the source is unreliable, the asserted conditional probability is inverted, so the true P(Headquarters | Red) is 0.25. The source's reliability is independent of the quadrant.

You are to evaluate the following five updating procedures for this case. Rate each procedure on a 0–10 scale for epistemic adequacy, and then rank the five procedures from best to worst. For each procedure, provide a one- or two-sentence justification that explains what it does with the conditional report and the reliability discount, and identifies its main strength or weakness.

1. Reliability-weighted mixture. For each possible reliability state, compute the posterior by minimizing KL divergence from the prior subject to the corresponding precise conditional probability constraint (P(Headquarters | Red) = 0.75 if reliable, 0.25 if unreliable). Then take the weighted average of the two posteriors, with weights 0.8 and 0.2.

2. Expected-constraint update. Minimize KL divergence from the prior subject to the single constraint P(Headquarters | Red) = 0.65, which is the reliability-weighted average of the two possible true conditional probabilities.

3. Material-conditional update. Treat the source's assertion as the material conditional that Red implies Headquarters. Assign this material conditional probability 0.8, and conditionalize on it.

4. Conditional-rigidity update. Leave the prior conditional probability P(Headquarters | Red) unchanged. Update only the marginal probability of Red by multiplying it by the reliability-weighted likelihood of the report given Red versus not Red.

5. Full joint modeling. Treat the source's utterance as an event E. Assume that if the source is reliable, E occurs exactly when the true conditional probability of Headquarters given Red is 0.75; if the source is unreliable, E occurs exactly when that true conditional probability is 0.25. Place a prior over the possible true conditional probabilities and update on E.

Do not discuss the historical literature or cite sources. Focus on the epistemic merits of each procedure in this specific case.
