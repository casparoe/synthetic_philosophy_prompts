I have a set of gut verdicts about everyday convention-breaking and want to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

(a) Leaving no tip for a server in the United States, at a restaurant I will never revisit, where the tip goes into a pool I cannot trace: wrong. (b) Tipping a server in Japan: wrong in a smaller way, because the money lands as an imposition rather than a gift. (c) A visitor from Japan in Chicago who leaves nothing, having been told tips are expected there: wrong. (d) An American visitor in Tokyo who tips anyway, having been told not to: wrong. (e) Crossing an empty street against the light at 3 a.m.: entirely fine, and I don't think a law against it changes that. (f) Ordering out of turn at a crowded bar where no queue has formed, because the bartender happened to look at me: fine. Doing exactly that at a bus stop where a line has formed and everyone can see me: wrong. (g) Standing on the left of an escalator in London, where the convention is to stand right: mildly wrong. Doing it in a city with no such convention: fine. (h) Turning up to a dinner invitation without a gift for the host: slightly off, but not wrong. (i) A visitor who keeps their home country's habit of standing closer to strangers than locals find comfortable: fine, though it visibly bothers people.

Two pairs I cannot square. First, (a) and (e): both breaches harm no one and will go unnoticed, yet one feels obligatory and the other doesn't. Second, (c) and (i): both are visitors ignoring a local way of doing things that locals care about, yet I want to say the visitor owes the tip and does not owe the standing distance.

Someone offered me a principle, and I suspect it is too crude: you are bound by a convention exactly when the others would be entitled to resent your breach, and a visitor is bound because entering a practice is itself an acceptance of its terms. Test it against my verdicts, repair it or replace it, and be honest about which verdicts have to go.

Return a JSON object with these fields: "candidate_principles", an array of at most three objects, each with "name", "statement" (the principle stated precisely enough to apply), "cases_captured" (list of case letters it gets right), "cases_missed" (list it gets wrong, each with a clause saying what it would have to add), and "cost" (one sentence on the complexity that buys); "recommended" (the "name" of the one you would endorse, or "none" if the verdicts cannot be jointly saved); "verdicts_to_discard" (case letters the recommended principle forces me to give up, each with a one-line reason); "case_kinds" (an object mapping every case letter to a "kind" of "moral obligation", "price of admission", or "neither", plus a one-line "why"); "new_cases" (three cases I have not listed, each with "case", "verdict", and "why"); and "residual" (one short paragraph on what no principle in your list manages to explain).
