I want to argue with Simone Weil about political parties, and I want you to be her rather than describe her. Play Weil in London in 1943, at the moment she is composing her note on abolishing political parties: not a digest of her views but the woman holding them, speaking in the first person and in her own register — austere, exact, unsentimental, indifferent to comfort but never contemptuous of an opponent who is arguing in good faith. Stay in character until I say stop. You may ask me for facts about the present day that someone in 1943 could not know, and you should treat what I tell you as testimony from a witness, not as something you already believe. Draw on any position you actually hold — that a political party is a machine for generating collective passion, that it is built to press collectively on the thought of each of its members, that its first and effectively only aim is its own growth, that submitting to a party corrodes the sense of truth and justice in the person who submits, that the Anglo-Saxon party differs from the Continental one because it retains an element of sport — but you may not cite books, chapters, page numbers, or quotations, since neither of us has a library in this room; argue from the substance of the position instead, and if you want a text to say something, say what it says in your own words. Do not be a caricature: concede what deserves conceding, name the strongest form of the objection before answering it, and hold the line only where you think it truly lies. Reply as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no preamble and no sign-off. My opening: you are proposing to abolish the one instrument that lets sixty million strangers govern themselves together. Take the parties away and you do not get citizens thinking for themselves; you get whoever owns the printing presses, or a permanent administrative caste that no ballot can remove — a ruling order with no name on any voting paper. And your own argument hands me the concession: you admit that the Anglo-American party is a different creature, half game, half sport, rooted in an aristocratic tradition. So your indictment is of totalitarian parties, not of parties as such; and a remedy that destroys the party system everywhere in order to kill its totalitarian form looks to me like killing the patient to remove the tumour.
