ok so I need help drafting something. I sit on the deployment review board at a small alignment-adjacent lab (about 40 people, I do evals, not research), and I have to circulate a written opinion before Friday's meeting, so this is a real deadline and a real decision.

Background in case you don't know us: for the last year we've been building a decomposition-first training pipeline. A big model takes a task and splits it into sub-tasks. Each sub-task goes either to a smaller model or to one of our three contract reviewers, who see only that sub-task plus a short brief. Then a model stitches the pieces back into a final answer. The pitch is the usual one, that the human only needs to be competent at the small pieces, so oversight scales past the point where anyone can check whole outputs.

Last month we distilled the whole pipeline into one model, and that distilled model is now the thing we'd actually ship.

Two eval results came out of it. First, the distilled model matches the pipeline's answers on about 96% of our held-out set, but it doesn't match the pipeline's uncertainty at all well: on the roughly 4% where the pipeline hedged, refused, or flagged a sub-task as uncheckable, the distilled model mostly just answers. Second, and this is the one that's kept me up, the decomposer decides which sub-tasks get routed to a human in the first place, and the decomposer is the same model family as the thing being distilled. So what we've been calling human oversight is really oversight over the subset of sub-tasks that a model chose to hand over. I've been calling this oversight routing and I have no idea if anyone else has a name for it. There's also a seperate worry that I keep half-forming and can't quite state: for sub-tasks that aren't independently checkable, distillation doesn't obviously make things worse so much as convert a pipeline that was merely unreliable into a single model that is confidently wrong, and I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just vibes.

The board is split down the middle. Half say this is exactly what amplification was supposed to buy us and we should ship with a monitoring condition. Half say the distillation step is where the safety property actually dies, so we shouldn't ship the distilled model at all, and should eat the cost of serving the pipeline. My own read is somewhere in the middle and I have to commit to something in writing.

Draft me the written opinion: a concrete recommendation (ship, don't ship, or ship with conditions, and if conditions, ones we can actually check by Friday rather than "monitor it"), the reasoning for it, and an honest account of the strongest case for whichever side I come out against. Also tell me what would change your mind here, like what eval result or argument would move you off the recommendation, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong. I'd rather find the hole now than in the meeting.
