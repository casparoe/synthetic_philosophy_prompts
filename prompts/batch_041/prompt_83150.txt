My cognitive science professor gave us this summary of the poverty of the stimulus argument last week and I wrote it down as faithfully as I could. He said: Chomsky's poverty of the stimulus argument shows that kids can't possibly learn language just from what they hear. The data they get is a mess — slips of the tongue, false starts, half-sentences — and nobody ever corrects a child's grammar; parents respond to whether what the kid said is true, not whether it's well-formed. The killer example is question formation. To turn 'The woman who is singing is happy' into a yes/no question, you move the auxiliary of the main clause to the front, giving 'Is the woman who is singing happy?' But a kid who was just generalizing from the input would go for the simpler linear rule and front the first 'is' instead, giving 'Is the woman who is singing is happy?' No child ever says that. Since the kid never hears anything that would rule out the linear rule and never gets told the right one, the only explanation is that the structure-dependent rule is built in. That's what universal grammar is — the set of grammatical rules babies are born with, which is why a child can learn any language it's raised among and why adults who move to a new country never quite get the grammar right. So the argument proves that language can't be acquired by any general-purpose learning mechanism; it has to be a special innate language organ.

I'm pretty sure some of this is off. I've heard that people have pushed back on the negative evidence thing, and 'universal grammar' doesn't sound like it's supposed to be a list of rules. But I don't know enough linguistics to seperate what's a fair simplification from what's flat wrong, and honestly the nativist story does seem to fit how effortlessly kids pick language up, so I'm inclined to trust it. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me what a careful nativist would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's actually a misrepresentation? And at the end, does the conclusion he draws — that general-purpose learning is definately ruled out — actually follow from the parts that survive?
