My old roommate Nadia is the most self-consistent person I know about everything except one topic, and it's driving me nuts. She's not a scold — she doesn't recycle, she thinks most ethical consumerism is theater, she'll eat anything that's about to be thrown out — but she has this very specific pattern of verdicts about whether one person's tiny contribution matters, and I can't reverse-engineer the rule she's running. Here's every data point I've collected from arguments with her over the years. She skipped the last general election and says that's fine: her district has about 70,000 voters and the seat was safe by 20,000, so "the arithmetic doesn't lie." But she made a point of voting in our ward's school-board election, which was decided by four votes last time, and says skipping that one would've been wrong. She's been vegetarian for six years and says buying a supermarket chicken is wrong — when I said her dollar can't possibly change how many chickens the store orders, she said the chicken "only exists because someone like me pays for it," which I don't fully get. She flies to see her parents in Portugal every summer, knows that flight is far and away the worst thing she does to the climate, and says it's fine, because "the plane's going either way and the seat gets sold either way." She won't touch one particular fast-fashion brand and says buying from them is wrong even though she's certain one more shirt changes nothing at the factory. She has never once rinsed a jar or sorted her recycling, on the grounds that one bin is a rounding error. And last month at a barbecue she happily ate a burger, pointing out that the meat had already been bought and whatever she didn't eat was going in the bin anyway.

I can't tell whether she's running a rule about making a difference (wrong when your act is the thing that tips something over the line), a rule about being the one who pays for a wrong (wrong when you're the one commissioning it, whether or not it changes the total), or some combination of the two, and I suspect her actual verdicts fit more than one of those. So I want three things. First, what are the best candidate rules here, and which of her verdicts (if any) rule which candidates out? Second, she's currently chewing on two new cases and I want her predicted verdict on each, with your reasoning. She's been invited on a field trip that needs a small charter flight, which will only run if eight people sign up, and she'd be the eighth — she keeps asking me whether that's the same as her summer flight or not. And separately she's thinking about buying a whole lamb for a party from a farm she likes, where she'd pick out one specific animal that the farm is going to slaughter that week anyway for its normal orders, so the total number killed doesn't change; she says that one's obviously fine, "because it's the same number of animals." Third, which of those two is the better test of which rule she's running, and what should I ask her next to settle it? And if it turns out her verdicts just aren't consistent, tell me which pair is doing the damage.
