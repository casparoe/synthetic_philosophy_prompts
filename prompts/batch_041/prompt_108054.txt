I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for well over a decade, and the habit that has stuck with me most is the insistence that a belief be written down as a number — a credence, ideally with a likelihood ratio attached — and then made to pay rent in predictions. I also have a lay interest in Jain philosophy, particularly the doctrine of anekāntavāda, the claim that reality is many-sided and that every assertion is true only from a standpoint. I would like you to translate that doctrine, together with its technical machinery — nayavāda, the analysis of standpoints; syādvāda, conditioned predication, in which every claim is uttered under a syāt qualifier marking it as holding from a standpoint rather than without qualification; and the sevenfold scheme of saptabhaṅgī — into the vocabulary I actually use: priors, posteriors, credences over propositions, model uncertainty, mixtures of experts, calibration, log-odds, and the map/territory distinction.

As I understand the seven modes, a thing is asserted to be, from a standpoint; not to be, from a standpoint; both, from a standpoint; inexpressible, from a standpoint; and then the three combinations of being and not-being with inexpressibility. I may have the order slightly wrong, but the structure is what matters. For each mode, say what it becomes in my vocabulary. Does syāt function like conditioning on a standpoint, so that each naya is a model in an ensemble and the many-sidedness is just the spread of the ensemble? Does the third mode, both is and is not, amount to a paraconsistent assignment, or can a Bayesian represent it as two credences under different partitions without contradiction? And what is the fourth mode, the inexpressible one, supposed to be: a claim that no probability can be assigned, a claim that the proposition is not well-formed, or something the framework simply cannot say?

Then the part I care most about: the residue report. Which parts of anekāntavāda survive translation cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in the rationalist toolkit? I am especially interested in three candidates for residue. First, the Jain claim seems to be that the many-sidedness is a feature of reality itself, not merely of our uncertainty; my vocabulary tends to treat disagreement and partiality as epistemic, and I want to know whether it can even express the metaphysical version. Second, the doctrine is embedded in an ethical and soteriological project — non-harm, the limiting ideal of omniscient knowledge — and I suspect my vocabulary will strip that out; is the stripped version still the same doctrine? Third, the inexpressible mode looks like a place where the probabilistic framework may not merely lose information but actively misdescribe the target.

Tell me, finally, whether the residue is a defect of my vocabulary — something it should be able to express but cannot — or a sign that the Jain claims are doing work that looks clearer in their own idiom than it would if restated as claims about credences. I would rather have an honest report of what does not survive than a flattering one. I know the rationalist material well and the Jain material only as an interested amateur, so please keep the translation faithful to the Jain side rather than smoothing it into something I already believe.
