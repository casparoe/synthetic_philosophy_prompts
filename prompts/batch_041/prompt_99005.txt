Ok so a friend in my game theory reading group gave me this argument and I'm pretty sure it proves way too much but I can't find the bad step. Here it is. (1) A strategy is irrational exactly when it's never a best response to any belief about what the other player will do. (2) So if strategy s is never a best response, then for every belief there's some strategy of mine that beats s against that belief. (3) In particular take any pure strategy j of the opponent — against the belief "opponent plays j for sure", something beats s. (4) And against a fixed pure belief, if anything beats s then some *pure* strategy of mine beats s (just take the max over my pure strategies). So for each opponent pure strategy j there's a pure strategy i_j of mine that beats s against j. (5) So there's a pure strategy of mine that beats s against every opponent pure strategy, i.e. s is strictly dominated by a pure strategy. (6) Which means mixed strategies never do any work as dominators, and iterated elimination can always get by with pure ones. But (6) has to be false. My example: I have pure strategies A, B, C and the opponent has L and R. Against L, A gives 3, B gives 0, C gives 1. Against R, A gives 0, B gives 3, C gives 1. C is never a best response — the better of A and B gives at least 1.5 whatever the opponent does — and it's strictly dominated by the 50-50 mix of A and B, which pays 1.5 in both columns. But no pure strategy of mine beats C against both L and R. So (6) is wrong and one of these steps is broken. Which one, and what does fixing it actually cost me? I'd rather not give up (1) or (2), and I don't want the fix to just be "ban mixtures from the analysis". I especially want to know what happens to the *iteration* once I admit mixed dominators: if the mixture that kills C is built out of A and B, and some later step kills B, was killing C ever legit? Does the order of elimination matter, and what is the surviving set even supposed to represent? Lead with the one step you reject, then justify it, and keep the whole thing under 350 words.
