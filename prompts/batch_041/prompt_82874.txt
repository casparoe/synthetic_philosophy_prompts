I retired about three years ago after 35 years in geology — mostly paleo work early on, then basin stuff for the oil side — and since then I've been trying to teach myself philosophy. The Tuesday book group is doing one of those "is philosophy useless" books and I'd rather show up with an essay than an opinion, so I'm asking you to write the essay and I'll steal from it.

Here's the ask. Take the question of whether philosophy makes progress seriously and defend an actual verdict. No "it depends what you mean by progress" shrug — you can spend some time on that question, but then commit.

Two things I want worked in, because they come out of my own field:

First, my field definitely made progress, but not the way people usually mean. We never settled what a species is. That fight is older than I am and I'd bet it outlives me. What changed is that we got better instruments and better bookkeeping, turned the unanswerable version of the question into a pile of answerable ones, and then more or less quietly stopped caring about the original. Is that progress, or is it changing the subject and calling it progress? And sometimes we did settle something big — the drift business, obviously — and it's worth asking what was different about that case, whether it was evidence or generational turnover or just that the holdouts died off.

Second, I want you to try a specific three-part test on philosophy and tell me how it does. (a) Elimination: fewer live options, with bad ones dying and staying dead instead of getting resurrected every generation under a new name. (b) Toolkit: new formal machinery, new distinctions, new ways of making an argument checkable by someone who disagrees with you. (c) Settlement: big questions actually closed. My hunch is philosophy is strong on (b), weak on (c), and I honestly can't tell on (a) — the recycling of old positions is what makes me suspicous. Argue me out of that hunch or further into it, but don't just list examples at me.

Then handle the obvious objection, because I keep running into it: maybe I'm holding philosophy to a science-shaped standard and the standard is the mistake. So say what progress would even look like in a field that isn't in the business of predicting things, and whether philosophy clears that bar instead. If your verdict ends up being "philosophy progresses, just not in the way philosophers hope," fine — but then be specific about what the progress is and how we'd know it when we see it. To keep yourself honest, pick at least one debate that looks genuinely dead or genuinely settled and one that looks like pure recycling under new vocabulary, and say what distinguishes them.

Don't bother with citations or a reading list, I can't check them and I don't care who said what. Just the argument. Four to six paragraphs, written for someone who's read a fair bit but was never trained in it.
