I'm in a philosophy of physics reading group that has spent the term circling the thermodynamic arrow, and a friend in the group has built an argument that I can neither accept nor properly dismantle; I want a diagnosis, not reassurance. The background we have been working with is the now-familiar thought that the second law is not a law in the strict sense but a statistical regularity underwritten by a low-entropy boundary condition at one end of time, and that the directionality of thermodynamic processes is therefore not written into the dynamics but imported from that condition. My friend has pushed this line to a conclusion that genuinely disturbs him, and two other members keep muttering that it is just the old reversibility objection dressed up, though neither of them can say where the trouble lies. I should say up front that I am attached to two of his starting points — the time-reversal invariance of the dynamics, and the conviction that the arrow of time is discovered rather than stipulated — and I would rather not surrender either unless I have to, but I want to know honestly what each surrender costs.

His argument runs as follows. (1) The dynamical laws governing the constituents of ordinary matter are invariant under the replacement of t by minus t. (2) The apparent one-directionality of thermodynamic processes is not built into those laws; it emerges only once a low-entropy boundary condition is imposed, and given such a condition entropy grows with overwhelming probability toward both temporal directions. (3) That boundary condition — the sort of posit often called a Past Hypothesis — is not derivable from the laws and is therefore a contingent fact about the actual world. (4) Every scrap of evidence we have for it is a present-day record: memories, written and photographic traces, fossils, strata, cosmological data, and the correlations among current microstates generally. (5) Each such record is itself an ordered, low-entropy structure, and its evidential value seems to depend on the very thermodynamic regularity it is supposed to underwrite. (6) Because the dynamics are time-reversal invariant, for every model of the present data in which the records were produced by a low-entropy past there is a mirror model, equally compatible with the laws and equally fitted to the same present microstate, in which the low entropy lies in the future. (7) So nothing in the data favours a low-entropy past over a low-entropy future; the choice of temporal orientation is something we bring, not something nature imposes. (8) And if we take the statistical reasoning seriously without smuggling in an orientation, the most economical explanation of our rich, highly ordered records is not a vast low-entropy past but a local fluctuation — a brief ordered pocket containing observers with apparent memories — in an otherwise high-entropy universe. (9) But we are plainly not entitled to conclude that we are such a fluctuation, and we are plainly not free to decide that entropy was higher in the past and will be lower in the future. (10) Therefore one of the steps is wrong.

Tell me which step to reject, and be specific. For the step you name, explain why it looked fine to my friend and what, exactly, is doing the illicit work — whether it is an equivocation between the temporal orientation of the reasoner and some imagined orientation-neutral standpoint, a scope or symmetry slide, a step valid only under a reading the earlier premises do not support, or a genuine but under-appreciated assumption. Then price the alternatives: if the argument is in fact valid, say which plausible premise is least costly to abandon and what each of the obvious moves — denying that the relevant laws are time-reversal invariant, insisting the boundary condition is a posit adopted for explanatory unification rather than inferred from records, denying that the mirrored inference is an inference available to anyone, or conceding that the data really are orientation-neutral — leaves us with. I would like to keep (1) and (9), so tell me squarely whether that combination is coherent or whether keeping both is exactly the mistake. Finally, give me the one-paragraph reply I should have ready for the member who insists it is all just the reversibility objection, and flag the weakest point in whatever diagnosis you hand me.
