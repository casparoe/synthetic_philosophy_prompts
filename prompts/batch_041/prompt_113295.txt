Ok so my reading group has been doing imprecise probabilities and a friend dropped this arguemnt on me and now I can't stop picking at it. I'm pretty sure it proves too much, but I can't find the leak and its driving me up the wall.

(1) A rational agent's preferences are complete: for any two options she either prefers one or is indifferent between them.
(2) If her preferences are complete and satisfy the usual ordering/continuity/independence-ish axioms, they're representable by a utility function, and her choices among uncertain options come out as expected-utility maximisation against some probability function over the states.
(3) If there were genuinely two or more probability functions that equally well fit her choices, then her preferences over acts would have to be incomplete -- there'd be pairs of acts where she neither prefers one nor is indifferent.
(4) So an agent who's representable at all has just one probability function. Precise credences.
(5) The imprecise probabilist says that when the evidence is ambiguous, a rational agent's state is a set of probability functions, not one.
(6) From (1)-(4), any agent whose choices are representable at all has a single probability function.
(7) So imprecise probabilities either describe no rational agent, or describe agents with incomplete preferences.
(8) And incomplete preferences are a genuine defect and not just an aesthetic one: an agent who neither prefers A to B nor B to A nor is indifferent can be walked around a loop, paying a little each time she's asked to trade A for B and then B for A.
(9) So imprecise probabilities are either empty or a theory of defective agents.

His conclusion is that imprecise probability is a non-starter. I don't accept that, but I also can't put my finger on what's wrong -- my gut says the trouble is somewhere in the middle of the chain and he's sure it's at the end, and I don't trust either of us.

Which step do I give up, and what's the bill? I'd like to keep (5), since I think imprecise credences are a real phenomenon and not just sloppy thinking, but I want to know what that commits me to. If you can, price the moves for each of the live camps: the imprecise-probability defender, the completeness-and-precision Bayesian, and whoever thinks the real story is vagueness rather than gaps. And tell me whether (8) is doing any independent work or whether the whole thing collapses without it.
