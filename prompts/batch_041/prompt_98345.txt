I’ve been reading rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums for years, and a friend who writes for one summarized the inefficacy objection to me as follows:

“The inefficacy objection shows that individual contributions to collective harms are morally irrelevant. Since my emissions, my meat consumption, or my single vote never changes the outcome, the expected harm of any one of these acts is zero. So the only rational response is to work for systemic change; personal guilt about consumption is a category error. Utilitarians, who care only about outcomes, are therefore committed to saying that personal vegetarianism or recycling is pointless. Deontologists, who care about universalizability, can at least say that it would matter if everyone did it. The real lesson is that virtue ethics is the only framework that can make sense of green lifestyle choices.”

I suspect this is unfair, but I can’t articulate precisely where. Please audit it claim by claim: what would someone working on individual contributions to collective harms accept, what is a standard simplification, and what is simply wrong? Does the inefficacy objection establish moral irrelevance, or does that conclusion depend on a contestable causal model of individual difference-making? Does the alleged utilitarian commitment to the pointlessness of personal vegetarianism or recycling follow, or do rule-consequentialist and expected-marginal-impact replies block it? Does the contrast with deontology and virtue ethics survive correction? Finally, say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
