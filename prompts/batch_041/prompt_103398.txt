Write me a short story about a man who doesn't do something. I know that sounds thin, so here's the constraint that makes it worth doing: build it so a careful reader can't settle whether he caused what happens. Concretely, set up the events so that (a) if he'd acted, the bad thing wouldn't have happened, so the plain but-for test points right at him, and (b) the thing he fails to do is stop a process that was itself going to stop the harm — so his omission only matters because it removes a preventer, and there's no moment where he does anything at all. Make everything else in the chain normal: everyone else acts the way you'd expect, the machinery and the schedule and the weather all behave, and the only strange thing in the whole story is his stillness. Keep it ordinary — a night shift, a factory floor, a neighborhood, whatever fits — and don't let a narrator tell me he caused it or that he didn't; just lay out the sequence and let me see the two ways you could tell its causal history. Have one character say the but-for test settles it and another say that failing to stop a stopper isn't causing anything, and make both of them sort of right. End it right at the point where I have to decide, and dont tidy it up.
