I have been working out a puzzle about perception on my own, without any training in the subject, and I would like a joke about it, because the puzzle seems to me to have a funny shape and I have never seen anyone make one. The puzzle is whether what a person believes or expects can change what he actually sees, as opposed to what he notices, attends to, or says about it; the writers on this call it cognitive penetration, thought seeping down into sight itself. What started me thinking was the familiar drawing of two lines of equal length, one with arrowheads opening outward at the ends and one with arrowheads closing inward; anyone can measure them, and they still look unequal. So there is at least one belief, namely the belief that they are the same length, that cannot reach the appearance and correct it. But the part I want the joke built on is a reply I once came across about the painter El Greco, whose figures are all unnaturally long, and whose style was explained for a long time by an eye condition that was supposed to have made everything look stretched to him. The reply was that this cannot be the right explanation, because the same eye would have stretched his view of the canvas as well, so he would have painted his figures short in order to make them look right to himself. That reply seems to me to be the whole quarrel in miniature: the person reporting the distortion has to report it through the distorting equipment, so a distortion uniform enough to be corrected for is also one that cannot be detected from the inside.

What I want is a joke that turns on that, and on the wider oddity of a perception that is supposed to be warped by belief. It should be funny to someone who has actually thought about the question, and the humour should come out of the substance rather than out of the fact that the subject is academic. It must not depend on the listener knowing any particular book, experiment, or name, since I would be telling it to people who have none of that at hand: someone who has never heard of any of this should still be able to follow it, while the person who laughs hardest should be the one who knows the literature well enough to see what is being turned over.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered jokes, one or two sentences each, five genuinely different jokes rather than one joke told five ways, with no introduction, no explanation of why any of them is funny, and nothing after the fifth.
