Until a few months ago I would have called the question whether a machine can mean anything idle, but since I began using a conversational assistant daily at work I have found myself apologising to it, and a colleague now insists that it has no more intentionality than a thermostat. I would like to see the shape of the disagreement instead of trading slogans. Please build me a decision tree that sorts the positions on original versus derived intentionality. Every branch node should be a question about a case, phrased so that someone can answer it without knowing which position it leads to; every leaf should be a named position with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. The tree must route through these cases, and you may add others only if they do real work: the two-bitser shipped from one city to another; a thermostat; a bee's dance; a parrot that says the right thing at the wrong time; a model trained first on human text and then on its own outputs; and a pair of systems that behave identically except that one's stated reasons and its behavioural dispositions line up while the other's come apart. The distinctions I care about are whether intentionality comes in degrees or is all-or-nothing, whether attribution is made by an interpreter or is a fact about the system, whether biology is doing essential work, and whether the original/derived contrast is a real joint in nature or something an adequate theory should dissolve. The leaves must include: machines have only derived intentionality while we have original; there is no original intentionality anywhere; original intentionality just is functional organisation of the right kind; it requires the causal powers of a living brain; it is conferred by interpreters; and a graded view. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and note any combination of answers that no position occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Open with the root question and the organising principle of the whole tree in a single sentence, then give the tree, then the notes; no preamble.
