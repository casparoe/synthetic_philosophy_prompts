Ok so I'm a PhD student working on cross-modal perception and I keep hitting the same wall in my reading group. Molyneux's question -- a man born blind, taught by touch to tell a cube from a sphere, then made to see; could he tell which was which by sight alone, before touching? -- has been argued over for three hundred years, and the empiricist side and the nativist side are more or less where they started, which is odd given that we now actually have the surgical cases people used to just speculate about. I want a diagnosis of the standoff, not a verdict on who's right.

My hypothesis (just a hypothesis) is that there are two or three different questions wearing one name -- can the subject deploy the concept/word correctly, is there cross-modal transfer of shape information, is visual space itself innate -- and the two camps slide between them, so that each can always point at the question its own answer fits. But I dont think that's the whole story. I also suspect a real disagreement is hiding in what 'by sight alone' is meant to exclude (touching, reaching, remembered touch, or some general spatial capacity that both modalities plug into), and that the empirical cases can't settle it anyway because 'the first moment of sight' is a fiction -- acuity comes back over hours or days, plenty of patients were never fully blind to light, they move their heads around, etc., so both sides can read the same messy data as confirmation.

So is this a verbal dispute about 'sight alone' and 'immediately', a genuine disagreement about innate versus acquired spatial content, or people answering different questions under one heading? Where exactly do the sides actually part, and given your diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress -- a cleaner operationalization, a distinction, a kind of case -- or should we just expect it to sit there? Say so if part of it is ill-posed.
