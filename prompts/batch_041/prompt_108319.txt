I just recieved my US citizenship last month — grew up in Denmark, been in Minnesota for 15 years — and my family is throwing a dinner party for me on Saturday. I have to give a short speech, maybe 4-5 minutes, and I'm definately not going to just do the usual thank-you-America thing. The whole process has made me weirdly obsessed with the trust question: my Danish relatives think Americans are paranoid about government, my American friends think Danes are naive, and I keep going back and forth on which attitude is actually more reasonable.

My uncle, who teaches political science, has this idea he calls the loan/audit model. Trust in government is like a loan you extend to officials; suspicion is like an audit you run on them. A loan with no audit is just a gift, and an audit with no loan is a blockade. He says a reasonable citizen is doing both, just on different schedules. I want the speech to actually test that idea against the Scandinavian and American cases, not just name-drop it. I also want it to distinguish trusting a person from trusting an office — and social trust from political trust. Because I trust my Danish caseworker but not the Danish state, and I trust my American neighbor but not the DMV. That distinction seems like the real crux. And I want it to actually say what trust is: a prediction about what officials will do, or a commitment to treat them as accountable? Those are different things, and I suspect the two countries might be answering different questions.

The audience is half Danish, half American, plus my uncle who will absolutely roll his eyes if I say something glib. So it has to be smart enough for him without losing everyone else, and it shouldn't make either side feel like I'm calling them idiots. I want it to land somewhere, though — to actually argue which attitude is more defensible and why, not just say both cultures have strengths.

Can you write the speech? Please give it to me with these headers so I can see the beats:
- Cold open (about 80-100 words)
- The Danish case (about 150 words)
- The American case (about 150 words)
- Where I land (about 200 words)
- The toast (about 60-80 words)

No citations, no reading list — this is a dinner, not a seminar. And don't put any quotes in my mouth; paraphrase if you need to.
