ok here's a thing i keep tripping over and i want you to sort out what it commits us to. the rules that govern what you're allowed to do to animals in research or in food production — the ones that make you get a committee to sign off, use anaesthetic, provide enrichment, that whole apparatus — almost everywhere draw the line at vertebrates. mouse: paperwork, protocols, a named vet. honeybee: nothing, do what you like. and then a few places have patched the list, e.g. the UK's sentience act from a couple years back covers all vertebrates plus octopuses and lobsters. so now a lobster is in and a bee is out, and i genuinely can't tell whether that's a principled update or just whoever had the better campaign. the same system that demands environmental enrichment for a lab rat will still let you drop a lobster in boiling water in a restaurant kitchen.

what i want is the implicit theory of moral status this whole arrangement presupposes. is the line supposed to be a fact about where sentience begins — a threshold, in or out — or is it a confession that we can't tell and we're being conservative about the animals we can recognise? those are different theories and i suspect the law is running one while the science it cites is running the other, since the people who actually study this talk about sentience as graded: nociception and crude valenced states low down, richer capacities higher up, no obvious joint in nature to cut at. so: does a practice that draws a bright line presuppose sentience is binary? or can a graded view plus a cutoff license the same practice? and if so, is the cutoff doing epistemic work (below here we can't tell) or moral work (below here nothing counts)? distinguish what the practice strictly needs from what it just fits nicely with.

then the part i care about most. suppose we took the graded picture seriously and tried to write a rule. someone proposes scaling the required protection by your credence that the creature is sentient times how bad the suffering would be — a shrimp gets a sliver, a pig gets a lot, a thermostat gets zero. is that coherent or does it smuggle the threshold back in through the credence? if it is coherent, what concretely has to change: do we bin the species list and go by capacity, and what happens when the capacity claims are contested, given that the contested cases are the ones where the stakes are tiny per animal and enormous in total? and is there anything left of "it's a vertebrate" as a legitimate proxy, or is that just fossilised taxonomy doing moral work? reconstruct the theory, tell me which bits are load-bearing and which are historical residue, and say where the practice is underdetermined enough that several rival views would all license it.
