For a novel I'm writing, I need a document rather than a scene: the joint statement two of my characters could sign after years of arguing past each other about the bootstrapping problem. One is a dogmatist — a perceptual experience justifies a belief about the world on its own, with no antecedent assurance that the faculty behind it is reliable — and the other an epistemic conservative, who holds that a faculty's deliverances can never amount to knowledge until one has independent reason to trust that faculty, so any bootstrap in which a faculty certifies itself is illegitimate and makes knowledge far too easy. Draft what they could jointly put their names to: the claims both accept about what the bootstrapping argument actually shows and about where the real justificatory work gets done; the residual disagreements, each written in wording its own side would endorse; the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them; and, for each crux, a concrete case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I'd also like you to say whether any part of their quarrel is merely verbal. For one test case, imagine a device — call it the Nomograph — that measures some quantity and, beside every reading, prints its own verdict that it is in good order; a user reads it many times, finds the verdicts consistently favorable, and concludes that it is reliable. Format the result as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
