I'm a science-and-ideas journalist and I've fallen down a Berkeley rabbit hole for a piece on what idealist metaphysics does (or doesn't do) to economics, and I've come out with a package of commitments I can't tell hangs together. So, audit me. (1) Reality is mind-dependent in something like Berkeley's sense — no mind-independent material substance sitting behind our ideas, though ordinary objects like coins and tables are perfectly real. (2) Money has no intrinsic value; gold, silver and paper are at bottom tickets or counters for recording and transferring claims on real wealth. (3) Real wealth is labour and industry — the elements plus human work, not bullion. (4) A poor country like eighteenth-century Ireland is poor largely because its coin has been drained away and its credit starved, so the fix is more circulating credit, and in the earlier editions he was pushing a national bank. (5) The real end men aim at is power, and a well-governed state should aim to give each member power in proportion to his industry. (6) All the same, the legislature should meddle in fashion and manufacture fresh wants among the poor, since the creating of wants is the likeliest way to produce industry — his line being that peasants who ate beef and wore shoes would work harder. (7) And he treats the Irish poor's cynical content with dirt and beggary as itself a cause of their poverty, a moral failing rather than a purely structural one. I don't know which of these is the weak link, so please don't just tell me they're all in tension and leave it there.

What I want is a proper audit: which of these pairs are genuinely pulling against each other, which only look like they are, and how severe each real tension is — rank them, don't flatten them into a list of equivalently bad problems. The one I keep circling is whether (2) can actually do the work (4) needs it to do: if money is only a ticket, it's hard to see how a shortage of tickets could be the cause of real poverty rather than a symptom of something else, and yet he clearly thinks the drain of coin is doing real damage. But I suspect there's at least one more real clash lurking in (5) versus (6) and (7), and I'd like to know if I'm right about that or just pattern-matching. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what it would cost — the cheapest revision that restores consistency, and what gets lost by taking it. And flag the one that looks damning but dissolves once you see what he's actually claiming, because I think there is one and I want to know if I've got it right. Also, could you write it up in American English? My editor's American and she'll only complain about my spelling otherwise.
