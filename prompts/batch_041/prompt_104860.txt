You are a philosopher specializing in the ethics of public memory and commemoration. I sit on our city's memorial review commission and want your help predicting a decision. Please think carefully before answering: begin by listing the candidate principles that could be driving our past rulings, then reason about the new case, and end by naming the case that would best separate those principles. Do not cite scholarship or real-world controversies; work only from the record I give you.

Our rulings to date:

1. We voted to remove an equestrian statue of a frontier militia captain, erected in 1907. His public reputation rested almost entirely on a punitive campaign against a neighboring Native settlement; the dedication address praised the taming of the frontier; and the region's tribal community petitioned for removal.
2. We voted to keep, with an added interpretive plaque, a statue of a merchant who endowed the city's library and hospital. It was erected in 1884 by subscribers grateful for those endowments. The merchant had also been a notorious duelist and a domestic tyrant. We held that his public standing rests on the endowments, not on his private conduct.
3. We voted to rename a street honoring a Reconstruction-era governor who had organized a paramilitary that suppressed Black voting. The street had been named in 1966 by a council that simultaneously resolved against a desegregation order, and Black civic organizations asked for the change.
4. We voted to keep a statue of a novelist, erected in 1912 by a literary society to honor her fiction, notwithstanding essays in which she expressed contempt for the laboring classes.

The new case: a statue of a celebrated engineer, honored for designing the city's bridges and water system, erected in 1935 by a civic association whose dedication speeches praised the founders' stock and the exclusion of newer arrivals. Descendants of those excluded groups now make up a large part of the city, and some of their organizations have asked us to remove the statue.

Predict our verdict on the new case. Identify at least two candidate principles that fit our record equally well (and any others you see), state what each predicts here, give your best guess with your reasons, and say which further case would most cleanly tell the principles apart. If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome, tell me what principle we would be committing to with each possible choice.
