I'm a PhD student (animal cognition, so this is adjacent to my thesis but not exactly my area) and my department has roped me into hosting our podcast. Next week's guest is a philosopher who argues that insect farming is one of the biggest moral problems going, via the scale argument: the numbers are so large that even a low credence that insects are sentient gives them a massive expected moral weight. What I find interesting is that she doesn't treat the fanaticism charge as a reductio -- she's happy to follow the arguement where it goes.

Draft me 10-12 interview questions that stay in the weeds of her actual position rather than getting her to restate the headline. I need at least one question each on:

1. the numbers: how she gets from population estimates (she uses something like a trillion-plus farmed insects a year, and says wild insects are several orders of magnitude more again) to an expected moral weight, and why she multiplies credence by number instead of demanding a threshold of confidence before the number matters at all;
2. her move of replacing the yes/no question of sentience with a continuous degree of sentience -- does that actually dissolve the threshold problem or does it just relocate it, and what stops the degree from being stipulated rather than measured?
3. the fanaticism objection head on: if tiny probabilities times huge numbers can dominate, what stops the same structure making us care about nematodes, or bacteria, or the bare possibility that any given object is sentient? What does she think separates insects from those?
4. the per-insect weight: even granting sentience, insect pain might be extremely dim -- does the argument survive if the moral weight per insect is near zero, or does it secretly need insects to suffer badly?
5. the competition between arguments of this shape: there are lots of uncertain-but-possible moral patients (insects, nematodes, wild animals generally, future digital minds). If the expected-value logic is right, doesnt it just leave them all competing with no priority ordering at all?
6. the science: how much rests on the empirical work on insect nociception and behaviour, versus being a purely decision-theoretic point that goes through even if the science says nothing?

For the three or four where she obviously has a stock reply, give me the follow-up that's waiting for that reply. Skip the warm-up stuff about how she got into philosophy. And end on what she'd have us actually do differently on Monday, given that the whole thing hangs on a probability nobody can settle.
