In a historiography seminar, a colleague invoked Ranke's line that history should be told as it really happened, and used it to shut down the suggestion that historical narratives are shaped by present-day concerns: the implication was that anyone raising that suggestion is ignoring the sources. I want a menu, not a single interpretation, of the precise claims that slogan could be making, ordered from nearly trivial to very strong. Consider at least these: that there is a past independent of our descriptions; that historical statements are answerable to evidence; that the historian can and should set aside present interests; that the sources dictate a single narrative; that the historian can attain a God's-eye view; and that the German eigentlich means not only actually but also essentially, so the slogan carries a claim about the true nature of events. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something no historian could accept, and say which reading would actually support the colleague's dismissal. End by noting which readings a Carr- or White-style sceptic can grant while still pressing the worry.
