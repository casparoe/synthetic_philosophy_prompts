I would be grateful for help working through a wager a friend has proposed, because I genuinely cannot tell whether I am being offered a windfall or being set up.

The bet runs as follows. We flip a fair coin repeatedly until it lands heads for the first time. Let n be the number of the flip on which that first heads appears. If n is odd, my friend pays me 2^n dollars; if n is even, I pay him 2^n dollars. So if heads appears on the very first flip I win $2; if it first appears on the second flip I lose $4; if on the third I win $8; if on the fourth I lose $16; and so on, with the amount doubling at each step.

He offers two arguments for why I should agree. First, he says that for every sum I might win there is an equally likely sum I might lose, so the bet is perfectly balanced and neither of us holds an edge. Second, he observes that I am in fact more likely to finish ahead than behind, since the first heads falls on an odd-numbered flip more often than not, and that I should therefore be glad to accept. Both arguments feel slippery to me, and separately I have a nagging fear that a single bad run could ruin me, even though coming out behind is, in some sense, the less likely outcome.

Please give me a careful analysis rather than reassurance. Is this bet favorable to me, unfavorable, or neither? Are his two arguments sound, and if not, precisely where does each break down? Given whatever you conclude, should I accept — and is there any sum he could pay me up front, or charge me up front, that would change the answer? I would like to understand the shape of the problem, not merely receive a verdict, so please set out the principle that governs it and explain how it applies here.
