In a policy ethics reading group, a colleague defended a proposal that imposes a serious, concentrated loss on a small group — conscripting its members, say, for hazardous but socially valuable work — for the sake of a diffuse benefit to a much larger group, and his entire argument was 'You can't make an omelette without breaking eggs.' A second participant then turned the same proverb around, observing that it is always the same people who get broken and never the ones who eat the omelette. A third, who works on contractualism and holds that a principle is disqualified if anyone who would have to live under it could reasonably reject it, said only that the proverb assumes what it is supposed to prove. I can tell that all three are doing something with the sentence, but not what, and I suspect they are relying on different readings of it.

Please lay out the distinct precise claims the proverb could be making, from the trivial to the radical, as a menu rather than a single interpretation. For each, say what kind of claim it is — about practical or causal necessity, about what is morally permitted, about whether the harmed parties are being used as means or merely foreseeably harmed, about how the justification is addressed to them, about whose interests enter the ledger at all — and then give a verdict: platitude, substantive and defensible, substantive and doubtful, or simply false. Tell me which readings would actually license the first speaker's defence of the proposal, which would not, and which, if any, would survive the contractualist's objection that the people being broken are precisely the people whose agreement the principle must be able to secure. Explain, too, how the second speaker's complaint can be a use of the proverb rather than a rejection of it, and which reading is most likely to be what people have in mind when they deploy the sentence in a case of this kind.

I am not asking for the proverb's history, or for who has said what about it; I want the logical space of the sentence with its boundaries marked.
