I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and for the past year I've been sitting in on a reading group about AI alignment. The same argument keeps resurfacing and never resolves. One camp holds that nothing deserves the name 'safety guarantee' unless it holds in the worst case — against an adversary who gets to choose the inputs, the training data, or the deployment conditions — and reasons as though alignment should resemble cryptography, where a scheme counts as secure only relative to a defined adversary. The other camp treats that demand as either unattainable or a category mistake, and reasons as though alignment should resemble medicine: we act on average effects, with no proof of universal safety, and we should work on systems that actually exist. The two sides talk past each other, and I can't tell what kind of disagreement I'm watching.

I'd like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement about how the world is, a verbal dispute over the word 'guarantee', two groups answering different questions under a single heading (one asking what property to aim for, the other asking what can be proved), or a hidden empirical question — namely whether any meaningful worst-case guarantee is even achievable for learned systems? My working hypothesis is that 'worst case' is radically under-specified: worst case over what class of inputs, what space of models, what distribution of deployments? If that's right, the two camps may be computing different quantities and then comparing them, so the apparent clash is partly an equivocation that dissolves once the reference class is fixed — though it may then reopen as a genuine, tractable engineering question. A second possibility is that the split is really a disagreement about decision-making under uncertainty, something like maximin versus expected value, wearing engineering clothes. If either hypothesis is right, say so; if I've misdescribed the camps, correct me using what each side actually claims rather than a caricature of it. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a definitional fix, a formal result, an experiment, or a decision to change the question — or whether this is an impasse we should expect to persist, and why. Be explicit about which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who know the field; I don't want a contested position handed to me as the consensus.
