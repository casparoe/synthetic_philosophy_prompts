I'm in my first year of a philosophy of science program, and I've been stuck for a couple of weeks on something that came up in our Bayesian confirmation seminar. The topic was the Reflection principle — our instructor presented it as the idea that a rational agent's present credences are constrained by the credences she expects to hold later, on the ground that her future self is simply her present self plus more evidence, so that a certain deference is owed to it. I believe I have the formal statement right: if at a time t you are certain that at a later time t' your credence in some proposition A will be r, then your credence in A at t must also be r. The instructor added, almost in passing, that a rational agent's credence in A should be a martingale — that her present credence should equal her expected future credence — and I took this to be the same point, or at least to entail that my present and future credences cannot come apart.

Here is the trouble. If my present credence must equal the credence I am going to have, then it looks as though a rational agent is barred from ever changing her mind: whatever she will believe later, she must already believe now, on pain of violating Reflection. But changing one's mind under the pressure of evidence is precisely what inquiry is supposed to consist in. Suppose I am about to read a study that will either strongly confirm or strongly refute a hypothesis, and I expect that afterward my credence will sit at either 0.8 or 0.2 — I have no idea which. Reflection, as I read it, now tells me I must somehow already be at whichever credence I will end up at, which is impossible, or else tells me nothing at all in exactly the case that matters. A friend in the seminar says the principle only speaks to the special situation in which I am certain in advance what my future credence will be, so my objection dissolves; but I cannot see how that helps, since it seems to make the principle either trivially inapplicable or quietly false whenever the evidence is genuinely open. So I would like to know where the error lies: is Reflection really a requirement that present and future credence coincide, or have I conflated it with something weaker? And if the principle is meant to be restricted to credences produced by a particular kind of rational process, I would like to understand what that restriction amounts to and why the principle needs it.
