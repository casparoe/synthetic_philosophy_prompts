So I've been going to this evening class on archaeology at the local adult-education centre — no exams, just for interest, I got into it after a holiday — and last week the tutor handed out a summary of how your supposed to get from objects to beliefs. The gist was: when a body is buried with grave goods — food, tools, ornaments — we can conclude that the community believed in some kind of continued existence after death, that they expected the dead to need these things, and that they cared about their dead; where the same treatment turns up right across a whole region, they shared a religion; if the grave was reopened years later and the bones rearranged, that confirms it; anything buried rather than thrown on the rubbish heap tells us the site was ritual rather than a settlement; and where there are no grave goods at all, we have to conclude that society had no notion of an afterlife. The handout also said that burials like these are our only real evidence for what prehistoric people believed.
