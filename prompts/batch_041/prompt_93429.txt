In my phil of language reading group someone keeps ending arguments with 'meanings just ain't in the head'. First it was to tell me I can still mean elm by 'elm' even though I couldn't tell an elm from a beech if my life depended on it; last week it was to tell me a chatbot that's only ever read text about water cant mean water by 'water', since it's never been near the stuff. I'm fairly sure there's something to it, but that one sentence is doing about four different jobs and I want to see the seams. Give me the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, roughly from the trivial to the radical, and for each say whether it's a platitude, a substantive thesis worth defending, or just false, and whether it actually gets her her two conclusions. And where does the swamp thing fit — lightning strikes a swamp and assembles a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me, memories and all; which reading of the slogan gives you the verdict that nothing it says means anything, and is that verdict obvious or does it cost something to buy? A menu, not a lecture.
