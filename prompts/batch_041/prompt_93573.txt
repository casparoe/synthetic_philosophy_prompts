I sit on the advisory panel that certifies citizens' initiatives for the ballot in my country. One has now qualified, and it is causing us real difficulty. It would amend the constitution to bar the supreme court from annulling referendum results, oblige judges to give effect to any initiative that passes even where it conflicts with the bill of rights, and provide that these very clauses may be repealed only by a two-thirds majority at a further referendum. Our statute permits us to block an initiative only where it is 'manifestly incompatible with the constitutional order as a whole' -- a test we have never had to apply. The panel is split. Some hold that our proper role is to protect the majority's right to settle its own constitutional arrangements; others that we would be licensing the demolition of the rights that make majority rule worth having; and one member adds that the entrenchment clause is void on its face, since a democratic people cannot bind itself. Please draft the written opinion I shall table at Thursday's meeting. It should reach a definite recommendation -- certify, block, or certify in part -- give reasons that the initiative's sponsors could actually answer, and treat the strongest argument on the side you reject as honestly as the side you favour.
