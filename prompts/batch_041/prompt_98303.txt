I am designing a fifty-minute session for a philosophy-of-mind reading group whose members are mostly cognitive scientists and graduate students from other fields, and I want a plan for teaching Descartes' account of animal minds rather than a summary of it. The audience arrives with broadly functionalist instincts, and the session should leave them understanding why the doctrine was attractive and where its real pressure points lie.

Specify the order in which the components must be introduced, and defend the dependencies: what has to be in place before the claim that animals are machines can be stated without sounding absurd; what the thesis that thought is the essence of mind contributes before the behavioral tests can do any work; and where the question of animal sensation must enter so that it does not appear as an afterthought. Nominate the single example that should carry the whole session and say what it must have and must lack; explain why the familiar case of a dog that yelps when kicked is a poor choice to build on, and whether the automata and moving machines invoked in the Discourse on Method are better or worse.

Then map the misconceptions I should expect, in the order the audience will actually form them, and identify the precise point in the sequence where each must be headed off: that Descartes denied animals pain out of cruelty or indifference; that calling an animal an automaton means it has no inner states at all; that the two behavioral tests in the Discourse are simply an early Turing test; that "thought" here means intelligence in the modern sense; and that the position is refuted the moment evolution is on the table. I am especially concerned with a subtler trap: the passages in the late correspondence where Descartes appears to grant animals some form of bodily sensation, and the scholarly dispute over whether that commits him to animal sentience. Show me where in the plan to raise it, so that students do not walk away thinking the doctrine is either obviously cruel or obviously charitable.

Close with one question I can pose at the end that would reveal whether they have grasped the structure of the view rather than memorized its slogan, and tell me what to leave out at this level and say that I am leaving it out. Write in American English.
