So I'm on a small team that ships a risk-scoring tool — the kind of thing that gets used for bail decisions or benefits fraud triage, though ours is boring and internal. For about a year now we've been having the same arguement and nobody's moved an inch. Here's the setup: our score is calibrated within each group we look at, which everyone agreed was the bare minimum, but when we started checking error rates we found the usual thing — the groups have different base rates, so you can't have a score that's calibrated in both groups and also has equal false-positive and false-negative rates, not unless the score is basically perfect. One camp says: fine, that's a theorem, so this is a values question, we pick which error we'd rather make and who we'd rather make it on, and we write it down and defend it. Another camp says we're arguing about the wrong thing entirely — that picking a metric is just laundering a political choice through a number, and the real problem is the target itself, since we're predicting arrests or fraud flags, which are themselves the output of a biased process. A third person keeps saying the whole literature is about observational parity — same scores, same error rates — and that if you ask the causal question instead (would this person's score have come out differently had they been a different race?) the impossibility results don't even bite, and everyone else says thats just changing the subject. And a fourth keeps insisting this all evaporates with a better model and more features, that the impossibility is an artifact of us being bad at prediction.

My hunch is that we aren't actually disagreeing about the math, or even really about values, but that at least two of us are answering different questions under one word. Is that right? And if so, which questions? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the fairness literature — and I want to know what would actually count as progress here. Is there a distinction, a formal result, or a reframing that would dissolve this, or is it bedrock all the way down and we should just document our choice and stop? Be honest about that last possibility.
