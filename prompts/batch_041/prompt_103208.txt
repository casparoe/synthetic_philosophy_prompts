ok so I keep running into this thing and I don't know if it has a name. Say I'm trying to decide which of two situations is better, and they only differ in one respect that you can dial up and down. Amount of pain in a life, say, or number of years lived, or how many people are affected. If I line a bunch of these up from clearly-great to clearly-terrible and I only ever compare neighbors -- this one's better than the one right below it -- every single comparison seems obviously right. No moment where I'm squinting and going "eh, hard to say." Neighbor to neighbor is always easy. But when I chain them together I end up committed to the top one being better than the bottom one, which is nuts, because the bottom is like a year of torture and the top is like a stubbed toe. Same thing happens comparing jobs that differ only in salary, or cities that differ only in commute time. So one of the neighbor comparisons has to be wrong, or "better" isn't doing what I assumed, but I can't find the bad one because they're each individually obvious.

The part that bugs me is this doesn't feel like the usual "where do you draw the line" thing with vague words, like when does a heap stop being a heap -- cuz there I'm at least unsure about the borderline cases. Here I'm not unsure about any single case. And it doesn't feel like just "some people have circular preferences," cuz it's not me flip-flopping, it's that the comparisons themselves can't all be true at the same time.

So: does this have a name? And more to the point, how is it different from the heap/vagueness thing and from just having weird circular preferences? What's actually going on -- is the culprit the thing I'm comparing (pain, years, whatever), or the word "better," or the assumption that if A beats B and B beats C then A beats C? And what are the legit moves for getting out of it? I don't want a label and nothing else, I want the thing actually carved up, with where the edges are and what it isn't. American English please.
