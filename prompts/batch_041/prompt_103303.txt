I have come to hold the following view and want to know what would count against it. Sentience is a matter of degree: the capacity for valenced experience can be present to greater or lesser extents, and moral status is a continuous function of it, so that a bee, a fish, a dog, and a person differ in moral weight rather than in kind, while a thermostat sits at the bottom of the same scale rather than outside it altogether. I am not after the familiar doubts about whether insects or fish feel anything; I want the defeaters for the framework itself, and I want them assessed on their merits rather than merely listed.

First, what would have to be the case for the very idea of degrees of sentience to be incoherent rather than merely hard to measure, and how much does the view depend on there being a single natural scale, given that the plausible dimensions — intensity, richness, temporal integration, the span of valence — may not be commensurable with one another? If there is no privileged scalar, does the picture survive in some weakened form, or does it quietly collapse into a pluralism that is no longer the view I stated?

Second, the position needs the gradation of the capacity to carry over into a gradation of status. A rival grants that sentience is graded but insists that moral status is thresholded: an entity either has interests or it does not, and nothing in between. What observations, arguments, or possible cases would decide between these two, and which of them, if any, should push me toward the threshold view rather than away from it?

Third, what does my position predict that the threshold view and the all-or-nothing view do not, and does it in fact risk anything, or has the appeal to degrees insulated it from disconfirmation? I would like the defeaters sorted by how decisive they are, with an explicit division between those a committed proponent of the view would concede and those they would simply dismiss, and a candid verdict on whether the view is unfalsifiable and whether that is a problem for a position of this kind. If the view turns out to be committed to a determinate answer to a question such as whether one person outweighs a very large number of bees, I would regard that as especially revealing; tell me whether it is so committed, and what follows for it either way.
