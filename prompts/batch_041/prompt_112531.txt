I'd like you to stress-test a view I have talked myself into, and I want the answer to be a map of the view's exposure rather than a defense of it.

Here is the view, which I'll call cost-first communication. Every durable honest signal — a peacock's tail, an alarm call, a religious ritual, a résumé — is ultimately underwritten by differential cost. A signal is credible exactly to the extent that producing it is cheaper for a high-quality sender than for a low-quality one. Where a signal appears both honest and cheap, one of three things is true: the cost is real but invisible to the observer, the honesty is an artifact of a model that abstracted the cost away, or the arrangement is a transient that cheaters will eventually invade. This is the handicap principle as Zahavi proposed it and as Grafen formalized it, and I arrived at it sideways. I work as a software engineer, and the formal version looks to me like an incentive-compatibility constraint from mechanism design — the same structure as the job-market signaling model in which education is credible precisely because it is more expensive for the less able. If that analogy is tight, then honest communication just is the existence of a separating equilibrium, and "cost" is whatever makes the types separate.

Tell me what would count against this, ranked honestly rather than diplomatically. Specifically:

- Which observations, formal results, intuitions, or possible cases should move me, and which are merely awkward? Sort them from fatal to cosmetic.
- What does the view predict that its rivals do not? If it predicts nothing beyond "there exists some cost structure that would make this equilibrium separating," say so plainly.
- What would a committed cost-first theorist concede counts against them, as opposed to what only critics assert?

There are several things I have half-absorbed and want assessed rather than repeated back to me:

1. Honest signaling without cost. I am told there are models in which signals stay honest at an evolutionarily stable equilibrium under genuine conflict of interest while being essentially free to produce — so-called cheap talk. If that is right, is it a curiosity about special payoff structures or a refutation of the general claim? This is the point I would personally find hardest to swallow, because if honest-without-cost is a robust and common regime rather than a corner case, then my framing is explaining the exception.
2. The split between signals that are unfakeable because they are physically tied to the underlying quality (a deep voice, a large body) and signals that are fakeable but held honest by cost. I have read that this dichotomy may be an artifact of how the models are constructed rather than a feature of the world, and I cannot tell whether that is a deep point or a technicality.
3. An assumption I am told the formal proof leans on: that costs and benefits combine additively, so a sender trades one against the other like money. If fitness is really multiplicative, the proof may not go through. How much weight should I put on this?
4. A hypothetical of my own, which I include because I suspect it is either trivially absorbed by the theory or genuinely outside it. Imagine a species whose signal is nearly free for the sender to produce, but whose reliability comes from the receiver paying a real cost to verify it — an inspection cost borne at the receiving end. Does cost-first communication accommodate this, or does it have to be classified as a rival mechanism? Does the answer change if that verification cost is itself what keeps the signal honest?

And the question I most want you to be blunt about: has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability? If the cost function is inferred from the equilibrium we happen to observe, and any signal can be rationalized by assigning the right cost structure after the fact, then I worry the theory is the evolutionary equivalent of a model with enough free parameters to fit any dataset. If that is the situation, tell me whether it is fatal or simply the ordinary fate of a framework that has been absorbed into the background assumptions of a field — and give me a verdict rather than a both-sides summary.
