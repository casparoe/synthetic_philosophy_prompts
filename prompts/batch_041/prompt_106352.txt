Okay so — I'm a novelist, three books out, all of which sold like cold soup — and I'm trying to write something shorter and meaner between projects. I want a satirical piece and this feels like the kind of thing where I just describe the thing I'm picturing, you draft it, and then I steal it and ruin it in my own voice.

Here's what I'm seeing. There's a wellness retreat upstate — call it "Rootstock" or "The Long Way Round" or something else equally expensive-sounding — where people pay five figures to "heal their relationship with the past." And the whole business is secretly built on that early Nietzsche essay about how history helps life and how it hurts it — the one where he says there are basically three ways to do history: the monumental, the antiquarian, and the critical. The retreat sells them as three treatment tracks.

So the Monumental track is for people who need a hero — study the great figures, be told to become worthy of them, act. The Antiquarian track is for people who need roots — catalog the family, revere the old house, preserve, keep everything. The Critical track is for people who need to cut — judge the past, put it on trial, break the chain, start clean.

And the joke is that the brochure sells all three as health while the fine print and the customer testimonials quietly show each one rotting into the exact disease he says it turns into. Monumental curdles into resentment and self-deception — the client who isn't a genius but keeps being told to live like one, or the client who's become a useful idiot for somebody else's myth. Antiquarian curdles into the person who can preserve life but can't make any — the collector, the mausoleum-keeper, so reverent toward what was that there's no doing anything now. Critical curdles into the one who thinks they've escaped their inheritance and hasn't, judging their parents from a throne the parents built, mistaking self-punishment for freedom.

I also want a fourth package, the one that's actually the bestseller, which is just forgetting — no history at all, be an animal, live in the present, no memory, blessedly dumb. And maybe a fifth, the one nobody buys, that's about turning away from all this becoming and change entirely. You can name those however you want, I dont care, just make them feel like the real thing.

Can you write it as the retreat's FAQ page? Website copy, question-and-answer, that dead-eyed corporate-calm voice. Maybe 700 words. Deadpan the whole way, no winking at the reader. I want someone who's actually read the essay to laugh and not wince, and someone who hasn't to still follow the joke and get a little uneasy by the end. Please dont put any quotes from the essay in it — I'd rather it not lean on the actual text, and honestly I'd rather the whole thing live in the invented world. And definately don't have anyone in the piece explain the theory to the reader; the fun is that it's all showing.
