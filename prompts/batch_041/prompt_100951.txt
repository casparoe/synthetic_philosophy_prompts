Her cousin Ash says she didn't — she wrote a recipe, or a set of instructions, and calling it a work of art is cheating, because a work of art has to be something that can be encountered.

Roughly how it went:

1. Lena: the score is the piece. Music is an abstract thing, like a proof. A proof doesn't stop being a proof because nobody read it, and my piece doesn't stop being a piece because nobody heard it.
2. Ash: but a proof is a fact about numbers, true whether or not anyone writes it down. Your piece doesn't exist as music until air moves. You made a plan for music, not music. And don't say a score counts as a work just by existing — a score counts because it can be played. Yours is defined by never being played, so its one essential function is ruled out by its own terms.
3. Lena: that proves too much. Paintings sit in museum vaults for decades, nobody sees them, and we still call them paintings — we'd still call them art if the museum promised never to display them. If "nobody will experience it" disqualified a thing, half the art in storage isn't art.
4. Ash: the vault painting can be seen, you've just arranged that it won't be. It's still the kind of thing made for eyes. Your piece is the kind of thing made for ears, but you've made the not-being-heard part of it — you built the refusal into the work. So either the work is the sound-structure, in which case the refusal is just a fact about you and not about the piece, and fine, it's a piece; or the work is the refusal, in which case the score is a prop and the art is the act of withholding. Pick one.
5. Lena: false choice. It's a piece of music and it's also a piece about not being heard; things can be two things. And anyway, I've heard it — I played it in my head while writing it, so it's had an audience of one.
6. Ash: then you're not defending the case you started with. You started with "a work no one will ever hear is still a work." If you've heard it, it's a work with an audience, and that's just private art. If your real claim is "art needs no audience," your own example doesn't show it, because the moment you need the head-performance to save the piece, you've admitted the piece needs a listener.
