Ok so I'm in a comparative philosophy seminar and my assignment was to reconstruct Zhuangzi on death as an actual argument — something you could state, defend, and attack — instead of the usual "he's chill about it, it's all transformation" hand-waving. I did it, and now I'm woried I've made him hold something nobody could hold. Here's the reconstruction:

(1) We can't know whether death is bad, or whether the dead regret dying — the only witnesses are on the living side of the line, and nobody reports back.
(2) You have reason to fear or grieve something only if you know it to be bad.
(3) So we have no reason to fear our own death, and no reason to grieve when someone dies.

(1)-(3) gets me the stuff I need: the drumming on the tub when his wife died, the skull who turns down the offer of being restored to life because the joys of the dead beat a king's, the line about maybe loving life being a delusion and hating death being like a man who wandered off as a child and forgot the road home. It even gets the man from Lu who goes through the whole mourning performance with dry eyes and is still reckoned the finest mourner in the state.

The trouble is (2), and I honestly can't tell whether the trouble is really with (2). If (2) holds it doesn't just kill grief over the dead, it kills every forward looking emotion — I don't know the surgery will go fine, or that my kid will be ok, or that moving cities won't wreck my career; I have evidence for these things, but evidence isn't knowledge in whatever sense (2) seems to need. So (2) makes hope, dread, relief and grief all unwarranted, which I won't accept. And it cuts the other way too: if I can't know death is bad, I can't know it's good, and yet he sings — so either the ignorance point proves way too much, or it isn't the thing actually doing the work in him. Which step do I cut, and what does the cut cost me? If I drop (2), what do I put in its place so that some grief comes out warranted while the drumming still comes out as wisdom rather than a stunt — and does the replacement still cover the wife and the skull, or does it quietly only cover my own death? I'd like to keep (1), that's the part that sounds most like him, but tell me if keeping it is exactly what's forcing the rest.
