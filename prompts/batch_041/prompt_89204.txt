(a) Explain why two rational bidders can drive the price above £1. (b) A student in the seminar said the lesson is just "don't fall for the sunk cost fallacy". Is that the right diagnosis? (c) Would promising yourself in advance never to bid above £1 fix the problem?'

The answer: 'In a dollar auction the price can climb past £1 because of the way losing works. Once you're the second-highest bidder, dropping out means you pay your bid and get nothing, so it always looks better to raise by 10p and keep a chance of winning the coin. Each individual step is rational, so the two bidders ratchet each other upwards. For (b), I think the seminar student was basically right: people keep bidding because they've already put money in and don't want to waste it, so they throw good money after bad. That's the sunk cost fallacy, just applied to bidding. For (c), yes -- if you commit in advance to a £1 ceiling you'll walk away before you start making a loss, so the trap is avoided. Really the only fully safe move is not to enter the auction at all.'
