I'm writing the take-home essay for a senior seminar on precommitment and constitutionalism -- Ulysses contracts, entrenchment, all of it. Every year half the class answers by saying the constitution binds the future self the way Ulysses binds himself, and stops there. What I want them to actually see is that in the story the binding gets done by someone else: Ulysses has the crew tie him to the mast and tells them in advance to ignore him when he begs to be cut loose. So the interesting question isn't whether a self can bind itself, it's who holds the rope and what gives them the standing to overrule the self they're holding. I want one essay question that forces them to say whether the constitutional case even has a "crew", whether a court is better understood as the rope or as a co-author, and what that does to the charge that entrenchment is undemocratic. Give me the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme with the must-hit moves, marks out of 20, and a one-liner for each move on how students typically botch it -- I want to be able to see from the scheme why a B answer is a B and not an A. Model answer should lead with its bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and dont restate the question. Mixed law/philosophy crowd, so keep the rational-choice formalism out of it.
