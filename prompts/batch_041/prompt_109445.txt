Ok so I'm running a session on Descartes for a reading group next week and I want to try a tier-list exercise on them before I inflict it on anyone else, so humor me.

Take as fixed the thing basically everyone grants: that while he's actually in the middle of thinking or doubting, Descartes can't be wrong about his own existence. The question is how much further each of the following claims gets on that basis alone. Sort them into tiers, from "the cogito by itself basically gives you this" at the top down to "this needs an extra premise Descartes hasn't payed for yet" at the bottom, with one sentence per item on why it lands where it does. If two of them are really the same claim in different clothes, collapse them into one and say so. And if one of them isn't even a claim about what the cogito proves, don't force it into a tier — say what kind of claim it actually is instead. Dont bother telling me who says what, I know the literature well enough, I just want the ranking. Tiers with labels please (Tier 1, Tier 2, ...), and tell me if the bottom tier ends up empty.

(a) That thinking is happening — some thought occurs — but nothing yet about an owner of it. The worry that all he's earned is that there's a thinking, not that anyone's doing it.
(b) That there's an owner, a subject, but a very thin one: "I" here works like a demonstrative, just picking out whoever happens to be doing the thinking at that moment, with no essence and no persistence built in.
(c) That the whole thing isn't an inference at all but a performance, since you can't seriously assert that you don't exist while you're in the middle of asserting it, so there's no premise-conclusion structure there to assess in the first place.
(d) That he's a thinking thing or substance — something whose entire nature is to think, and which is therefore really distinct from any body.
(e) That he exists as the same self over time — the one who doubted yesterday and the one thinking now are one and the same.
(f) That he's a unified subject: all these thoughts are his, had by one consciousness, rather than just a bundle of occurrent states.
(g) That whatever he clearly and distinctly perceives is true — the cogito being the first item in that class, so it licenses the general rule.
(h) That he is not his body, and so materialism is false of him.

Then finish with a one-liner: if you had to state the strongest conclusion the cogito on its own supports, what is it?
