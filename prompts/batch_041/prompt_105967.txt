I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of fifteen years, so I've absorbed a fair amount of modal metaphysics secondhand — enough to know that 'could have been' behaves strangely on a counterpart-theoretic reading, not enough to have a settled view of my own.

What I want from you is a joke about the disagreement between counterpart theory and transworld identity. The setup should be mundane; the punchline should turn on the actual content of the two views rather than on the fact that philosophers enjoy arguing about modality. Someone who knows the literature should laugh, and someone who doesn't should come away with a rough sense of what the disagreement is about.

I'd rather it not lean on the politician who lost the election, which is the example everyone reaches for. Two features of the counterpart-theoretic picture strike me as more promising. First, the counterpart relation is allowed to be non-transitive and non-symmetric, so a counterpart of a counterpart of you need not be your counterpart, and the reverse need not hold either — chains of 'what I could have been' are permitted to drift. Second, ordinary claims about what you could have been get reparsed as claims about what some other thing is like, which changes how consolation, regret, and advice sound. A scene in which one person tries to comfort another — a rejection, a regret, a path not taken — seems like the natural setting, and I'd prefer the joke not resolve by having either party win the argument.

Give me one joke of no more than about 150 words, with a clear setup and punchline. If a second version with a different punchline occurs to you, include it. After the joke, add a single sentence saying what it turns on. Please don't include citations, references, or a reading list; I want only the joke and that sentence.
