Before I read any further proposals about how a state should raise its army, I want the requirements set out: what must any adequate account of justified conscription be able to do? Give the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it. The cases to cover should include at least Switzerland, where able-bodied men are liable for service, those found unfit pay an income-linked exemption levy, conscientious objectors serve longer in civilian work than soldiers serve in uniform, and a recent referendum to replace male conscription with a gender-neutral civic duty was defeated by a wide margin; Sweden, where liability is gender-neutral on paper but only a small fraction of each cohort is actually called; Finland, where the great majority of the male cohort serves, terms vary with the role trained for, total refusers are imprisoned, and the demilitarized Åland islands are exempt; and a state that has just gone all-volunteer and is debating whether to bring conscription back. Among the distinctions the account must not collapse: being liable to be called, owing service, and paying a tax in lieu of it; conscription as coercion and conscription as an in-kind tax whose incidence falls on those with the least valuable alternative use of their time; what a state may do to its citizens and what citizens owe their state; peacetime registration and wartime call-up; a force usable only on its own territory and one that can be sent abroad. It must also reach a definite verdict on conscientious objection and on civilian service as a substitute, and it must be consistent with what is actually known about how conscript and professional forces perform and about whether service produces the civic attachment claimed for it, as well as take a position on the internal-political side of force design, where a dispersed part-time militia is harder to aim at the citizenry than a standing army and correspondingly slower to aim at anything. Then say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of one camp, and flag the subset you think cannot be jointly satisfied, especially any conflict between requiring that the burden be shared by everyone and requiring that the force be effective under specialization. Finally, say what the list owes to a state that has forsworn alliances and offensive capability, so that its army has no conceivable use except the defense of its own territory.
