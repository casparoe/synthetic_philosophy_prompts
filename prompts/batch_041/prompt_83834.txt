I am a third-year undergraduate enrolled in a seminar on naturalism and the philosophy of mind, and I have been asked to write a term paper in the genre of an agenda: not a survey of a literature, but a defended claim about which open questions in some area matter most, and why. I would like to use the paper to sort out a framework that my seminar invokes constantly and never quite pins down: the contrast between cranes and skyhooks, together with the accusation of greedy reductionism, which my classmates and I deploy as though it were a settled technical term. My difficulty is that I can see at least a dozen candidate questions in this vicinity and no principled way to tell which of them the others depend on.

Please help me construct the agenda. I would like you to rank the candidate questions below, adding any important ones I have overlooked, by how much other work in the naturalistic project depends on their resolution, and by how tractable each is for someone in my position: a philosopher with roughly three weeks, a 4,000-word limit, and only armchair resources together with whatever secondary sources I can lay hands on.

The candidates I have so far:

(a) Is "skyhook" a substantive category or merely a pejorative? That is, is there a principled criterion for an explanation's doing no work, or does the label express nothing beyond incredulity at a posited discontinuity?

(b) Can a genuine discontinuity still be a crane? Transitions such as the origin of the genetic code, of language, or of normative judgment seem at first glance to require something unsupported; how would we determine whether such a transition is produced by prior processes or is a true skyhook?

(c) What distinguishes greedy from legitimate reductionism? Does the difference lie in how many levels are skipped, in which levels are skipped, or in what the explanation loses? Is "greedy" anything more than "reduction that leaves out something the critic cares about"?

(d) Does the crane program presuppose a contested metaphysics of levels — that higher-level features are always assembled from lower-level cranes, with no genuine downward causation or strong emergence? If so, how much of the framework is an empirical bet and how much is stipulation?

(e) Could there be a vice opposite to greedy reductionism — an explanatory style that is too crane-like, piling up intermediate levels until the account becomes unfalsifiable, a tower of cranes that could accommodate anything? If so, what would a criterion for enough levels look like, and does this threaten the program from the other direction?

(f) Is the origin of the first replicator the paradigm crane, or the one place where a skyhook is genuinely required?

(g) Is the intentional stance itself a crane or a skyhook — does the framework apply to its own author's central posit, and does it survive the application?

(h) Does the framework extend to normativity and to mathematics, or does a crane-style explanation of these inevitably leave a residue that functions as a skyhook?

For each question I want the reasoning rather than the verdict alone: what makes it upstream or downstream of the others, what would count as progress on it, and what development over the next few years would move it up or down my list. Then I want you to commit. Tell me which single question I should write the paper about given my constraints, and give me your own best first pass at the answer you would defend — not a survey of positions, but the position you would take, along with the strongest objection to it that you can see and your reply to that objection.
