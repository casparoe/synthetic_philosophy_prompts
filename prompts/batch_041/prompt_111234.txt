I keep having the same argument with a colleague and neither of us can move the other, so I want a diagnosis rather than someone telling me who's right. The question is whether the interpretability work we do on neural nets — probes, sparse autoencoders, activation patching, feature visualization, the circuit-hunting stuff — is actually uncovering representations the network has, or whether 'representation' is just a word we drape over a description that happens to be useful to us. She's a realist about it. Her line is that when a linear probe decodes a concept, steering along that direction flips the behavior, and the direction shows up again in a different task, that's about as strong as evidence for a representation ever gets, and the skeptics are just moving the goalposts. I'm the skeptic. My line is that a probe can find a decodable direction for almost anything you train it to find, that the features you get out depend on what you asked for going in, and that nobody has ever told me what would count as the network NOT representing something — so as far as I can tell the claim can't fail and therefore isn't doing any work. She thinks I'm denying the science. I think we're talking past each other and I can't pin down how.

Here's my hypothesis, take it or leave it: the word 'representation' is doing at least two jobs and we keep swapping them. One is the information-carrying sense — an internal state that correlates with some distal feature, an indicator. The other is the use sense — a state the system actually reads out and computes with, stably, across contexts, such that removing it does specific downstream damage. My suspicion is that the realists have the use sense in mind and the evidence mostly bears on the indicator sense, and that a lot of the heat comes from that gap. But it could also be that we're not disagreeing about the word at all, but about what a criterion of representation should even look like — her bar is causal-interventionist and mine is something closer to 'tell me what the alternative hypothesis predicts,' and if that's the crux then no amount of new probing results will settle it. Or maybe it's a levels-of-description thing and 'does it have representations' is just incomplete until you say at what level.

Diagnose it for me. Engage with what each side actually says rather than surveying the literature, tell me what kind of disagreement this is, and be honest about what, if anything, would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, an experiment that could come out either way. And please separate what's genuinely settled in this area from what's still contested among people who know it well; don't hand me a contested position as if it were the consensus. No headings or bullet points or bold, just continuous prose like you'd write in an email.
