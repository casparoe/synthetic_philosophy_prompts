Question: In the careenium thought experiment, a vast arena contains countless simmballs—physical tokens that collide, stick, and rebound according to local mechanical rules—and their interactions are said to implement a symbol-manipulating process. Does the careenium vindicate the claim that symbols (or their meanings) are causally efficacious, or does it show that only the physical substrate does the pushing? Explain how the symbolic level relates to the mechanical level, and assess whether the symbolic description is merely a convenient gloss or a genuine causal-explanatory level. (10 points)

Student answer:

The careenium settles the question in favor of the substrate. At the mechanical level, every event is fixed by the masses, velocities, and collision rules of the simmballs. Nothing else can make a difference. So if "symbols push the substrate" means that meanings exert a force over and above the physical forces, the careenium shows that this is false. There is no extra causal power at the symbolic level. The simmballs do all the pushing; the symbols are just patterns that supervene on the mechanics.

This does not mean the symbolic level is unreal or useless. We can describe the same system in two ways: as a physical system and as a symbol-manipulating system. The symbolic description compresses a lot of mechanical detail and can be predictive, because the mechanical rules are set up so that the symbolic regularities hold. So the symbolic level is real in the sense of being a pattern in the mechanics, and it is explanatory in the sense that it gives us a tractable way to predict the system's behavior.

But the symbolic level is not autonomous. Its causal powers are inherited from the mechanical level. If we wanted to explain why a particular simmball moved, the full story would be in terms of collisions, not in terms of the meaning of the symbol. So Dennett is right that the careenium shows that symbols don't push the substrate around; the substrate pushes itself. The symbolic level is just a description, not an additional cause.

The only caveat is that, for Dennett, the intentional stance is still legitimate. We can treat the careenium as a system that has beliefs and desires, and this is not just a fiction. But this legitimacy is pragmatic: it works because the system's behavior is predictable from the intentional stance. It does not mean that the beliefs themselves are doing any causal work. So the careenium supports a kind of instrumentalism about content: content talk is useful, but the real causation is always mechanical.
