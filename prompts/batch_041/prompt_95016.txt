Ok, so I'm a science-and-ideas reporter and I've got a story that's turned into a philosophy problem and I could use a hand.

The story: a hospital installs an automated sepsis watch. It's not a robot, it doesn't *do* anything — it just sits on the vitals feed and pings the on-call nurse when it thinks somebody's crashing. On one ward it was silently broken for six weeks. A patient died: nurse never got a ping, didn't escalate, doctor wasn't called, everyone did their job as far as they knew. The family's lawyer says the system killed him. The vendor says software can't kill anybody, it never touched the man, the most you can say is it didn't save him. And then the wierd part — the vendor's real defense is that the thing was *designed* to keep a human in the loop, so the not-pinging was the whole point, not a glitch.

What I want is a number and the guts behind it. My credence that the system's failure to ping is a cause of the death in the same sense that the nurse pushing the wrong drug would be a cause: about 0.6, and honestly I pulled that out of the air. My editor's at like 0.15 and thinks I'm being sentimental about the software. So give me your own number, then break it into the sub-questions it's actually riding on — a rough probability for each, one line of justification for each. The ones I can see from here:

1. Is causation at bottom difference-making — would the outcome have gone differently? — or is it some kind of real connection running through the world, production or mechanism? And can a non-event have either one?
2. Does an omission only count as a cause relative to a norm that got violated — a duty, an expectation, a this-thing-is-supposed-to-work? If so, is the norm doing metaphysical work or just conversational work, i.e. picking out which cause we bother to mention?
3. Is "the same sense of cause" even a determinate thing to ask about? Or is cause a tool with different jobs — blame, prediction, explanation, control — and the question dissolves?
4. The one I can't shake: if the vendor had shipped a version that actively pushed a wrong dose, everyone would say it killed him. Is that asymmetry a fact about the world or a fact about us? And does it shift at all when the failure was designed in on purpose?

Then combine them into a number. Then tell me which of those terms my 0.6 is most sensitive to, and where my editor and I actually disagree — is it term 2, or does she just not treat the thing as a causal agent at all? And what would move me 0.2 in either direction: somthing about the metaphysics, or just learning how hospitals and courts end up treating cases like this.
