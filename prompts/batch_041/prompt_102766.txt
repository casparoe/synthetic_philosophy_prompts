I host a philosophy podcast, and my next guest defends quantum immortality: the thesis that on a no-collapse reading of quantum mechanics, for any lethal event there is always a branch on which the same person survives, so first-person experience never comes to an end. Draft eight to ten interview questions that move past the reflexive "isn't that unfalsifiable?" challenge and engage the position on its own terms. I want at least one question on the measure problem -- why the survivor's first-person perspective should be weighted by branch amplitude at all -- with a follow-up ready for the likely reply that survival is a matter of self-location rather than of probability. I also want one question on how the view interacts with anthropic selection, specifically why the fact that a survivor invariably finds himself alive does not count as evidence for the thesis, and one question that forces the guest to say what a quantum immortalist should actually expect in a quantum game of Russian roulette, in which each round kills with probability 1 - 1/n: is the number of rounds he should expect to experience finite, and how does that sit with the claim that death is never experienced? Press as well on whether branching yields a single continuing subject or a succession of duplicates, and on what the view loses if it is the latter. End with a question about what could in principle count against the thesis, given that its central prediction is one only a survivor can ever be in a position to report. For each question, note the answer the guest is most likely to give and the follow-up that makes that answer hardest to sustain.
