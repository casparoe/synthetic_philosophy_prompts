A friend of mine has left a marriage of fourteen years, and the way he explains it is that he has finally stopped pretending. He says he spent half his life being someone he was not — a husband, a homeowner, a man with a particular kind of job — and that he could not go on living inside a life that did not belong to him. He is not lying, as far as I can tell; he seems more at ease than I have ever seen him. He calls me most evenings now, and I never know what to say to him.

What I cannot get past is the shape of the explanation. When he says that this is not who he is, he is not telling me what he has decided; he is telling me what he has discovered, and there is nothing left to argue with. When I press him, he says he had no choice. That is the part that stops me: a man who says he had no choice does not sound free, and a man who walks out on his family because it is not who he is does not sound constrained, yet he is saying both at once. My wife thinks he is simply selfish and dressing it up, and that anyone who genuinely wanted to leave could just say so. I think she is half right and half wrong, and I would like to know which half. Is there a real difference between being true to yourself and using your self as an excuse? If there is, what does it consist in — and is it the kind of difference anyone could detect from the outside?
