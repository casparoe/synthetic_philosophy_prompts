Everyone keeps applying costly signaling and the handicap principle to absolutely everything — peacock tails, stotting gazelles, religion, charity, big-game hunting — and I keep watching the same arguement play out and dont really get why nobody moves. The skeptics say the theory is unfalsifiable: if the trait is costly, fine, that's the handicap; if it isn't, then the real cost is hidden somewhere or it's a different kind of signal, so no observation could ever count against it. The defenders say the math shows that honesty requires the cost to bite harder on low-quality senders than on high-quality ones, so it's a genuine constraint that makes real predictions. My hunch is that the two camps aren't actually disagreeing about animals — they're either disagreeing about what 'cost' means (a fitness hit the sender pays, versus any reason a faker can't get away with it), which would make it partly a verbal spat, or they're disagreeing about what counts as a good explanation, which is a philosophy-of-biology fight wearing a biology costume. So diagnose it for me: verbal, substantive, or two different questions under one name? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, an experiment that could really falsify it, or nothing, if it's unfalsifiable by construction? Tell me too where your diagnosis is most likely to be wrong and what would change your mind.
