I'm a backend engineer with no real philospohy background, but I've been thinking about AI alignment and landed on a view I want to stress-test. When people say an AI should be aligned to human values, I don't think the target should be an aggregate of what people actually prefer — not the average, not the sum, not some market-style weighting. It should be the set of values that could be justified to everyone affected, where each person's preferences count in proportion to how much they'd be affected by the AI's decisions, but nobody's conception of the good gets to count as more reasonable than anyone else's. I've been calling it stake-weighted public reason. So the AI stays neutral between ways of life except where they conflict, and when they conflict it favors the worst-off. What would tell against this? I want the strongest objections, ranked by how much they should actually worry me, and which of them someone who holds the view would have to concede. What does it predict that a plain preference-aggregation view doesn't? And be honest about whether the view is so qualified that nothing could count against it — if so, is that a problem or just what any view like this ends up looking like?
