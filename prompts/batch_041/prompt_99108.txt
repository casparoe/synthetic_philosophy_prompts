I'm a philosophy professor and I'm putting together a deliberately unserious session for my decision theory class on games whose expected value just isn't there. Not St Petersburg, everyone's heard that one. I mean the Pasadena game: you flip a fair coin until it lands heads, and the payout on the nth flip flips between a gain and a loss and gets larger as n grows, roughly doubling each round, so the expectation comes out as a series that only converges conditionally. Add the terms up in the obvious order and you get a finite number; add them up in some other order and you can get any number you like, positive, negative, or unbounded, and no long run of actual plays ever settles down to anything either. The upshot being that whether the game is worth playing turns out to be a fact about how you do the bookkeeping rather than about the game.

What I'd like from you is a joke built on that, and I mean one whose punchline actually turns on that content: someone who knows the game should laugh because it's about the undefined expectation and the reordering, not just a generic infinity gag, and someone who doesn't know it should still get that there's a real point underneath. A disputed inheritance, or a game show, or a very confused actuary would all work as a setting, but use your judgement.

Format: exactly five numbered beats, a sentence or two each, no preamble and no explanation tacked on after the last one. Cheers.
