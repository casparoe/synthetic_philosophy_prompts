Here's the setup. Two positions. (A) the constitutive / quietist line: the tortoise doesn't show that deduction is unjustified, it shows that demanding a non-circular justification of a basic rule is the wrong kind of demand in the first place. Rules of inference aren't the sort of thing you derive from further premises; the whole appearance of a gap comes from letting the tortoise turn "if P then Q" into one more premise and then asking for yet another rule to get from premises to conclusion. Nothing was ever missing, so there's no gap to fill. (B) the vindication line: that's a dodge. The tortoise locates a real gap — nothing in the premises, not even the premises plus the conditional, obliges the conclusion unless you already accept the rule, and if you already accept the rule you've assumed exactly what you wanted to justify. So the live question is whether there's some non-circular vindication: a pragmatic argument, a transcendental argument, or the claim that inferring is partly constitutive of believing anything at all, so that a creature who genuinely refuses modus ponens isn't occupying a coherent position.
