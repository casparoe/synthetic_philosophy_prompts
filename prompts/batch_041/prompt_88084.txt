I'm a retired chemist — forty years in industry, mostly catalysts — and since I stopped working I've been trying to actually read some philosophy instead of just the science pages. Fair warning, I don't have the vocabulary for this.

Here's what I keep bumping into. Almost nothing I "know" about the world is anything I ever saw myself. I know how old the universe is because a long chain of people I'll never meet said so. I know a particular reaction gives a particular product because a paper said so — I never ran it, and I'd bet the person who wrote the paper never ran it either; a student did, or a machine did, or some earlier lab did, and it got handed along. Somewhere in that chain nobody has the thing first-hand, and I'd still say I know it, and if you said I didn't I'd think you were being silly. When someone in a meeting questioned a number we didn't re-run anything, we said "that's what the literature says," and the doubter had to make a case, not the reporter.

What I can't work out is whether "someone reliable told me" is a way of knowing in it's own right, or just shorthand for a longer bit of ordinary reasoning — they've got no reason to lie, people like them are usually right, someone would have caught it by now. I lean towards it being its own thing, because the shorthand story never quite closes: my reasons for trusting the chain aren't things I checked either.

I have a feeling Indian philosophy already has a name for this, because I keep reading that they had a short numbered list of the basic ways you can know something, and one entry on the list seems to be exactly this — knowing by being told. I think one of the schools is called Nyāya but I honestly can't keep them apart. So: is there a term, which school is it that says this is basic rather than a seperate case of reasoning, and what's supposed to make it work? I can see that the teller has to be competent and honest, but that just moves the problem one step back, and it doesn't tell me why I'm allowed to lean on the whole anonymous apparatus instead of on any particular person.

And I don't just want the label. I want the edges. Where's the line between this and plain inference from evidence about what somebody said? Does a chain of reports with nobody at the end who ever saw the thing still count as the same concept, or does it break? Is there a name for the opposite view — the one that says no, this is never basic, everything you get from another person is really just reasoning from what you noticed about them? If there turns out to be no term for what I'm describing, please say so rather than handing me a made-up one dressed up as tradition.

Last thing: tell me how confident you are in what you're telling me, and flag the point you're least sure of. I'd rather see the shaky bit than get a confident lecture — I'm too old for confident lectures.
