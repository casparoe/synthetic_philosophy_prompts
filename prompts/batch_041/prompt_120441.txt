I am assembling the specification an account of Moorean absurdity would have to meet before I could rely on it in an argument about whether the defect in such cases lies in the belief or only in its expression, so an account that has already settled that question is of no use to me. Write out the desiderata: the data an adequate account must accommodate, the distinctions it must not collapse, the explanations it owes, and the overgenerations it must avoid, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. Do not name authors, works, or readings, and do not survey or grade existing accounts; I will do that myself once I have the list.

The data, in my own formulations:

1. It is raining and I do not believe that it is raining.
2. It is raining and I believe that it is not raining.
3. It is raining and I do not know that it is raining.
4. It is raining and she does not believe that it is raining.
5. It was raining and I did not believe that it was raining.
6. Sentence 1 written into a letter I seal today and open in a year.
7. Suppose it is raining and I do not believe that it is raining; my beliefs are then out of step with the weather. Also: it is not the case that it is raining and I do not believe that it is raining.
8. Probably it is raining and I do not believe that it is raining. It is possible that it is raining and I do not believe that it is raining.
9. It is raining and my credence that it is raining is 0.4.
10. A weather station prints: it is raining and this station does not register that it is raining. Stipulate that the station has no beliefs and asserts nothing.
11. Speaker A says that it is raining; speaker B says that A does not believe that it is raining.

One formal fact I want the list to engage rather than dodge: belief distributes over conjunction, so anyone who believed sentence 1 would believe its first conjunct and would thereby make its second conjunct false; the content is one that cannot be truly believed. Call a content with that profile belief-refuting.

The requirements should settle at least these points: whether sentence 1 is false, or only absurd to assert; whether it can be believed at all, and on what grounds; what separates the omissive form from the commissive one; what makes person and tense matter, given that 4, 5 and 6 look unremarkable; which embeddings cancel the absurdity and which do not, with a single explanation covering the pattern; whether the knowledge version in 3 belongs to the same phenomenon; whether the graded and credal cases in 8 and 9 fall under it or beside it; and what the account says about the station in 10. My provisional reactions, which an account may overrule if it gives a reason: 8's probability case sounds worse than 8's possibility case, and 9 sounds mild rather than absurd. Also require that the account not rest on infallible knowledge of one's own beliefs, since I want to keep the ordinary possibility of being wrong about oneself while still locating what is wrong with the Moorean case.
