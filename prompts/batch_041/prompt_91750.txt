I'm a second-year maths student and I've fallen down a rabbit hole on Skolem's paradox. I think I understand the mechanics fine: take ZF, if it's consistent it has a model, Löwenheim–Skolem hands you a countable one, and yet inside that model the sentence "there is an uncountable set" comes out true — the would-be bijection that would witness countability just isn't one of the model's own sets. Every exposition I find stops there and tells me it isn't really a paradox, that countability is only ever relative to a model. Fine. But I want to know what actually survives that, because I suspect some things do. What are the most important implications of the paradox — the ones that don't dissolve along with it? I'm especially chewing on three. First, whether there's any fact of the matter about how big a set is that's independent of which model or metatheory you happen to be standing in, or whether "the reals are uncountable" is ultimately just a claim about the ambient theory. Second, what it does to the idea that a formal system can pin down a determinate meaning for a word like "uncountable" at all, as opposed to leaving it hostage to interpretation. And third, the one that's been nagging me: if a machine or a person reasons purely by shuffling ZF-symbols around, could it be churning out all the right theorems while its word "uncountable" means something seperate from mine — and would that even matter, or is it a distinction without a difference?

Please don't just tell me the realist has replies and move on; I want the actual consequences spelled out and ranked by how hard they bite, and I'd like you to flag any of them that are really just confusions on my part rather than genuine implications. I'm also curious whether the whole thing changes if you go second-order, since I've read in passing that that's supposed to block it, but I don't trust my understanding of why. Also, if it turns out that some alleged implication only bites against a position nobody sensible holds, say so — I'd rather have fewer real consequences than a long list of ones that only worry people who beleive something silly.
