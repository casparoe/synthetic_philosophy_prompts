I've just gone through Schopenhauer on music (Book 3 of World as Will and Representation, I think?), and I want to know what I actually have hold of.

So do this for me. Take his theory of music and restate it point by point in the only vocabulary I'm fluent in: waveforms, spectra, the harmonic series, attack transients, critical bands and masking, the missing fundamental, why a violin and a flute on the same note don't sound alike. I want an honest translation, not a metaphor. Where he says music copies the Will itself while the other arts only copy the Ideas, what's the nearest thing in my terms — is it anything more than organized sound with no propositional content? Where he reads the texture as a picture of his own metaphysics, bass down at the bottom and melody up on top, am I just hearing a claim about which part of the spectrum attention latches onto?

Then the part I actually care about: what didnt survive. Which claims have no counterpart at all in acoustics, which only look like they translate (I suspect his harmony and my consonance are not the same animal), and which go vague once you strip the metaphysics off. And tell me whether the leftover is a blind spot in my vocabulary or a soft spot in his theory.
