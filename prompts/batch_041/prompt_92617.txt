Ok so here's where I've landed, and I want you to try to knock it down. I've become convinced that the rational move in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma — when the other player is genuinely reasoning the way I am, same info, same logic, no funny business — is to cooperate rather than defect. And more broadly I've bought the evidentialist picture: what makes an act rational isn't the outcome it causally brings about, it's what the act tells you about how things are. What sold me was Hofstadter's luring lottery, or at least my memory of it: everyone can mail in as many free entries as they want, and the prize gets divvied up based on how many entries there are in total, so we're all better off if each of us sends exactly one than if each of us stuffs the box, even though every individual is tempted to stuff it. Same structure as the twin dillema. You don't cooperate because it makes the other guy cooperate — you cooperate because your reasoning is his reasoning, so the same answer falls out on both ends. That feels airtight to me and I suspect it isn't.

So: what would count against it? I want the cases, arguments, intuitions, and formal results that should actually move me, ranked by how decisive they'd be, from fatal to merely awkward. I'm especially curious about the objection that the causalist in these setups walks away richer than I do — is that a real problem, or just sour grapes about one-shot luck? And about the weird self-referential cases where the evidentialist recommendation is unstable, where the act you should pick depends on whether you think you'll pick it. Which of these would a committed evidentialist concede as a genuine cost, versus wave away as a trick case? Be honest about whether the view has been patched into unfalsifiability by its standard fixes, and whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind or just the price of doing business.

Two more things I'd want addressed. Does the whole thing secretly collapse into some other position — a Kantian universalizability thing, or rule consequentialism in disguise? And if the superrational move only works when the other party is my twin, what happens when they're only approximately me? How much similarity is enough before cooperation stops paying, and is that threshold a principled thing or an arbitrary fudge?

Write it as continuous prose, please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just paragraphs the way you'd put it in an email.
