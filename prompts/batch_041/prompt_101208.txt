Fine, whatever. But Portugal in 2001 didn't legalize, it decriminalized: personal possession of small amounts stopped being a crime and became an administrative matter handled by these dissuasion commisions, trafficking stayed criminal, and at the same time they ramped up needle exchange, methadone, supervised consumption rooms and so on. So the state is still very much in the business of steering users around — just not through the prison system. I want a real case analysis, not a summary: who is the relevant actor here (the user, the commission, the dealer?), what exactly the coercion is at each step, and where Mill's framework actually has something to say versus where it just goes silent, because I suspect it was never a theory of regulation at all — only a threshold for when criminalization is allowed.

There's also the time-slice thing that eats at me: if I harm my future self, is that harm to others or harm to self, and does Mill's "individual" even survive being sliced into a sequence of selves?
