I am a high school student writing a long essay for my film club's spring showcase, and I would like you to take on the role of a philosopher of art who specializes in the aesthetics of tragedy — someone at home both in the classical debate about why audiences take pleasure in painful works and in the close reading of individual films. Before you write anything, please think carefully and brainstorm on the page: set out the main candidate solutions to the paradox of tragedy, give each one a fair statement in a sentence or two, and only then start deciding which of them can survive contact with the film I want you to read.

The film is Grave of the Fireflies, the 1988 Studio Ghibli feature written and directed by Isao Takahata, adapted from Akiyuki Nosaka's semi-autobiographical short story. I have watched it twice, and what I need is not a summary but a philosophical reading, so I will just flag the elements I care about most. The film opens with the boy dying in a train station and then runs the story as a memory: the two siblings, having lost their mother in the bombing of Kobe, move in with a resentful aunt and then leave to live in an abandoned air-raid shelter, where they catch fireflies for light; the fireflies are dead by morning, and the little girl buries them and asks why they had to die so soon. The boy is proud, refuses to go back to the aunt, steals, and lets his sister starve; she dies, he cremates her and keeps her ashes in a candy tin, and the film ends with the two of them as ghosts, healthy and calm, looking back at their own story from a bench above the modern city. The director rejected the label of anti-war film, and the story is anchored in real deaths: Nosaka lost his adoptive father and his home in the bombing of Kobe, and his young sister died of malnutrition in the weeks afterwards, of which he later described himself as having been preoccupied with his own survival and as having eaten food that should have gone to her.

What I keep running into is this. The paradox of tragedy as I have met it in class and in what I have read assumes that we are pleased by the painful work, or at least that we are drawn back to it, and the proposed solutions all seem to need one of those assumptions: Aristotle's catharsis, which says the painful emotions are purged or clarified; the account I associate with Hume, which says the pain is converted into pleasure by the eloquence and artistry of the telling; the suggestion that we are comforted by knowing it is only a performance; and the suggestion that what pleases us is our own response, the satisfaction of having been moved as the situation deserves. But Grave of the Fireflies is famous as a film people refuse to watch twice while insisting that everyone should watch it once, and in my club those were usually the same people. So I would like you to tell me which of the paradox's proposed solutions are even addressed to this case, which collapse into circularity or into something close to obscenity when the work is a memorial for children who really died, and whether the right conclusion is that the paradox is a real puzzle, a pseudo-problem, or a real puzzle badly stated.

Please organize the answer as follows. First, the menu: the distinct solutions, each stated precisely and each given a verdict — trivial, plausible, false, or simply not applicable here. Second, the reading: which of them actually illuminate the film, with reference to specific scenes and to what the film does with memory, pride, and the aunt, and where the film resists the reading and I should say so. Third, the verdict on the paradox itself. Fourth, and most valuable to me, what the film contributes to the philosophical debate that argument alone does not — whether the ghostly frame and the child's question about the fireflies dramatize something the theories of tragedy leave out.

Do not give me a bibliography, a reading list, or citations: I cannot look anything up, and I would rather have your reasoning than a list of names. If you are unsure of a detail about the film, do not assert it, and do not put anything in quotation marks unless you are certain of the wording — paraphrase instead. And please do not simply survey the positions: I want you to commit to a verdict and defend it, while being honest about the parts of the film that your verdict does not fit.
