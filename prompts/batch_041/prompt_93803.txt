I am an actuary by training, and I have a working command of the vocabulary of risk pooling, moral hazard, adverse selection, actuarial fairness, experience rating, self-insurance versus risk transfer, ex ante versus ex post compensation, and expected-value calculation over uncertain states of the world. My acquaintance with political philosophy is casual. I would like you to restate, entirely in the vocabulary I actually use, the debate internal to luck egalitarianism: the family of views holding that distributive justice should shield people from the effects of unchosen (brute) bad luck while leaving them to bear the consequences of their own deliberate gambles (option luck), together with the familiar objections that this stance is harsh toward the imprudent and demeaning toward the unfortunate.

Treat brute luck as a risk the individual did not knowingly underwrite, option luck as a risk knowingly retained, and the design of distributive institutions as the design of a compensation-and-pooling scheme. Be honest in the translation: do not quietly import actuarial assumptions -- that risk is fungible, that preferences are fixed and known, that any misfortune can in principle be priced and hedged -- and then present the result as if it were what the philosophers meant.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly? Is the brute/option luck distinction, for instance, just the question of who is the more efficient bearer of a given risk, so that the moral dispute collapses into an allocation question? Which claims survive only with distortion? And which cannot be expressed at all -- in particular, does the luck egalitarian's notion of responsibility, or of desert, or of the specific wrongness of leaving someone to a misfortune they did not choose, have any counterpart in a framework that treats every contingency as, in principle, a priceable one? Conclude by telling me whether the residue marks a defect of my vocabulary, in that insurance simply cannot say something that matters morally, or whether it is a sign that the philosophical claim was less determinate than it sounded -- that responsibility was doing rhetorical work which a sharper analysis dissolves.
