My little brother is getting married in about three weeks and I've somehow ended up giving the toast, which is scary on its own since I'm not a talker, but it's extra strange because of who they are. Both of them studied physics, both of them are the type to have a picture of Einstein on the fridge, and they pulled me aside and asked me to please not do the usual two things: not the 'you two were meant to find each other' stuff, and not the 'what a brave free choice you're making today' stuff. They said both of those are lies and they'd rather hear something true. Then my brother said, if you want to know what we actually think, go read what Einstein said about religion and about free will, because that's basically where we live. So I've spent two weeks on this and I'm definately in over my head. What I've got so far is: he didn't believe in a God who watches you and hands out rewards and punishments, but he also got genuinely irritated when people called him an atheist, and he talked about a kind of awe at the order of the world that he connected to Spinoza and to the feeling scientists get. And then seperately he said he didn't believe in free will at all, which is the part that's messing me up, because he still spent his life telling people to be decent and clearly thought it mattered what you do. My own half-baked theory is that the no-free-will thing is exactly why he was so kind, that he never blamed anybody and just tried to understand them, and I think that might be the thing to say to my grandmother. But I honestly dont know if that's his view or something I made up in the shower.

Here's what I want from you. Write the toast, roughly five minutes read out loud, for a room of about forty-five people. Half of them are his college and lab friends, who will notice instantly if I attribute something to Einstein that he didn't actually say, and the other half is our family, including my grandmother and a great-aunt who are churchgoers and are already braced for a godless wedding, plus two cousins who are around twelve and will be bored no matter what. So it has to land for people who know exactly what he thought and for people who only know the fridge magnet. And I need the Einstein stuff to do real work, not decorate. The reason they asked for it is that this is actually a hard toast to write: if you can't say they were fated to meet, and you can't say they freely chose each other, what is there to say about two people standing up and promising each other things for the rest of their lives? That's the question I want the toast to actually answer out loud, in words my grandmother can follow, and the answer has to be something his position can really support and not just something sweet. If the honest answer is that his view leaves him with nothing to say at a wedding, then say so, because then it has to be a different toast, maybe one about what promising means when nobody's keeping score. Somewhere in there I'd like a moment that turns to the grandparents directly, since they're the ones who need it most, and a moment for the two of them that takes their 'we didn't choose each other' line seriously instead of patting them on the head.

Two things about how you answer. First, no citations, no reading list, no recommended books, I'm not going to read anything else and I can't look things up anyway, and honestly I'd rather you put everything in plain words rather than quoting him, because I have no way to check a quote and if I misquote Einstein at my brother's wedding his friends will never let it go, so if you do use his actual words anywhere, tell me loud and clear which ones those are. Second, and this matters more to me: be upfront about which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely argued about by people who know the subject. Like whether it's even fair to call him a Spinozist, or whether he ever actually worked out how his determinism squares with him telling people to be good, or whether my shower theory about his kindness is something he said or just something I want to be true. Don't smooth those over and don't hand me the contested version as if everyone agrees, and if the toast takes a liberty with any of it, I want to know where.
