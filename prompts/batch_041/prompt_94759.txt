Alright, I have to give a comment at a workshop on cultural evolution next month and I want to get my own position straight before I open my mouth, so I'm writing it out as a list and asking you to audit it. Here's where I've landed:

1. Culture is a genuinely Darwinian process. Heritable variation in what gets transmitted, differential transmission, lineages — the whole thing, and I take the substrate-neutral reading of it: one abstract algorithm, many realisers, so the fact that what's being transmitted is ideas rather than nucleotides is beside the point.

2. The meme is still the right unit for this. If differential transmission is going to do real explanatory work you need a replicator, i.e. something whose causal role is to get itself copied, with enough fidelity that you can trace lineages and let selection accumulate small improvements over generations. That fidelity is also what underwrites cumulative culture — the ratchet, where each generation starts from the last one's gains instead of from scratch.

3. But I also think cultural inheritance is reconstructive rather than replicative. The learner rebuilds the item out of cues and her own priors; it isn't copied off the model. So fidelity is low, lineages blend, and what survives a transmission event is partly a product of the reciever's own dispositions rather than of the item transmitted.

4. And cultural evolution is Lamarckian in a way genetic evolution isn't — acquired modifications get passed on — which I take to be the main structural diference between the two.

5. The best explanation we have for the spread of genuinely costly prosocial norms is cultural group selection: groups whose norms made them better at coordinating and outcompeting their neighbours spread at the expense of groups without them.

6. At the same time I'm fairly convinced most cultural change isn't adaptive in any interesting sense — it's drift plus content biases and prestige bias (copying whoever's high-status) — so for most of what we observe I'd rather not invoke selection for group benefit at all.

Which of these actually conflict, which only look like they do, and how bad is each conflict? Order them by severity, give me the cheapest repair for each real one, and say honestly what the repair costs me. I genuinely don't know which of the six is the weak link, so don't be diplomatic about it.
