I retired from a career in the physical sciences a few years ago and have since been reading my way, rather unsystematically, into the philosophical literature on self-locating belief. Before I commit anything to paper, I would like a candid audit of where I have ended up, because I half suspect that some of these commitments are quietly incompatible and I would rather be told now than by a referee.

Here is the package.

(i) I accept the self-sampling assumption: in reasoning about my own place among observers, I should proceed as though I had been drawn uniformly at random from the actually existing observers in my reference class.

(ii) I find the Doomsday argument broadly sound: that I find myself roughly the sixty-billionth human, rather than one of a vastly larger total, should lower my estimate of how many humans there will ever be.

(iii) On the Sleeping Beauty problem — a fair coin determines whether I am woken once or twice, with my memory erased between wakings — I side with the thirders: on waking, my credence in heads is one third.

(iv) I am not a self-indicationer. I do not think a hypothesis gains credibility merely by positing more observers, and I treat the following as a reductio: a philosopher argues that because a theory under which the universe contains vastly more people makes his own existence vastly more probable, that theory is almost certainly true.

(v) I take self-locating reasoning to be objective: there is a single correct answer to such questions, one that does not shift with how the reasoner happens to delimit the reference class.

Would you tell me which of these genuinely conflict and which merely appear to? I would like the real tensions ranked by severity, each with an account of what would have to give to repair it and what that concession would cost; the merely apparent tensions identified as such, with a word on why they dissolve; and, if one of the genuine conflicts is doing most of the work, a note on which single revision would clear up the most. I have no stake in any particular item surviving the audit.
