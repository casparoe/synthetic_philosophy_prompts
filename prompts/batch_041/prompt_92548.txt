I'm leading a book club on H.G. Wells's The Invisible Man next month and want to read it against Glaucon's challenge in book 2 of the Republic -- the ring of Gyges, the claim that anyone with perfect impunity would act unjustly, so justice is just a bargain the weak strike. Wells basically runs the experiment: Griffin gets invisibility and goes on a rampage. But the novel seems to cheat in two interesting ways. Glaucon hands the ring to a just man and asks what he'd do; Wells hands it to someone already rotten -- he robs his own father, who kills himself -- so the experiment varies the person, not just the incentive. And his invisibility leaks: footprints in the snow, mud, food sitting visibly in his stomach, so he's never really unimpeded. So does the book fail as a test of Glaucon, or does it show something the thought experiment can't -- that being unseen is itself corrosive, so a fully social person who just happens to go unobserved isn't really imaginable? And where does it land: with Glaucon, with Socrates, or with neither, given that the mob that beats him to death looks alot more like punishment catching up than justice loved for its own sake? Which scenes should I put in front of the group, and where does the reading have to admit the novel and the Plato just don't line up?
