I teach a 300-level seminar on rational choice and decision theory — 16 students, one 90-minute session — and I want to run something where they *feel* the money pump from the inside instead of nodding along at the argument. By the end I want them to have been walked around a preference cycle by somebody else and to have paid for the privilege, and then in the debrief to see exactly what happened to them and why anyone thinks it's a problem.

The thing I'm worried about is the usual version, where you just tell them "imagine your preferences go A over B, B over C, C over A" and then have them act it out. That's a role-play, not a trap. I want them to actually hold the cycle — most of them, at least — without noticing it until afterward. My plan is to hand out three small real prizes (cheap, tactile, snacks or little goody bags) each described by three attributes, and rig the attribute rankings so that a person who decides pairwise by "which one wins on more attributes" ends up with A over B, B over C, and C over A. If that works, then a broker offering to swap your current item for the one you prefer, for a small fee in tokens that later buy something small, will walk them around the cycle and back to the start, minus fees, with every individual trade being one they'd happily endorse at the moment they make it. I'd like you to make this actually work on the page — the exact three attributes and their rankings, the comparison sheet, the broker's offers, the token economy, and a timeline for the 90 minutes.

Two things I want built in beyond the basic pump. First, a second short round that's a *different* pump: something like an escalating dial where every notch up is a clearly worthwhile trade (small reward, small cost) but going all the way to the top is obviously awful — so the debrief can separate "you got pumped because your preferences cycle" from "you got pumped because your preferences shift over time," since those are different diagnoses with different fixes. Second, the debrief has to deal head-on with the student who refuses the first trade: if they can see the cycle coming and just sit still, is that rational, or does rationality now require them to act against the preference they have right in front of them? I want the activity to make the pump feel damning and then have the debrief be honest that exploitability being the test of irrationality is itself contested.

So: rules, materials, the comparison sheet spec, the broker's script, the payoff scheme (no real money), the timeline, and the debrief that maps each moment onto the pieces of the idea. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it — I already expect some students won't cycle and a couple will smell the trap early. And finish with a frank note on what this activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself. Write it like you're handing me a lesson plan I can run cold.
