I am a retired palaeontologist who has, over the past few years, been reading philosophy in a somewhat unsystematic way. I have become interested in the ancient debate over sortition, and I have hit a passage in Aristotle’s Politics that I cannot get straight. Since I do not have a critical edition to hand and my Greek is non-existent, I shall paraphrase the passage as I have it from a translation I read some months ago, and I would be grateful if you would help me understand it. The passage, in my paraphrase, goes roughly as follows: Aristotle is distinguishing between the way different constitutions appoint to office. He says that it is agreed to be democratic to appoint by lot, and oligarchical to appoint by election; and he adds that a mixture of the two is characteristic of a polity or constitutional government. He then seems to suggest that the lot is democratic because it treats all citizens as equal, whereas election is oligarchical because it allows the better sort to be chosen. Yet he also criticises the democracies that use the lot for offices requiring expertise, and he seems to think that a well-constructed constitution will use election for some offices and the lot for others. What I cannot understand is how these claims hang together. If the lot is democratic because it embodies equality, and election is oligarchical because it discriminates, then why would a good constitution mix them rather than simply choose the one that is just? Is Aristotle making a claim about justice, or about stability, or about the division of labour among institutions?

I am asking this not only as a historical question. I have been working out a proposal of my own for a lottocratic assembly, and I want to know whether Aristotle’s passage helps or hinders it. My proposal is a two-stage body: first, a large pool is selected by lot from the citizen body; second, the members of that pool elect from among themselves a smaller steering committee and a chair. The idea is to combine the equalising effect of the lot with a filter for competence and deliberative skill. I had thought this was a straightforward way to get the best of both worlds. But reading Aristotle makes me wonder whether the two devices are not merely different selection mechanisms but different principles of legitimacy, and whether combining them produces a coherent principle or merely a compromise that will be unstable. First, explain the passage and the distinction between lot and election as Aristotle uses it, including what you take to be the strongest reading of his position and the main interpretive difficulties. Second, evaluate my two-stage proposal in the light of that reading: does it capture anything like Aristotle’s mixed constitution, or does it conflate two incompatible ideas? Be explicit about how confident you are in your interpretation, and single out the point in your answer about which you are least sure.
