I'm prepping a session on costly signaling and I'm so sick of peacock tails. The textbook story is that the signal is honest because the signaler eats the cost himself. I want a story that leans on that hard. Premise I've been chewing on: what if the cost gets externalized instead? Somebody proves they're committed by destroying something the whole group depends on — a reservoir, a granary, the only bridge out of town — so it's a signal no bluffer would ever send, except the people reading it are the ones paying for it. Is that still a handicap signal, or is it a threat wearing a signals clothes? Write me a short story, maybe 1500-2500 words, set somewhere specific enough that the cost feels real and itemized rather than abstract. Nobody should explain the theory out loud in dialogue, and please don't end with a tidy moral — I want the reader to have to figure out on their own whether the character is trustworthy, coercive, or somehow both at once, and whether the people around them had any real option to ignore the signal. Continuous prose only, no headings or bullet points or bold text.
