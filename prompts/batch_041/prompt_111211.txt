Ok so I've been messing around with a little analogy toy that I think sits right on top of the Copycat / FARG idea of conceptual slippage — you know, the Hofstadter and Melanie Mitchell letter-string thing where abc turning into abd is the seed, then you ask what happens to ijk, and all of a sudden 'make the last letter the next letter' vs 'change the last letter into d' vs 'extend the whole pattern' pull apart from each other, and which one wins depends on what makes the rest of the analogy hang together.

My toy domain is short melodies written as scale degrees in a major key. Seed: 1 2 3 goes to 1 2 4 — do re mi becomes do re fa, i.e. the last note gets raised a step. Now the target: 3 4 5, mi fa sol. And already 'the same change' splits three ways. Raise the last degree by one scale step: 3 4 6, stays in the key. Transpose the whole thing up a step: 4 5 6, also stays in the key. Or keep the exact semitone pattern and slide it chromatically — mi-fa is a semitone and fa-sol is a whole tone, so shifting that up a semitone lands you on notes the key doesn't even contain. So 'raise', 'same', 'last', 'step' — every one of those little concepts has a neighborhood of near misses, and which reading counts as the real one seems to depend on which one makes the rest of the analogy cohere. Same story if I write the melodies as up/down contour instead of scale degrees, or as raw MIDI numbers.
