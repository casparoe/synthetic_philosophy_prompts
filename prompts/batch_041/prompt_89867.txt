I'm a novelist, three books in, no philosophy training past a couple of undergrad survey courses, and I've got a working vocabulary for people: what a character wants vs. what they need, the lie they believe about themselves, backstory, arc, the second-act turn, unreliable narration, subtext, the gap between what someone does and the reason they give for doing it. I've read Nietzsche's genealogy of morals twice now and the strange thing is it reads to me like craft advice. So do me a favour and restate the whole thing in my language, as faithfully as you can, move by move, and then tell me what didnt survive the trip.

The moves I want translated: the strong calling their strength good and the weak calling their weakness good, with that second one being a revaluation done out of resentment rather than an honest appraisal; resentment itself as the engine of that revaluation; guilt and bad conscience as aggression turned inward when it couldnt go outward; the ascetic ideal as a way of still having a project once you've lost everything else; and the method itself, the thought that showing how a value came to be tells you something about what its worth.

Then the residue report. Here's what I already suspect doesn't come across, tell me if I'm right: in fiction, a backstory explains a character without debunking them. Knowing why someone turned out like that is what makes them legible, even loveable; it never seems to lower the value of what they believe. Nietzsche seems to want the origin story to take the value down a peg. Is that a real difference in the objects, or does my vocabulary just lack a move — some way of seperating explaining a thing from explaining it away? And what about the slide from meaning-for-a-person to function-for-a-type, does any of that survive when you force it through a novelists terms?

Last thing: is the residue a hole in my vocabulary, or a sign the original was less clear than it looked?
