ok so I just spent like three hours arguing with my friend Jenna about psychiatric diagnosis and I need someone to actually lay the thing out properly, because we kept sliding past each other and I'm not sure I lost but I definately didn't win.

Heres where we got stuck. I said the interesting thing about psychiatric categories is that they're what Hacking called interactive kinds. The people being classified can find out how they're being classified, and then they change: they start noticing symptoms they hadn't noticed, they reinterpret their whole life through the label, they join communities organized around it, and sometimes the actual presentation of the thing shifts over a generation. So the category is partly making up the people it describes rather than just picking out a kind that was already sitting there. Jenna said fine, but that happens with everything. Gifted kid. Left-handed. Introvert. Only child. Middle class. Any category people can apply to themselves and then act on will loop back on itself. So looping can't be what makes psychiatric classification special, and if the arguement is just "psychiatric categories have looping effects, therefore they aren't natural kinds," then the premise is true of tons of categories nobody thinks are fake.

Where I think I maybe have a reply: the categories that loop without anyone worrying are ones that never claimed to be tracking a nature in the first place. Nobody thinks middle class is a biological kind. But a psychiatric category is supposed to be picking out a dysfunction in the person, something that would be there whether or not we'd invented the word, and it's exactly that claim the loop seems to undercut, because the loop runs through the very thing the category says is independent of it. Jenna's counter was sharper than I expected: she said, fine, then give me a test. A category is real if it survives the loop being cut, i.e. if you could delete the word tomorrow and the people would still be there, unchanged, waiting to be re-found. She thinks the contested diagnoses fail that test and the obvious ones pass it, and that this is all the social-construction talk ever amounts to. I said that test is doing way more work than she thinks and I couldn't say why.

The bit I really couldn't answer is the mixed bag. Down syndrome and Huntington's are sitting in the same manual as stuff that looks like it's mostly held up by the loop, and the same clinics, the same diagnostic codes, the same waiting rooms treat them as the same sort of thing. If looping only hits some of the manual, does the critique of diagnosis survive at all, or does it just become a claim about a few categories that happen to be trendy?

What I want: an essay that takes a position. Not "it's complicated, both sides have a point." I want a verdict on whether looping effects actually show anything about psychiatric categories being socially constructed or non-natural, and if they do, exactly what they show and what they don't. If you think the argument survives Jenna's "but everything loops" objection, say what the loop in psychiatry has that the loop around introvert doesn't, and say whether her delete-the-word test is the right test or a bad one. If you think it doesn't survive, say what's left of the critique of diagnosis once you give that up. And deal with the mixed-bag problem, since that's the part I actually got beaten on. Reach a conclusion and defend it.
