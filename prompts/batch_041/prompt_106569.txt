I was at a workshop last week and ended up arguing with a colleague over drinks about whether we should weight the far future more heavily, and he pulled a move that I've now seen in about five different arguements and have never had a name for. It goes like this: look, he says, if there end up being a hundred trillion people spread across the future and only ten billion of us around now, then the odds are I'm one of the hundred trillion rather than one of the ten billion — so I should expect to be a future person, and the future is where the moral weight sits. Part of me felt the pull of it, but another part felt he'd won just by positing a big number, as if wanting there to be more people made it more likely that he's one of them. And you can run the same move anywhere: if most observers turn out to be simulated, then I'm probably simulated; if God laid out a million universes and only one of them contains me... The funny thing is it doesn't seem to need the numbers to be actual, just possible.

He insists it's all just ordinary reasoning about being a random member of a group — the same thing you do when you say a ball pulled from an urn is probably red because there are more reds in there. I can see that, but I keep feeling there are two different moves bundled up in his version: one where you count the members who actually exist, and one where you count the members who could exist, and that second one seems to let you prove whatever you want just by inflating the crowd you'd be a member of. I teach a seminar on decision theory, so I'm a little embarrassed I don't have the vocabulary for this. So — does this have an established name, or do the two things I'm trying to separate have names? And more to the point, where exactly does the line run between the honest version and the trick version? Is it about which observers you're allowed to count, or about what it even means to be a 'random' one of them, or something else? I don't want a bare label; I want to know what's actually doing the work, and why the inflating version strikes even people who happily accept the honest version as a straight-up cheat.
