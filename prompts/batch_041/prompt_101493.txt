I'm a postdoc and next month I have to give a paper at a small two-day workshop on levels, explanation and reduction -- the kind of thing where everyone knows each other. The room is roughly two camps. Camp one is philosophers of biology who grew up on Dennett, a couple of them basically have the crane/skyhook vocabulary wired into their reflexes, and they'll be polite but they think the distinction is one of the great clarifying moves and that anyone who questions it is either confused or secretly a creationist. Camp two is three or four people from a systems-bio / complexity institute who think emergence is a real, measurable phenomenon and who get visibly irritated when a philosopher says it's just a slogan; they don't know the Dennett stuff at all and don't care about it, they just think the philosophers are doing word-games while they're computing things.

My paper is basically this: the crane/skyhook distinction, as it's actually used in practice, does no independent work. Nobody ever discovers a skyhook; what happens is you decide an explanation is bad, and then you call it a skyhook, and the label adds nothing to the reasons you already had. Same for greedy reductionism -- it's defined by taste, not by any criterion, so it just means "reduction I find distasteful." To keep it from being a pure complaint I've bolted on a positive criterion of my own. Roughly: an explanation is a crane only if you can lay out the steps and each step is independently attested somewhere else -- some other system, some other lineage, some other domain -- so that no single step exists only to make the endpoint come out. If a step is attested nowhere except in the story that needs it, the story is a skyhook no matter how gradual it sounds. I run this against two cases to show it cuts in directions Dennett wouldn't like: (a) the standard RNA-world-to-genetic-code narrative, where I claim the codon-assignment step is a skyhook by my criterion even though everyone treats it as the paradigm crane, and (b) the Menger-style "money emerged spontaneously out of barter" story, which I claim comes out as a crane by my criterion even though the people who like that story would be horrified to hear it called gradualist. I also spend a section arguing that the complexity camp's insistence that there's a formal information-theoretic measure of emergence doesn't rescue the crane/skyhook talk, because the measure and the label are answering different questions.

Draft me my Q&A prep sheet. I don't want a defense of the paper, I want to know what's coming. Give me the objections this specific room will actually raise, roughly in the order they'll come and with a sense of how much damage each one does, phrased the way camp one would phrase it and separately the way camp two would phrase it -- their versions will not sound alike at all and I need to hear both. For each one, a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft or where I'm bluffing. Tell me what I should concede up front, what I should refuse to concede, and which objection is the one I'm praying nobody asks, plus how I should handle it if someone does.
