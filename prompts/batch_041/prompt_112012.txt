Hey, I'm putting together a bunch of discussion prompts and could use your help on one. Please take the role of someone who's spent years working in the metaphysics of personal identity -- persistence, fission, uploading, all of that -- and think carefully before you answer; maybe brainstorm a few candidate labels first, then tell me which one, if any, actually earns its keep.

Here's the thing I keep running into and I dont have a word for it. In arguments about whether you survive being gradually replaced -- neuron by neuron swapped for silicon, say, or a brain rebuilt over months -- people on both sides keep doing this thing where they slide between two different ways of deciding how many people are present. By one way of counting, the person is a single continuous thing that persists through the whole process; by the other way of counting, there comes a point where the old person is gone and a new one has started. Each way, on its own, is perfectly coherent. The trouble is that a single argument will use the first way to say 'obviously you persist through step 47', and then quietly use the second way to say 'obviously the thing at the end isnt you', and it never notices that it switched. It's like an equivocation, except its not a word that changes meaning -- it's the criterion for what counts as one person that changes, mid-argument, usually without anyone flagging it. I've seen the same move in fission cases and in arguments about whether two half-brains are one person or two.

So: is there an established name for this? If there is, what is it, and what's the nearest thing that isnt it -- I assume it's somewhere around the problem of the many, and around sorites-style stuff, and around plain old equivocation, but I want to know exactly where the lines fall. And if there isnt a name, could you coin one and define it tightly enough that I can tell it apart from those neighbours and say where its edges are -- does the switch have to go unnoticed, do both criteria have to be independently legitimate, what happens if the person doing it is fully aware? Don't worry about citations, I just want the carving.
