I'm a science-and-ideas writer putting together a long feature on the philosophy of pleasure, and I keep running into a problem I can't decide is a genuine crisis or a bit of philosophical throat-clearing. Hedonism — the view that pleasure is the one thing that is good for us — needs "pleasure" to name something; but when you actually look at the things we call pleasures (the taste of a ripe peach, the resolution of a proof, the ache of a good stretch, the relief when a headache lifts), it is hard to say what they have in common. There seems to be no single sensation-quality they all share, and the usual fallback — that they are all "wanted" or "liked" — threatens to change the subject from what pleasure feels like to what we do about it.

I want your honest credence — a number, not a survey of positions — that there is a single substantive property in virtue of which all and only pleasures are pleasures; that is, that pleasure is a unified kind rather than a disjunctive list held together by a word. Then decompose it. Give me rough probabilities for at least the following sub-questions, plus any others you think belong: (1) whether some distinctive felt quality — a "hedonic tone" — is common to every pleasure; (2) whether, failing that, an attitudinal or functional account (pleasure as the object of a pro-attitude, say) can unify the class without collapsing into circularity, or into a theory of desire rather than of pleasure; (3) whether the kind we care about is phenomenal or evaluative — whether hedonism even needs a single felt property, or only a single source of value; and (4) whether the class might be unified "determinably" rather than "determinately." On that last point a colleague has been pressing an analogy I cannot assess: she suggests that pleasure might be a determinable — like color, where red and blue need share nothing but their status as determinates of one determinable — so that pleasures would require no common felt core, only a common role as specifications of a single determinable. Tell me whether that analogy is doing real explanatory work or quietly assuming the unity it is meant to earn, and fold your verdict into the relevant term.

Finally, tell me which single sub-question your overall number is most sensitive to, and what argument or piece of evidence would move it most. I'd rather have a defensible decomposition with one weak joint flagged than a confident number I can't interrogate on the page.
