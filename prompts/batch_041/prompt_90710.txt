I'm a novelist — literary fiction, four books, and for the last two years I've been living inside the world of a new project, keeping what my agent calls a world bible and what I privately call the bedrock file: the two or three hundred entries that I treat as simply given, from which everything else about the place is supposed to follow. Over dinner after a reading, a philosopher friend told me that my bedrock file and my habit of deriving consequences from it are doing exactly the work of the contemporary debate about grounding and fundamentality, and I've spent my evenings since trying to decide whether he was being generous or was onto something. So here is what I want from you. Take that debate as it currently stands — the thesis that some facts obtain in virtue of others and that this in-virtue-of relation is a genuine, unitary, non-causal, non-identity form of dependence with formal features like asymmetry, irreflexivity and transitivity; the further thesis that some facts are fundamental in the sense of being ungrounded, and that these are the bedrock of reality; the disagreement between those who think the fundamental is one thing and those who think it is many; the objection that grounding is a single word doing a family of quite different jobs and ought to be replaced by a plurality of specific dependence relations; and the live possibility that there is no fundamental level at all, that the chain of in-virtue-of descends without end — and restate all of it, as faithfully as you can manage, in the vocabulary I actually think in: canon versus apocrypha, seed facts versus consequences, stipulation versus what merely falls out, the world bible and its bedrock entries, retconning, foreshadowing, the difference between what the reader is told and what is true in the fiction, and the logic of the story by which one thing in a narrative is what makes another thing so.

Be strict about it. I have read enough metaphysics to follow the arguments but I am not a philosopher, and I will notice if you quietly import the assumptions of my craft and call the result a translation. Then give me the residue report, and treat the residue as the point of the exercise rather than as an embarrassment: which of the metaphysicians' claims come across cleanly, which come across only with distortion and what exactly has to be bent, and which have no expression in my vocabulary whatsoever. My own suspicion, which I'd like you to confirm or overturn, is that everything I have is reader-facing — my categories are all about what can be shown, inferred, or withheld from someone encountering the story, never about what makes a fact in it obtain — and I want to know whether that is a real limitation of the novelist's vocabulary or whether it exposes something the philosophers themselves have left vague: whether I can represent fundamentality at all, or whether my craft has only relative priority and no bedrock, only ever a deeper stratum and never a floor. Close by telling me which of these questions I should stop trying to answer in my own terms.
