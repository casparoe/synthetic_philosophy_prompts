I'm drafting a position paper for a seminar on epistemic rationality and want a consistency check before I commit to it. Here is what I hold. Accuracy, understood as closeness to truth, is the only fundamental epistemic good; coherence, explanatory power, and understanding matter only insofar as they conduce to accuracy. The fundamental norm governing credence is to maximize expected accuracy, where expected accuracy is computed from the agent's own credences. Probabilism is not a brute constraint but is justified by a dominance argument: incoherent credence functions are accuracy-dominated by coherent ones, and that is why coherence is required. Accuracy is to be measured by a strictly proper scoring rule, such as the Brier or the logarithmic score. Conditionalization is justified because updating by it maximizes expected accuracy from the agent's prior. And I want to insist that an agent can be perfectly coherent and still epistemically irrational: someone who never updates on evidence, who is dogmatic and closed to all information, is doing something wrong, and accuracy-first epistemology should be able to say what.

I have a nagging sense that not all of this can be true at once, but I can't tell which parts are actually pulling against each other and which only look like they do. Please audit the set: tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension and rank those tensions by severity, say which apparent conflicts dissolve once you look closely, and for each real tension identify the cheapest way to restore consistency and what that fix costs me. Don't just list problems; tell me which single commitment I should be most prepared to revise and why. Please write it in continuous prose — no headings, bullets, bold, or tables, just the kind of explanation you'd give in a long email.
