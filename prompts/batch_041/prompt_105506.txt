I've fallen down the Newcomb's problem rabbit hole — the boxes, the predictor, the million and the thousand — and I can't stop flip-flopping. When I picture the predictor as just a really good statistician who's usually right, I two-box; when I picture it as a perfect simulator of me, so that my choice is already baked in, I one-box. Whats wierd is that everyone agrees on the setup and the numbers, its been argued for decades, and smart people still land on opposite sides and never budge. My hunch is that it isn't really a disagreement about the right answer but about what a "decision theory" is even supposed to deliver — a rule for which act is rational given what you know, or a rule for what kind of agent to be. Is that the real fault line, or is something else doing the work — a hidden disagreement about whether the prediction is settled before you choose, or about what "rational" even means, or two camps talking past each other because the same word means different things to them? Ground the diagnosis in the moves each side actually makes, and tell me honestly whether anything could move this — a distinction, a proof, a new twist on the puzzle — or whether it's just permanently stuck.
