Elizabeth Anscombe objected to Oxford's conferring an honorary degree on Truman, arguing that he was a murderer for ordering the atomic bombings. Yet she was not a pacifist, and she held that killing in war can sometimes be justified; so her objection must come down to the scale of the civilian deaths — that the bombings were disproportionate or indiscriminate. But if killing civilians as a side effect is sometimes permissible, why should the atomic bombings count as murder, while the conventional firebombing of cities, which killed civilians on a comparable scale, does not? I would like to understand what she took to make Truman's decision murder rather than a tragic but permissible act.
