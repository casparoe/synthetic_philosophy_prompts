For about a year now a small discussion group I go to at the public library -- none of us has any training in philosophy -- has been stuck on one question: when someone who seems just as careful and well-informed as you disagrees with you, should you move toward their view, or may you keep your own? One side says you must split the difference; refusing is just arrogance, and if you never adjust for an equal's disagreement you are treating yourself as the one person who cannot be wrong. The other side says an equally matched opponent gives me no reason to budge, because the only way I could decide their reasoning is better than mine is by judging it with the very faculty whose verdict is in dispute -- and that the split-the-difference rule, applied to itself, tells the first side to move toward us, since we are their equals and we hold the opposite view. We tried to sharpen it: suppose the other person has a long record of having been right. Side one said weight their opinion accordingly; side two said that only moves the problem, since the only way I can know their record is good is by trusting my own judgment about what is true -- I am still measuring them with my own ruler.

Nobody has moved an inch, and my own suspicion is that the argument is not really about disagreement at all. It is about something upstream: whether a given body of evidence permits exactly one reasonable conclusion or can permit several. If it permits only one, then one of the two peers must be making a mistake, and splitting the difference looks compulsory; if it permits several, both can be reasonable and there is nothing to split. Or perhaps the two sides are answering different questions under one heading -- what makes a belief reasonable, versus what to do once you know you are unsure.

Please diagnose this rather than just granting each side a point. Is my suspicion right, or is the real fault line somewhere else -- a verbal dispute over the word reasonable, an unnoticed factual assumption about how people's judgments are correlated, or a disagreement about what rationality is in the first place? Tell me what kind of disagreement this is, and commit to it: what would count as progress here, and is the stalemate a sign that the question is badly posed? I would rather have a definite verdict with reasons than a list of options.
