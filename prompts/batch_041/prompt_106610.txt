Write a sestina for a graduate seminar in environmental ethics, on the precautionary principle. Use six end-words drawn from the principle's own vocabulary — for instance threat, doubt, proof, burden, wait, act — and let the form's fixed permutation do the argumentative work: the same terms should return for each new imagined technology the way the same demand returns for each new risk, never resolved, only re-arranged. By the closing stanzas the words should have shifted places enough that proof is finally being demanded of inaction as much as of action. The poem should hold in view both the weak reading (uncertainty is not by itself a reason to postpone measures) and the strong reading (the burden of proof falls on whoever proposes the activity), and should dramatize the gap between them. It should take the standard objection seriously — that applied without a stopping rule the principle is incoherent or paralyzing, since waiting is itself an action with its own uncertain costs — and leave that objection standing rather than settling it. The result should read as accurate to someone who knows the debate and as a real poem to someone who has never heard of the principle. Write in American English.
