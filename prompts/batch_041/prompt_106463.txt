Right, so this is a bit of an odd one but I've been going round in circles on it and I need a second brain. A friend of mine, Sam, has gotten really into evolution over the last year or so, mostly off podcasts and internet arguments, and she has one move she makes constantly. Whenever some trait of a living thing comes up she asks: is this an adaptation, meaning evolution actually built it for a job, or is it just a byproduct, a side effect of something else? She calls the byproducts "spandrels" — she got it from some old paper about the blank spaces you're forced to fill in under the arches of a cathedral dome — and her whole sermon is that not everything is an adaptation, half of what people call adaptations are really just spandrels. Fine, fair enough. The trouble is I've now collected enough of her actual verdicts that I feel like I should be able to work out what rule she's really running, and I can't.

Things she says ARE adaptations (built for a job):

- the eye
- the panda's "thumb", the fake sixth finger
- getting a fever when your ill
- the white part of the human eye, the bit that makes it easy to see where someone's looking
- sweating
- goosebumps when your cold

Things she says are NOT adaptations, just side effects:

- male nipples
- the human chin
- belly buttons, obviously
- the fact that blood is red
- goosebumps when you hear a song you love — she says that's just the cold machinery going off at the wrong time

Now next week three things are coming up and I genuinely cannot predict her. First, the appendix — I've read that it might actually be doing something for us now, housing good gut bacteria and that, even if it's basically a leftover of a bigger gut chamber our ancestors had. Second, our craving for sugar and fat — clearly something evolution favoured back when food was scarce, but it's definately not doing us any favours now. Third, morning sickness — I read somewhere that it might not be a malfunction at all, that it could be protecting the baby from stuff in food.

So what will she say about each of those? I'm less interested in the answers than in the rule she's actually running, the other rules that fit every verdict above just as well, what each of those rules predicts about the appendix, the sugar craving, and morning sickness, which rule you'd bet she's really on and why, and which of those three cases — or, if none of them does it, what other trait we haven't talked about — would actually tell the rules apart. Basically I want to know whether she's asking "did evolution build this for something", or "does it do something useful now", or "is there real evidence of selection, otherwise I'm calling it a spandrel", or something else entirely. And if the honest answer is that she's just inconsistent, say so.
