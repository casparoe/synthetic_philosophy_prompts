I have been trying to write up a short piece on the quarrel between Great Man history and structural explanation, and I keep arriving at an impasse that I would like you to diagnose rather than resolve. The stock case is the counterfactual: would there have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler? Both sides accept the same record — the fragility of the Weimar Republic, the Depression, the parliamentary deadlock, the conservative elites around Hindenburg who believed they could use the movement and be used by it; and on the other side, Hitler's particular ideological fixations, his timing, his appetite for gambles between 1932 and 1934, his cultivation of personal allegiance rather than a party programme. Nobody is contesting facts. Yet the exchange goes round: the structural side holds that the pressures would have produced some authoritarian or fascist regime whoever stood at its head, and treats the intentionalist–structuralist split within the historiography of the Third Reich as a local instance of the same argument; the other side holds that the thing we are actually asking about — the regime that industrialized murder — is unintelligible apart from this man.

I do not want a verdict on whether Hitler was decisive. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement, and I want you to test my hypothesis rather than adopt it. My hypothesis is that one question is doing the work of two — an occurrence question (would some comparable regime have consolidated?) and a form question (would it have had these features?) — and that each side has quietly taken one of them. If that is right, is the debate verbal, or does the split merely relocate the problem, since the form claim is about a particular event-token and inherits the same evidential difficulty? And what is that difficulty, exactly? I can see at least three candidates: that the antecedent is underdetermined (Hitler never born, Hitler dead in 1923, Hitler without the racial ideology are all "without Hitler" and license different answers); that there is no second run and no controlled comparison, so such claims are answerable only relative to a similarity metric on possible worlds that we choose rather than discover; and that causes may be contrast-relative, so that the two sides are not even disagreeing about the same why-question. I would also like to know whether part of the heat is non-epistemic — whether one side hears structural explanation as exculpation and the other hears Great Man explanation as moralizing — and whether that can be separated from the argument proper.

Reason from the structure of the two positions rather than from a survey of the literature. Commit to a diagnosis; tell me where your reasoning is most likely to fail, and what argument or consideration would move you to a different one.
