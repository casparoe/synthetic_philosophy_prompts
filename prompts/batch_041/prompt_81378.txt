Since moving in with my partner, who eats meat, I have had to say out loud why I have been vegetarian for years, and the conversation has gone badly in a way I did not anticipate. My sister asked whether I still intend to fly to her wedding in another country; when I said yes, she told me she now understood my vegetarianism to be a matter of taste rather than conviction. I do not think she is right, but I also cannot produce the principle that distinguishes the two cases, and I have begun to suspect that the package of views I actually hold is not jointly tenable. What I want from you is an audit rather than a verdict: which of the following pull against each other, how badly, and for each conflict what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me.

The package is this. (1) In a national election, my single vote has no realistic chance of deciding anything, and I do not think there is a duty to vote that can be grounded in the difference one vote would make; I vote, but I would describe it as expressive or civic rather than consequential, and I do not think a person who stays home has done anything wrong. (2) I have been vegetarian for years, and my reason is not my market impact — I accept that if I stopped buying, roughly as many animals would be raised and killed — but that I do not want to be a participant in the practice, since my demand and my money are part of what keeps the system running. (3) I am flying long-haul next month and I do not think I am doing anything wrong, for the same reason as in (1): the emissions attributable to my seat are imperceptible against the global total, and I cannot see how an obligation attaches to an act that makes no detectable difference to anything. (4) As a general criterion, I am drawn to what I have been calling the replaceability test: if removing my act leaves the harm exactly as it would otherwise have been — another passenger takes my seat, another shopper takes the steak — then I have not done anything wrong, because I was never needed. I think that test is right. (5) I also think there is a genuine distinction between doing harm and merely allowing it, and not merely a pragmatic one; I would not trade it away for a cleaner theory. (6) I canvass at weekends for a party, and I would think less of a friend who could not be bothered to vote.

What I want is a ranked audit: which of these are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, how severe each real conflict is, and for each one what would have to give — a principle, a verdict on a case, or the way I have been reading a case — along with what that concession costs me elsewhere. Where several repairs are available, say which single revision would clean up the most, and be explicit about what it would leave me unable to say. I would rather be told that one of my verdicts has to go than be reassured that everything is fine, so please do not dissolve the difficulties by quietly weakening the claims until they no longer touch.
