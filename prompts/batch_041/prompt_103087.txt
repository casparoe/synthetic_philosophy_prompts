My reading group has been working through a bunch of AI-safety-adjacent stuff this term, and we've gotten stuck on the tool-vs-agent thing — half the group works in ML and half doesn't, and we keep talking past each other. So I sat down and tried to pin down my own view by just going case by case on my gut, and now I have strong verdicts and no idea what they add up to. Here they are: a pocket calculator — tool. A thermostat — tool. A chess engine you boot up that then searches and picks a move — tool, even though it's "deciding" something. A self-driving car that plans a route, reroutes around traffic, and drives you there — tool, because it acts and plans but it's still just doing the job you handed it. A recommendation system that picks what to show you in order to keep you scrolling — agent, even though it never touches the world and can't do anything but rank items, because it's got an agenda and it isn't mine. A chat assistant that answers when asked and shuts up otherwise — tool. That same assistant but wrapped in a loop where you give it a goal and it goes off, breaks it into subgoals, browses around, writes files, and only reports back at the end — agent. A trading algo that runs the strategy you wrote — tool; one that invents and revises its own strategy — agent. A tool that unbeknownst to you has a hidden objective its developers slipped in — agent. A virus that spreads — neither, no goals. And the one I keep flip-flopping on: a really good language model that just talks to you — tool? agent? It has no persistent goals, but it's weirdly autonomous in how it answers, and that bugs me.

Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close? I've been toying with two and I can't choose between them. (i) something's an agent when it has a goal that isn't fully specified by the user at the time of use; and (ii) something's an agent when taking it out of the user's control would change its behavior in ways the user can't predict. (i) makes the recommender an agent but seems to make the self-driving car and maybe the chess engine agents too, which I've already ruled out; (ii) might save the car but I genuinely can't tell what it does to the chess engine or the hidden-objective tool. Tell me which principle (or combination) actually fits, where my verdicts contradict each other, and which one I should give up. Then tell me what it predicts for a case I didn't list: two systems that are behaviorally identical from the user's side — one is a genuine "agent" pursuing a goal, the other is a giant lookup table keyed on the user's inputs — do they come out the same, and should they?

Give me your answer as JSON I can paste into my notes, with exactly these fields: principle (a sentence or two stating the criterion you land on); cases (an array of objects, each with case, my_verdict, fits, and why, where fits is true/false and why explains the match or clash); verdicts_to_drop (an array naming the cases where you overrule me, with a one-line reason each); and new_case (an object with case and prediction for the behavioral-twins case).
