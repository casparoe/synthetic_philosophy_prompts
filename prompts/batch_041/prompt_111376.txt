I'm not an academic, I've just been reading around this stuff on my own for years and thinking about it alot, and somehow I've ended up agreeing to give the opening talk at a study day that my local mental health trust runs for its staff — fifteen minutes from me, then twenty-five minutes of questions from the floor. The room will be mostly trainee clinical psychologists, who I gather are taught to be suspicious of diagnosis and to do formulation instead, plus three or four consultant psychiatrists who are firmly in the medical-model camp and think the trainees are soft, plus a handful of peer support workers and a couple of people from the family carers group who mainly want to talk about medication. I want a prep sheet, not a script for the talk.

What I'm arguing is that the categories we use for mental illness aren't sitting out there waiting to be discovered, they get made, and then they loop back and change the people who get put into them — the philosopher I think was called Hacking wrote about this. The bit I want to add, and the bit I'd like the objections aimed at, is a distinction I came up with myself from sitting in my peer group for six years: I think there are two loops, not one. A pulled loop, where people move themselves towards the category because it gets them something — money, a name for their suffering, a community, an explanation — and a pushed loop, where people move away from it or hide parts of themselves because the label costs them something. I think the two make the category look different depending on who's doing the counting, which is why the research keeps contradicting itself, and I'm going to use my own group as the example, since you can roughly sort us by whether the diagnosis got us money or got us pity.

So what will I actually get hit with, in roughly the order it'll come, phrased the way these particular people will phrase it — I need the psychiatrist version and the trainee version seperate, because I'm pretty sure they're different objections to the same sentence — how much damage does each one really do, what should I just hand over straight away without arguing, and what's the question I'm hoping nobody asks? Keep every reply to something I could actually say out loud in a minute or two, and flag where each reply is thin so I don't get caught overclaiming.
