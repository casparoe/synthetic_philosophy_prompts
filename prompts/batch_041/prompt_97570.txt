I am a fourth-year PhD student in moral and political philosophy, midway through a dissertation chapter on distributive justice, and I have spent the past year caught between two positions that keep talking past each other. The first is the prioritarian in the tradition of Parfit: benefiting a person matters more, morally, the worse off that person is, and this priority tracks absolute welfare levels rather than anyone's relative position, so the view is supposed to be immune to the levelling-down objection that embarrasses telic egalitarianism. The second is a telic egalitarian who holds that inequality is intrinsically bad — that a state of affairs can be worse merely because some people are worse off than others — and who maintains that the levelling-down objection either begs the question or, when stated carefully, bites the prioritarian just as hard.

Please act as a professional philosopher and formal axiologist and draft the joint statement these two camps could genuinely both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Work slowly and carefully. Begin with a short brainstorming pass, before you draft anything: first, list the claims that look like real agreement rather than truce language; second, list the candidate cruxes; third, flag any disagreement you think is merely verbal, saying which one it is and why. Only after that, write the document itself.

The document should have three parts. First, a numbered Agreed claims section. Second, a Residual disagreements table, with a row per question and columns for the prioritarian's formulation, the egalitarian's formulation — each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — and a final column saying whether the disagreement is substantive or verbal. Third, a Cruxes section: for each crux, a case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance.

Both parties have agreed in advance to argue within a concrete formal setup rather than by trading intuitions, and I want that setup to do real work. Welfare is measured on an interpersonally comparable cardinal scale that may take negative values; a social welfare function has the form W = the sum over persons of f(u_i), with f increasing and concave over the positive range. Two technical disputes have become sticking points and must be handled explicitly. First, how should f behave over the negative range? A concave increasing f cannot be a plain power function there, and the choice among extensions changes the view's implications in cases involving lives below the baseline — cases both sides care about. Second, is the concavity of f a feature of the welfare measure itself or a separate moral weighting, and does that distinction make the priority view a genuine third alternative or merely a relabelling of utilitarianism with a concave welfare scale?

I also want at least one case that is not the standard two-person levelling-down case. I am especially interested in whether the levelling-down objection has an intrapersonal analogue: consider a single life that could be either uniformly at welfare 10 in each of two periods, or at 15 and 5 with the same total, and ask whether either side is committed to ranking the uniform life above the uneven one even though the uneven life leaves the person better off in one period and worse off in the other, and what that shows about whether the objection is really about distribution across persons or about something else. Add a case with negative welfare levels, where the priority weighting's behaviour is contested, and one case involving a good that is not itself welfare, so that the absoluteness of the priority view can be probed.

Write the statement so that both parties could put their names to it without either feeling it was quietly written from the other's premises. Do not include citations, references, reading lists, or appeals to what particular authors have said; I need the substance and the formulations, not the bibliography, and the document has to stand on its own for a reader who cannot look anything up.
