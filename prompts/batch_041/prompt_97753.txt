I am a retired physical chemist who has spent the last several years reading philosophy and would now like to attempt a project of my own; I am applying for a one-year visiting fellowship and need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea is this. The literature that draws epistemic and decision-theoretic morals from computational complexity — that no bounded agent can be ideally rational because its problems are NP-hard or undecidable, that logical omniscience is unattainable, and so on — leans almost entirely on asymptotic results, i.e. claims about how cost grows as problem size tends to infinity. Having spent a career on finite systems, I find this suspicious in a way I have not seen pressed: an asymptotic intractability result is compatible with a problem being easy at every scale any agent could ever reach, and an asymptotic tractability result is compatible with ruinous cost in the regime that actually matters. My suspicion is that the philosophical force of these arguments rests on an unexamined idealization, and that the honest question is not whether a problem is hard in the limit but where the hardness begins and whether any achievable agent lives on the far side of it. I would like the proposal to state that question so that it has a determinate answer; to explain why the existing positions — both the pessimists and the critics who retreat to bounded resources — leave it open; to set out a method month by month, something mixing parameterized and average-case complexity, the satisfiability-threshold results as a model of a sharp onset, and a careful sorting of which complexity claims are limit claims and which are claims about finite regimes; and to be candid about what would count as the project failing, for instance if it turned out the philosophical arguments never really depended on the asymptotic idealization, or if the finite-size question proved formal with no bearing on any actual agent. Tell me what scope is realistic for a year and where the proposal should narrow. No citations; I will add those myself.
