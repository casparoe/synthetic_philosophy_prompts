so my friend Dan and I got into a three hour fight last night about the morning star and the evening star, and I want to turn it into something. Dan is a total hardliner about names — his line is that a name just points at a thing, so once you find out that the morning star and the evening star are the same planet there is literally nothing left to be puzzled about, and anyone who says there's a leftover difference in 'meaning' or 'cognitive content' is just quietly changing the subject to psychology. I think he's wrong in a way that matters but at 1am all I could do was wave my hands around. Help me write the thing I couldn't say.

I want it to be an advice column — a Dear-Abby / Ask-the-expert page — where the columnist is a completely sincere Dan-type, and every letter he answers is secretly the puzzle. So: a reader writes in with what looks like a relationship problem. She fell for her partner under the evening star; she bought one of those silly star-naming certificates and had the star engraved on a locket; the partner keeps telling her the evening star and the morning star are the same star, so the locket is fine, so her sentimentality is misplaced, so she should stop making a thing of it. She can't tell if he's lying, confused, or just being cruel. There's a second letter too, short, from someone whose marriage is in trouble over a substitution in a promise — he promised her the evening star and now claims he already gave her the morning star, same star, no take-backs.

The columnist should answer all of it with impeccable hardliner hygiene: same object, therefore same thing, any difference you feel is a fact about you and not about the words. He must never slip — no 'well, in a sense...' — and his advice should be genuinely reasonable given his premises, even kind, in a slightly smug professional way. The comedy has to come from the philosophy, not from him being an idiot: give him the best lines his side actually has (informativeness is pragmatics, belief reports are opaque for their own reasons, anything else forces you to posit spooky third things between word and object). If someone who has taught this material reads it, they should laugh and wince, not roll their eyes at a straw man. And by the end, with nobody stating it, a reader who's never heard of any of this should feel the question he can't answer: how does finding out that a = b ever tell you anything, if a and b are the same thing?

Give it a column title, then the letters, then his replies. Deadpan the whole way, no winking at the reader, no names of real philosophers, no citations, nothing like 'as X argued' — it should read like an actual syndicated column. Around 450 words. A typo here or there is fine, it's an advice page.
