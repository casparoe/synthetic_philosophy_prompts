I'm preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on aggregation, and I want a decision tree that sorts a reader's verdicts in the torture-versus-dust-specks case into an identifiable position. Fix the case as follows: either one person is tortured for fifty years, or an astronomically large number of people each receive a speck of dust in the eye for an instant — a nuisance each of them notices briefly and then forgets. Every internal node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to: whether differences in welfare too small to matter individually can still make a state of affairs better or worse; whether enough of those small increments must eventually, at some finite quantity, outweigh any fixed large harm, or whether the ordering is lexicographic, with a threshold that trivial harms can never cross however many of them accumulate; whether the comparison is between the states of affairs people end up in or between the prospects they faced beforehand, so that a lottery giving everyone an equal chance of being the tortured person is weighed differently from a settled outcome; whether harms may be summed across persons at all, or only within a single life; whether a discomfort that is forgotten and leaves no trace counts as a harm; and whether the argument turns on the number of people being infinite rather than merely too large to write down. Give each leaf a name where one is in common use and otherwise a one-line description, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment — above all, what it must say about the variant it was introduced to avoid. Test the tree against at least these variants: many specks against a moderate harm to one person; an enormous number of specks accumulated within a single life against that same person's fifty years of torture; and specks falling on people whose lives are otherwise barely worth living. Mark any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and say of each unoccupied combination whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely undefended. Aim for roughly a dozen nodes.
