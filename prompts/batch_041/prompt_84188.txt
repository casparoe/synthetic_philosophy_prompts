Every year I teach a seminar that ends up half philosophers and half people from the med school, and every year the same fight about Szasz eats the last three weeks of the term. The Szaszian side says the whole vocabulary of mental illness is normative in disguise — that a "symptom" is just a piece of conduct somebody has decided is bad, and that the "dysfunction" talk is the disapproval restated in biological vocabulary. Their exhibits are the cases where the diagnosis tracked nothing but the era's tastes: drapetomania, the declassification of homosexuality in 1973, the way grief got booted out of the depression criteria a few years back. The other side always answers with the obvious cases — the woman who hears voices telling her to hurt herself, the man who can't leave the house, the kid who stops eating — and says that whatever you call it, something is broken there, and there's nothing mere about it. Then somebody brings up migraine or fibromyalgia and asks why "no lesion" is supposed to be a refutation in psychiatry but not in the rest of medicine, and we're off again. Szasz himself was fine with voluntary psychotherapy and mainly wanted the disease label and the coercion gone, which nobody in the room quite knows what to do with.

I've stopped believing were having the argument we think we're having. My hunch — and it's just a hunch — is that the metaphysical question (is there really a dysfunction?) and the ethical one (is psychiatric authority legitimate?) are being fought out under a single heading, with the first standing in as a proxy for the second. But maybe not: maybe it's a substantive fight about what a disease is, or a verbal fight about the word that would evaporate once we fixed the vocabulary, or a fight about which cases count as data — both sides read the 1973 decision fine, they just read it differently. Give me a diagnosis rather than a verdict, built out of what each side actually claims instead of a survey of the literature, and then tell me what would count as progress here: a distinction, a criterion, a case, or an argument that there's no progress to be had, because the concept is doing institutional work — handing out excuses from responsibility, insurance money, and in the bad cases a locked ward — that no amount of conceptual analysis is going to settle.
