I'm putting together a proposal for a year-long MA thesis and I've got a half-baked idea I want you to turn into something submittable. Everyone working on endurantism vs perdurantism fights over material objects, persons, maybe events: does the thing persist as one entity wholly present at each time, or as a series of temporal parts? My hunch is those cases are too friendly to both sides -- we have strong pre-theoretic intuitions pulling each way and no way to make either side pay for its choice. So I want to run the debate on institutional entities instead: a marriage, a corporation, a sports team, a band. Take a marriage. It's clearly the same marriage for decades, people refer to it as one thing, they say things like "our marriage has changed" -- but it has no body, no location beyond its participants, and its persistence seems to depend on ongoing social recognition and the participants' commitments and intentions. So: is the marriage wholly present at each moment it exists, or does it perdure as a sum of temporal parts? And if its identity is partly constituted by how people treat it, does either answer even apply, or does the case expose an assumption both sides share?

Turn this into a proposal: a precise statement of the question, why the standard positions leave it open, what I'd actually do month by month over the year, what a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing. Don't bother with citations or a reading list, I'll add those. Be straight with me about what's settled versus genuinely contested here -- don't hand me a contested reading dressed up as the consensus. Max 900 words.
