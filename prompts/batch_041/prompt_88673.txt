I want to know what principle, if any, my verdicts about criminalization add up to. Here they are.

A competent adult with a terminal illness wants to die and a doctor helps him: I think that should be lawful. Two adults agree to a duel and one is killed: I think the survivor should be prosecuted for murder, consent or no. An adult advertises for someone willing to be killed and eaten; a volunteer, fully informed and not depressed, goes through with it: I think the killer should be convicted of murder, and the volunteer's consent should count for nothing. A man pays a surgeon to amputate a healthy leg because he finds the stump erotic: I think both should be prosecuted. Two men agree to a private fight, no weapons, and one is permanently blinded: I think that should be lawful, as boxing is. Adult siblings carry on a long-term sexual relationship, no children, in private: I think that should be lawful, though it turns my stomach. A man has sex with an animal that shows no distress: I think that should be unlawful. A healthy adult sells one of his kidneys to the highest bidder: I think that should be unlawful, though I can't say who is harmed by it. A man leaves written instructions that after his death his body be skinned and the skin displayed in a shop window as an advertisement; he has no living relatives and no one is harmed by the display: I think it should be unlawful. And private heroin use, and nudity at a designated beach: lawful.

My own attempt at a principle runs like this: the law may forbid an act only when it wrongs someone other than the actor, and consent excuses only when the act serves the consenter's own good. On that reading, a killing done for the killer's pleasure or profit is not excused by consent, because it uses the consenter's body as a means, while a killing that ends the consenter's suffering is excused because it serves him. That handles the doctor, the duelist, the cannibal, and the amputee, but I can't see how it reaches the animal or the corpse, it says nothing about the kidney sale, and I suspect it convicts the boxer who blinds his friend.

Tell me what principle, or small set of principles, best captures the list; which of my verdicts it forces me to give up; and what it predicts for a case I haven't listed. Say how confident you are in each part of the answer, and single out the point you are least sure of.
