I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on Bayesian updating, and I want a decision tree that sorts the positions people actually hold about the Judy Benjamin case. You know the one: Judy parachutes into a field split into red and blue halves, each half split into second and third company quadrants; she starts with equal credence in the four cells; then she gets a radio message that amounts to the conditional that if she's in red, she's in second company (not a factual report that she's not in red third). The point is that the message doesn't tell her whether she's in red or blue, only something conditional. I want the tree to start from the question of what the evidence is, and branch on the commitments that make the update rules come apart. Leaves should be plain-language positions like treating the conditional as a material conditional and conditionalizing, minimizing relative entropy from the prior, keeping the prior over red and blue and only changing the conditional credence given red, Adams conditioning, the evidence is not a proposition so no probabilistic update rule applies, and anything else you think is a live option. For each leaf, give one sentence on the most awkward commitment. Also, flag any two leaves your tree can't seperate. And to make it harder, add a new twist: suppose the message instead is that if she's in red she's in second company and if she's in blue she's in third company. Does that change the tree? If so, where? I want the tree to be usable by someone who doesn't already know the names of the positions, so phrase every node as a question they can answer without knowing where it leads. Keep it to maybe 6-8 leaves. Also, no citations or reading lists, obviously.
