I chair the research review panel for a mid-sized health system's innovation fund -- small money, maybe $4m a year, mostly for things the big federal agencies won't touch. There's a proposal on the agenda for Thursday and I've changed my mind about it three times this week, so I want a written opinion I can circulate to the other seven panel members before we vote.

The case: a university/community partnership wants $600k over three years to test a group-based rites-of-passage program for kids aging out of foster care. Small groups, a mentor, a physically demanding trip, no curriculum. The practitioners insist the thing only works if it's genuinely uncertain and unscripted for the kids. Their claim is that participants come out with a durable sense of belonging, and that this shows up as less placement breakdown over two years.

Here's the fight. Three of us think their's nothing testable in it. You can't blind it, you can't standardize the "dose" (they say standardizing it kills it -- "the program is the relationship"), and when our stats person asked what result would count against them, the PI said a null on placement breakdown would, but also that the thing they actually care about -- the felt sense of belonging -- isn't something our validated scales capture, so a null on the scales wouldn't tell us much either way. My colleague said, only half joking, that this is astrology with a budget line. The PI says psychotherapy was in exactly this position before anyone built decent trials for it, and that if we demand she pre-specify one disconfirming observation we're applying a rule to her that we didn't apply to the music-therapy project we funded last year.

Then there's a second problem, which is that our own rules contradict each other. After the "Bright Start" mess two years ago -- we funded a program, it produced nothing, and the lead said afterwards that the results were never measurable in principle -- we adopted a rule that every project must state in advance what observation would count as disconfirming, and we've been strict about it. The same year we adopted an equity rule saying the panel has to weigh community-embedded and experiential knowledge as evidence in its own right. The community partner here has submitted testimony from about 200 former participants, and one panel member says that under our own rules that testimony is evidence and we can't wave it away just because it isn't a p-value.

So: draft the opinion I'll circulate. Give me a bottom line -- fund, fund with conditions, or reject -- with whatever conditions, seperate review points and time frame you'd actually attach, and tell me what we should say to the PI. Then give me the general criteria, because this keeps coming up and I'd rather we had a policy then eight people's guts. And argue the other side honestly; I lean toward funding with conditions, but I genuinely can't tell whether that's judgement or cowardice.
