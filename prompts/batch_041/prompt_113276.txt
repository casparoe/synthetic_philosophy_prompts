I sit on my state's parole board and next week we hear Dwayne's case -- 22 years in for a killing he did at 19. The prison psychologist's report calls his near-perfect conduct record "sphexish," by which she means something like the old story of the digger wasp who, if you nudge the cricket an inch while she's inside inspecting her burrow, comes back out and reruns the entire routine from the top, over and over: his compliance looks like a fixed script that resets rather than a sign he could handle anything genuinely new. We just adopted a new release standard and the operative line is that an inmate has to show "deliberative agency, not merely rote compliance." One of my collegues says that standard is either impossible to satisfy or trivially satisfied, since you can redescribe anybody's best behavior as a bigger routine -- his, hers, ours -- and another says if that's right then the whole notion of being free enough to deserve release is a fiction. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before we vote: a concrete recommendation (release, deny, or release with conditions), a time frame attached to it, and an honest run at the strongest argument on the other side.
