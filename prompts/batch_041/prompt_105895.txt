I've been part of a Stoicism reading group for about a year now — we started with the popular daily-meditation genre and have been working our way backward into Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius — and the organizer has asked each of us to write up our own working position so the others can press on it. Before I hand mine in, I'd like a consistency check, because I suspect it's less tidy than I've been letting on. Here is roughly where I've landed.

(1) The dichotomy of control, as I understand it: the only things genuinely up to me are my own judgments and choices; everything else — my health, my reputation, whether my projects come off, whether the people I love thrive or die — is not. (2) The only genuine good is virtue, roughly wisdom, justice, courage, and self-discipline, and the only genuine evil is vice; everything else, including health, wealth, and life itself, is indifferent. (3) All the same, I take it to be plainly rational to prefer health to sickness and friendship to isolation, and to take ordinary prudent steps to secure them — I don't think (2) commits me to behaving as though it made no difference whether I get cancer, and I'd say the same about the people I care about. (4) I think grief, love, and even anger at injustice are appropriate, sometimes admirable responses to a world that really does contain loss and wrong, and I actively don't want to become the sort of person who feels none of them; the idea that the goal is to feel nothing strikes me as a misunderstanding, maybe even a kind of spiritual deadness. (5) I also think I have real obligations to try to make the world better — to give, to organize, to oppose unjust policies — and that failing to do so is a genuine failing on my part, not merely the forfeiting of some optional preferred indifferent.

What I want from you is an audit rather than a pep talk. Which of these pull against each other, and how badly? I'd especially like to know which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like they are — I have a nagging sense that at least one of these is quietly doing work the others won't let it do, but I can't tell which one, and I'd rather not find out the hard way in front of the group. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad; for each genuine one, say what would have to give and what that would cost me; and if a single revision would clean up the most, tell me which. If it turns out the whole thing hangs together better than I fear, say so plainly.
