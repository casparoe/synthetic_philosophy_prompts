Hey — I've got a short dialogue to write for my phil of mind seminar and I want to do it on sphexishness. You know the one: the digger wasp drags a paralyzed cricket to her burrow, goes inside to inspect the nest, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's in there she just re-drags it and runs the whole inspection routine again, on and on. The question I want the dialogue to fight about is wether that's us, just with more steps.

So: two characters, both sharp, neither a strawman. Don't give me the boring version where one says 'we have reasons' and the other says 'all instinct.' I want them arguing over a specific test that one of them proposes, which you should build into the dialogue: an agent counts as sphexish about a habit to exactly the degree that it can only drop the habit when the interruption arrives in a form it already has a subroutine for. So the flexibility is real, but it has a shape, and the shape is the part you can't see from the inside. Call it second-order sphexishness if that helps.

The pro-wasp side has to make that stick using ordinary human material — phobias, addiction, someone who'll only reconsider a belief if the objection comes from the right sort of person or in the right tone. The other side has to say where the line falls and why it isn't just a fancier routine, and they can't get out of it by simply asserting that reasons aren't routines. Real back and forth, each one answering the last thing the other actually said.

Two constraints: nobody wins by gesturing at quantum randomness or at 'science will settle it eventually,' and don't lean on the wasp anecdote being literally true — I've read it's contested (the wasp apparently does adapt after enough repetitions, and the re-inspection might be doing something useful anyway), so treat the wasp as the picture rather than the evidence.

End with one of them giving up something real. 600ish words.
