A friend of mine who teaches early modern philosophy has folded dream skepticism into her intro course, and she has arrived at a pattern of verdicts I cannot fully reconstruct. On Zhuangzi's butterfly dream — the man who cannot tell whether he was a man dreaming he was a butterfly or is now a butterfly dreaming he is a man — no knowledge. On an ordinary nightmare in which you sincerely believe you are being chased: no knowledge. On a lucid dreamer who concludes mid-dream, on the basis of a tell she has learned to trust, that she is dreaming: knowledge. On someone who wakes and correctly believes she has just been dreaming: knowledge. On ordinary waking perceptual beliefs: knowledge. On someone who keeps having false awakenings and cannot tell whether she is awake now: no knowledge.

The case she has not yet ruled on: a dreamer who has been asleep for what feels like years, in a dream-world as regular and law-governed as this one, whose beliefs about that world are accurate with respect to it and formed by processes that track it well, and who has no evidence at all that she is dreaming. Does she know there is a table in front of her? I would rather have the rule set than the verdict: which rules the earlier rulings are jointly compatible with, what each of them predicts here, which you would bet on and why, and which further case would force the choice.
