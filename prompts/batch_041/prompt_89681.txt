My opening: the demon case is old news, so here's a new one. Suppose the world is rigged so that admiration is self-fulfilling -- if I genuinely admire a thing, it thereby becomes admirable, mud included. No demon, no threat, no bribe. So the reason I now have to admire the mud is a reason about the mud itself: my admiring it would make it admirable. And yet it still smells like exactly the wrong kind of reason -- it doesn't show the mud was admirable, only that my attitude would manufacture the admirability. So either your distinction sorts reasons by whether they're about the object, in which case this is a right-kind reason and your own view says the mud is valuable, which is absurd -- or it sorts them some other way, and you owe me a statement of it that doesn't quietly assume the answer.
