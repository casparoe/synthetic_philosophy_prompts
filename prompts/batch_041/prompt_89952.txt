**Question 3 (35 points).** A lab trains a small transformer to act in a synthetic gridworld. At each step the model receives a rendering of the grid and must emit one of four moves. In the training distribution every cell is rendered in its own background hue, so grid coordinates and hue are perfectly correlated. The lab trains a linear probe on the residual stream at layer 6 and finds that a two-dimensional linear read-out recovers the agent's coordinates with 99.3% accuracy on held-out episodes. They take the two directions spanned by the probe weights and run an intervention: at layer 6 they overwrite the residual stream's projection onto that two-dimensional subspace with the projection taken from a different input. The model's emitted move matches the donor input's move in 88% of cases. As a control they overwrite a randomly chosen two-dimensional subspace of matched norm, which changes the emitted move in 6% of cases. The lab writes in its abstract that "the model represents the agent's position."

In lecture we distinguished three claims one might make about a network and a feature F:

(i) the *carriage* claim: the network's internal states carry information about F;
(ii) the *use* claim: those states are consumed by downstream computation in a way that is sensitive to F;
(iii) the *explanation* claim: citing states with F-content is part of the best explanation of the network's behaviour, rather than a convenient redescription of it.

Assess the lab's conclusion. For each of (i)–(iii), say whether the evidence as described establishes it, and if not, say exactly what is missing. Then say whether "the model represents the agent's position" is a defensible summary of the results, and if you think it needs qualification, state the qualification. (35 points)

---

**Student's answer.** Short answer: the lab has earned the first two claims; the third is not a separate empirical claim at all, though their wording still needs tightening.

*Carriage.* The probe result is good evidence that position information is present in the residual stream at layer 6 in a linearly accessible form. This is not trivial: a 99.3% linear read-out shows the information is not merely implicit in the weights but is available to a linear map from a single forward pass. The usual caveat applies — a probe has its own inductive bias and can succeed by latching onto something that merely predicts position — but 99.3% on held-out episodes is high enough that we should treat carriage as established.

*Use.* This is exactly what the patching experiment is designed to show, and it does show it. If overwriting the projection onto the probe's subspace changes the emitted move to match the donor's, then downstream computation is reading that subspace; the 6% figure for a random norm-matched subspace confirms that the effect is specific to these directions rather than an artefact of perturbing activations at layer 6. The control also disposes of the obvious confound. Since hue and position are perfectly correlated in the training distribution, one might worry that the probe has simply found a hue direction; a hue direction would not have survived the random-subspace control, so the two directions the probe identified are position directions.

*Explanation.* Here the abstract goes further than the evidence, but not by much. Once you have shown that a subspace carries position and is consumed by downstream computation, there is no further fact of the matter about whether the content description "earns its keep" that the causal story leaves open. To explain the behaviour by citing a state with position-content just is to cite the state that the downstream computation is sensitive to. The eliminativist who insists that talk of representation is a mere façon de parler about input–output regularities — behaviourism in new clothes — has to deny that internal states have causal roles in producing behaviour, which is simply false of these models. So the third claim follows from the second; it is not an additional empirical bet.

*Qualification.* The lab should be careful with the word "the". They have identified a two-dimensional subspace, but the probe's basis within that subspace is arbitrary up to rotation, and nothing in the experiment shows that the network's own format is a coordinate code rather than, say, a distributed code spread over the same subspace. The abstract should say that the model carries and uses position information, which is all the results support; that is weaker than "represents" in the philosopher's sense, but it is the only sense in which "represents" is well defined for a system like this.
