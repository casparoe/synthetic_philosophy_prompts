I've been chewing on this since a seminar and it's genuinely driving me up the wall. I'm quite drawn to the Ubuntu / relational picture of persons — the idea that you're a person through other people, that personhood is something you come to have and keep hold of within a community rather than a private possession you carry around on your own. But I built an arguement that seems to make the view say something monstrous, and I can't find where it comes off the rails. Here it is:

(1) To be a person is to stand in certain relations to others — relations of recognition, mutual regard and shared life.
(2) Relations aren't properties a thing can have all on its own; they hold between a thing and something else.
(3) So nobody is a person in isolation, and nobody is a person independently of the community that stands in those relations to them.
(4) If the community's relations of recognition are what make someone a person, then when a community withdraws recognition the person-making relation is gone.
(5) When the relation is gone, the personhood goes with it.
(6) So a community can strip someone of their personhood simply by disowning them — casting them out, treating them as nothing.
(7) Whatever has no personhood has none of the moral protections that attach to persons.
(8) So the outcast is owed none of the moral consideration owed to persons, and the community that casts them out does them no wrong in that regard.
(9) Therefore the relational view licenses ostracism and worse.

I think (9) is absurd, and I don't want to give up the relational core — (1) through (3) feel like the whole point, and I'd sooner bin (8) than (1). But I can't see how to bin (8) without dragging something out of (1)-(5) with it, and that's what's bothering me. Someone in the group said the trouble is that I'm treating recognition as if it were the community's to grant or withhold at will, but I couldn't see how that's not just restating the view. I've also heard people distinguish relations that are internal to a thing from ones that are merely external, and I wonder whether that's doing any work here, but I can't make it land.

So: which step do I reject, why did it look fine when I wrote it, and what does rejecting it cost me? If there are two live options, price them seperately — I want to know what each one forces me to give up.
