I am a high school student preparing a paper for a classics seminar, and I am trying to work out what I actually think about drunkenness before I write about Plato. I have been reading Plato's discussions of drinking, including the part where an older Athenian argues that supervised drinking parties can be a form of education and a test of character, and the scene where Alcibiades arrives drunk. Going by my gut on cases, here are my verdicts. A philosopher at a symposium drinking wine to loosen his tongue and speak truths: acceptable. A soldier drinking before battle to steady his nerves: not acceptable. A grieving person drinking to forget a loss: not acceptable. A young person drinking at a supervised festival, under the eye of elders, to learn what drunkenness does to them: acceptable. A legislator drinking while drafting laws: not acceptable. A person drinking alone in secret: not acceptable. A person drinking to test and train their self-control, in a setting where others can stop them: acceptable. A person drinking because everyone else is and they want to fit in: not acceptable, but less bad than the soldier. A person drinking to honor a god or a guest: acceptable, even if they get drunk. A person drinking to escape a truth they already know: not acceptable. My rough principle is that drinking is acceptable when it is public, supervised, and aimed at self-knowledge, and unacceptable when it is private, unsupervised, or used to escape. But that principle does not explain why the soldier drinking to steady his nerves is unacceptable while the philosopher drinking to loosen his tongue is acceptable, since both are using wine for an instrumental purpose. Is there a principle that gets all of these verdicts, or close, and what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a student drinking at a party because they are afraid of being left out, where no one is watching and no one will stop them? If the principle is Platonic, what would Plato say about that case? Which of my verdicts would the principle force me to drop? I want a precise principle, or a small set of candidate principles, with the case that motivates each clause, its verdicts on cases I have not listed, and an honest statement of which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
