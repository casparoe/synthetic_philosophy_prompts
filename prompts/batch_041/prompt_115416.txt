Please argue for the following claim: that a system can have a genuine reason to do something even though nothing in the system represents that reason, and that this is not just a façon de parler. To make the case concrete, imagine a thermostat whose bimetallic strip bends and closes a circuit when the room cools; suppose I say the thermostat "turns on the heat because the room is cold." I want to claim that this is not merely a loose metaphor but a real, if minimal, instance of a reason for action—one that no part of the thermostat entertains. Then I want to know where the line falls: what must be added to a merely causal sequence before we are entitled to speak of a reason at all? Argue that your proposed condition is the right one, and say what it rules out that a purely dispositional or information-theoretic account would wrongly rule in. One paragraph, no more than 300 words.
