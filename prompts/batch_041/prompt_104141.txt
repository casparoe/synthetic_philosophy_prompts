ok this is kind of a weird question but it's been bugging me for months. I've been reading up on how different countries deal with the awful things their own country did in the past -- Germany and the Nazi era, Japan and the war, Turkey and the Armenian stuff, the American South and slavery and Jim Crow. And I keep seeing a pattern I don't have a name for.

Here it is, roughly. The places that make the biggest public show of remembering -- museums, apology days, whole units in school, monuments, the works -- are often the ones where the remembering itself turns into something to be proud of. Being the kind of country that "faces its past" becomes a moral badge you get to hold up against the countries that supposedly sweep it all under the rug. Every memorial I've wandered through seems to end with a room about the lessons for all humanity, but nothing about who's still living with the aftermath or who's still profiting. And the memory always seems to settle on stuff that's comfortably far back -- nobody's around anymore who could be made to pay for it or fix it.

So the remembering gets loudest exactly when it's become safe, and it ends up flattering the rememberers more than it helps anyone. Is there an established term for that? "Memory culture" and "coming to terms with the past" just describe the activity, they don't capture this twist. And I don't think it's plain hypocrisy or white guilt or virtue signaling either, those feel to thin. If there's a name, whats it, and how is it different from the nearest ideas? If there isn't one, can you name and define it for me so I can tell it apart from genuine remorse, from nostalgia, and from just forgetting? Honestly I care way more about where the edges of the thing are than about the label itself.

American English please.
