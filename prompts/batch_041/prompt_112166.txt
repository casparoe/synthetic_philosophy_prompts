I want a thought experiment built to order on heterophenomenology — Dennett's method of treating a subject's reports as texts to be explained rather than as readouts of experience, and then producing a neutral account of what the subject believes from the intentional stance.

What the case is for: I want to test whether heterophenomenology can be a method for the science of consciousness at all, or only for the science of belief. That fixes what counts as cheating — if the case stipulates that the subject's report is or isn't about a real experience, or if the researcher is just sloppy, it's no good.

The specs:

- Realistic and small: one subject, one ordinary lab session, nothing fancier than a screen, a button and a timer. No scans, no lesions, no anaesthesia, no split-brain, no blindsight, no zombies, no inverted spectra, no invented technology.
- The subject is as good at reporting as a person gets — years of trained introspection behind them, not a naive undergrad — and nothing about their report is careless or confused.
- No behavioural conflict anywhere: their discrimination, timing and error patterns line up perfectly with what they report, so the heterophenomenologist can't catch them out on performance.
- The report mustn't be explainable as compliance: the subject volunteers a feature of the experience that nobody in the study mentioned, so you can't write it off as demand characteristics or as them parroting the experimenter's vocabulary.
- Done properly, with no sloppiness allowed, the neutral account still has to land somewhere the subject rejects. Specifically: they accept every sentence of it as a description of their beleifs and their behaviour, and reject only its silence — the implication that there is nothing further left to describe. Not that they're wrong about the world, but that the method says there is nothing more here.
- Their objection can't be a bare appeal to first-person authority, and the researcher mustn't smuggle in any assumption one way or the other about whether the subject is conscious.
- And the same facts have to come out differently under the rival approach that treats the subject's trained description as first-class data in its own right rather than as a text to be explained, so the case actually discriminates between the two methods.

Then walk me through it as a numbered list of exactly five items, no intro and no conclusion, the first one a short paragraph and the rest a sentence or two each: (1) the case; (2) what the neutral account says and exactly which bit the subject rejects; (3) what the first-person-as-data approach says about the same case, and why it can't just be waved away; (4) which single feature of the case is doing the work, and whether the conflict survives taking that feature out; (5) how a defender of heterophenomenology would say this is the method working as intended, and what that defence costs them.
