ok, Neurath rabbit hole again, and I want fiction out of it. Background if its not in your cache: in the 20s and 30s Otto Neurath ran a museum in Vienna that turned social statistics into pictures (the Isotype thing, Gerd Arntz drew the pictograms), and the founding rule was definately that you never show "more" by drawing one figure bigger — you draw more identical figures, because otherwise nobody can tell whether you mean height or area. Neurath never thought pictures could carry the load alone; he treated the whole thing as a helping language that always ran alongside ordinary words.

So: a short story, ~1500 words, about a fictional institute that decides to go all the way and ban words from its charts entirely. The protagonist is a transformer — that was the literal job title in the real project, the person who takes a messy report and works out how to draw it. Hand her a batch of public-health claims for a travelling exhibition and let the story turn on the ones that won't go into pictures: zero cases this month; the average household has 2.4 children; this would have killed 400 people if the vaccine hadn't shipped; the gap is inside the margin of error; we found no lead in the water, versus we didn't test for lead. Her boss is a true believer — anything undrawable is a pseudo-statement and gets dropped — and he shouldn't be a strawman, give him his best case. She starts a private notebook of the things she can only say in words, which is of course the exact thing the institute exists to abolish.

Third person, keep the period texture light: if you'd have to look a fact up, invent around it. No lecture at the end, and nobody explaining the philosophy out loud. I want to feel the squeeze, not hear it described.
