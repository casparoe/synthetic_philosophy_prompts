For the graduate seminar I run on anthropic reasoning, I have been trying to isolate exactly where my colleague and I part ways on the fine-tuning argument, and I would rather have your own numbers than a survey of the literature. Let me state the target claim precisely, so that it has a truth value. Fix the evidence E as the conjunction of two things: the dimensionless constants and initial conditions of our universe take values that permit long-lived stable structures (stars, chemistry, planets) and, eventually, embodied observers; and those values are, as far as we can presently tell, not fixed by any deeper theory we possess. Let H_D be the hypothesis that those values were settled by a purposive intelligence, and H_N the best non-purposive alternative you can articulate — a multiverse with some distribution over constants, a single universe with an unknown selection mechanism, a brute fact, whichever you take to be the strongest candidate. I want your credence that the likelihood ratio P(E | H_D) / P(E | H_N) is at least ten — that is, that the data favour design over the best mindless alternative by an order of magnitude — with the reasoning decomposed rather than asserted. If you think the ratio is simply ill-defined, say so and give me your credence that it is ill-defined instead.

Please assign a rough probability to each of the following sub-questions, one line of justification apiece, then tell me how you combine them into the final figure: (1) that there is a non-arbitrary measure on the space of possible values, without which any claim that the life-permitting range is small may be empty; (2) that, given such a measure, the structure-and-life-permitting region really is a small fraction of it; (3) that the design hypothesis is determinate enough to carry a well-defined likelihood, and that a designer would plausibly have produced these values rather than others; (4) that the best non-purposive alternative does not itself already render E probable — in particular, that whatever distribution over constants it assigns was not simply chosen after the fact to fit; (5) that observer selection — the fact that we could only ever have found ourselves in a universe permitting observers — does not by itself neutralise the evidence; and (6) a refinement I have been pressing on my colleague, namely that among the values permitting observers at all, the subset that also permits those observers to measure the constants and reconstruct the laws may be far narrower, so that our universe looks tuned not merely for life but for legibility. Does conditioning on our being able to do physics add genuine, independent evidence, or is it entirely absorbed by the bare fact that someone is here to ask? Finally, tell me which single sub-question your own figure is most sensitive to, and which sub-question most likely accounts for the gap between someone who puts the likelihood ratio well above ten and someone who puts it near or below one.
