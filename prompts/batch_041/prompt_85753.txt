ok so I want a fake advice column — Dear Abby style, a syndicated "Ask the..." page — where the columnist is a sincere, unironic believer that emotions just ARE judgements. Get the judgement right and the feeling goes away. She's not a strawman, she's the smartest version of that view: no one's angry at a fact, only at a verdict about the fact; the feeling is the verdict; if the fear survives, you haven't really changed the belief yet; and so on. Deadpan throughout. She never winks, never breaks character, never admits the theory is in trouble.

The comedy has to come from the letters. Give me 3-4 short ones from ordinary people, and each one should be a case the judgement view genuinely strains on — the fear of flying that outlives the statistics, the grief for a parent where the writer says they wouldn't give the judgement back even if they could, the anger that arrives before any verdict has been reached, the love that seems to sit in your chest rather than your opinions. She answers every one of them in good faith from inside her theory, using whatever the best version of it has: occurrent vs dispositional, the belief is tacit, the feeling is a leftover habit, the letter-writer doesn't really believe it. What I don't want is her accidentally conceding the main point — the one move she can't make is "yes, the feeling can outlast the judgement and that matters" — but a reader who knows this stuff should be able to watch her straining and reconstruct the other two options (perception, plain feeling) just from what she keeps having to explain away. Let her have one testy paragraph aimed at "the perception people" without naming anyone, since thats the rival she's actually fighting.

700-800 words total including the letters, with a column title and a little sign-off tagline at the end.
