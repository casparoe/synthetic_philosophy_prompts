I want two ways of thinking about politeness compared, and I want the comparison pressed hard.

The first treats truthfulness as a duty that does not bend to social convenience. On this view, a lie — even a small one told to spare someone's feelings — wrongs the person lied to, because it treats them as someone to be managed rather than addressed; bluntness, though it may be unkind, at least respects the hearer enough to say the true thing to their face. White lies and the indirection that shades into them are moral failures, and a culture that institutionalizes them — the polite refusal that means no, the compliment that means nothing — has institutionalized a small wrong.

The second view has no settled name that I know of, so let me fix one for the sake of the comparison: call it the register view. On the register view, directness and indirectness are not moral categories at all but registers of a shared communicative code, on a par with levels of formality or the choice between two words for the same thing. A white lie is a register choice, not a moral one; the failure it risks is not wrongdoing but misfiring — being read as a different register from the one you intended. What separates Dutch directness from Japanese indirectness, on this view, is not that one culture is more honest but that the two have calibrated different registers for the same underlying moves.

Please compare the two. What does each predict about, say, a guest who tells the host the meal was delicious when it was not; a doctor who gives a patient a prognosis without softening it; a Japanese subordinate's indirect refusal that a Dutch manager takes for assent; a white lie told to a dying person; and a visitor who either adopts the local register or keeps their own? For each view, say what is really doing the work — a duty, a convention, a shared code, a relationship — and where the two views genuinely conflict as opposed to merely redescribing the same facts in different vocabularies. Then tell me which you find more defensible, and name the case or distinction that would most cleanly decide between them. I am less interested in a verdict on white lies than in whether the register view earns its keep: does it dissolve the moral question, or does it quietly smuggle a moral standard back in under the heading of misfiring?
