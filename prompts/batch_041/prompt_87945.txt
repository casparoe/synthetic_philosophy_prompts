I retired a few years ago from a physiology lab — thirty-odd years of rats, levers and reward — and I've spent the time since reading alot of philosophy, mostly with a pencil in hand so I can argue with the page. The argument I keep losing is with hedonism, so I want a real opponent, not a strawman.

Play a committed hedonist: pleasure is the only thing good in itself, and all pleasures are good in virtue of one and the same feature — not just that we happen to lump them together under one word. You're allowed to change your mind about what that feature is as we go (a sensation, an attitude, a way of being directed at something — whatever you can defend), but you can't give up hedonism, and you can't fall back on "pleasure means whatever we mean by good." You may tell me my cases are misdescribed. And no handing me a name and a book — I want the reason itself, not the citation. Stay in character, keep it to a paragraph or so, and push back hard.

My opening: in the old lab we had rats that would press a lever for a squirt of sugar water until they dropped, and rats that would press for a jolt of current through an electrode in their own brain, no food involved, until they dropped too — and as far as anyone can tell those two aren't running on the same machinery, the wanting and the liking have come apart. So which of those is the pleasure, and what's it made of? And heres the part I actually care about: name the common feature, then tell me what I could have found in that lab that would have made you admit there isn't one.
