I run a small philosophy of mind reading group -- 14 people, mostly grad students, 90 minutes a session -- and we've just spent two weeks on levels of description and whether the higher-level story is doing any real causal work. Next week I want to do something hands-on instead of another paper.

The thing I want them to feel is Hofstadter's careenium. The picture, as I remember it from his strange-loop book, is a huge arena with countless tiny magnetic balls in it (he calls them simmballs) bouncing around and clumping into bigger, slower-moving lumps that drift about and bump into each other. At the lump level you can tell a lovely story: this lump chased that one, the big one blocked the doorway, the little one got herded into a corner. At the ball level nothing is happening except balls jiggling and sticking. So: do the lumps push the balls around, or is every push done by the balls, with the lumps as mere bookkeeping? I want them to feel the pull of the epiphenomenalist answer (only the bottom level really does anything) *and* the pull of the answer that the lump story isn't a fiction (the pattern is real, it supports explanations and counterfactuals the ball story can't even state), and to feel how those two are supposed to be compatible rather than a contradiction.

Design me a runnable 90-minute activity for this. Constraints: no computers, no wifi, just bodys, index cards, chalk, and whatever's in the room. Ideally they end up operating the system at both levels themselves -- some of them are the substrate, some are the interpreters or controllers -- so that "intervening on the lump" either works or doesn't, and they find out which by trying it. I dont want a lecture up front; the words careenium, simmball, and epiphenomenalism shouldn't come out of my mouth until the debrief.

Give me: rules and roles, materials, a timeline, how it's scored or won, a debrief that maps specific moments onto the two positions and onto the causal-exclusion worry (the feeling that the bottom level is already complete, so the top level has nothing left to do), what will predictably go wrong the first time and how I should handle it, and an honest note on which part of the idea the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself.
