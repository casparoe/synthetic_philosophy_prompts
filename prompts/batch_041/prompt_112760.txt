I'm a science journalist working on a feature about the free will wars, and I need a poem to open the piece. The hook is Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, the one that says you do what you do because of the way you are, so to be ultimately responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, but you can't be responsible for the way you are because that would require you to have created yourself, which is impossible. I want a poem that doesn't just explain this but enacts it.

Specifically, I want a pantoum -- you know, the form where the second and fourth lines of each stanza become the first and third lines of the next, and the whole thing loops back on itself at the end. The looping repetition should feel like the regress: every attempt to ground your responsibility in some earlier choice just hands you back the same problem, the same unchosen self. The voice should be someone who has absorbed the argument, who really believes no one is ultimately responsible, but who still catches themselves blaming a friend or feeling proud of something they did, and can't quite shake it. Maybe the poem ends with the first line again, so the reader feels the trap. Keep it maybe 4 or 5 stanzas, no need for rhyme if it gets in the way, but the repetition has to be exact. I dont want a philosophy lecture in verse; I want something that a philosopher would read and say, yeah, that's the argument, and that a non-philosopher would read and feel a little vertigo. (I'm not looking for citations or anything -- just the poem.)
