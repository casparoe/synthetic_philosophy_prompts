ok so ive been reading about this project from Hofstadters group called Letter Spirit, where the goal is a program that designs a whole alphabet in one consistent style once you show it a handful of letters. the drawing isnt the interesting part, its the thing nobody could ever quite pin down: what makes an 'a' an 'a'. you can slant it, fatten it, give it an enormous bowl and no tail, draw it like its from a script youve never seen, and it still reads as an a. push a little more and it flips into a 'd' or a 'q' or just a squiggle. so theres something that survives all the surface variation and its not any one feature, its more like the job each part is doing. and style is the other half of it: a style isnt a property of a single letter, its a commitment you make across all of them.

write me a sestina about that. six end words, and i want them to be the six things a person would name if you asked what makes an a an a. bowl, stem, counter, tail, axis, whatever you think belongs on the list, your call, but they should be parts that only mean something by the role they play. keep the permutation, thats the fixed skeleton, the thing that doesnt change no matter whose hand it is. but each stanza should be a different hand, different diction, different syntax, different temperature, so the six words keep coming back around doing new work. the point should be that the essence isnt in any single stanza or any single part, its in the relation between them.

one more thing and this is the part i actually care about. i want the hard case in there, the a thats been pushed just past the line. do it in the fifth stanza or the envoi: let one of the six end words show up as a homonym doing the wrong job, so the same word is present but the part isnt, and leave it to the reader to decide whether the poem still has six of everything. dont resolve it, dont explain it in the next line.

and please no platonic form talk, no greek, no soul of the letter business. if the poem is going to say what the invariant is, it has to say it in terms of what the strokes are doing.
