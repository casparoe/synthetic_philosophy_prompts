My officemate Nadia is a formal epistemologist who has somehow made "how do you update on a conditional" her entire personality, so every Friday the rest of us feed her little updating puzzles and write down what she says. The setup we always start from: a target is either in the northern sector (N) or the southern sector (S), and the relay tower either has it in range (R) or it doesn't. Her priors: P(N) = P(S) = 1/2, P(R|N) = 1/4, P(R|S) = 1/2, so P(N&R) = 1/8, P(N&~R) = 3/8, P(S&R) = 1/4, P(S&~R) = 1/4. Her stated rule, which she is insufferable about, is: take whatever the message announces as a constraint on my posterior, then pick the posterior that minimizes relative information (KL) from my prior.

Her verdicts so far:

- Scout says the target is in range. She says P(N) = 1/3.
- Scout says he'd put it at 30% that the target is in the north and in range. She says P(N) = 0.6.
- Scout says he'd put it at 70% that the target is in range. She says P(N) = 31/75, about 0.413.

(I checked all three and they are indeed the minimum-relative-information answers from her prior subject to the announced constraint, so she isn't just being cute.)

Now the case I actually care about. The scout says he can't tell her which sector it's in, but he can tell her that if it's in the north then it's in range. What does she say P(N) is? I'm less interested in the number than in which rule she's running: I think at least two defensible readings of a conditional message fit everything above and give very different answers here, and I want to know which you'd bet on, what each reading predicts, and why you'd favor one. Then tell me which case would actually separate those readings, because I have a feeling the case I just described is a bad separator — I can think of a pair of readings that will agree on it by accident, and I'd rather find one where they don't. And if you think the honest answer is that she'd refuse to commit until I tell her how the scout decides what to say, fine, but then still tell me what she'd say if she just took the message at face value.
