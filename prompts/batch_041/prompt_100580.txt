I've been reading the Iliad with a small book club, and it has dragged me into an argument with a close friend that I keep losing on grounds I can't quite articulate. His position is that the Trojan War is simply a fiction: the poems were composed many centuries after any supposed events, the only physical trace is a mound whose destruction is as easily explained by earthquake or accident as by siege, and the whole thing is therefore an invented foundation myth onto which later Greeks projected their self-image. I keep wanting to reply that something must have happened, but when I press myself for a reason I find I am only reporting a feeling. I would like you to convert the feeling into a number, and to show me its scaffolding rather than just the number.

So: what is your credence that there was a real military conflict in the Late Bronze Age, in the region where the poems place it, of roughly the kind the tradition presupposes — a coalition besieging a fortified city — regardless of whether any individual named in the poem existed or any narrated episode occurred as told? Please state a single probability, then decompose it into the sub-questions it actually rests on. I would expect those to include at least: how much weight the absence of contemporary attestation deserves for an event of this kind in this era; how much independent purchase the archaeological traces at the candidate site genuinely give; how reliable an oral tradition can be at carrying a kernel of event across several centuries; and how we should divide credence among something like a real siege, a real but very different conflict later attached to a famous site, and no historical event at all. Assign each a rough probability with one line of justification, say how they combine into your final figure, and tell me which sub-question the number is most sensitive to — the one where a modest change in your estimate would move the total most.

Then, briefly, run the same decomposition for King Arthur and for the Exodus, so that I can see whether the method produces the same shape of answer in each case or whether these traditions are genuinely different in kind — and if they differ, tell me which sub-question is doing the work of the difference. Finally, tell me how much confidence to place in the whole exercise, where the reasoning is most likely to be wishful or to beg the question, and single out the one claim in your decomposition that you are least sure of.
