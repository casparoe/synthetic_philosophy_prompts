I'm a second-year PhD student working on animal signalling — honest signalling, the handicap principle, indices versus signals, all that literature — and my partner is in comp lit and has got me reading Tolstoy on art. The more I read the more I feel like a huge chunk of it should just fall out of my own vocabulary, and I can't tell if thats because he was onto something or because I'm translating a moral theory into a descriptive one and quietly flattening it. So what I want is this: take his account of art and restate it as faithfully as you can in the terms I actually think in. Art as the transmission of feeling from a sender to receivers by way of external signs; the claim that how well the transmission works depends on how individual the feeling is, how clearly it comes across, and how sincere the artist is; the long middle section about most of what gets called art being counterfeit, churned out by trained professionals who feel nothing for an audience that wants to be flattered and distracted; and the payoff, that real art is the kind that can move anybody anywhere — peasant, soldier, lady — while the fashionable stuff just binds little cliques together. Do it with senders, receivers, indices vs. honest signals, cost and handicaps, receiver psychology, manipulation and arms races, ritualisation, coalitional bonding, transmission fidelity — whatever actually fits, and tell me where it fits badly.

Then the residue report, which is the part I care about. Does his sincerity requirement survive at all, given that in my world a signal is reliable because it's costly to fake, not because the sender means it? Isn't his verdict on exclusive art exactly backwards from what the theory would predict — that exclusive art is an expensive, hard-to-fake badge of membership, so of course it exists and of course it's stable, and condemning it is a bit like condemning peacocks? And what about the religious criterion, the idea that the art of a period should be judged by whether it expresses the highest religious perception of that period — is there anything in my vocabulary that could carry that, or is it just the kind of thing a signalling framework structurally can't say? What I really want to know is whether the residue means my vocabulary is missing something real about art, or whether it means he was doing something else entirely — telling people how to live rather than explaining what art does. Be blunt, I'd rather find out I'm wrong than get a diplomatic answer.
