In my department a colleague argues for metaontological deflationism with an analogy to the reclassification of Pluto: astronomers settled whether Pluto counts as a planet by voting on a definition rather than by discovering a fact, and so — he says — the standoff between the mereological nihilist, who denies that any composite object exists, and the universalist, who holds that any collection of things composes a further thing, is likewise a matter of where we choose to draw a line rather than a substantive discovery about what there is. From this he concludes that the composition debate is a verbal dispute, that the two sides share all the underlying facts and merely talk past each other, and that the sensible response is to stop doing ontology and instead get clear on our conventions.
