So I'm running a six-week adult-ed course on the Holocaust and the idea of modernity, and I want to end on a film, but I'm sick of teh reading where Schindler's List is just one good man saving some people. Can you read the film against the Historikerstreit and against Bauman? What I'm chasing is a specific tension. Bauman's whole point is that the killing came out of modern bureaucracy -- division of labour, chains of command, keeping your hands clean while you do your small task -- and that the Holocaust wasn't a throwback to barbarism but something modernity made possible, even normal. Meanwhile the Historikerstreit was that 80s fight in Germany over whether the Holocaust is unique or one atrocity among others (Nolte's camp saying it's comparable and partly a reaction to Bolshevism, Habermas's camp saying that line relativises and normalises it). My problem is Amon Goeth. Spielberg gives us a man who shoots prisoners off his balcony for sport, a sadist -- and that looks like the exact opposite of Arendt's banality-of-evil point and of Bauman's petty bureaucrat. So is the film quietly working against the structural story? Does it make evil personal and monstrous when the theory insists it was ordinary and administrative? And if it does, is it doing the thing Habermas was scared of -- handing us individual villains and individual redemption (the list, the survivors at the end, the whole breakdown where he wishes he'd sold more of his possessions) so that the modern order never actually gets indicted? I also want you to be honest about where the film resists this and I'm wrong -- where does it actually show the machinery, the paperwork, the ghetto administration, the way the system turned people into tasks? Is there a reading where Schindler isn't a hero at all but just another cog who happened to be vain enough to keep his workers alive? And I vaguely remember Lanzmann hated it, called it kitsch and objected to seeing all of this through a German's eyes -- is he right that a film like this can't help but betray the thing it's about? Finish by telling me wether the film shows something the historiography can't argue, if anything.
