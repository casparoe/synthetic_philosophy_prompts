I'm in a small reading group on hedonism and we keep circling the same argument, so I want to commission a dialogue instead of another session. Two characters, equally sharp. One is a lexical threshold negative utilitarian — she holds that there's a line of suffering so severe that no amount of lesser pleasure can ever outweigh it, so it's impermissible to inflict even for enormous good. The other is a flat commensurabilist: pleasure and pain sit on one scale, everything's tradeable if the numbers come out right. I dont want the threshold person winning by being obviously the nicer one, and I dont want the commensurabilist written as a cartoon who's secretly fine with torture. Both need real arguments.

The case I want them to fight over is made up, so neither can just cite whoever already wrote about it. Call it the Ledger. A city gets a machine — physically implausible is fine, I dont care how it works — that runs on one child's continuous agony, lifelong, held in a locked ward. In exchange it produces a mild but genuine and lifelong pleasure for every other person alive. The threshold person wants to say the child's pain is off the scale entirely and the deal is unthinkable however many beneficiaries there are. The commensurabilist wants to say fine, but then you're the one refusing a world that's net better by any measure, so produce the principle that isnt just squeamishness or a refusal to do arithmetic.

Push them into the weeds. What exactly makes a pain 'lexical' — its intensity, its duration, the kind of experience it is, or the fact that it's imposed on someone who never consented? Is lexicality a claim about a single experience or about a whole life, and does it attach to the world being worse or to the act being forbidden? If you keep steepening the commensurabilist's curve, does the threshold view collapse into just a very steep tradeoff, or is there a discontinuity that survives? Can the commensurabilist actually name the exchange rate, or does 'everything's tradeable in principle' dodge every concrete question? Let the threshold person press on whether a mild pleasure for billions even retains its value when it's purchased that way, and let the commensurabilist press on whether 'lexical' is doing any work beyond 'I really dont like this one.'

They should each land a real blow and each concede something, and end without a tidy resolution — I want to see where the disagreement bottoms out, whether it's a disagreement about value or about what a person can be asked to endorse. Write it as a dialogue with turns, not an essay with two names attached.
