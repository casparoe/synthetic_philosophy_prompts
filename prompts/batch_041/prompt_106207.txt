Ok so I'm putting together a session for a phil-of-bio reading group on the species problem, and I'm sick of the format where everyone lists the same dozen species concepts and then we all shrug and say 'well, it depends what you want the concept for'. I want to do something more concrete. Can you act as a philosopher of biology who's actually refereed in this area and knows which of these debates are live versus which are basically zombies?

Start by brainstorming out loud: give me the species concepts that are genuinely still in play — not the historical curiosities, but don't leave out the unfashionable-but-defensible ones either — and one line each on what that concept says a species is. Include at least the biological, phylogenetic, ecological, cohesion, general lineage, and homeostatic-property-cluster views, plus whatever else you'd actually defend.

Then score each concept from 0 to 1 on the five criteria below — these are mine, for the syllabus, so please use these and don't swap in the standard textbook desiderata — give a weighted total, and finally a tier list (S/A/B/C/D) with one line per concept on why it landed where it did.

Criteria, weights in brackets:
1. [0.30] No fake lines: it should only count as a species groupings that are real, causally integrated units, not arbitrary cuts in a continuum.
2. [0.20] Weird-case portability: it has to give a sane verdict for asexual lineages, allopolyploids that speciate in a single generation, ring species, hybrids, and fossils — not just ducks and deer.
3. [0.15] Law and conservation: its verdicts should be usable for endangered-species listing without absurdities, e.g. every slightly distinct local population becoming a protected 'species', or a hybrid swarm counting as two species at once.
4. [0.15] Nomenclatural cost: adopting it shouldn't force us to throw out the ranked Linnaean system and the type-based codes — or if it does, I want that cost priced in explicitly.
5. [0.20] Honest pluralism: it should be able to sit alongside the other concepts without the whole thing collapsing into 'a species is whatever a given biologist feels like today' — i.e. it should say something about what's shared across the concepts, not just carve out its own turf.

Then the part I actually care about (alot of the above is just setup):
(a) which concept wins on my weights, and which one you'd bet on if the weights were yours instead;
(b) whether any two of my criteria can't be satisfied at once — I have a suspicion that 1 and 3 are in tension, and I want to be able to show the students where the real fight is;
(c) flag every score that's really a guess about a case you're unsure of, rather than smoothing it over.

Think it through carefully before you commit to the numbers, and please don't hedge your way out of giving actual scores.
