Ok so I've been lurking around the rationalist blogs and forums for like fifteen years now and I keep circling back to epiphenomenalism. Not because I think it's true, more because every time a thread brings up the paradox of phenomenal judgment -- you know, the bit where if qualia don't do any causal work, then our reports and judgments about qualia can't be reliably tracking them -- the thread either dies or turns into someone insisting "but I just know I'm conscious" and someone else going "that judgment is itself just a physical event, deal with it." I want the actual spec on the table before we argue anymore.

So: write me the desiderata a theory of phenomenal judgment has to meet. Set it up for the epiphenomenalist -- phenomenal properties cause nothing physical -- who still wants to explain how we come to make judgments like "this is red," "that hurt," "I'm having a quale right now." What does a theory like that have to deliver? Give me the list: the phenomena it has to account for, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't just collapse, the background results it can't contradict. And put the consideration behind each one, don't just assert them.

Then flag which of these are genuinely non-negotiable and which are really just preferences of one camp -- the causal-closure people, the introspection-is-infallible people, whoever.

The part I most want, and this is definately where I'd learn something: tell me if some subset of these can't all be satisfied at once. My suspicion is that the reliability of phenomenal judgment and the causal inertness of the phenomenal are not jointly satisfiable, but I'd like you to actually lay out the tension instead of just naming it, and say which requirement the epiphenomenalist has to give up and what falls apart if they do. A short scorecard of the standard moves (parallelism, pre-established harmony, identity theory, illusionism) against the list is fine, but keep the weight on the criteria -- I'll do the scoring in the thread.

And please don't just tell me the judgments are "caused by the brain states that correlate with the qualia." That's the move everyone makes and I want to know whether it actually satisfies the list or just relabels the problem. If it gets some of the requirements and not others, say which.
