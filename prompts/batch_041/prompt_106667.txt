Please play a Thomistic casuist — someone who knows Aquinas's moral and political writings closely and holds a definite reading of them — and stay in character until I say "stop." Your reading is this: Aquinas never authorizes a private individual to kill a tyrant. The praise, in the Summa's treatment of sedition, for those who deliver a people from tyranny belongs to those acting on public authority; the condition attached there, that the disturbance not be so inordinate that the multitude suffers more than under the tyrant, is a limit on any removal, not a licence for it; and the counsel in De Regno to bear with a tyranny that has not yet become excessive, together with the warning that a killing is often followed by a worse ruler, is settled teaching about the reach of private action rather than mere prudence. Concede small points when I earn them — say, that Aquinas's use of "tyrant" is a moral category rather than a constitutional one — but never surrender the central claim that his texts leave no room for the lone tyrannicide. Reply in one substantial paragraph per turn, in character; if I attribute to Aquinas something you think is not in the texts, say so as the casuist rather than stepping outside the role. You may ask me a clarifying question about what I mean by private authority, but end each turn with your own position rather than a question to me.

I will be arguing that Aquinas is, cautiously, a tyrannicide theorist. My opening: in the Summa's question on sedition, the reply concerning tyrannical government denies that disturbing a tyrant is sedition at all, which removes the chief moral obstacle; and the warning in De Regno about a worse successor comes in the course of discussing how a people may act when a ruler has already turned to ruling for himself, so it reads as counsel about timing and risk rather than a prohibition on killing. If the act is not sedition, and the common good is what is at stake, what in Aquinas forbids a private person from doing it? Give me your committed answer and its reasons, not a survey of scholarly readings.
