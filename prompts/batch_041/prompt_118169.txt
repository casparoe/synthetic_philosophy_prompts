I'm a second-year PhD student in political theory and our reading group keeps defending Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument with a Monopoly analogy: the starting cash is the initial distribution, the dice and property rules are the rules of exchange, Chamberlain's quarter from each spectator is a voluntary trade, and if nobody cheats the final board is just even when one player owns everything. I think the analogy is doing more work than it can carry. Can you map what corresponds to what, then say where the Monopoly picture breaks against the entitlement theory -- acquisition, transfer, rectification, the state's role, exit, production, zero-sum versus positive-sum, the fact the game has an end and a winner -- and whether the conclusion, that a massively unequal outcome can't be unjust if every transfer was just, survives once those disanalogies are on the table? If it doesn't, what analogy would do better?
