My little sister's wedding is in three weeks and I'm giving the toast, and I've gotten myself stuck on something I can't stop chewing on.

Quick picture of the room. It's outdoors, no church, they wrote their own vows. My mom's side is Catholic, and my aunt has already told me twice that a promise with no God behind it is just a nice feeling you had one afternoon. The groom's brother is a lawyer, and he says "I promise" is a handshake with extra steps — it only means anything because we all agree it does, so the minute the agreement stops working for you, nothing is actually holding you. And most of my friends are 26 and think a promise is basically a stay-true-to-yourself thing, where the real sin is betraying who you are. Three completely different pictures of what a promise even is, all in one backyard, plus the two of them standing there.

Here's what's got me. I don't think I could say what makes a promise bind. Not the pretty version — the actual one. When I promise my friend Dana I'll drive her to the airport at 5am, what did I just do? I didn't predict the future; I know I might oversleep. It isn't a contract; nobody's suing me. It isn't a feeling; at 5am I might feel nothing at all. And here's the part that really gets me: if I blow it off, she's the one who gets to be angry at me. Not my boss, not my mother, not even the general public. If I steal something or lie to a stranger, anybody can call it out. But a broken promise belongs to one person. She can also just let me off the hook — nobody else can do that for her, not even my mother, who forgives me for everything. That feels like it's the whole thing somehow, and I can't get it to sit still.

There's a wrinkle too: my dad's been gone six years and I still feel like I owe him something I promised him, which makes no sense if a promise is between two living people, one of whom can hand you a receipt.

Now the wedding part. Their vows have a line in them I can't decide if I love or hate — something like "we promise to keep choosing each other" and "we promise to keep renegotiating." Is that a promise, or is it a way of not making one? If the deal is that you can always reopen the deal, what did you tie yourself to? A promise to keep promising seems like it's either the most honest kind or not a promise at all, and I honestly can't tell which.

Also, a friend said something I half believe: a promise you keep because you promised is worth less than one you keep because you want to. That sounds wise, and it also sounds like it would make the vows worthless on exactly the days they'd matter most.

So: write me the toast. Four or five minutes, 600–700 words. It has to land in that backyard for the lawyer, for my aunt, and for my friends, without telling any of them they're wrong about something they'd defend at dinner, and without turning into a lecture. No God talk either way — half the room would flinch and the other half would nod, and I'd lose one of them no matter what I said. I want the toast to actually turn on the answer to why promises bind, not have a philosophy paragraph stapled to it. If the answer is one thing the toast says one thing; if it's another thing it should be a different speech. And no quotes from famous writers, no names of philosophers, nothing I'd have to get right — I'd butcher it and my uncle would know. If you want to use a position, describe it in plain words.

Don't preface it, don't tell me what you're doing, just start with the toast. Then, after the speech, give me a short note: where in it you took a side on something people actually fight about, how confident you are in that side, and the one claim in the whole thing you're least sure of. Definately flag the least-sure part — that's the one I'll be thinking about at 3am.
