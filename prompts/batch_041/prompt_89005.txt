I'm running a reading group on the science of consciousness this semester and I want the first handout to be a decision tree instead of me lecturing at people. Heres what bugs me: every time we hit global workspace theory, half the room treats it as a theory of phenomenal consciousness and half says it was only ever about access, and nobody can tell me what actually turns on that disagreement. So build me a one-page flowchart that walks a student from a few commitments to a named position on what global workspace theory (and its rivals) are really theories of.

Ground rules:

- No branch node may name a theory or a theorist. Every node has to be a question someone could answer from the cases, or from a claim about what consciousness is -- not from already knowing which team they're on. Names only at the leaves.
- Build the branches first out of the logical space and then drop the positions into the slots, so I can actually see which slots stay empty.
- Start with the target of explanation: when someone calls a state 'conscious,' do they mean the subject can report it, that its available to guide thought and action, or that there's something it's like to undergo it? Then let the tree pull those three apart.
- I need a branch for the possibility that the phenomenal/access gap is really a gap between two kinds of access -- access-for-report vs. access-for-guidance-and-control. I've been pushing that line and I want to see where it lands people. If it lands them somewhere nobody actually occupies, say so and diagnose why.
- Cases that have to show up as nodes: Sperling's partial report, the no-report paradigm work on binocular rivalry and continuous flash suppression, blindsight, inattentional blindness and the gist studies, the feeling that peripheral vision is rich, attention without awareness vs. awareness without attention, and dreaming.
- Leaves must include at least: global workspace as a full theory of consciousness, global workspace as access-only with a leftover phenomenal residue it doesn't explain, higher-order theories, local recurrence views, illusionism, and panpsychism or neutral monism. Add whatever else the logic needs.
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't separate, and say what extra node would do it, or whether nothing will.
- Mark the unoccupied combinations and say whether nobody's there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
