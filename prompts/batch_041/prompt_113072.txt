I sit on the ethics & public policy committee of a mid-sized professional association (~40,000 members, mostly clinicians), and at next month's meeting we have to vote on a 'personal climate responsibility' resolution that our sustainability working group drafted. As written it (1) urges members to cut their own air travel and red meat, (2) asks them to log whatever they cut in a voluntary app, and (3) commits the association to match every logged kilo of CO2 with an offset purchase out of our own budget, up to a cap. It was written mostly by junior members.

Two things make this hard. A past president I respect has circulated a one-page dissent saying the whole thing is incoherent: no single member's flight or steak makes any measurable difference to anything, so the resolution either demands something that can't be justified or it's moral theatre, and either way we spend our credibility on the easy thing while our pension still holds a serious chunk of fossil fuel equities — which he says is the only item actually on the table that moves emissions, and the one we're not touching. The working group's reply is that his argument would dissolve every collective obligation anyone has ever had, and that a body like ours has to say something.

I don't need a survey of the free-rider / collective action literature; I've read the usual drop-in-the-bucket material, and so has everyone else on the commitee. What I need is the written opinion I'll circulate before the vote: a concrete recommendation (adopt as written, adopt with specified amendments, reject, or defer to a named date on a named condition), the reasoning for it, and a fair statement of the strongest case against my own recommendation — including the pension point, which I suspect is the real crux and which I don't want waved away. Be straight with me about which parts of this are genuinely settled among people who work on it and which are live disputes, and don't hand me a contested line as if it were the consensus.

Then a short JSON block I can paste into the minutes, with fields: recommendation (string), one_line_reason (string), amendments (array of strings, empty if none), strongest_objection (string), settled_vs_contested (object with two arrays of strings: settled, contested), what_would_change_the_recommendation (array of strings), and confidence (number between 0 and 1).
