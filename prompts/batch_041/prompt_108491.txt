I am a philosopher preparing a seminar on cross-national sex differences, and I need help naming a specific inferential move rather than a correlation. The pattern is this: in countries with more egalitarian institutions and greater female labor-force participation, certain sex differences in occupational preference or personality are sometimes larger, not smaller. Commentators then treat the residual difference as the signature of nature: once material and legal constraints are equalized, whatever difference remains must be biological. But consider two countries with identical scores on standard equality indices, one of which has a strong cultural norm that men should be engineers and women nurses, while the other has no such norm. If the first shows the larger sex difference, the same data are equally compatible with a cultural explanation—egalitarian institutions remove economic pressures that once pushed men and women toward similar roles, allowing culturally transmitted gender norms to express themselves more freely. The egalitarian country is not a culture-free baseline; it is a particular cultural configuration. The move I want to name is the inference that reads the residual difference as nature's fingerprint while treating culture as a constant that has been controlled away. Is there an established term for this move? If not, coin one and define it, distinguishing it from the correlation itself, from the broader tendency to treat unexplained variance as nature, and from ordinary biological hypotheses. I want the carving, not just a label: what exactly is assumed, what would count as a counterexample, and when the inference might be legitimate. Please begin by brainstorming candidate concepts before giving your answer.
