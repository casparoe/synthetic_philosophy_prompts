I hold that the world happiness rankings measure happiness — that the ladder scores are a valid indicator of how well people's lives are actually going — and that a country trying to climb them is therefore pursuing something worth pursuing. What would count against this? I want a map of the position's exposure rather than a list of caveats: what it predicts that its rivals don't, which observations or arguments should move me, and where it has been qualified into something no evidence could touch.

Two things to keep in view. First, the aim I'm defending is to climb the table, so part of what I want is a rank and not just an absolute score — I'd like to know whether aiming at a relative position is incoherent, or merely zero-sum in a way that changes what a government should do. Second, here is the test I would currently treat as decisive: if a country could raise its score by making its people's lives worse — for instance by getting respondents to report what they take to be the expected answer — I would drop the position. Tell me whether that is the right test, and what else should move me, including whether the ladder's reference point is genuinely shared across cultures.
