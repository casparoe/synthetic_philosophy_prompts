I am a novelist, and I keep circling a problem that I suspect philosophy has already named, though I cannot find the name.

Two scenes, both of which I have written and neither of which I can get right. In the first, a woman has wanted one thing since childhood — to run the family shop, say, or to be a surgeon. The wanting is entirely hers: nobody stands over her, nobody has lied to her, and if you asked she would give an accurate account of the alternatives. But the want was planted before she could have refused it, by people who had one idea of what she should become, and watered so patiently that it is no longer a mask she wears but the shape of her wanting. In the second, a man gladly accepts an arrangement — the terms of the only job he has ever been offered — and defends it in argument, and defends it partly because it is the only arrangement he has ever seen. In both scenes I want to say the agreement is real: no false belief was induced, no threat was made, the person is grown, of sound mind, and unambushed. And in both I am unwilling to let the agreement count for everything, though I could not tell you what is missing from it.

Is there an established concept for this? It seems to sit near two things — the idea that wants can be shaped by circumstance and then sincerely reported as one's own, and the older suspicion that some desires are not really the person's own — but those look like claims about welfare, or about oppression, and I need the version that bears on whether an agreement is binding. So: does the configuration have a name; which adjacent concepts come closest, and where exactly does each fail to fit; and is my second scene the same phenomenon as my first or a different one in similar clothes (a shaped want versus a shaped menu of options)? If nothing established fits, coin a term and stake out its edges. In particular, say whether the defect lies in the agreement, in the situation, or only in our confidence about it, and whether it vanishes when the shaping was kind. A bare name will not help me; I need the carving. Answer in under 400 words.
