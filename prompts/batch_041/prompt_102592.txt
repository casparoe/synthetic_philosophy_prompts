I'm sitting in on a phil of langauge seminar and we've just done Twin Earth and semantic externalism, and I've got a bee in my bonnet about dragging it over to AI. What I want from you is the most important implications of externalism -- the idea that what our words mean depends on the environment and on a causal/historical chain running out to the stuff in the world, not just on what's going on inside our heads -- for whether a large language model actually means anything by its words at all. Please don't just hand me the textbook Putnam water/H2O story, I've heard it a hundred times; start from the machinery and push it somewhere new.

The cases I want you to actually lean on: Putnam's original Twin Earth (same stuff in the head, different referent out in the world); Burge's arthritis case, where a man believes the arthritis in his thigh is flaring up, but his word still means the community's arthritis -- joints only -- so he's got a false belief rather than some private concept, and the same belief would be true in a community that used the word more loosely; and Burge's slow-switching case, where someone shuttles between two communities that use a word slightly differently and never notices, so the contents of her beliefs quietly shift without her ever being able to tell from the inside. Now, a model has no eyes, no hands, no causal contact with any water, and no community of speakers correcting it -- only a corpus. So what are the implications? Does it get narrow content but no wide content, and if so does that mean it can't refer at all, so its sentences come out neither true nor false? Or does the corpus itself count as the causal chain, in which case externalism might actually say the model does mean something, and the twist is that it means whatever the corpus's usage meant -- a weirdly deflationary result. Tell me which way the externalist has to go and what each fork costs.

And then the bit I actually care about: the most important implications for me, a person who argues with chatbots. Can I disagree with one? Can it lie to me? Can two models trained on different corpora mean different things by the same sentence, and does any of this bear on whether "understanding" is even the right word? I'd much rather you be honest about where externalism goes silent than force it to give an answer.
