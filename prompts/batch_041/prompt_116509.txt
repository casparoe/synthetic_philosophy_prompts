So design the game. The core thing I want: they have to try to intend to do a costly thing later, where by the time they'd actually have to do it the prize is already banked and they'll have no reason to. And I do not want a magic oracle — I want a live predictor, another student, with their own skin in the game, so that "I hereby decide to intend it" has to survive a motivated reader of intentions. That's the bit the students need to bang into.

Give me the roles, the payoff scheme (no real money — chocolate, points, small priveleges, whatever), a minute-by-minute timeline for the 90 minutes, and how the prize gets handed over before the costly act. Then a debrief that maps what actually happened onto Kavka's puzzle: the two horns, and why you can't just choose the intention.

Please build in these beats:
- Round one: they can try to decide to intend, freely, no tricks. Let them hit the wall themselves — the predictor doesn't believe them, or does and they fold at the moment of truth.
