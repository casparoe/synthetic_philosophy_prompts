I run a small futarchy pilot for a regional transit agency — the idea being that the board votes on the values and a prediction market tells us which of two policies actually delivers them. Four or five decisions a year, each one tens of millions of dollars and hard to undo. It has been going badly and I need a procedure I can actually run, not a defense of the idea.

Two things went wrong this year. On the fare question, the market said adopt, and then our analyst pointed out that the number we'd been reading off the board — the value of the metric if the fare change passes — isn't that number at all, it's the odds the change passes times the value it has if it does, and we'd been treating the product as the thing itself. On the depot question, the metric we'd pre-registered was going to be measured by the very contractor the policy would hire, so the board overrode the market, and now nobody trusts the process.

Write me the procedure. The questions to ask in order, starting before we even open a market, what counts as a sufficent answer to each, where the procedure stops with a hard no — there are decisions we should never run through this — and where it hands off to the board because the question is values rather than beliefs. Who is allowed to trade, and whether we subsidize the people the decision actually lands on, should be seperate gates with their own answers. It has to be runnable by a staffer with no economics training, in about a week, and it should say who signs off at each gate.

Then run it on three cases: (1) raise fares 15% versus hold fares and cut weekend service, metric is ridership plus farebox recovery; (2) pick vendor A or B for a scheduling system, metric is on-time performance in 18 months; (3) a one-year guaranteed-income pilot for 300 riders, metric is employment among participants. At least one of these should come out as don't run a market on this, and I'd prefer it to be the one I'm most attached to. Finish by telling me what the procedure can't do — I know it can't tell me whether my metric is the right metric, but I want to know exactly where that gap sits and what the least-bad patch is.
