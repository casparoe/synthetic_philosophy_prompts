Two people are arguing about van Fraassen's Reflection principle — the rule that if you're certain you'll be, say, 90% confident in something at some later time, then you should already be 90% confident in it now, i.e. you should defer to your future self. One of them thinks it's just obviously true, since the later you will have all your current evidence and then some. The other thinks it only holds when the future credence gets produced by a process you'd now endorse, and to press the point he offers this case: tonight you're dead certain that by tomorrow you'll be 90% sure your relationship can still be saved, and you know the reason is that tomorrow you'll have spent the morning rereading old love letters, which you know has a habit of talking you into things you'd see through today. Write out their back-and-forth — both of them sharp, neither a strawman, each one actually responding to the other's last move — and let them fight it out over whether the second guy's restriction saves Reflection or just quietly empties it of content.
