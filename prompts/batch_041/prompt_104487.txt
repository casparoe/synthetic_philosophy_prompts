So I just lost three hours to an argument with my friend Priya about that Escher print where two hands are each drawing the other one, and I need to write down what I actually think before I see her again, because right now I honestly can't tell if I have a consistent view or just vibes.

Here's my gut, case by case.

- The hands. Genuine. This is the real thing, the gold standard, the picture you'd point at to explain it to a kid. Nothing above it, no hand outside the frame.
- Two mirrors facing each other. Not the same thing at all, even though it's the same shape -- each one makes the image the other one makes, same as the hands. But it's just a regress. Nothing's happening. A photocopier pointed at its own output would do the same, and nobody calls that profound.
- A constitution containing the rule for how to amend itself, that rule included. Genuine. The system is writing its own ground rule.
- An agency that issues rules binding every agency including itself. Fake. There's a statute over its head and a legislature behind that. Somebody outside always signed the thing that let it bind itself.
- A compiler that compiles its own source code. Genuine, but it doesn't feel strange to me, and I can't tell whether that's because it isn't or because I stopped being impressed by computers in 2004.
- A dictionary that defines every word in it using only words from inside it. Mirror case. Looks closed. The meaning isn't in there.
- A sentence that says of itself that it's false. I want to say this isn't a loop at all, just a busted sentence. But then Godel pulls basicly the same trick and I'm perfectly happy to call that a loop. So one of those two verdicts has to give, I just don't know which.
- The whole Hofstadter thing about the self being one of these loops. Half in. Some days it's obviously right and some days it's a pun.

And the one I keep flipping on is the hands again. Because by my own mirror verdict the hands should be fake: there's an artist outside the frame who drew both of them, and paper underneath, and I'm looking at it from a level neither hand can reach into. So either the hands are the gold standard and my "somebody's always outside it" test is garbage, or the hands are the mirror case and I've been wrong about them my entire life. Also, for what it's worth, Hofstadter says a tangle like this always has a level underneath that the loop can't reach into -- the paper, the machine running the program -- and if that's true then maybe none of my "genuine" ones are genuine and Priya wins.

Priya's position was that I'm just tracking how clever a picture makes me feel, that every item I called genuine has a level outside it too (the hardware under the self-compiling compiler, the legislature over the agency) and every item I called fake has a loop buried in it somewhere, so there's no line to draw. I don't think thats right but I couldn't say why in the moment.

So: is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? State it properly, each clause with the case that forces it. Then tell me what it predicts for two cases I haven't ruled on -- a grammar book written in the language it describes, and a market where the traders price things using a model of the market they're pricing in. And be straight with me about which of my verdicts it can't save. If I have to give one up, tell me which one, and please don't rescue them all with something so hedged that it stops saying anything.
