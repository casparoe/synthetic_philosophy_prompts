My advisor and I are building a small app that watches how a person spends their time and money over a few weeks and then offers advice meant to fit their priorities — it is trained on what people actually do, not on what they say they want. We have been piloting it with about thirty volunteers, and two of the cases have split the lab, so I would like an outside read on how to handle them and, more basically, on whether our whole premise is well posed.

The first is a volunteer I will call Dana, whose data is about as clean as it gets: she orders takeout on nearly every weeknight and never opens the meal-planning feature. Trained on her behavior, the app concluded that convenience matters more to her than cooking and began nudging her toward faster options. In her exit interview she was upset. She says she does want to cook and eat better, that she orders out because she is exhausted from a job she cannot change right now, and — her words — that she would have cooked more if our app had not kept putting takeout in front of her. The second case is a volunteer who consistently picked the cheaper of two nearly identical items, week after week, until we added a third and more expensive option to the screen, at which point his choice between the original two flipped. Our engineer says the data speaks for itself and we should simply fit the choices; my advisor says we are measuring something real but mislabeling it. I can see both, and I cannot yet say what, if anything, would count as Dana's real preference as opposed to the preference her circumstances and our own menus produced.

What I want is advice on the concrete decision in front of us: when our app infers what someone wants from what they do, when should we take the behavior at face value, and when should we treat it instead as a report about the person's situation — and how would we tell the two apart using only the kind of data an app like ours can actually collect? If you think the honest answer is that what she really wants is not a fact we can recover from choices at all, say so plainly and tell me what we ought to be optimizing for instead.
