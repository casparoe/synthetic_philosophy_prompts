So I've been covering this spat for like three years now — the one between the people who think consciousness needs a "theater," a single spot (or single moment) in the brain where all the contents of an experience get assembled and lit up, and the people who say that's a leftover from a picture we should've ditched, that content gets fixed in a lot of places at a lot of times and there's no finishing line where it all comes together. They keep talking past each other on panels and honestly it's getting embarrasing to moderate.

What I want from you is the document these two camps should've written together: a joint statement that an honest member of each side could actually put their name to. So I need:

- the stuff both can sign off on — about what the brain is doing, about what the word conscious is supposed to pick out, about what would count as an explanation for either side;
- the real disagreements, each one written so that the person who holds it would say "yes, thats my view," not some strawman;
- the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move somebody;
- and for each crux, the test, case, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two things I want baked in, because the debate always dies without them. First, keep apart the claim that there's a single locus where content converges from the claim that there's a single subject who has the experience — I've watched half these fights turn out to be people sliding between those two. Second, use this case: imagine a brain-reading device that can only ever read one small patch of cortex at a time, and a subject doing a task where her report seems to hinge on two patches that light up roughly half a second apart. Ask what each side predicts about whether she had one experience or two, and whether the answer flips if the device could read both patches at once. While you're at it, I want the statement to say something about whether "fame in the brain" (or whatever the drafts people want to call it) is a real proposal or just a metaphor, and whether the theater people are committed to the theater being a physical place or only a logical requirement.

Format it as a numbered list of agreed claims first, then a table of disagreements with columns for the question, each side's position in their own preferred wording, the crux, the shared test, and each side's prediction. No lit reviews, no reading lists, no citations — I can't check them and neither can you.
