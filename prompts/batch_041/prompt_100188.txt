I'm not a philosopher or anything, just someone who reads popular science and gets stuck on things, and I've been chewing on a pattern I keep running into. It started when a friend tried to explain evolution to me and I kept asking how the eye knew it needed to get better, and he kept saying it didn't know, that the knowing-looking part was the result of a process with no knower. Then a few months later I was messing around with a little chatbot and had the same weird feeling in reverse: it gave me a correct answer to a logic puzzle, and I caught myself assuming somewhere inside it must have understood the puzzle, even though I know it's just predicting words. And it's not just those two. A thermostat wants the room at 70. A chess engine sacrifices a knight. Slime molds solve a maze. Ant colonies decide which food source is best. A river finds the shortest path downhill. In each case there's something that looks like a mind, or at least a purpose, or a bit of understanding, and then the actual explanation goes around the mind instead of through it: the appearance is produced by something blind, dumb, and mechanical, and the mechanical thing is doing all the work. The bit I can't get past is that this seems like one move that keeps showing up in totally different places, but I dont know if it's one thing or just a family of metaphors.

Is there a name for it? I have a feeling people have talked about this around Darwin and around Turing, but I don't know the actual term, and I don't want to be fobbed off with emergence or the intentional stance if those aren't it. What I really want is the carving: what exactly is the pattern, what's the nearest established concept, how does the thing I'm describing differ from that, and where are the edges -- like, is a thermostat the same kind of case as a language model, or is that stretching it? Also, is the interesting part the process that produces the competence, or the fact that we keep describing the product as if it had the competence, or are those two different phenomena that get lumped together? If there's no name, can you coin one and define it with a couple of boundary cases? Please give it to me as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no intro or conclusion.
