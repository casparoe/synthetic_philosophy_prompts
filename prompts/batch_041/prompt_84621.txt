I've been reading George Santayana's The Sense of Beauty (1896) on my own — no philosophy classes, just picking through it slowly at night — and I've hit a wall early on, in the part about the nature of beauty. The gist as I understand it: beauty isn't a property sitting in the object, and it isn't just any old pleasure either. It's pleasure that we've somehow come to experience as a quality of the thing itself. He calls it "pleasure regarded as the quality of a thing." So when I say the sunset is beautiful, I'm reporting a pleasure of mine but treating it as if it were a feature out there in the sunset.

Heres what I can't get past. If that's right, then beauty is anchored in my own feeling, first person, and it seems like nobody else's say-so could ever give me a reason to think something is beautiful. Me telling you a movie is beautiful is just me reporting my pleasure — why should that move you at all? And yet this happens constantly in real life: critics, teachers, friends, strangers on the internet, and sometimes it works. A friend tells me an album is beautiful, I keep listening, and eventually I hear it. So I want help sorting out whats actually going on in that case, and what Santayana would say about it.

Specifically: (1) Is there a real difference between someone's testimony causing me to eventually find something beautiful — like a nudge that retrains my ear — versus it giving me a reason to believe it's beautiful before I feel anything? Does Santayana's view leave room for the second one at all? (2) If beauty is objectified pleasure, how do we make sense of arguing about it? Why does it feel like we're disagreeing about the object rather than just comparing moods? (3) Does his account make other people's aesthetic judgments pointless by definition, or is there some way another person's judgment can legitimately guide mine? I'm especially interested in the case where I trust someone's taste in general and they point me toward something new — am I being irrational if I go in already expecting beauty?

I don't need a whole history of aesthetics, just help untangling this specific knot, and where you think Santayana's own text supports or undercuts what I'm saying. Also if this puzzle has a name, tell me what it is.
