I'm a retired microbiologist -- 35 years at a bench, mostly quorum sensing in soil bugs -- and since I hung up the pipettes I've been sitting in on a philosophy of mind reading group at the local university. They've asked me to run one session: 90 minutes, 14 people, mix of grad students and a couple of faculty, and I get to pick the topic. I want to do the do-these-things-actually-understand-anything business, because as far as I can tell the whole fight is about what would even count as evidence, and everyone in the room already has a verdict.

What I want from you is a game, not a lecture. My rough idea -- throw it out if it's bad -- is that the group becomes the machine. One person takes the input, several layers pass notes to each other, a last person writes the answer, and the thing I care about is that nobody in the chain ever sees the input and the output at the same time. Then we run it again with twice as many people and a fatter rulebook, the answers get noticably better, and I want them to feel the pull of saying a threshold has been crossed. And one round where we quietly swap the scripted chain for somebody answering out of their own head, without telling the questioners which round is which.

So: rules, roles, materials (index cards, a die, whatever), a way to score it that stings a bit without money, and a timeline that actually fits 90 minutes. Then the debrief, with the mapping from each moment to the real arguments -- including the one I'm half sure is a dodge, where you say the whole room understands even though no person in it does. I also want the debrief to separate two things my group keeps sliding between: nobody could tell the chain from an understander by what it says, versus the chain doesn't understand. Those aren't the same claim and I'd like them to catch themselves switching.

Don't let anyone name the standard thought experiments up front. And tell me honestly what will go wrong the first time I run this, and what the setup simply can't show -- I'd rather say that out loud than have someone catch me out.
