I am writing a paper that defends a broadly deflationary account of truth, and before I commit to it I want the specification of what a theory in this area has to deliver to count as adequate. Please write the desiderata for a theory of truth, putting the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them rather than on scoring the contenders. I will add citations myself, so do not give me references or a literature review — refer to positions and arguments by description, and write the list so that it stands on its own. Assume the theory is meant to be put to work in semantics and in the philosophy of logic, not merely as a device for paraphrase.

Organize the list around three jobs that I take truth-talk to do, and tell me whether treating them as one notion is a mistake or a convenience: first, an endorsing job, where the truth predicate lets us affirm a content we do not restate, as the equivalence schema is meant to capture; second, a generalizing job, where it lets us say something about indefinitely many contents at once, as in blind ascription; third, what I am calling the standing-of-inquiry job, where truth serves as the norm or aim of belief and assertion rather than as a device for endorsing or generalizing. For each job, say what a theory must do to discharge it, and whether a single notion can cover all three or whether the third forces a covert inflation of the first two.

I also want the requirements that are not about those jobs, each with the consideration that motivates it: how the truth predicate must behave in contexts that are not simple declaratives, including indexical, ambiguous, and non-assertoric sentences, and the liar family; whether a theory owes an account of the truth of contents that no speaker of our language could express; whether it must explain why true belief tends to issue in successful action; whether it must accommodate the intuition that truth has something to do with correspondence; whether meaning or compositionality needs truth as a theoretical notion; and any transparency or conservativeness constraint that ought to be placed on the truth predicate. For each requirement, state whether it is genuinely a constraint on any theory or a preference of one camp, and mark the ones you take to be non-negotiable.

Then the part I most want. Say whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and if so which; my own suspicion is that the standing-of-inquiry job and the endorsing job pull against each other, so that one can have a thin truth predicate for endorsing and generalizing, or a norm-governing aim of inquiry, but not both under a single deflationary heading — but I want your judgment rather than a restatement of mine. Finally, identify the single requirement that excludes the most otherwise promising candidates, and give a brief, non-exhaustive indication of how the standard deflationary options (redundancy, disquotationalism, prosententialism, minimalism, and expressivist or functionalist treatments of truth) fare against that one requirement. A short table is welcome for that last part.
