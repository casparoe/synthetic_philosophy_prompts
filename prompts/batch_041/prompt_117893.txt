Write a joke about the moral equality of combatants. Any form is fine — a one-liner, a short dialogue, a setup and punchline — but the joke should turn on the substance of the disagreement rather than on war in general: a pun on "collateral damage" or a crack about military intelligence won't do.

The disagreement, briefly, so that the joke can be built from it: on the traditional view, soldiers on both sides of a war have the same permissions and liabilities in fighting, so the justice of their war makes no difference to what a soldier may do or to what may justly be done to him; on the revisionist view it makes all the difference, because a soldier fighting for an unjust cause is contributing to unjustified harm and is therefore liable to be killed, while a soldier fighting for a just cause is not — so the permissions come apart on the two sides, even though neither soldier can tell from inside the war which side he is on.

Let the joke exploit something the two views genuinely dispute — the gap between being liable to be killed and anyone's being permitted to kill you, or the fact that the symmetry view applies a single set of rules of engagement to two morally unequal causes — and build it on the following invented case rather than on a stock example: two conscripts, one from each side, both drafted, both convinced their cause is just, both wounded in the same exchange of fire, end up in adjacent beds in the same field hospital. The punchline should depend on that situation. Someone who works on this debate should find the joke funny rather than a sign that I don't know what the debate is about; you can take it that the audience knows both positions, so there is no need to name anyone.
