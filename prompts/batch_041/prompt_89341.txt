ok so heres something that bugs me about how everyone talks about AI models.

When a chatbot gives a wrong but confident answer, people say it "believes" something false, or that it "hallucinates", or that it's "trying to please you", or "being evasive", or "confused", or "lying". Theres a whole cottage industry of interpretability work that goes looking for the models "beliefs" in its activations, builds "lie detectors", steers its "intentions". But then the same people, sometimes in the very same piece of writing, turn around and say its just next-token prediction, theres nobody home, stop anthropomorphizing, it doesnt want anything.

I want you to take that split seriously and ask what it presupposes. The framing I keep running into is the one where you can describe the same system from a physical stance (weights and what theyll do), a design stance (what it was built to do, what its for), and an intentional stance (treating it as a thing with beliefs and desires so you can predict it). And the further claim that some patterns are "real" in the sense that they earn their keep by letting you predict and compress, even if theyre not the fundamental story.

So: does the everyday practice of saying a model "knows", "lies", "wants", "is confused" commit you to anything about whether those intentional descriptions latch onto real patterns, or can you talk that way and still think theres nothing there but weights? Which stance is each bit of the practice actually running on -- when an eval says the model "knows the fact but wont say it", which stance is that claim even made from? Is there a real inconsistency between the anthropomorphizing talk and the "just next token prediction" deflation, or are they answering different questions and compatible after all? And if you decided the intentional stance picks out no real pattern here, only a handy fiction, what would have to change -- do we lose "hallucinate" and "deceptive", and could we keep the interpretability results that were phrased in those terms?

Try to separate what the practice strictly needs from what it just goes along with, and tell me where its genuinely underdetermined -- several views might license all the same talk.
