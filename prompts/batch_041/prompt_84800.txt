I am setting the final examination for a graduate seminar in metaethics, and a recent experience has made the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem unusually concrete for me: I was offered a significant professional advantage on the condition that I sincerely adopt a certain evaluative attitude, and I found I could not say whether the offer was a reason for the attitude or only a reason to bring the attitude about. I want one essay question that prevents students from simply reciting the demon case. Build it around a case in which the incentive to hold an attitude is also evidence that the attitude is fitting, say, a reliable judge offers a reward for believing a proposition, so the offer both pressures the belief and raises its probability. The question should ask what, if anything, distinguishes a right-kind reason from a wrong-kind reason in that case, and whether the distinction can be drawn without circularity. Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks. The marking scheme should name five must-hit moves that separate an A answer from a B answer and, for each, the specific mistake that should cost marks. The question must be answerable without citing any texts or authors.
