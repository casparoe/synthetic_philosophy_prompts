A friend and I have been playing a game on a rectangle of squares. The top-left square is poisoned. A move is to take any square together with every square below it and every square to its right; whoever is forced to take the poisoned square loses. I claim the first player must win, on this reasoning: if the second player had a winning strategy, I could open by taking the bottom-right corner, which removes nothing but that single square, and thereafter play the second player's strategy as though it were my own, substituting an arbitrary square whenever that strategy names one that is already gone. A square already eaten can only help me, so I would win — contradiction. My friend is unimpressed: I have not told him a single move to make, so he says I do not know how to win, and cannot even be sure that I would. Should I concede the point, and would it be reasonable for me to bet money on the outcome? What exactly does my argument establish, and what has to be true of a game for this style of argument to work at all?
