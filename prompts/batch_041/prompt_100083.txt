I'm a novelist — 300 pages into a book whose main character is a young actuary who gets pulled onto a dodgy weather-derivatives desk in the late nineties, and to write her convincingly I've had to actually make up my mind about what probability is, which I'd somehow avoided for forty-odd years. So here's where I've landed, and I'd like a consistancy check on it before I start revising the whole thing around these views:

(a) Chances are real features of the world. When I write that the coin has a 1-in-2 chance, I mean something about the coin and the flip, not something about my ignorance, and not just a fact about what happened on previous flips.
(b) I also think the world is deterministic. Given the force of her thumb and the air in the room, the outcome was settled before the coin left her hand. There's no genuinely open branch anywhere, in the book or in the world.
(c) A single case can have a chance. The coin gets flipped once, on page 298, and never again in the history of the universe, and I still think it's true that it had a 1-in-2 chance of coming up heads — same for the final draw of a lottery that's being shut down forever afterwards.
(d) The numbers in her tables — the 0.4% mortality rate, the 1-in-100-year storm, the 3% chance of default — are nothing but frequencies. That's all "probability" means in those sentences: the fraction of some reference class that did the thing. I get twitchy when characters in the book treat those as facts about one particular person or one particular storm.
(e) And when it's my own belief we're talking about, my credence should just be the chance whenever I know it. Seventy percent chance of rain, I'm seventy percent confident. Hedging is for people who haven't done the maths.

What nags at me is (a) and (e) sitting in the same paragraph — I have a feeling those are supposed to be rival theories and that I'm holding both like an idiot. And (b) feels like it's quietly strangling (a) and (c): if the flip was settled before it left her hand, in what sense was there a 1-in-2 chance rather than a chance of 1? But I also suspect that at least one of the things bugging me is my own confusion between two different jobs the word "probability" is doing, and I don't want to gut a novel over a phantom. So go through these, tell me which ones actually pull against each other and which only look like they do, rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be and what I'd lose by taking it. If there's one single change that clears up the most for the least cost, name it. And please don't send me a reading list, I'm not going to read it — I want the audit, not the syllabus.
