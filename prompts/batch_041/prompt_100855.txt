I have been reading around the attempts to explain what it is for a thought, a signal, or an inner state to be about something by appeal to biological function and history rather than to a mind that reads it off, and before I go any further I want a clear statement of what a theory of that kind would actually have to deliver. Write me the requirements list: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the findings it must stay consistent with, with the consideration behind each requirement, and say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp. The purpose I have in mind is modest but specific: I want a theory usable by someone who wants to say what an organism represents without smuggling in an interpreter, not a theory that also has to settle consciousness. I am most interested in whether the requirements can be met together, and if some subset cannot, which subset that is, since that would be the result I care about most.

Here are the cases I keep worrying at, and I would like each to earn its place on the list rather than be waved at. A bird has an innate alarm call that its ancestors gave for hawks; the hawks are gone and what now sets the call off is a drone of similar shape. A signal that once carried information about food has been taken over for courtship, so that nothing about food need be true of the caller. A creature has an inner state that is never in fact correct, because the thing it was to be about no longer exists anywhere. A state whose conditions of correctness seem to come apart between something narrow, such as a shape or a wavelength, and something broad, such as a danger or a kind of thing, with nothing in the organism's history to settle which is meant. A mechanism that produces a state reliably in a creature that never puts it to any use. And I want the theory to allow a state to be simply wrong in the ordinary way, a false belief or a misfiring detector, without that making it about nothing at all. If it is useful to add which of the familiar positions, the ones that fix content by what the mechanism was selected to detect and the ones that fix it by what it is now used for, fail which requirement, add that briefly, but the list is what I am asking for. Keep it to continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, the way one would set it out in a letter. And please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, rather than presenting a contested view as the consensus.
