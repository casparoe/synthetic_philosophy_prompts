I am a retired insect ecologist who spent forty years in agricultural research and who, since retiring, has been reading moral philosophy and serving as a lay member of a regional pest-management ethics committee. The committee reviews proposed field trials and emergency exemptions for pest-control technologies. It has no formal charter; its minutes are terse, and I have come to suspect that its past decisions are consistent with more than one principle. I would like your help in predicting what it will do with the next proposal, and in seeing which principles its record actually commits it to.

The committee's decisions to date are these:

1. It approved a field trial of a synthetic pheromone mating disruptant for codling moth in apple orchards. The compound is volatile, affects only codling moth, kills no insects directly, and can be discontinued at any time. Some organic growers complained about drift and cost, but the committee approved.

2. It rejected an emergency exemption for a broad-spectrum pyrethroid spray against aphids in a watershed containing wild salmon. The spray would have prevented a substantial crop loss, and the growers intended to kill only aphids, but runoff would foreseeably kill aquatic invertebrates and damage salmon habitat. The committee rejected it.

3. It approved a sterile-insect-technique program for medfly. The program irradiates and releases millions of sterile flies; some non-target fruit flies are caught in monitoring traps and killed, and there is a small risk that the released strain could displace a native relative. The committee approved, citing the fact that the program replaces repeated broad-spectrum spraying and can be halted.

4. It rejected the introduction of a generalist parasitoid wasp for biological control of an invasive scale insect. The wasp would probably reduce insecticide use, but it might attack native moths, and once established it could not be recalled. The committee rejected it.

5. It approved a contained greenhouse trial of a systemic neonicotinoid on ornamental plants. Runoff was captured, no flowering plants were present, and the committee judged pollinator and waterway exposure to be effectively zero.

6. It rejected outdoor use of the same neonicotinoid on a flowering crop during bloom, because pollinators would be exposed, even though the treatment would have reduced spraying.

7. It approved a push-pull intercropping system that used a repellent plant and a trap crop to reduce pest infestation without synthetic pesticide. The system required more labor and diverted some land, but the committee approved because it was non-toxic and easily reversed.

Now a company proposes a field trial of a new sequence-specific RNA interference agent, R-114, for control of an introduced beetle pest in a mosaic of treated and untreated fields. R-114 is applied as a spray, silences a gene required for reproduction in the target beetle, and does not kill vertebrates or pollinators. It also silences the same gene in a closely related native beetle that shares a 19-nucleotide sequence; that beetle is a common detritivore and predator of pest eggs. The effect on the native beetle is expected to be sublethal but to reduce its reproduction. R-114 persists in soil for two growing seasons, though application can be stopped. The company estimates that the trial would reduce broad-spectrum insecticide use in the area by about 70 percent. The native beetle is not endangered, and the target beetle is not a vertebrate.

I am less interested in a bare verdict than in the committee's implicit rule or rules. What are the plausible principles that fit its seven earlier decisions equally well? What does each principle predict about R-114? Which precedent is the closest analog, and which principle should the committee adopt if it wants to be consistent with its own history? If the precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, say what principle the committee would be adopting with each available verdict, and identify the further case, not R-114 itself, that would best distinguish the competing principles. Please give me a reasoned recommendation, with the closest analog identified and an honest statement of what principle the committee would be adopting either way.
