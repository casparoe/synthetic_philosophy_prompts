I've been thinking alot about this "cluelessness" thing with consequentialism — the worry that we can't really know whether our actions do good or harm in the long run. But I don't think that's the real problem. My view is that the problem isn't that we're bad at predicting consequences, it's that there's no such thing as "the consequences" of an action at all — consequences only exist relative to some description we pick, and different descriptions hand you totally different sets, so "do the most good" doesn't even point at a determinate thing to do. What would count against this? Rank whatever should actually move me, most decisive first, and tell me straight whether I've just worded my view so that nothing can touch it. Would someone who takes the usual cluelessness worry seriously say I'm making a different point, or just botching theirs? And which bits would a consequentialist simply shrug off? Honestly the thing that'd move me most is a concrete case where the description-relative stuff turns out not to matter.
