I'm three drafts into a novel that I keep describing to people as "a book about a machine" and I'm starting to believe it myself. The setup: a retired instrument-maker has spent thirty years building a table the size of a swimming pool, and on it a few thousand little steel marbles roll around under magnets; the marbles clump into shifting blobs, the blobs seem to chase and block each other, and by chapter nine two of my characters are having a full-blown argument about whether the blobs are doing anything or whether the marbles are doing everything and the blobs are just a story we tell about them. It's a riff on that old careenium thought experiment — Hofstadter, I'm fairly sure, the one where tiny magnetic marbles ("simms", I think he calls them) clump into "simmballs" and you're supposed to ask who shoves whom around in there — but I dont want to just restage his version. I want a case built to order, and I'd rather it be yours than mine.

Hard constraints, please treat these as load-bearing and not decoration. (1) Everything on the table has to be the kind of thing you could buy at a hobby shop — magnets, soleniods, springs, ball bearings, photocells. No new physics, no quantum dice, no hidden homunculus in the cabinet, no late reveal that the table is secretly one big mind. (2) There has to be one concrete event — say a particular marble going left instead of right at a particular instant — that has both a complete parts-level story (this marble got shoved by that marble because the magnet under it switched on because...) and a clump-level story (the left-hand blob was collapsing and the marble got caught in the squeeze), and the clump-level story has to be the one that lets you steer the thing: intervene on the clump-level fact and the marble really does go the other way, and you can do it again next Tuesday. (3) The case has to make three positions come apart in a particular way. The parts-only view has to say the blob never pushes the marble, the marbles push each other and "blob" is bookkeeping. A real-patterns view — the one that says a higher-level pattern is real just in case it's robust and you can predict and intervene through it — has to say the blob does push. And a strong-emergence view has to also say the blob pushes, but for a different reason: one that implies the parts-level facts don't fully fix the clump-level ones.

Here's the tricky bit, and it's the whole reason I'm asking. I need the real-patterns person and the emergence person to agree about the verdict on every intervention anyone inside the story could actually perform, and to come apart only over a counterfactual nobody in the story can test — something like whether the exact same parts-level history could have run and produced a different clump-level outcome. So the disagreement has to be invisible from inside the machine's world. Then do the work: after the scenario, walk through what each of the three says and exactly why, tell me which single feature of the case is carrying the weight (my hunch is it's that every possible intervention is itself a parts-level event, but I want you to tell me), what happens to the whole thing if I cut that feature, and how the parts-only person would try to show the case is rigged — and whether the attack works. It's for a scene where neither character is allowed to win by pointing at the gears, so if the case can be won by pointing at the gears, that's a failure and I need to know that too.
