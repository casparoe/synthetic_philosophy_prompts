I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of religion, and I am writing a commentary for a workshop on divine foreknowledge. The paper under discussion is my supervisor's, and she works in a broadly Molinist idiom. Her framework divides divine knowledge into three categories: natural knowledge, her label for God's pre-volitional knowledge of what is necessary and of what any possible creature could or could not do by its nature; middle knowledge, her label for God's pre-volitional knowledge of what each possible creature would freely do in any complete set of circumstances it might be placed in; and free knowledge, her label for God's knowledge of what actually comes about, which depends on his decree. She insists, as Molinists characteristically do, that middle knowledge is pre-volitional, but I have never heard her say whether that constraint bears on the content of a counterfactual's antecedent or only on what grounds the counterfactual's truth.

For several weeks our reading group has been assigning counterfactuals to exactly one of these three categories, and her rulings have not wavered:

1. If Peter were in the courtyard, he would deny. — middle knowledge.
2. If a stone were released from a height, it would fall. — natural knowledge.
3. If Peter were offered a large sum to apostatize, he would refuse. — middle knowledge.
4. If Peter had been raised among the Essenes, with all other relevant circumstances fixed, he would not have denied. — middle knowledge.
5. If Peter had been struck mute before the fire, he would not have denied. — natural knowledge.
6. If there were a creature whose nature is indistinguishable from Peter's, and it were placed in the courtyard, it would deny. — middle knowledge.

Our next session is built around a case she has not yet ruled on:

7. If God had decreed to create a creature with Peter's nature except that timidity takes the place of his actual boldness, and had also decreed to place that creature in the courtyard, it would deny.

I have to forecast her ruling on 7. Please do not stop at the label. Set out the rules that fit her rulings on 1 through 6 equally well; say what each of those rules predicts about 7; give your best guess as to which category she will assign it, with your reasons for preferring that reading of her; and name the further counterfactual that would most efficiently force the rival rules apart.
