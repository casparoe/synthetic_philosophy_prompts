Not a philosophy person at all — I'm a backend engineer — but I've been goign down a rabbit hole on how to split a fixed pot fairly between exactly two parties (compute budget between two teams, a bonus pool, whatever). I've ended up with a list of princples I'm pretty sure a fair split should obey, and I want a sanity check on whether they actually hang together or whether I've just collected axioms that each sound nice but can't all hold at once.

(1) Never leave value on the table — if there's an outcome where both sides do better, take it.
(2) It shouldn't care who's who: swap the two parties and the split just swaps.
(3) You can't change the answer by changing the units a party measures its own satisfaction in.
(4) If one side's best possible outcome gets better, with the other side's best possible outcome left unchanged, that side shouldn't end up worse off than before.
(5) The split shouldn't move just because a new option appears that neither side would ever pick — irrelevant alternatives shouldn't matter.
(6) What happens if there's no deal at all (the fallback) should shape the answer, not just the best cases.

I have a nagging feeling these don't all survive contact with each other, but I can't tell which pairs are genuinely fighting and which only look like they are. Rank the conflicts by severity, tell me which are only apparent, and give me the cheapest one to drop plus what I actually lose by dropping it.
