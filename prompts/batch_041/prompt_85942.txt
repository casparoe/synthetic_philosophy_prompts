I host a small interview series on philosophy, and my next guest defends a positive pure rate of time preference: she holds that future welfare should be discounted for the bare fact of its being later, not merely for the chance that it never arrives, and she grounds the discount in a condition of reciprocity or answerability that future persons cannot meet. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press the view rather than let her restate it. Include at least one question on the familiar complaint that the date on which a benefit falls is not in itself a reason to care about it less, with a follow-up for her likely reply that the present stands in a special relation to us; at least one on the asymmetry that we do not discount past suffering, and what that asymmetry shows about her ground; at least one on whether what she defends is genuinely a pure time preference or whether the reciprocity condition is doing the work, in which case it would also discount the welfare of anyone unable to reciprocate, including distant strangers and the severely incapacitated, and whether her ground has anything to say about a person discounting her own later welfare, where no second party is involved; and at least one on aggregation, namely what a positive rate does to the claims of people who would live very far off in time, and whether the rate is defended partly because it keeps the total finite. Close with a question about what would change her mind.
