Having spent a career in the natural sciences and only lately taken up philosophy, I keep returning to a question I cannot settle: whether higher-level entities ever push the lower-level substrate around, or merely summarize it. Reading about the careenium — a box of tiny magnetic marbles ("simms") that clump into larger clusters ("simmballs"), the clusters being where the meaning is supposed to reside — has only sharpened my unease, because my verdicts on analogous cases seem to me hard to square. I am confident that natural selection pushes gene frequencies around: "the environment favored the thicker beak" strikes me as a genuine causal claim, not a redescription of who happened to be born and who died. I am equally confident that a person's decision to raise her arm pushes her motor neurons. Yet when a starling flock wheels as one, I do not think the flock's turn pushes the individual birds; each is only tracking its neighbors, and that is all. A thermostat's setpoint does not push its bimetallic strip, and yet the ballgame that lets out at the stadium plainly causes the traffic jam, and the jam in turn causes me to miss my train, even though I would deny that the jam as such pushes any individual car. My tentative hunch is that a higher level counts as a genuine cause only when the parts are tracking it rather than merely producing it, but I cannot tell whether that saves the verdicts I want or merely relabels my preferences. Is there a principled line through these cases, which of my verdicts does it force me to abandon, and what does it imply for the careenium itself — do the simmballs push the simms, or only the reverse?
