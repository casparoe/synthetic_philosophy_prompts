I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've been trying to write down where I stand on what it takes for a system to genuinely represent something, since the question keeps coming up in arguments about AI. Before I commit to this in writing, I want a consistency check. Here is where I've landed:

1. There is a real distinction between the intentionality a system has in its own right and the intentionality it borrows. A road sign, a sentence, and a calculator mean what they do only because someone uses them to mean that. A person means things on her own, and I would say a dog does too. This is roughly the original-versus-derived distinction I associate with Searle.

2. Whatever grounds that distinction cannot be a fact about what is going on inside the system. A case I have seen attributed to Dennett makes this vivid for me: two internally identical coin detectors, sitting in environments containing different things, are correctly described as registering different things even though their internal states match. So content is fixed at least partly by environment and history, not by internal state alone.

3. I am a naturalist. I don't think any non-physical ingredient is needed, so whatever makes intentionality original has to be a physical or functional feature of the system or of its history.

4. My own beliefs have original intentionality. I am not willing to say that my belief that it is raining is merely a convenient way for others to predict my behavior.

5. What a system represents is fixed by what it was selected or designed to do, that is, by its function. This is also what makes misrepresentation possible: a frog snaps at a passing pellet, and we say it misrepresented a fly.

6. Concretely: I have written code in which a variable holds a temperature reading and drives a fan. I don't think that variable represents the temperature in anything like the way my belief that the room is warm does. It is just a state that happens to co-vary with the temperature and drive behavior.

7. Current language models have at best derived intentionality. Their content is borrowed from the text they were trained on and from the purposes we put them to.

I suspect that some of these pull against each other, but I can't tell which. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each real one, give me the cheapest fix and say what that fix costs me elsewhere. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most at once. I'm not looking for reading suggestions, just the audit.
