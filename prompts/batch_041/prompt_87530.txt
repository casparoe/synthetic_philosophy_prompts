I'm a novelist — three books in, none of them historical, so this is new territory for me — and I've somehow been talked onto the little committee that's putting together our town's First World War centenary exhibition at the county museum. Not my idea, but my neighbour runs the museum and I owe her about nine years of favours. The trench letters, the ration books, the medals, the whole tasteful arrangement is sorted. What we're stuck on is the last wall, the one that tries to say something about why it happened and who, if anyone, was to blame. The draft text a volunteer wrote says, roughly, that Germany and Austria-Hungary carried the main responsibility for starting the war. Two of the committee are furious about it. One, a retired schoolteacher, says you simply cannot blame a whole nation for anything — a nation isn't a person, it has no mind, half the people in it were peasants who'd never heard of Sarajevo, so the sentence isn't just unfair, it's meaningless, and printing it means doing propaganda instead of history. The other, who's read a lot more than I have, says that's a dodge: of course "Germany" isn't a person, but the men who ran the foreign office were, and they made choices, and when a state has options and definately chooses this one, saying "nobody is to blame" is itself a moral claim, and a cowardly one. I have to write the panel text and I hold the deciding vote, and honestly both of them sound right to me at different moments of the day, which is a horrible way to walk into a committee meeting. I keep having this feeling that they're arguing about two seperate things while using the same word.

Can you help me figure out what the actual question is here, and what a sensible person could reasonably put on the wall? I don't want a bibliography or a reading list, I won't chase down citations, and please don't lean on some famous book you assume I've read. I just want to understand the shape of the disagreement well enough to write three or four honest sentences, and to know whether the retired teacher is making a real point or just making a fuss. And don't just tell me everyone's a little bit right — if I'm confused about something basic, or if the whole way I've set this up is muddled, say so straight out rather than being polite about it.
