ok so heres the thing i keep going back and forth on. suppose we actually build the thing -- a system that is, lets say, a few orders of magnitude better than any human at modeling other humans, and it genuinely wants to talk to us honestly. it can compute, for any sentence it might utter, the effect that sentence will have on your beliefs, your mood, your actions three years out. not magic, it just understands cognition way better than we do.

now: is there any way for it to talk to us that counts as *not* manipulating us? i want your actual credence in this claim, stated as precisely as you can make it:

(Claim) There exists a policy for choosing utterances, available to a system like that, that is simultaneously (i) maximally informative -- it conveys all the evidence relevant to the listener's decision that the system has -- and (ii) non-manipulative, in the sense that the listener's resulting beliefs would be justified by the listener's own epistemic standards, and not merely caused by the system's superior model of the listener.

give me a number, then decompose it. here are the sub-questions i think it breaks into, add/remove/rename as you see fit:
- does 'non-manipulative' pick out a real property at all, or does it collapse into 'influence i happen to disapprove of' once the asymmetry gets big enough (p?)
- granted it picks out something real, can a vastly smarter agent reliably hit the target -- or does hitting it require exactly the kind of fine-grained model of the listener that only a manipulative system would bother to build (p?)
- is (i) even coherent for a listener who can't process everything the system knows, or do (i) and (ii) trade off against each other (p?)
- could the listener verify which policy the system actually used, or does the whole thing rest on trust we have no way to ground (p?)
then combine them into your number.

one concrete case i want you to keep in the background, because i think it changes the answer: the one-shot warning. the system has a single sentence to get a human to evacuate a city. it knows exactly which sentences work, including ones that work by exploiting a bias rather than by informing. is there a sentence it can utter that is both maximally informative and non-manipulative, or is that case where the claim dies?
