A colleague with whom I co-convene a reading group on scientific classification has been working through a long list of candidate natural kinds, and I have kept a record of her verdicts. The chemical elements she counts — gold, carbon, and the rest — because their instances share a constitution that explains why they behave alike. Water she counts as well, and heavy water gives her no pause: she treats it as a variety of the same kind rather than a different substance. Jade she refuses, because the name covers nephrite and jadeite, two minerals that share little beyond a look and a market. Biological species she counts, though she denies that they have essences, holding that a species is bound together by gene flow and a shared ecology. Race she refuses. She refuses to count the vitamins, which she calls a functional category defined by a role in human nutrition rather than by anything its members share. Prion diseases she counts. Major depressive disorder she refuses, calling it a clinical convenience rather than a discovery. Ecosystems she refuses too, on the ground that their boundaries are drawn by the investigator rather than found.

Next week the group takes up metabolic syndrome: the diagnosis given to a patient who has three or more of a list of five markers — central obesity, raised triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, raised blood pressure, and raised fasting glucose — each read against a cutoff. No one disputes that the five markers tend to travel together in a certain population of patients. What is disputed is whether they do so because of a single underlying process, such as insulin resistance, or whether the label simply collects several distinct conditions that happen to co-occur.

I want to predict her verdict. Set out the rule or the rules she is most plausibly running; I am confident she has a principle and not merely a list of favourites, though I would also like to be told if no single rule fits the whole record and which of her verdicts pull against the others. For each candidate rule, say what it implies about metabolic syndrome and why. Then tell me which rule fits her record best, what verdict that rule yields for metabolic syndrome, and how confident I should be that she will in fact give it. Finally, name the category I should put to her next in order to force the surviving rules apart, and explain what it is about that case that does the separating work.
