I'm doing lexical documentation on an under-described language (upper Sepik, two-year project, I'm the only linguist on it) and I have to put an English gloss on roughly four thousand headwords before the dictionary goes to the community language committee, who have the final say and who may well end up using it in the bilingual school programme — so I can't write something vague and hope nobody notices. Elicitation happens in a trade language I speak decently but not natively, and it doesn't mark plural on nouns at all, which means half my questions about one-versus-many come back ambiguous. The one that's eating my time is a word for a small furry animal, call it tumu, which turns up whenever one of the things is around. My colleague glosses it as the animal; I keep thinking it might be better treated as a stage, or as an undetached part of one, or as just the stuff. What I've got to work with is this: no plural marking; numerals take classifiers, and tumu takes the same roundish-shape classifier as a lot of other nouns, including ones for lumps and fruits; there's no general word for part or piece, only a verb-ish form used when you break a bit off food; and there's one word for same, which is what I've been using to ask whether this tumu is the same as that tumu — except my collegue says that if tumu means rabbit-part then same shifts too and my test is circular, which I think is right and which leaves me with nothing. I'm not asking you to adjudicate whether Quine's indeterminacy is a real phenomenon; I need to know what to write in the dictionary.

So: give me a decision procedure I can run per headword in about twenty minutes at the desk. The tests in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because whats at stake is a documentation convention rather than a fact about the language. Then run it on three cases — tumu; a palm-tree word that also names the fruit; and a word for a stretch of river, or river water, I genuinely can't tell yet — and include at least one where the procedure tells me something I won't like. It has to be honest about where it can't settle anything: I take the point that any body of behavioural evidence is compatible with a systematic re-assignment of what the words pick out, so tell me what the documented default should be once the tests run out, and how to record the alternatives without pretending I've resolved them.
