I manage a team that builds autonomous decision systems, and for about a year I have been in a recurring argument with our legal, safety, and ethics reviewers about whether systems like ours create a responsibility gap. The disagreement has not moved, and I would like a diagnosis rather than another round.

The positions, as concretely as I can state them: One group holds that when an autonomous system takes an action that no designer intended, no operator specifically authorized, and no human could have foreseen in that token case, there is no person who satisfies the ordinary conditions for blame or liability. On this view the gap is real and is a reason to restrict deployment. A second group holds that responsibility is a social practice of assigning answerability and repair, not a metaphysical property that must be traced to an individual act of will; we can and do assign responsibility to owners, operators, and designers, so the gap is an artifact of an individualistic picture. A third group says the real issue is not moral responsibility at all but accountability infrastructure: logging, audit, insurance, licensing, and the practical ability to halt or revise the system. A representative case we keep returning to is a system that continuously updates its policy from operational data; its behavior is predictable at the population level but not for any particular decision. The engineers say it is doing what it was built to do, legal says no one authorized that particular decision, and ethics says the engineers should have known the class of decisions.

My hypothesis is that this is not one disagreement but several, and that the word responsibility is concealing at least two different things. I have been using a distinction between trace responsibility, by which I mean the ability to reconstruct why the system did what it did and who set the relevant objectives and constraints, and steering responsibility, by which I mean having the standing and the practical ability to revise those objectives or constraints. It seems to me that the first group is often talking about a trace gap, the second about the social distribution of steering responsibility, and the third about the institutional machinery that could make either one effective. I may be wrong about that, and I do not have formal training in philosophy.

What I want from you is a diagnosis of the impasse. Is it a verbal dispute about the word responsibility, a substantive moral disagreement about the conditions under which blame is appropriate, a hidden empirical question about predictability and control, or several different questions being answered under a single heading? If it is substantive, what exactly do the sides disagree about: the facts, the standards of evidence, which cases count as data, or bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for? If it is partly verbal, what is the substantive question underneath? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, an empirical result, a change in the question, a legal or institutional reform, or nothing, because the disagreement is not the kind of thing that gets resolved? Please engage the positions as I have stated them rather than giving me a survey of the literature.
