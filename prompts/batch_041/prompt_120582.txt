I'm a third-year philosophy student, and I've been asked to contribute a couple of minutes of material to a departmental comedy night. Please write me one joke about the intentional stance and AI systems. The setup should be a hypothetical company that trains its chatbot by reinforcement learning from human feedback — a procedure that only makes sense if you treat the model as something whose behaviour can be shaped by rewarding it, that is, as an agent with preferences it is trying to satisfy — and which then, once the model is sued, insists in its terms of service that the model has no beliefs and no intentions whatsoever. The punchline should turn on the instrumentalist reading of the stance: attributing beliefs is a predictive strategy one can adopt and drop at will, not a claim about what is really going on inside the machine, so the company's two positions are not simply inconsistent — they are the same instrument, turned in whichever direction pays. It should be funny to people who know Dennett's three stances, and shouldn't require any background beyond that. The audience will be mostly philosophers, with a handful of machine-learning students who will notice anything sloppy.
