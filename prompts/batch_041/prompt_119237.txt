I'm in this wierd spot where I write a small newsletter about rationalist-adjacent ideas, and for the next issue I want a piece of sustained satire on the whole coherent-extrapolated-volition thing — the idea that we should do what our idealized selves would want, where the idealization means more knowledge, faster thinking, and better character. A friend of mine who is way too into prediction markets keeps saying it's obviously the right basis for aligning AI, and I want to poke fun at that without just doing the usual but whose values? dunk. Could you write an Onion-style product review, maybe 600–800 words, of a consumer subscription called something like IdealMe or Reflect, which promises to extract your coherent extrapolated volition from a few quizzes, your Spotify history, and a webcam interview, and then make all your major life decisions for you? The review should be written by a satisfied customer, deadpan, with a star rating and a would-recommend-to-a-friend line. The jokes should come from the actual structure of the idea: the gap between your stated preferences and your higher-order preferences, the fact that extrapolating even one person's preferences is underdetermined, the way any extrapolation procedure has to pick some idealization of you (more reflective? more informed? more your true self?) and that choice does a lot of hidden work, the possibility that your extrapolated self would look back at your current self with pity, and the way the service might quietly launder socially adaptive preferences into what you really wanted all along. I don't want it to be a generic tech-dystopia bit; the humor should be recognizable to someone who has actually read the CEV literature and argued about it at 2 a.m., but still readable to someone who hasn't. Also, if you can work in a fake customer-support exchange where the agent explains that the algorithm cannot tell you what you would want if you had the character you wish you had, because that version of you would also have different wishes, that would be great. And maybe one detail where the app's onboarding asks you to rank 30 ice cream flavors, then asks how you'd rank them if you had a PhD in ethics and unlimited time to reflect. No citations or anything needed.
