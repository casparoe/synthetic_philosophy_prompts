Ok so I write about science and ideas for a living, and I've fallen into a hole I cant climb out of, and I think you can help.

The hole is called lexical threshold negative utilitarianism. I tripped over it while reporting something adjacent — longtermism, the EA crowd, the usual — and my rough gloss goes like this: suffering counts for more than pleasure, easing it beats creating its opposite, and then somewhere up the scale there's a line where suffering gets so bad that no amount of pleasure on the other side can ever buy it back. Below the line, pleasure and pain are just two ends of one ruler. Above it, I guess they aren't. That's my best stab and it might be a bad one.

What I can't do is figure out whether that's what I actually beleive, or which version of it. So let me just tell you what my gut does on cases, and you tell me what theory I'm running.

- Me standing on the train for forty minutes so a stranger gets the seat: obviously fine, I do it without thinking. Little pain, little pleasure, they trade off.
- One person tortured for a day so that a whole stadium of people each get a year of bliss: no. Not at any number. Add zeros to the stadium forever and I still say no.
- Given a hundred bucks to spend, I'd ease one person's severe, grinding pain before I'd buy ten people a really great day.
- But I'd buy those ten people the great day before I'd spend it on one guy's stubbed toe. A stubbed toe is a stubbed toe.
- And here's the one that ruins me: if the only other option were torturing one person for a day, I think I'd let ten million people each have a genuinely miserable week. Not torture — just unrelenting pain. Which means enough ordinary agony does outweigh torture, and I've just contradicted myself two bullets up.
- Possibly the same contradiction wearing a hat: I'd give someone a lifetime of steady contentment in exchange for one week of agony they'd never even remember. Unless the week is bad enough, in which case I wouldn't, and I can't find where the line moved.

So: do these add up to lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, or to a cousin of it, or to two theories sharing one coat? Give me the precise principle — or the two rival ones — that captures the most of what I said, tell me what it predicts about a case I didn't list, and be straight with me about which of my verdicts I have to give up to keep it. If the honest answer is that I'm holding two commitments that don't fit, say that too.

Last thing, and maybe this is the actual question: is "lexical threshold" doing any work here, or is it a fancy way of saying that really bad things are really bad? And my hunch — that pleasure is basically just the absence of pain, and pain is the only one of the two that's actually there — does that fall out of my verdicts, or am I importing it from somewhere else?
