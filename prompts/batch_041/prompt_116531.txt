I'm trying to get my head round a tension inside Molinism that I think usually gets glossed over, and I want a really careful explanation of it rather than the standard summary.

The setup, as I understand it: God has middle knowledge, which is prevolitional knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, like "if Bob were in that situation, he would freely do X." These are supposed to be contingent, not necessary like mathematical truths, but also not dependent on God's will, because if they depended on his will they'd just be free knowledge and come after the decree. So they sit in between natural and free knowledge. Fine so far.

Where I get stuck is: what makes one of these counterfactuals true? One route says it's grounded in the creature's individual nature, so God supercomprehends that nature and thereby sees what that creature would freely do. But then the choice looks like it follows from the nature. And if it follows from the nature, two bad things seem to happen: the truth looks necessary rather than contingent, since the nature is just what it is, and the creature's choice looks determined by its nature, which kills the libertarian freedom the whole view needs. But if we dont ground it in the nature, what's left? Either a brute true counterfactual just floating there with no truthmaker, which lots of people find unacceptable, or grounding it in the creature's act, which seems circular, since the act doesn't exist yet at the point where God is supposed to know the counterfactual.

Can you walk me through this properly? Specifically: (1) lay out the grounding problem for counterfactuals of creaturely freedom as sharply as you can, including why the prevolitional status matters; (2) explain the supercomprehension / individual-nature route and whether it can avoid collapsing middle knowledge into natural knowledge; (3) explain the main alternative moves, like brute counterfactuals, grounding in the creature's essence, grounding in the free act itself, and whatever else is on the table, and what each one costs; (4) tell me which response you think actually works, or whether the threefold division of divine knowledge is unstable for a deeper reason I haven't noticed.

I dont need names, dates, or citations. I want the conceptual structure and a clear map of where teh real pressure points are.
