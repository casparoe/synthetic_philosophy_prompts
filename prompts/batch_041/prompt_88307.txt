I'm a retired geologist — 30 years in the oil business — who's spent the last couple of years reading philosophy, and I've bogged down in the procreation asymetry. The claim, as I understand it: coming into existence can be bad for you (pain is bad, and if you don't exist there's no pain, so that's good even though nobody's around to enjoy the good), but it can't be good for you in the same way (pleasure is good, but if you don't exist, the absence of that pleasure isn't bad for anybody, so nothing is lost). I see why people want it — without something like it you seem to slide straight into the conclusion that a colossal population of barely-worth-living lives beats a small happy one — but the debate around it has clearly stalled. The people who find it obviously true keep restating it in four lines, and the people who think it's incoherent keep saying you can't compare a person's life to nonexistence, and that the asymmetry only looks plausible if you help yourself to an impersonal 'good' in one line and refuse it in the next. Nobody has moved in decades, and I don't think either side is being stupid.

So I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a real disagreement about facts or values, or a verbal one about what 'better' and 'harm' are doing when one of the things being compared doesn't exist? Is it two camps answering different questions under one heading — one about which outcomes are good, one about what we owe to people who are or will be actual? Is there a hidden formal question underneath, e.g. whether a short list of innocuous-sounding conditions on ranking populations can all hold at once (I've read that results like this exist, which would mean the asymmetry can't be derived and has to be taken as primitive)? My own half-formed hypothesis is that everything turns on whether 'better' has to be better *for* somebody, and that once you allow it to be better simpliciter you've quietly changed the subject — but tell me if that's just me importing my old habit of asking what a comparison is relative to. And whichever way you diagnose it, tell me what would actually count as progress here: a distinction, a proof, a change of question, or nothing at all, and why.
