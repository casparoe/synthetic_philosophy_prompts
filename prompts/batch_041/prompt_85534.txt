Write the joint statement that a committed internalist and a committed externalist about reasons for action could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The internalist holds that a consideration is a reason for an agent only if sound deliberation from that agent's existing motivations could lead him to act on it; the externalist holds that an agent can have a reason to act that no such deliberative route reaches. Open with a numbered agreed-claims section: what both accept about the work reason-statements do (explaining action, guiding deliberation, licensing advice and blame), about the status of the stock cases (the amoralist, the fully informed but wholly unmoved agent), and about which of their differences they now judge to have been merely verbal, turning on how the word reason is being used. Then a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated in wording its own side would accept, the crux it turns on, what each side would count as evidence against its own view, and, for each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Treat two things as cruxes and say whether either is one both sides agree would actually move the debate: an agent whose concern for a friend is erased by a targeted intervention, leaving her fully informed and otherwise psychologically intact, so that the question is whether the reason survives the erasure; and the dilemma that internalism is either vacuous, if sound deliberation may generate new motivations, or false, if it may not.
