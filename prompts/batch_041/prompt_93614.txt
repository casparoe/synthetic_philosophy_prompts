Write a joke about the dark forest hypothesis: the proposed resolution of the Fermi paradox on which every technological civilization is a hunter moving silently through a dark forest, required to destroy any civilization whose location it learns, because the chain of suspicion (each side must worry about the other's intentions, and about the other's estimate of its intentions, and so on without end) and the prospect of a sudden technological explosion make trust and accurate threat-assessment impossible.

The joke should be genuinely funny to someone who already understands the hypothesis, in the sense that its punchline turns on the hypothesis's actual logical structure rather than on generic cosmic dread or on the mere fact that space is vast and dark. To keep it from sliding into the standard telling, build it on two consequences of the hypothesis that are easy to overlook.

The first is that the hypothesis makes silence the dominant strategy for every civilization, which means that silence carries no information. A civilization that hears nothing cannot tell whether it is alone in the galaxy or surrounded by neighbors who are all perfectly quiet; the emptiest possible universe and the most crowded one are observationally identical.

The second is that if silence is the only safe thing to transmit, then the only truly safe broadcast is the news that one is already dead — a public record of one's own destruction, which no one has any incentive to investigate and every incentive to leave alone.

The joke should land on the collision of these two points: a forest whose silence might be emptiness, or might be a crowded room in which every civilization is holding its breath and quietly filing its own obituary. It may be as short as it likes and take whatever form suits it — an exchange, a log entry, a one-line punchline — but the humor should come from the logic rather than from a character remarking on how quiet everything is and shuddering.
