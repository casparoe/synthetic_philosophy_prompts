I'm on sabbatical and I've been camped out in a pretty unglamorous archive — the administrative records of a mid-sized public institution, the sort of thing nobody writes poems about — and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for.

The archive isn't thin, that's the thing. It's bloated. But the bulk of it is disputes. Grievances, complaints, audits, letters from lawyers, minutes of the meetings where somebody was furious, contracts renegotiated because one side threatened to walk. The ordinary stuff — two people agreeing on something and both being satisfied, a favor done and forgotten, a rule followed without anyone remarking on it — leaves almost nothing behind, because nobody opens a file to protect themselves from a thing that went fine. So the picture of the past I come away with is far more litigious and anxious and adversarial than the past could plausibly have been, and the distortion isn't random: it's manufactured by the very act of keeping records. People write things down defensively, and the anticipation of conflict is what generates the paper.

I know the neighbours of this. Survivorship bias, obviously. The whole literature on archival silence and who gets left out of the record. But those are about absence, or about who's missing. This feels like the inverse: not that the powerless vanish, but that what survives is skewed toward the contested, so the record tilts not toward the powerful as such but toward whatever was worth fighting over. And I honestly can't tell whether that's just documentary bias with a mechanism bolted on, or a seperate thing with its own shape.

So — does it have a name? If it doesn't, give it one, define it, and tell me where it stops. Does it hold for personal papers, for oral history, for a world where storage is free and nothing ever gets thrown away — does cheap storage kill the effect, invert it, or just relocate it? I don't actually care about the label. I want the carving: what this is, what it isn't, and where the edges are. And if I've just reinvented something you already know about, say which thing, and why my version is the worse one.
