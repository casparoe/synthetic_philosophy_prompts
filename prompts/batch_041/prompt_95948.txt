After thirty-five years running an experimental physics group, I have spent the better part of my retirement reading metaphysics, and I have arrived at a position I did not expect to hold and cannot yet defend to anyone's satisfaction. I have been asked to present it next month at the fortnightly metaphysics reading group of a mid-sized British university, and I would like you to prepare me for the questions.

The position, briefly, is this. Both parties to the old dispute between endurantism and perdurantism assume that persistence must be a matter either of numerical identity — one thing wholly present at every moment of its career — or of parthood, with a thing extended through time as a whole is extended through space, possessing distinct parts at distinct times. I want to argue that physics supplies a third primitive for which neither side has any room: what is carried along a trajectory. What physics actually tracks through time is not a substance and not a series of parts but a set of quantities conserved along a continuous path through a four-dimensional manifold — energy and momentum, charge, and their kin. I call the maximal such path a carrier, and I want to say that for a thing to be the same thing over time just is for its states to belong to one carrier. On this account the pull of endurantism (the carrier is one) and the pull of perdurantism (the carrier is spread out) are both explained and neither is vindicated. I also take it to explain why a flame, a vortex, a species and a stone feel object-like in different degrees, and to let identity over time be exact precisely where the relevant conservation is exact.

The room will not be kind. The regulars are mostly endurantists in a broadly neo-Aristotelian idiom — substances, powers, hylomorphism, and a standing suspicion that I have confused the order of explanation; two of them are card-carrying four-dimensionalists who know the literature on temporal parts far better than I do; and one is a philosopher of physics who regards the importation of physics into metaphysics as a category error and will say so early. They will not all object in the same vocabulary, and that is rather the point.

What I need is a prep sheet, not a defence, and I do not want a survey of the literature or any citations. Give me the seven or eight objections I will actually get, ordered by how much damage each would do if I fumbled it, and set them out as a table with these columns: Objection — phrased as the person would actually put it, in their own idiom; Who raises it — which wing of the room, and in what vocabulary; Likelihood — high, medium or low; Damage — a rating from 1 to 5; My two-minute reply; Where the reply is soft — the concession I should make or the flank I would be leaving open. Then, beneath the table, a single JSON object with the fields most_dangerous_objection, question_i_hope_nobody_asks, concession_to_make_up_front, weakest_point_in_my_position, and one_line_answer_to_the_unwanted_question. Please write in British English throughout.
