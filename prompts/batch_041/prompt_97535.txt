I've been going down a rabbit hole on the old question of whether religion actually makes people behave better, and whether a society can keep trusting itself once the churches empty out. I'm not an academic, just someone who got obsessed after a long argument with a friend, and I've got maybe a year of evenings and weekends to actually read and think about this properly. So I want to spend that time on the questions that actually matter, not just whatever's most fun to argue about. Can you lay out the main open questions in this area and rank them by which are most worth working on first, with your reasoning? I care alot more about the reasoning than the list itself: for each one, is it upstream of the others, is it tractable for a lone person with no lab, no budget and no stats training, and would answering it actually change anything downstream -- or is it the kind of question where you could settle it either way and nothing else moves?

The thing that got me started is that the two sides seem to be talking past each other because they mean totally different things by better -- one camp means less crime and more charity, the other means less hypocrisy and cruelty -- and I suspect the definitional question, what more moral is even supposed to mean here, is doing way more work than anyone admits, maybe enough that it should be settled first. Then there's the bit that really nags me: whenever people say religion makes us trust strangers more, I can never tell if the actual engine is belief in a god who's watching, or just dense community ties and the fear of being talked about. And that seems to bleed straight into the secular-trust worry, because if it's really just community and reputation, then secular societies might be fine as long as they can rebuild that some other way. Am I right to think those two questions (what counts as more moral, and belief versus belonging) are the load-bearing ones, or is there something I'm not seeing that everything else secretly hangs on? And tell me what would change your ranking -- like what kind of finding or argument would push a question up or down.
