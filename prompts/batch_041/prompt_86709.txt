My kid starts kindergarten in September, so of course I've spent the last three weeks reading about school choice instead of sleeping. I've got a note on my phone with all the arguments in it and I cant get them into any order. Could you sort them into tiers for me, by how much weight each one should actually carry if you were seriously trying to work out whether a school-choice system is a good idea? Not by how often you hear them said, I mean how much they should count.

Heres the list, in my own words. Parents know their own kid and their own values better than a district office does, so they'll make better calls than an assignment algorithm will. Schooling is part of how a family raises a child and the state has no business dictating what a kid gets taught about religion or sex. If schools have to compete for students, bad ones either improve or close. It isn't a new privilege, rich families already buy into good districts by buying houses, so giving everyone the option is levelling up rather than favouritism. But if what actually matters is being ahead of the next kid for university places, choice just re-sorts the queue and burns extra resources without changing who ends up where. A shared school is where kids from different backgrounds have to deal with each other, and sorting quietly erodes that. My kid's learning depends on who else is in the room, so when the motivated families cluster, the kids who move gain and the schools they left behind drain. And the families who'd have fought to fix their assigned school are exactly the ones who leave it. And a kid has an interest in not being locked into their parents' worldview before they're old enough to weigh it up. And the boring one, which is that as far as I can tell the voucher and charter studies mostly find small and mixed effects, so we might be arguing about a small effect size.

My partner says I'm overthinking it and my sister, who homeschools her three, says I'm not overthinking it nearly enough. So here's what I want out of this. Tell me which of those are really the same argument wearing different hats, which ones pull against each other even though the same people tend to use them together, and which are about what choice does to the system as a whole rather than about what it does for my own kid, since I suspect I've been muddling those. Then say which items gain or lose weight if the question is homeschooling specifically rather than vouchers or a public-school lottery, because everyone talks about those as if they were one thing and I don't think they are. And if I've left off something that belongs in the top tier, tell me that too.

No citations please, I'm not writing a paper. Also no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — I realise asking for a tier list and banning tables is a bit of a contradiction, so just say in an ordinary sentence which tier each one lands in and write the whole thing as prose, the way you'd explain it to me at the kitchen table. If a couple of the items honestly can't be put on the same scale, say that instead of forcing them.
