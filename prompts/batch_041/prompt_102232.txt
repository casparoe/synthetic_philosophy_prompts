My half-formed idea: 'competence without comprehension' gets applied to Darwin and to Turing as though its one thing, and I think it's really two. Take a chess engine that beats every human. We say it plays without comprehending. But somebody comprehended something somewhere — whoever wrote the evaluation function, or whoever's games it learned from. So comprehension didn't disappear, it got relocated; call that displaced comprehension. Natural selection is the genuinely strange case, because there's no upstream comprehender to point to at all. What I want to pin down: is there a principled way to separate competence-without-comprehension from displaced comprehension, or does the line just track whether we can find a human upstream to credit?
