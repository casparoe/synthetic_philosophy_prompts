I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized health-tracking app (about 4 million UK and EU users) and I sit on our internal data governance board — not because I know anything about ethics, I'm just the engineer who has to implement whatever they decide. There's a decision on Thursday and I've been asked to write the opinion that goes in the minutes, so I'm drafting it myself.

The case: we want to license a slice of users' logged symptom and workout data to a university research group. It's pseudonymised, no names, and the research is genuinely worthwhile — it's on a rare heart condition. The problem is that the users with that condition are a tiny group, maybe 900 people, and for them the data is nearly as identifying as a name would be, since the condition is rare and the researchers also get postcode and age band. For everyone else the risk is basically nil. Our head of data has scored it with the "disclosure budget" we adopted last year: you estimate the re-identification risk for each affected user, add them all up, and if the total is under the threshold you ship. The total comes out comfortably under, so her view is that we're fine, and anyway it's all averages and statistics, nobody's actually harmed.

Something about the budget feels sneaky to me — it lets 900 people carry almost all of the cost as long as the crowd is big enough to make the average look tiny. But I can't say why that's different from any other risk we spread across a user base, and I'd guess half the board will just say they can opt out and the other 4 million have nothing to hide. My vague feeling is that the budget treats privacy like a quantity of stuff we all own jointly, when what people actually seem to care about is being able to live without being watched by whoever happens to hold the data.

Can you write the opinion itself, in British English (our minutes use British spelling)? I need a concrete recommendation — ship it, ship it with conditions, or don't, with a timeframe — plus what we should do about the budget itself, and an honest account of the strongest point on the losing side, because two board members will definately raise it and I don't want to look like I've ignored it.
