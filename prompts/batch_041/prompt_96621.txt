Here is the article's characterization, as best I can reproduce it:

"Everybody has beliefs and desires; the puzzle is what they are. Dennett's answer is to stop looking inside the head and ask instead what we are doing when we predict one another. He distinguishes three stances we can take toward any system. From the physical stance we use physics and chemistry to work out what it will do. From the design stance we assume the thing was designed for a purpose and predict that it will do what it is supposed to do — we don't need to know how a thermostat's wiring works to say it will switch on when the room gets cold. From the intentional stance we treat the system as a rational agent, ask what it ought to believe and desire given its situation, and predict that it will act accordingly. Each stance is a level of description of the same system, and the higher ones rest on the lower ones: you can only take the design stance toward something whose physical behavior you could in principle predict, and you can only take the intentional stance toward something you have already identified as having a design.

Dennett's most interesting claim is that the entities these stances posit are real. A pattern is real, he says, when describing it lets you compress and predict the data better than you could otherwise; that is what makes the glider in Conway's Game of Life a genuine inhabitant of the Life world rather than a pattern we merely project, and it is what makes beliefs genuine features of people. Beliefs are therefore as real as tables and electrons. But the same system can always be described from all three stances, and no stance is privileged over the others, so there is no deeper fact about which description is correct — only facts about which description is convenient for our purposes. When Dennett says beliefs are real patterns, he means they are real for us: indispensable instruments for getting along with one another, not items in the furniture of the universe. This is why he thinks it is a pseudo-question whether a thermostat, a chess program, or a corporation 'really' has beliefs; any answer is just a choice of stance. And this is how he dissolves the mind-body problem: mental talk is simply a higher-level description of the same physical facts, and once we see that, there is nothing left over to explain."
