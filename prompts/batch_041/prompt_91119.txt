I've been trying to apply the frame / outer / inner distinction to cases where the three levels pull apart, and my verdicts don't cohere. As I read the scheme in Gödel, Escher, Bach, the outer message is what a thing says outright, the inner message is what it reveals implicitly about its sender or its making, and the frame message is what an interpreter must already have in place for the thing to be readable as a message at all.

My verdicts. A stop sign at a school crossing: the outer message is the command to stop, but the message I care about, that I am inside a system of traffic law and expected to comply, is frame and not inner. A wedding ring: that I am married is outer; that we are the kind of people for whom this symbol is a vow is frame; that we chose this one together is inner. A restaurant sign requiring employees to wash their hands: the real message is that it is posted because the inspector requires it, and I want to call that inner, even though every such sign carries it. A published paper: that it is addressed to a community that will try to replicate it is frame; that the authors ran the experiment thirty times and are reporting the run that worked is inner. A friend's silence when I tell them my news: that silence is a move in this conversation is frame; that they disapprove is inner.

My tentative principle: a message is frame when every token of the type carries it, and inner when it attaches to this token's history, maker, or occasion. That handles the ring, the paper, and the silence, but it forces the restaurant sign into the frame, and my gut says no. What I want is a clause separating type-general messages about the interpreter's situation from type-general messages about the sender's attitude, but the stop sign's frame message is equally a fact about its sender, that someone with authority over me put it there, so I cannot see what does the separating.

Give me the principle, or the smallest set of candidates, with the case that motivates each clause, what it predicts for a park bench whose armrests are spaced to prevent lying down and for someone who answers a question with a question, and an honest statement of which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. Say how confident you are in it and which clause you are least sure of. No more than 500 words.
