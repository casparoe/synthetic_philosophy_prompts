Construct a thought experiment for a graduate seminar on reasons without reasoners. The target is a distinction I want the case to make vivid: a rationale is weakly free-floating when a system’s behavior is rationally interpretable but the rationale is not represented anywhere in the system; it is strongly free-floating when the rationale is not represented anywhere and is not underwritten by any selection history either. I want a double dissociation between these two. In the first arm, a system has a robust selection history for a behavior, but the behavior as currently produced is not rationally interpretable under any description that does not already import that history; the rationale has come loose from the mechanism. In the second arm, a system with no selection history for a behavior produces a pattern that is rationally interpretable only by attributing a rationale that no component represents and that no function underwrites. Constraints: both arms must be ordinary, non-fictional systems — a plant, an insect, a thermostat, a market, a slime mold, a bureaucracy, or the like; no swampman, no demons, no perfect predictors, no stipulated conscious states; each arm no longer than a paragraph; the two arms must be matched on every incidental feature, and you must name the single feature whose variation produces the double dissociation. After the two arms, do the following. For each arm, state what the etiological (selected-function) account, the instrumentalist or interpretationist account, and a mechanistic account say about whether a free-floating rationale is present, and why. Say which account is committed to the strong form and whether that commitment is a virtue or a vice. Identify the single feature doing the work in your case, say what happens to the dissociation if that feature is removed, and give the strongest reply available to a defender of the account that fares worst. Do not stipulate the verdicts; they must fall out of the accounts.
