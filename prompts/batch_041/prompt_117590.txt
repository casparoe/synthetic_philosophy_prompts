I am the wellbeing-appraisal analyst in the strategy unit of a large development fund. The board votes four times a year on rounds worth hundreds of millions, and my memo supplies the ranking: three working days per proposal, one staff economist, no outside consultants, and a chair who signs off personally on anything I flag as a values question rather than a measurement question. The board wants a single ordered list with a score beside each proposal.

The trouble is that more of what we are asked to fund changes how many people there will be, not only how well off the people already here are: disease eradication, settlement and irrigation schemes, agricultural intensification that supports more people at a lower living standard, and schooling-and-family-planning packages that raise living standards while shrinking the next generation. Our scoring is total wellbeing, discounted; a trustee who knows the literature has started calling our rankings obscene, and I think she is right, but I cannot drop the metric, because the mandate is to fund what does the most good and the board will not accept a metric that can be gamed by ignoring people who do not yet exist.

Write me a decision procedure I can run, in order, for recommending on a proposal that changes the size or composition of the population. Say what evidence each step needs, what counts as sufficient, where the procedure ends in a recommendation, and where it must hand the question to the chair because the question is one of values rather than measurement. Tell me which distinctions have to survive the simplification (mere additions versus trade-offs in which someone is made worse off; lives above versus below the line at which life stops being worth living; a ranking of outcomes versus a rule about what may be chosen) and which I can drop so that someone who is not a philosopher can run this in three days. Be explicit about where it will misfire, and say what to do when the comparisons it produces come out cyclic.

Then run it on three proposals: (1) a mortality programme that adds a large number of future people whose lives are comfortably worth living, lowering the average and making no one worse off; (2) a cheap subsistence and sanitation package supporting a very large population whose lives are only just worth living, against an alternative that leaves the region thinly populated and spends the same money raising far fewer people to a high standard; (3) a schooling and family-planning package that raises living standards for everyone it reaches while reducing the number of future people. If the procedure tells me to recommend (2), say so plainly and tell me how to write the memo.
