I never studied philosophy, so sorry if this is a dumb question, but this has been bugging me for weeks and my brother-in-law (he's a nurse) has stopped even wanting to talk about it. He told me about a patient at his hospital who died because nobody looked in on him for about six hours. Nobody did anything wrong in the sense of doing anything — no wrong drug, no botched procedure, nothing. He just wasn't checked on. And everyone there talks about it like the staff *caused* his death. But how can you cause something by not doing anything? When I think about cause and effect I think about a thing making another thing happen — a push, a spark, a pill going down a throat. If nothing happens, there isn't a thing there to do any causing, so "caused by not checking on him" sounds to me like "we think you should have checked on him" wearing a lab coat, i.e. a moral judgement dressed up as a fact about the world.

He says I'm being silly and that of course you can cause stuff by not doing it. His example was that if you promise to water someone's plants and then don't, you caused them to die. That doesn't convince me either — the plants died because nobody watered them, which is a seperate fact, and the "you caused it" part is just tacked on afterwards because we're annoyed at you. My own version: I left a window open and my laptop got soaked in a storm, and everyone including me says I caused that, but again, the rain did the soaking. So my question is whether the whole idea of "causation by omission" is actually nonsense that survives only because we want someone to blame, and if so, whether that means a big chunk of ordinary blame and even law is built on nothing.

But if it isn't nonsense — if an absence really can be a cause — then I'd like to understand how that's supposed to work, because I genuinely can't picture it. Is my window case the same kind of thing as the nurse case, or is there something different going on between them that I'm not seeing? And I suppose the thing I really want to know is whether I'm wrong to think that if omissions can't cause anything, then nobody can be held responsible for what they don't do, or whether that's a leap I'm making that doesn't actually follow. Take it slow with me, I don't know the vocabulary for any of this.
