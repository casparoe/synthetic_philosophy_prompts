I've been in a reading group on religion and the state for about a year now and we keep having the same arguement about France, and it never moves. Roughly: one camp says a state can't really be neutral if it lets people wear religious signs in schools and public jobs, because the point of the public sphere is that everyone meets there as a citizen and nothing else, so exemptions for the headscarf, the kippah, the turban are just the state quietly endorsing religion and handing the loudest community a veto. The other camp says the opposite: refusing the exemption is the state taking a side, because the neutrality that matters is the state not favouring one religion over another or over none, and making a Muslim girl take her scarf off to sit an exam isn't neutrality, its the majority's culture dressed up as universalism. Both sides quote the same slogan -- the state should be neutral -- and then accuse the other of betraying it. Nobody has shifted in a year. Someone in the group keeps saying the whole thing is really a difference between an "open" laïcité and a militant one, but I'm not sure that's doing any work or is just a nicer name for the same split.

My working theory is that this isn't really a fight about the facts of integration or even about how to read the French tradition. I think 'neutrality' is doing two jobs under one word -- neutral among religions versus neutral of the public sphere from religion altogether -- and that once you split it, the disagreement is really about something else again, namely whether the state can be a bearer of a culture at all, and whether the thing being protected is the individual or the group. But I might be wrong; maybe there's a hidden empirical claim underneath about whether visible religious markers in schools actually slow integration, and the whole thing is a disagreement about evidence dressed up as principle.

Can you diagnose it properly? I want to know what kind of disagreement this actually is -- verbal, substantive, a clash of unarguable starting points, or two camps answering different questions under one heading -- and what would actually count as progress here. A distinction, a piece of evidence, a reformulation? Or is this one of those where no progress should be expected and the best you can do is see why each side is stuck? Use what each side actually says, not a survey of French history.
