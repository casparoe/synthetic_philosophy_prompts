Rudolf Carnap died in 1970 and so never encountered the current revival of the claim that cheap computation and ubiquitous data have dissolved the old objection to central planning — that no planning board can collect and process the dispersed information a price system aggregates. Suppose he had lived to read it. Carnap held that philosophy is conceptual engineering rather than discovery, that the choice between language frameworks is a practical decision about which one serves our purposes rather than a question with a truth value, and that questions posed inside a framework are settled by that framework's own standards; he was also a socialist and pacifist who joined the Independent Social Democratic Party in 1918. Predict what he would have made of the computational-planning argument, separating (a) the internal question whether a planner could in principle compute a satisfactory allocation, (b) the framework question whether planning or market is the better vocabulary for economic talk, and (c) the practical decision whether to plan. Say where he would have parted ways with Otto Neurath, who wanted a socialist economy to calculate in physical quantities rather than in money, and whether he would have judged any part of the modern dispute a pseudo-problem. Label your extrapolations as extrapolations.
