Ok so I've been chewing on the centipede game for like ten years now and I think I've finally landed on my position, wanted a sanity check before I inflict it on my reading group. Heres where I am:

(1) The backward induction argument is valid. Not "valid modulo some shaky step" — valid, every step. At the last node you take, so at the second-to-last node you take, and the induction runs all the way down and theres no node where anything sneaks in.

(2) The centipede is a reductio of the standard picture of practical rationality. Any theory that has two rational players shut the game down at node one for pocket change, when passing all the way down would've paid them a million each, is a theory of suckers and not a theory of rationality.

(3) Rationality is transparent. If I'm rational then a rational other player can predict what I'll do, and I can predict them. "I'll just be unpredictable" isn't a move a rational agent has available to them.

(4) What's rational is the policy, not the act. The person who cooperates down the centipede makes more money over a life than the person who grabs at node one, so being a cooperator is the rational thing to be.

(5) I'm a causal decision theorist. An act is rational in virtue of what it causally brings about, here and now — the fact that being a cooperator has good downstream effects doesn't make any particular act of cooperating rational.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and whats the cheapest one to give up? Rank the conflicts by how bad they are rather than telling me it's all just tangled.
