So two people in my reading group, Maya and Jonah, got into it after our session on social construction last week, and it spilled over into the bar afterwards and never got settled. I was just sitting there listening, not on either side. Quick context: a mutual aid group in our town started printing little slips of paper — an hour of helping somebody (walking a dog, tutoring a kid, hauling furniture) gets you one slip, and the slips are taken at the farmers market and a couple of shops on Main. Everyone calls them "hours." Anyway, the exchange went roughly like this:

1. Maya: the hours are money, full stop. Money is just whatever a community accepts as a medium of exchange — there's no extra ingredient, no permission slip. Which means "it's socially constructed" can never by itself be a criticism of anything: money's constructed and nobody's upset about it.

2. Jonah: no, the difference is the state. Legal tender, taxes payable, debts you can be sued over — the dollar has an entire apparatus behind it. The slips are scrip, or IOUs at best. And that's exactly why he thinks the social-construction talk about race is empty: if "constructed" is compatible with something as ordinary as money, then saying race is constructed has said nothing critical about race.

3. Maya: you're running two different things together. There's "social attitudes cause the people in the category to be the way they are" — that's race: the sorting and the stereotypes actually change people's bodies and lives — and there's "the attitude is part of what it is to be in the category," which is money: being money is just being taken as money. Money is the second kind. And for that kind the criticism moves: you don't say the category is empty, you say its edges are answerable to power and to nothing else, and that we could run the practice differently.

4. Maya also floated a test: a category is really constructed only if you can describe a world with all the same underlying facts but nobody applying the category. If you can't, it's tracking something.

5. Jonah: that test is way too loose — it would make "widow" and "citizen" and "best friend" constructed in whatever sense you're worried about, and it wouldn't separate a category like "registered voter," which is answerable to a fact, from one that isn't. And back to his main point: you still need a separate premise — that the category does harm, or serves no legitimate point — before any criticism falls out of "constructed." Construction on its own is silent.

6. Maya: right, so what. You don't need a God's eye view to criticize a rule of a game you're playing; you just need to be able to picture a different rule. And saying race is constituted by social practices doesn't mean there's no fact of the matter about who's in the category — there is, it's just a social fact, same as with money.

7. Jonah: then you've given up your own sorting. Race is both — constituted by the classifying practice *and* causally reshaping the people classified. So causal vs. constitutive isn't two piles of cases, it's two things you can say about one case. And if you admit that, then "race is constructed" can't be what does the debunking; the false biology is what gets debunked, and that's a separate claim.
