so my friend Dev and I got into a two-hour arguement at the bar last night about Death in Damascus and now I want to turn it into something. In case the name means nothing to you: someone in Damascus runs into Death (or an assassin, take your pick) at the market, works out that they're fated to die there, and flees to another city — except the killer had already predicted every move they'd make, so the escape was pointless and they die in the new place anyway. The nasty bit is that fleeing looks self-defeating in a way ordinary decision theory can barely even describe.

Write me the excerpt from the self-help book that would get sold in a world where this is a real, repeatable situation — a "7 Habits" thing for people who are about to be predicted to death. Invent the title, keep it deadpan, no winking, roughly 600 words, formatted like a chapter with two or three numbered "principles" plus a short letter from a reader who's asking for help. Voice: an extremely confident life-coach who thinks decision theory is basically a productivity hack.

The humor has to come from the decision theory actually being right, so here's the shape I want. The coach tells you to ignore the fact that you've been predicted — the prediction already happened, it can't be changed by what you do now, so just maximize the outcome given you're still breathing and flee. That's the causal line and it gets the reader killed. But the same coach, in the same book, insists you absolutely must pay up in the counterfactual mugging and one-box in Newcomb's problem, because "you want to be the kind of agent who..." — i.e. totally happy to reason from correlated prediction when it's cheap and from causation when it's expensive, and never once notices. The reader's letters should get steadily more doomed as they follow the advice, and each should be one or two sentences, because the coach should barely engage with them.

By the end I want a reader who knows the terrain to see that the coach is just picking whichever theory saves face in each case — without the coach ever saying it, and without you ever naming causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, or updatelessness. Don't explain those either, just let the inconsistency do the work. And no citations or reading list obviously, that's not the point.
