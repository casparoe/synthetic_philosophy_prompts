Since my daughter was born, I have found myself thinking about possibility and identity in a way I never did before. I have a set of intuitions about what I could and could not have been, and I would like to know what principle, if any, captures them. Here they are. I could have been a lawyer instead of a teacher: true. I could have been born in a different country: true. I could have been born to different parents: false. I could have been a woman: true. I could have been Napoleon: false. I could have been a rock: false. I could have been a person who is a perfect duplicate of me in every respect, living in a duplicate universe: false. I could have been a person who is a perfect duplicate of me in every respect except that they are made of silicon rather than carbon: false. I could have been a person who has my memories and personality but no body at all: false. I could have been a person who has my body and personality but no memories of my actual life: true. I could have been a swarm of bees that collectively implements my psychology: false. I could have been a person who is a time traveler and is my own parent: false. My tentative principle is that I could have been anything that shares my origin and my body, but not anything that shares only my memories or personality. Does that principle capture all my verdicts, or do I have to give some up? Does it commit me to counterpart theory or transworld identity, or is it neutral between them? And what does it say about a case I have not considered: could I have been a person who is a perfect duplicate of me in every respect except that they are the result of my body splitting into two identical persons?
