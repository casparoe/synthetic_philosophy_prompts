Ok so I've somehow become the permanent referee in a three-year argument between two of my best friends, and I want them to stop yelling and write down the thing they should've written a year ago — a joint statement, an adversarial-collaboration document, whatever you call it. Here's the setup so you can see the two camps. Friend A is a local councillor and a reciprocity person: he thinks the welfare state is only justifiable to the people paying in if it's a scheme of give-and-take, that adults who can work and won't are taking from adults who do, and that conditionality — work requirements, job-search conditions, sanctions for turning down a reasonable offer — isn't cruelty, it's the thing that makes the whole arrangement fair to the taxpayer. Friend B runs a food bank and is an unconditionalist: she thinks the deserving/undeserving distinction is rotten, that conditionality manufactures the very fraud and dependency it claims to police, that "reciprocity" is doing no real work once you notice who's already given what (unpaid care, low-paid work, years of contributions before things fell apart), and that the decent baseline is money without a means test on your character.

What I want is the document the two of them could both actually sign. So: a numbered section of claims they agree on — about what the welfare state is for, about which facts are facts, about what's genuinely contested — and then the real disagreements, each one stated in wording that the side who holds it would accept as fair (so A's view phrased the way A would phrase it, B's the way B would, not a strawman of either). Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move somebody. I already suspect two of them. One is whether reciprocity requires an actual contribution or only willingness and capacity — because A quietly needs the first and B only ever argues against the first, so they might be closer than they sound. The other is whether conditionality is supposed to work through incentives (people respond to the taper, so it's an empirical claim about whether work pays after childcare and travel) or through desert and expression (it's about what people owe, in which case I want to know what either of them would count as evidence against their own side). For each crux, give me a concrete case or piece of evidence that both would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. Three cases I'd like them to commit on: a single parent offered a night shift that nets her less than her benefit once childcare's paid; someone who misses two appointments because of untreated depression and gets sanctioned; and a proposal to drop sanctions entirely and instead top up anyone who takes a low-paid job.

A few things I'd like the document to nail down that I don't think either of them has ever stated cleanly: entry-conditionality (conditions to get in) versus exit-sanctions (punishments once you're in) — those are seperate and they keep sliding between them; conditionality attached to cash versus to housing or in-kind help; and the difference between calling a person undeserving and calling a claim undeserving, which I think is where half the heat is. Also, if any part of what they've been fighting about turns out to be purely verbal — same substance, different words — say so and say which part, because I'd love to be able to tell them that to their faces. Don't bother with who's right in the end, that's not the assignment, and please don't pad it with reading lists or citations, they both know the literature better than I do. I want the working document: agreed claims numbered, then a disagreements table with the crux column and the predictions column filled in.
