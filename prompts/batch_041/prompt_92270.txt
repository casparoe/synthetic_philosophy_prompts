So I'm a backend engineer by trade, no philosophy training at all, but I got sucked into the interpretability stuff at work after we shipped a model and then couldn't explain why a patch that killed a bad behavior in the dev model did nothing in prod. I've been reading a bunch of papers where someone points at a handful of neurons or a little subgraph and says "this is the mechanism for X" — and I keep noticing that when you retrain the same architecture on the same data with a different seed, the picture comes out totally different, and everyone shrugs and says it's the same mechanism, just permuted around. But when the behavior itself changes, they say the mechanism changed. Both of those can't be a free pass. So before I read any more of these papers, I want the spec first: what does a theory of "same mechanism" for a neural net actually have to deliver?

Give me the desiderata list. I want the cases it has to get right: same run re-analyzed, different seed, model scaled up, a smaller distilled model that reproduces the behavior, two independently trained models that fail in the same weird way, a model where you change one thing and the behavior survives but the internals shift, and a model where the internals look identical but behavior diverges under distribution shift. I want the distinctions it can't collapse: same mechanism vs same function, mechanism vs implementation detail, a real mechanism-level change vs noise, and "the mechanism changed" vs "our probe stopped seeing it." And I want whatever constraints fall out of how we actually use these claims, because when someone says two models share a mechanism they're implicitly betting that a patch will transfer and that the same intervention will break the behavior the same way — so the account has to say what exactly is being bet on. For each item, give me the case or the practice that motivates it, and flag which ones are non-negotiable versus which are just house preferences of one camp (the individual-neuron people vs the sparse-dictionary people vs whoever). Then the part I actually want: which subset of these can't all be satisfied at once. I have a strong suspicion that "invariant across seeds," "sensitive enough to flag a real change," and "computable on a model I can actually afford to run" are not jointly achievable, and if so I want that said out loud instead of buried. Don't bother scoring current theories against the list, I'll do that part myself once I know what the list is — I just want to stop definately confusing "same mechanism" with "same diagram."
