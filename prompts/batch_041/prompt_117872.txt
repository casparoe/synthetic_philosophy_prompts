I am building a diagnostic handout for a graduate seminar on the epistemology of testimony, and what I want is not a survey but an instrument: a decision tree that carries a student from a starting commitment she may already hold to a determinate position on what justifies a hearer's belief in what she is told. Root the tree in a single case: a hearer who has no evidence at all bearing on a speaker's reliability — no track record, no information about her competence or honesty, no background view about the trustworthiness of speakers in her community — is told that p and comes to believe it. Is she justified? Does she know? Every branch node below the root should be a question about a concrete case that a student can answer without being able to tell which position her answer leads to; no node may be phrased as a version of "so are you a reductionist about testimony or not?"

Fix the following stipulations so the instrument has a working vocabulary. A zero-evidence hearer is as described above. A thin-evidence hearer does have grounds for assessing the speaker, but only grounds she inherited from earlier testimony. A view is transmissive if the hearer's justification is the speaker's own, and generative if the hearer's belief is justified whether or not the speaker had anything to transmit. A view is occasion-bound if the hearer must have her grounds available at the moment of acceptance, and background if a general standing basis will do.

The tree must satisfy the following. It should have between nine and twelve leaves and eight or fewer branch nodes, with a node permitted to have more than two children. At least these positions must appear as leaves, described in plain terms rather than by the names of their defenders: (a) the hearer must be able to reconstruct the speaker's reliability from evidence available to her, with the requirement imposed anew on every occasion; (b) the same, except that a general background of experience discharges it; (c) the hearer needs no evidence at all, because she has a standing entitlement to accept what she is told; (d) the hearer's justification consists in the speaker's having assumed responsibility for the truth of what she says, so that the hearer's relation to her is interpersonal rather than evidential; (e) the hearer's belief is justified because accepting testimony is a reliable or virtuous practice, whatever the hearer could articulate in its defence; (f) testimony is not a distinct epistemic category at all and is assessed like any other evidence. Include at least one further leaf that the space admits and that I have not listed, and at least one branch testing whether the hearer's assessment of a speaker's credibility must track features relevant to reliability — a hearer whose word is systematically discounted on grounds irrelevant to it should come out either as suffering an epistemic failure in her audience or as the object of an external social wrong, depending on where she falls.

For each leaf, give one sentence naming the commitment that will embarrass its holder most. Flag every combination of answers your tree leaves unoccupied, and say briefly whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely undefended. Flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate, and name the further question that would separate them. Present the logical space first — the questions, and which combinations of answers they permit — and only then locate the positions within it. Use no proper names, no citations, and no reading list; the instrument should stand on its own.
