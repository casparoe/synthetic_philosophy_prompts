Somehow that detonated into a two-hour fight about whether reasons can exist without anyone having them. Rosa brought up this idea of free-floating rationales -- I'm pretty sure it's Dennett's -- where a system can embody a rationale, some kind of goal-directed structure, even though nothing in the system represents the goal or is aware of it. Her line: the notion is real and doing indispensable work in biology and in AI. Dev's line: it's either just biology's talk of function dressed up in nicer clothes, or it's flatly false, because if nobody holds the reason then there's no reason, only causes.

They went around at least six times. (1) Rosa: we say the bee dances in order to tell the hive where the nectar is, and that in-order-to explanation isn't decoration -- it predicts and it explains. Dev: that's a causal story about selection, and causes support predictions too, no reasons needed. (2) Rosa: fine, then account for error. Dev: that's just malfunction relative to the ancestral environment, and you can define malfunction by selected effect with no reasoner in sight, so you've renamed function and lost nothing. (3) Rosa: no, the rationale is richer than function -- it's the same structure that shows up in artifacts and in minds. A chess engine plays to win even though nothing in it wants to win. Dev: the programmer wanted it to win, so the reasoner is the programmer, and your free-floating rationale is just a designer's intention at a distance. Rosa: then you've relocated the reasoner but you still need the rationale as its own thing, because the engine can misevaluate a position, and that error is the engine's, not the programmer's. (4) Dev's big one: indeterminacy. Any behavior fits infinitely many rationales -- maximize nectar, maximize inclusive fitness, follow the sun compass, avoid ultraviolet. Which one is *the* free-floating rationale? If there's no fact of the matter, it isn't a posit, it's a projection. Rosa: the same indeterminacy hits beliefs and desires, and the answer there is real patterns -- some descriptions are predictively superior, so they earn their keep as real. (5) Dev: then the whole thing is instrumental at best, and Rosa is quietly a realist about it, which is the actual disagreement. (6) Rosa: maybe, but so is anyone who says a heart has a function. Dev: reasons are things you can be held to, revise, weigh; a free-floating rationale can't be any of that, so calling it a reason erases the distinction that matters. Rosa: the bee's dance can be wrong, the immune system can misfire, so there's a thin answerability after all. Dev: thin enough to be worthless.
