I chair the technical certification board of an independent consortium whose member labs submit claims about their models for public endorsement. Our charter permits certification only of a claim that rests either on a proof or on an argument whose failure would require a coincidence; in practice this second clause has been read as licensing a presumption of independence, i.e. the default that two quantities are uncorrelated unless something specific connects them, so that their expected product is the product of their expectations. The charter does not say how strong a connection must be before it defeats the presumption.

On Thursday the board votes on an application from a lab that wants us to certify that its model's accuracy on a sealed evaluation set — held by a third party, assembled after the training run, never touched by the training pipeline — is an unbiased estimate of its accuracy on the field distribution. Three facts are undisputed. First, the lab trained two hundred configurations and submitted the one that scored highest on the sealed set. Second, the sealed items were generated by the third party from a distribution the lab itself specified. Third, the third party's item-generation script drew on a model of the same family as the applicant's, in order to filter out ambiguous items. The lab argues that each of these is at most a thin connection, and that a default can be defeated only by a mechanism that plausibly matters.

Two members have circulated proposals I want the opinion to address. One would replace our practice with a bright-line rule: presume independence only where the applicant can point to a randomization device it did not control. The other, a dissenting memo, argues that until someone produces a formalization of this kind of reasoning that accepts the sound applications and rejects the spurious ones, the board should suspend certification of any claim resting on an independence presumption, on the ground that our present practice cannot tell the two apart and that a heuristic which can be turned to any conclusion is no evidence at all.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should give a clear recommendation on this application — certify, reject, or certify with conditions, including a time frame — state a standard our reviewers can actually apply to future cases, and give an honest account of the strongest point on the side you come out against.
