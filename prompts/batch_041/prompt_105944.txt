I serve on the grantmaking committee of a small foundation that has set aside roughly three researcher-years, plus a modest workshop budget, for conceptual work on the moral status of invertebrates, and I am drafting the call for proposals this week. The committee keeps invoking what it calls the scale argument: insects are estimated to number in the quintillions, the probability that any given insect is sentient is plausibly tiny but not zero, and so the expected moral weight of insects could dwarf that of vertebrates even if the welfare of a single insect counts for almost nothing. My dissatisfaction is that the argument gets discussed as one thesis to be affirmed or denied, when the interesting disagreements are all internal to it. Does "sentience" here mean phenomenal consciousness, or the capacity for valenced experience — and does the argument even need the stronger notion? Is nociception plus some form of centralized integration the right kind of evidence, or are we importing a vertebrate-shaped template and then mistaking its failure to fit for a finding about insects? Is the probability a sharp number at all, or an imprecise interval, and if the latter, what happens to a decision rule that multiplies an interval by an astronomical population — do we get a determinate verdict, or does the arithmetic of expected value quietly presuppose a precision the evidence cannot supply? Should the argument be run as a bet on expected value, or recast as a precautionary rule that does not require assigning any probability? And does the same structure recur, with the same force, for nematodes, soil arthropods, and other taxa whose numbers exceed the insects'?

Please help me prioritize. I want to know which open questions or lines of work most deserve the three researcher-years, in what order, and why — and I care far more about the reasoning than the list. For each candidate, tell me whether it is upstream of the others, whether it is tractable for philosophers and decision theorists rather than only for neurobiologists, how neglected it currently is, and how much a shift in its answer would actually change what a funder or an animal-advocacy group does on the ground. Tell me which candidates I have failed to name but should have, and which of the ones I have named are overrated — questions that are absorbing to argue about but whose resolution would leave the practical picture essentially untouched. Finally, say what evidence or argument over the next two years would move a given item up or down the ranking, and which single disagreement, if settled, would most reduce the uncertainty in all the others.

Close with the ordered shortlist as your last line, in the form \answer{...}, with each entry a short label for the question or line of work and the ordering given by position.
