I keep running into a comparison that I suspect is carrying more weight than it can bear, and I would like you to audit it. The comparison is between an infinite universe and Hilbert's hotel: if the hotel already has infinitely many rooms, all of them occupied, any number of new guests can still be found a bed, and so — the argument runs — a world containing infinitely many people is not made any better by containing more of them, because the newcomers can be paired off one for one with the people who were already there. My friend uses this to conclude that the sheer number of future people is nothing we have reason to care about, and that anyone who agonises over how many lives the far future will hold has confused counting with valuing.

How far does the comparison actually hold? Set out the correspondences, in a table if that helps — what plays the part of a guest, a bed, a life going well, the manager's aim — then say where the mapping breaks, separating the breaks that matter for the use my friend is making of it from those that do not, and tell me whether his conclusion survives once they are on the table. I am less interested in a tour of the literature than in knowing exactly which step the hotel picture smuggles in, and what comparison would do the job better. Please answer in British English.
