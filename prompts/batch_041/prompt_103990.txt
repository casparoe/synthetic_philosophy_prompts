People always say things like "depression is 40% genetic" or "height is 80% genetic," and I used to picture it like a recipe — 80% genes, 20% upbringing, like flour and water. I'm now pretty sure that's the wrong picture, so here's the right one as best I've got it.

Heritability is a fact about a group, not a person. When a trait is 80% heritable, it means that among the particular people who got measured, living in the particular range of environments they lived in, 80% of the differences between them tracked genetic differences. It tells you nothing about any one individual, and nothing about what would happen if you changed the environment. And I guess a heritable trait is basically one that runs in families and gets passed down from parents to kids.

The thing that finally clicked for me is that it's about the spread of differences, not the ingredients. Imagine one kind of seed planted across a bunch of different soils — you'd get different heights, and none of that difference is genetic, because the seeds are all clones. Now imagine those same cloned seeds in soil that's exactly the same — now there are no differences left to explain, so heritability is just undefined. And if you could somehow make everyone's environment identical, then whatever differences remained would be 100% heritable by definition, even though the environment would still be doing all the work of making the trait exist in the first place. So a high number doesn't mean a trait is fixed or that the environment can't move it. Height, I'm pretty sure, actually got more heritable over the last century as nutrition and disease got more equal between people, even while everyone got taller.

My cousin says she saw a study where the same disease comes out around 90% heritable in one country and like 20% in another, and honestly I can't get my head around how that's possible, it feels like it should be one number.

The part I'm least sure of is groups. I've read that the heritability of IQ is higher in richer families, which I figure means genes matter more for rich kids' intelligence. And it seems to me that if some group scores lower on a trait than another group, and the trait is heritable, then the gap between the groups is probably genetic too — my friends keep saying that's the classic mistake but they never explain it properly.
