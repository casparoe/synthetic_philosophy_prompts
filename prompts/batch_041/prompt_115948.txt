I chair my university's research ethics committee. A team has submitted a study in which participants discuss a moral dilemma with an LLM that is prompted to state a view, give reasons, and revise that view when pressed; it keeps no memory between sessions, and the apparent change of mind is generated within the conversation by its system prompt. The researchers want the information sheet and methods section to say the model holds and revises beliefs, arguing that this is the only vocabulary that makes the interaction scientifically tractable. One board member says this misleads participants into treating a statistical system as a moral agent; another replies that belief-talk is just Dennett's intentional stance, a predictive convenience with no metaphysical commitment, and that banning it would impose a contested theory. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- with a time frame, distinguish the intentional stance from literal belief attribution, say how much the study's validity actually turns on which reading is correct, and propose specific wording for the consent form and methods section that the researchers could live with. Treat the instrumentalist objection at its strongest rather than dismissing it. Close by saying how confident you are in each recommendation and naming the point you are least sure about. Write in British English.
