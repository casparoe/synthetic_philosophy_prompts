Right, so here's my situation. I run a small grant programme — enough money to keep maybe six people going for three years on purely conceptual work, no empirical ML, no labs, just thinking and writing. The area I've decided to push is indirect normativity: the whole family of ideas where instead of asking what's right, you ask what some improved version of you, or of us, would end up wanting — better informed, more coherent, thinking faster, less biased, whatever the idealisation happens to be.

Here's the thing that's been nagging at me. Everyone in this area seems to quietly assume that if you idealise the deliberator enough, the answer converges — you sand off the biases, you pour in the information, and the verdict settles down to one thing. But I'm not convinced the order you do that in doesn't matter. Fix the values first and then add the information, versus add the information and let the values drift, versus just add a huge amount of reflection time and let it run — I can easily imagine those three landing in different places. And if that's right, then 'what the ideal agent would want' isn't well-defined until you say which idealisation path you took, and choosing the path is itself a substantive moral decision, which is exactly the thing the whole manoeuvre was meant to help you avoid. Call it path-dependence of idealisation. I don't know whether someone's already named it.

What I want from you: rank the open questions in this area by how much they should shape where my six people spend three years. Not a list — I want to know which questions are upstream of the others, which are genuinely tractable for philosophers sitting in a room rather than for people who need data, and which look juicy but wouldn't change much downstream even if you settled them. And I want you to tell me honestly whether the path-dependence worry is the crux I think it is or a distraction I've talked myself into.

Please take a definite position on the ranking. Actually commit to an order and defend it — don't hand me a menu of options with pros and cons and leave me to sort it out myself. If you think I'm wrong about where the crux lies, say so, and say what the real crux is instead. I'd also rather you seperate out the questions where you're confident from the ones where you're essentially guessing, since I'll be spending real money on this and I don't want false confidence dressed up as a plan.
