My sister's a pediatrician and also a mom, and last winter she landed in an awful spot: brutal respiratory season, one PICU bed left in the entire region, and two kids who needed it — her own daughter, and a stranger's kid she'd never met. She picked her daughter. I think anyone would, and I told her so. But what's eating her isn't the choice, it's the inside of her head. She says that in the moment she just went to her kid, no reasoning at all, and that's the only part she doesn't feel sick about. What wrecks her is everything after: she keeps catching herself assembling a defense — "parents are allowed to favor their own," "the two cases were basically comparable on the numbers anyway," "somebody has to make these calls" — and she says each version of the defense feels like another small betrayal, like if the love were the real thing she wouldn't need the argument. She's a doctor and her hospital expects her to triage impartially, so I figured some of this is just professional guilt, but she says no, it's not that she broke a rule, it's that she went looking for the rule in the first place, and that when she hears herself making the case she doesn't recieve the person talking.

I keep telling her the decision was obviously fine and to stop beating herself up, and she keeps saying I'm answering a different question than the one she's asking. I think she might be right and honestly I can't even tell what her question is. Is there something real in this, or is it just guilt looking for a philosophical costume? And if it is real — which part is the problem? Is it that she *considered* whether it was okay before acting, or that she's still justifying it afterwards, or that she wants a general principle that would cover the case at all? Those feel like three seperate complaints to me. My own guess is that maybe the rules are for judging from the outside and just aren't meant to be the thing running through your head while you're in the middle of it, but then I don't know what the rules are for at all, or what kind of answer could actually settle this for her rather than just make her feel better.
