I'm a reporter, science and ideas, mid-career, so I've written about a fair amount of philosophy without ever being trained in it. Right now I'm on a long piece that has me rereading Nietzsche's genealogy of morals, and the whole time I'm reading I keep trying to translate it into the only vocabulary I actually think in, which is newsroom vocabulary. So do that for me, properly.

Here's the vocab I mean. Sourcing and attribution (who told you, and what did they have to gain), cui bono, conflicts of interest, the story a source tells about itself vs. what the documents show, the fact-check, the difference between reporting a claim and adjudicating it, the wall between the news pages and the editorial pages, and the house rule that we lay out the evidence and let the reader decide. I know that toolkit cold; assume I dont need any of it explained back to me.

Restate the genealogy in those terms, one piece at a time: the method itself (digging up where a value came from and treating the origin as the story), and then the actual findings — the good/bad versus good/evil business, ressentiment, the bad conscience, the ascetic ideal. As faithfully as you can, and without just swapping newsroom words in as labels. If a sentence of Nietzsche's turns into a sentence a metro editor would actually let me print, say so. If you have to fudge it, fudge it and flag it.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. Some things I'm already suspicious of:

- My trade has a hard wall between is and ought, so I can report that a value came from somewhere ugly, but the second I say that this discredits the value I'm editorializing. Nietzsche clearly wants the sting, not just the backstory. Is there any version of the punch that survives in newsroom terms, or does the fact/value wall mean the whole argumentative point falls outside what my vocabulary can carry? Related: we officially treat origin stories as non-refuting (a claim isn't false because a creep thought of it first), so is genealogy even an argument by our lights, or is it something else wearing the costume of reporting?

- Cui bono is our version of a functional explanation and it's great for the priest, the church, the state. But the ascetic ideal — the thing that makes life bearable is also the thing that turns on life — doesn't obviously have a beneficiary in the way a conflict-of-interest story needs one. Does my framework have any way to say that, or do I end up reducing it to priests wanting power and lose the actual claim?

- The reporter's voice is supposed to come from nowhere: nobody's interest, no standpoint. Nietzsche's critique seems to need a standpoint (life, flourishing, whatever you want to call it) that he never quite nails down. Is my nobody's-standpoint a real counterpart to his problem, or is it a different problem that only looks similar?

- And the historical status of the whole thing. I genuinely can't tell whether I'm reading history, a just-so story, or something in between, and my training says if you cant source it you cant print it. Does that reveal a gap in my vocabulary, or does it reveal that the genealogy is doing something that was never really a factual claim at all?

Last thing: tell me whether the residue is my problem or Nietzsche's. I'd rather find out my trade can't say something important than walk away thinking I've understood a book I've only translated into headlines.
