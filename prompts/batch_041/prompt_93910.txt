I'm a backend engineer, no real philosophy background beyond a couple of podcasts and a lot of arguing in code review, and I run a monthly lunch-and-learn discussion group at work. About 13 people: mostly engineers, two PMs, one designer who actually reads this stuff. 75 minutes, one conference room, whiteboard, and everyone has a phone they will definately be looking at. Half the room is sold on the idea of picking decision-makers by lottery — I keep seeing "sortition" and "citizens' assembly" tossed around in AI governance stuff and it sounds very clean — and the other half thinks it's a joke. I want an activity where both halves have to actually feel the thing instead of just arguing about it.

I don't want the standard "you're a jury now, go deliberate, isn't democracy heartwarming" exercise. Four things I want them to trip over, and I want the rules to make them trip, not me explaining it afterward:

- the lottery only decides who's in the room; somebody else still decides what question gets asked and exactly how it's worded, and that person wasn't drawn;
- everyone has an equal shot going in, and after the draw almost nobody has any say, and the ones who weren't drawn just get to watch it happen;
- if you'd rolled the dice again you'd likely have gotten a different answer to the same question, so what the group "decided" is a sample, not the population;
- when it goes badly, there's nobody to vote out.

Design the activity. I need the setup and materials (assume a hat, index cards, a timer, and some way to draw names at random), roles for the people who get drawn versus the ones who don't, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75, and small stakes that are actually real — the decision should bind us for the next session, something like what we do next month or how we spend the group's tiny budget, not a pretend vote. The room is genuinely close to 50/50 on most things we argue about, so a redraw really could flip the outcome; I want that to be visible and felt rather than hypothetical. Also tell me what I should pre-decide and keep quiet about as the facilitator, and when to reveal it. Then the debrief: which moment in the activity maps onto which of those four things, how I connect them out loud, and what predictably goes sideways the first time I run this and how to patch it mid-run.

Please just commit to one design. Make the calls, tell me why you made them, and don't hand me three variants to choose between — I'll never decide. If you had to bet on the single moment that will actually land on this crowd, tell me which one and why. And be straight with me about the part of the idea the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to just say out loud, plus anywhere the design is quietly cheating. No reading list or citations please, I'm not going to read them.
