Write a short poem, no more than twenty lines, designed as a round—a song in which voices enter in succession and overlap—for three voices on the intransitivity of better than. Spectrum arguments present a sequence of outcomes in which each adjacent pair seems to be related as better to worse—A better than B, B better than C, and so on—yet the first does not seem better than the last; transitivity would forbid that gap. Invent a concrete spectrum (for instance, lives that trade a small gain in one good against a small loss in another, each adjacent step intuitively an improvement) and make the reader feel the force of every local comparison. The round should carry the argument: let the three voices enter in succession, each stating one link in the chain, so that when they overlap the poem generates a loop rather than a hierarchy. Leave the contradiction unresolved in the final overlap. Do not include citations or footnotes. After the poem, add a brief prose note saying how confident you are that the poem captures the philosophical issue and identifying the one point you are least sure of—whether it is the intransitivity itself, the choice of spectrum, or the claim that the round form tracks the argument.
