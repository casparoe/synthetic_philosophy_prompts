I'm working on an essay about the Judy Benjamin problem and want your comments on the central argument. The problem, as I understand it: Judy is dropped into a swamp and knows the area is divided into four quadrants—Red Army and Camp, Red Army and Headquarters, Blue Army and Camp, Blue Army and Headquarters—and she assigns equal prior credence to each. She then receives the conditional information that if she is in the Red Army, she is in the Headquarters. The standard I-divergence minimization rule, often taken as the natural extension of Jeffrey conditionalization to conditional information, yields posterior credences of one-third for each of the three remaining possibilities. But this seems wrong: it lowers her credence that she is in the Red Army from one-half to one-third, even though the information tells her nothing about whether she is in the Red or Blue Army. My proposed fix is to add a constraint to the I-divergence minimization: the marginal credence of the antecedent—that she is in the Red Army—should remain unchanged at one-half. With that constraint, the posterior credences become one-half for Red Army and Headquarters, one-quarter for Blue Army and Camp, and one-quarter for Blue Army and Headquarters, which matches the intuitive result. My questions: Is this constraint principled, or is it an ad hoc patch? Does it generalize to other cases of updating on conditionals, including ones where the antecedent is not independent of the consequent in the prior? Are there counterexamples where preserving the antecedent's marginal credence is clearly the wrong thing to do? And is there a deeper rationale—perhaps about the difference between learning a conditional and learning a material implication—that would justify the constraint or replace it with something better?
