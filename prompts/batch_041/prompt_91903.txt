I host a political theory podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher whose forthcoming book defends what she calls a two-stage solution to the boundary problem — the question of how a demos is constituted before it can legitimately decide anything. Her position, as she sets it out in a working paper I have read, is this. The membership question and the ordinary question must be answered by different mechanisms, because the same body cannot both constitute itself and act. She therefore posits a hypothetical global assembly, convened on no territorial basis, whose sole competence is to fix the rules by which particular demos are bounded — she calls this the constitutive stage — after which ordinary democratic decision-making proceeds within the resulting bounded demos, the ordinary stage. The constitutive stage is authorized, on her account, by the principle that all affected interests have a claim to inclusion; the ordinary stage then operates on whatever narrower franchise the constitutive stage has settled. She insists that this dissolves the circularity rather than merely relocating it, and that it is a claim about legitimacy rather than a blueprint for institutions.

Write me eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question each on: (1) whether the constitutive stage merely relocates the boundary problem, since the global assembly must itself have a boundary — who or what is excluded from it, and on what principle; (2) whether her all-affected-interests authorization over-includes, dragging in future generations, foreigners, and non-human animals, and if she restricts it, what does the restricting; (3) how her position differs from the older idea that subjection to coercion, rather than mere affectedness, grounds a claim to membership, and why she prefers her criterion; and (4) what work the word legitimacy is doing — whether the two-stage device is a thought experiment showing that the boundary problem is not a paradox, or an institutional proposal that must answer to feasibility. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and make at least two of the follow-ups press her on whether the constitutive stage is doing any independent work or is just the ordinary demos under another name.
