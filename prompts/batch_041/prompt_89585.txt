I am building a teaching demonstration for a first-year seminar on the philosophy of time, and I want to be certain about what the demonstration can and cannot establish before I commit to building it. The apparatus is a small two-dimensional simulation of colliding hard disks whose microdynamics are exactly reversible, in the sense that reversing every velocity sends the system back along its own history. I let it evolve from a very low-entropy starting configuration, record the trajectory, and pass the recording — coarse-grained, with positions and velocities rounded onto a finite grid — to a small classifier whose only job is to guess whether it is being shown the clip forwards or backwards. The moral I want the demonstration to carry is that the thermodynamic arrow is not written into the laws of motion but is inherited from the boundary conditions, and in particular from the fact that the run begins in a minute corner of the state space.

Please set out the most important implications of that boundary-condition view for this particular demonstration, ordered by how much each one constrains what I may honestly claim. For each, say what it rules out my saying, and what it would take for the demonstration to show the contrary. I am especially concerned with four things. First, what the classifier must be supplied with for its task to be well posed at all. Second, whether the classifier may itself be a subsystem of the simulated world, and what changes if it is. Third, whether the coarse-graining of the recording is doing essential work or is merely a convenience. Fourth, what the demonstration would still show if I ran it with boundary conditions that are low-entropy at both ends of the run. I would also like to know which of these implications follow from the boundary-condition view specifically, as opposed to following from reversibility or from determinism alone, since I do not want to credit the boundary conditions with consequences that belong to something else. Finally, I keep being tempted by the thought that if the classifier can reliably distinguish forward from backward, then the arrow must after all be in the dynamics; please say what the boundary-condition view implies about that temptation, and whether the demonstration can support any claim about the direction of time itself rather than only about gradients in entropy.
