I've got a short essay I'm trying to finish and I want your comments before I send it to anyone. The thesis, roughly: when we call something "corruption" we aren't describing a transfer, we're applying a verdict that only makes sense inside one particular picture of the state — one where there's a single public, offices are held in trust, and the official's obligations run to strangers rather than to kin. Where the operative moral unit is the extended family or the patron-client chain, the same transfer reads as loyalty, as an obligation discharged, as a debt paid. A gift that creates a debt is the opposite of a bribe in that grammar. So the universalist move — corruption is corruption everywhere — is doing metaphysics, not measurement, and it's smuggling in an institutional picture and calling it a definition.

The part I think is actually mine, and the part I want you to go at hardest: I split graft into leakage and recirculation. Leakage is money that leaves — offshore, hard currency, gone, nobody downstream ever sees it. Recirculation is money that moves to kin and clients and gets spent where those people live: school fees, funerals, a roof, a cousin's shop. My claim is that the standard perception-based indices are close to blind to that difference. They mostly register the second kind, because the first kind is invisible and the second kind is exactly the sort of thing that gets noticed and gossiped about. So a lot of what gets scored as rot is a redistribution system with bad PR. I'm not saying it's fine. I'm saying the score is measuring visibility, not harm.
