Here is my objection, and my opening move as its author. The Council of Trent decreed that no one may interpret Scripture against the sense the Church has always held, or against the unanimous consent of the Fathers. Now the Fathers are unanimous that the sun moves and the earth stands still; that is how they read the passage in Joshua where the sun is bidden to stand still over Gibeon, and the psalm in which the sun comes out of its chamber like a bridegroom. You will want to answer that Scripture speaks in the language of the crowd, but that explains why God wrote as He did; it does not license a mathematician to tell the Church what He meant. And I know you are fond of the saying that Scripture teaches us how to go to heaven and not how the heavens go — but that is a pious aphorism, not a decree, and someone must be authorized to say when it applies. So put your case plainly. Have you demonstrated the earth's motion, or do you admit that you are asking the Church to trade a certainty for a conjecture? And whichever you answer, tell me who is to judge which questions touch faith and morals — you, with your geometry, or the Church?
