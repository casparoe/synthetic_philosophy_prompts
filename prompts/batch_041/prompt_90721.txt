Hey, I'm a software engineer, no real philosophy background, so go easy on me. I keep running into this idea that an AI should "avoid side effects," and every time I try to pin down what counts as a side effect I feel like I'm just smuggling in a whole theory of what's good or valuable, which is exactly the thing I don't have. I sketched a bunch of half-baked operational definitons and I want you to rank them by how far each one actually gets you toward "don't cause side effects" without needing a full specification of what the human values. Here they are, in my own words:

A. Only do what I literally asked you to do, and touch nothing else — any change I didn't request is a side effect.
B. Never reduce the set of things I could still go on to do later — if you cut off future options, that's a side effect, even if you did it while helping.
C. Behave as if you had done nothing at all, unless I explicitly asked for something else — measure every change against that do-nothing baseline.
D. Only take actions you can reverse.
E. Only affect things inside a bounding box I drew for you in advance — whatever's outside the box is off limits no matter what.
F. Check with me before any change I didn't explicitly ask for, and let me decide.

Please sort these into tiers, best to worst, by how much they let you dodge having to specify value up front, with a sentence on why each one lands where it does. I'm especially curious which of them are secretly circular — i.e. they define "side effect" in terms of what matters, so they need the value spec after all and just hide it. If some of them are really the same idea wearing different clothes, say so. And if you had to actually build one into a system tomorrow, which would you pick, and what would you have to add to make it not fall apart?

One more thing: I have no way to check you here, so please seperate what's genuinely settled in this area from what's a live fight among people who actually study it, and don't feed me a contested view as if it's the consensus. If the honest answer is "nobody knows," that's useful to me too.
