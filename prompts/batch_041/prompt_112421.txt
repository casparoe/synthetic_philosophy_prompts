For a graduate seminar handout on the intentional stance, I want a one-page decision tree that takes a reader from the claim that we can predict and explain a thermostat's behaviour by crediting it with a belief about the current temperature and a desire to be at the setting, and lands them at a named position on the original-versus-derived intentionality divide; the pedagogical point being to expose what, if anything, actually turns on the word 'original'. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: each branch node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing where it leads — whether the difference between the thermostat and a human lies in the kind of state involved or in the stance we adopt towards it, whether there is a fact of the matter about what a system represents independently of any interpreter, whether biological function or evolutionary history is what makes a state representational at all, whether the predictive success of the intentional stance counts as evidence that the attributed states are real. Put concrete cases at the nodes: the two-bitser, a Venus flytrap, a chess engine, a bacterium swimming up a chemical gradient, the printed word 'cat'. The leaves must include Dennett's own position, a Searle-style intrinsic intentionality, teleosemantics, eliminativism, and a view on which representation goes all the way down (panpsychist or panintentionalist). Where the tree delivers a combination no one occupies, say whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot prise apart. Please state how confident you are in each classification and single out the placement you are least sure of, and write in British English.
