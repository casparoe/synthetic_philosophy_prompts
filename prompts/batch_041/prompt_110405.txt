Write a joke for me. Its subject is Fitch's paradox of knowability, and I want a joke that could not have been written without following the argument, so let me set out the material first.

The paradox, as I understand it, runs as follows. An anti-realist holds that every truth is knowable: for any proposition p, if p is true, then it is possible for someone to know that p. Apply the principle to the proposition that p is true and that no one knows that p. If that conjunction could be known, then whoever knew it would know each of its conjuncts; so they would know that p, and they would also know that no one knows that p, from which it follows, since knowledge entails truth, that no one knows that p — including them, who supposedly do. So the conjunction is not knowable in any circumstances, and hence, by the anti-realist's own principle, is not true. But if p is true while that conjunction is not, then someone does know that p. Since p was arbitrary, every truth is known: the principle meant to leave room for discovery abolishes it instead.

Now transplant this into comedy. Let a joke's being funny stand in for the truth, let someone's recognising that a joke is funny stand in for knowledge, and let the anti-realist principle become the genial thought that no joke is funny in a way that is in principle beyond anyone's recognition — any joke that is funny is one that someone could, in suitable circumstances, come to find funny. Run the Fitch move on that principle and see what it implies about jokes. The analogy creaks in places — recognising that something is funny is not obviously factive, for one — and you may exploit the creaks, but do not let the joke collapse into a list of disanalogies.

The joke should be performable in under a minute: a short exchange between two speakers, a sketch premise, or a one-liner, whichever the material best supports. Its punchline must turn on the argument rather than on the familiar comedy of logicians and academics, and a reader who knows the paradox should laugh rather than merely nod. It should nonetheless carry enough of the machinery within itself to be intelligible to an attentive listener meeting the idea for the first time, so do not presuppose familiarity with the paradox, and avoid names, dates, and citations.
