I'm building a session for a small grad reading group (8 people, 90 min) around the Einstein's-brain thought experiment — you know the one, where a book contains a complete state-by-state description of Einstein's brain, so that in principle the whole thing could be run off the page. I want a decision tree that starts from the gut reaction people actually have ("it's paper and ink, obviously it's not thinking") and routes them to a named position, so that by the end they can see they were disagreeing about three or four different things and not one. Keep it to six or seven branch nodes, and make every node a question someone can answer without knowing which label it leads to — concrete cases rather than "do you accept functionalism?". The nodes I'd like on the tree: (a) what fixes which computation a physical thing implements, and specifically whether a rock can implement one too, or whether the implementation has to be carried by the thing's own causal powers rather than by some description we can cook up; (b) whether consciousness needs a particular kind of stuff or just the right organisation; (c) whether it matters that the book only "runs" if something works through it at a crawl, say a century a page — is that a difference in degree or in kind, and what is actually doing the running (the book, a reader grinding through it, or nobody at all); (d) the case where a person memorises the whole description and recites it to themselves over forty years — does the reciting count as the same thing as the book doing it? Leaves should include at least: yes and it's just slow; no, wrong stuff; no, it's a description of a computation and not a computation; yes but only trivially, since everything implements everything under some description, so you need a non-trivial notion of implementation before the question is even askable; and the one that says there's no fact of the matter here and we're really asking what it's useful to say. Tell me which leaf someone lands on if they take the trivial-implementation horn, and flag any two leaves your tree genuinely can't separate — I suspect the slow one and the trivial one collapse into each other. Finally, say which combinations of answers come out empty and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
