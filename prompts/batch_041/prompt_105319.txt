I chair our university's research-communications review committee. We sign off on how findings from our labs are described in press releases, policy briefs, and legislative testimony, and we have fallen into an inconsistency I can no longer treat as mere sloppiness: the same result is called established in one venue and hedged in another, with no change in the evidence. My suspicion is that the inconsistency tracks something real — that whether a claim counts as known may depend on what is at stake in acting on it, not only on the strength of the evidence, and that the standard for applying the word may shift with the conversational context doing the asserting. I would like you to turn this into a decision procedure the committee can actually run.

Please supply: the questions to ask, in order; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; the point at which the procedure returns a refusal to assert; and the points at which it must hand the matter to a person because the question is about values rather than evidence. Assume members with no philosophical training, roughly fifteen minutes per claim, and that I sign off.

Two matters the procedure must confront rather than finesse. First, when it tells us we may not say that our researchers know something, is the correct reading that they do not know it, or that our own context has raised the bar for saying so — and what turns on the difference for what we do next. Second, whose stakes set the bar: those of the people who will act on the claim, or those of the people who produced it.

Then run the procedure on three claims: a finding of ours about a drug interaction that a regulator may act on; that same finding as described in a grant renewal; and a finding in which I am personally invested, where I take the evidence to be strong but the procedure returns a refusal. Finally, tell me plainly whether a procedure of this shape can be faithful to the view that stakes bear on knowledge, or whether any usable procedure must quietly become a view on which the evidential threshold is fixed — and if the latter, what we give up by adopting it.
