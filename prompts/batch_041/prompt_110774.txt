Please describe the most important implications of risk-weighted expected utility theory for how a small charitable foundation should decide between a safe programme with a certain, modest benefit and a risky programme with a small probability of a much larger benefit. The foundation’s trustees disagree about risk: one is risk-averse in the sense captured by a convex risk function, one is risk-seeking in the sense captured by a concave risk function, and one is an expected utility maximiser. I would like to know what the theory says about the rationality of each trustee’s attitude, whether the risk-averse trustee’s attitude is a genuine attitude to risk rather than a reflection of diminishing marginal utility, how the foundation might aggregate their preferences into a single decision, and whether the theory implies that the choice between the two programmes is indeterminate when risk attitudes differ. Please also say what the theory implies for the foundation’s policy of never diversifying across both programmes.
