So I'm a longtime lurker on LessWrong and the rationalist blogs, and I've been down a Hofstadter rabbit hole ever since a friend lent me Gödel, Escher, Bach, and I've ended up with a take I want to present at our department's reading group next week. The take, roughly: Hofstadter's three-level story about messages -- the frame level that just tells you this is a message and to decode it, the outer level that tells you how to decode it, and the inner level that's the actual content -- secretly collapses to two levels, because the inner message is just the outer message of some nested sub-frame. There's no principled point where decoding stops, so the whole idea of meaning having a location is a bit of a category error, like asking what street address the number seven lives at; meaning isn't located, it's a process of nested frame-decoding that never bottoms out in a final inner thing. I think this is basically right but I'm aware its the kind of claim that either sounds profound or trivially dumb depending on who's in the room, and I really don't want to get shredded.
