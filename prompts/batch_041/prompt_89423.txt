I'm putting together a handout for a reading group on the epistemology of markets — half finance people, half philosophers — and I want it to be a decision tree that takes a reader from "ok, I buy the no-trade results" to a definite position on what a market price is actually telling you. So the tree starts from a commitment everyone in the room already has: if we all begin with the same prior, and it's common knowledge what each of us has seen and that each of us is rational, then we can't be left with probability estimates we both know differ — which means there's nothing left for us to bet on. The tree shouldn't be about that result. It should be about what follows for markets, and about how far it follows.

Every branch node should be a question the reader can answer without knowing where it leads. I think these nodes have to be in there, but add or drop whatever the space actually needs:

- whether trade happens mainly because people disagree about the future, or mainly because people want different things out of it (hedging, insurance, liquidity, needing the cash) — a lot of the downstream leaves collapse if you answer "mainly the second"
- whether disagreement in priors is itself rational, or always some kind of error
- whether "no free lunch" (you can't systematically beat the price) is the same claim as "the price is right" (the price is your best estimate of value). Loads of people run these two together; I want the tree to force them apart and say what has to be added to get from one to the other
- whether prices aggregate dispersed information at all, and if so whether the aggregation needs conditions nobody thinks hold — complete markets, everyone knowing the model, nobody learning anything from the price itself
- the incentive question: if the price already reflects everything, who's paying for the work of finding things out? I want a leaf for the position that this makes fully-informative prices self-undermining, and I want the tree to say whether that's meant as a refutation of efficient prices or just as a reason they're only approximately efficient
- whether the reader would rather say the no-trade result shows markets are epistemically special, or shows that the traders in the model are too thin a picture of real markets.

Leaves should carry the name of the position where a name is in use (efficient-market, common-prior Bayesian, heterogeneous-priors, "it's gains from trade not disagreement", the "trust the price, it knows more than you do" one, the self-undermining-prices one) and "unnamed" where there isn't, plus one sentence on the most awkward thing each leaf has to swallow. Where two leaves come out identical on every question, say so rather than pretending the tree separates them, and flag any answer-combination that's coherent but that nobody seems to hold — and tell me whether that's because it's incoherent or just because nobody's bothered.

Keep it to about 8 leaves and no more than 6 branch questions, and please dont put any references or reading in it, I just want the tree itself.
