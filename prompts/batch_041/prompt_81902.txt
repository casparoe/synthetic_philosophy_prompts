I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade — the Sequences, the agent-foundations and cooperative-AI corners — and I need a short research proposal for a one-semester project: a paper of roughly 8,000 words, which I also want to pitch as a workshop talk to a room that includes actual game theorists.

The idea I've been chewing on: a safe Pareto improvement is, roughly, a modification of a game that leaves every player better off with certainty — the selling point being that you don't need to know anyone's utility function or trust anyone's good will, because the improvement holds however the others play. That's why the notion gets used to talk about cooperative gains without pretending we can infer preferences from behavior. But every time I look at how these results are actually established, the safety turns out to be safety relative to a class of assumptions about play: that iterated removal of dominated strategies is common knowledge, that isomorphic games get played isomorphically, that players who can commit keep their commitments, that cheap talk is inert. Those aren't facts about the world; they're a model of the other players, and I can't tell whether they're load-bearing or bookkeeping.

What I want the proposal to be about: whether there is a version of safe Pareto improvement whose guarantee doesn't run through a contestable model of how other agents play — whether safety can be cashed out structurally, from features of the situation or from a commitment technology that is itself ex post verifiable, rather than by restricting the hypothesis class over play. Concretely: (a) can we state a non-trivial improvement that holds across an interestingly large class of play rules, or is there a collapse result — as the class grows toward anything goes, do the surviving improvements shrink to the trivial ones, like adding an option nobody takes or relabelling a game? (b) if the answer is no collapse, is the resulting framework just inverse reinforcement learning with the inference moved from preferences to play rules, so that the same underdetermination worries return and safe always meant safe relative to my model? (c) does anything change if the players are themselves uncertain about each other's play rules and know it?

Turn this into the proposal: state the question so that it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing treatments leave it open rather than settling it, describe what I'd actually do month by month (I'm happy with formal work — small games, explicit definitions, proofs where they're gettable — but I don't want to promise a general theorem I can't land in a semester), name the approach and say why it beats the obvious alternatives, say what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing, and argue that it's worth doing. Skip the bibliography and literature review; I'll add citations myself. End with a short section on what would change my mind: which consideration, if it went the other way, would make the framing misconceived rather than merely in need of refinement, and where the reasoning is most likely to break.
