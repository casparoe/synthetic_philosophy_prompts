I sit on the awards committee of a scientific society, and we meet on Thursday to settle a disputed prize. Two researchers reached the same result within weeks of each other; one published first but left a gap that a third party closed only later, while the other's manuscript, circulated privately beforehand, contains the cleaner and more general derivation. Our bylaws say only that the prize recognizes whoever "made the discovery," which settles nothing, and the committee is split between awarding it to the first publisher, splitting it, or deferring the whole matter to a working group. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete disposition and give the committee reasons it can actually act on, including an honest statement of the strongest case for the disposition I would be rejecting. I want the memo to do some conceptual work rather than split the difference: it should say what we are really rewarding — being first, being the one who made the result robust and usable, or being the one without whom the field would have waited — and it should take a position on whether the fact that the result was, as the nomination letters put it, ripe for the taking should count against either claimant at all. Distinguish the sense in which a discovery can be inevitable (the surrounding pieces were all in place) from the sense in which the specific formulation remains a contingent achievement, and say which sense our decision should track. Finally, say how the committee should treat the earlier partial contributor, and propose a general rule for future cases that does not simply collapse into awarding everyone who was working nearby.
