I recently read a popular article on population ethics that summarized the non-identity problem as follows, and I would like to know how much of it to trust.

'The non-identity problem, usually credited to Parfit, concerns choices that determine not only how well off people will be but who will exist. Suppose a woman conceives while taking a drug that causes a mild disability, and conceiving later would have produced a different child. The child who exists cannot claim to have been harmed by her mother's choice, since the alternative available to her was not existing at all: the disability did not make her worse off than she would otherwise have been. This refutes the person-affecting view — the view that an act is wrong only if it makes someone worse off — because that view cannot say what is wrong with the mother's choice, or with a policy that consumes resources on which future generations will depend. The wider lesson is that people who do not yet exist have no moral standing in present decisions: whatever we do, some future person will owe their existence to it, and no such person can be worse off for what we did. We are left choosing between the total view, which counts lives by their total value regardless of who lives them, and the repugnant conclusion that comes with it.'

Please audit this paragraph. Going claim by claim, tell me what a philosopher working on population ethics would accept, what is a fair simplification, and what is a misrepresentation. I am especially interested in whether the non-identity problem really refutes the person-affecting view, and whether the conclusion about future people's moral standing follows from the parts that are accurate. (The disability case itself strikes me as showing that the child has nothing to complain about, so I suspect the article is right up until its last two sentences.) And what does the paragraph leave out — in particular, how the non-identity problem is related to but distinct from the repugnant conclusion, and whether it arises only in cases where different choices produce different people?
