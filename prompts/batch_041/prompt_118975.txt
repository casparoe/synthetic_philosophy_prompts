I've basically been talked into risk-weighted expected utility. The picture: a rational agent isn't just choosing between outcomes and their probabilities, she also gets to care about *how* the chances are spread out — treating a coin-flip shot at a good thing differently from a sure smaller thing isn't a mistake about the odds, it's a legitimate feature of how she weighs chances. I think a lot of what gets called risk aversion is this and not an error, and I like that it doesn't just declare the Allais-style choices irrational. Now play skeptic for me: what would actually count against it? I don't want "but expected utility is the norm" — I want the observations, cases, intuitions and formal results that should move someone who already leans this way, ranked from decisive to merely awkward.

Two spots I suspect it's weakest, so dig in on these. First, the risk-weighting function looks like it can be fitted after the fact to almost any pattern of choices over a fixed menu of gambles — if it can absorb anything, has the view quietly stopped risking anything, and is that a fatal problem or just the price of a theory that's part descriptive? Second, cases where the probabilities themselves are murky, or where the bad outcome is catastrophic but extremely unlikely — what does the view have to say there, and does it end up recommending something a sane person would reject?

Also tell me which of these the view's own defenders would already concede count against them, versus what they'd brush off, and whether there's any real test it can fail. It'd help to have one concrete case that separates it from plain expected utility and one that separates it from a rival that also lets you weight probabilities but differently.

Take the role of a decision theorist who knows this literature cold, and think carefully — brainstorm first, steelman the view before you start swinging at it, and don't hand me a strawman.

Format: a JSON object with these fields — position_summary (one sentence), decisive_defeaters (array of objects, each with defeater, why_it_bites, what_evidence_would_settle_it), awkward_defeaters (array of strings), conceded_by_defenders (array of strings), insulation (object with well_insulated true/false, why, whether_that_is_a_problem), separating_cases (array of two objects, each with case and divergent_prediction, one vs plain EU and one vs the rival probability-weighting view), and bottom_line (a short paragraph).
