Bit of an odd one. I'm a second-year biology student, and my flatmate is doing a philosophy MA, so we end up arguing about this sort of thing over dinner, and there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I cant find a word for. Here's the setup. Everyone agrees you can read the order of amino acids in a protein off the base sequence of a gene. Everyone also agrees you can read a tree's age off its rings. In both cases there's a reliable correlation you can exploit to get from one thing to the other. But nobody says the tree rings code for the tree's age, and almost everyone, including my textbooks, says the gene codes for the protein. When I push on what the difference is supposed to be, the answers keep shifting: it's that the mapping from bases to amino acids is arbitrary, or it's that the cell actually uses the sequence as a representation, or it's that the sequence can be wrong -- a mutation can "say" the wrong thing in a way that a tree ring can't be wrong about the age of its tree. My flatmate insists the last one is the real one and the other two are distractions; my genetics lecturer says the whole thing is a metaphor people should stop reifying. And when I say fine, it's a metaphor, but it's clearly doing work in the explanations, everyone nods, and then uses it literally again in the next sentence.

So what I want to know is whether there's an established name for the thing I'm circling here: a physical arrangement that is supposed to be about something, where being about it requires more than a correlation but less than a convention. If there is such a name, which of those three criteria (arbitrariness, use, being-able-to-be-wrong) actually belongs to it, and what happens to the other two -- do they get demoted to symptoms, or to something else entirely? I'd like the nearest existing concepts spelled out and exactly how each one falls short of what I've described, and if nothing quite fits, then coin a term and tell me where its edges are: what's a borderline case, what's clearly outside it, and what turns on the choice. I'm not after a label on its own and definitely not a reading list -- I want the carving. Keep the whole thing under 400 words.
