Design a case in which Aumann's agreement theorem's hypotheses hold exactly, yet the agreement it certifies is epistemically empty. Two agents share a prior over a small finite state space, each is a correct Bayesian, and at the actual state their posterior probabilities for some ordinary proposition are common knowledge, so the theorem forces them to the same credence. Specifications: at most six states, with the prior, both information partitions, and the actual state written out and each agent's posterior at each state computed; the meet of the two partitions must be strictly coarser than each of them, so that neither agent's evidence is common knowledge; the common credence must be identified as the conditional probability of the proposition given what the two agents commonly know, i.e. the conditional expectation given the meet of the partitions, and exhibited as a probability-weighted average of their cell-wise posteriors rather than as a midpoint; each agent's own evidence must leave the proposition exactly as uncertain as the prior did, while the two cells they occupy at the actual state intersect in a way that settles the proposition, so that they agree on an uninformative credence though between them they know the answer; and you should compute the expected squared error of the agreed credence and of the pooled credence and show the former is strictly larger. Then give the variant: alter one feature so that the theorem no longer applies and the two rationally disagree at that same state. Finish with which feature is doing the work, how a defender of the reading that rational agents with a common prior cannot disagree would respond, and how much of that reading the case leaves standing. This is for a discussion of whether the theorem shows rational agents converging on the truth; a case that stipulates the agreement rather than deriving it from the prior and the partitions does not meet the brief.
