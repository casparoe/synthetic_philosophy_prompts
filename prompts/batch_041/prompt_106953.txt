Consider a two-level system sometimes called a careenium: a large collection of bouncing balls (simmballs) whose micro-trajectories are compressible into a small vocabulary of symbols, even though no part of the system reads or represents those symbols. Compare two accounts of the relation between the symbol level and the ball level: (A) the symbols are causally efficacious—they push the substrate around, so symbol-level interventions are genuine interventions on the substrate; (B) the symbols are a compact description of the substrate but add no causal push of their own. Which account is more defensible, and what would have to be true of the compression for (A) to be more than a useful fiction? In answering, separate the predictive usefulness of symbol-level generalizations from their causal-explanatory status. Answer in no more than 400 words.
