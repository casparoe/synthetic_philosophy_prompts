I am trying to get clear on a pattern that shows up in a small, artificial problem domain, and I do not know whether it already has a name or a standard analysis. The domain is a family of puzzles in which a solver is shown a short string of symbols and must transform it into another string by analogy: a sequence of three symbols changes into a sequence of three symbols, and then a different sequence must be changed in the same way. The symbols are deliberately ambiguous. The same mark can be read as a letter with a next letter, as a numeral with an arithmetic value, as a shape with a left-right orientation, or as an object occupying a position in a row. What interests me is what happens when a solver starts with one reading, gets a partial answer that feels wrong, and then, without abandoning the puzzle, switches to another reading. The switch is not a random guess. It is motivated: the first reading leaves an awkward remainder, the second reading absorbs that remainder, and the second reading is also consistent with the original example. But the switch changes what counts as the same kind of thing, so the solver's earlier steps are no longer steps in the same problem; they are reinterpreted as something else. If the solver then switches back, or switches a third time, the history of the solution becomes a chain of locally justified re-descriptions rather than a single deduction.

I can give two instances. In one, a solver treats the rightmost symbol as the one that must change, then treats the rightmost position as a place where a new symbol must be inserted, then treats the whole string as a sequence of run-lengths rather than a sequence of symbols. In another, a solver begins by matching symbols pairwise, then decides that the real correspondence is between a symbol and the gap after it, then decides that the gaps themselves form a string. Each move is reasonable given the state of the puzzle, and each move makes the previous move look like a mistake that was not a mistake.

I want to know whether there is an established concept for this exact phenomenon: not just analogy-making, not just ambiguity resolution, not just revision of a hypothesis, but the specific way the basic furniture of a small problem domain can slide under the pressure of a partial solution while the solver experiences the slide as progress. If there is a name, I want the nearest names and an account of how they differ from what I have described. If there is no name, I want a term and a definition, with boundary cases: what would count as an instance, what would be merely ambiguous notation, what would be ordinary trial and error, and what would be a genuine change of problem rather than a slippage within one. Please do not just give me a label; the carving is what I need.

At the end, say what would change your mind about the identification, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
