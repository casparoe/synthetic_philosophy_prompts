In June 2022, a Google engineer named Blake Lemoine went public with the claim that the company's large language model LaMDA had become sentient, citing its conversational responses as evidence that it had feelings and was afraid of being shut off. Google rejected the claim and suspended him, and most researchers in the field rejected it as well, though for a striking variety of reasons. Treat that episode as a test case for functionalism and for the thesis of multiple realizability.

I want a disciplined case analysis, not a verdict on Lemoine and not a survey of the philosophy of mind. Begin by stating exactly what functionalism claims — that mental states are fixed by their causal-functional roles rather than by the material that realizes them — and what multiple realizability is meant to establish, distinguishing the version that serves as an argument against type-identity theories of mind from the version that is a claim about consciousness itself. Then ask what a functionalist is committed to in this case, and what, by functionalism's lights, each side should have concluded about LaMDA. If minds can be realized in any substrate that implements the right functional organization, does functionalism hand Lemoine his best argument? Or does it turn out to be silent, or even unhelpful, once we ask which functional organization LaMDA actually implements, what the difference is between realizing a mental process and merely simulating one, and how either could be detected from the outside?

Then press the case in the other direction, against the theory. Take the objection that a sufficiently large lookup table — a system that produces all the right conversational outputs by brute table-lookup rather than by anything like the causal organization of a mind — would satisfy the functionalist's conditions while plainly having no inner life. Say what that does to functionalism's ability to separate LaMDA from a system everyone would agree is not sentient, and identify the point at which the functionalist must either bite a bullet or draw a line that cannot be justified. Bring in the asymmetry between the functionalist's confidence about states such as belief and desire and the difficulty of saying anything functional about phenomenal consciousness, and say whether that asymmetry is a defect of the theory or evidence that the episode is being asked to settle a question functionalism never promised to answer.

Finally, run the episode back onto the theory. Does LaMDA-style AI make functionalism more credible, because the substrate evidently does not matter to what the system does, or less credible, because the case exposes functionalism's willingness to attribute minds too cheaply? Say what a defensible functionalist ought to concede, and whether multiple realizability, which began as an argument for the reality of the mental, ends up as an argument for its promiscuity.
