Two of my housemates got into a fight at the kitchen table and I couldnt referee it, so I'm outsourcing it. It started over the app our landlord rolled out: instead of opting *in* to the building's package (cleaning service, package lockers, the gym nobody uses), you're enrolled by default and have to go uncheck a box to get out, and if you do nothing you get charged. Sam and Noor went at it for an hour.

Sam's line: the app is just a tool. It's a form with a checkbox. Nobody's forcing anyone -- if you don't want it you uncheck it, ten seconds. Blaming the software is like blaming a pen for what you signed. Whatever politics there is lives in the landlord's decision, not in the app.

Noor: that's backwards. A default isn't a neutral resting state, it's a decision about what happens to everyone who never touches the form, which is alot of people. The app doesn't merely permit enrollment, it manufactures enrollment out of inaction. The politics is in how the thing is built, not in anybody's head.

Sam: 'manufactures' is doing the work there. If I leave my door unlocked I 'manufacture' a burglary, but the door isn't a burglar. And you can undo the default in ten seconds -- you can't say a thing is doing something to you that you can trivially undo. Weakness isn't coercion.

Noor: I never said coercion, I said politics. And notice you needed a counterfactual to make your door point -- an unlocked door is only a problem against a baseline of locked ones. Same move sinks you: the default is political because of the baseline it replaces, and the baseline it replaced was 'you have to ask to be charged.' So the politics is in the difference the thing makes, and that difference is built into the artifact, not into an intention.

Sam: then every artifact that changes anything is political and the word means nothing. A can opener changed things. And you're sneaking intent back in -- someone chose that baseline, someone chose the default. It's choices all the way down, which is my point.

Noor: the point is the choice is *stored* in the artifact and keeps running after the chooser is gone, on people who never met them. That's the difference between a decision and an object that carries one.

I genuinely think they were each onto something and also talking past each other. Can you go through it move by move -- who's right at each step, where exactly they're talking past each other, and what each of them is missing?
