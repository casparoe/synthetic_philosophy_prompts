Please explain the conceptual issue at stake in the idea, often associated with Hofstadter, that an 'I' could be distributed across several brains. Imagine a person's memories, habits, and self-model are gradually replicated in three others, so that after the original dies there are three people each of whom sincerely says 'I remember being her' and each of whom is causally downstream from her, though none has her original brain. Set aside whether this is physically possible and ask what the persistence claim could mean: numerical identity, causal-informational continuity, survival of a pattern, or a normative relation such as being the rightful bearer of her commitments. Distinguish these readings, say which would make the claim that she persists in others trivial and which substantive, and explain why the word 'distributed' encourages a slide between them. No citations or literature review are needed; I want the conceptual map and the precise ambiguity.
