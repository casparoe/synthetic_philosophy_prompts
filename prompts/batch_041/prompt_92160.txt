Then she says flat out whether cooperation takes hold.

- Big anonymous population, two players matched once at random and never again, nobody watching. Payoffs T=6, R=5, P=1, S=0. Her call: no, cooperation never gets going.
- Same population, but now the two keep meeting round after round with an indefinite horizon — a 95% chance of another round each time — and they recognize each other and remember everything. Payoffs T=6, R=5, P=1, S=0. Her call: yes, cooperation shows up and holds.
- Same repeated, recognizable setup, but with a fat temptation: T=20, R=5, P=1, S=0, still a 95% chance of continuing each round. Her call: still yes.
- One-shot again, but this time the two players do know exactly who the other is — they just will never interact again. T=6, R=5, P=1, S=0. Her call: no.

She's rock-consistent on all four, but I've thought about it alot and I genuinely can't tell what she's keying on, and I want to stump her at my seminar next week. Here's the case I built:

Two players matched with each other over and over, indefinite horizon, 90% chance of another round each time, perfect recognition and perfect memory of the whole history. Payoffs: T=100, R=3, P=0, S=-1.

Lay out the couple of different rules that fit every one of her earlier calls just as well, say what each one predicts for my new case, tell me which you'd bet she's really using and why, and then name one other setup — not this one — that would best seperate those rules.
