Consider a proposal I will call the responsiveness criterion: a case counts as genuine specification gaming only when the proxy's loss of validity is counterfactually dependent on a goal-directed process — that is, only when some agent would have reallocated effort toward the proxy had the proxy been defined differently, holding the underlying objective fixed. On this criterion, the cases a standard taxonomy labels extremal — where a proxy degrades merely because we select the best-scoring item from a large pool, with no responsive agent doing the selecting — are not instances of gaming at all, but a statistical artifact of selection (regression to the mean) that shares only a name with the real phenomenon. A corollary I am unsure about: if the criterion is right, the law should be restated as a claim about optimization pressure rather than about measures, and should predict no degradation under satisficing even when the same proxy is maximized up to a fixed threshold.

My own credence that the responsiveness criterion draws the boundary in the right place is about 0.45; a colleague puts it near 0.85. Give me your number, decomposed: the probability that selection on a proxy with no responsive agent can nonetheless count as gaming; the probability that the counterfactual test is even well-defined when the "agent" is an evolutionary, market, or institutional process rather than a person; the probability that the criterion does genuine classificatory work rather than relabeling cases we already understood; the probability that the satisficing corollary holds given the criterion; and any further terms you think belong — a rough probability and one line of justification for each, then the combination. Finish by naming the single term your final number is most sensitive to and the argument or example that would move it most.
