I am rebuilding the week on the limits of knowledge in my second-year epistemology course around Fitch's argument, and I want to present it as delivering a modest but genuine result: there are truths that cannot be known, namely the Moorean ones of the form "p, but nobody knows that p," and verificationism fails precisely because it overlooked them. The seminar will culminate in an exercise in which students generate their own examples of unknowable truths; I intend to seed it with the three categories where I take unknowability to bite hardest — facts about the remote past, facts about other minds, and facts about the distant future. Please help me get the framing right. Since the class has already covered Moore's paradox, how should I explain why "p, but nobody knows that p" cannot be known, and how do I carry that explanation over to the historical, psychological and future-directed cases, so that students leave with a clear sense of which truths are the unknowable ones?
