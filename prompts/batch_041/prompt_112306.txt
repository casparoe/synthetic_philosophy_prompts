I'm applying for a small internal grant — about a semester of part-time work — and need a short research proposal, roughly a page and a half, on how to update credences when the evidence itself is uncertain. Treat what follows as a half-formed idea to be developed rather than assessed.

When you learn a proposition outright, you condition on it. When you merely become more confident of it — you don't learn that it is true, only that it is now more likely — the usual fix is to raise your credence in the proposition and rescale the rest, holding fixed the probabilities of your hypotheses conditional on the proposition and on its negation. (This is the rule usually called Jeffrey conditionalization; I care about the substance, not the label.) Two things about it bother me. It tells you what to do only relative to a partition you have already chosen, so the substantive work seems to be done by a choice the rule itself cannot justify. And if you receive two pieces of uncertain evidence in succession, the order in which you absorb them can change where you end up.

My hunch is that both symptoms have a single source: the rule refuses to represent the process that produced the evidence, and instead treats a fragment of that process as primitive. I suspect it is best understood as a special case of a more general update in which you explicitly model how the evidence was generated.

Write the proposal: state the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the existing treatments leave it open; set out the method concretely; say what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and give a realistic scope for a semester of part-time work.
