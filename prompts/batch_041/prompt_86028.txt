I'm putting together materials for a reading group that keeps going round in circles on the same thing, and I want to make them do the adversarial-collaboration exercise properly. The two camps are these. On one side, someone who thinks weakness of will is basically a problem of hyperbolic discounting: present bias, preference reversals over time, quasi-hyperbolic or beta-delta discounting, commitment devices and Ulysses contracts, the whole sophisticated-versus-naive business (including the slightly odd result that being sophisticated about your own future bias can leave you worse off if you can't actually commit). On the other side, someone who thinks all that is beside the point, because akrasia in the Aristotle/Davidson sense is acting against your own better judgement at the moment of action, and a discounting model can't even state that thought — it takes preferences as given, so it has no room for a judgement that isn't just a preference, and no room for the distinction between judging something best and wanting it most.

Write the joint statement the two of them could actually put their names to. I want:

(1) a numbered list of claims both accept — about which phenomena each side is trying to explain, about what the evidence shows (preference reversal in people, the same patterns in pigeons and rats, commitment-device studies, choice-then-pay type designs), and about what each side grants the other has got right;

(2) the residual disagreements in a table, each one phrased in wording its own side would accept rather than a compromise neither would sign;

(3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling things would move at least one side. My guesses: whether a preference reversal at 4pm counts as irrationality at all or just as the person's preferences having changed; whether a sophisticated agent can be akratic in any interesting sense, or has simply stopped having a better judgement to flout; and the multiple-selves problem — if the agent is a series of time-slices with conflicting preferences, who exactly is being weak-willed?;

(4) for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Don't smooth it over, and if part of the fight turns out to be verbal — both sides using 'will' or 'judgement' for different things, say — then say which part and why. Where the discounting framework is just silent rather than wrong, say so instead of making it answer. No citations needed, I know the literature.

Answer in British English please.
