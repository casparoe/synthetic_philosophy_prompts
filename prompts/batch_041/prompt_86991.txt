Each half is split again into two equal pieces, one held by Second Company and one held by Headquarters Company. She gets on the radio and asks where she is. The voice can't say, but it tells her one thing: if she's in Red territory, then she's in the Second Company area. Then the radio goes dead.

Two other characters are in the ditch with her, and they start going at it. The first one says: fine, take your four hunches, cross out whatever doesn't fit what the radio just told you, then nudge what's left as little as you possibly can — the number basically falls out on its own, and he claims he can actually put a figure on it. The second one says: hold on. The message told you something about Second Company. It didn't tell you a damn thing about Red versus Blue, and there's no single smallest nudge that's forced on you here. He reckons her Red-vs-Blue odds shouldn't budge at all, and that anybody who gets a different figure out of this is running math the situation never licensed. They're both sharp, neither one is a strawman, and I want real back-and-forth — each of them answering the other's actual last point instead of delivering a speech past it. I want at least one moment where somebody has to give ground or reframe something, and I want the reader to feel the pull of both sides. Please dont hand me a reading list or a pile of names to go look up; I don't have time to chase down papers, I just want the argument itself, worked out properly.

Give me the dialogue. Then on its own line at the very end, put \answer{...} with your best judgment of what her new chance of being in Red territory should be — a fraction, or the word undetermined if you think the argument genuinely doesn't settle it.
