I've been going down a rabbit hole about Gödel's rotating universe and I want a proper essay out of it. Here's the thing that's bugging me. Gödel found a solution to Einstein's field equations where the whole universe is filled with swirling dust and there's a negative cosmological constant tuned just so. It's a legitimate solution, no singularities, but it has closed timelike curves. In plain terms, a rocket can fly a continuous, ordinary-looking path and end up at an event in its own past. Gödel took this to be a big deal for the philosophy of time. His line, roughly, was that if a physically possible world has no global time order of the kind we normally assume, then the objective lapse or passage of time can't be a necessary feature of reality; time in some sense is ideal, a projection of our own minds, or at least not the robust thing common sense takes it to be.

But I keep thinking the argument either proves too much or too little. On one side, you might say: closed timelike curves show that this particular spacetime lacks a global time function. They don't show that time itself is unreal. Plenty of spacetimes lack a preferred foliation; that's just general relativity being general relativity. Maybe objective passage was never supposed to be a global order of events anyway. On the other side, if you're a certain kind of idealist about time, the Gödel universe is a nice model: it gives you a world where the distinction between past and future can't be drawn globally, so the felt passage of time can't be tracking a global feature of the world. And then there's a middle view: Gödel's universe might show that objective becoming is not a necessary feature of every physically possible world, but not that it is absent from our world or that the concept is incoherent.

I want you to write an essay on this controversy. Don't just explain what Gödel said or what the physics is. Weigh the strongest versions of the opposing positions and reach a verdict: does the Gödel universe give real support for the ideality or unreality of time, or is the whole inference from closed timelike curves to the philosophy of time a mistake? If you think the answer depends on what you mean by time, say which meaning you're using and why that's the relevant one.

Please structure it like this:
