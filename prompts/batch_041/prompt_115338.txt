I'm a backend engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and for the past few months I've been sitting in on a weekly reading group about nationalism and cosmopolitanism. The argument keeps circling one question — whether sharing a state, a language, or a passport gives me claims on a stranger that a stranger elsewhere does not have — and I've noticed that people on both sides keep winning by quietly assuming a different job description for the theory they're attacking. One camp wants an account on which a border is no more morally weighty than the colour of my eyes; the other wants an account that explains why a state may take my money and hand it to a poor old man inside the border rather than an equally poor one outside it. Before I read any more candidates, I want the specification instead of another one. So write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of special obligations to compatriots has to deliver. I want the cases it must get right, each with the consideration behind it; the distinctions it must not collapse; and a clear separation between the requirements that are genuine and the ones that are merely the prejudices of one camp wearing the costume of a criterion.

Please make the list strong enough to handle a few situations that the usual framing seems to hide. There is the two-by-two I keep running into: people who share a nation but not a state (a diaspora, a stateless people, a nation split across borders) and people who share a state but not a nation (a naturalised citizen who never picks up the culture, a country held together by institutions alone). There is the emigrant who naturalises somewhere else — do the old obligations attenuate, persist, or convert into something else? There is conscription, where the theory has to say whether the state may compel me to kill and die for co-nationals in a way it could not compel me to do for strangers. And there is the question of nesting: if I owe something to my city, my nation, and to humanity at large, are those obligations additive, ranked lexicographically, or genuinely rivalrous — and can a theory be foundationalist about the small circle and derivative about the large one, or the other way round? The list also has to be neutral in a way I can state out loud: a thoroughgoing cosmopolitan who thinks compatriot partiality is a moral error should be able to satisfy every requirement on it, otherwise the list is rigged and I'll be able to tell. Flag which requirements are negotiable and which are not, say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates, and tell me whether any pair of them cannot be satisfied together — that last part is the one I actually care about. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scoring; no citations needed, I'll chase those down myself, and a couple of lines at the end on how strict cosmopolitanism, liberal nationalism, and the associative-ties view fare against the list is plenty.
