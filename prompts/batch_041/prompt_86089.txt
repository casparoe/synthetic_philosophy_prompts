I've spent the last few months trailing an art critic for a profile — let's call her Vera, she's been reviewing for forty years and has very settled instincts — and I've ended up with a side project: working out what she actually believes about whether you can learn anything about a work of art just by being told about it. Apparently philosophers have a whole fight about this, which I did not know before I started.

Anyway I've got five verdicts out of her, and now a new one has come up. Here they are, as close to her words as my notes allow:

- A conductor friend told her that a piece she'd always heard as shapeless is actually in sonata form, with the recapitulation comeing back in the wrong key. Vera: that was real learning. She says she knows something about the piece now that she didn't know before, and she hears it differently.
- A friend of hers, a guy who reads thrillers on holiday and never talks about books, told her a new novel was devastating. Vera: she can't know that until she reads it. All she knows is that he was devastated.
- A sommelier told her that a wine she was about to taste had a wet-stone quality. Vera: real information. She'll know what to look for.
- An art historian told her that a painting she'd loved for years was a forgery painted in the 1930s. Vera: yes, that's knowledge, and it changed how she sees the thing.
- A taxi driver told her the new Bond film was rubbish. Vera: she's got no idea whether it's rubbish, and she doesnt think shes learned anything about the film.

The new case: a conductor she's known for twenty years and trusts completly tells her, right before the downbeat, that the slow movement she's about to hear is the most beautiful thing ever written. Does she come away knowing something about the music, or only something about him?

What I want: the rules that fit her five verdicts (I suspect there's more than one and they've been hiding behind each other), what each of those rules predicts about the new case, your best guess at which rule is really doing the work and why, and the one further case I should put to her that would seperate them.
