I'm a science journalist — long features, mostly medicine and space, which means I spend a lot of my time talking to patients, whistleblowers, grad students, and occasionally some guy who got a once-in-a-lifetime photo of a launch. Which also means I spend alot of time deciding whether money should change hands, and right now I do that by vibe, which is embarrassing. Can you build me an actual decision procedure?

Ground it in the philosophy of commodification — the repugnant markets stuff, the arguments about which goods can and can't be bought and sold, and why. What I need from it specifically: it has to keep strait three separate kinds of objection, because they give different verdicts. There's the kind that's really about coercion or exploitation — the payment is wrong because of the power imbalance, so better terms could fix it (pay more, offer something non-cash, make sure the person actually understood what they were agreeing to). There's the kind that's about meaning, where the exchange itself is the problem and no better terms help. And there's the kind about what happens to everyone else if this becomes normal — the norm of not paying for X erodes, and now nobody can get an honest X. A step doing coercion work should end differently from a step doing meaning work, and if your procedure can't tell me which one it's using at each stage, it isn't a procedure, it's a mood.

Requirements: something I can run in about five minutes, usually alone but sometimes with an editor on the phone. Questions in order. What counts as a sufficient answer at each step. Where it stops with a flat no. And where it hands off to a person because the question is values rather than facts — and say who that person should be. Keep the language plain enough that I could paste it into a Slack channel for the rest of the desk.

I also want it to handle the gradient, not just cash: cash vs. paying "for their time" vs. expenses vs. a gift vs. paying someone adjacent to the source (a fixer, a translator, the source's lawyer, the source's institution). Plus who's paying — me personally, the outlet, or a fund we keep for exactly this.

Run it on three: (1) a patient with a rare disease, I want a long on-the-record interview and her medical records, she wants $500 and says it's for her time; (2) a source who'll hand over a big hospital billing dataset if I pay him for his time, he says he'll lose his job otherwise; (3) a lab offering to fly me out and put me up for a week to see their instrument. I have a hunch the procedure is going to tell me not to pay the patient, and I want it to actually reckon with why that bothers me — it feels like protecting the newsroom's conscience at her expense, and "we don't pay sources" has a way of meaning "we'll take the story and you get nothing."

Two more things. Newsrooms already have rules here that don't line up — you can buy a photo, you can't buy a quote, you can accept a lunch but not a flight. Tell me whether that inconsistency is defensible or just history, and where your procedure lands relative to it. And tell me what the procedure can't handle: cross-border cases where the payment is illegal for the source, cases where I'm the one who'd profit from the story, and anything where there's simply no price to compare against.

Start with the bottom line in one sentence — the single rule you'd want me to remember if I forgot everything else — then the procedure. No preamble, no restating my question.
