I did a maths degree and drifted into reading philosophy on the side, and there's a shape of disagreement I keep running into that I don't have a word for. The concrete thing: my platonist friend thinks mathematical statements are true because there really are numbers and sets and functions out there that they describe. My nominalist friend thinks there are no such things and that maths is a usefull fiction, or a heap of conditionals about what follows from what. And yet the two of them agree, theorem by theorem, on which sentences are true — every single one. The disagreement never surfaces inside the mathematics; it only surfaces when you ask what the sentences are *about*. Same in the physics case: one thinks the numbers in a law of nature stand for genuine abstracta, the other thinks they're bookkeeping, and neither can point to a proof or an experiment that pulls them apart.

So is there a name for a dispute with this shape — both sides accept every sentence in some domain, and they disagree only over what those sentences commit you to or what they're about — and a name for the further move where someone says that because of this the dispute was never a real disagreement at all? I know platonism and nominalism label the two sides; that's not what I'm after. I want the term (if it exists) for the *structure* of the standoff and for the diagnosis that it's empty, and I want the line drawn between that diagnosis and just refusing to do metaphysics. If nothing established fits, coin something and tell me what it includes and leaves out — and say whether the same structure turns up in, say, the free will debate or whether it's seperate to maths. Don't just hand me the label; I care about the carving, i.e. what the thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating my question.
