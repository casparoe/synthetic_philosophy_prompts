I teach a seminar on autonomy and influence, and the thing that keeps tripping me up isn't the textbook cases — it's a pattern I've started noticing everywhere and can't find a home for. Example one: in a department meeting last term, a colleague got me to change my vote on a hire. He never said anything false, never leaned on me, never even really argued — he just chose which of the files we looked at first, let a couple of silences run long, and asked the one question he knew would land. I changed my mind, and honestly I'd change it again; I still think the candidate he favored was better. And yet I came out of that room feeling handled, and I still do. Example two: my sister-in-law is a therapist, and she says flatly that she never manipulates her clients. But she absolutely picks which question to ask and when, which interpretation to float and which to sit on for a month, and she'd say that's just what competent practice is. Example three, the one that bothers me most: I write my exams in a particular order, and I know perfectly well that the order moves the answers around, and I keep doing it, because there's no non-moving order to use instead.

Here's what I can't get a grip on. Everything I've read about manipulation seems to build in that the manipulator goes around the person's reasoning — plays on a bias, hides the real motive, applies pressure that isn't a reason. But in these cases nothing is bypassed. I reason, I weigh, I conclude, and the conclusion is one I endorse on reflection and would endorse again. It's the material I was handed that was chosen for me — the order, the salience, the timing, what got left in the drawer. And when I raise it, people say well, that's just persuasion, all persuasion is selective, you can't present every consideration at once, and by your standard every teacher and every friend is a manipulator. Which I don't accept, but I also can't say precisely why not, and that gap is what's eating at me.

So: is there an established concept for influence of this shape — influence that runs through a person's rational capacities rather than around them, that survives their retrospective endorsement, and that nonetheless seems to compromise something? I don't want a label slapped on it; I want the carving. Where exactly is the line between this and ordinary selective persuasion, is the difference in the mechanism or in the outcome or in who's setting the terms, and does it come apart from nudging and from the usual manipulation literature in a way that earns its own name? If nothing established fits, coin something and give me the boundary cases — the ones where you'd say no, that's just teaching, or no, that's just conversation.
