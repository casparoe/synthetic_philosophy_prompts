Last night I got into a two-hour argument with my old roommate, who's now a philosophy grad student, about whether he could have been a doctor instead of a philosopher. I said sure — same guy, different job; the world where he's a doctor contains him, just with different things true of him. He said that's incoherent, nobody exists in more than one world; what makes the sentence true is that some other guy in that world, a lot like him, is a doctor. I said that's just relabeling the same fact, and we went around like that for two hours, him bringing up Lewis and the problem of accidental intrinsics and the objection Kripke made about Hubert Humphrey (that if all we're saying is that a counterpart of Humphrey won the election, then we're not talking about Humphrey at all), me saying it all sounded like bookkeeping.

Here's the thing, though: I'm a backend engineer and I spend basically every day in git, and somewhere in hour one I started suspecting the whole argument maps onto stuff I know cold — content-addressed blobs and trees, commit hashes, refs and branches, parent pointers and reflogs, three-way merges, cherry-picks, rename detection with its similarity threshold, dangling objects after a gc. So I want you to actually do the translation, properly. Take the debate — transworld identity, counterpart theory, the point that the counterpart relation is a similarity relation that isn't symmetric or transitive and shifts with context, the accidental-intrinsics motivation, the Humphrey complaint, whatever else you think is load-bearing — and restate it in git's vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report: what carries over cleanly, what only carries over with distortion, and what has no expression in git at all. For each piece of residue, tell me whether it's a hole in git's model or a sign that the philosophers are arguing about less than they think.

Concretely, I want to know: is a blob reachable from two branches the same blob or two counterparts, and does git's answer actually settle anything or is it just a convention of the storage layer? If a file's identity is its content hash, does git give you individual essences or the opposite? Does rename detection being a tunable threshold mean git is on my friend's side? And when lineage is sitting right there in the parent pointers, why would anyone need the similarity relation at all?

Be honest about which parts of this are settled among people who actually know the area and which are live disputes — don't hand me the counterpart-theory line as if everybody agrees with it. And write it as plain prose, no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, just like an email to a friend.
