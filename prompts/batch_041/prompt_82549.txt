ok so next thursday I have to present at our debate club's open floor night and the crowd is kinda rough so I need to prepare. My thing is about how the student council votes, and I think I found a real problem with majority rule, or at least a small crack in it.

Here's the setup I'm putting on the whiteboard. The council has to decide whether to fund the spring trip. Three members. There are really two questions hiding inside it -- do we have the money, and is the trip worth it -- and the trip only gets funded if both are yes. Member 1 says we have the money but the trip isnt worth it, so no. Member 2 says we don't have the money but it's worth it, so no. Member 3 says yes to both, so yes. Now count: on "do we have the money" it's 2-1 yes. On "is it worth it" it's 2-1 yes. On "fund the trip" it's 2-1 no. Same three people, same votes, and "the majority" says two different things depending on what you count.

My thesis: the council should vote on the parts (money? worth it?) and then just do whatever follows from those, instead of taking a final up-or-down vote. I want to argue that isn't a sneaky trick, it's the only way to avoid landing on a decision that nobody actually supports for reasons they'd accept. My sister does poli sci and says theres a name for this, the discursive dilemma, and that I'm not the first person to notice, which honestly annoyed me.
