I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently taken up philosophy, and I have been trying to understand the debate over aesthetic testimony—roughly, whether one can acquire a justified aesthetic belief on someone else's say-so. Acting as a professional philosopher with expertise in aesthetics and the epistemology of testimony, please draft a satirical FAQ, about 700 words, for a fictional regulatory body called the National Board of Aesthetic Testimony. The board licenses citizens to lend and borrow aesthetic justification. The piece should be deadpan and legible to a reader who knows the pessimist/optimist divide about aesthetic testimony, though it must not quote or cite any actual text or depend on any real names. Before drafting, brainstorm briefly about which positions and tensions you intend to satirize; then write the FAQ in numbered sections with a fictional director's signature. It should address, at least: why a first-person report such as I found it moving might be admissible while a third-person verdict such as it is moving is rejected; how the board adjudicates disagreement between two licensed experts; what happens when a borrower lacks firsthand acquaintance with the work; and one invented bureaucratic category—for example, non-transferable affective warrants or testimonial escrow—that makes a genuine philosophical point. The humor should come from the epistemology, not from arbitrary absurdity; do not mention living people.
