Ok, my opening. I'm taking James's side. Your Peirce tells me truth is what inquiry is destined to arrive at in the end, but nobody is ever going to be at the end, so you've cashed out truth in terms of a fact no conceivable experience could ever confirm -- which is exactly the kind of transcendent thing a pragmatist is supposed to dissolve. James at least gives you something you can look at: does the belief work, does it hold up against the rest of experience and let you go on. And your view has the same disease in miniature, because a claim about what an infinite community of inquirers will end up believing is either an empirical prediction nobody can ever test or a metaphysical posit wearing a lab coat. So which one is it, and if it's the prediction, how is your pragmatism anything more than James's minus the honesty about whats doing the work?
