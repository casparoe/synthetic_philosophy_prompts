ok so next month I'm giving a paper at a kinda weird interdisciplinary workshop and I need you to write me the Q&A prep sheet, because I definately know what's going to happen in that room.

My thesis in one line: almost everything we call creative is a variation on something that already exists. You take a working thing -- a form, a genre, a design, a proof, a recipe, a tune -- and you twiddle one knob: change a single rule, flip a default assumption, nudge one parameter, hold everything else fixed, and see what falls out. The genius-out-of-nowhere story is mostly backwards; the interesting move is always "the same, but different." This is the Hofstadter line (his Scientific American column from the early 80s about variation on a theme being the essence of imagination, later collected in Metamagical Themas), and I'm trying to push it into a sharper, more testable claim than he did. My stock examples are things like a murder mystery where the detective turns out to be the killer, a chess variant that rewrites exactly one rule, a sonnet that keeps everything but inverts the rhyme scheme, a restaurant that keeps the whole menu and swaps a single ingredient. I claim these are the norm, not the exception.

The audience is the whole problem. It's a mixed room. Roughly half are psychologists who study divergent thinking -- Alternative Uses Test, Guilford, Torrance, scoring people on fluency/flexibility/originality, the whole psychometric tradition. Then there are two computational-creativity people who build novelty metrics and figure you can just measure how far a new artifact sits from everything that came before it. And two ML folks who will absolutely bring up scaling and emergent abilities and insist that "it's just recombination of the training data" can't be the whole story because you get outputs that clearly weren't in the data.

Give me the prep sheet. I want the objections I'll actually get from this room, not the generic "but what even is creativity" ones. Order them by how much damage they'd do to me, not by how likely they are. Phrase each one the way that specific camp would phrase it -- the psychometric people, the novelty-metric people, and the scaling people are all going to come at me differently and I need to hear the difference in their own words. For each: a two-minute reply, and a blunt note on where my reply is soft. And tell me the one question I'm secretly hoping nobody asks.

Format: skip the throat-clearing. First sentence should be the single objection most likely to actually sink me and why, then work down from there. No preamble, and don't restate my setup back at me.
