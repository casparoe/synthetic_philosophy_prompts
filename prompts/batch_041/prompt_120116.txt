I hold a view I call clustered realism about historical periods: the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution are real but thin objects, not natural kinds and not mere retrospective inventions, because each tracks a cluster of institutional, economic, and epistemic changes that is robust across independent lines of evidence, while the boundaries between them are conventional. What would count against this? I want the strongest defeaters sorted by how decisive they would be, including cases where the clustering is an artifact of which evidence survives, where the same cluster appears in a period we do not label that way, and where the periodization is better explained by modern disciplinary or political interests. Which of these would a defender have to concede, and which could they dismiss as merely awkward? Finally, does the view risk becoming unfalsifiable, and if so, is that a problem for a view of this kind?
