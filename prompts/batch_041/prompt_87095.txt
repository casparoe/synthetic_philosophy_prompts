I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of mind writing on pain and its affective dimensions. At a joint case conference with the neurology department last week, a clinician summarized a patient with pain asymbolia — a patient who reports pain but shows no withdrawal, grimacing, or concern, and who denies that the sensation bothers him — with the one-liner that pain is not suffering. The clinician then used it to argue that the patient's pain does not require aggressive treatment, since suffering is what matters clinically; a philosopher in the room used the same line to argue that pain cannot be intrinsically bad, since this patient has pain without the suffering that supposedly makes pain bad. I can see that the line is doing different work in the two mouths, but I cannot state the precise claims it could be making. Please lay out, as a menu rather than a defense of any single reading, the distinct theses that the one-liner could express — about the relation between nociception and pain, between the sensory-discriminative and affective-motivational dimensions of pain, between pain's unpleasantness and its undesirability, between occurrent pain and suffering, and about whether the badness of pain is intrinsic, extrinsic, or relational. For each reading, say what it commits one to, whether it is trivial, substantive, or false, and whether it would actually support the clinician's conclusion about treatment or the philosopher's conclusion about intrinsic badness; where two readings are easily conflated, supply a case or question that would distinguish them. I am especially interested in whether pain asymbolia should be read as showing that pain is not essentially aversive, or instead as showing that aversiveness can be dissociated from pain while remaining one of its normal components. The deliverable should be a disambiguation I can circulate before the next case conference, with the readings arranged from weakest to strongest and with the clinical and metaphysical implications marked separately. I am not asking for citations or a reading list; I need the conceptual menu itself.
