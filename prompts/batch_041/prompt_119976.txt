I'm writing a novel about a couple who nearly never met — a train one of them missed, a party the other nearly skipped — and the whole book hangs on whether their kids could have existed if that train had run on time. Which sent me back to Back to the Future, a film I loved as a kid and now realise is far more philosophically confused then people give it credit for.

Read it through the necessity of origin — Kripke's line that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg, so anyone conceived a minute later is somebody else entirely. The photo of Marty and his siblings fading out is the film dramatising exactly that: George and Lorraine don't get together, so those particular children are never conceived. Fine, that's the strict thesis, and it's also oddly gradable — the picture fades rather than blinking out, as if existence came in degrees.

But then the ending hands us the shiny new 1985: same Dave, same Linda, same Marty, just wealthier and happier, with a George who's been a different man since the dance. If his life went that differently, the timing of everything downstream shifts, so by the strict view those aren't the same kids at all. So is the film quietly running on a weaker thesis — that you only need the same two parents, not the same sperm and egg? Or does it just not care, because the reunion only works if we don't ask?

Take both the film and the philosophy seriously: where it dramatises the strict thesis properly, where it slides into parental essentialism or plain incoherence, and what it shows about the counterfactual I'm writing — if my narrator's children in the near-miss world are different people, is the near-miss a loss of *them*, or only of some other children nobody will ever miss?

Then give me the gist as a JSON object, fields as follows: `verdict` (one of strict origin essentialism / parental essentialism / neither / the film is incoherent about it, plus a sentence of justification); `scenes_that_carry_the_thesis` (array of objects with `scene` and `what_it_shows`); `scenes_that_undercut_it` (same shape); `what_the_film_shows_that_the_argument_cant`; `what_it_gets_wrong`; `implication_for_my_novel`.
