write me a parody advice column — a Dear-Abby / Ask-Polly type thing — set inside the longtermism / EA scene. Format: a letter from an invented reader, then the columnist's deadpan reply, maybe a little italicized editor's note at the end. Around 700 words total.

The letter is from a twenty-something guy who runs a small giving circle and has been reading about the simulation argument. He's got himself into a knot: he keeps hearing that the sim argument is supposed to *dampen* fanaticism about the far future — like, if we might be sims then something something and suddenly you're allowed to just pay your rent instead of donating to asteroid-defence research. But he cant make the dampening actually work on his spreadsheet, and now hes paralysed, and his girlfriend wants to know why he spent Sunday night on the forum again. He wants the columnist to settle it: does the sim argument really license him to stop caring so much about the far future, and if so how much exactly, and can he get it in writing.

The columnist's reply has to be deadpan and in-universe, and the funny should come from the arguments being *right*, not from the columnist being a dunce. So the reply should actually engage the machinery: the simulation argument's three-way fork and which horn is doing the work here; the difference between "the future is astronomically large" and "a vanishingly small chance times an astronomically large number" as two separate sources of fanaticism; whether being simulated lowers the expected value of our future actions or instead just relocates the value to the simulators' level; the uncomfortable symmetry where, if simming us damps the far future, it seems to damp ordinary near-term charity too (and whether thats a reductio or a feature); and the point that "we're probably simulated" doesn't by itself tell you the far future is worthless, it tells you you don't know who's counting. The columnist should never announce the moral — no winking, no "of course this is all silly" — and should stay genuinely helpful-sounding, like a real advice columnist who just happens to have a views about decision theory under tiny probabilities.

Keep it legible to someone who's never heard of the simulation argument; don't lean on named papers, and dont put anything in quote marks unless it's the invented reader talking. End with the editor's note implying the columnist is themselves a simulation, but play that completely straight.
