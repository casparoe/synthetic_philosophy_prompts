He says the guy who 'survives' quantum suicide is exactly like one of those planes that came back -- he only ever finds himself in the branches where he's still breathing, so of course from the inside it feels like he can't die. Nothing spooky, no actual immortality, just a selection effect, like how every lottery winner you meet happens to be a winner. He uses this to say the whole idea dissolves: quantum immortality isn't a fact about the world, it's an artifact of only ever being able to look from inside one branch.

I mostly buy that there's something selection-y going on, but it also feels like the analogy is doing alot more work than it should, and I can't put my finger on where it slips. Then the real question: if the analogy only half-works, does his conclusion survive? Is quantum immortality really just survivorship bias, or does the disanalogy leave some genuine claim standing?
