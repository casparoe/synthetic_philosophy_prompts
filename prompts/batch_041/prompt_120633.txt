Ok I need a sanity check on something that's been bugging me for weeks.

I run a tiny meditation app — about 4,000 paying subscribers, me and two part-timers. It's doing fine, and it's doing better than it was because I spent the winter rebuilding the sign-up flow. Here's everything I changed:

- The free trial auto-converts and we don't send a reminder email. We could. We just don't.
- Cancelling means filling out a form and waiting a day or two for a human to get to it.
- The pricing page says 3,847 people upgraded this week. Real number. I picked it because it makes you feel like everyone's ahead of you.
- The streak nudge goes out at 10:30pm, because that's when wound-up people buy things. I checked the data — the ones who convert at that hour are the anxious ones, which is kind of the whole reason they downloaded a meditation app in the first place.
- And the cancel screen basically tells you that cancelling means your peace of mind wasn't worth eight bucks a month.

None of it is a lie. Thats the defense I keep making to myself, and it's also whats bothering me.

My friend is a philosophy grad student and she says I'm manipulating people. She has this distinction where persuasion works through your reasoning and manipulation works around it. But half my list doesn't touch anybody's reasoning at all — it just makes the no button annoying — and the rest of it is just true stuff. Is her distinction even the right one? Which of those five things is actually over the line? She says if I have to ask then I already know, which is not an answer. She also says it depends on the person, so the same website is manipulative for some customers and not others, and she says the real test is whether a customer would still be fine with it if I explained exactly what I do. That last one feels right and also feels like it would make everything marketing does manipulation. Help.
