I'm trying to get my head around a bit in teh Zhuangzi where three friends agree that whichever one dies first, the others will sing about it. When one of them actually dies, the other two sing over the corpse, and Zigong is scandalized. Confucius later tells him these are people who wander outside the frame of things, treating life and death as a single body or as transformation, so ordinary funeral rules don't apply. I get the surface story, but I don't really see what the singing is supposed to do in Zhuangzi's view of death. Is it just a provocative image, or does it express a specific metaphysical claim about death as transformation? How does it connect to the other image where the Great Clod gives us form, life is labor, old age is ease, and death is rest? And is there a tension here: if death is just transformation, why does Zhuangzi elsewhere have characters weep or seem to mourn? I'd like help unpacking the passage, especially the singing and the outside-the-frame idea, without just getting the standard line that Taoists accept death.
