ok so there's a thing I keep bumping into and I want your help pulling out what it assumes.

When a famine happens, the whole international response runs in one register: it gets handled like a natural disaster. Appeals go out the way they do after an earthquake, agencies airlift food in, and the people on the ground very deliberately don't name whoever caused it — because the government that diverted the food or wrecked the farms is also the government that controlls access to the people starving. Neutrality is the price of admission. Then the exact same event gets described in completely different language in other rooms. Historians talk about relief policy in Ireland, about collectivization in China. Sen's whole thing about Bengal was that the food supply wasn't really the story, the story was who could actually get at food. And international criminal law now treats deliberately starving civilians in wartime as a war crime. There's a further wrinkle too: the word "famine" only gets applied once the mortality numbers have already crossed a threshold, so it's a label you earn after the fact, and in practice getting it applied tends to depend on the cooperation of the government in question — which is a bit rich when that government is part of why it's happening.

So what does the practice actually presuppose? Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no conclusion:

1. what view of the line between natural and political causation the whole arrangement commits you to;
2. which rival views it genuinely rules out, as opposed to what it merely happens to fit comfortably with — i.e. where is it underdetermined?
3. whether the neutrality norm and the war-crimes norm can be held at the same time by the same person without incoherence, or whether that's just compartmentalization;
4. what it presupposes about the relationship between explaining a famine and blaming someone for it;
5. what would have to change if we dropped the presupposition and treated every famine as a political event from day one.
