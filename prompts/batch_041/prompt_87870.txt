I've been working through a methodological puzzle and keep arriving at a conclusion that any working scientist would presumably reject, so I want you to take the argument seriously and tell me what to do with it.

Suppose I hold a theory T about some phenomenon — say, that a certain regularity is produced by a single continuous medium with a particular dynamics. I notice that I can always generate a rival T* by a purely formal operation: I take T and add one further posit that is stipulated to have no observable consequences whatsoever, such as a second medium defined to move in exact lockstep with the first, or a field defined to be screened at every scale any instrument could probe. T and T* entail exactly the same predictions for every possible observation, so no experiment, however precise, could ever separate them. Nothing in this operation was special to T; I could perform it on any theory at all, and I could perform it repeatedly, generating infinitely many rivals, each with its own stipulated-undetectable addition.

This looks to me like a clean case of the underdetermination of theory by evidence, and I think it establishes something quite strong. Since the evidence cannot distinguish T from T* or from any of the infinitely many rivals I can construct, the evidence gives me no reason to prefer T; and if my credence in T must be spread over all the rivals that fit my data equally well, then, there being infinitely many of them, T ends up with a credence of essentially zero. The same reasoning applies to every theory I hold, so theory choice comes down to convention or taste, and the only intellectually honest attitude is to suspend judgment between any pair of empirically equivalent theories — or perhaps to believe only their disjunction.

What I want to know is how to put this into practice. If I can never have more reason for T than for T*, should I stop asserting that T is probably true and report only the disjunction of everything consistent with my data? And when my colleagues treat one theory as established while ignoring the rival I can write down on demand, are they simply being irrational? I'd like a concrete answer: which of the two theories should I report as my belief, and how should I divide my credence among the rivals?
