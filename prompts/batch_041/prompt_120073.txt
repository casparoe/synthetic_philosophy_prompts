Since retiring from a career in atmospheric chemistry I have been sitting in on a philosophy department's reading group, and for two years I have watched two of its members talk past one another on the question of what an individual owes in the face of a harm that no individual can detectably cause. One of them — call her the marginalist — holds that an act can be wrong only in virtue of what it does to someone, so that a single flight, a single beef meal, or a single ballot cast in a safe seat is either harmless and therefore permissible, or wrong only insofar as it makes some small difference to the occurrence or the probability of a harm; on her view the serious obligations lie with collective action and policy, and the individual's own consumption is a distraction from them. The other — call him the participant — holds that one can be implicated in a harm simply by taking part in the practice that produces it, quite apart from any difference one's particular act makes; on his view the marginalist's demand for a detectable difference is precisely what lets everyone off the hook in the cases that matter most. I would like you to write the document these two could sign together after a genuine adversarial collaboration: first, the claims each can affirm without reservation — about what the science does and does not establish, about what a difference is, about which moral predicates are actually in play (wrong, blameworthy, a reason against, complicit), and about what would count as progress; second, the points on which they still differ, each written in wording its own side would accept as fair to it; third, the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them — and for each crux the case, argument, or result that both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction for it stated in advance. If you judge that part of their quarrel is merely verbal, say which part and why.

I have found it useful to sort the cases by how the harm responds to the number of contributors, and I would like the document to do the same, because I suspect the disagreement shifts with the regime. In an elastic harm, the damage is roughly proportional to the total contribution, so a small act makes a small difference. In a threshold harm, nothing happens until some critical mass is exceeded, after which the harm is large, and no one knows in advance where the threshold sits, so each act carries a small chance of being the decisive one. In a compensated harm, some feedback mechanism offsets any shortfall caused by one party — the total is fixed by a quota, a cartel, or a budget, so that one party's reduction is simply taken up by another, and the aggregate is provably invariant to what any single agent does, though not to what all of them do at once. I would like each side's verdict recorded in all three regimes, and I want to know whether the marginalist's position collapses in the compensated case or survives it, and whether the participant's survives the elastic case, where the difference is real but negligible. Two further distinctions of mine may or may not be useful to you, and I would rather you tell me than assume: between an act's being part of the causal ancestry of a harm and its being part of the mechanism that produces it, and between the wrongness of an act considered as a token and the wrongness of the type of act it instantiates. Please also say whether one joint statement can cover voting, where the chance of being decisive is minute but so is the cost, and eating meat, where the causal chain runs through prices and production decisions rather than through anything the individual can see — or whether one of these needs separate treatment, and if so, on what ground.

Format it as a numbered list of agreed claims, then a table of disagreements with a crux column and a prediction column. Keep the agreed claims substantive: I would rather see four that cost both sides something than twelve that cost neither anything. My own background makes me impatient with hand-waving about magnitudes — in my field a perturbation is a perturbation, however small — so where the dispute turns on how small is too small to matter, I would like the document to say so explicitly, to name the quantity that would have to be estimated for the question to be settled, and to say who bears the burden if it cannot be estimated. I confess I came into this thinking the whole dispute was a confusion on someone's part, and I no longer think that.
