A colleague and I have spent the better part of a year re-running the same argument about the liar paradox, and it has stopped producing anything. He is a dialetheist: he takes the liar reasoning at face value and concludes that the liar sentence is both true and false, and he treats explosion — the inference from a contradiction to anything whatever — as an optional rule that a paraconsistent logic is free to refuse. I am not a dialetheist. When I press him I end up saying that once he refuses explosion he is no longer talking about negation; he replies that I am assuming explosion in order to prove explosion. Each of us thinks the other is assuming what he wants to prove.

What kind of disagreement is this, really? I want a diagnosis, not a verdict, and not a survey of positions. Is it a substantive disagreement about which logic is correct, a verbal dispute about the word "not," or two people answering different questions under one heading — he about which formal systems are worth studying and what follows from what within them, I about the meaning of a natural-language operator? Do the two sides even share a notion of logical consequence: a model-theoretic one in which a counterexample settles a dispute, versus a normative or proof-theoretic one in which a rule stands or falls with its role in a practice? Which parts of the impasse are formal questions that a proof could settle, and which are questions about what counts as data for a theory of meaning — the paradoxes on one side, the ordinary inferential practice of mathematicians and speakers on the other? My own hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis, is that the hinge is what a logical theory is answerable to: whether it must preserve the meaning-constitutive role of the connectives as they function in our practice, or whether it may be judged by how well it handles a small set of hard cases. If that is right, say so and say why; if not, say what the hinge actually is. And tell me whether the exclusion problem — the thought that a dialetheist who accepts both a sentence and its negation cannot reject or disagree with anything, since denial would take the form of asserting the negation — is a genuine difficulty for his position or an artifact of classical metatheory, and then what, if anything, would count as progress here: a derivation, a distinction, a change of question, or nothing at all.
