I chair the quality-of-care committee at a psychiatric hospital, and we're finalizing the report on a death last spring. A patient on a high-risk observation protocol was supposed to be checked every 15 minutes; the night nurse skipped two checks in a row, and when she finally went in the patient had already died of a pulmonary embolism. The pathologist says it probably wasn't survivable by the time anyone could have reached her, but the timeline's fuzzy enough that a faster response might have changed things. Our report template forces us to record either that the missed checks *caused* the death or that they were merely *a contributing factor*, and those two findings carry very different consequences for the nurse.

I keep going in circles. On a but-for test the missed checks are a cause — but then so is the fact that no one else looked in, and the fact that the protocol only required q15 and not continuous observation, and we can't list the whole world as a cause. But if omissions can't be causes at all, we can never hold anyone responsible for failing to do something, which seems wrong. And nobody on the committee even agrees on what the alternative course of events is supposed to be that we're comparing against.

Draft the committee's finding memo. Pick one finding, give the reasoning, and deal honestly with the reading I'm most tempted by on the other side. The nurse's union rep will read this, so it has to be defensible.
