Our city's overdose-prevention board votes next month on a new proposal, and I'm trying to predict our chair's vote. Her record: she voted to fund a supervised injection site for heroin even when the province's legal exemption looked shaky; she voted against funding a supervised smoking room for crack, citing thinner evidence, low overdose lethality, and complaints from the surrounding block; she endorsed decriminalizing possession of all drugs up to a three-day supply while opposing any move to legal retail sale; she voted against banning an unregulated herbal opioid agonist of the kratom type, despite pressure from the state, on the grounds that its overdose lethality is low; she voted to emergency-schedule a synthetic cannabinoid after a poisoning cluster even though she conceded the ban would push users to the black market; and twice she has voted against measures she said she personally favored because she judged them likely to cost the program its operating license.

The new proposal is a supervised consumption site for smoked methamphetamine in the district with our highest stimulant mortality, bundled with voucher-based contingency management for stimulant use disorder. There is no reversal agent for methamphetamine, the deaths are cardiac and supply-driven rather than poisoning-driven, and the evidence for supervised smoking of stimulants is much weaker than for supervised injection, though contingency management has a strong trial record. The council is split, and a hostile member has already promised to attack the board's exemption if we approve.

Predict her vote and defend the prediction. Identify the rule she seems to be running and the rival rules that fit her record just as well; say what each rival predicts about this proposal; name the earlier decision that is the closest analog; and name the further case that would best discriminate between the rivals. If her record genuinely underdetermines the outcome, say what principle the board would be adopting with each choice.
