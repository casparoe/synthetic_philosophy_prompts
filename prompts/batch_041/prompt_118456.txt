I'm writing something about why norms and conventions stick around long after anyone remembers what they were for -- and why people keep following ones they'd all privately be happy to drop. Before I read any more of the literature I want the spec first: what does a theory of convention actually have to deliver? Give me the requirements list, with the case behind each one.

The cases I care about, roughly: driving on one side of the road; money; the meanings of words; who queues where; which fork you use; units of measurement. Notice that nobody agreed to any of these, nobody decreed most of them, and yet they arent optional in practice. You can't unilaterally switch, but a whole country can, painfully, in a day. Some conventions look one-sided or exploitative -- the norms about who gets to interrupt, or of deference to certain people -- where it's not obvious that everyone gains. And then the odd one: two AI agents trained to coordinate invent a signalling protocol neither was programmed with and no human can read. Is that a convention? Does the theory have to say yes, and what would it cost the theory if it said no?

The constraints I'd want on the list: it can't assume agreement, decree, or anything written down; it can't collapse convention into custom, fashion, or mere statistical regularity; it has to explain how conventions start, die, and flip; it has to say whether conforming gives you a reason, and where that reason comes from; and it has to leave room for criticizing a convention from outside, since we do that constantly and don't think we're just being irrational.

The part I most want is where you tell me which of these demands can't all be met at once. My suspicion: 'arbitrary' and 'binding' pull against each other -- if the left side of the road is as good as the right, why does it matter which one I'm on? -- and so do 'nobody agreed' and 'it obliges you'. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly; that's the result, not a failure. And tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidates.

Then, briefly, how the standard pictures do against the list: the coordination-plus-mutual-expectation one, the game-theoretic/evolutionary one where conventions just emerge from repeated play, and the one that treats conventions as norms with force of their own. No scorecard, no citations -- I'm not going to look anything up, I just want the criteria.
