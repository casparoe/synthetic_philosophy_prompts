I'm putting together a case for a seminar on moral dilemmas and I need an example that actually does some work. Not a trolley, not a ticking bomb, not doctor-with-five-patients. Something an ordinary person could plausibly land in.

What I want: a scenario where someone is genuinely required to do A and genuinely required to do B, can't do both, and there's no clean way out. And I want it built so it survives the four cheap moves people use to dissolve these cases:

- "they can't really be obliged to do both, since ought implies can" — so make sure neither obligation evaporates just because you notice the conflict
- "there's obviously a priority rule" — so make the two demands come from different sources (one from something they chose, one from something they didn't), and make the case symmetric: swap the two parties' roles and the description reads exactly the same, so nothing in the case can privilege one side
- "they should've seen it coming / they set it up themselves" — so no earlier moment where they could have dodged it, and no prior fault that created it
- "it's just uncertainty about which one is really required" — so it should be clear both demands are real and the problem isn't missing information

Also: no partial compliance, no delegating, no stalling for time, no clever third option that half-satisfies both. And dont make the two options come out equal in value — equal outcomes just gives you "either is fine", which isn't a dilemma. I want them incommensurable, not tied.

Then do the work:

1. Name one theory on which this is a genuine dilemma (both are required, so whichever way they go they've done something wrong) and one on which it isn't (there's a determinate right answer). Walk through what each says and why the second one gets to say it.
2. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work. If I delete that feature, does it collapse into an ordinary resolvable conflict? Say what it collapses into.
3. Play defense for the other side: what's the most plausible move a defender of the second theory would make to argue it was never a dilemma after all?

Keep the scenario to one paragraph, keep it realistic, and dont bother with citations or reading lists — just the reasoning.
