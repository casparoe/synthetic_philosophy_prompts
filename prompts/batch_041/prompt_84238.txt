Had a two-hour row with a mate last night about whether a volcano can 'cause' a revolution, and I've come out of it with a pattern I can't put a name to. It runs like this. He says the Tambora eruption gave us the year without a summer, which is why we got Frankenstein — the volcano doing the work of an agent, deciding things. I push back, and he backs off: obviously I'm not a geographical determinist, the climate was one factor among many, human choices mattered. Fine. Ten minutes later the volcano is deciding things again, and when I ask what would've happened without it he says counterfactuals aren't the kind of question historians can answer. So the non-human thing gets full agency in the telling and takes on no causal commitments the moment anyone asks. Plagues ending feudalism, a drought toppling a kingdom, germs doing the work of empire — same two-step every time, and the case where the volcano changed nothing never comes up at all.

Whats the name for this, if there is one? I dont think 'environmental determinism' is it — he'd be insulted by the label and he isn't claiming the outcome was inevitable. But I don't just want a label; I want the thing carved up: what exactly this move is, what the nearest established concept is and how it differs from it, and where the edges are. If nothing established fits, coin something and give me its boundary cases. Also keep what's genuinely settled in the literature apart from what's contested — don't sell me a live dispute as the consensus. British English, ta.
