Someone in a discussion about whether frontier AI labs could agree to slow down ended my proposal with the remark that you can't beat Moloch, because it's a multipolar trap. I suspect that line is doing more rhetorical work than its content can bear, so I want a precise accounting of it. Set out the distinct theses the name could be expressing — from the near-trivial (there are situations in which individually rational choices yield a collectively worse outcome) through the structural (competition systematically selects for the agents and behaviors that make things worse) to the fatalist (any unilateral attempt to exit harms the exiter without improving the outcome) — and for each say whether it is true, contestable, or false, and what would be needed to establish it. Say also whether a race to the bottom is the same phenomenon as a multipolar trap or a different one, since the first looks like a bad equilibrium that agents choose under fixed payoffs while the second looks like a process that changes which agents and payoffs there are. Finally, tell me which reading the remark would need in order to actually defeat my proposal, and whether that reading is one anyone should accept.
