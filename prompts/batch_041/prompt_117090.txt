I am a backend engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have been trying to follow the current technical debate over how to deploy frontier models safely. I keep losing track of which disagreements in that debate are substantive and which are terminological, so I would like a decision tree I can work through and then hand to a small study group of engineers. The tree should take a reader from a few bare commitments about what a safety measure is *for* and deliver them to a named position in the dispute between control and alignment. Treat the following working distinction as the fixed vocabulary of the exercise, and do not treat it as a claim about anyone's actual usage: control measures are safeguards that are supposed to hold even if the model is misaligned and actively trying to get around them — a weaker trusted model watching the outputs, tripwires that shut the system down, restricted affordances such as no network access and no ability to copy itself, sandboxing, human review of a sample of actions; alignment measures are those that aim to make the model actually want what we want, so that no adversarial assumption is needed in the first place.

Please build the tree to the following specification.

1. Every internal node must be a question a reader can answer from their own engineering judgement about a concrete case, not a question that can only be answered by naming a side. Draw the cases from scenarios such as: a model that passes every evaluation you can run but was trained with a concealed trigger; a monitor that is itself a model, and so itself a candidate for misalignment; a system whose only affordance is emitting text to a human; a deployment where the model's misalignment would only become detectable after the harm is done; a safeguard that functions only so long as the model does not know it is there.
2. Every leaf must carry the name of a position, or be explicitly marked as unnamed if no one holds it, together with one sentence identifying the commitment that position is least comfortable defending.
3. The branch structure should turn on distinctions such as: whether the safety argument must hold under worst-case model behaviour or only on average; whether it may rely on the model being ignorant of the safeguards; whether the monitor is trusted by assumption or trusted by argument; whether a bounded capability envelope is what is really doing the work; and whether the model's dispositions matter at all, or only its observable outputs.
4. Include at least one combination of answers that no one occupies, and say briefly whether it is empty because the position is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or merely because it has not been explored. Where two leaves cannot be separated by any question in the tree, flag them rather than papering over the gap.
5. Aim for roughly six to eight branch nodes and seven to ten leaves, no more than two sentences per node, and a format that reads as a handout rather than an essay.
