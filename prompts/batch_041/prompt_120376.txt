OK, I'm a science journalist — features and profiles, mostly the ideas beat — and once every couple of years I get pulled back into the Pluto wars: the 2006 IAU vote in Prague, the new dwarf-planet category, Eris, New Horizons, the planetary scientists who still refuse to say Pluto isn't a planet. My editor wants the anniversary piece and I don't want to write the usual is-it-or-isn't-it thing; its been done to death. I have a half idea I'd like you to turn into an actual analysis.

The idea: treat the whole affair as a test case for the natural kinds debate — the question of whether the sciences carve nature at its joints or whether we do. I want it argued in both directions.

Theory onto the case. Take the main positions people hold about natural kinds — that they're defined by an essence; that they're clusters of properties held together by some underlying mechanism; that kindhood is interest-relative and plural; that natural kind is wishful thinking and the whole notion should be dropped — and say what each one predicts about the Pluto fight. The thing I keep circling is that everyone in this fight agrees on every fact. Same orbit, same composition, same history, same neighborhood. The only disagreement is about the category, and one side settled it with a vote. So: is the dynamical criterion — whether a body dominates its orbital zone — doing something different in kind from the intrinsic ones, and does that matter for whether planet could be a natural kind at all? Can a room of astronomers stipulate a kind into existence? When the other side says the IAU got it wrong, wrong in what sense — is there a fact of the matter they're right about?

Case onto the theory. Does the episode actually support any of those positions, or does it quietly embarass all of them? I have a hunch that planet is closer to weed than to gold — a category whose boundaries track what we're doing — and that if that's right it's bad news for anyone who wanted planet to be a natural kind. Tell me if the hunch is lazy, and if not, which of the positions survives it.

Finish with which position comes out of the case looking best, and where the theory just runs out and I'm back to reporting on what a committee did in Prague. Don't bother with citations or a reading list, I'll fill those in.
