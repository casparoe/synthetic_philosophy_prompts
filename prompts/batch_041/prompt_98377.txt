OK so I'm presenting at our cognitive science center's reading group next week and I want to be ready for the Q&A. Twelve-ish people: mostly computational functionalists who think the substrate is irrelevant to the mind, a couple who work on personal identity and are firmly in the "identity isn't what matters anyway" camp, one developmental neuroscientist who thinks functionalism is fine on paper and useless in practice, and one person from the religion department who knows alot of Buddhist no-self philosophy. Not a job talk, low stakes, but I'd rather not get eaten alive.

The thesis I'm defending: gradual neuron-by-neuron replacement — you swap one biological neuron for a functionally identical silicon one, let things settle, swap the next, and so on until the whole brain is silicon — is NOT survival. The original dies somewhere in there, and what walks out the far end is a successor: a person who believes they're me, whom everyone will keep treating as me, but who isn't me surviving. And I'm arguing this is not just biological chauvinism. I think the functionalist's own commitments force the conclusion, once you seperate preserving a function from preserving the process that bears it.

Here's the wrinkle I actually want to spend my time on, because I think it's what makes the case bite. Everyone's first move is some version of "well obviously you survive, there's no moment where you're unconscious, the stream never breaks." So imagine a crossfade version: instead of flipping neurons one at a time, each biological neuron's contribution ramps smoothly down over hours while its silicon twin ramps smoothly up, so no neuron is ever off, and the whole system never stops computing for an instant. Seamless by construction. I claim this changes nothing — that the "no gap, so it's still you" reply does all its work with a picture of consciousness as a thing that can be interrupted, and once you drop that picture the crossfade is just a slower copy.

What I want from you: the objections I'm actually going to get from THIS room, not the generic ones. What should I concede up front to the functionalists so I don't lose them in the first five minutes?
