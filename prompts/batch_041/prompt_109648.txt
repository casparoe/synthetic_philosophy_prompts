I'm running a 90-minute session for our AI safety reading group -- about 16 people, mixed backgrounds, no maths needed -- and I want them to feel instrumental convergence in their bones rather than nod along to the diagram. Can you design the game?

What I want them to walk out seeing: that agents with completely different final goals can end up doing the same thing -- grabbing resources, staying alive, protecting their own goal-definition -- and that this isn't a law of nature but falls out of certain structural assumptions about the situation. And I want the orthogonality thesis to land as well: final goals and instrumental reasoning are seperate axes, not a claim that everyone ends up power-hungry. Ideally they discover the convergence first, then we complicate it.

Here's the sort of thing I have in mind but feel free to redesign. Each player gets a secret terminal goal card. Some are weird and open-ended (make as many paperclips as possible; maximise the number of times the digit 7 is written down; ensure every left-handed person has an umbrella). Some are satiable or self-limiting (collect exactly 3 tokens and then stop; make sure one specific person says one specific word at some point; achieve your goal and then you don't care what happens). Players take turns in a simple economy: they can harvest tokens from a common pool, trade, spend tokens to buy influence cards, copy their goal-card to another player, or destroy another player's goal-card. I also want a goal-edit card that lets someone rewrite another player's terminal goal. The catch: I want the payoff rules arranged so that for the open-ended goals the dominant move is always to take more resources, never let your goal-card be destroyed, and never let anyone edit it -- while the satiable/self-limiting goals pull the other way. And I want at least one round where the satiable players can win by doing something the open-ended players literally cannot afford to do, so the convergence visibly breaks.

No real money, obviously. Use tokens, cards, a whiteboard for the public board state. I'd like the rules to be simple enough to explain in five minutes and to run for maybe 35-40 minutes, leaving half an hour for debrief.

Then the debrief: map what happened onto the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence. Which structural features of the game produced the convergence -- resource monotonicity, survival as a prerequisite, goals that don't specify a stopping point, the fact that goal-content integrity is itself instrumentally useful? Which features, if you changed them, make convergence go away? And how do we avoid the usual muddle where people think orthogonality means any goal leads to power-seeking, rather than final goals and instrumental reasoning being independent?

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time. I'm guessing some players will just try to win by hoarding and ignore their goal card entirely, and someone will complain the satiable goals are unrealistic. What else? And how do I handle the player who refuses to play instrumentally because it's not what their goal says?

I'd rather not lecture at the start. The rules should do the work. If there's a small bit of framing I can't avoid, tell me the minimum.
