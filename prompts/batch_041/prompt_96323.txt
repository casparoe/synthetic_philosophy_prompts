I am a third-year PhD student in the history and philosophy of science, finishing a chapter on how to appraise theories whose central predictions are indexed to an open-ended horizon — theories that no present evidence can falsify, only a future that keeps receding. I want a credence I can actually defend in the chapter, not a literature survey: no reading lists, no citations, no appeals to what some authority is supposed to have settled. Give me the reasoning with its weak joints marked, and do not answer that it depends on your philosophy of science; commit to a view and then tell me what would dislodge it.

The case is Malthusian. Here is the proposition, stated so that it could be false:

P: The Malthusian mechanism — that where a population's capacity to reproduce outruns the growth of its subsistence, the surplus is eventually absorbed by endogenous population growth, so that gains in per-capita living standards are not sustained — is a correct, law-like claim about human populations generally, and the failure of Malthusian predictions to be borne out in the industrialised world reflects the operation of a preventive check that Malthus himself recognised, rather than falsity in the mechanism.

Give me your credence in P, then decompose it into the sub-claims you think it actually factors into. I would use these, but restructure them if you think mine are the wrong joints:

1. the mechanism holds of the pre-industrial record and of contemporary low-income economies;
2. the mechanism survives the admission of the preventive check — that is, that counting falling fertility as a Malthusian check does not empty the theory, given that the historical fertility decline coincided with rising rather than falling living standards;
3. the industrialised escape is properly described as a check operating rather than as an exogenous technological break that the theory never anticipated;
4. the theory had determinate enough content for its prediction to be late rather than wrong — i.e. its horizon was bounded somewhere;
5. the verdict tracks the world rather than the appraisal norms we happen to use.

Give a number and one line of justification for each, say how they combine, and give the product.

Then classify the rescue moves the theory has undergone — narrowing scope to closed or pre-industrial economies, widening the dependent variable from mortality to any welfare-reducing adjustment, relocating the mechanism from the national to the global scale, deferring the predicted event past any testable horizon — and say how much of your credence rests on each being a legitimate refinement rather than an immunising stratagem. Add any route I have missed.

Finally: I put P at about 0.35 and my supervisor puts it at about 0.7. Tell me which sub-claim that disagreement most likely lives in, which sub-claim your own number is most sensitive to, which term you are least confident in, and what evidence would move it most.

End with a single final line in the form \answer{0.xx}, containing your credence in P.
