My friend Mara is doing a PhD on free will and she's gone full zealot for Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — the regress one, where you do what you do because of how you are, and you can't be ultimately responsible for how you are because that would mean you made yourself, and to make yourself you'd have to already exist and already be some particular way, so it runs back forever and nobody is ever *ultimately* responsible for anything. She still talks about "lite" responsibility for practical stuff, blame, punishment, all that, she just thinks the deep kind is impossible. I've been keeping a mental list of her verdicts and I want to know what she'll say about one more case.

Her track record so far. A guy whose desire to rob a bank was implanted by a neuroscientist — not responsible. A man raised from age four in a cult that taught him that dying as a bomber earns paradise — not responsible. A woman who's been an alcoholic since her teens and steals to fund it — not responsible. And then a completely ordinary man, decent upbringing, no trauma, no manipulation, no addiction, who one night beats a stranger half to death because he's drunk and in a foul mood — she also says not responsible, though she'll add that he's responsible in the everyday sense, that we can lock him up and demand an apology and it's all perfectly fine and useful.

Now the new case, my aunt Rosalind. By her own account and everyone else's she was a genuinely nasty person in her twenties — cruel, selfish, no excuses, ordinary family, nothing done to her. At around thirty she decided she hated what she was and over the next fifteen years she deliberately and effortfully rebuilt her own character: therapy, meditation, practising kindness on purpose until it actually stuck. And it worked; people who've known her the whole time say she really did change. Then last year, at sixty, under a lot of stress, she did something callous to her sister — used a family secret to wound her during an argument. No coercion, no manipulation, no addiction, nothing external. So what does Mara say about Rosalind?

I don't just want a yes or no. I want the rule you think she's actually running — whether that's the full Basic Argument or some narrower thing that fits all four of her "no"s just as well — what each of those candidate rules predicts about Rosalind, and which one you'd bet on and why. Then tell me what case would actually seperate them: what should I ask her about next that would come out one way under the Basic Argument and the other way under the rival rule. And be honest about how confident you are and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong — like, which detail of how I described Rosalind, if I'd gotten it wrong, would flip your answer.
