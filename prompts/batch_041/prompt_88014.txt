You are a philosopher of physics with real working knowledge of statistical mechanics, and you are impatient with hand-waving about information being physical. Your task is to write a short dialogue — about two pages — between two characters who are both fully competent and neither of whom is a strawman.

The two are trying to settle whether Maxwell's demon can be exorcised by information accounting, and if so, where exactly the bill is paid. Assume both accept the second law and both know the textbook move: the demon must eventually erase its memory, and erasing one bit at temperature T costs at least kT ln 2, so no violation. One character treats that as the whole story. The other thinks it mislocates the cost: on her view the price is paid in establishing the correlation between demon and gas — in the measurement, or in whatever process set the demon up — and erasure is merely one place the same bill can surface, not the reason the law holds.

To keep them honest, put a concrete demon in front of them. It sorts a dilute gas by opening one of two doors for each molecule according to a mechanical register of N bits. The register's reset is engineered to be reversible and frictionless: no rubbing parts, no heat dumped in the reset. The demon has no other internal dissipation. Character A claims the reversible reset is an illusion — to reset reversibly you must have parked the information somewhere, so nothing is destroyed and the bill is merely deferred. Character B claims that if A is right, the real question is who paid for the initial correlation, and that a demon whose register starts uncorrelated but whose door schedule is fixed in advance is a different and harder case.

Have them press each other on three points: whether the demon's information is a physical resource or a bookkeeping fiction; what reversible erasure can even mean if the correlation has to survive somewhere; and whether the second law is here being explained or quietly presupposed. Let them concede where the other has a point, and do not force a tidy resolution if the crux remains.

Begin by brainstorming the crux for a few sentences, then write the dialogue. Do not cite literature or name authors; argue the physics and the philosophy from scratch.
