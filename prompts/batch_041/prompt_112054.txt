You are a philosopher of mind with a historian's familiarity with the early literature on machine intelligence and a teacher's interest in what education actually is. I am assembling a seminar on the idea, commonly credited to Turing, that one might build a child machine and educate it rather than program it, and I want the specification before I look at any particular reconstruction of the idea. So think carefully first about what that proposal is even trying to describe, and then write me the desiderata that any adequate account of a machine that learns — or, more ambitiously, of a machine that is educated — has to meet. Fix the purpose as follows: the account should be good enough to serve as a framework for thinking about present-day systems that acquire their competence from data, without pretending that training and education are the same thing.

Give me the requirements with the consideration behind each: the phenomena it must explain; the cases it must get right, including the case of a pupil who is never explicitly told what the target is and the case of learning that occurs with no teacher at all; the distinctions it cannot collapse, such as training versus education, rote versus understanding, compliance versus initiative, and a mistake that teaches versus mere noise; and the results it cannot contradict, among them the general lesson that any learning presupposes some prior bias or inductive commitment. Include the requirement that the account say what is actually transmitted when someone is educated — not facts, but habits of thought, or a capacity for judgment — and the requirement that it explain how a teacher can aim at a determinate competence while being unable to specify it in full. Then tell me which requirements are negotiable and which are not, flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied (I expect the pair about the teacher's inarticulacy and the pupil's determinacy to be the interesting one), and say which single requirement does the most to rule candidates out. End by saying briefly whether the child-machine picture survives the list.

Write this as continuous prose, in the manner of a long email to a colleague: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables.
