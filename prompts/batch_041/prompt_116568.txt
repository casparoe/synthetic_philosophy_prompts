My strata council has spent three meetings arguing about whether a seated e-scooter counts as a vehicle under our building bylaws. The bylaws ban vehicles from the lobby and then list examples: bicycles, skateboards, e-scooters, mobility scooters. My neighbor uses a seated e-scooter because of a back injury; the building manager says it's obviously a vehicle, my neighbor says it's a mobility aid. The bylaw also says no motorized vehicles, and her scooter has a throttle but she mostly uses pedal assist. I got weirdly obsessed with this and now I can't stop thinking about the sorites problem. I'm pretty sure I'm an epistemicist: there's a fact of the matter about whether it's a vehicle, we just can't know where the line is. My cousin, who does philosophy, is a supervaluationist: she says there's no fact of the matter, just many admissible precisifications, and the bylaw is indeterminate for borderline cases. We keep talking past each other and I've definately lost the last two arguements.

Write the joint statement that the two of us could actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section: what we both accept about vagueness, about the bylaw, about what needs explaining, and about what the other side gets right. Include what we both say about higher-order vagueness (the vagueness of where the borderline cases start) and about whether the dispute is partly verbal.

2. A disagreements table: the precise points where we still differ, each phrased in the wording that side would accept for its own view. Separate genuine disagreements from ones that were about words, and if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument we both agree would bear on it, write down each side's prediction about it in advance, and say what each side would count as evidence or argument against its own view.

Use our e-scooter bylaw as the running example, not the bald man. Don't give me a reading list, citations, or bibliography. I don't need the history of the debate, just the document. And please don't make either side a strawman: my cousin's view has to be as strong as mine, and if there's a version of epistemicism or supervaluationism that handles the bylaw better, say so.
