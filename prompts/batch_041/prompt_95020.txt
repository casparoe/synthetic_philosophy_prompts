My home town's chamber of commerce has asked me to give the after-dinner speech at their annual dinner -- I'm the only person in the room who's ever taken an economics class, so apparently that makes me the expert. The problem is the whole town has worked itself into a panic about the trade defecit. A plant moved overseas two years ago, alot of the small manufacturers are hurting, and the mood in that hall is basically: tariffs, protect our industries, keep our money at home. I don't want to stand up and tell them they're all idiots, because a lot of them really did lose work. But I also don't want to just nod along and validate the panic. What I'd love to do is give them Hume -- the real Hume of the money-and-trade essays -- served up in a way that actually works over dinner.

So write me the speech. Eight to ten minutes, warm, a little funny, nothing that sounds like a lecture. It has to do real work, not decorate: get them to see why Hume thought a country that hoards its money has misunderstood what money even is, why he thought money flows around and finds its level like water, and why he thought a rich trading partner is good news rather than a threat. Also, two econ professors from the local college will be at the head table and they'll notice if I get Hume wrong, so keep me honest on the substance. And one more thing -- don't let me pretend his whole gold-and-silver flow story just carries over unchanged into a world of paper money. I know that's a genuine stretch and I don't want to be the guy who waves it away. Handle that honestly, without losing the room.
