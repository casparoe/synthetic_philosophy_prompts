My day job is writing novels — four of them, none about schools — but a couple years ago I got appointed to our town's school board, and somehow I ended up chairing the "choice and authorization" subcommittee. We meet Thursday. I'm the one who has to circulate the written opinion the night before, and I'm stuck, so I need you to draft it.

Two items are on the agenda and I'm pretty sure they have to be decided together, which is part of what's tying me in knots.

Item one: an application from Northside Classical Academy to open a K-8 charter, 450 seats, phasing in over four years starting with K-3. The design has some things in it I've never seen on our docket. Their lottery gives a 20% preference to "founding families" (people who signed on during the two-year planning period), a 1.5x weight to applicants from our three lowest-income census tracts, sibling preference, and a one-mile geographic preference. Every family has to sign a covenant: 30 volunteer hours a year, a commitment to read aloud at home nightly, and agreement that a family that misses the covenant two years running goes on a "support plan" that can end in non-renewal of enrollment. There's no opt-out from the required Latin sequence or the "moral formation" seminar. And 11% of per-pupil funding goes to a for-profit management company under a five-year contract that auto-renews.

Item two: a proposed rule letting homeschooled and private-school kids take up to two classes a semester in district schools and try out for our teams.

Background you'll need. District enrollment is down 9% over five years, three elementaries are at about 60% capacity, births keep falling, and the per-pupil money follows the kid. Our staff projects the charter alone costs the district around $1.4M a year at full scale, and the part-time enrollment rule would add to that, though nobody can tell me by how much. Our state statute says we must weigh "negative fiscal impact on the district" and whether a school will "serve a student population that reflects the community," but it doesn't say which one wins when they point in opposite directions. Two years ago this board denied a similar-sized application from a dual-language school run by Latino parents, on fiscal-impact grounds. The Northside applicants pointed that out at the hearing, at length, and they're right that it looks bad.

The thing that keeps me up: parents get to exit, kids don't get a vote, and if schooling is partly a race for position, then one kid's win is another kid's loss, and I have no idea how a public body is supposed to price that. Also, half our families can't front 30 volunteer hours, and I don't think that's a small detail.

So: draft me the opinion, about 1,000 words, in American English, memo format. I need a clear bottom line for each item — approve, deny, or approve with conditions — and if it's conditions, spell out exactly what they are and by when the school has to meet them. Give the strongest argument on the losing side a fair run before you knock it down; two of my colleagues have made up their minds already and will be reading this looking for a fight. Tie it to the record instead of just gesturing at principles, say how the two items should be handled together, and skip the footnotes.
