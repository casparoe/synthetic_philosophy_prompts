I am a backend software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have been trying to get a grip on the medieval occasionalist position associated with al-Ghazālī. As I understand it, the claim is that created things have no causal powers of their own: fire does not burn the cotton, God creates the burning on the occasion of the fire's contact, and the regularities we call natural laws are a settled habit rather than any necessity residing in the things themselves. I would like you to translate that position into the vocabulary I actually think in, and then tell me what did not survive the translation.

My working vocabulary is event-driven distributed systems. I think in terms of schedulers and event loops, mutable state versus pure functions, message passing versus shared memory, happens-before and causal ordering in logs, idempotent replay, and mocking or stubbing a dependency so that work appears to originate in the stub while really being done by the harness.

Please do two things, in this order. First, give me the most faithful translation you can manage: describe what a system would look like if the occasionalist thesis were true of it, and then re-describe its ordinary causal talk — statements of the form "the write caused the cache invalidation" or "the request caused the timeout" — in that idiom. Be explicit about what plays the role of the sole agent, what plays the role of the occasion, and what plays the role of the thing that merely appears to cause. I am more interested in a translation that preserves the structure of the thesis than in one that makes it sound plausible.

Second, the residue report. Sort the claims into three buckets: those that carry over cleanly; those that carry over only if I accept some distortion, with a note on exactly what gets bent; and those that have no expression at all in my vocabulary. I am particularly interested in whether my vocabulary can distinguish the occasionalist thesis, on which a single agent really does all the work, from the Humean thesis that we never observe any necessary connection between events and that the apparent power is a projection of ours. My suspicion is that from inside my vocabulary these two collapse into the same thing, and I want to know whether that collapse is a limitation of how I think about systems or a sign that the two positions were never as far apart as the textbooks imply. If the position as I have stated it is a caricature, say which part is the caricature and what the actual claim is. Finish with your verdict on whether the residue marks a defect in my framework or a genuine unclarity in the original.
