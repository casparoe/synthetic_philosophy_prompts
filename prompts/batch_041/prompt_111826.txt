so this has got personal in a way I wasn't expecting. my job got carved up in the spring — the firm put a model on the part of my work that clients actually pay for and left me with the admin and the chasing and the bits nobody bills for. I've been the guy telling everyone automation is going to be great, and I basically still think that, but two months of this has made me want to check whether my views on it actually fit together. can you do the audit for me? here's roughly where I've landed:

1. the real goods people get out of work are the structure, getting good at something, being needed by someone specific, and the people. not the money, and not "contributing to society" in the abstract.
2. I also think most "my work is meaningful" talk is cope — it's the story you tell to make a pointless or degrading job bearable. if you have to keep reminding yourself it matters, that's usually a sign it doesn't.
3. automation is on balance good and I want it to keep going. take the drudgery off people, hand out the dividends, done. I'm not one of the smash-the-machines people.
4. but here's the thing thats been eating at me: what actually gets automated isn't what's degrading, it's what's expensive. nobody's asking "is this work miserable?" they're asking "can I do this cheaper?" so the well-paid jobs that happen to be good ones go first, and the grim ones that pay badly stay, because there's no money in automating them.
5. related: I don't think the value of a job is priceable. the stuff in (1) is real, but it isn't in the wage.
6. I'd still want to work even if the money was handled.

is this a consistent package or am I fooling myself somewhere? go through them and tell me which pairs are genuinely pulling against each other and which only look like they are — I suspect at least some of what's bugging me is only apparent. for each real tension, rank it by how bad it actually is, say what the cheapest way out is and what that costs me, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, because I'd rather change one thing than four.
