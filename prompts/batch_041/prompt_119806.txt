I spent 35 years doing experimental physics and only lately got into philosophy, so bear with the naive framing. I keep meeting this move: Gödel showed a formal system can't prove its own consistency, but we can supposedly see that our own reasoning is consistent, so we must be more than any formal system, hence no machine can think. Hofstadter's big Gödel-Escher-Bach book runs the same loop from the other end, up from self-referential formulas to a self that knows itself. Before I buy any of it I want the spec. Give me the desiderata: what an account of that analogy, from self-reference to self-awareness, has to deliver -- the results it must square with, the cases it has to get right, the disanalogies it cant wave away (what exactly is supposed to be the formal system here, the whole mind or just its reasoning?), the distinctions it can't collapse, and what would even count as the thing being wrong. I'm a scientist, I want to know what would falsify it. Put the case behind each item, flag any pair that can't be had together, and tell me which single requirement does the most damage to the usual version of the argument. If the standard repair is "just add the missing sentence as an axiom," make sure the list has something that deals with that. I dont want a verdict yet, I want the criteria.
