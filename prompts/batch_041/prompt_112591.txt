Write a satirical press release — headline plus roughly 600 words — from a fictional mechanistic-interpretability startup (call it ClearBox) announcing that it has isolated, and will begin licensing, the internal representation of the concept of Tuesday in a frontier language model. Keep the register deadpan corporate: dateline, an invented spokesperson, a tiered price list, a closing boilerplate paragraph. The humor must come from real methodology rather than invented nonsense, so build the jokes on four things: the gap between what a linear probe can decode from a model's activations and what the model actually uses; superposition, and the polysemantic neuron that responds both to Tuesday and to divorce; the fact that ablating a direction and observing no behavioral change can be read either as a failed causal claim or as a representation that is simply not load-bearing; and the multiple-comparisons problem of scanning millions of directions for one that lights up on a concept. Keep every technical claim accurate enough that a working interpretability researcher laughs rather than winces, and aim the satire at the overclaiming — the slide from decoding a property to asserting that the model represents it — rather than at interpretability as such. The sharpest joke should be that ClearBox markets itself as realist about representations while its own terms of service disclaim that any representation exists. Include one quoted line from an invented outside critic, and keep the whole piece legible to a reader who has never opened an interpretability paper.
