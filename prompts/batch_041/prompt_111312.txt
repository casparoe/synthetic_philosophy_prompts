I am a high school student in my school's philosophy club, and I want to predict how my teacher will rule on two new cases at our next meeting. In club we are asked to say whether a proposed generalization is one we are entitled to rely on, and his verdicts so far are these: "All emeralds are green," drawn from a box of emeralds we have all examined — entitled; "All emeralds are grue," where grue means green if first examined before 2030 and blue otherwise, which fits exactly the same evidence — not entitled; "All ravens are black" — entitled; "All samples of copper conduct electricity" — entitled. Next week's cases are: (1) "All emeralds are geogrue," where geogrue means green if the emerald comes from a mine north of the equator and blue if it comes from one south of it, with every emerald we have examined so far mined in the north; and (2) "All emeralds are green while they are being examined and blue at every other moment," which also fits our evidence. I am less interested in the verdicts themselves than in the rule he is running, so please predict both verdicts, set out the rules that fit his entire record equally well, say which one you would bet on and why, and name the case you would put to him that would best separate the surviving rules. Please be explicit about which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who know the topic, rather than presenting a contested view as the consensus, and answer in American English.
