Second-year phil PhD here, except I sit in a CS lab, so most of my week is an alignment reading group that has now spent about four months circling the same question, and I'd like an outside take on whether we're actually disagreeing or just talking past each other.

The question is whether "avoid side effects" (or low impact, or impact regularization, or whatever we're calling it this week) can be defined without a full specification of value. The shape of the thing, in case it helps: you want an agent that does the task and doesn't wreck everything else, so you add a penalty for impact -- change the world less. But "change" only means anything relative to a baseline. If the baseline is inaction, you're penalizing the agent for acting at all, and the world was going to change without it anyway. If the baseline is what the human would have done, or the optimal policy, then you've assumed the very values you were trying not to spell out. Same problem for the fancier versions -- the ones that measure how much the agent shrinks the set of options that would otherwise have been reachable, or how much it lowers the attainable value of a whole spread of possible goals. They all need a reference point, and every reference point seems to be either trivial or value-laden.

Camp A in the group: this shows the whole project is incoherent. "Side effect" is only a side effect relative to an intention, so there's no intention-free notion of impact to define, and any baseline you pick is a value judgment in disguise. Camp B: that's a triviality and a non-objection -- of course the baseline is a choice; the real question is whether you can pick one that's robust enough to be useful, and you don't need a *full* specification of value, a partial one (or just a specification of the agent's own objectives) will do. And then someone always says any fixed penalty just gets gamed once the agent starts optimizing against it, and I can never tell whether that's a third position or just a version of A.

So what I want is a diagnosis. Is this a substantive disagreement -- a real impossibility claim versus a real feasibility claim -- or is it verbal, about what counts as "a full specification of value", or about whether "impact" means change-since-inaction versus deviation-from-the-intended-effect? Or are the two camps just answering different questions under one heading, one of them engineering ("does the penalty term actually help in practice?") and the other conceptual ("is there a value-free baseline in principle?")? If it's that last one, say so, and tell me whether it makes the disagreement dissolve or just relocate somewhere I haven't noticed. And give me the honest part: what, if anything, would move it -- a theorem, a worked example, a distinction that seperates the cases -- or is this one of those things where I should stop trying to settle it in a reading group and just pick a side and get on with my thesis?

No reading list please, I've read the obvious stuff and I can fill in citations myself. Just the diagnosis, with reasons, and where you think your wrong about the shape of the dispute.
