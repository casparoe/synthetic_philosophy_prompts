Write an essay on whether pragmatic encroachment and epistemic contextualism are genuinely rival accounts of the knowledge attributions we make in high-stakes cases, or whether one can be absorbed into the other without loss. To keep the discussion concrete, work through an invented case and its variants. Ada, a pharmacist, is checking a dosage; her evidence is the same throughout — the label reads one thing, but she has a faint memory of a correction — while what turns on the dose varies: in one version an error is a minor inconvenience, in another it kills a child. Now separate the subject's stakes from the attributor's. Consider Bea, an idle colleague asking out of curiosity, and Cass, the child's parent about to administer the dose; each may assert "Ada knows the dose," and we can vary their stakes independently of Ada's. Say what a contextualist, a subject-sensitive invariantist, and a plain invariantist must predict across the resulting four combinations, then test each against a nested attribution — whether Cass's "Ada knows the dose" and Cass's "Ada knows that Bea knows the dose" can come apart. Reach a verdict: identify the single account (or an explicit hybrid) that best explains the full pattern, and defend it against the strongest objection available to its rivals.
