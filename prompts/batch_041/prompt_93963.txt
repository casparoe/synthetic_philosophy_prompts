i want the tree built questions-first, positions-second, so the branches are decided by concrete cases and not by whether someone happens to like the word "pessimism." the positions i'd like it to be able to land on: full optimism (testimony is just another source of moral knowledge, same status as perception); pessimism (testimony can give you true belief maybe but never knowledge); the middle view where you can get knowledge but not understanding, so you know the act is wrong but don't grasp why and something is still missing; a view where testimony works for general principles but not for particular judgments about what to do here and now; and a relational view where whether the testimony counts depends on the testifier being part of your moral community and answerable to you, not just on whether what they say is true.

make every node a case someone could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. some i want in there: (a) someone reliable tells you a specific act is wrong and you believe them — do you know it's wrong or only believe it? (e) someone you trust as a moral exemplar tells you a thing is fine that your own reasoning says is fine but that still feels off to you — what does trusting them amount to here?

then place the positions as leaves, and for any combination of answers that no position occupies, say whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. flag any two positions the tree can't actually separate instead of papering over it. and tell me honestly whether the AI case forces a genuinely new position that doesn't fit any of the five i listed, or whether it just collapses into one of them.
