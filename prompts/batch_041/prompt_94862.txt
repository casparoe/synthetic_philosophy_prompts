I think "why Britain and not China?" picks out a real contrast, and the divergence is a genuine single puzzle with an answer, not just two separate questions — why did Britain industrialize, why didn't China — bolted together and given a misleading coat of paint.

My friend's pet idea, which he's convinced sinks me: a why-question is really three parts. The thing you're explaining, the set of alternatives you're contrasting it against, and what you're allowed to count as relevant. Change the contrast class and you've changed the question. He says if I swap China for India, or Japan, or even the Netherlands and get the same answer every time, then "not China" was doing no work and I'm really just asking "why Britain?" — which is fine, but it's a different and much less interesting question than the one I think I'm asking. And he says China is only in the contrast because of hindsight: nobody in 1750 would have picked it out as the salient "could have been them."

Sort them by how decisive they'd be, and tell me which ones a defender of my view would have to concede rather than just wave off. And be honest about the nasty possibility: can I always just redraw the contrast until the question is well posed again? If I can, is that a real problem for me or is it fine?
