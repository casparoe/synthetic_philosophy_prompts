So my reading group has drifted from phil of mind into metaethics and Priya dropped this on me on the walk home. I can't tell if there's a bad step in it or if I just have to eat the conclusion.

(1) An experience can be bad for the one having it only in virtue of its phenomenal character -- what it's like -- and not in virtue of its causes, its effects, or how it's embedded in some larger structure. If the badness lived in the causes, you'd have changed the subject from suffering to something else.

(2) What's intrinsic to a phenomenal character is intrinsic to it, full stop. It doesn't come in degrees depending on how simple the bearer is, or on whether the experience is itself a part of some bigger experience.

(3) Russellian monism / panpsychism -- which I'm sympathtic to, mostly because I can't stomach strong emergence -- says phenomenal or proto-phenomenal properties are fundamental and ubiquitous. On that picture there's no point in the story where you get to say that here, for the first time, things started to matter to themselves.

(4) So take any fundamental entity bearing a phenomenal property of the suffering-ish kind. By (2) that property is bad to the same degree no matter how it's embedded. So it's just bad, there, at the bottom of physics.

(5) Hence the total amount of suffering in the universe is fixed by fundamental physics plus the distribution of phenomenal properties, not by facts about nervous systems or selves.

(6) Which means a view I actually hold -- that there's a lot of pointless suffering and we should be reducing it -- turns into a view on which literally everything is a site of harm, and the ordinary project of reducing suffering is both impossible (you can't switch off fundamental properties) and absurd (swatting a fly and splitting an atom come out as comparable catastrophes).

(7) But (6) is absurd. So one of the steps is wrong.

Which one do I reject? I'd give up almost anything before I accept (6). And tell me whether the "proto-" in (3) is doing real work or is just a promissory note -- because if it's the latter I don't think I get to keep (3) either.
