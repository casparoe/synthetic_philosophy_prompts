I'm on the board of a small regional mutual-credit network called the Cloyne Exchange (yes, named after the bishop, we're that kind of org). We have a vote next Thursday on whether to pilot a new unit of account we're calling hour notes. Right now our ledger is denominated in dollars, but the proposal is to issue notes each representing one hour of standard labor, redeemable in services from member businesses, with no commodity reserve and no central bank backing. Instead we'd have a guarantee fund: member businesses pledge to accept the notes for future services. There's also a demurrage rule: notes lose 1% of their value each month if they sit idle, to keep them circulating. Half the board thinks this is just a local currency that will inflate into oblivion or collapse the moment people lose confidence. The other half thinks it's exactly the kind of thing Berkeley was talking about in The Querist, where money is more like a ticket or counter than a lump of metal, and real wealth is the power to command labor and industry. I want a memo that takes the philosophical question seriously: is money fundamentally a commodity, a credit relation, or a claim on future production? And what does that imply for our vote?

I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, so I'm allergic to vague hand-waving about trust and community. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action -- approve the pilot, reject it, or approve with conditions -- with a time frame and an actionable bottom line. Engage the strongest argument on the losing side honestly, not as a straw man. Don't give me a history lesson or a reading list; I need something the board can actually act on. I definately want the Berkeley angle to do real work, not just be decoration.

After the memo, give me a JSON object with these fields: recommendation (one of 'approve', 'approve with conditions', 'reject'), conditions (array of strings, empty if none), core_philosophical_claim (string, one sentence), strongest_counterargument (string, one sentence), implementation_timeline (array of objects with 'step' and 'deadline'), and confidence (a number from 0 to 1).
