I'm a second-year PhD student working on evaluation of ML systems, and I'm the student rep on my department's AI Research Governance Comittee. We're deciding whether to approve a pilot programme where a model gets to run its own experiments on the shared cluster: it writes code, launches jobs, reads logs, and proposes follow-ups, with no human in the loop for each step. The panel is split on process vs outcome supervision. One side wants process supervision: the model has to show its plan and intermediate reasoning, every tool call is logged, and a human signs off at checkpoints before it spends more than 50 GPU-hours or deletes anything. The other side says that's theatre: we can't trust the model's stated reasoning, and the only thing that matters is whether the results are any good, so let it run and evaluate the outcomes (reproducibility, benchmarks, whether it actually found something). The lab has offered a compromise: process supervision for the first three months with a fixed 150 human review hours, then switch to outcome supervision if there are no incidents. I'm sceptical about the compromise, but the memo has to be fair.

I have to draft the panel's decision memo for Thursday. Can you write it? It should recomend a concrete course of action, approve/reject/approve with conditions, with a time frame and exactly what gets logged and reviewed. And it should take the strongest version of the losing side seriously, not just say process supervision is safer. I'm especially worried about two failure modes: the process looks fine but the outcome is subtly wrong in a way nobody notices for months, and the outcome is good but it came from a process we would never have approved if we'd seen it. The memo should be something the lab can actually work with, not a philosophy essay. No citations needed.
