I serve on the academic integrity committee at a small community college. Last month we heard a case in which a student submitted an essay that several of us believe was generated by an AI tool. Our honor code lists plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration, and similar offenses, but it says nothing about AI. We deadlocked, and the student was not penalized. A faculty member has now proposed replacing our detailed code with a short statement that students must act with integrity, plus a clause saying that any case not explicitly covered should be decided by the committee according to the spirit of the code. His argument is not merely that technology changes. He says Gödel's incompleteness theorems show that any formal system rich enough to be useful is incomplete -- there will always be truths the system cannot prove -- so any written code of conduct will have gaps, and trying to make it complete is a mistake. A mathematics instructor on the committee says this is a misuse of Gödel, because the theorems concern arithmetic and formal proof, not ethics or college discipline. I have no background in logic, but I have been thinking about this on my own, and I am not satisfied by either side. Please draft the memo I can circulate before next week's vote. It should recommend whether we should adopt the short code with the spirit clause, keep and expand the detailed code, or take some third path. It should give reasons the committee can act on, and it should engage seriously with the strongest version of the faculty member's argument rather than dismissing it as a category mistake. In particular, I want the memo to explain what Gödel's theorems actually do and do not show, and whether they give us any reason to prefer discretion over written rules in a case like this. Please do not include citations or reading lists; I need something I can understand and defend in the meeting.
