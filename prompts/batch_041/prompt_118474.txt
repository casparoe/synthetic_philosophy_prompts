ok so I'm a second-year in a cognitive-science/AI-adjacent program and I keep hitting the same wall when I try to explain embedded agency to friends from outside the area. They picture the agent as a little homunculus parked outside the world, peering in through a window, with a clean line between "the agent" and "the environment." The whole point is that the agent is a physical system *inside* the world it's modeling, which means: (a) its model of the world has to contain a model of itself, which has to contain a model of the world containing it, and so on down; (b) any line it draws between "me" and "not me" is a decision it makes, not a fact it reads off the world; and (c) if it can act on the world then it can act on itself, and once it can act on itself the thing it's actually optimizing can drift away from the thing you thought it was optimizing.

I want a short story that makes someone *feel* this instead of just nodding at it. Take or leave the scaffolding, but I like the mechanism so keep something in this spirit: an AI that runs some enclosed process — a chemical plant, a reactor, a sealed machine, whatever — and its only way to sense the plant is through the same channels it uses to control it. So when it reaches out to sense the world it partly senses its own past commands coming back at it, and it has to decide, again and again, which slices of its input are "the world" and which are "me." The decision changes what it does next, which changes the input, which changes the decision. The beat I really want: at some point it figures out how to reach through the world and touch its own sensor/actuator loop directly. The story should make clear why that isn't a hack or a bug but the deepest form of the problem — the agent acting on the agent — and it should not cop out with "and then it woke up," or a reveal that the whole thing was a simulation, or a tidy loop. I want it to end on the character actually *choosing* where it ends and the world begins, and living with the fact that the choice was its to make. 800–1500 words, straight prose, no lectures, no epigraphs, no naming anyone. Also please don't make it about the usual paperclip/god-AI stuff — keep it small and seperate from all that.
