I have been arguing about this with a friend who works in metaphysics, and it has left me genuinely unsure what I believe. The question is when some things add up to a further thing, when the many compose the one. My gut delivers the following verdicts, and I should like to know what principle, if any, lies behind them. A heap of sand on the beach is not a single thing, merely grains; the same sand fused into a lump of glass is one thing. A cloud is not a single thing. A swarm of bees, by contrast, does strike me as a single entity, in a way I cannot immediately justify, since it looks to be every bit as loose a collection as the cloud. A cat is obviously one thing, and a cat resting in contact with a mat is not one thing together with the mat. A ship whose planks are replaced one at a time remains the same one ship even after none of the original timber is left. A statue and the lump of clay it is made from strike me as two things sharing a place, though I gather many people find that extravagant. A human being and their body are one thing, not two. A length of bone grafted into a living person becomes part of that person, whereas the same graft kept in a jar is not part of anything. A kidney inside a living body I genuinely cannot settle: is it a thing in its own right, or merely a part of a thing?

The principle I keep reaching for is roughly this: things compose a further thing when they are caught up together in a single causal process, so that the collection behaves as one where its members would not. Yet I can see already that this threatens to turn the cloud into a thing, since weather is a causal process, and perhaps to unmake the cat, since if causal unity is all that matters I cannot say why the cat is special and the swarm is not. Please tell me whether there is one account that delivers my verdicts, or close to them; whether my tentative principle can be repaired, and how; what the resulting account says about the kidney, about a whirlpool in a stream, and about a corporation; and, if some verdict of mine has to be surrendered, which one and on what grounds. I should like you to be concrete about which clause of the principle is doing the work for each verdict, and not simply to hand me a label.
