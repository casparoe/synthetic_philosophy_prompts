ok so for my bioethics seminar (autonomy and consent, meets tuesdays, which is why im writing this at 1am) the final assignment is a creative response to the advance directives unit and i want to do a poem, not another reading response like everyone else.

heres my idea and i need help executing it. i want a pantoum. reason: in a pantoum you arent allowed to change the words when they come back. lines 2 and 4 of each stanza have to show up again as lines 1 and 3 of the next stanza, and the last stanza folds the opening stanza's first and third lines back in. so a line can only change its meaning by changing its position, never its wording. which is kind of exactly the structure of a written directive — the words get fixed at a moment when the situation theyre about doesnt exist yet.

the voice: the whole poem is spoken by the later self, the one the directive is now being applied to, and the repeated lines are the clauses of the directive that this person keeps having to say out loud (to a doctor, to a daughter, to themselves in the mirror, whatever works). so the same clause comes back every stanza, and by stanza 4 or 5 it has to be doing something different — first it sounds like consent, later like someone elses sentence in your mouth.

stuff i want it to get right:

- the later self should be, on the whole, content. not scared, not confused, not obviously incompetent. thats the case that actually bothers me. if the poem makes them pitiable it cheats.
- the earlier self shouldnt be a tyrant either. somewhere in there i want the possibility that the directive was a kindness the earlier person did for the later one, not just a cage. but i dont want the poem to decide which it was.
- put at least one conditional clause in a line that gets repeated — something that only holds as long as i still know where i am, or as long as i can still say your name. when that line comes back a second time it should land hard without the wording changing at all.
- concrete and ordinary. a specific food, a specific walk, a name that recurs. no abstractions, no autonomy-talk.
- 5 quatrains, 20 lines, plain speech, no forced end rhyme (loose rhyme is fine if it falls out naturally). it gets read aloud in the last class so it has to work in the ear.
- last thing: dont resolve it. no verdict on whether the earlier self wins. the seminar has been split on this all term and i want the poem to leave the split open, which the form should help with since the closing lines are literally the opening lines.

and if you can, after the poem tell me which repeated line you think changed the most between its two appearances and why that one — i want to say something smart in the reflection paragraph.
