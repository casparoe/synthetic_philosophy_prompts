I chair a historical memory commission in a country that emerged from a civil war two generations ago. Our draft report contains a chapter establishing that the current constitution, together with several foundational land and property statutes, rests on an armed seizure of power and on customs that were never consented to and are, in several particulars, unjust. One faction insists the chapter must be published in full: the commission's mandate is truth, and citizens are entitled to know that their legal order has no title in justice, only in force and long usage. The other faction argues that publishing it would corrode the habitual deference on which obedience depends — that once people are told their laws are not just, the state will have to fall back on force alone, which is the very outcome the commission was created to prevent. Both sides are citing Pascal's reflections on justice and force, and his claim that those who understand the arbitrariness of social rank should keep an outward conformity while holding a truer thought inwardly.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action — publish the chapter in full, withhold it, or publish it with specified modifications — and justify that recommendation. Engage the strongest version of the position you reject rather than a caricature of it, and be explicit about what you take Pascal's view to be, including whether it is a view a democratic commission can consistently adopt.
