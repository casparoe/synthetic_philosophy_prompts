I write a science-and-ideas column, and I've agreed to provide a short comic turn for the closing session of a quantum-foundations workshop — something I can read aloud in about thirty seconds that will actually make a room of physicists and philosophers of physics laugh rather than wince. I want to stay well clear of the exhausted setup in which a Copenhagenist, an Everettian, and a Bohmian walk into a bar; the humor needs to sit on a distinction that people who know these interpretations would recognize as genuinely load-bearing, and it needs to be intelligible to a lay reader who merely gets the shape of it.

Would you write that joke for me? I'd like you to build it around a single mundane event that all three interpretations would describe differently — an outcome being delivered, a measurement result arriving, a choice being made — and to derive the comedy from each interpretation's actual machinery rather than from the personalities of its adherents. Please make the punchline turn on at least one of the following, handled accurately rather than as a slogan: (a) the Copenhagen insistence that there is a measurement whose occurrence is indisputable but whose definition is left primitive; (b) the Everettian picture in which every outcome is real, decoherence yields only an approximate and environment-dependent notion of branching, and the surviving observer still has to reconstruct probability through self-locating reasoning; or (c) the Bohmian picture in which exactly one trajectory is actual and guided by its own branch's wavefunction, so that the remaining branches are, for practical purposes, causally inert.

Keep it brief and in good taste, and invent whatever small fictional setting you need. A two-line setup and a punchline will do, and a compact dialogue is equally welcome. Please don't append an explanation — if the joke needs a footnote to be funny, it isn't.
