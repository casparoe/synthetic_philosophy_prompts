I cover physics and philosophy for a general-interest science outlet, and for the past few weeks I've been sitting in on a working group devoted to the relationship between special relativity and the passage of time. The argument that keeps splitting the room is the Rietdijk–Putnam one: because simultaneity is relative, two spacelike-separated events can each be judged earlier than the other, depending on the inertial frame, and if no frame is privileged in what it says about what exists, the argument concludes that all events are equally real — the block universe, with no objective present and no genuine becoming. I have collected the responses people keep reaching for, and I want an outsider's assessment of them rather than another round of the same seminar fight. For each of the following, rate how much it genuinely defuses the argument while keeping faith with relativity — that is, without quietly reinstating a preferred frame or an absolute simultaneity — and say whether it survives as a live option or is a verbal dodge. Give me a tier list (four tiers, from serious contender down to changes the subject), with a sentence or two on why each item lands where it does: (a) privileging a frame, such as the one in which the cosmic microwave background looks isotropic, as the one that tracks real becoming; (b) the tenseless reading, on which the argument trades on a confusion, because "exists" should be read tenselessly and the tensed "now" is not a feature of reality at all; (c) presentism, on which only the present is real and relativity's verdict about simultaneity concerns our dating conventions rather than what exists; (d) the growing block, on which past and present are real but the future is not, with the edge of reality somehow identified; (e) the deflationary move, on which the argument shows at most that the truth-conditions of tensed sentences vary by frame, not that the world is a block, so becoming belongs to descriptions rather than to reality; (f) the indexical move, on which "now" works like "here," picking out the speaker's location on their worldline, and needs a privileged frame no more than "here" needs a privileged place; and (g) local becoming, on which becoming is real but local, so that each worldline carries its own objective past–future distinction and there is no global now to relativize.

One member of the group also floated a proposal I have not seen written up anywhere, and I would especially like it rated alongside the rest: that the whole block-versus-present dispute is a disagreement not about the physics but about which of two incompatible ontological packages we should adopt given that same physics, and that we should settle it by something like explanatory or pragmatic criteria rather than by intuition about becoming. Tell me where that belongs in the tiers and, more usefully, whether it is genuinely distinct from moves (b) and (e) or just a redescription of them. To close, tell me which single response does the most work and which does the least, and flag any two entries on the list that turn out to be the same move in different clothes.
