2. "The fact that I was willing to put five years in is itself evidence that this is worth doing, and that information doesn't evaporate just because the money has."
3. "If I walk away now, everyone watching me learns that I quit when things get expensive. Keeping going protects my reputation for finishing things, which is worth more to me than the project is."
4. "I know what I'm like: if I let myself quit whenever the costs are sunk and the future looks grim, I'll quit everything. Finishing what I start is a rule I keep for my own good."
5. "This is part of who I am. Dropping it would make me a different sort of person, and not one I want to be."
6. "In the world we actually live in, the things that have survived years of my effort are disproportionately the ones worth finishing — so the rule usually pays off, even if it's invalid as stated."
