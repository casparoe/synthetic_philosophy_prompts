So my friend and I just burned two hours arguing about whether Rome actually fell, and his whole case rests on a picture I couldn't shake. Empires, he says, are basically organisms: they're born (a founding), they grow (conquest, expansion), they mature, they get old and corrupt, and then they die. He read some fat book about the life cycles of civilizations that runs entirely on this, and he uses it to say Rome's collapse was inevitable and basically biological — nothing anyone could've done — and, by extension, that whatever empire we're sitting inside right now is just somewhere on the same curve. My gut says the metaphor is doing all the work, but I couldn't articulate why in the moment. I tried the Latin thing on him — Latin didn't die, it turned into French and Italian and Spanish, so maybe "Rome" didn't die either, it just turned into other things — and he said that's just cope.

What I actually want is a real audit of the thing. Give me the correspondence table first: what exactly is supposed to map onto the birth, the growth, the maturity, the old age, the death — and which of those have genuine referents in Rome's case versus just being read in from the metaphor. Then tell me where the organism picture breaks. Does an empire have anything like a single lifespan, a germ line, a moment of death you could actually put a timestamp on? I keep thinking the parts have seperate clocks — the Senate, the army, the church, the language, the roads all start and stop at different times, and no organism is like that. And I'm wondering whether some other biological or ecological picture fits better: I half-remember something about how a field doesn't "die" when it goes from grassland to forest, it just succeeds into a different community, and there's also that old puzzle about a ship getting all its planks replaced one by one — if one of those is a better model, say so and say what it changes. Finally, the part I actually care about: does his conclusion survive once the analogy gets patched up or thrown out? If the life-cycle picture is what's making "inevitable decline" sound true, what's left of the claim that Rome fell — or of the claim that it didn't?
