Suppose John von Neumann had survived into the present and had followed neuroscience and machine learning continuously since the 1950s. On the picture of brain function he sketched in his later work—mixed analog and digital computation, statistical rather than strictly logical language, heavy redundancy to make unreliable components reliable, and a sharp contrast between the brain's architecture and the stored-program digital computer—what would he make of contemporary deep learning? Specifically, would he see backpropagation-trained transformers as a vindication, a distraction, or a useful but biologically implausible special case? And what would he say about a neuromorphic system that uses stochastic spiking neurons, analog in-memory weights, and local Hebbian learning rather than global backpropagation: closer to his conception of the brain than either standard digital hardware or current artificial neural networks, or not? I am less interested in a survey of his actual writings than in a defended extrapolation. Please give your best guess, flag where the extrapolation is most likely to fail, and say which later finding—about cortical computation, learning rules, or hardware—would most change your mind.
