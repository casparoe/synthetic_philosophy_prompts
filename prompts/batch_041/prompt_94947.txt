I am in a graduate seminar on scepticism and epistemic closure, and for the past fortnight two friends and I have been arguing over whether my own reactions to a run of cases add up to a single theory of knowledge. My suspicion is that something in the family of tracking accounts — the views on which whether I know depends on how my belief would have behaved had matters stood otherwise — will come closest, but I want to know how much of what I actually think such a view can survive. My verdicts, as flatly as I can state them, are these. I know that I have hands. I do not know that I am not a handless brain in a vat being fed these very experiences: the vat story would account for everything I now take myself to see and think, and nothing available to me from the inside rules it out. I do know that I am not in a coma, and this is not mere bravado, since a coma would not be producing this stream of experience at all — that hypothesis undercuts the very evidence I would have for it, which the vat story does not. I do not know that 91 is not prime, which I believe only because a stranger once assured me of it; I could not have produced the factors myself, and had he told me it was prime I would have believed that instead, yet it is not prime, and nothing could have made me believe it falsely. I know that the roses outside my window are healthy, notwithstanding that I have been told the gardener would have replaced them overnight with identical specimens had they been blighted, so that I would have believed as I do even had they been dying. I do not know that it is three o'clock when I glance at a clock which, unknown to me, stopped at exactly three o'clock twelve hours earlier. I know that my neighbour is at home when I see his car in the drive and he is in fact at home, even though he takes the bus on the days he stays in and drove today only because of an early errand, so that in the nearest worlds in which he is home the drive is empty and I would have concluded he was out. And I have no inclination whatever to say that the traveller who happens to be looking at the one real barn in a county of papier-mâché façades knows that what he sees is a barn.

These do not sit comfortably together, and I would rather have that out than paper over it. The second and third verdicts are about hypotheses of the same shape and come out opposite ways. The fifth, sixth and eighth all look like cases in which I would have believed as I do even had I been wrong, and yet I claim knowledge only in the fifth. The fourth runs the other way: that belief is one I could not have held falsely, and still I deny that it is knowledge. The seventh turns on a counterfactual about how I would have judged had the drive been empty, which I am inclined to think has nothing to do with whether the belief I actually hold is knowledge. And the first two, taken together, look like a plain failure of closure, which I am willing to accept but would like to see stated as a principle rather than a shrug. What I want from you is the principle, or the smallest set of principles, that delivers these eight verdicts; if nothing delivers all eight, I want an honest accounting of which ones have to be surrendered and why the survivors are the ones worth keeping. Say also whether the account has to individuate the way a belief is formed narrowly or broadly in order to get the cases right, since I suspect that is where the real work is being hidden. I am especially keen to see what your preferred account predicts about a case I have not listed, so please construct one and give its verdict. You need not supply citations, a reading list, or the names of who has said what; I am after the structure of the position and would rather you reason it out than gesture at a literature.
