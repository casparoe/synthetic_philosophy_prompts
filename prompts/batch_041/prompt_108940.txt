I'm trying to get my Molinist position straight before I have to defend it in front of people who know the debate better than I do, and what I want is a consistency check, not a summary of the literature. Here's what I hold.

(1) Before God decides anything about creating, he already knows what every possible free creature would freely do in whatever situation he might place them in — the counterfactuals of freedom are true, and God knows them.
(2) Creatures have libertarian freedom: their choices aren't caused or determined by anything, God included.
(3) God didn't make those counterfactuals true and doesn't decide them; they're true independently of his will.
(4) Every true proposition has to be grounded in something — truths don't just float.
(5) God is sovereign over what actually happens; nothing slips past his plan and he can count on getting the outcomes he wants.
(6) God is not the author of anyone's sin and isn't morally on the hook for it.

Which of these actually conflict, which only look like they do, and how bad is each? Order the real ones by severity, say what exactly would have to give in each case, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most. I'm not fishing for reassurance — if the package is broken I want to know where.
