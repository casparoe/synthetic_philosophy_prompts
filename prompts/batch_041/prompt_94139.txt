The idea is about Dewey. Dewey thought democracy was itself a form of inquiry, and that inquiry was experimental — the community investigates its shared problems and settles on what he called warranted assertibility rather than on anything like a final truth. Fine. Whats been bugging me is that the policy world has since grown something that looks very Deweyan — evidence-based policy, randomized trials, doing what works — and in one respect it looks anti-Deweyan. The randomized trial needs a control group and needs to be repeatable. A democratic decision has neither: you cant run a society twice, you cant hold the culture fixed while you vary the policy, and the people being experimented on are also the experimenters and the ones who have to live with the outcome. An experimenter is normally outside the system shes testing; here she is inside it.

So I want to pull apart two senses of experiment and ask which one Dewey actually needs. Interventionist: you manipulate a variable and compare the result against a baseline or a control. Exploratory: you try something, watch what consequences come, and revise — no baseline, no control. My suspicion is that Dewey only needs the exploratory sense, and that once you see that, the slogan that democracy is an experiment either turns out to do real work or turns out to be a flattering metaphor with nothing under it — and I genuinely dont know which.
