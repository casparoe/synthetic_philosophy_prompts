I've been in a political theory reading group this term and I'm supposed to bring my own position next week, so I want a consistency audit before I embarrass myself in front of people who actually know the literature. Here's where I've landed on the "dirty hands" thing. (1) I think dirty hands is real — sometimes the only way a politician can do what the office demands is to do something that is genuinely morally wrong, and it's still the right call to make. (2) I'm a consequentialist about political ethics: what makes a political decision right is the state of the world it produces, full stop, and if the numbers favor it that settles it. (3) But I also think there are a few things that are just off the table no matter what — I'd say torturing a kid to extract a code is wrong even if it saves thousands, and I don't care how the arithmetic comes out. (4) I don't think politicians get a special morality; they're held to the same standards as the rest of us, no exemptions, no "the office made me do it." (5) And when someone actually does the dirty deed, I think the guilt they carry afterward is the right response — it's not a hangover or a psychological bug, it's the act's wrongness still registering somewhere, and someone who feels nothing afterward has shown they're not fit for the job.

I should say up front that one of these is doing more work than I realize and I can't tell which. I also got into it with a friend who thinks (5) is flatly in tension with (1) — her line was, if it really was the right thing to do, then feeling guilty is just irrational self-flagellation, and you can't both call it right and call it a wrong. I told her she was wrong but I couldn't actually say why. My hunch, which I'm not attached to, is that (2) and (3) can't both survive, but I suspect I'm wrong about where the real fault line is and that the obvious clash is a decoy.

So go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how bad each one is — rank them by severity rather than dumping them all out as equally bad — and for each genuine tension say what would have to give and what it costs me to give it up. If some of the apparent tensions are just me misreading my own commitments, say so and explain why they dissolve. Then tell me the single cheapest revision that cleans up the most, and what I'd be losing by taking it. Please don't just tell me to "become a tragic pluralist" and call it a day — I want to know which specific commitment is doing the damage and what the least costly way out actually is.
