A provincial injury-compensation tribunal has published reasoned decisions for years, and legal commentators describe its approach as luck-egalitarian. Its record: it awarded full compensation to a pedestrian struck by a drunk driver; refused it to a motorcyclist injured while riding without a helmet; awarded it to a claimant with a degenerative genetic condition; awarded it to a worker injured while carrying out an employer's instructions he had no reason to distrust; awarded it to a claimant who took a hazardous job because no other work existed in his town; and refused it to a claimant injured while attempting an online stunt he had watched others perform. Now before it: a claimant who took a second job as a delivery courier, drove uninsured because the platform's pay left nothing for premiums, and was gravely injured in a crash that was his own fault. What will the tribunal decide, and what should it decide to remain consistent with its own record? Set out the rules that fit its earlier awards equally well -- for instance one that compensates brute bad luck but never option luck, and one that compensates losses the claimant had no fair opportunity to insure against -- say what each predicts here, give your best guess with reasons, and name a further case that would tell the rules apart.
