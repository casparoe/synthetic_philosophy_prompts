I am putting together a two-page proposal for an exploratory grant from my department that would buy me a course release and a summer — call it nine months of part-time work — toward one paper, and I would like you to draft it. The paper is on the utility monster.

My half-formed idea is this. The monster is almost always discussed as a possible being, and the argument is about whether its possibility shows that aggregative views deliver absurd verdicts. I suspect this mislocates where the force of the argument lies. The monster does no work that a formal condition on the axiology cannot do, and the condition it appears to violate is the Archimedean or continuity requirement familiar from the aggregation literature — the requirement that a small enough gain to one person can always be outweighed by a large enough loss to another, or to sufficiently many others. If that is right, then the monster case and the repugnant conclusion are not two independent objections to consequentialism; they are two instances of a single schema, one that should be stated precisely, and they may turn out to violate different conditions within it. What makes this worth a paper is a tension the framing exposes. Lexical priority for the worst off — the structure people reach for precisely in order to resist the repugnant conclusion — is itself a failure of the same continuity condition, running in the opposite direction. So it will not do to say that the monster shows continuity must be abandoned; the real question is which departures from continuity are principled and which are not, and I suspect that question is the whole of it. A second thing I want the paper to settle is whether the schema needs the monster as a member of a fixed population or as a newly created being, since the two readings appear to test different parts of a view.

Please turn this into the proposal: a question stated precisely enough to have a determinate answer; an account of why the existing positions — unrestricted aggregation, prioritarianism, lexical priority for the worst off, and the various strategies for denying that a monster is possible at all — leave the question open rather than settling it; the approach, staged across the nine months, together with a defence of a conceptual and formal approach over the obvious alternatives; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a scope that a course release and a summer can genuinely cover. If you think the framing collapses — if, for instance, monster-vulnerability turns out to be a feature of the domain of application rather than of the axiology, or if the continuity diagnosis is simply wrong — say so, and propose the nearest question that survives the objection, since that would be a better paper than a defence of a broken analogy.

Write it so that it can be read by a philosopher who works on ethics but not on population axiology, and state any technical notion you need rather than gesturing at it. I will handle the citations myself, so refer to positions and arguments rather than to authors and titles, and please do not include a bibliography or a reading list.
