I run a monthly discussion night at a community centre — usually 15-18 people, mixed ages, no philosophy background, 90 minutes with a tea break in the middle. Next month I want to do the "singleton" idea — the one where a single decision-maker ends up in charge of the whole world — and I want people to feel why it's tempting before they feel why it's frightening, rather than me standing up and explaining it.

Can you design the session? My rough thought is a game where everyone's harvesting from a shared stock of something and it keeps collapsing even though we're allowed to talk to each other, and then at some point one person ends up in charge and it actually works — but I can't work out how to make that second part happen without it feeling rigged, and I dont want to just hand someone a crown and say "look, a singleton." I'd rather the group stumble into it and only realize afterwards what they've built. The thing I most want them to run into is that whoever ends up on top gets there by being first or fastest or loudest, not by being the person anyone would have chosen, and that once it's done you can't undo it.

So: rules, what to put on the tables, how long each stage takes, and a debrief that maps what happened back onto the idea. Tell me what usually goes wrong the first time someone runs a thing like this and how to fix it on the fly. And be honest about what the game can't show — I assume the timescale is one of them, and the fact that in real life there's nobody standing at the front who can call time.
