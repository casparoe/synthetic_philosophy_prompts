Right, so I'm in second year and I'm doing that animal welfare ethics module (the one that's basically "who counts and why"), and last week's seminar was on aquaculture and invertebrates and honestly I've not been able to stop thinking about it since. Our tutor said the interesting question isn't whether prawns feel pain, it's what you're supposed to do while the science is still open — and then she said something I wrote down, that in practice the burden of proof only ever runs one way: you need extraordinary evidence before anyone will admit an animal suffers, and basically none before you carry on assuming it doesn't. That's the thing I want the piece to be about.

For my portfolio I want to do something satirical rather than another essay. She's fine with that as long as it's actually funny to someone who's read the material and isn't just a sneer at farmers. So here's what I want:

Write me the script for a self-guided audio tour of the visitor centre at a big prawn farm that runs public tours. About 600–700 words, split into numbered stops. Invent the farm and the tour — please don't use a real business or a real person, and don't make up any citations or references, because I'll get in trouble if it looks like I'm citing things that don't exist.

The voice is the whole thing: relentlessly cheerful, family-friendly, welcoming, completely deadpan. No winking, no nudging the reader, nothing that announces it's a joke — it should read exactly like the real thing you'd hear through a plastic handset on a lanyard.

The satire has to come out of the actual husbandry, not out of turning anyone into a villain. Things I'd like it to cover, all delivered as perfectly sensible, even admirable, practice:

- Eyestalk ablation. Our lecturer explained it: you cut off one of the female broodstock's eyestalks because there's a hormone in there that stops her ripening her eggs, so without it she won't spawn in captivity. Apparently it's routine at basically every shrimp breeding facility in the world, it's done without anaesthetic, and it's already banned under the organic rules in Europe. The audio guide should present it as careful, professional broodstock management and be quietly proud of how quick and tidy the technique is.
- Killing by ice slurry, explained as a lovely cold nap.
- The nociception-versus-pain distinction (reflex versus feeling) deployed as a shield: they want proof of pain before they'll change anything, and they never notice they're holding themselves to a standard they'd never accept for a pig or a chicken.
- The fact that all their welfare figures are really production figures — survival rate, growth, feed conversion — and they genuinely can't see why that isn't the same thing.
- A bit of "we're funding the research" and "we'll act the moment the science is settled", plus the stunning kit being expensive and nothing in the rules actually requiring it for these animals.
- Let the vocabulary do some of the work too — "harvest", "biomass", "stock", "processing" — the guide should never once say kill or slaughter about its own animals.

Two last things: can it be in British English please (recognised, colour, programme, all of that — I keep getting marked down for it), and could you have one stop where a child asks a question and the answer is technically true and completely damning.
