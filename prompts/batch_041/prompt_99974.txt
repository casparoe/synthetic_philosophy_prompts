ok so quick context: i'm a software engineer, never took a single philospohy class in my life, but my partner is finishing a masters and she's been going on about why promises actually bind and i want to actually get it instead of nodding along. her birthday is coming up and i want to give her a poem about it.

here's what i've pieced together so far. there seem to be two stories people tell. one is that a promise binds because i chose to make it - i put my will behind it, so it's mine to keep and (maybe) mine to take back. the other is that it binds because you started counting on it - the force comes from your reliance, not my intention. and the annoying part is each story gets a case wrong. if it's only my will, then a promise i regret is no more binding than a new years resolution i abandon in february. if it's only your reliance, then a promise to someone who never knew about it, or to someone who's now dead, or to a kid too small to count on anything, seems to lose its grip - but it really doesnt feel like it does. i promised my grandfather on his deathbed that id finish his boat, and nobody on earth is relying on that, and i still feel like i have to.

so: write me a villanelle about it. two refrains, one for each of the two stories (make it obvious which is which, but dont name any philosophers - she'd see right through the name dropping and i want this to actually be good). let the refrains alternate through the poem and then land next to each other in the last two lines, so the form itself shows why neither story alone can carry the whole thing. 19 lines. keep it accurate enough that my partner doesnt roll her eyes at me, but it should still read like a poem someone would actually want, not a flashcard. no citations or dates please, i wouldnt know if you made them up.
