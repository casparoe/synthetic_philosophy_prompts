Alright so I've been thinking a lot about how thoughts get to be *about* stuff, and I think I've finally got a package together, but I want it stress-tested before I start saying it out loud to people. Here's where I've landed:

1. A thought, or any little signal in the head, means whatever evolution built it to keep track of. Its job, basically. What it was selected to do. Not just whatever happens to set it off right now.
2. So a frog's bug-detector means BUG even when it snaps at a falling leaf. A misfire doesn't change what the signal is about.
3. There's always a definate answer to what a mental state is about. We might not be able to work it out, but it's never the case that a state half-means one thing and half-means another. No fuzzy middle ground.
4. If you could pop a perfect copy of me into existence out of nowhere — same atoms, same wiring, but no parents, no ancestors, no history at all — there'd be no thoughts or meanings inside it. It would walk around saying "I'm hungry" and dodging traffic, but nothing in there would be about anything.
5. Meaning gets locked in by history and stays put. Once a signal was built to track X, it means X forever, even if X disappears and the thing now reliably fires at something else instead.

What's bugging me is a bird I made up. Say there's a warbler whose alarm call got selected because it warns the flock about one particular big hawk. The hawk dies out. A new predator shows up, some ground cat, and the same alarm now goes off for the cat and still saves lives. My gut says the alarm now means the cat — it's doing the job of tracking the cat. But rule 5 says it still means hawk, full stop. Then a friend jumped in and said, hold on, what was it selected for in the first place — the hawk, or "big dangerous thing", or "predator", or just "the shape that means run"? If the selection story doesn't actually pin that down, rule 3 starts looking shaky too. And another friend said rules 2 and 4 can't both be right, though I honestly don't see why.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, and how bad is each one? Please don't hand me a list where everything is flagged as a problem — I'm pretty sure at least one of the worries above is a non-issue and I'm just spooked by it. Order the real ones by how bad they are, tell me the cheapest fix for each and what I'd be giving up by taking it, and if there's a single change that would clean up the most at once, say which one.
