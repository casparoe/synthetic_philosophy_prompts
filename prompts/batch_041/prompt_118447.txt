There's a distinction I keep needing and I dont have a word for it. I'll put two claims at the same odds — I'd say each is about 70% likely — and yet I feel completely differently about them, and I think the feeling is tracking something real. The first is 70% because I have a pile of different pieces of evidence pointing that way, none of them decisive alone, collected years apart. The second is 70% because of one decent study I read once, plus the fact that it sounds right. At 7:3 I'd take either bet at the same price, so by the numbers they're identical. But with the second, almost any new scrap of information moves me — one contrary anecdote and I'd flip — while the first is stubborn, new stuff just gets absorbed. And if I had to stake something important on one of them, or defend it in a hostile room, I'd take the first every time, even though I've just told you the odds are the same. A friend says this is just a second-order belief: I'm really less sure of my 70% in the second case, so I should be saying 60%. That doesn't feel right. I did price in my uncertainty, and if you pushed me again I'd still say 70%. So is there an established name for this gap between how probable a claim is and how much evidence is standing behind that probability? I care less about the label than about how the standard treatment carves it up: what exactly is supposed to differ between my two cases, is the difference in me or in the evidence, and how does it come apart from the Ellsberg sort of thing where people simply dislike betting when the odds are unknown?
