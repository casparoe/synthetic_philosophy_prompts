I'm assembling my own take on moral worth for a seminar next week and I want to check it hangs together before I commit to it out loud. Here's where I've landed: (1) an action has moral worth only if it's done from the motive of duty rather than merely in accordance with it; (2) acting out of love or loyalty can be morally worthy too -- a parent who looks after her kid without ever once framing it as her duty isn't doing something less good for that; (3) what confers worth is acting for the right reasons, and I take the right reasons to be the ones the situation actually gives you, not the ones you believe you have; (4) moral worth has to be entirely within the agent's control -- I don't see how you can earn or lose it by luck; (5) supererogatory acts, when someone actually does them, are at least as worthy as obligatory ones. Some of these I've held for years and some I only half beleive. Tell me which ones actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and rank the real tensions by how serious they are -- and for each one, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me?
