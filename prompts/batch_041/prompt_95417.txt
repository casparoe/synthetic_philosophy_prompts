For a while now I've been arguing with a friend about whether it makes sense to say that a chatbot believes things — that it takes some city to be the capital, that it wants to be helpful, that it is trying to get the answer right. We have each dug in, and I would rather turn the fight into something more useful. He takes what I will call the stance view: to say the system believes something is just to adopt the handiest way of predicting and making sense of what it outputs, and if that way of talking earns its keep, that is the whole of what it means — there is no further fact about whether it really believes anything. I take the realist view: believing is a real property, and a system has it only if its states are answerable to reasons — if it can be wrong, can come to see that it is wrong, and changes its mind for the reasons themselves and not for the dressing-up of them — which I doubt these systems can do.

Write the joint statement the two of us could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims we both accept about what the system's behavior actually is and about what belief-talk is doing when it works; the exact remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; the cruxes; and for each crux a concrete case or test we would both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I keep returning to one test — put a decisive argument against something the system just said, and watch whether it revises, and whether it revises for the reason or merely because of how the argument was worded — but I cannot work out how to turn that into a fair experiment.

One suspicion I cannot settle on my own: we may mean different things by the word believes — I a standing fact about the system, he a way of forecasting its next output — and if that is part of it, say which parts of our dispute are really about words. Separate what is genuinely settled from what is contested among people who know this area, and do not hand me a consensus where there isn't one.
