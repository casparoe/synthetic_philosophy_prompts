I work on evaluation infrastructure for machine-learning systems: benchmark harnesses, held-out test sets, contamination and leakage audits, threat models, and blinding procedures for human raters. I know the Turing test chiefly as an evaluation protocol, and I have always read the extrasensory-perception discussion in Turing's 1950 Mind paper as an embarrassing period piece — the one place where a founding document of my field lets a crank hypothesis into the design spec, before the serious business of learning machines gets under way.

I want that reaction tested rather than confirmed. Restate the ESP objection as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary — not as an analogy but as a genuine translation. Treat the interrogator as a rater, the separated rooms and the teleprinter link as an isolation boundary, the interrogator's verdict as a measurement, and the possibility of telepathy as a threat to the soundness of that measurement. Be explicit about the shape of the threat model: where the channel runs, who is source and who is receiver, what observable signature the confound would leave, and what a hardened harness would have to do about it. Since Turing proposes that a valid version of the game would require housing the interrogator somewhere impervious to telepathic influence, and remarks on how difficult such shielding would be, say what that proposal amounts to as an engineering requirement rather than as a curiosity. Also state plainly what he takes the evidence for the threat to be and how strong he takes it to be, because that determines whether the comparison with ordinary leakage is fair.

Then give me the residue report. What in his argument translates without loss? What survives only in distorted form — if my notion of leakage presupposes a physical carrier and his worry is precisely that no physical isolation suffices, say so and say what the distortion costs. And what has no counterpart at all in my vocabulary: is there anything in his treatment that evaluation engineering cannot express, and if so, is that a gap in my framework or evidence that his argument was never as precise as it looks?

Two verdicts I want specifically. First, whether his acceptance of the statistical case for telepathy is doing real work in the argument or is decoration that could be stripped out without changing the conclusion — that is, whether some version of the objection survives for someone who assigns psi a negligible prior. Second, whether the residue includes a demand I do not and cannot meet: that a test be sound against every possible channel, not merely against those in a plausible threat model. If that is what he is asking for, tell me whether it is a reasonable requirement on an evaluation protocol or an impossible one that no protocol could ever satisfy.
