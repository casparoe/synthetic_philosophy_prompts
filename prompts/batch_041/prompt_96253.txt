I chair the standing methodology committee of a national public-health agency, and I need the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's release meeting. Our charter permits us to state a finding publicly only when it is supported to at least 95% confidence. The report now before us bundles roughly thirty component findings -- each independently assessed above that threshold -- into a single executive summary asserting them all jointly: that the intervention is safe, that it is effective, that it is cost-effective, and so on through the list. The director intends to publish that summary as one claim. Two members object that the probability of the conjunction is far below 95%, so on our own charter the summary cannot be stated at all; two others reply that since every component is already assertable, the summary merely restates what we have licensed. There is a further wrinkle I want you to address: our annual report has always carried a standing note that some claim in it is probably mistaken, and no one supposes that note makes the report unspeakable -- yet the same reasoning that would forbid the summary would forbid the annual report too. Please recommend a concrete course of action for Thursday -- publish the summary, publish only the components separately, or publish the summary with stated qualifications -- and give me a rule the committee can apply to the next report, with an honest account of the strongest case against your recommendation.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating of the question.
