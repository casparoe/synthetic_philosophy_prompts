I was at a friends place last week and two people there, Maya and Dev, got into this long argument about social construction, and I've been chewing on it since. Can you work through it with me and say who's got the better of it at each stage? I really want the step-by-step, not just an overall verdict.

Dev kicked off with: money is the clean example everyone uses — a dollar bill is just paper, it's worth something only because we all treat it as worth something, so its value is constructed. Maya said sure, but that's exactly why the money example is a trap, because "socially constructed" gets used for two totally different things. Money being constructed is basically just a convention, everyone's in on it, and it works. Race being constructed is nothing like that — nobody signed up, the categories get imposed on people, and they do harm. Bundling them under one label hides the difference.

Dev came back: the mechanism is the same though. In both cases the kind exists because of collective attitudes rather than some natural fact; how nice it is to be on the receiving end is a downstream moral difference, not a metaphysical one. He said if you only call the nasty ones "constructed," you've turned a descriptive claim into a euphemism for "bad."

Maya said no, look closer at money: for money there's an actual rule — this thing counts as money — and the rule is constitutive, it's what makes it money, and everyone who uses money knows the rule. There's no analogous accepted rule for race; the categories get assigned to people whether they accept them or not, and the assigned don't get a vote. So the money model predicts a symmetry and consent that race doesn't have.

Dev: but race does get used as exactly that kind of rule sometimes. Under apartheid or Jim Crow, "this person counts as white / as colored" is literally a rule determining what you may do — where you sit, who you marry, what school you attend. That's a status function if anything is, so the model fits fine.

Maya: then notice what's doing the work. The money rule is public, iterated, and everyone, including the broke, operates inside it as the rule, and the whole system is self-referential — a dollar is worth something because people will accept it as payment, and they accept it because it's worth something. Race isn't self-referential like that and the acceptance isn't mutual; the powerful write the rule and the classified just live under it. So the analogy smuggles in consent and reciprocity that arn't there.

Dev: fine, but that's an argument that they're constructed differently, not that one is constructed and the other isn't. And then — this is the bit I keep thinking about — he said he doesn't see how you ever get from "constructed differently" to "not constructed," unless you've already decided that constructed has to mean the consent-y thing.

I honestly can't tell who's winning.
