my friend is a graphic designer and she's got a bee in her bonnet about Neurath — the Vienna Circle guy, the one who wanted a language made entirely of pictures and who liked to say that "words divide, pictures unite". She reckons we already have one, so the "is a picture language even possible" question is closed: airport signage, IKEA instructions, the little care symbols on a shirt label, emoji. If a flat-pack manual can walk you through a wardrobe with zero words, and every airport on earth can tell you where the emergency exit is no matter what language you speak, then a purely pictorial language isn't a pipe dream, it's just underrated. And she pushes it further: she says this shows the stuff only words can do is a defect rather than a feature — if a claim can't be drawn, it wasn't worth asserting. She also points out that Neurath's museum turned statistics into rows of little repeated icons, so the picture language was already handling serious content, not just toilets and wardrobes.

I want an audit of that, not a history lesson. Start with the correspondence table: what's playing the role of a word, a sentence, syntax, a proposition, a truth-condition, the meaning of a sign? Then tell me where the mapping breaks. I'm especially curious about the things pictures look structurally bad at — negation, either/or, if-then, all/some, tense, might — and anything you can't point at: electrons, inflation, next Tuesday, someone else's toothache. Also the particular-versus-general thing, since a drawing seems to show *that* cat rather than cats as such. Then the smuggled-in part: signage and manuals only work because they sit on top of a mountain of word-taught conventions and expectations, and because the reader can always fall back on asking a human or reading the small print, whereas a language has to stand on its own two feet.

So: does the existence of wordless signage tell us anything about whether a picture language could do the work of a verbal one, or only that pictures are good at a narrow set of jobs? And if her analogy does break, what would be a fairer comparison for what Neurath was actually after?

Also, say how confident you are in each part of that, and flag the bit your least sure about.
