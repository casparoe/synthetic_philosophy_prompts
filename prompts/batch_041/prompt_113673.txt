ok so I need a poem for a talk I'm giving, and I have to explain the setup because the whole thing depends on it. The microscopic laws of physics don't care which way time runs — play any interaction backwards and its still a legal sequence — but the world obviously does care: coffee cools, eggs don't unscramble, we remember yesterday and not tomorrow. The standard line is that the asymmetry isn't in the laws at all but in the starting conditions: the universe began in a wildly low-entropy state and everything since has just been the climb back up. So the arrow is a boundary condition wearing a law's clothes.

What I want is a poem, 14-20 lines, that can be read top to bottom and bottom to top. Both readings have to be grammatical and make sense, but they shouldn't say the same thing: read one way it should be events happening — causes, a before and an after, something at stake; read the other way the same words should go flat, a description with the story subtracted, a list of things just sitting there. The reversal is the point, and the difference between the two readings should be exactly the thing you don't get from the laws. If you can manage it, put one of those words that means opposite things depending on which way you take it — still, left, fast — at the hinge, and let it do opposite work in the two readings. No entropy talk, no "arrow of time" cliches, no italics or notes explaining it to me: if the reader needs the explanation the poem's failed. Meter and rhyme are your call, subject is your call, but I have to be able to read it backwards line by line without squinting.
