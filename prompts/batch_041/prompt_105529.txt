I am writing a short paper for a seminar on self-knowledge and belief, and a classmate has given me an argument that I cannot shake. He says Moorean sentences are not merely awkward to assert but semantically contradictory, and he thinks this follows from premises no one denies. The argument runs: (1) A sincere assertion of a declarative sentence is a commitment to the truth of that sentence. (2) A sincere assertion also expresses the beliefs that the sentence, in the speaker's mouth, is about. (3) The sentence It is raining but I do not believe that it is raining has two conjuncts; in asserting it, one is committed to both. (4) The first conjunct, It is raining, when asserted, expresses the speaker's belief that it is raining. (5) So in asserting the whole sentence, the speaker is committed to believing that it is raining and to not believing that it is raining. (6) No one can be committed to a contradiction. (7) Therefore the sentence is contradictory, not merely unassertable. (8) Therefore it cannot be true. (9) But I can describe a case in which it is true: a person who has excellent evidence that it is raining, whose belief-forming capacities are temporarily blocked by a denial syndrome, and who says, without irony, It is raining but I do not believe that it is raining. She seems to get her own case right. If she does, (8) is false.

I want to know which step to reject, and what rejecting it costs. I am attached to (1) and (9), and (4) seems to me to be the whole point of calling assertion the expression of belief, so if I have to give something up I would rather not give up (4). But I also do not want to say that the sentence is contradictory, because then I cannot explain the denial case. Tell me where the argument goes wrong, whether the flaw is an equivocation between what a sentence asserts and what a speaker expresses, or something else, and then say what happens to the higher-order variant I believe it is raining but I do not believe that I believe it is raining, and to the credence variant It is raining but my credence that it is raining is low. In each case I want to know whether the same diagnosis carries over or whether the argument has to be rebuilt, and which premise of the original argument would have to be weakened to accommodate it.
