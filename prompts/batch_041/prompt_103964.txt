I cover science and ideas for a general-readership magazine, and over years of reporting on artificial intelligence I have picked up a working but secondhand fluency in machine learning — training distributions, memorization versus generalization, interpretability probes, the difference between what a system carries in its weights and what it fetches at inference — though I have never trained a model myself. I would like the Meno problem, the question of why knowledge should be worth more than a true belief that guides action just as reliably, restated as faithfully as the terms allow in that vocabulary: true belief as an output a system can produce without being able to reproduce, knowledge as a disposition carried in the weights, and Plato's image of the statues that must be tied down as whatever keeps the disposition from escaping. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the puzzle survive the translation intact, which survive only in distorted form, and which have no counterpart at all — in particular, can the demand that the knower be able to give an account of the reason why be expressed in a framework that describes competence without a knower? Finally, tell me whether that residue marks a limit of my vocabulary or instead exposes something the machine-learning picture quietly denies.
