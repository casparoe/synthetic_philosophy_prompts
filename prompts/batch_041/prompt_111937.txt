Write me a short story that dramatises the way knowledge can fail to close — the sort of thing Nozick's tracking view lets happen — but I don't want a lecture in a lab coat, I want an actual story with a plot and people in it.

Here's a setting you can use or riff on. It's a few decades from now and there's a cheap consumer gadget (call it whatever you like; I've been calling it a "counterfactual lens" but you'll do better) that shows you what you yourself would have believed if things had gone slightly differently. Its got a dial on it — a "radius" — that sets how different the scenario is. Turn it right down and it shows you what you'd have believed if the traffic light had been red; crank it up and it shows you what you'd believe in a world where you never had a body at all. And here's the catch that makes it interesting: the reading changes depending on where you set the radius, so whether your belief "tracks" the truth turns out not to be one tidy fact about you — it depends on how you dial it.

I want the story to follow someone whose job needs her to chain her knowledge together — a structural engineer signing off a bridge, a surgeon, a forensics auditor, pick whatever fits — someone who knows each link in a chain and is therefore perfectly sure she knows where the chain ends. Put her in front of the lens, and let her discover the awkward thing: she knows the individual links in the tracking sense, but she can't know the conclusion, and the gadget is showing her exactly why, differently at different radii. Let her work out what to do when a life depends on the conclusion while only the links are "known". Give me at least one other character who thinks closure is obviously fine and argues with her about it — no villain, just two reasonable people.

Don't explain the philosophy at me — no named theories in the prose, no lecturer standing off to one side. Let the situation do the work. British English please, and keep it under about 1,500 words.
