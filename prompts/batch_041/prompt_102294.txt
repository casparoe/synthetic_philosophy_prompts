Me and a couple friends started this little movie-and-music club where the whole point is arguing about whether something is real art or just entertainment, and I've been reading up on Tolstoy's take on it (the "What Is Art?" thing, or a summary of it anyway) because it matched my gut better than anything else I'd come across — that art is when somebody's actual feeling gets passed into you, that it's good art when the feeling is a good one and it gets through to everyone plainly, and that fake art is stuff that just imitates or shows off or borrows someone else's move. But when I line it up against my own reactions everything tangles, and now I want to know what principle actually fits, even if it means giving up on some of my reactions.

Here's how I come out on things. My grandmother's lullaby, which she made up herself, slightly out of tune: real art, no question. A very clever atonal string quartet that I can't follow for the life of me but the two music students in our group swear by: I want to say not art, or at least not for me, but I can't tell if I'm just locked out of it. A forger who paints a whole "lost" Vermeer from scratch and it's beautiful and it moves me — then I find out it's fake and it goes dead for me, so: not art. A cheesy patriotic march that gives me chills and also makes me a little ashamed of myself: art, but bad art. A cover of a song that wrecks me harder than the original ever did: art, and I don't care that it's borrowed. A jingle for a local mattress store that's been stuck in my head for 15 years: I refuse to call it art even though something clearly got transmitted. And the one that broke my brain — a computer program that writes music, nobody behind it, and it made my friend cry, and she still calls it art, and I genuinely don't know. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? If Tolstoy's is nearest, tell me which clauses I'd have to loosen and which of my verdicts I'd have to drop, and then what the repaired principle predicts about something I haven't listed, like a piece that only moves me because I've been told it's a masterpiece.
