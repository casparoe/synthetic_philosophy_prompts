I am a PhD student in philosophy working on just war theory. At a seminar on the moral equality of combatants, a colleague waved away the objection that soldiers fighting for an unjust cause are not the moral equals of those fighting for a just one by saying, 'All's fair in love and war.' I suspect the slogan is performing several different jobs at once, and that only some of them bear on the doctrine. Please lay out the precise claims that one-liner could be making — from the trivial and descriptive to the radical and normative — and say for each whether it is plausible, whether it is compatible with the moral equality of combatants, and whether it would actually support using it to dismiss the objection. I am particularly interested in whether it amounts to a symmetry thesis (combatants on both sides have the same permissions), an independence thesis (the justice of the war makes no difference to conduct within it), a claim about equal liability to attack, or something stronger, such as the suspension of all moral and legal restraint. Distinguish what the slogan strictly entails from what it merely evokes, and end by saying which reading the colleague most likely needed. No bibliography or citations, please; I want the conceptual menu. Answer in British English.
