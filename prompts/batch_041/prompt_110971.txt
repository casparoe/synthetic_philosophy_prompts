Some friends and I have a small reading group going on the idea that you can have competence without comprehension — the Darwin/Turing move where something behaves in a smart-looking way but the real explanation of the behavior runs backwards from the way we'd naturally tell it (no designer, no rule-grasper, just a process doing the work). One person in the group keeps raising what she calls the "cheap trick" objection: everytime someone offers a case of competence without comprehension, the comprehension has just been relocated, either into the person who built the thing or into whatever process shaped it, so nothing has actually been shown. I want to see if a really clean case can beat that objection, and I'd like you to construct it for me.

Here's the spec I want you to hit. Give me a matched pair of systems — and I mean matched, they take the same inputs and give the same outputs, and they're equally reliable across the whole task you've defined, so that no experiment you could run on either system could tell you which was which. Exactly one of them has an internal model that is what makes its outputs correct; the other one's correctness has to be fully explained without any representation of the task anywhere inside it. Both have to be artifacts — no people, no animals, neither of them can be a clever human quietly standing in for the system, that's cheating for our purposes. And it has to be something two of us could actually build in a garage over a weekend out of hardware-store junk; no evolution by natural selection, no machine-learning training runs, no demons or perfect predictors, nothing that quietly needs a supercomputer or a few billion years. Also please stay off the obvious examples — no Chinese room, no "thermostats have beliefs," no AlphaGo, no slime molds solving mazes, I've heard all of those and they're not what I'm after.

Once you've built the pair, do the demonstration: walk through what makes the non-comprehending one non-comprehending given that its behavior is indistinguishable from its twin's, then tell me which single feature of the case is carrying the load, what happens if I remove or tweak that feature (does it just collapse back into ordinary design?), and how the cheap-trick objector goes after it — where exactly does she say the comprehension got relocated, and what's your reply. If some part of the spec turns out to be unsatisfiable, say so plainly and explain why, rather than quietly dropping the constraint.
