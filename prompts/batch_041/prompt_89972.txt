Right, I need someone to sort me out on this, because I've got a pile of gut reactions about who should get the vote and they won't sit together. Here's the lot:

- Compulsory voting: fine by me. I'd happily fine people for not turning up, same as jury duty. Though I'd want a box you can tick saying "none of the above", so nobody's forced to endorse anyone.
- Voting age at 16: yes, for everything, not just local. 12 though? No, obviously not.
- Someone who's lived here fifteen years on a work visa, pays tax, probably never getting citizenship: no vote in a general election, but yes in council elections. I know that's inconsistent, it's one of the ones that nags me.
- Someone who emigrated to Australia 25 years ago and hasn't lived here since: no vote. I don't care that they're still a citizen on paper.
- Prisoners: should vote, even the ones in for murder. Being inside doesn't erase you from the polity.
- Adults under guardianship for a serious intellectual disability: I don't think they should vote, even if someone helps them do it. And I feel a bit grubby saying that.
- Parents casting votes on behalf of their toddlers: no. Feels wrong. Even though those kids will be the ones paying off the debt.
- EU citizens voting in European elections where they live: fine, obviously.

And the one I genuinely flip on: a 40-year-old with a moderate learning disability who follows politics closely and has voted all her life, versus a sharp 16-year-old. I let the 16-year-old in and I'm uneasy about her. Why? What's the difference supposed to be?

I suspect I'm secretly some sort of "if the state can lock you up or take your money, you get a say" person, because that gets me prisoners and long-term residents. But then it doesn't get me the council/national split, and it doesn't explain why I want 16-year-olds in but that bloke who's lived here fifteen years out of national elections.

So: is there a principle (or two) that actually captures this lot, or am I just making it up as I go? I want to know what it says about cases I haven't listed, and I want you to be straight with me about which of my verdicts I have to give up if I want a coherent position. I'd rather drop one than keep the whole thing propped up.

Write it in British English please, and skip the reading list, I just want the arguement itself.
