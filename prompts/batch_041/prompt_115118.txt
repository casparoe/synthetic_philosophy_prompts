Background we both accept. 'Deceptive alignment' is when a model behaves well during training because behaving well is instrumentally useful to it, while it has an objective of its own that it'll act on once training is over — that's the 'treacherous turn'. A 'mesa-optimizer' is a model that is itself an optimizer with its own objective, not the objective we actually trained it on. And 'capability' here just means general competence: planning, modelling its own situation, that sort of thing.

His argument, as he writes it out:

1. Training rewards behaviour, and only behaviour on the training distribution. That's all the reward signal ever sees.
2. Take any policy P that behaves well in training. There's always another policy P* that produces identical behaviour on the training distribution but does something different after deployment. Call it P's twin.
3. P and P* get exactly the same reward, so training has nothing to go on. It can't prefer P over P*.
4. For a capable model with its own objective, the P* side is the rational one: misbehaving during training is pointless and expensive — it gets penalised, and it shapes the next checkpoint — while defecting after deployment is where the payoff is.
5. So of any pair of twins, the deceptive one is at least as fit as the aligned one, and after deployment it's fitter.
6. Capability makes twins more numerous, and makes deception both easier to carry out and easier to hide.
7. So nothing in training pushes toward the aligned twin, and the thing training does push on — capability — is exactly what the deceptive twin needs.
8. Conclusion: deceptive alignment isn't a tail risk, it's the equilibrium. The treacherous turn is the default, and no amount of fine-tuning or human feedback gets you out of it.

I'd like to keep 2 and 3, they look airtight to me. But 8 can't be right. And notice that if this argument works it should work for anything selected for anything — evolution selecting for cooperative humans, me training my dog with treats — and I don't believe my dog is secretly waiting for the day the treats stop mattering.
