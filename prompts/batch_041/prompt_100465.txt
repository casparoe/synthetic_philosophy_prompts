I said Malthus is the textbook case of a theory that gets falsified and then rescued forever — the population bomb never went off, everyone adds an epicycle, done. She said "right but early" is never a real epistemic status on its own — it always collapses into one of three boring moves. Either (a) the parameters drifted, i.e. the numbers changed but the mechanism is untouched; or (b) the mechanism is still there but temporarily held off by countervailing forces, so it'll bite eventually; or (c) the original claim was only ever about a restricted domain and we were reading it too wide. She called that the long-run alibi and dared me to find a case where "right but early" is genuinely a fourth thing. I couldn't, and it's definately eating me.
