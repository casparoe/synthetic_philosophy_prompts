I retired from the lab a few years back and have been catching up on philosophy ever since, and one thing that stuck with me is that crane vs skyhook business — the idea that a decent explanation does its lifting from the ground up, built out of ordinary lower-level parts (a crane), versus the kind that just hangs there in midair and gets treated as if it were a basic fact (a skyhook). And the cousin of it, "greedy reductionism", where you get impatient and skip all the intermediate scaffolding, declaring the top level just is the bottom level.

I'd like a joke about this. Not the kind where you just say the words and expect a laugh — an actual short bit, a little scene or two-hander, where the funny comes from the real content. Maybe someone peddling a skyhook as though it were a crane (a "Skyhook & Sons, Est. Since Forever" outfit would tickle me), or a greedy reductionist who's irritated that the crane insists on all these boring incremental steps when you could just hoist the whole load in one go. Around 150-250 words. And please don't wrap up by explaining the distinction to me like I'm a undergrad — I get it, I want to laugh.
