The question was: "Explain the problem of the unity of the proposition, show how Bradley's regress is supposed to generate it, and assess whether the regress is vicious." This is what he wrote:

"Propositions are the things that can be true or false, and they seem to be made up of parts: in 'the cat is on the mat' the proposition contains the cat, the mat, and the relation of being-on. But if a proposition is just a bundle of its parts, then what makes it a single thing that says something, rather than a mere list? Call that the problem of the unity of the proposition. Bradley's regress is supposed to show that you cannot build the unity out of the parts by adding a relation. Suppose the proposition 'aRb' is unified by the relation R holding between a and b. But R is itself a part, and for it to connect a and b it has to be connected to them, which needs a further relation R1 between R, a and b; and then R1 needs a relation R2, and so on. So the attempt to explain unity by appeal to a relating relation generates an infinite regress. Bradley thought this showed that relations are not ultimately real — that the very idea of a plurality of related things is incoherent — and that reality must therefore be a single seamless whole, which is roughly the view he calls the Absolute. The same structure is basically the Third Man argument in Plato's Parmenides, where the Form is introduced to explain the resemblance of particulars and then needs a further Form to explain its resemblance to them, so Bradley's regress is really just that old argument in a new setting. The standard move at this point is to say the regress is vicious, so nothing can be a proposition; but the more common modern line is to deny that the regress is vicious at all, and to hold that the unity of the proposition is sui generis. Frege is usually credited with solving the problem, and his solution is the sense/reference distinction: the proposition 'the cat is on the mat' has a sense (the mode of presentation) and a reference (the truth value), and the sense is what unifies the parts. Wittgenstein in the Tractatus takes a different line, treating the proposition not as a thing with parts but as a fact, i.e. a configuration of names, so that there is no need for a further relation to glue them together. So on balance the regress is not vicious; it just shows that you shouldn't try to analyse propositional unity relationally. The point of the problem is that the unity has to be primitive."
