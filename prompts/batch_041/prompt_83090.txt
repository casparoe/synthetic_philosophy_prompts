So I've been reading alot about futarchy lately and I can't tell if it's brilliant or insane. Short version of how I understand it, so we're on the same page: instead of voting on policies, you vote on a metric you want to go up — life expectancy, median income, whatever — and then prediction markets decide which policy to run, by betting on whether the metric goes up more under policy X than under not-X. You vote on what you want, markets figure out how to get it.

Write me a dialogue between two people arguing about this. One is genuinely convinced it fixes a real failure mode of ordinary democracy and has thought hard about why the objections fail. The other is a serious skeptic who also knows the proposal cold. Neither is a strawman, and they should actually respond to each other's specific points instead of talking past each other — I want to see them concede things and then re-attack, not just trade slogans.

I especially want them to get into the thing that's been bugging me. In the real world you only ever see one branch. The market says "the metric is higher if we do X," so we do X, and then we only ever get to observe the X world. So how does that bet actually settle? Whoever resolves the market has to work out the counterfactual some other way — a model, a control group, someone's forecast of what would've happened — and then haven't we just swapped a fight about policy for a fight about the model? Is that fatal, or just an engineering detail that some clever contract design fixes? I want them to really dig into that one.

If it fits naturally, let them also scrap over: whether these markets are actually good at what they're good at (aggregating spread-out private info, like who wins an election) but bad at questions that need a shared causal model (does this tax hike grow the economy); what happens when the effect you care about shows up in ten years and everyone's already cashed out; who sets the metric in the first place and why that step doesn't just collapse back into a normal vote; and what stops everyone from gaming the number the second it becomes the target.

Keep it as two people talking, not a lecture with two mouths.
