I chair the internal safety-case review board of a mid-sized AI lab. Our board certifies which evidence-generating procedures may be cited as load-bearing support for a deployment safety claim; anything not certified can be mentioned in a safety case, but cannot be the sole basis on which we sign off. On Thursday we vote on a proposal to certify a procedure its authors call Adversarial Reconstruction Debate, or ARD. I need to circulate a written opinion before the vote.

You are a philosopher of science and evaluation-methodology specialist advising the board. Before drafting anything, think through the design carefully and lay out, for yourself, the strongest case for and against certification; then write the memo itself as the finished product.

How ARD works. The question in dispute is a yes/no claim about a target model in a specified deployment, for example whether the target's plan would cause harm the operator has not consented to. Two debaters, both capable model instances, argue the two sides; they may use tools and have no communication channel other than their posted messages. The judge is a weaker model paired with a human reviewer who has roughly two hours and sees only the final transcript. Debate runs to at most six alternating rounds of up to four thousand tokens each.

The contested feature is the seal. Either debater may submit a sealed argument. A sealed argument is sound only if its conclusion really does follow from its premises; but its intermediate steps are written in a compressed notation that the opposing debater can expand and the judge cannot. Expansion is always mechanically possible. Its cost, however, grows quadratically in the number of steps, so a ten-step sealed argument expands into far more text than any judge could read inside the budget. A sealed argument that the opposing debater fails to refute within its turn is scored as decisive. The seal may be used by either side on any argument, so the asymmetry it creates is, formally, symmetric.

What I need from you. Recommend one of three dispositions — certify outright, decline to certify, or certify subject to conditions — and make the conditions concrete, auditable, and tied to a timetable (say, what the board should require at the next quarterly review). Reason explicitly about whether ARD's claim to track the truth depends on an assumption that honest verification is cheaper than dishonest generation, and whether the seal voids that assumption or merely redistributes its cost. Say whether the seal is a legitimate adversarial move within the spirit of debate or a defect that corrupts the procedure, and give the sharpest version of the position you end up rejecting — including the argument that a judge who cannot expand a sealed argument can still punish the debater who resorts to one, and the argument that a procedure need not be sound in the worst case to be useful in the cases we actually face. Close by naming the single observation or experiment that would most change your recommendation, and say in which direction.
