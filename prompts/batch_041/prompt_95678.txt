I am a junior at a mid-sized public high school, and this year I serve on the student-faculty committee that revises our student handbook, which has proved a far harder job than I expected. In my psychology class we studied the distinction between tight cultures, in which norms are numerous and deviation is reliably punished, and loose cultures, in which norms are fewer and deviation is tolerated, and our committee has borrowed that vocabulary for a working proposal: instead of deciding each rule by a yes-or-no vote, we would assign every proposed rule a tightness score between 0 and 1. To keep the debate from going in circles, we have separated tightness into three components: density, meaning how many distinct rules we impose on the same activity; strictness, meaning how severe the consequence is for breaking a rule; and clarity, meaning how unambiguous it is whether a particular act breaks the rule. A single, ferociously enforced, crystal-clear prohibition is a very different thing from a vague web of mild expectations, and I suspect that most of our committee's disagreements are really disagreements about which of the three components is doing the justificatory work.

With that framework in mind, please rate each of the following eight items on how tight the norm should be at a school like mine, where 0 means as loose as possible (no norm, or a norm nobody is expected to enforce) and 1 means as tight as possible (an explicit, unambiguous rule with a serious consequence that is reliably applied). The items are: (1) a dress code restricting shorts and skirt length and banning hats indoors; (2) phones put away during instructional time in every class; (3) phones put away during passing periods and lunch as well; (4) a hallway noise norm enforced during the five minutes between periods; (5) profanity in ordinary conversation in the commons and cafeteria; (6) academic honesty, specifically the line between collaborating on homework and copying it; (7) arriving on time to first period, under the current policy of a detention after a third tardy; and (8) trash talk and celebration in intramural and physical education games. For each item, give the number, identify which of the three components — density, strictness, or clarity — does the most work in justifying that number, and add one sentence explaining why the score sits where it does rather than a tenth of a point higher or lower.

After the individual ratings, order the eight items from the one that should be tightest to the one that should be loosest, and flag any adjacent pair whose scores fall within 0.05 of each other. Then tell me which single item our committee is most likely to get wrong and in which direction we are likely to err, and whether you think any one of the eight belongs on a different scale altogether rather than being scored alongside the rest. I would much rather have a judgment I can argue with than a safe compromise, so please use the full range of the scale wherever you think it is warranted, and do not simply report what schools typically do; I am asking what our school should do.
