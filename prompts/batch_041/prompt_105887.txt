I am not an academic and have no degree in this field, but I have been reading about prisons on my own for about a year, largely because I kept being told that the choice is between punishment and rehabilitation and that framing never seemed to describe what actually struck me. I would like to apply for a small foundation grant that supports independent writing, and the application requires a short research proposal of roughly two pages.

My half-formed idea is this. The contrast usually drawn between the Norwegian approach and American mass incarceration is punishment versus rehabilitation. But what stands out to me in descriptions of Norwegian prisons is neither retribution nor therapy. It is a different picture of what a prison sentence is: the sentence is treated as an interruption of an ordinary life rather than a removal into a separate world. The day is meant to resemble the day outside, work and study and family contact continue, and the staff are meant to be part of ordinary life rather than a separate caste. People describe this as making prison life normal, and I notice that this is not the same as making it rehabilitative. A person could have a very normal day and still leave no better than they came in, and a person could be reformed under a regime that resembles nothing outside. Nor is it simply the same as being lenient: I have read critics say that the flatness and boredom of a normal-seeming day can be its own kind of punishment, and that making confinement resemble freedom can be more disorienting than an honest wall.

So my question is whether the real difference between these two systems is a difference in the aim of punishment — desert on one side, reform on the other — or a difference in the more basic idea of what the institution is: a place that holds you apart, versus a place that keeps your life going. And if it is the second, does that idea have a justification of its own, or is it only a humane-sounding way of describing comfortable confinement?

Please turn this into a proposal. It should state the question so that it has a determinate answer rather than a mood; explain why the standard punishment-versus-rehabilitation framing leaves it open; set out how I could actually make progress on it with the time and means I have, given that I cannot run a study or gather data and this has to be an argument worked out on paper; say what a real result would look like and what would count as the project failing to establish anything; and give an honest account of scope for a two-page piece written over a few months by someone outside the field. Please do not include citations or a reading list; I will track down sources myself.
