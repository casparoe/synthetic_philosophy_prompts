Until two years ago I lived somewhere that 'lemonade' meant the clear, fizzy drink and 'cider' meant something alcoholic; I now live where the first names a cloudy still drink and the second names a juice, and I have caught myself standing in front of the same sort of bottle thinking thoughts I could not have thought before, with nothing inside my head having changed. That has made the question of what fixes the content of a word or a thought newly personal, and I would like the specification before I read any further. Write me the desiderata that any adequate theory of content must meet: the cases it has to get right (Twin Earth and its variants, slow switching, deference, indexicals and self-location, empty and fictional terms), the distinctions it must not collapse (content versus reference, the speaker's meaning versus the word's meaning, narrow versus broad content), and the constraints arriving from elsewhere (compositionality, the causal and explanatory role of content in psychology, first-person authority), each with the consideration that motivates it. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the prejudices of one camp, tell me which subset cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement eliminates the most otherwise attractive theories. A short scorecard for internalism, standard externalism, and two-dimensionalism is welcome if you keep it brief.
