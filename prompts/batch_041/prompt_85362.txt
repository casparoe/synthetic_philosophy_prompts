My oldest friend and I spent most of a long drive arguing about a scheme of his that I could not refute and could not accept, which is why I am setting it down here. The scheme sorts minds into four grades. The first is a creature that does the right thing because its ancestors' mistakes were paid for in advance, in its wiring, and it never learns anything itself. The second learns from its own experience, keeping what pays and dropping what stings. The third can run the trial and error inside itself, trying a few courses of action in imagination before committing, so that its mistakes cost it nothing. The fourth is the third plus a stock of tools it did not invent and could not have invented alone — words, numbers, maps, written rules, everything a culture hands down — so that its inner rehearsal is far stronger than its bare equipment would allow. He calls the four the Darwinian, the Skinnerian, the Popperian and the Gregorian grades, and he treats them as rungs on one ladder, each rung a property of the creature standing on it.

What I could not get him to place are the cases where someone stands on a rung that belongs to somebody else. My brother-in-law drives a city he has never learned; the routes were worked out by other people's trial and error and are read to him turn by turn, and he drives with the calm of a man who knows every street, though if the device dies he is lost. A junior doctor I know follows a sepsis protocol; it is the compressed residue of trials she could never run and does not understand in detail, and she is excellent with it and, I suspect, dangerous without it. A colleague of mine does not so much think about certain problems as ask a particular senior person and take the answer. None of them is doing the third-grade thing — the trying-out, the letting the hypotheses die in his stead — and none is quite doing the fourth-grade thing either, because in the fourth grade the rehearsal is still supposed to be theirs, merely better equipped. Here the rehearsal happened, or is happening, in another head, and only the conclusion is handed across; and when the other head belongs to a person with interests of his own rather than to a printed table, that strikes me as a difference in kind rather than in equipment.

So: is there a name for this? I am not asking for a label to file it under. I want to know what the thing is and where its edges fall. Are the satnav, the protocol, the colleague, the price on a market that summarizes thousands of other people's judgments, and the notes I left myself last year and now treat as gospel one phenomenon or several? Which of them belong together, and on what principle? If a creature that lives this way deserves a fifth rung, what would it be called, and what would distinguish it from the fourth; if it is not a rung at all but only a way of leaning on rungs other people built, I want to see the argument for that too. And I would like to know what the idea predicts: does it say, for instance, that such a creature will be systematically wrong about how much it knows on its own, and that taking away its helper leaves it worse off than if it had never had one? If you can give me a case that would tell two readings of it apart, so much the better.
