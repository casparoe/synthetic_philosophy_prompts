I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training to speak of (one ethics elective in undergrad that I mostly slept through), and I've been going back and forth about this with a friend who's deep into effective altruism. I want to just lay out where my gut actually lands on a bunch of cases and have someone tell me what principle I'm implicitly running, because I'm pretty sure I'm being inconsistent somewhere and I can't find it myself.

Here's where my gut goes. Five dollar coffee: obviously fine. Four hundred dollar mechanical keyboard when a forty dollar one works: fine, I don't feel even a twitch. Used car at 9k instead of 4k: fine. Two week trip to Japan instead of donating that money: fine, definately fine. But skipping my cousin's wedding to go to a charity fundraiser where I'm not even needed: not fine, and I'd deserve whatever flak I got. Cancelling a standing dinner with a friend because something better came up: not fine. Giving 10% of my salary: that feels above and beyond, praiseworthy, not owed. Giving nothing at all, ever, spending everything on me and my family: feels wrong but I'd say I'm within my rights. Quitting a career I'm good at and enjoy to go do something with more impact: not required, no way. And then: if my brother needs 6k or he loses his apartment, I have to give it, no question. If I'm the only match for a stranger's bone marrow transplant, I think I have to do that too, even though it's a week of my life and a needle in my spine. I'd also drop 3k on surgery for my dog without blinking, and I'd have to think about 3k for a stranger's medical bill, which my friend says is monstrous and I sort of agree and sort of don't.

The two that don't fit for me. First, I set up one of those round-up donation things on my card at 1% and felt like a good person; then I realized I could set it to 20% and genuinely not notice the difference in my month, and I still felt like 20% was alot to ask and 1% was what a decent person does, and I can't defend that at all. Second, I'd fund my kid's piano lessons at 2k a year before I'd give 2k to something that saves a life, and if you make me say that arithmetic out loud, I'm saying piano lessons outrank a life, which I don't believe for a second. My friend says this just proves I'm a hypocrite and should give until it hurts; I don't buy that either, the world where everyone gives until it hurts sounds awful and also seems to make ordinary life, mine and the recipients', impossible.

So is there a principle that gets most of my verdicts? I've tried the simple maximize-the-good version (kills almost everything I called fine), I've tried a don't-actively-make-things-worse version (kills the bone marrow verdict and probably the brother one), and I've tried a I-owe-nothing-beyond-what-I'd-want-others-to-owe-me version (lets the never-donate verdict through, which I don't want). Give me a principle stated precisely enough that I can actually check it against these, tell me which of my verdicts it has to overturn and why, and say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed: taking a job that pays 80k more but does measurable harm, with the 80k going to effective charity. And if the honest answer is that my verdicts don't come from one principle, give me the two or three candidates and the tradeoff between them rather than a fudge. My friend keeps saying supererogatory and I just nod.
