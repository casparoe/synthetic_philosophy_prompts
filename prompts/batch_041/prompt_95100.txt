I'm a novelist at work on a book whose narrator is a lapsed seminarian, and for the past several months I've been reading Augustine on lying — the early dialogue on lying, and then the later book against lying that he wrote for Consentius in connection with the Priscillianists — partly for the narrator's cadence and partly because I've fallen into a difficulty I can't get out of. Augustine opens by setting jokes aside on the ground that the joker's tone and mood give evident indication that no deceit is intended, so that a joke is not a lie at all; yet by the time he grades lies into their several kinds, a jocose sort turns up in the list as the lightest of them, which seems to put the joke back among the lies after all, merely at the bottom of the heap. And the criterion he uses throughout is not the falsity of what is uttered but the doubleness of the heart — the intention to deceive — which means that fiction, false as it is, sits in the same sanctuary as the joke, while a man who states a truth he believes to be false lies in the strict sense, and a man who states a falsehood he believes true merely blunders. I want a joke that turns on this machinery rather than gesturing at it.

So: write me one. It should be a joke a working novelist could tell at a dinner party without sounding like he is reciting lecture notes, and it should be genuinely funny to someone who has actually read Augustine on this subject — the punchline should turn on the real content of his position, whether that is the double heart, the jocose exemption and its later demotion into the graded list, the fact that the helpful lie to save a life is finally refused even though it harms no one, or the odd consequence that the liar is judged by his intention and not by whether what he says is so. It should not be a generic joke about saints, bishops, or the impossibility of honesty in publishing. One version is enough if it lands; if you think the material supports two different jokes, one turning on the definition of lying and one on the ranking of lies, give me both and say in a sentence which you think is stronger and why. No citations, no bibliography — I know the texts, I want the joke.
