Semi-urgent, seminar's in two days. My grad students have all read the usual stuff on Pascal's mugging and they've settled into the same three moves — cap your utility function, refuse to maximize expected value under imprecise probabilities, or mumble something about fanaticism being irrational. I want you to argue the opposite: that none of that is needed, because the mugging isn't a well-posed offer in the first place. The mugger's "one in a googol" only means anything relative to a prior, and there's no canonical prior over the kind of hypothesis space he needs — a world with a god who punishes you for not handing over your wallet is one point among an endless spread of weird worlds, and nothing picks out its measure. So the honest reply is that the offer has no expected value at all, rather than that we should stop caring about expected value. Please make that argument properly, including the part my students will immediately throw back at me, which is that we assign tiny probabilities to bizarre hypotheses all the time (am I a Boltzmann brain? is the world five minutes old?), so your position has to explain why the mugger's hypothesis is definately different in kind and not just in degree. Don't just assert "no uniform prior on an infinite set" and walk away; show where the prior has to come from and why the defender can't simply stipulate one. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and dont restate the question.
