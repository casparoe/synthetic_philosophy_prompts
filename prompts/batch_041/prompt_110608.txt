I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for something like a decade, and I have grown tired of watching the same argument about insulated communities stall on vocabulary: everyone reaches for the echo chamber or the epistemic bubble, means something slightly different by it, and the thread turns into a fight about labels instead of cases. I want to replace that with an instrument. Please write me a decision tree — a diagnostic flowchart — that takes a stubborn group consensus as input and routes it to a named diagnosis.

Build it from the logical space, not from a list of names. Start from the one thing every case in this family shares — a consensus that has become insulated from outside challenge — and split on questions about the case. Every branch node must be a question someone could answer about a concrete community without already knowing which diagnosis it leads to; if a node can only be answered by someone who already has the label in hand, it is the wrong node. Place the names on the leaves only once the space is laid out, and say for each split what work it is doing.

The angle is mine, so use my cuts rather than the textbook ones. First, supply versus credibility: is the counter-evidence being kept from arriving, or is it arriving and being pre-discredited on contact? I will call these insulation and inoculation, and I want the tree to treat them as genuinely different mechanisms rather than as two degrees of the same thing. Second, who is turning the screw: is the distortion a by-product of the members' own dispositions and choices, or is it produced by someone — an algorithm, an editor, an authority — who benefits from it? Third, sincerity: are the members genuinely convinced, or privately doubtful and managing the cost of saying so? Fourth, visibility: does the community hold a false theory of its own practice, or does it know exactly what it is doing and endorse it? Fifth, symmetry: would the community's own dismissal-rule, turned on its own core claim delivered by an outsider, also dismiss that claim? A community that keeps its standards of rigor fully intact but applies them only outward seems to me a distinct diagnosis rather than a version of the others; call it outward-only skepticism and give it its own leaf.

I want at least these diagnoses to come out as leaves: plain informational isolation; inoculation by pre-discrediting, the paradigm echo chamber; algorithmic filtering that the member neither chose nor has any theory about; sincere private doubt that is publicly suppressed with everyone reading everyone else correctly; the same but with everyone misreading everyone else; and outward-only skepticism. Add whatever else the space demands.

Use these cases as test material and make sure each lands somewhere specific: a forum that banned a topic by open vote and can state its reasons; a group whose leader's failed prediction is reinterpreted as a test of commitment; a thread where an argument is waved off as concern-trolling from an outsider but welcomed from a regular; a server where each member assumes the others are convinced and none of them is; a reader whose feed is curated by a recommender and who holds no beliefs about why it shows what it shows.

Constraints: eight to twelve leaves, no path longer than four questions, and written for someone who has read a couple of blog posts and no epistemology — the questions should be answerable about a case, not by naming a theory. Note any combination of answers that no real community occupies, and say why it is empty — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unvisited. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate rather than papering over the gap.

Finally, tell me how confident you are that these splits carve the space rather than restating the labels — particularly the insulation/inoculation cut, which I suspect may be doing less work than I want it to — and single out the one leaf or split you are least sure of.
