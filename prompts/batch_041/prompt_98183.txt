I'm on my college's academic integrity board and we've got a case that's weird even for us. A student ran their draft through an LLM to get an outline and two topic sentences, then rewrote everything themselves. The professor says that's "substantial unauthorized assistance"; the student says it's just brainstorming. Our honor code uses the phrase "substantial unauthorized assistance" but never defines it, and we have two precedents that pull in different directions: a 2019 case where a student used a grammar checker and we found no violation, and a 2022 case where a student used a paraphrasing tool and we found a violation. Half the board thinks there's a fact of the matter about whether this case is substantial, we just have to figure it out; the other half thinks "substantial" is vague and that borderline cases like this have no determinate answer at all.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Monday's hearing. It should recommend a concrete outcome for this case -- violation or no violation, and if violation, what sanction -- plus a prospective rule for how we should handle similar LLM-assistance cases going forward. I also want the memo to say which meta-semantic framework we should use for interpreting "substantial": epistemicism (there's a sharp line, we just can't know where it is) or supervaluationism (borderline cases are truth-value gaps, and a rule counts as true only if every admissible way of making it precise agrees). Don't just pick the one that's convenient for the outcome you want; give the strongest case for the other side, and say what it would imply for this student. Keep it to about a page, and write it so the student and the professor could both recieve it without thinking we rigged the process. I don't need a literature review or citations -- just a clear recommendation, the reasoning, and a seperate section on the losing side's best point.
