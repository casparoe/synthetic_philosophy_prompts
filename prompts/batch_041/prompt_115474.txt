It's small money, like a few hundred bucks at a time, but we meet every other week and we always end up arguing about stuff nobody actually knows — will the robotics club's showcase sell enough tickets to cover the venue, will the new film club still exist in March, that kind of thing. We get maybe twenty minutes per proposal and the activities director has the final say, but she usually just goes with whatever we vote for. People keep saying things like 'I'd say there's a 70% chance' when they definately made the number up, and then we vote on the fake number.

I read a bit about imprecise probabilities, where instead of one number you're supposed to give the range you'd actually be willing to bet at, and it made me think our whole process is backwards. Can you write me a procedure I can actually run in a meeting? The questions to ask in order, how to turn somebody's made-up 70% into an honest range, when the ranges are so far apart that we should go get more info instead of voting, and what to do when the ranges are so wide that the decision barely depends on the probability at all — I think that's when you pick whatever isn't terrible in the worst case? Also how to tell when one option is better no matter what the real number is, versus when it's a genuine toss-up.

Then run it on two made-up proposals: a club asking for money for an event they swear will break even, and a brand new club that might fall apart by spring. I'm rooting for the film club one, so honestly it'd be useful to see the procedure tell me no. And tell me where the procedure stops helping and it's just us deciding what we care about, because I don't want to act like a checklist is making a values call for us.
