I'm a brand-new staff attorney at the state sentencing board, and part of my job is to guess what the commissioners are going to do with a case before they do it. I cant get a read on them, so I want your help.

Here's the track record, roughly, from the last couple years:

- Guy blew a .19, ran a red, killed a nine-year-old: 12 years.
- Guy blew a .21, ran the same red, nobody hurt, pulled over a block later: 10 years.
- Woman who skimmed 2.3 million off a charity over six years, no record, and by sentencing she'd lost her job, her license, her marriage, and was basically broke: 3 years.
- Man with six prior burglary convictions who took a laptop out of an unlocked car: 6 years.
- Plant supervisor who falsified a safety inspection report. Nothing blew up, nobody was hurt, but the file said basically every plant in that industry fudges these: 5 years.
- Nineteen-year-old lookout in an armed robbery where his friend shot the clerk dead. He says he didn't know about the gun. He got 3 years, the shooter got 25 to life.

My boss keeps saying the board is "retributivist, like everybody in this state," and I honestly dont see it. And one of the commissioners talks constantly about "hard treatment" versus "the message," which I don't fully follow, but it seems to matter to him.

The case in front of us: a 54-year-old woman with no record spent about four months planning to kill her ex-husband, and finally put what she thought was a lethal dose of a prescription sedative in his whiskey. She'd bought it off a website; it was a sugar pill. He woke up groggy, that's it. She confessed the next morning on her own and has been a wreck since — lost her job, her two kids won't speak to her, her ex has publicly forgiven her and asked us not to lock her up. No priors, no sign she'd ever do anything like this again, and the file notes that attempts like this basically never get reported or caught, so it's not as if anyone out there is watching this sentence and changing their behavior.

What do you think they'll give her? I'm less interested in the number than in what rule the board is actually running — I can see two or three different stories that fit all six of those cases — and which story this new case actually puts pressure on. Then tell me what kind of case I should be watching for that would settle it, because I'd rather know what's really driving this than keep guessing. No need for real cases or cites, just the logic.

Format, please:

1. The number — one line: your best guess in years, plus a one-sentence why.
2. The rules that fit their record — two or three of them, a couple lines each on how each one explains the six cases above.
3. What each rule says about her — a few lines per rule.
4. My best guess and why — one paragraph.
5. The case that would settle it — one paragraph: describe the case and what each rule would predict about it.
