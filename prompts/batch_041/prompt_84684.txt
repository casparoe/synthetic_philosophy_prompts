My friend and I got into a two-hour fight about Newton last night — he's convinced the theology is load-baring for the physics and I always figured it was just a pious appendix tacked on. We kept circling back to this bit from the General Scholium:

"For God is a relative word, and has a respect to servants; and Deity is the dominion of God, not over his own body, as those imagine who fancy God to be the soul of the world, but over servants. The supreme God is a Being eternal, infinite, absolutely perfect; but a being, however perfect, without dominion, cannot be said to be Lord God; for we say, my God, your God, the God of Israel, the God of Gods, and Lord of Lords; but we do not say, my Eternal, your Eternal, the Eternal of Israel, the Eternal of Gods; we do not say, my Infinite, or my Perfect: These are titles which have no respect to servants. The word God usually signifies Lord; but every lord is not a God. It is the dominion of a spiritual being which constitutes a God; a true, supreme or imaginary dominion makes a true, supreme or imaginary God."

I can parse the words fine but I don't feel like I actually get what he's doing. Why is he so insistent that "God" names a relation — dominion, being someone's lord — rather than a bundle of attributes, and what does he think hangs on that distinction? Help me understand the passage. Don't send me to a reading list, just explain the move and what it's aimed at. Keep it under 350 words.
