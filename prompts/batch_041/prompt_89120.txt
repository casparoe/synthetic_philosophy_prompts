Write a pantoum in the voice of a proponent of Gödel’s view, addressed to a physicist who has just defended the objectivity of becoming. The occasion is Gödel’s 1949 rotating-universe solution: a cosmos of rotating dust in which closed timelike curves pass through every point and no global time slice exists. Let the form do the argument: the interlocking repetitions should feel like a world-line that keeps returning to its own past, and the final quatrain should close the loop by bringing back the opening line, so the poem cannot exit the universe it describes. The poem should make clear that the difficulty is not merely that time travel is permitted but that the solution leaves no global now for the travel to cross, and it should end on the idealist conclusion about time—not as a slogan, but as something the loop has forced. Aim for five quatrains, metrical but not necessarily rhymed, and accurate enough that someone who knows the rotating-universe solution would recognize the physics rather than just the name.
