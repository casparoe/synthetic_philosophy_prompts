I'm in a philosophy of science class and we've just done Popper on verisimilitude, followed by the Tichý/Miller objection. I can follow the arguement but I can't tell whether it's a proof or a card trick, and it bothers me, because if it goes through then "science progresses toward the truth" isn't just false, it's unstatable — and so is the pessimistic meta-induction, since you can't say our successors will be further from the truth than we are if no false theory is closer to the truth than any other false theory.

Here's the reconstruction my study group came up with.

(1) Since even our best theories are false, Popper ranks them by content: A is at least as close to the truth as B iff every true consequence of B is a true consequence of A, and every false consequence of A is a false consequence of B. (More truth content, less falsity content.)

(2) Let A and B both be false. Take any true consequence t of A and any false consequence g of A. A entails t and it entails g, so it entails t ∧ g; and since g is false, t ∧ g is false, so it's part of A's falsity content. By (1) B entails it too — but t ∧ g entails t, so B entails t. Since t was arbitrary, every true consequence of A is a true consequence of B, which by (1) means A and B have exactly the same truth content.

(3) Our notes say the falsity content comes out the same as well. I couldn't get that half to work myself, but I'll take their word for it.

(4) So no false theory is closer to the truth than any other false theory. Newton, Einstein and the flat-earthers all come out exactly on a par, and a theory that's off by one percent comes out on a par with one that's off by a factor of a billion.

That can't be right, and I'd rather keep that than keep (1) — but I want to know what I'm paying. So which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost? Is the padding in (2) — wrapping a false consequence up with a true one so the true one gets smuggled into the falsity content — a legitimate use of "consequence", or is that where the sleight of hand is? Someone in my group reckons the fix is to count only consequences that are false in their own right rather than because of a bad conjunct, and that the people who measure distance between possible worlds never had this problem to begin with. Do either of those actually escape (2), or do they just pay the same bill later? And if it's (1) that has to go, what does the replacement have to look like to keep the Newton-beats-flat-earth judgement?
