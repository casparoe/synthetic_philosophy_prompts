I'm a backend engineer and I somehow ended up on our company's engineering standards council (about 200 engineers, monorepo, 40ish services). I have to write the written opinion that goes out before Thursdays vote and I'd rather not just wing it.

Background: we've got two competing conventions for the event payloads our services publish to the shared bus. Team A (the older services, most of them) uses one shape, and Team B built three new services last year using a different shape because nobody ever wrote the first one down. Honest answer: neither is better. Same typing, same perf, same everything. The only reason most services use Team A's shape is that the very first service used it and everyone copied it. Team B didn't know that, invented their own, and now we have two.

The council wants to pick one and be done. But Priya on the council keeps saying you can't do that. She read some old philosophy book about conventions and now she says a convention isn't a rule you vote on, it's just what everyone does because everyone expects everyone else to do it, and you only get one when people see other people already doing it and copy along. She says if we pass a resolution we'll end up with a document and two conventions. Raj says that's overthinking it: we're the standards council, standards are literally our job, we write it down, we announce it, people comply or their PR gets bounced.

There's also a wrinkle: the event bus is public-facing and about a dozen external partners consume it. All of them are on Team A's shape, and a few have told us they physically can't change for at least a year, if ever.

Write me the memo I can circulate. I need a concrete recommendation (which shape, or neither — say which and why), a timeline, and what actually makes it stick rather than just getting announced. Please take Priya seriously instead of just asserting the council's authority, because I think she's onto something and I can't articulate it. But also tell me what Raj is right about, since I don't think he's just wrong either.
