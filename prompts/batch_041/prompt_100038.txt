The question (worth 10 marks) was:

"Dr. Osei is the only cardiologist within 300 km of Nadia's town, and Nadia cannot travel. He recommends a procedure, and the alternative is a daily medication. The two options have the same expected benefit and, in fact, the same risk of death within five years. Dr. Osei tells Nadia that 2 in 100 patients die within five years on the medication, and that 1 in 50 patients die within five years on the procedure. Both statements are accurate. Nadia has mild cognitive impairment, but she can repeat the numbers back to him. Dr. Osei's fee is three times what the regional health service pays for the same procedure at its own hospital. Nadia signs both the consent form for the procedure and the fee agreement.

Using the four elements of informed consent covered in this unit, assess whether Nadia's consent to (a) the procedure and (b) the fee is valid. For each, identify the strongest objection to validity and say whether it succeeds."

And here's what the student wrote:

"The four elements of informed consent are disclosure, understanding, voluntariness and competence, and all four must be satisfied for consent to be valid.

Disclosure is satisfied: Dr. Osei told Nadia the risks of both options. He gave the procedure's risk as 1 in 50 and the medication's as 2 in 100, which are the same risk expressed differently, but since both statements are literally true this is not deception, because deception requires a false statement. So disclosure is fine.

Understanding is satisfied because Nadia could repeat the numbers back to him, which shows she understood them.

Competence is satisfied. Mild cognitive impairment does not automatically remove capacity, and she can retain the information and make a choice. Competent patients are the final authority on their own values, so the way the risks were framed cannot invalidate her consent - the decision is hers to make.

Voluntariness is satisfied because nobody threatened her or forced her to agree. The fee is far higher than elsewhere and she has no alternative within 300 km, which is coercive, but she still agreed.

So consent to the procedure is valid, since all four elements are present and there was no deception. Consent to the fee is not valid, because the monopoly position makes the arrangement coercive and she had no real alternative. In future Dr. Osei should present both options' risks in the same format."

The rubric is: 4 marks for correctly stating and applying the four elements, 4 marks for the analysis of the two consents, 2 marks for the conclusion.
