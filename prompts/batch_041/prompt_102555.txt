ok so I'm meant to be presenting my take on hedonism at a reading group next week and every time I try to write it down the parts start fighting each other, so I'd like a consistency check before I embarrass myself. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Welfare hedonism: the only thing that's intrinsically good for a person is their own pleasure, plus the absence of pain.
(2) There's a distinctive feel to pleasure. When you're enjoying something you can, at least in principle, notice it, and there's something common to all the enjoyings — a single phenomenal signature that makes them all pleasures rather than just pleasant-looking states.
(3) Pleasures are commensurable. Take any two episodes and there's a fact of the matter about which holds more pleasure, and the currency is basically intensity multiplied by duration.
(4) I don't accept desire-based accounts, where a pleasure is just a pro-attitude or a wanting-to-be-in-a-state. If that's what pleasure turns out to be, hedonism collapses into a desire-satisfaction theory and loses the thing that made it attractive.
(5) Mill's higher/lower distinction is right. The pleasure of finally getting a difficult proof is a greater pleasure than the pleasure of a warm biscuit, and not just because there's more of it or it lasts longer — it's qualitatively better, a better kind of pleasure.
(6) I've been tempted by a fix for (2) and (5) together: say pleasure is a determinable and the particular pleasures are determinates, the way scarlet and crimson are both red without there being one particular shade of red that they both are.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and which one has to go? Please rank the real tensions by how bad they are rather than telling me they're all equally costly, and for each say what the cheapest repair is and what it costs me. And if the determinable/determinate move in (6) actually earns its keep, tell me what I'm buying with it — because I suspect it rescues one thing and quietly sabotages another. Don't just tell me hedonism is unfashionable, I already know that.
