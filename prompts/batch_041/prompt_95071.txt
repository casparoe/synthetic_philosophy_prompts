I'm in a seminar on path dependence this term and the professer keeps saying history is locked in, and I've realized I dont actually know what I believe — so I want to write out my commitments and see if they survive contact with each other. Here's where I've landed. (1) Some big outcomes were genuinely tipped by small events: a battle going the other way, a ruler dying at the wrong moment, a standardization decision that went one way by a hair. (2) What turns a small event into a lasting difference is increasing returns — network effects, learning by doing, sunk costs, coordination. My toy version, which I've been using to shut up my friends, is this: suppose each adopter makes the next adoption 3% cheaper, then after roughly forty adopters the runner-up technology has to be about three times better to flip the market, and nobody is going to run that experiment, so the early accident is now the equilibrium. (3) At the macro level, though, I think the big structural stuff — farming, states, cities, sustained growth — is overdetermined. Some other path would have arrived there, later and uglier maybe, but it would have arrived. So the surface facts (which country, which language, which firm) are contingent while the shape isn't. (4) I'm a realist about counterfactuals, with a caveat I picked up from someone in the seminar and have adopted as my own: ask what would have happened if this one thing had been different, and I think there's a fact of the matter; ask what would have happened if the last thousand years had gone differently, and the question is too underspecified to have any answer at all, which means would-the-same-outcomes-have-arrived-anyway isn't even evaluable until you say what's being held fixed. (5) And I'm generally suspicious of lock-in talk — a friend of mine says lock-in is just switching costs with a dramatic name, and I half agree: locked in usually means expensive to reverse, not impossible, and somebody almost always has an incentive to pay the switching cost, which is why the QWERTY story as it's normally told strikes me as more of a morality tale than a finding. One more thing I want out of all this, without knowing whether it's consistent with the rest: I want the accidents to matter, not to be noise on the way to a destination that was going to happen anyway.

Can you audit this? I don't want a tour of the literature, I want to know which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, how severe each real clash is, and what the cheapest revision is. If something has to give, tell me which and what I lose by giving it up — and specifically tell me whether my toy model in (2) is being asked to carry more weight than a 3% adoption discount can carry.
