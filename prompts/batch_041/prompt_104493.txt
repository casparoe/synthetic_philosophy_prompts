I am preparing an episode of a philosophy podcast on Avicenna's flying man argument. My guest defends a specific reconstruction: the thought experiment is meant to establish the immateriality of the self, not merely the possibility of self-awareness without a body, and its force depends on Avicenna's doctrine that the soul is always present to itself. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that probe this reconstruction rather than invite a summary of the argument. Include at least one question on the gap between the thin conclusion (a self can be aware of itself without sensory input) and the thick conclusion (that self is an immaterial substance); one on whether the argument is phenomenological, demonstrative, or both; one on the objection that the scenario simply stipulates away the body and therefore cannot prove immateriality; and one on the role of the imagination in the setup. For each of these, add a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give. Close with a question about what would count against the reconstruction. The questions should be answerable by philosophical reasoning alone, without reference to editions, commentaries, or reading lists, and should be written in British English.
