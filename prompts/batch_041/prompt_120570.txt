I never went to college so go easy on the jargon, but I'm on the board of our town's volunteer-run community center and I have to get a written opinion in the other members' hands by Tuesday's meeting.

Background: about three years ago we voted to buy and fix up the old Grange hall into a year-round community center, with a commercial kitchen and a gym floor. We've raised and spent around $410,000, most of it from a town grant plus a long grind of bake sales, car washes, and one very generous estate gift. We've also put in maybe 6,000 volunteer hours. Now the contractor says the foundation and roof need about $650k more before we can open anything, and the building inspector won't let us open a single room without it. Our treasurer ran the numbers and even if everything goes right we'd be running roughly a $40k-a-year deficit for the first five years, and membership has actually gone down, not up.

Half the board says stop, we're throwing good money after bad. The other half, led by Deb, who started the whole thing and put in $60k of her own money and pretty much every weekend for three years, says we can't quit now, we've definately come too far, and besides we promised our donors and the town.

Here's what I can't get past. Everybody keeps saying the money's gone, ignore it, like that ends the discussion. And fine, I get that about the money. But two things bug me. First, I read a summary of some psychology paper a while back arguing that the don't-waste-it feeling isn't just a dumb glitch, that it's a rule that's useful in general and only misfires in certain cases, and if that's true I'd like to know which case we're in. Second, the money being spent is one thing, but the promises aren't spent. We told people this was happening, and some of them gave money they couldn't really afford. That feels different from money to me, and I don't know if the usual reasoning even covers it. On top of that our treasurer keeps warning that if we bail now, the town council and the big donors will remember next time we come asking, and Deb says if we quit, the volunteers will scatter and we'll never get them back.

Can you write the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote? I need it to actually recommend something concrete, keep going, stop, or keep going only if some specific condition gets met, with a time frame, and to give the real reasoning, including the strongest argument for whichever side I end up against, not a strawman. And please don't just tell me sunk costs are irrelevant like that settles it. I want to know whether that rule really does cover promises and the town remembering us, or whether those are just more costs and I'm fooling myself. If honoring what we've already put in is sometimes the reasonable thing, say when and why. If it never is, say why the promise stuff doesn't change that.

Also tell me how confident you are in the parts that lean on the psychology and decision research, and flag the one point you're least sure about, because I don't want to stand up at the meeting sounding more sure than I am. American English, please.
