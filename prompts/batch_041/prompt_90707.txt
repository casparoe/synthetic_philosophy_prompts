I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for well over a decade, long enough to remember when taking the outside view was treated as a near-universal solvent for bad forecasting, and long enough to have watched it do very little work on the questions I actually care about. I am putting together an application for a one-year research fellowship — stipend only, no teaching, roughly nine to twelve months of working time — and I want the proposal to be about something I keep circling back to: whether the outside view can be made to work for one-shot macro-historical questions, using the durability of the present international order as the running case. The standard move is to assemble a reference class of past great powers or hegemonies, count how many lasted how long, and let the resulting base rate discipline whatever inside-view narrative one is telling about the present. My half-formed suspicion is that this is not merely difficult but ill-posed, for two reasons that the usual discussions run together. First, the reference class is assembled from a historical record that is itself selected by the outcome: we know about polities that ended loudly because ruins, sackings and chronicles leave traces, and we systematically under-observe the ones that dissolved quietly, were absorbed, or simply transformed into something we then classify as continuous rather than as a case of the original thing ending. Second, the units are not exchangeable draws, because later actors can read the earlier record and adjust; a reference class that its own members have studied is not a sample in the relevant sense, and it is unclear whether that is a fatal problem or merely a reason to treat the outside view as a constraint on the inside view rather than as a source of point probabilities.

Please turn this into a proposal: state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing positions on reference-class forecasting and on the outside view leave it open, set out at least two candidate repairs (for instance, treating the reference class as a generative model rather than a population, or restricting the outside view to bounding the inside view rather than replacing it) and say which considerations would decide between them. Describe the method concretely — what I would actually do, month by month — and be honest about which parts are conceptual argument and which would require assembling historical data, since I have no research assistance and no web access while drafting. Say what a successful result would look like, including a worked demonstration of how a forecaster should use, or refuse to use, such a reference class on one concrete question about the current order, and what would count as the project failing. Finish with a candid assessment of whether a year is enough, or whether the honest answer is that the question needs to be narrowed further before it is worth funding. I will add citations myself, so please do not include a bibliography or reading list.
