I have been following the debate over illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — roughly, the view that there is no phenomenal consciousness of the kind realists intend, only introspective representations that misrepresent us as having it — for years now, across philosophy-of-mind papers and the long consciousness threads on rationalist forums, and it has not moved an inch. The realists keep pressing the self-defeat objection, that an illusion is still something that seems a certain way to someone, and the illusionists keep replying that the objection simply assumes the very thing under dispute; each side restates its case, and the temperature never drops. My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis rather than a conclusion, is that this is not one disagreement but two sharing a name: a conceptual or verbal dispute about whether the folk notion of phenomenal consciousness has any determinate referent, and a substantive dispute about what an explanation of introspection is required to accomplish — whether it must explain a seeming or only the reports and judgments that there is one. Diagnose it for me: is the impasse verbal, are the two sides answering different questions under one heading, is a hidden empirical or formal question doing the real work, or is it bedrock intuition that neither side can argue for? Engage what each side actually says — the self-defeat objection, the appeal to the meta-problem, and the distinction between phenomenal properties, phenomenal concepts, and phenomenal beliefs — and then tell me what, given your diagnosis, would count as progress here, or why none should be expected.
