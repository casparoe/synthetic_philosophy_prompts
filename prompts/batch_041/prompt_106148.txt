For a graduate seminar in comparative epistemology, construct a thought experiment that places a Nyāya analysis of knowledge and a standard internalist justified-true-belief analysis on opposite sides of a single, ordinary-life episode. The episode must involve a subject who forms a true belief through one of the four pramāṇas — perception, inference, comparison, or testimony — and the specifications are as follows. First, in the episode as told, the Nyāya analysis must classify the cognition as a pramā (genuine knowledge) even though the subject can supply no justifying reason for it and has no second-order assurance that the cognition is veridical, while the internalist analysis must deny that it is knowledge for exactly that reason. Second, holding every physical fact of the episode fixed, you must alter exactly one feature so that the two verdicts reverse: the cognition remains true and remains justified on the internalist analysis, but the Nyāya analysis withdraws the status of pramā. Constraints: the setting must be mundane — no clairvoyance, no divine or superhuman testimony, no brains in vats, no faculties that are not ordinary human ones; the episode must be tellable in a single paragraph; and the operative pramāṇa must be one about which a competent reader might initially be tempted to name a different pramāṇa, so that defending the classification is part of your task.

After the two cases, do the following. State which pramāṇa is operative in each, and why. Give the verdict of each analysis and trace it to the specific commitment that produces it — on the Nyāya side, say what role is played by the reliability of the causal process that produced the cognition, by the presence or absence of a defect (doṣa), and by the question whether the subject must be able to certify the cognition's validity. Name the single feature whose presence or removal flips each verdict, and say what becomes of the case if that feature is deleted. Then explain which of the two directions — Nyāya granting knowledge where internalism withholds it, or the reverse — is the harder to construct, and why. Close by saying how a Nyāya defender would attack the internalist's verdict in the first case, and whether the disagreement is finally about the conditions on knowledge or about what the two theories are even asking.
