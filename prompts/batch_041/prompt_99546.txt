Ok, I need a hand with something, and I'd rather you really think it through then fire off a list — start by brainstorming a bunch of candidate questions, then cut down to a ranking. Take on the role of a philosopher of science who works on causation, reduction, and levels of explanation, and who's allergic to hand-waving about "emergence."

Here's the toy I keep chewing on. Picture a box the size of a warehouse, filled with an astronomical number of tiny hard balls — call them simmballs — bouncing around and colliding like billiard balls. Nothing else in the box: no fields, no forces except contact. Now supose the walls and the starting arrangement are set up so that certain stable clusters of simmballs keep recurring, and each cluster reliably gives rise to a particular next cluster — cluster A shows up, and shortly after, cluster B; B shows up, then C. Chain enough of those together and the box looks like it's doing logic: these patterns play the role of premises and conclusions, and the transitions look like inferences. Call each recurring cluster a symbol and the whole contraption a careenium.

Two readings of it. (1) Only the balls do anything. "Symbol" is a label we paste onto a pattern, and saying a symbol caused the next symbol is like saying the sunrise caused the rooster's crow. (2) The symbol level is real in its own right: once the pattern is stable, the symbol is what makes the next symbol happen, and the ball story is a lower-level description of the same goings-on rather than the only true one.

I don't want you to just pick a side. What I want is a research agenda. I've got roughly the budget to support two people for two years, or one person for four, on the philosophical end of this — no lab, no simulations beyond whatever runs on a laptop, and no deferring to "we'll know once we have a theory of everything." Which open questions here are worth that money? Give me a ranking and defend it: for each, why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for someone whose tools are arguments rather than equations, and what would move it up or down the list over the next year or two. Also tell me which ones are probably pseudo-questions that dissolve rather than get answered, and which look sexy but wouldn't change anything downstream if they got settled.

Two wrinkles in the toy that I suspect are doing more work than they look like they are, so please address them rather than treating them as decoration. First, the box comes with a poker: you can reach in and nudge a single simmball. Nudge it and the same symbol usually still shows up downstream, but sometimes the whole chain of symbols reorganizes. Second, you can swap individual simmballs out for identical ones, one at a time, and the symbols don't notice — the pattern survives wholesale replacement of its parts.

And while you're at it: if you think my framing has already begged the question, say where.
