Me and my mate Dev had a two-hour arguement last night about whether dispositions are actually real. My line was the boring one — a glass being fragile is just the fact that it would break if you hit it, there's no extra ingredient in the glass called fragility, it's shorthand for a bunch of facts about what would happen. He reckons that view's been dead for decades, and then he gave me a whole speech. He's doing an MA so he said all of it very confidently, which isn't the same as being right. Here it is as close to how he said it as I can get:

'A disposition is just a thing's tendency to behave a certain way once it's triggered — a glass is fragile because it would break if you struck it. The old analysis said that's all there is to it: something is disposed to break when struck just in case it would break if struck. Then two kinds of counterexample turned up. Fink cases: a sorcerer puts a spell on the glass so that the instant you strike it, it turns unbreakable — so it wouldn't break if struck, but it's plainly still fragile. Mask cases: same thing really, you wrap the glass in bubble wrap, so it won't break when you hit it, and it's still fragile. Both show the conditional coming out false when the disposition is still there, so the conditional analysis is dead — you can't say what a disposition is by talking about how the thing would behave. Which means dispositions have to be primitive, irreducible features of the world, and that's why everyone's a dispositional essentialist these days and thinks the laws of nature just are the dispositions of things. Mind you, the sorcerer stuff is philosophers cheating — real glass doesn't have spells on it, so the whole debate's a bit of a parlour game.'

I'm fairly sure the last bit's nonsense, and I definately couldn't tell him what the difference between his two kinds of case is meant to be, though I've got a feeling there is one. And I can't work out whether the conclusion follows from the bits before it even if those are all fine — I don't see how 'you can't analyse a disposition as a conditional' gets you to 'dispositions are primitive', let alone to 'everyone's a dispositional essentialist now'.
