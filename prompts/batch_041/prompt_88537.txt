I am a science journalist who has spent the past several years reporting on the experimental literature on perception, and I have been awarded a one-year residential fellowship at an interdisciplinary institute. The condition of the award is a two-page research proposal for the project I will actually pursue during the year, and I would like you to draft it. My half-formed idea is this. The long controversy over whether perception can be cognitively penetrated is almost always conducted as a single yes-or-no question about a single phenomenon, and each side marshals its evidence — ambiguous figures, expectation effects, attention, emotion and motivation, learned categories, occurrent belief — as though all of it bore on the same claim. I suspect this is why the dispute has settled so little. The cases do not obviously concern one thing, and, more to the point, the philosophical consequences that are supposed to hang on the answer — whether perceptual experience can itself be assessed as reasonable or unreasonable, whether there is a non-conceptual given, whether perception is modular, whether a perceptual belief can be justified independently of the background beliefs of the perceiver — do not all hang on the same empirical claim. My hunch, which I would like tested rather than assumed, is that the consequences sort according to whether the penetrating state is itself epistemically assessable (the kind of state that can be correct or incorrect and can answer to reasons) rather than according to whether it is 'cognitive' in the loose sense the literature uses.

Turn this into a proposal. It should state the question precisely enough that it admits a determinate answer; explain why the positions already on offer leave that question open rather than settling it; describe what I would actually do month by month over the year, and why this approach rather than the two obvious alternatives, namely a survey of the empirical literature or a defense of one side of the existing dispute; say what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project's failing; and give an honest assessment of whether the project is too large for a single year and, if so, what should be cut. Please do not include citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; I will supply those myself.
