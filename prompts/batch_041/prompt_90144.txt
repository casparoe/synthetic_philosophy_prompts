I am a novelist at work on a book whose central character treats karma not as atmosphere or metaphor but as a serious account of moral desert, and in the course of inhabiting her convictions I have come to share a version of them. I would like you to try to dislodge me from it.

The position I hold is roughly this: once karma is detached from the imagery of a ledger kept by a judging power, it can be restated as a claim about desert — that an agent's actions render that agent fittingly liable to consequences whose moral quality answers to the moral quality of the actions, and that this fittingness is a matter of desert and not merely of causal consequence. Stated so, I find it more defensible than I expected. What should count against it?

I do not want only the objections an unsympathetic outsider would raise. I am more interested in those that arise from inside the traditions that take karma most seriously — above all the difficulty that a doctrine of no-self appears to leave no one for whom the consequences could be deserved, and how the problem shifts if one instead posits a persisting self who carries the desert across lives. I would also like to know how the view fares against the familiar difficulties: that a person cannot remember the deeds for which she is said to be answerable; that suffering is distributed so unevenly that proportionality looks like wishful thinking; that conduct is so entangled with luck and circumstance that desert has little to attach to; and that a karmic law operating with necessity may be a causal story rather than a moral one at all.

Please sort these by how much they should actually move someone who already holds the view — which are fatal, which merely awkward — and say which a committed defender would concede as counting against her and which she would dismiss as confused. Finally, give me an honest judgment about whether the view, once qualified in the ways its defenders favor, risks becoming unfalsifiable, and whether that is a defect in a theory of this kind or simply the shape a metaphysical theory takes. Tell me, too, which single objection you expect I would find hardest to answer, and why.
