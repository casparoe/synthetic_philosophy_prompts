I'm a doctoral student in metaphysics and philosophy of language, and my supervisor has asked for a short position statement on the unity of the proposition ahead of my upgrade viva, so that she can see where the joints are before I commit to a chapter. I've been living with Bradley's regress for the better part of a year, and I've arrived at a package I'm rather fond of, though I have a nagging sense that two of the pieces are quietly incompatible and I can't work out which. What I hold, as plainly as I can put it: (1) propositions are the primary bearers of truth and falsity; (2) they are mind-independent, in the sense that the proposition that such-and-such is the case would have been true or false whether or not any thinker ever entertained it; (3) they are structured entities with constituents — objects, properties, relations — rather than unstructured sets of possible worlds, and their truth-conditions are fixed by those constituents and by how they are arranged; (4) that arrangement is not itself one more constituent, so there is no copula, no extra unity property, no further relating relation over and above the things related; (5) Bradley's regress is a genuine and vicious objection to any account that explains unity by positing a relation that does the relating, so I want nothing to do with that strategy; (6) I am a naturalist, in the sense that I think every fact has an explanation terminating in the physical or the structural, and I am allergic to brute primitives posited precisely because they resist explanation; (7) it is a datum that two thinkers can grasp one and the same proposition, and that in doing so they stand in a relation to a single abstract object.

For what it's worth, the positive story I have in mind is that the constituents of a proposition are fused rather than merely aggregated: the fusion is not a further item in the world, but neither are the constituents just heaped together, and the difference between the fusion and the heap is supposed to be internal to how the constituents are rather than a relation that binds them. I'd like you to audit this package rather than defend it. Tell me which commitments pull against each other, which pairs only appear to conflict, and for each real conflict what the cheapest repair is and what that repair would cost me elsewhere in the set. Order the findings by severity, and give me exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion; each item should name the commitments involved, say how badly they clash, and say what would have to give. If one of the clashes I seem to be inviting turns out not to be a tension at all, include it anyway and say why it dissolves.
