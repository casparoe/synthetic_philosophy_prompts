PhD student in bioethics here, mid-dissertation on enhancement, and I keep running into a pattern in the empirical and policy literature that I don't have a decent word for. Curious whether it already has a name or whether I should just coin one and move on.

The pattern has two halves that seem to feed each other. First half: something enters as a real treatment for a fairly clear-cut condition, and then over the years the condition's boundaries loosen — the threshold drops, the diagnostic criteria widen, an "at risk" category gets inserted in front of the diagnosis — until the intervention is something that basically everyone past a certain age, or below a certain score, is now supposed to be on. Nobody ever decided to turn a therapy into an enhancement; the definition of the disease just migrated until the treated state became the normal state. Second half, mirror image: something starts out clearly on the enhancement side — sharper memory, steadier mood, better focus, more muscle than you'd have on your own — and then gets relabeled as a treatment for an under-recognized condition, usually so that somebody will pay for it.

The bit I actually care about is what happens when you put the two halves in sequence. Once the enhancement has been reclassified as a therapy, the first move runs again on the newly minted "disease," so the enhancement's baseline becomes the new floor of normal, and anything under that floor starts to look like a deficit that needs correcting — which licenses treating it, which moves the floor again. It's a loop rather than a slope, and every individual turn looks locally reasonable. Nobody at any single step is doing anything obviously shady, which I think is why it's so hard to argue against in a committee meeting.

I know the neighborhood, roughly: medicalization, disease mongering, diagnostic creep or expansion, the general worry that the therapy/enhancement line won't hold still, the species-typical-functioning picture of health that all this quietly assumes. But those seem to name one direction at a time, or they name a social process without the feedback structure. What I want is the concept for the loop itself — the mutual redefinition of "disease" and "normal" by way of the intervention, such that the intervention keeps re-justifying itself as therapy. Does that already have a name somewhere (STS? health econ? the disability literature, where I suspect I'd find the sharpest version and just haven't looked properly)? If it doesn't, can you give me a name and a careful definition — what exactly the thing is, what it isn't, how it differs from plain medicalization, from a slippery-slope argument, from ordinary supply-induced demand — and where its edges are, i.e. cases where the loop doesn't start or actually runs backwards? I care way more about the carving than the label, so please don't just hand me a term and call it done.

Secondary question, lower priority: does the same loop show up outside medicine? I have a hunch about giftedness programs and about fitness wearables, but I might be overfitting my own pattern onto them.
