I'm a backend engineer by training — no formal philosophy beyond a single undergraduate ethics elective, plus a group chat that has drifted from restaurant recommendations into arguments about intention and rationality — and I've now lost two weekends to a thought experiment a friend described to me as Kavka's toxin puzzle. As I understand the setup: an eccentric billionaire puts a vial of toxin in front of you and promises a million dollars tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you intend to drink it tomorrow afternoon; the toxin will make you painfully ill for a day but cause no lasting harm; the money is in your account hours before the drinking time arrives; and you are entirely free, once paid, to change your mind and pour the vial away. The twist, as I gather, is that a rational person reasons that he will not drink it once he is paid, and therefore cannot sincerely intend to drink it now, and therefore cannot collect.

What I actually want is a number with its derivation, not a verdict on whether the puzzle is deep. Here is the variant I care about, and I want your credence that a rational agent can collect the money in it. Replace the billionaire's judgment with a machine that has perfect and exact knowledge of your present physical state and pays out at midnight if and only if your brain is, at that moment, in whatever physical configuration constitutes your intending to drink the toxin tomorrow afternoon. Stipulate that the machine is reliable, that it inspects you at midnight, and that intending is not some extra fact floating above your brain but is realized in whatever way your intentions generally are — the same kind of thing that is present when you genuinely intend to show up to a meeting. Now decompose: how much of your probability rides on (a) whether there is any deliberative route by which you could come to occupy that configuration — whether intending-to-drink is something you can adopt as a means to an end, the way you can adopt a policy or a plan, or whether it is more like a state that merely happens to you once you are convinced; (b) whether the configuration the machine detects would still count as intending if it was produced by your wanting the money rather than by any inclination toward the toxin, given that you know you will not drink; (c) whether the puzzle exposes a genuine gap between what makes an action rational and what makes an intention rational, as opposed to an exotic payoff structure that a sufficiently clever agent could game; and (d) whatever else you think belongs in the sum.

Give me a rough probability for each term with one line of justification, tell me how the terms combine into a final number, and then tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would move it most. For calibration: I put myself at about 0.4 that the money is collectible in this variant, while a friend in the same chat is at 0.95 and insists I am simply confused about what an intention is — so I would also like to know which single term the two of us most likely disagree about, and whether that is the same term you are least confident in.
