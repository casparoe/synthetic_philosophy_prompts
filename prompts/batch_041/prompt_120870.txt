I've been going back and forth with two mates for months about a pair of puzzles and I want to write something funny about them — a judgment from a small claims court, I think.

Puzzle one: this bloke Omega flips a coin and tells me how it landed, and it landed the way where he asks me for £100. If it had gone the other way he'd have handed me £10,000 instead, but only because he'd already worked out that I'm the sort of person who pays up when asked. So either I hand over the hundred, or I tell him where to go, and either way I spend the rest of the week thinking about the branch that didn't happen.

Puzzle two: a note comes through the door, and the sender only ever sends it when exactly one of two things is true and not both — either my house is crawling with termites, or I'm going to pay him a thousand quid. So if I pay, no termites; if I don't pay, termites. The termites cost a hell of a lot more than the thousand, which is the only reason it's a real decision and not just a scam.

Dan says obviously you pay both times, that's the whole point of the thing. Priya says obviously you don't — the coin's already landed in the first one, and in the second one paying doesn't kill a single termite. I reckon they're each answering a different question: one of them is asking whether the choice was any good, the other is asking whether the person who made it is any good. And a court can only ever get its hands on the second one. The coin and the termites were settled before the case opened; the only thing left to put on trial is my character.

So write the judgment. About 800 words, a bored district judge who's had both cases that morning: in the first, I payed up and I'm now suing Omega to get my hundred back; in the second, the note bloke is suing me for the thousand I never sent. Make up the case name, don't use a real one, and no real cases or quotes anywhere in it, I'd never be able to check them. He has to actually work both arguments properly — why paying looks like the only sane move, why it looks like setting fire to money, what "cause" can even mean when the only thing Omega can do to you is know you in advance — so that someone who's argued about this for years laughs instead of wincing, and someone who hasn't still follows it. Don't let him wriggle out of it by declaring the whole business silly and unenforceable, and keep him off the jargon (if he has to name the idea at all, have him get the name a bit wrong). End it so I honestly can't tell whether the man who paid was a mug.
