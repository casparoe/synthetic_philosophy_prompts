Give me your credence in a fairly narrow proposition: that the familiar generational labels — Boomer, Millennial, Gen Z — name real kinds rather than convenient periodizations. Treat 'real kind' as the conjunction of three claims, and say separately how much each is carrying: (a) the label supports projectible generalizations about its members that survive controlling for age and period; (b) the traits it groups share a common causal origin rather than being a bundle of unrelated trends; (c) the label is robust under small redrawings of its boundaries, so that moving the Gen Z cutoff by a couple of years leaves the generalizations intact.

My own number is about 0.6; a colleague who thinks cohort talk is mostly marketing is at 0.15. Give me yours, decomposed: the probability that age, period, and cohort effects can be separated from survey data at all (they are linearly dependent, so something must be assumed — say what you assume and what it costs); the probability that, conditional on separation, a common mechanism exists; the probability that the boundaries are anything other than conventional; and the probability that these labels are what one might call interactive kinds — classifications that loop back and change the people classified — and whether that loop strengthens or weakens the kind-claim. Put a number and a one-line justification on each term, combine them however you actually endorse, then tell me which term you are least confident in, what single piece of evidence would move it most, and whether my colleague and I most likely disagree about the identification problem, the mechanism, or the boundaries.
