Here's where I've landed. I've been calling it lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism, mostly because it's fun to say.

1. Welfare is the only thing that's intrinsically good or bad, and right action maximizes it. Straightforward consequentialism — no side-constraints, no agent-relative restrictions.
2. Welfare is hedonic. Pleasure and suffering, that's the whole story. And they sit on one scale: a single currency, continuous, interpersonally comparable. Any amount of pleasure can be weighed against any amount of suffering and there's a determinate fact about which is larger. That's what lets me say one person's ordeal is worse than another's, and it's what lets me sum across people at all.
3. There's a floor. Below the floor, suffering is lexically prior to pleasure: no amount of anybody's pleasure, however large, and no number of beneficiaries, however many, can make it worth pushing someone below the floor. Down there, suffering doesn't trade against anything. The case that got me here: I'd give up any finite quantity of aggregate delight — a billion person-hours of mild contentment, whatever — to stop one person being tortured for a week. Not a close call for me.
4. Above the floor it's plain additivity. Pleasure and suffering trade off at a fixed going rate, the same rate everywhere above the line, and then we just sum across people. No priority weighting, no diminishing returns, no extra machinery.
5. The floor isn't a sharp line. It's vague, like 'heap' or 'bald'. There's no fact about the exact point where it kicks in — a zone that's clearly below, a zone that's clearly not, and a blurry middle.
6. Ordinary lives are clearly net positive. Your average person's life, grief and toothaches and boredom included, is worth living, and I think that's about the least controversial thing I believe. Same for most animals, most of the time.
7. The repugnant conclusion — the claim that a gigantic world of people whose lives are barely worth living beats a smaller world of flourishing people — strikes me as obviously false. Honestly, a big part of why I want the floor in the first place is that I thought it was what blocks that.
8. And I'm not a pleasure-is-merely-the-absence-of-pain person. A happy animal's happiness is a good thing in its own right, and I'd fund making lives go well, not just less badly. I've been told this clashes with the 'negative' part but I dont see it.

I cant shake the feeling that at least two of these are pulling against eachother and I genuinly don't know which.
