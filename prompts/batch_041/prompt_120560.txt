Here's the thesis I'm defending, and I expect it to rub people the wrong way: the canonical commons dilemma is misidentified. What we call the tragedy is almost never a one-shot prisoner's dilemma over how much each person takes; it's an anti-coordination problem over when and where people take, played repeatedly, and it stays cooperative as long as appropriators can observe each other cheaply. The two remedies everyone reaches for -- fence it off, or have the state set a quota -- are solutions to the prisoner's-dilemma version, and they quietly assume the opposite information structure from the one that actually holds: they assume the central authority can see the resource while the users can't, when in reality it's the users who can see each other and the authority that's blind.
