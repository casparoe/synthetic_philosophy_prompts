I got into an arguement at a party last week with a friend who's a set theorist (I'm the philosopher in the friendship) about whether the axiom of choice is actually *true*, and it turned out neither of us could say anything that wasn't just restating what we already believed. The trigger was a seminar where the lecturer mentioned that Zorn's lemma is equivalent to the well-ordering theorem and to AC over ZF — which I knew — but hearing it put that way made me realize I've been leaning on Zorn my whole mathematical life (every vector space has a basis, every nonzero ring has a maximal ideal, every field has an algebraic closure) without having any settled opinion about whether it's true of the universe of sets, as opposed to being a convenient axiom that happens not to blow anything up. I'd put my credence around 0.7 that AC, and hence Zorn, is true of the intended universe. My friend, who's some kind of structuralist, would maybe go 0.15, and I think mostly because she thinks there's no fact of the matter at all, not because she thinks there's a fact and it's no.

Give me your own number on the proposition 'the axiom of choice is true of the intended universe of sets' — but what I actually want is the decomposition, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each term. How much rides on whether mathematical realism, or at least realism about sets, is right in the first place? How much on whether, granting realism, the intended model is determinate enough for a statement independent of ZF to have a truth value (Skolem paradox business, the multiverse picture where CH and AC can go either way in equally legitimate universes)? How much on the intrinsic case — whether something like the iterative conception of set gives you any reason for or against? How much on whether the weird consequences (Banach–Tarski, non-measurable sets, well-ordering the reals) count as evidence *against*, given that we'd happily call a physical theory false for implying something absurd? And how much on the extrinsic case, that AC is load-bearing all over ordinary algebra and analysis? Then combine them, give me the number, and tell me which single term it's most sensitive to and what you'd have to learn to move it by more than 0.1. Please don't just answer 'it depends what you mean by true' and stop — if you think it's ill-posed, put a number on the no-fact-of-the-matter option and tell me what that number is, and I'll treat everything else as conditional on there being a fact. Oh, and while you're at it: given my 0.7 and her 0.15, which of those terms do you think is where we're actually disagreeing?
