Ok so this is a weird one and I'm not even sure it's a real question, but it's been nagging at me for weeks. I'm moving abroad in the spring and I've been trying to sell my motorbike, and the whole process has done a number on my head. My reasons for selling are completely boring — can't take it with me, need the money for a deposit — but every time someone comes round to look at it I can practically see them running the calculation: why's he selling, what does he know. And I'm doing the exact same thing on the other end, because I'll need a car when I land, so I've been scrolling listings and every time something is priced suspiciously well I think 'right, what's wrong with it'. A mate of mine got an unsolicited cash offer on his flat last year and spent a fortnight trying to work out what the buyer knew that he didn't. And I've just started putting a bit of money into an index fund, so now I read the business pages, and when a company announces it's buying back its own shares I have the same reflex — what do they know.

The thing that's bothering me is that the reasoning only seems to run in one direction. I know precisely why I'm selling and it isn't information, it's just life admin; but I've got no way of knowing that about the bloke selling me his Corolla, so I shade my offer down, and he shades his price up, and if we both took the logic all the way we'd conclude nobody should ever buy anything second hand. Which is obviously rubbish, because the second hand market is gigantic. So either the inference is wrong or there's a limit on how far you can push it, and I can't tell which. The only thing that seems to loosen it is when the other side puts something on the line — the dealer who throws in a twelve month warranty, the shop that lets you bring it back — and I notice my suspicion drops the second someone offers me a guarantee, even though in principle a guarantee is just another thing to be suspicious about.

I've got a vague sense there's literature on this. I think there's a famous used-car argument about sellers knowing things buyers don't, and something about how in certain conditions trade shouldn't happen at all, but I've never actually read any of it and honestly I don't want to be handed the name of the famous thing, because I don't think that's what I'm circling. In my case there's no hidden defect and no hidden fact — the engine's fine, I'd tell you if it wasn't — and yet the shading happens anyway, purely because you can't get inside my head to check my motives. Is there a name for that specific move, from 'this person is willing to deal' to 'this person must know something', and for whatever's left standing after everyone has run that move on everyone else — the trades that still happen? If the answer is that the survivors have to be trades where at least one side wants something other than information — they need cash now, they want the risk off their books, they just disagree about the future — is there a term for that class of motive, and does it matter whether the disagreement is a genuine difference of opinion or just one of them being wrong? Basically: is this one thing, or two or three things I'm bundling? Where are the edges — is my no-hidden-facts version the same phenomenon as the hidden-defect version, or a weaker cousin that gets treated seperately? I'd much rather have the distinction drawn properly than a bare label, and if the honest answer is that what I've noticed doesn't have its own name and is just the mush between two things that do, I'll take that.
