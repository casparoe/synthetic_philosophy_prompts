My girlfriend's grandad is in the middle of this awful thing where he signed a living will years ago saying no tube feeding, and now he's got fairly advanced dementia, is cheerful as anything, and says he wants to be kept alive, and the family is tearing itself apart over which version of him to listen to. That's the occasion, but what's actually bugging me is that I keep noticing the same shape in totally unrelated arguments and I don't have a word for it. The shape is roughly: a decision made by someone at an earlier time is treated as binding on that same person at a later time, and the later person's current preference loses, even though the later person is the one who has to live with the outcome. The reasons people give come in two flavours that I don't think fit together. Flavour one: it's the same person, so his earlier decision just is his decision, and you can't be released from yourself. Flavour two: the earlier him was the real him, or was competent and clear-headed in a way the present him isn't, so the earlier judgement should win. Those pull in opposite directions -- if it's the same person all the way through, why does the earlier vote count double? And the two flavours get used interchangeably in arguments about contracts ('you signed it, so your bound even if you'd give anything to get out'), oaths, constitutions binding future generations, and this guy on a forum I read who insists that if you volunteered for the army you forfeit the right to complain about the war.

Here's what makes me think these might be two different phenomena wearing one word. When I set up a website blocker or hand my passwords to a friend, my future self has the same preferences and is just weaker, so overriding him feels like helping him. In the dementia case the later self's preferences have genuinely changed -- it's not weakness, it's that a different set of things matter to him now. Different structures, same "but he decided" move.

So: is there an established name for this family of cases, or for one of the sub-cases? I know there's the whole business about tying yourself to the mast and self-binding, but I'm pretty sure that's the weakness case and not the changed-values case, and I want to know what the changed-values one is called, if anything. And honestly the label is the smaller half of what I want -- I want the carving. Is what's supposed to bind the later self his identity with the earlier one, the earlier one's superior competence, or the fact that a life is meant to be authored by one person over time rather than lived moment to moment? If there's no term, coin one and tell me exactly which cases fall inside it and which outside, because I genuinely can't tell whether the contract case and the dementia case are the same phenomenon or just share a slogan.
