I sit on the steering committee of a small foundation that has set aside roughly two million dollars to fund conceptual work on AI alignment over the next eighteen months -- philosophy, decision theory, and the more theoretical end of safety research, explicitly not empirical machine learning or engineering. My job this month is to write the framing memo that will guide which proposals we solicit, so I need a defensible answer to a question the committee keeps circling without ever settling: is "the alignment problem" one problem or several, and if several, which of them are upstream of the others? The disagreement is not merely terminological, and I have come to think that is itself a clue. One colleague means the specification problem -- how to state what we actually want in a form that could be optimized at all. Another means the gap between the objective we train on and the objective the resulting system effectively pursues. A third means something closer to political philosophy: whose preferences get aggregated, and by what procedure, once a system is powerful enough that its choices bind everyone. A fourth treats it as a control problem -- keeping a capable system from doing things we did not sanction. A fifth thinks the whole framing is a mistake, and that what is really at stake is a problem about evaluation: we cannot, even in principle, tell from the outside whether a system is aligned, so the interesting question is epistemic. I have been going back and forth with these colleagues for months, and we each keep winning arguments on our own terms, which I take to mean we are not actually disagreeing about the same object.

I do not want a survey of these positions; I want you to tell me which of them, if resolved, would resolve or dissolve the others, and which are largely downstream. Please write the memo as though you were the advisor rather than the author: rank the open conceptual questions by how much other work depends on them, say for each why it is upstream or downstream and how tractable it looks for philosophers and theorists rather than engineers, set out the trade-offs you are making (importance versus tractability versus neglectedness), and tell me what developments over the next eighteen months would most change the ordering. If you think the one-versus-several question is itself the crux -- that the committee is stuck precisely because the framing is doing hidden work -- then say so and defend that instead of a ranking. Assume the committee is sophisticated but not specialist, and that we will not fund anything we cannot explain to our board in two paragraphs.
