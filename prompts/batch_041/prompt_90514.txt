Bit of a weird one, and I keep going back and forth on it. I keep ending up in arguments about the claim that consciousness is only one — Schrödinger kept saying something in this vein in his later writing, and it's all over the Upanishad-adjacent stuff — and I've realised the sentence gets used for at least three different pictures without anyone flagging the switch. One: my mind is a genuine part of a bigger mind, the way a room is part of a house, so the many are real but contained. Two: there's no bigger mind and no parts either; the many are just the one thing seen from different angles, so nothing is actually divided, it only looks that way. Three: the many are a straight-up error, one thing misperceived as many, rope-mistaken-for-snake style. Only the first gives you a container; two and three relate the one and the many in totally different ways, yet it's the same sentence every time. Is there a name for this cluster, and specifically for picture two as against one and three? My test case: if there's only one consciousness and I stub my toe, is the pain I feel the same pain you would feel, or is it only mine? One and three seem to give different answers and I can't tell if two gives a third. Don't survey the options at me — tell me which reading you think is actually right and defend it, and if there's no established name, coin one and tell me what it rules out. No headings or bullet points please, just write it out like an email.
