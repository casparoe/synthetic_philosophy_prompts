Ok so I've been reading about facilitated communication — the thing where a facilitator holds or supports the hand or arm of a non-speaking autistic person and the person types out messages. Sometimes poetry, sometimes quite sophisticated stuff, and in the 90s sometimes accusations of abuse against the person's own family, which got taken seriously by courts and child protection agencies for a few years. Then controlled tests came along: show the client one object and the facilitator a different one, and the message names the facilitator's object. Alot of the abuse accusations were retracted, professional bodies put out statements against the practice, and yet it never fully died — there are still practitioners who say the tests were badly designed and some users for whom they insist it works.

What I want is this episode run through Dennett's three stances and his real-patterns idea. Which stance were the facilitators taking, and were they entitled to it? The intentional stance predicts the behavior of the dyad pretty well, which is exactly why it caught on and why it felt so obvious to the people doing it. So does the real-pattern criterion — a pattern that compresses the data and supports counterfactuals, as opposed to one you can only gerrymander case by case — actually rule the attribution out, or does it only rule it out if you already know where the mechanism lives? Then flip it: maybe there really was a real pattern, but it belonged to the facilitator-plus-client system rather than to the client. If the intentional stance is holistic like that, does it even have the resources to say the facilitators were wrong, as opposed to just attributing beliefs at the wrong level of aggregation?

And then what does the case do back to the theory? Is it a counterexample to instrumentalism about the stances, a vindication of it, or a case the framework was never built to adjudicate? Clever Hans seems like the obvious parallel, so use it if it earns its keep, but tell me where it doesnt. Dont hedge by telling me the science is settled or that the literature says such and such — no citations, I want the mapping, and an honest account of what the three stances can and can't decide here.
