For a seminar I have been asked to circulate my philosophical profile on consciousness in advance so that the others can press me on it, and I would like a consistency audit before I send it round.

(1) Physicalism, in the strong sense: the physical facts fix all the facts; no fact of the matter goes unsettled by how things are physically.

(2) Mary's case: someone raised in a black-and-white room who has learned every physical fact about colour vision nevertheless comes to know something, on first seeing red, that she did not know before, and I am content to describe what she gains as knowledge — indeed as a piece of propositional knowledge.

(3) What she gains is knowledge of a physical fact under a concept she could not have possessed without the experience. The relevant phenomenal concepts are recognitional: they pick out perfectly ordinary physical properties, but they are available only to a subject who has undergone the experience, and they refer to nothing non-physical.

(4) Consequently the knowledge argument fails. I accept its central premise; I reject the inference from "she knew all the physical facts and yet learns something" to "there are non-physical facts".

(5) The explanatory gap is an artefact of that conceptual asymmetry rather than a metaphysical gap in the world.

(6) Zombies: I find a zombie world — a physical duplicate of me with the lights off — genuinely conceivable, not merely imaginable in some shallow sense, and I take conceivability to be a defeasible but real guide to possibility.

(7) Inverted spectra, by contrast, I treat deflationarily: the scenario is an epistemic puzzle about which physical property a subject's colour concepts happen to track, not a glimpse of non-physical qualia.

I have the feeling that not all of these can be true together, but I cannot locate the trouble myself, and I would rather you did not simply reassure me. Tell me which of these actually pull against each other, which only look as though they do, and what the cheapest repair is in each case. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, say explicitly which apparent tensions dissolve and why, and finish by naming the single revision that would resolve the most at the least cost — and what adopting it would cost me elsewhere.
