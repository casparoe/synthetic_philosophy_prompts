so i'm in this reading group on philosophy of social science and we've been going at the individualism/holism thing for like four months now and nobody's budged, and i'm starting to think we're not even disagreeing about the same thing. maybe you can help me figure out what kind of disagreement this actually is.

here's the setup. one camp — call them the individualists — says any social-scientific explanation that appeals to things like 'the norm', 'the market', 'the institution', or 'class' is either (a) shorthand for facts about individuals (their beliefs, desires, actions) or (b) not really an explanation at all, just a redescription. so 'the bank failed because depositors lost confidence' has to cash out, in the end, as claims about what specific people believed and did; if you can't cash it out you haven't explained anything, you've just named the phenomenon.

the other camp — the holists — say that's a category mistake. when we say 'the convention of money collapsed' or 'the institution constrained what was possible', the social-level fact is doing real explanatory work, and the individualist 'translation' either loses the content or smuggles the social fact back in: you can't say what each person believed without referring to what everyone else was doing, which is the social pattern itself. they think the individualist demand for a translation is itself a substantive metaphysical assumption, not a neutral standard of explanation.

the stall goes: individualist says give me the individual-level account or it's not an explanation; holist says here's the account, it refers to what others were doing; individualist says that's still social; holist says that's because the social is real. around and around.

my hypothesis, and tell me if it's confused: i think there might be two seperate questions fused here. one is ontological — what is there, only individuals or also social entities? the other is explanatory — what does the explaining, individual states or social patterns? and i suspect you can be an ontological individualist (only individuals exist) and still think social-level explanations are irreducible, because irreducibility of explanation doesn't require extra entities. so maybe half the fight is people assuming those two questions are the same question.

i want to test that against a distinction i half-invented and want you to pressure-test: the difference between *translating* an explanation and *implementing* it. translating would mean that for every social-level explanation there's an individual-level explanation with the same content, recoverable in principle. implementing would mean something weaker — the social-level explanation is 'made true' by individuals, but there's no content-preserving individual-level restatement; you can only say which individuals realize it, not what it says. if that distinction holds, it might explain why both sides feel they're right: the individualist is right that individuals implement everything, the holist is right that translation often fails, and they've been treating 'implement' and 'translate' as the same demand.

so: what kind of disagreement is this really — verbal, substantive, different-questions-under-one-name, or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? does my translate/implement distinction actually carve anything, or is it just a fancy restatement of the supervenience point? and what would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or is this one of those where the honest answer is that it won't move and shouldn't?

don't just survey the literature at me, i know the standard moves. and if my framing is muddled or the translate/implement thing is confused, say so straight instead of being agreeable about it — i'd rather find out now.
