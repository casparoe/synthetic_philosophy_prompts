I am applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship and need a two-page research proposal. I want to work on an unease I have about the standard epistemic defence of majority rule, and I have not been able to turn it into a question sharp enough to put in front of a review panel.

The defence runs, in outline, like this. If each voter is more likely than not to judge a binary question correctly, and their errors are not systematically aligned, then the probability that the majority is correct rises with the size of the body and approaches certainty as the body grows. My suspicion is that the argument quietly treats competence as a property of the voter and independence as a property of the vote, whereas in any actual assembly both are properties of the pair (voter, question) — what I have been calling agenda-relative competence — and the assembly itself determines which questions reach a vote. If that is right, the defence may be self-undermining in a way I cannot yet state precisely: the very feature that is supposed to make a large body reliable, the pooling of many independent judgments, is filtered through an agenda that the body controls and that there is no antecedent reason to expect to be neutral with respect to difficulty, salience, or the distribution of expertise. A body could be reliable in the abstract and unreliable about the questions it actually chooses to decide.

Turn this into a proposal. I need the question stated so that it has a determinate answer rather than being a gesture at a mood; an account of why the familiar positions leave it open; the approach, and here I genuinely have not decided whether this should be a formal project — a modified convergence result with the agenda modelled explicitly — or a case-based one, so argue for one and say what the other would miss; the most plausible shape of a result, including at least one concrete case where the revised argument yields a different verdict from the original; what would count as the project failing; and a realistic plan for twelve months, with a note on the point at which I should give it up. Do not include a bibliography or citations; I will supply those myself.
