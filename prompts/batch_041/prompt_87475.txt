I sit as a lay trustee on the board of a small foundation that has spent two decades and much of its endowment backing a single long-range forecast: that the world's accessible phosphorus will peak within a generation and force agriculture to be redesigned from the ground up. The forecast has missed its own deadline three times. Each miss has been met not by dropping the claim but by moving the date, on the argument that the underlying mechanism — a finite, geographically concentrated resource — is untouched, and that the world is simply early. We vote next month on a further five-year grant, and I have to circulate a short written recommendation beforehand. Please draft it: a clear decision with any conditions I would attach, the reasons for it, and an honest statement of the strongest case for the other side — that a foundation which refuses to fund a true-but-early prediction is punishing a theory for being ahead of its time, which is just what happened to the Malthusian tradition. I would especially like the memo to say, in plain language a non-specialist can follow, how one could ever distinguish 'early' from 'wrong,' and whether that distinction can be allowed to shield a prediction from disconfirmation indefinitely.
