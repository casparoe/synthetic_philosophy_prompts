so I run a little reading group on how people end up believing weird stuff about politics and science, and next week we're on the difference between two ways of being sealed off from the other side. I want to close the session with a poem, and I want the form to carry the distinction rather than the vocabulary, because "bubble" and "echo chamber" have gotten so sloppy that I'd honestly rather the poem never uses either phrase.

Here's the distinction as I've got it, so you can bake it in. In the first case you just never run into the other view. Nobody's hiding it from you — it's simply not in your feed, not at your job, not in your family — and if it did show up you'd probably argue with it in good faith. The second case is the opposite: you run into the other view constantly, but you've already written off anyone who holds it as either dumb or paid off, so every encounter just tightens the loop. The first is a gap you don't know is there. The second is a machine that runs on the other side's existence.

Write me a poem that makes that differnce audible. My idea, take it or leave it: a first part where the form itself has a hole in it — set up a rhyme or a refrain that the poem never pays off, so something is missing and the reader can feel the absence without being told what's absent — and then a second part that's all return and repetition, a refrain coming back so many times it stops meaning anything, so that the same kind of line that sounded like conviction in part one sounds like a tic by part two. Keep it under about thirty lines, no fixed meter needed.

The real constraint: someone who knows the distinction should be able to point at the seam where the gap turns into the machine, and someone who doesn't should still finish the poem feeling like they've stood in a room with no windows and then a room with one window that's been painted over from the inside. Don't resolve it into a moral at the end, and please don't let it turn into a poem about smartphones or social media — that's the cheap version of the whole thing.
