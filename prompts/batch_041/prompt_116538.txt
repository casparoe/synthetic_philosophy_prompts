Alright so background: I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for like ten years now, mostly the EA-adjacent corner of it, and I keep bumping into the same cluster of worries about effective altruism -- the measurability stuff (Goodhart, when a measure becomes a target etc.), the earning-to-give thing and its hidden costs, and the institutional critique (movement incentives, where funding actually flows, the fact that you don't really control your own impact). I've read enough takes that I want my local meetup to *feel* these rather than sit through another discussion post about them.

Here's my situation: I run a monthly meetup, ~18 people, mix of grad students and young professionals, some already deep in EA, some just rationalist-curious. Two hours in one room with a projector and a whiteboard, plus a break. I want to design a game/activity that makes them run into the force of these critiques themselves before anyone names them.

Rough sketch I've been kicking around, call it "the legibility machine." Phase 1: teams of 4 get a budget of 100 credits to allocate across ~5 "interventions." Each intervention card shows a measurable output and a credits-per-output ratio, so e.g. one looks amazing on paper (1 credit buys 10 units of the metric) and one looks terrible (1 credit buys 3 units). Teams race a public leaderboard scored on the metric. Then at the end I reveal a hidden "true value" multiplier per intervention -- and the ones with the best measured ratios are the ones that were already saturated / fungible / actually low-value, while the low-legibility one is the real winner. So the team that tops the leaderboard is the loser on true value. Phase 2: one volunteer plays a big outside funder who can only see the public leaderboard and has to place a big grant, which enacts the institutional critique -- your money goes where the legible signal points, not where you'd want it. Phase 3 (career): everyone privately picks earn-to-give vs direct work vs movement building, then draws event cards that load hidden costs onto each track (moral licensing, salary ratchet, the dream job turns out to require some complicity, burnout, low counterfactual impact), and we see how the private choices shake out once the hidden costs are public.

What I need from you: turn this into a actually runnable thing. Full rules and materials (the intervention cards with concrete numbers and hidden multipliers, the event deck, the leaderboard, the funder role), a payoff scheme that needs no real money, and a timeline that fits two hours with the break. Make the hidden values concrete enough that the twist lands -- don't leave it abstract. The debrief should map specific moments in the game onto each of the three critiques (measurability bias, earning to give, institutional/funding dynamics), and I want the ideas to show up in what happens, not in a lecture I give after. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of these critiques the game can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself. And one more thing -- it should feel in the weeds, not a generic "here's a dilemma" exercise. Assume the players are smart and have seen the obvious version.
