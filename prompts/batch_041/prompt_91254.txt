Ok so I've been chewing on sex work policy for about a year and I thought I'd finally landed somewhere, but a friend of mine who does labor law read my notes and said my views dont actually hang together, and now I can't stop seeing it. Before I write any of this up I want a straight audit. Here's where I am:

(1) Full decriminalization, New Zealand style — selling, buying, and third parties who run businesses. My reason is that it's the criminal law that makes the job dangerous, not the job itself.
(2) I don't buy the Nordic model's core premise, that buying sex is inherently an act of violence or exploitation against the person selling. That's moralizing, and it's the same move as saying a woman can't really be consenting to this kind of work. If it's work, it's work.
(3) That said, I do think the state has a legitimate interest in there being less of this market, not more. Not because selling is wrong, but because the people doing it usually aren't doing it from a position of full optionality, and fewer people in that position strikes me as a good thing.
(4) Legalization, Germany/Netherlands style, I'm against. Once you build a licensed industry you get registration, health requirements, brothels that cherry-pick whoever fits the paperwork, and everyone who can't or won't register — the undocumented, the street-based, the trans workers — ends up in the criminalized margins anyway. That's a two-tier system with better PR. And I dont want a registry or a special health regime under decrim either; the whole point is that the state stops treating this as its business.
(5) On method: the testimony of sex worker organizations should carry more weight than anyone else's here, since they're the ones who pay when the rest of us get it wrong.

My friend says (1) and (3) can't both be true. I half see it and half dont. So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how severe is each real conflict? Order them by severity, and for each real one tell me what has to give and what I lose by giving it up. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most mess at the lowest cost. American English please.
