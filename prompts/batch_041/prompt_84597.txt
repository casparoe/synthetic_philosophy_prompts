I am preparing the final examination for an upper-level seminar on moral responsibility and the reactive attitudes, and I need one essay question that a strong student could answer in ninety minutes. The question must not be answerable by reciting P. F. Strawson's essay on freedom and resentment; it should force students to think with his distinctions rather than summarize them. To that end, I have devised a scenario that Strawson did not consider, and I want the question to turn on it.

Imagine a community—call it the Regulated Community—whose adult members have access to a reversible neural intervention. The intervention dampens the affective components of resentment, indignation, and guilt. It does not impair memory, practical reasoning, moral judgment, or the capacity to recognize and respond to reasons. Members of the Regulated Community still deliberate about what is right, still criticize one another, still demand apologies, still impose sanctions for wrongdoing, and still modify their conduct in light of moral considerations. What they lack is the emotional sting of the reactive attitudes. They do not feel resentment when wronged, indignation when others are wronged, or guilt when they themselves do wrong. They can describe these emotions and predict them in others, but they do not undergo them. The intervention is freely chosen and can be reversed at any time.

Does Strawson's account of the reactive attitudes imply that the Regulated Community is impossible, because the reactive attitudes are constitutive of the participant stance; possible but morally impoverished, because something essential to interpersonal relationships is lost; or possible and morally adequate, because the reactive attitudes are not necessary for responsibility? In answering, students must address the distinction between excuses and exemptions, explain what Strawson means by the objective attitude and whether it is a single phenomenon, and say what the scenario reveals about the debate between the optimist and the pessimist about determinism and responsibility. They should also consider whether Strawson's own view is stable: does he treat the reactive attitudes as natural facts we cannot abandon, as justified practices, or both, and does the Regulated Community expose a tension between these claims?

Please write the exam question, phrased for students, together with a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme. The marking scheme should total 20 marks and should separate an A answer from a B answer by naming at least five specific moves that an A answer must make. For each of those moves, give the marks it is worth and a one-line note on how candidates typically get it wrong. Also include a short list of common misunderstandings that should cost marks, with a brief explanation of why each is a misunderstanding.
