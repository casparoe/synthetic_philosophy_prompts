I'm a science journalist at work on a short story for an anthology about mind and identity, and I'd like you to write the story. The premise: a near-future clinic offers dying patients the chance to have their self distributed across a small circle of volunteers — not by copying memories, but by spreading out the self-referential loop that, on the strange-loop picture, constitutes a self, so that the whole pattern exists only across several brains and never in one. My protagonist is a volunteer who agreed out of love and now lives alongside a borrowed interior life that is neither fully hers nor fully the donor's. I want the story to press the question at the heart of the strange-loop picture: once the loop is no longer closed inside a single skull, is the resulting thing still an 'I,' or has it become something else that only resembles one from the outside — and what would it be like from the inside? Dramatize the puzzle through the clinic's procedures, the protagonist's relationships, and the texture of her altered attention; please don't explain the theory or summarize the literature, since a story that glosses its own concept isn't a story.
