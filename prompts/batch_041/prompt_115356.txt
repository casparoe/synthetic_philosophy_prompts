Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Betterness is transitive, and there's a fact of the matter about which of two outcomes is worse.
(2) Aggregating tiny goods and harms across people is legit — I don't think "the separateness of persons" is a good enough reason to refuse to sum.
(3) So on the speck case (3^^^3 people each getting a dust speck vs. one person tortured for 50 years) I bite the bullet and take the torture, because the summed specks come out worse.
(4) I hold a priority view: the same benefit matters more when it lands on someone worse off.
(5) I also think there's a floor — what I've been calling a "sacrifice floor." You can't impose a serious harm on someone for the sake of a pile of individually trivial benefits, no matter how big the pile. Nobody gets fed to the machine just because everyone else would get a slightly nicer afternoon.
(6) I'm not a utilitarian and I think rights are real.

Where's the actual inconsistency?
