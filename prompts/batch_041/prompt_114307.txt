You are a philosopher of language and metaethicist with expertise in the semantics–pragmatics interface, non-cognitivist theories of meaning, and the logic of embedded clauses. Please think carefully before answering, and begin by brainstorming the possible fault lines in the argument below before you commit to a diagnosis.

A friend in my metaethics seminar has been pressing an argument on me for weeks. He thinks it refutes expressivism, but he also thinks it generalizes into something much bigger, and the bigger version is where I lose him. Here is the argument as he states it.

(1) When I utter a sentence like 'Lying is wrong', I am doing something — I am expressing a state of disapproval rather than describing a fact.
(2) The expressivist's distinctive claim is that this doing-something is not a mere side effect of the sentence's meaning; the expressing of the attitude just is what the sentence means.
(3) Now consider the sentence 'If lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong'. Here the clause 'lying is wrong' is not asserted, and I can utter the whole conditional without disapproving of lying at all.
(4) If the meaning of 'lying is wrong' were the expression of disapproval, then in (3) either the clause would carry its meaning while no disapproval is expressed — in which case meaning and use have come apart — or the clause would express disapproval, which it plainly does not.
(5) So the meaning of 'lying is wrong' cannot be the expression of disapproval; it must be a piece of detachable content that survives the loss of the act of disapproving.
(6) Therefore expressivism is false.
(7) But nothing in the reasoning from (3) to (6) depended on its being morality we were talking about. Run the same steps for 'Hooray for the rain', for 'Alas, it is raining', for a bare interjection like 'Ouch', for the imperative 'Shut the door', and for an epistemic modal like 'It might be raining'. In every case, embedding strips away the doing and leaves behind a meaning; so in every case the meaning cannot be the doing.
(8) So there is no expression anywhere whose meaning is its use. Every meaningful expression must carry a detachable, assertoric-style content.

I can live with (6) if I have to — I am not even sure I am an expressivist. What I cannot live with is (8). It seems to me we do know what 'ouch' and 'hooray' mean, and that their meaning has something essentially to do with what they are used to do, and I do not want to be forced to say that 'ouch' is secretly a tiny description of the speaker's inner state. So I assume something in the chain is broken, but I cannot find the break, and the usual things people say to me — 'the expressivist distinguishes force from content', 'the meaning is a rule for use, not the use itself' — sound to me like restatements of the problem rather than solutions to it.

Here is what I want from you. First, identify the step or steps where the argument goes wrong, if it does, and be specific about the type of error: is there an equivocation, a slide from a claim about a particular utterance to a claim about a sentence type, a scope or quantifier shift, or a step that is valid only under a reading of the earlier premises that the expressivist is not committed to? Second, explain why the offending step looked innocent — I want to understand the temptation, not just the verdict. Third, price the alternatives: for each premise I could reject to block the argument, tell me what that rejection costs me elsewhere. In particular, say what becomes of the embedding cases for truth-apt discourse if I reject (3) or (4); what becomes of 'ouch' and 'hooray' if I reject (8); and whether the residual problem — the one that survives after the equivocation is repaired, namely how the expressivist assigns semantic values to negations, disjunctions, and conditionals containing evaluative clauses — is a genuine debt or a bookkeeping problem. I would most like to keep (1) and (3) and the denial of (8); tell me whether I can have all three, and if not, which one I should surrender and what I lose by surrendering it.
