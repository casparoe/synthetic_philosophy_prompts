I'm a fourth-year PhD student midway through a chapter on the doing/allowing distinction, and I've hit a construction problem I can't solve by staring harder at the usual examples. My suspicion — and I want the case to test it rather than assume it — is that the line between doing and allowing does no independent work: once the cases are cleaned up, it turns out to be a proxy for either causal authorship (whose agency put the threat into the world) or the intended/foreseen line. The trouble is that in the familiar cases all three run together and nothing gets tested. The well-worn pair in which one man holds a child under the water while another stands by a bath and lets a child drown he could easily have pulled out makes both men intend the death, and it makes the author of the threat the one who moves his body. What I need from you is a case in which these criteria are pulled apart, and pulled apart in opposite directions, so that the doing/allowing line ends up alone on one side of the ledger. So: two agents, one ordinary scenario, in which the agent who comes out worse by the doing/allowing criterion is the better of the two by the intended/foreseen criterion, and the agent who merely allows the death is the one who intends it. The first agent does something that kills the victim, but only as a foreseen side effect of an end she actually has; the second keeps still, and her stillness is the means by which she gets what she wants, which is the victim's death. Hold everything else as fixed as you can: the same outcome and the same badness, alternatives equally available to both, no special role duties (no on-duty lifeguard, no parent), no prior relationship to the victim (a competent adult stranger to both), neither agent cruel or gloating, and the causal facts as close to symmetric as the world allows, so that neither contribution is the more decisive one and no verdict can be read off causation. Let the hazard be one that neither of them made — a natural one — so that authorship is neutral between them and the doing/allowing line has to carry the case on its own. The omission should also be sustained rather than a lapse: I want the second agent to have to keep not acting, so that even calling it an omission is already a bit of a stretch.
