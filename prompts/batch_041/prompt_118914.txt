I'm writing the critical chapter of my dissertation on non-empirical confirmation in fundamental physics, and I want to test my central argument before I commit to it. As I see it, two considerations settle the matter. First, the multiverse: to argue from the fact that our universe is fine-tuned for life to the existence of many universes is to commit the inverse gambler's fallacy — the error of a gambler who walks in on a double six and concludes that the dice must have been rolled many times before. Second, the plea made on behalf of string theory, that we should give it credence because no one has produced a viable alternative, is just an appeal to our ignorance: our failure to imagine an alternative tells us nothing about the world. Taken together, these suggest that so-called non-empirical confirmation is not a form of confirmation at all, but the wish that a theory could be accepted without evidence, dressed up in Bayesian notation.

Please act as a philosopher of physics with expertise in evidence, confirmation, and the interpretation of quantum cosmology, and think carefully before you answer. I don't need citations or a reading list — I need to know whether the argument as I've framed it holds up.
