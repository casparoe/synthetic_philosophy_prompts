I am a novelist, and one of my characters is a philosopher of mathematics with a weakness for telling jokes at dinner. I should like you to write one for her to tell, and it has to be a joke for people who know what a supertask is: funny because of the mathematics, not because lamps and infinities are incongruous when placed side by side.

Let the humour turn on the substance of Thomson's lamp — on the fact that the toggling is specified only up to the end of the task, so that the lamp's condition at the end has to be stipulated rather than discovered, and on the thought that this is what makes the task impossible to carry out rather than merely baffling. If you can, let it also glance at the contrasting case, a supertask whose outcome is fixed by its description and merely counter-intuitive — the urn that stands empty at the end although ten balls went in for every one taken out — so that one can feel the difference between a puzzle with a shocking answer and a puzzle with none at all.

Keep it short enough to be told across a table, and do not have anyone explain it afterwards.
