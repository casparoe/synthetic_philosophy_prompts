I am enrolled in a third-year seminar on moral responsibility and the ethics of blame, and I am trying to anticipate, before our final session, how the instructor will rule on the case she has set for discussion. Across the term she has issued verdicts on a series of cases turning on whether one person has the standing to blame another, and I would rather reconstruct the rule or rules she is actually applying than simply guess at her conclusion.

Her verdicts so far have been these.

(1) Nadia has been unfaithful to every partner she has had and is currently cheating on her husband. She is a colleague of Tom's and has no connection to Tom's partner. She tells Tom that he is contemptible for cheating on his partner. The instructor held that Nadia lacks the standing to blame Tom.

(2) Ken, the brother of Tom's partner, has never been unfaithful. He tells Tom the same thing. The instructor held that Ken has standing.

(3) Lena, Tom's partner, who has never been unfaithful, blames Tom. The instructor held that Lena has standing.

(4) Marcus, a close friend of Tom's partner, has never been unfaithful and condemns infidelity, though he is a repeat drunk driver. He tells Tom the same thing. The instructor held that Marcus has standing.

In passing, the instructor once remarked, only half-seriously, that the sole thing that really matters is whether the person doing the blaming would be willing to accept the very same criticism herself, and that everything else is a distraction.

The new case is the following. Priya cheated on several partners in her twenties and thirties; roughly fifteen years ago she stopped, has been faithful ever since, and now sincerely regards infidelity as seriously wrong. She is a colleague of Tom's with no other connection to him or to his partner, and she has never met his partner. She tells Tom that what he has done is contemptible.

Please predict the instructor's verdict on Priya's case. To make the prediction defensible, state the competing rules that fit her verdicts on cases (1) through (4); say what each of those rules predicts about Priya; give your best guess together with the reasons for preferring it; and describe one further case that would decide between the rules that remain in contention, indicating what verdict on that case would favour which rule. I would ask you not to cite any literature or name any authors, since I have to defend the prediction in class on the strength of the argument alone.
