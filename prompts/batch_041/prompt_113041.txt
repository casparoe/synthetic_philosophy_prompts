I keep circling back to Nietzsche's three ways of taking up the past — monumental, antiquarian, critical — and which of them does the most damage when it curdles. But I want to throw a fourth into the ring that I don't think he had in mind: outsourced history, where a person's whole sense of the past is just whatever a feed or a search box hands them that day, no effort and no selection they've made themselves. Could you rank all four — monumental, antiquarian, critical, outsourced — from most to least dangerous to someone's capacity to actually live and act, and give each a score out of 10 for how much it threatens that capacity? I want a definately committed ordering, not a tidy list of pros and cons — pick your top and your bottom and stand behind them. And if you reckon my fourth category is a dud, or is really just one of the other three wearing a costume, say so plainly and rank on that basis instead. Write your answer in American English.
