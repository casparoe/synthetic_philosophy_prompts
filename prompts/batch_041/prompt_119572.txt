the thing is this: normally we think you can only cooperate with someone if you can actually affect them, or send them a message, or at least meet them. but theres a way of thinking about decisions where what you choose is evidence about what other people in the same kind of situation choose, even when nothing you ever do reaches them. so two people who can never meet, never signal, can't affect each other at all might still sort of cooperate, because me picking the friendly option is a sign that the other one picked it too. on a small scale thats just a curiosity. but if the world is genuinely big - trillions of galaxies, other civilisations out there, or even just loads of agents that reason the way I do - then there are an enormous number of those unreachable agents, and the payoffs from all of them add up. and the strange bit is that this can mean the biggest thing riding on a decision I make in my kitchen is what some beings I can never meet end up doing, while what they do is tied to what I do in the evidence sense and not in any physical sense. I think theres something there but I also think theres a way of stating it that makes it sound like obvious nonsense, and I cant yet tell which bits are doing the work.
