Next term I'm giving the keynote at a fairly mixed one-day workshop on "societal collapse" — about forty people, mostly archaeologists, but with a handful of palaeoclimatologists and two or three complexity-science types who actually build the collapse models, and one or two anthropologists who get visibly twitchy whenever anyone says "society" as though it were a thing that can have a lifespan. I want a Q&A prep sheet, because the last time I gave this I got filleted in the discussion and I'd rather not repeat it.

My line is roughly this. What we call a collapse is usually an artefact of the unit of analysis the observer happened to pick. The thing that actually fails in these episodes is a particular mode of regional integration — the tribute flows, the long-distance exchange, the shared ritual idiom, the elite network that holds a landscape together politically — and when that integration goes, the populations mostly don't. They carry on, at a lower level of regional connectivity, often for centuries, and we call it a collapse because we were tacitly treating the integrated polity as the natural unit and its dissolution as a death. So the interesting variable isn't "did the society collapse", it's the scale at which coordination stopped working and who got to decide that this counted as an ending. I also want to say something sharper: most of the contemporary uses of the word — the decline narratives, the warnings aimed at us — smuggle in that same commitment, and that's why they keep sliding between descriptive and moral registers without anyone noticing.

The audience is the problem. The processualist will want a measurable proxy and will suspect I've redefined collapse until it's unfalsifiable. The palaeoclimatologist will think the whole thing is a distraction from the climate signal that's plainly in the record. The complexity modeller will say my "reorganisation" is just a lower-energy attractor and that's collapse by any name you like. And the anthropologist will say the unit was wrong from the start and I'm still assuming something called a society exists to fail.
