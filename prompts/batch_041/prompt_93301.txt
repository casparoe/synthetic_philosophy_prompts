I am putting together a short position paper for a graduate seminar on Plato, and before I defend it I want an audit of whether the following commitments can be held together. I state them as my own theses, not as claims about what any particular dialogue says.

(1) There are non-sensible, mind-independent objects, the Forms, and sensible things owe their character and their being to them: what a sensible thing is, it is by participating in a Form, and its being is derivative rather than self-standing.

(2) Each Form is what it is on its own. It is not identical with any sensible thing; it does not depend for its existence on sensible things or on any mind that grasps it; and no sensible thing is F in the same sense, or to the same degree, in which the Form of F is F.

(3) Each Form actually possesses the character it is the Form of. Beauty is beautiful, Equality is equal, Largeness is large. I take the Forms to be paradigms, that is, perfect instances as well as standards, and not merely concepts, rules, or the meanings of general terms.

(4) Knowledge and belief are distinct powers with distinct objects. Knowledge is of the Forms and belief is of sensible particulars; there is no knowledge of a sensible particular as such, and no belief, however well founded, that has a Form as its object.

(5) The ascent out of the cave can be made in this life. Education is the turning of the soul, and a mind can come to grasp the Forms while still embodied. Those who have made the ascent are nonetheless obliged to go back down and govern, and it is just, not merely expedient, that they do so.

(6) Justice is good for the person who has it. The just person is better off than the unjust, and not merely because of the consequences of being just.

What I want is an audit rather than a summary. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, which pairs only appear to conflict and turn out to be compatible once the right distinctions are drawn, and how severe is each genuine tension? Order the genuine tensions from most to least serious. For each, tell me which commitment would have to be weakened or abandoned, what the cost of that revision is, and whether the same tension could be relieved some other way at lower cost; then say which single revision would resolve the most while conceding the least. Assume that I do not already know where the trouble lies: if two of these cannot both stand, say so plainly rather than reassuring me that the package is broadly coherent. I am not asking for a reading list or for citations to the secondary literature; I want the structural problem. Please answer in British English.
