I am a public health epidemiologist, and my working vocabulary is the standard burden-of-disease machinery: person-time at risk, life tables, years of life lost and disability-adjusted life years, competing risks, exposure-response functions, attributable fractions, the counterfactual ideal that defines a burden estimate, and the sensitivity analyses we run when exposure is not randomized. I have followed the debate over wild animal welfare only as a careful amateur.

I would like that debate restated in my terms. The question at issue is whether habitat destruction — forest clearance, conversion to agriculture, urban expansion — reduces or increases the net suffering of wild animals. Three positions are in play. The first holds that it reduces suffering, because it reduces the number of wild animals, most of whom live short lives that end in hunger, predation, or disease. The second holds that it increases suffering, because it crowds and concentrates the survivors, degrades what habitat remains, and shifts the community toward species with enormous juvenile mortality. The third holds that the question is underdetermined as posed, since the answer depends on the choice of baseline and on whether welfare is aggregated in total or per capita.

Restate each position, as faithfully as you can, as a burden-of-disease claim: exposure, outcome, denominator, counterfactual, effect measure. Then give me the residue report. What translates cleanly? What translates only under distortion? What cannot be said in my vocabulary at all? Three difficulties seem likely to me — the outcome is not ascertainable, the exposure is not exogenous, and the population whose burden I would estimate is itself brought into and out of existence by the exposure, so that even the notion of a population at risk is strained — but I want your judgment on whether each of these is a defect of my framework or a sign that the original claim was never as precise as it sounded.

Two further things. I would instinctively want to report a quantity such as suffering-years averted per hectare converted, the way I would report cases averted per hundred thousand person-years; tell me whether that is a coherent analogue or whether it silently assumes the very thing in dispute. And tell me what my vocabulary would force the disputants to make explicit that they now leave unsaid, and whether the disagreements that survive translation are disagreements about measurement or about values. Argue it through; I do not want a list of sources.
