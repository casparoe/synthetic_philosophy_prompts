For a while now I've been following arguments about what an artificial intelligence should ultimately be trying to do on our behalf, and I keep finding two camps that talk past each other. The first holds that the target should be a cleaned-up version of what we want — our preferences as they would be if we were fully informed, thinking clearly, and free of our own inconsistencies — so that we don't freeze our present ignorance and confusion into something permanent; the term of art I've seen for this is "coherent extrapolated volition." The second holds that this is a recipe for someone else deciding what we really want, and that the target should instead be what people actually want now, handled with care: served where it can be known, left revisable, deferred to future people, and never locked in. I have no academic training; I've just been reading and thinking about this on my own. What I want is the document these two people should be able to sign together.

Please write it. Open with a numbered list of claims both sides can accept — about what each of them means by a "preference," what each agrees is genuinely wrong with the other's view when it's stated crudely, and what they agree the real problem is once the crude version is set aside. Then set out their remaining disagreements, each written in the strongest form its own side would accept rather than a caricature. Then list the cruxes — the sub-questions that, if settled, would actually move one of them — and for each crux give a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with what each side predicts about it written down in advance, and what each would count as evidence against its own position.

Three things I keep turning over, which I'd like you to fold into the cruxes rather than answer outright. First: a person may right now, calmly and after reflection, want one thing while knowing they would want another if they learned certain facts and reasoned it through — whose want is really theirs? Second: some of our wants were shaped by conditions we would ourselves reject — should the cleaning-up correct only our factual mistakes and leave our values untouched, or must it reach the values too, and if so, on whose authority? Third: is there even a single cleaned-up answer, or are there many, and does that tell against the whole idea being usable as a target at all?

If any part of this dispute turns out to be about words rather than substance, say which part and why. Keep the whole thing under 900 words, and present the disagreements as a table.
