I'm writing a novel where the heroine keeps waking into slightly different versions of 1930s Europe, and my editor — who did a history degree years ago — says the whole conceit is naive. Her line is: history is just structural forces (economics, geography, demography) grinding along, the great-men business is froth, so counterfactual history is a parlour game — nudge one thing and the whole causal web re-tangles so you can't say what would've happened, and its all inevitable anyway. She also reckons the philosophers are on her side: that a claim like 'if the archduke had stayed in bed, there'd have been no world war' isn't even true or false, because you can't rerun history, and since there are no laws of history like the laws of physics there's nothing to ground a counterfactual, and determinism means everything that happened definately had to.

Is she right? I don't want a reading list — just take it apart: which bits a philosopher of history would nod along to, which are quietly wrong, and which are just conflations of two things that should be kept apart. And tell me whether her real conclusion — that alt-history is pure fantasy with no grip on how things could actually have gone — follows from the parts that are true, or only from the parts that aren't. I mainly want to know where the line falls between a counterfactual that's respectable and one that's just wishful thinking, because that line is basically my book.
