I'm assembling a handout for a seminar on consent and want a sorting quiz: "Which theory of consent validity do you actually hold?" Write eight questions, each built from one of the cases below, with three answer options apiece and a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the live positions. Phrase every option as a verdict on the case — "her consent was valid," "it was invalid," "it was valid, but he still wronged her" — never as a label, and never ask anything like "do you think deception invalidates consent?", so that a reader who has never heard of any of these positions can still answer honestly.

Cases: (a) a surgeon calls a 1-in-2,000 risk of permanent nerve damage "rare" and says no more; (b) a man tells his partner he is sterile, knowing this to be false; she consents to sex and conceives; (c) a kidney seller accepts a fee well below what the intermediary is paid, having been told that this is the going rate; (d) a woman in early dementia can restate the risks of a trial treatment but cannot compare the probabilities, and agrees while her daughter objects; (e) a prisoner volunteers for a study that shortens his sentence; (f) a user clicks "I agree" to terms permitting the company to train models on her messages, unread; (g) a patient says "whatever you think best" and the surgeon performs a procedure she would have refused had she been asked; (h) a man agrees to sex he knows he will regret in the morning, drunk but lucid.

The key should separate at least: the view that deception invalidates consent only when it defeats what the consenter was actually agreeing to; the view that validity turns instead on whether the deceiver owed disclosure; subjective versus objective standards for coercion; the view that consent is a normative power rather than a mental state; competence as decision-relative versus a fixed threshold; and the separatist view that valid consent and wrongful conduct can coexist. Note which combinations of answers leave a position unoccupied, and say whether that is because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; flag any positions the quiz cannot tell apart. American English.
