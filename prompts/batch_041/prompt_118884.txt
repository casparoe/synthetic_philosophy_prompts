The claim that dreams are not experiences is defended along two very different lines, and I want the difference between them made precise. The first line, the Wittgensteinian one associated with Malcolm, is grammatical or verificationist: a first-person report of a dream is not a report of anything observed, so a dream is not an episode one is aware of while it happens. The second line, associated with Dennett, concerns temporal and phenomenal structure: if the only fact of the matter about a dream is the narrative offered on waking, then there need be no determinate episode unfolding during sleep for that report to be a report of. Explain what each line denies, what each is committed to about the epistemic standing of the dream report — evidence about a dream episode, or the only thing there is to be right or wrong about — and whether the second line can be held without the first or quietly presupposes it. Then tell me whether a third position I keep returning to is stable: that a dream is an experience, only a retrospective one, namely the waking experience of assembling the narrative. Finally, the dream argument in skepticism about the senses assumes that dreaming is a state of apparent perceiving; say what becomes of that premise, and of the argument, on each of the two lines. Give me a definite verdict on which line is stronger and on whether the retrospective view survives, with your reasons, rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me.
