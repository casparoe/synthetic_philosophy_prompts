I'm trying to get the semantics and pragmatics of slurs straight, and I've come to think the usual hand-wringing about them is overblown. Here is my reasoning. A slur's derogatory force is not part of its truth-conditional content; it is carried by an expressive or presuppositional layer that sits alongside the descriptive meaning. But content at the pragmatic level is cancellable — we cancel conversational implicatures routinely, and we can suspend a presupposition in a conditional or under negation. So it should be possible to neutralize a slur simply by embedding it in a context that explicitly disavows the derogation: an indirect report of someone else's speech, a conditional, a reductio, or the in-group reclaimed use. Suppose a community coins a term, say 'Glim', that begins as a slur against some group and later gets taken up within that group as an affectionate self-description; on my picture, the original insult is cancelled in those uses and the word simply becomes a label with an unfortunate history. Yet theorists keep insisting that even mentioning or reclaiming a slur leaves the derogation fully intact and continues to wound. Why do they say this? Isn't the insistence just a refusal to let cancellation do its work — a confusion of use with mention?
