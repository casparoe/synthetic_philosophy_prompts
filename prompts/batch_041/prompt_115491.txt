Among philosophers of science there is a dispute about Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes that has now run for half a century without either side moving, and what I want from you is a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict for one side. The apparatus itself is common ground: the hard core, the protective belt, the positive and negative heuristics, the contrast between progressive and degenerating problem shifts. The disagreement concerns what the apparatus is for. One camp holds that the criterion of progressiveness is available only retrospectively — that at the moment when a scientist must decide whether to defend the hard core or modify it, the methodology yields no verdict, and that a standard which can only ratify what has already happened is a way of narrating history rather than a way of guiding inquiry. On this reading the rational reconstruction is circular as well, since the historian's antecedent judgement about which episodes were progressive is precisely what the reconstruction then reports back as its result. The other camp replies that this misreads the ambition: the methodology was never offered as an algorithm for live decisions, rationality need not be instantaneous, and comparative judgements about programmes over an interval are substantive claims that the historical record can confirm or defeat.

My own hypothesis, which I hold loosely, is that the two camps are not disagreeing about the apparatus or about the history, but about what a methodology of science is supposed to deliver, and that they may be answering different questions under a single name: how the growth of knowledge is to be reconstructed, as against how a working scientist should decide what to do next. I would like that hypothesis tested rather than assumed. Two distinctions I would ask you to take seriously as tests of it: first, between the appraisal of a programme and the appraisal of a move within it; second, between degeneracy as a property of a trajectory across an interval and degeneracy as a property of a programme at a moment. If the disagreement survives those distinctions, say what kind of disagreement it is — verbal, substantive, evidential, or a clash of starting intuitions that neither side can argue for — and whether anything short of a change of question could resolve it; if it dissolves, identify exactly which distinction does the work. Either way, tell me what would count as progress here: a distinction, a historical claim that could be checked, a formal result, or nothing at all. Contested retrospective verdicts may be used as test cases — a hypothesis dismissed in its day as unfalsifiable and later vindicated, or a programme that looked progressive and then stalled — but I am after the structure of the impasse, not a survey of the literature.
