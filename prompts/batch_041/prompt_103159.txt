I write fiction, and I'm assembling a sequence of stories, each organized around a single philosophical problem that I want dramatized rather than explained. The one I'm working on now is the old puzzle about universals. Two different things can both be blue, or both be chairs, and it seems they share something — a single item, blueness or chairhood, somehow present in each of them; but if it is one item, how can it be in many places at once, and if it is many, what makes them all count as the same? I don't want a story that stages a debate among named philosophers. Instead, here is the device I'd like you to take seriously rather than treat as a joke: an archive whose keepers catalogue qualities themselves, not the things that have them, and whose hardest and most disputed case is a quality that, for the moment, nothing in the world instantiates. The story should make us feel what turns on whether such an uninstantiated quality is genuinely a quality or only an empty slot in a filing system. Please write it as a short story, under about 1,500 words, with a single point of view and a concrete setting, letting the problem surface through the characters' work rather than through exposition.
