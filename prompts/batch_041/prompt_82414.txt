My brother's a type designer and I want to put a joke in his birthday card, and the only thing he really cares about is letterforms. So the joke has to be about the letter 'a' — specifically about what actually makes every 'a' an 'a'.

Here's the material I want it built on, since he'll definately be annoyed if I get it wrong. Take a Garamond 'a', a Helvetica 'a', the 'a' in my six-year-old's handwriting, and the 'a' on a neon sign: they share basically zero pixels. The only thing they have in common is that we read them all as 'a'. 'a' is also one of the only two letters that comes in both a double-story and a single-story version (the other one's 'g'), which makes it look even more like there's no single shape at the bottom of it. Someone would say the grapheme is one thing and all the allographs are just variants, which sounds like it settles everything until you ask what the grapheme itself actually is. And there's this old AI project — Hofstadter and Gary McGraw, it's a chapter in Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies — where the program had to design a whole alphabet in one consistent style, and the hard part was that the more style it poured into a letter, the closer it got to a shape that was gorgeous and no longer that letter. So two pressures fight each other: still be an 'a', and be in the spirit of this particular alphabet.

Write me the joke. Requirements: the punchline has to turn on that real tension (category membership vs. style, or the idea that 'a' has no single essence, just a family of shapes and roles like bowl, stem, counter), not on some generic letter pun. No "a letter walks into a bar." No pun on 'a' as an article, that's the cheap one. It should be funny to someone who actually knows this stuff — if he reads it and thinks I have no idea what a letterform is, I've blown it. One joke, doesn't need to be long, a few sentences or a short scene is fine, and please don't tack an explanation on at the end.
