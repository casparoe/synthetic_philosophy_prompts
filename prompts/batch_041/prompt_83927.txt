Background, then the ask. I've been training little agents on gridworlds and I keep noticing a move — in other peoples writing and my own. Someone points at an agent that gives up reward to escape some state, or that will press a button that switches off a mild penalty, and treats that as evidence the state is unpleasant to it. Or they point at the negative term in the objective and say that's the pain. Both seem to me to prove nothing, or prove too much: the agent was built to maximise that number, so of course it dodges what lowers it, and you can get identical avoidance out of an agent that was never given any negative reward at all, just shaped to expect a later loss. Flip the setup and the behaviour mirrors, but nobody would say the mirror agent hurts. Still, I don't think the question of whether it suffers is empty — something feels like it's being missed and I can't name it.

Does this bind have an established name? I want the bind specifically, not the general worry: the only evidence for the inner state is exactly the behaviour the training setup was defined to produce, so "it hurts" and "it's a reward maximiser" predict the same data, and yet the moral question stays live. If there's no term, coin one and give me its edges — how it differs from the problem of other minds, from anthropomorphism, from functionalism about pain, and from the hard problem — with a couple of borderline cases. No reading list please, I want the carving, not the citations.
