I'm drafting a section of my dissertation that tries to distinguish genuine strange loops from ordinary feedback, and I need a worked case rather than another programmatic statement. Please construct a thought experiment to the following specifications.

Set-up. By a tangled hierarchy I mean a system with two or more levels of description in which influence runs in both directions — the higher level constrains what happens at the lower, and the lower in turn determines the state of the higher — so that tracing the levels in either direction brings you back to where you started. By the inviolate level I mean the level at which the rules constituting the tangle are fixed: a level that is not itself one of the tangled levels and so is untouched by the circularity, though it can be altered from outside the tangle.

Specifications for the case. (i) The scenario must be mundane — something that could occur in an office, a market, a city, or a biological system. Excluded: paradox sentences (no Liar, no Quine), impossible or self-depicting figures in the manner of Escher, time travel, quantum measurement, and self-modifying programs. (ii) Both directions of determination must be shown rather than asserted; I want the mechanism by which the lower level shapes the higher. (iii) The tangle must be constitutive, not merely epistemic — it must not be that the loop appears only because some observer lacks information. (iv) It must not be a bare part-whole relation, nor a simple causal loop with a single external driver. (v) At least one level must be described in normative or intentional vocabulary (a rule, a role, a convention) and at least one in mechanical vocabulary, so that the tangle crosses a vocabulary boundary. (vi) Name the inviolate level, show that it lies outside both tangled levels, and demonstrate what altering it does: changing the inviolate level must change the tangle, whereas acting from inside either tangled level can only reshuffle matters within the rules. (vii) The inviolate level must not be the laws of physics, God, or the programmer; it should be something a participant could in principle act upon, and the case should show what that action looks like. (viii) The scenario itself, apart from the analysis, should be no longer than a paragraph.

Then run the checks. (a) Delete the inviolate level and say exactly what results: a genuine vicious circle or infinite regress, not merely a system that is harder to analyse; if the case does not collapse, say so and revise it. (b) Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, and say what happens if that feature is removed. (c) Give the cheapest near-miss: a case that looks like a tangled hierarchy with an inviolate level but is really a well-founded loop with a hidden external cause, and state the diagnostic that separates it from your case. (d) State the strongest objection a critic will raise — most likely that your inviolate level is doing all the causal work and the tangle is decorative, or that your case is an ordinary control loop with a set point — and give the reply.

What the case is for: I need a worked example showing that circularity of levels does not by itself imply vicious circularity, provided there is a level at which the rules are fixed. A case that merely stipulates mutual determination, or that smuggles the inviolate level in as the real cause of everything, fails the brief; the circularity and its resolution have to fall out of the described mechanism.
