Right — I've just retired after forty years in a genetics lab, and rather to my surprise I've spent the last two years reading philosophy of language, mostly the stuff about how much of language is built in. I've come to a settled-ish view and I'd like a sanity check before I start boring my old colleagues with it. Here's where I've landed:

1. The poverty of the stimulus argument seems to me basically right. Kids end up with structure-dependent rules — they'll move the first auxiliary in the sentence rather than the one nearest the subject, or whatever the current example is — when nothing in the speech around them could tell them to do that. So some chunk of linguistic knowledge isn't learnt from the input.

2. I'm a Darwinian down to my boots. Whatever a mind comes with, it comes with because selection and development put it there. So if something is genuinely innate, there's got to be a story about how it got built, bit by bit, over evolutionary time.

3. But I've also watched a fair bit of what the big language models can now do, and honestly it's made me sceptical of a special language organ. If a domain-general learner chewing through text can end up with grammar, I struggle to see why we'd need a bespoke module.

4. I'm careful with the word 'innate' itself. I know linguists use it one way — roughly, 'not derived from experience' — and biologists use it another — 'genetically specified' — and I don't assume those two always line up. In the language case I think they both come out true, so it's never worried me much.

5. And I don't think it's all-or-nothing. Some bits of grammar are probably learnt and some probably aren't, and the interesting question is which is which.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how badly? Rank the real clashes by how serious they are, tell me for each what the cheapest fix would be and what I'd be giving up by taking it, and then tell me whether there's one single thing I could change that would clear up the most trouble at the least cost. I've a nagging feeling that two of these can't all be true at once, but I honestly can't see which two it is.
