I chair the trustees of the Wrenfield Trust, a grant-making charity endowed by a single donor a century ago with a broad educational remit. A faction of the board wants us to adopt a Stewardship Charter that would entrench our current policy of not making grants or investments for the purpose of advancing contested political causes. As drafted, the charter could be repealed only by a two-thirds vote of the sitting trustees at each of two meetings held at least twelve months apart, with the reasons for repeal published in full at the first of those meetings; and once repealed, the policy could not be re-enacted for five years. The board is split, and I must circulate a written opinion before the vote.

Please draft that opinion, of roughly a thousand words. It should recommend a concrete course of action — adopt the charter as drafted, adopt it with specified amendments, or reject it — and give the reasons the trustees could actually argue with, including the strongest case for the side you come out against. Two matters deserve sustained attention rather than a paragraph each. First, whether the logic of a precommitment device — the sort of thing that works because the later self shares the earlier self's ends but expects to be weak-willed — survives translation to a body whose successors may simply disagree about ends rather than suffer a failure of will; if it does not survive, say what, if anything, the charter can still legitimately do. Second, whether the amendment clause I have described is well designed: consider what it actually accomplishes, whether its cooling-off period and its five-year bar on re-enactment are symmetrical or perverse, and whether a different trigger or threshold would do the work better.

Argue from the merits rather than appealing to named authorities, and write in British English. Address the opinion to the trustees, with a clear bottom line in the first paragraph and the reasoning after it.
