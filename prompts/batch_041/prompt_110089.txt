I'm a fifth-year PhD student in the history and philosophy of science, and this year I sit as the graduate representative on my university's Environmental Humanities steering committee. The committee is finalizing the intellectual framing for a new cross-departmental program, and a dispute has broken out over whether we should periodize the program's historical arc around "the Anthropocene," and if so, where the period begins. One faction wants to anchor the whole thing at the mid-twentieth century, pointing to the unusually sharp and globally synchronous signals in that window; a second insists the story really starts with the early modern collision of continents and the demographic catastrophe that followed, and that the mid-century framing quietly absolves the earlier violence; a third argues that the very idea of a single global boundary is a category mistake, since what we're really dealing with are many uneven, locally timed transitions that no single line can capture. I've been asked to draft the committee's decision memo before our next meeting, and because I'm the only member with any philosophy-of-science background, everyone expects me to say something about what a period boundary even is and who has standing to draw it.

Please act as a philosopher of science with expertise in periodization, stratigraphy, and the value-ladenness of classification, and write that memo. I'd like you to begin by laying out, in your own words, the strongest version of each of the three positions before you commit to anything -- I don't want a survey that treats them as equally defensible. Then recommend a concrete resolution: does the committee adopt the Anthropocene as an organizing period, and if so, on what criterion should the boundary be drawn, and who should be treated as authorized to draw it -- our committee, the relevant natural-science bodies, or the affected publics? Give me an actionable bottom line with a timeline for the decision, engage the losing side's strongest consideration honestly rather than strawmanning it, and be explicit about what is lost under whatever criterion you choose.

One thing I'd like you to address head-on, because I keep getting stuck on it: if a boundary is selected because it is pedagogically or politically useful for a program like ours, is that a good enough reason to draw it there, or does a period boundary have to answer to some standard of getting the world right that those considerations simply cannot supply? Please be concrete, take a definite line, and resist hedging everything into "it depends."
