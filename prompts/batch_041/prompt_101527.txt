I've ended up refereeing a two-year argument between two friends and I want to force them to write the document they should of produced ages ago. One of them — call her the arc person — thinks there's a genuine directional tendency in large-scale human history towards less arbitrary domination (call that justice, roughly; we can fight about the definition later). The other — the tragedy person — thinks any apparent direction is a selection effect of how we narrate history: real gains happen, but they're local, reversible, and best explained by particular institutional accidents rather than by any tendency. They agree on heaps of facts and still talk straight past each other, so write the joint statement they could both put their names to after a proper good-faith adverserial collaboration.

What I want: a numbered section of agreed claims — including claims about what would actually count as evidence for or against a directional tendency, and the fact that both of them reject the naive version of their own view — then a disagreements table. In the table, for each genuine disagreement, give the wording each side would honestly accept for its own position (no strawmen), and mark each as substantive or merely verbal. I've a hunch at least one of them is verbal — probably about what 'tendency' or 'direction' even means — so say which, and why.

Then the cruxes. My hunches, but use your judgement: (a) the rewind test — if you reran the last five hundred years a thousand times with tiny perturbations, in what fraction of runs do you get something like the abolition of chattel slavery and universal suffrage, and does either side's number actually discriminate between the two views? (b) the unit question — whether the choice of currency (well-being, rights, non-domination, something else) is doing the real work, so that the disagreement shrinks or swells depending which you pick; (c) whether moral progress is self-reinforcing or self-undermining, i.e. whether gains tend to create the conditions for their own reversal, and how you'd tell; and (d) whether the alleged arc is a global tendency at all or a provincial one that just happens to run through Western modernity. For each crux, give the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance.

Don't bother deciding who's right, and please no citations, footnotes or reading lists — neither of them has library access and I don't want it collapsing into a bibliography. Can you do it in British English as well.
