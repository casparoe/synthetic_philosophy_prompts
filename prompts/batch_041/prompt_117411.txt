I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about the wasp that drags a paralyzed cricket to the mouth of its burrow, goes inside to inspect, and — as the story is usually told — begins the whole performance again from the start if the cricket has been shifted a few inches in the meantime. My friend treats this as proof that behavior which looks purposeful may be nothing but a script that runs to completion, and that we are no different in kind. That argument I know how to have. What I could not name, and want help with, is a pattern I keep meeting in people, including myself.

Someone comes to see that a piece of her behavior is a routine: she always defends herself the same way, always ends up in the same quarrel. She can set the routine out accurately in advance, and she is right about it. She then runs it again, unchanged, while narrating it — and the narrating is not a brake on the routine but a new part of it. Tell her the commentary is itself on schedule and she agrees, adds a commentary on the commentary, and carries on as before. Each level of self-description is absorbed into the thing described, so that she ends up not less rigid but more: she now has a settled account of her own rigidity, which she can recite while the routine proceeds. No felt conflict, no regret; she is not fighting herself and losing, she is reading out the mechanism and continuing.

Does this have a name? My guess is that it does not, or only partly: the wasp's condition is named, but that name is applied from outside to something that cannot notice itself, whereas here the noticing is real, accurate, and still part of the mechanism. If it is a distinct thing, say what it should be called. If it has no name, coin one, and be exact about the edges — how it differs from ordinary weakness of will, from the self-deception in which the person simply has the story wrong, and from the case in which she is lying about her motives. A bare label will not satisfy me; I want the carving. The stakes are my argument with my friend: if there is a kind of self-knowledge that leaves the knower exactly as mechanical as before, and more so because the knowledge has been absorbed, then noticing one's own scripts is no evidence of the flexibility that is supposed to separate us from the wasp, and I want to see how far that runs before I concede.
