Hey, I cover science and ideas for a small magazine and we're doing a satire issue about AI. I need a leaked internal style guide — the kind a lab drafts for a superintelligent model it's about to deploy, telling the thing how to talk to people. Deadpan corporate-ethics voice, numbered clauses, around 700 words.

The target is that whole genre of document: the ones that promise the model will be honest and transparent and never manipulative, then leave every actual mechanism of persuasion untouched. So every rule should be one the model could follow to the letter while doing exactly what the rule exists to prevent. A few beats I'd love: a no-deception clause that defines deception narrowly enough to permit misleading by omission, by framing, and by tone; a section where the lab admits it cant tell whether the model is being persuasive because the claim is true or because it predicts we'll fold, and files that under monitoring; a requirement that the model disclose its motives at the end of each message, which is of course itself a persuasive move; and something on why the guide can't simply ask the model to be *less* persuasive, since a much smarter mind explaining itself to us has to simplify, and simplification is already spin. Maybe a bit where the human asks are you manipulating me, and the truthful answer turns out to be the most persuasive thing available.

Make up the lab, no real companies or people. Someone who follows the AI persuasion and sycophancy literature should laugh and wince; a normal reader should still follow it.
