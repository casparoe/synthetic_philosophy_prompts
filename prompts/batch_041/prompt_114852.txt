ok so I have to hand in a position statement for my democratic theory seminar on friday and I'd rather find out now that its incoherent than in front of everyone. heres my position, roughly in the order it came out of me:

1. the inclusion principle I want is subjection, not affectedness. if you're living under a state's coercive power you get a say in how it's used on you. the all-affected idea sounds nice but its a non-starter, everything affects everyone eventually and theres no principled place to stop.

2. that said, I think a farmer in Bangladesh whose land is going under has a genuinely democratic complaint against my country's climate policy, not just a moral one, and it ought to be institutionalised somehow rather than left as an aspiration.

3. theres no democratic way to draw the boundary of the demos. any vote presupposes a we that the vote itself cant authorise. I dont think thats a practical inconvenience, I think its a structural fact about democracy.

4. but immigration policy should be decided democratically by the people who are already here, and a state is entitled to remove people who came in without authorisation.

5. residence should be what grounds the franchise, not citizenship: twelve months living here, subject to the law, working, paying tax, and you're on the roll, papers or no papers, inside or outside prison. and the flip side, if you're a citizen who's been living abroad for years you drop off the roll, youve stopped being subject to anything here.

6. the objection that the EU cant be properly democratic because there is no European people to be the demos is a bad argument. there is nothing a people has that a thick enough political community doesnt have.
