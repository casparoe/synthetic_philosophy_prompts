So I just lost two hours arguing with my friend Dan about whether King Arthur was actually a real person, and I've come out of it with something I can't put a name to.

His case was basically that Arthur can't be a pure invention, because so many different sources mention him — the Welsh material, the Latin chronicles, the French romances, spread over centuries. So I went and looked, and alot of it runs back to one twelfth-century writer, Geoffrey of Monmouth, who says he's just translating an old book in the British tongue that a friend gave him. Nobody has ever seen the book. The later writers who look like extra witnesses are mostly repeating him, sometimes saying so, sometimes not. There are one or two earlier scraps, but they're the ones so vague they could be about anybody. So the crowd of witnesses is really one witness, and that one witness is saying someone told him.

But the other half is what really bugs me. The earlier the mention, the vaguer it is: he's a figure in songs, he fought somewhere. The later you go, the more definite it gets — dates, battles, a crown, a kingdom. The story doesn't pick up any new evidence on the way, it just gets more confident. And then the confidence is what gets cited as the evidence. I noticed the same shape with Troy, where the poem comes first and a few hundred years later you've got writers giving you a date for the fall of the city, as if the date were the proof. And the Exodus fight looks the same from the outside: the story gets told with a route and a date, and then the route and the date get treated as the record of it.

The strange bit is that this is the opposite of the game of telephone. Telephone is about the message getting garbled and losing detail. Here it gets tidier. The hedges drop out, the specifics get added, so the version that reaches us is the most confident and the least hedged one, and we read that as the best version.

Is there a name for this? I don't just mean hearsay, and I don't just mean a source being unreliable. I mean the two-step where a bunch of copies of one story starts to look like independent agreement, and where the retelling strips the uncertainty out, so that the confidence a story has acquired ends up doing the work of evidence. Is that one thing with a name, or two, or nothing? If there's an established term I want to know how far it stretches — does it cover both halves or only one — and if there isn't, give me a name and a definition with the boundary cases, because I definately want to be able to say exactly what Dan's argument is doing wrong and what it isn't.

One thing though, please write it out as normal prose, no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, just paragraphs like you'd write in an email, I find all that formatting hard to read.
