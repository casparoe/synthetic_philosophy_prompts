My friends and I keep arguing about the word 'copycat' and I've realized I have pretty stable verdicts about where it applies and no idea what they add up to. A second school shooter who signs off with the first one's name: copycat, no question. A cluster of suicides at a school after a celebrity death got wall-to-wall coverage: copycat, but only if the kids had heard about the celebrity one -- if none of them had, it's just a cluster. A cover band playing the Beatles note for note: not copycat, that's what the songs are for. A handbag with the logo nudged two millimeters: knockoff, not copycat. Lifting a paragraph from someone else's novel into yours: plagiarism, and calling it copycat feels off. Painting a new canvas in Vermeer's style and signing it Vermeer: forgery, and I want to say not copycat either, though I can't tell you why that's different from the shooter. A startup that clones a rival's feature a month after it launches: copycat, definitely. A singer who puts out a song in the style of someone who just died: homage or pastiche, not copycat -- but the startup is also just copying a style, so what's the difference? A documentary that restages a famous heist: not copycat, that's reenactment.

Is there one principle that gets all of these? I'll take a complicated one if it saves every verdict, but tell me which verdict I'm going to have to give up, and what the principle says about a case I haven't listed -- a group that adopts another group's tactics, not a specific attack, just the method.
