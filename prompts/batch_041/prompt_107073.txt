I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and the surprise exam paradox keeps pulling me back, because every treatment I run into either dismisses the student's backward induction as an obvious fallacy or declares the teacher's announcement self-defeating, and neither verdict ever seems to have been earned. Before I settle on a solution, I want the specification. Write the desiderata: what any adequate treatment of the paradox has to deliver.

State each requirement together with the case or consideration that motivates it, and keep the requirements distinct rather than collapsing them into a single demand for a correct answer. At a minimum I expect the list to take a position on: which step of the backward induction is permitted and which is not, and why; the difference between the announcement being true and the announcement being known or believed; the last-day case and what makes it feel forced; and the fact that a surprise exam is plainly possible, so any treatment that proves surprise impossible has proved too much. Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp, and if two of them cannot be satisfied at once, say which, because that would be the most interesting result.

Then stress-test the list against two extensions. First, replace the student's certainty with credences: the exam counts as a surprise when the student's credence that it will occur that morning falls below some stated threshold, and the announcement is public. Second, run the induction over the students' mutual knowledge of one another's reasoning rather than over the days of the week. For each extension, tell me which requirements become harder to meet and whether the extension forces any requirement off the list. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out proposed solutions, and sketch what a treatment would look like that satisfies the non-negotiable ones. I do not need a survey of who said what; I want the criteria and your judgment about where they bite.
