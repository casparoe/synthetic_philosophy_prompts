I retired four years ago — thirty-some years of seismic work, mostly offshore, plus a long stretch doing earthquake hazard maps for insurers — and since then I've been trying to teach myself philosophy in the evenings, which is going about as well as you'd expect. Anyway, a friend lent me a book by a Swedish philosopher who writes about AI, and the bit that's stuck in my craw is his notion of a "singleton." As I understand it, it doesn't mean a single super-brain or a world government; it just means any world order in which there's one decision-making agency at the top, whatever that agency happens to be made of. He's careful to say it need not be monolithic — it could contain any number of independent agents each doing their own thing, the way a big liberal state does — and, more interesting to me, it need not even be visible. It could quietly enforce a few background conditions and otherwise leave everyone alone, so that nobody living inside it ever has any reason to suspect there's anything at the top at all. That last part is what I can't shake. Everyone's imagined singleton is a robot tyrant. I keep thinking the durable one would be duller than that: closer to a standards body, or a form you have to fill out, something that never has to use force because it has already made every alternative unthinkable.

So could you write me a short story along those lines? A few pages is plenty. I'd like it set in a mid-sized coastal city sometime in the 2060s, told from the point of view of somebody whose job is maintaining one ordinary piece of infrastructure — a records clerk, a calibration technician, a scheduler, whatever you think works — and who gradually works out what he or she is actually part of. Ground level only, please: no lectures, no villain, nobody giving speeches about the fate of humanity, and no ending where someone blows it up. I want the reader to piece it together from the details that this thing has no rival and never will, and to feel the strange mixture of comfort and dread in that. One more thing, in case it's useful: I think the creepiest version is made of people who each believe they're just doing there small, boring jobs well.
