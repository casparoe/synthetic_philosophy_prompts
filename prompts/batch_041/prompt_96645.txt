I am a senior in high school writing an extended essay on Buddhist ethics, and I have been working from a popular introduction to Asian philosophy whose chapter on Buddhism contains the following passage:

"Buddhist ethics is best understood as a form of consequentialism. The whole system is organized around reducing suffering and increasing happiness, and the precepts against killing, stealing, and lying are rules of thumb that the wise set aside whenever breaking them would produce more good overall -- which is why some Buddhist traditions are willing to bless a killing performed out of compassion. Karma is the mechanism that makes the arithmetic work: every action produces a definite quantity of pleasure or pain in the future, and the ethical life consists in maximizing the total. What makes this arithmetic Buddhist rather than Western is only the cosmology, since the total is summed over countless rebirths instead of a single lifetime."

I would like to know how much of this I can trust, and I would rather have the reasoning than a list of things to read, since I have no research library available to me. Please go claim by claim and tell me which parts a specialist in Buddhist ethics would accept as stated, which are defensible oversimplifications of a position that specialists themselves hold, and which a Buddhist defender of the tradition would reject outright, along with the reason for the rejection.

Two further things puzzle me. First, a friend objected that a view locating the moral quality of an action in the agent's intention, and denying that any enduring self bears the consequences, could not be a form of consequentialism at all. Is that objection sound, or does it misunderstand what a consequentialist is committed to? Second, the passage's closing sentence treats the distance between Buddhist ethics and utilitarianism as merely cosmological, and I notice that nirvana does not obviously figure in the passage's arithmetic as a quantity of pleasure. I would like to know whether that closing sentence survives correction, or whether the disagreements run deeper than the passage allows, and if so, where.
