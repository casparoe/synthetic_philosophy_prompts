My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about why promises bind, and we kept talking past each other because we never settled what an answer would even have to do. So before we trade any more theories, write me the specification: the desiderata that any adequate account of promissory obligation must meet.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say why it should be accepted even by someone who favors a competing account. I expect the list to cover, at least, the cases the account must get right — a promise the promisee has forgotten and no longer cares about; a promise made to someone who has since died; a promise extracted by pressure that falls short of duress; a promise whose content the promisor misdescribed; a promise made inside an acknowledged fiction or game — the distinctions it must not collapse (between being bound and merely having a reason; between the obligation's being owed to the promisee and its being a demand of the promisor's own integrity; between a promise and a prediction of one's own future conduct; between a promise and a private resolution of identical content); and whether bindingness is binary or a matter of degree.

Two further constraints matter to me especially. The account should say what is and is not transferable — whether the promisor's obligation, or the promisee's claim, can be moved to a third party without either party's consent — and what its answer reveals about where the bindingness lives. And it should say what a promise adds to a resolution with identical content, since my friend's position is that promising is only a more public, more emphatic way of resolving.

Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of one camp, and flag any pair you think cannot be jointly satisfied — I suspect the demand that the obligation arise from the promisor's act alone collides with the demand that it be owed to a promisee who is aware of it. Finish with a short scorecard of the main families (will-based, practice- or convention-based, expectation- or interest-based, rights-transfer) against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria.
