I took my retirement some years ago from an experimental chair in physics, and I have spent the years since reading philosophy with more appetite than method. I have arrived at a question about Gödel's rotating universe and what it is supposed to establish about time, and I should be grateful for an explanation that assumes no prior reading in the literature.

As I understand the construction, Gödel exhibited an exact solution of Einstein's field equations in which a homogeneous dust swirls about a common axis; the rotation drags the light cones round with it, so that as one moves away from a given worldline the cones tilt progressively, until sufficiently long trajectories close upon their own past. From this Gödel concluded that time, understood as an objective lapse from past to future, is not a feature of the world at all — a conclusion he took to favour an idealist over a realist reading of time, and which many commentators have since treated as a landmark. I can follow the geometry well enough. What I cannot get hold of is the logic of the step from the geometry to the conclusion, and I have two particular difficulties.

The first is what the rotating solution adds. The relativity of simultaneity already denies the world a single global present, and most philosophers treat that as a blow to one particular conception of time rather than as a demonstration that time is unreal. Moreover, ours is not a Gödelian universe, so I do not see how a feature of a merely possible world can settle a question about this one. Is the closed timelike curve doing work that the relativity of simultaneity cannot do, or is it a vivid dramatisation of the same point?

The second is that the conclusion appears to rest on a prior commitment about what would have to be true of the world for time to count as real — something like a global ordering of events, or an objective passage. If that commitment is doing the work, the argument looks like a reductio of one conception of time rather than a discovery about the world, and I should like to know whether that is a fair reading.

To keep the answer tractable, please distinguish three claims — that the world has no single global present; that the world contains no objective passage or becoming; and that time is unreal — and say which of them the rotating solution actually supports, and what further premise each remaining step requires. No more than 500 words, please, and open with the bottom line in a sentence or two. I want the argument itself, not a reading list.
