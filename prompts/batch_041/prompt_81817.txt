I am compiling a set of exercises in counterfactual intellectual history, and I want one that forces a reconstruction rather than a summary. The exercise concerns John von Neumann and the distinction between tool AI and agent AI, which postdates him by decades and which he never addressed.

By tool AI I mean systems invoked for a bounded task, carrying no standing objectives of their own, whose loop is closed by a human who supplies the next request, and whose state is either ephemeral or externally imposed. By agent AI I mean systems handed an objective that then select the intermediate steps themselves, call other tools, keep state across long horizons, and act at a granularity fine enough that no human overseer can inspect each step. Neither label is a term of art in his writings; treat the distinction as an artifact of our situation that you are projecting onto him, and say plainly where the projection strains.

Anchor the reconstruction in what he argued rather than in his reputation as a prophet of computing. Work from at least these materials: the contrast he drew between the idiom of the nervous system and the languages of mathematics and logic; his treatment of how a system assembled from unreliable components can be made reliable; his sense that the nervous system's memory is a problem our machine analogies may not fit; and his program for automata that construct and reproduce themselves, including the requirement that a self-reproducing automaton carry a description rich enough to specify its own constructor.

Then address the following, in order:

1. Would he classify a delegate agent as a genuine automaton in his sense, or as an instrument that has merely been given a longer leash? Name the specific feature of his automata theory that decides the question for him.
2. Does his argument about self-description and self-reproduction constrain the aspiration to agents that improve themselves without a matching increase in what the human supplies? State the constraint as he would state it, then say whether it survives translation into our setting.
3. If the control layer of a delegate agent is expressed in something like natural language and explicit symbols, does he expect that layer to be a temporary convenience or a ceiling? Ground your answer in what he took the brain's primary idiom to be.
4. On his account, where must error correction live in a system that acts over long horizons — in redundant components, in an external overseer, or in the organization of the whole? Would delegation raise or lower the amount of redundancy he thinks is needed?

Separate throughout what is documented in his own texts from what is extrapolation, and mark the extrapolations as such. Close by stating how confident you are in the reconstruction as a whole and singling out the one claim in it you are least sure of. Maximum 750 words.
