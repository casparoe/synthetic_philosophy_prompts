Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration on the combination problem should have produced: a committed subject-first panpsychist and a committed quality-first protopsychist, each of whom thinks the other has relocated the hard problem rather than solved it.

The subject-first panpsychist holds that the intrinsic natures which physical description leaves out are experiential all the way down: every fundamental entity is a subject with a rudimentary experience, and a human being's unified experience is constituted by, and is nothing over and above, a suitably organized collection of micro-experiences. The quality-first protopsychist holds that the fundamental intrinsic natures are not experiential and belong to no subject — there is nothing it is like to be a fundamental entity — but that they are protophenomenal: not experiences themselves, yet such that, when suitably organized, they constitute experience. Both camps agree that physics describes only structure and disposition, that the hard problem cannot be discharged by adding more structure, and that macro-experience is not a further fundamental ingredient added to the micro-level.

Write the document both could sign. It should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both accept — about what any solution must deliver, about the constraints that any account must satisfy (unity, the subject, and the structure and variety of experience), and about the moves that are unavailable to either of them; (2) a table of the residual disagreements, one row each, every entry phrased in wording its own side would accept; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and, for each, the argument or case both sides agree bears on it, together with the prediction each side records in advance; (4) for each side, what it would count as a reason to abandon its own position; and (5) an explicit verdict on which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal. Address directly whether the camps disagree about the relation of constitution or only about what stands in that relation; whether subject-first composition and quality-first composition are the same problem under two names; and what each view implies about the grain and the variety of experience — that experience is unified and richly differentiated while its supposed micro-components are many and of few kinds. I suspect one crux is whether experience is possible without a subject, and another is whether protophenomenal does any work that physical does not, but decide for yourself which apparent cruxes are real and which dissolve. No citations and no named authors: put everything in your own words.
