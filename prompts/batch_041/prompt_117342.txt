I cover science and ideas for a general-interest outlet, and I'm working on a piece where I drop a dead thinker into a live fight. This time it's Turing — the late-40s/1950 Turing, the one who thought you should build a child machine and educate it rather than program an adult, who messed around with unorganised machines and pleasure-pain setups and the discipline-versus-initiative question. The live fight: a big urban school district near me just voted to swap out its remedial math tutors for a fine-tuned model trained on the district's own transcripts, tuned with a simple reward signal — positive when a student's next answer moves toward the target, negative when the student disengages — plus a bonus for asking the student questions back. The districts argument is that the thing starts ignorant and gets better at teaching by being corrected, like a novice tutor.

Predict how Turing would land on this, at the specific joints: would he think a reward-shaped, corrected-by-feedback teaching machine is the right kind of thing to build, given how much weight he put on the child-machine picture? Would he care that it learns on kids' data instead of in some artificial teaching environment? And on discipline versus initiative, would he want it drilling the kid or provoking the kid?
