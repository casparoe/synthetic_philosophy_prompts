I recently joined the grant committee of a small animal-welfare fund, and our first major decision is whether to open a dedicated insect-welfare portfolio. Two of our scientific advisers have been arguing for months. One holds the scale argument: because insects are so numerous, and because even a very low probability that they are sentient could still yield an enormous expected amount of welfare, we should treat insect welfare as a serious priority now, under uncertainty. The other holds the evidence-first view: the probability is too low, the proxies too unreliable, and expected-value arithmetic with arbitrary priors and moral weights is not a sound basis for spending scarce money, so we should wait for better evidence. They agree on almost no formulation, and I need something they can both sign.

Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two camps. Use the following bookkeeping device to keep the disagreement precise. For any given insect taxon, distinguish three hypotheses: H0, no valenced experience; H1, valenced experience but no robust suffering or welfare states; and H2, robust suffering and welfare states. Distinguish the probability of each hypothesis from the moral weight assigned to it conditional on being true. Treat the expected moral value of an insect-affecting intervention as the product of four factors: the number of insects affected, the probability distribution over H0 through H2, the conditional moral weight, and the change in welfare per insect. Both camps should be able to accept this bookkeeping even if they disagree about every input.

The statement should contain a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including claims about what sentience would have to be, what the scale argument does and does not establish, and what kind of evidence could in principle bear on the question. Then include a disagreements table with columns for the crux, each side’s formulation in wording its own side would accept, the test or case both sides agree would bear on it, and each side’s prediction. At least one crux should concern whether expected-value reasoning is action-guiding under deep uncertainty; at least one should concern whether current evidence already clears some threshold for moral concern; and at least one should concern whether the conditional moral weight for insects could be comparable to that for vertebrates. Where a disagreement turns out to be merely verbal, say so and explain why. Do not adjudicate who is right, and do not include citations, reading lists, or references to specific authors. Keep the whole thing to a maximum of 850 words.
