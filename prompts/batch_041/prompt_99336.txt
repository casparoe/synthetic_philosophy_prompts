I run a small company that builds an assistant for warehouse operations. It was trained only on text — equipment manuals, incident reports, chat logs — and it now answers our operators' questions so reliably that we shipped it. A friend who studies language tells me it does not understand a single word it uses; it has learned only which strings tend to follow which. My lead engineer replies that this is a distinction without a difference, since understanding just is producing the right output in the right context, and the operators care only that the outputs are right. I keep going back and forth, and I have to decide where to spend next quarter's budget. Suppose I wire the assistant into the floor: cameras, weight sensors, barcode scans, so that when it says a pallet is too heavy for the lift, the word is hooked to a reading from a scale. Does that give the word meaning, or does it merely add another layer of symbols whose meaning is now equally in question — since the sensor's number is only "heavy" because a human wrote that label, have I not just relocated the problem? And if the assistant confidently answers questions about a machine it has never encountered, is that a symptom of the thing my friend is worried about, or just a separate defect? I would like your assessment of who is closer to right and what I should actually do, along with where your own reasoning is most likely to fail and what argument or evidence would change your mind. Please write in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — the way you would in a letter.
