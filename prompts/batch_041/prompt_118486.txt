A colleague has persuaded me that evolutionary psychology is a pseudoscience, and I want to know whether the argument holds. It runs as follows: the field explains traits by inventing selection pressures after the fact, and since the environment of evolutionary adaptedness — the ancestral conditions in which those traits supposedly evolved — can no longer be observed, none of its claims can be falsified; and an unfalsifiable claim that is nonetheless believed is a piece of pseudoscience. His exhibit is the suggestion that the nausea of early pregnancy is an adaptation that discouraged ancestral women from eating plant toxins during the window when the embryo is most vulnerable to them: the only way to test it, he says, would be to travel back and watch. I suggested that one might instead ask whether the sickness tracks the window of vulnerability, and whether it is rarer in societies whose staple foods contain fewer such compounds; he replied that this only fits the story to data already in hand. Is a claim of that kind genuinely unfalsifiable, and if so, does that place it outside science altogether? If not, what would a genuinely risky prediction for an adaptationist hypothesis look like, given that the environment it refers to is gone?
