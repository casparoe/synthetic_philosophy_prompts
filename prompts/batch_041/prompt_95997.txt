I run equality and inclusion at a large public hospital trust, and I am the person who signs off on staff requests to vary uniform, rostering and catering rules for religious reasons: about two a month, one a year that reaches a tribunal, and the occasional one that reaches the local paper. Our neutrality charter reads both ways at once. It says the trust takes no position on religion and that staff must not proselytise; it also says we will accommodate religious practice unless accommodation would be unreasonable. So I get requests that pull in opposite directions, and I decide them by instinct.

I want a procedure I can actually run, and a genuine operationalisation of laïcité rather than a slogan. Do the translation honestly: laïcité is usually sold as one commitment but strikes me as several bundled together — that the institution stays out of religion, that it treats everyone alike, that its own acts carry no religious meaning, and a further claim about emancipating people from their communities that I suspect does most of the work and is the hardest to defend. Tell me which of those can become steps an administrator can run and which are rhetoric. Distinctions I think the procedure must keep: whether the burden falls on the claimant or on others; whether the institution is the author of a practice or merely permits it; and whether we are being asked to be neutral in our reasons or neutral in our effects.

Then build it — the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the decision back to me as a value call — and run it on three requests: a theatre nurse who wants to wear a headscarf under her cap, a catering team who want pork off the only shared menu, and a registrar who objects to taking part in a procedure. At least one outcome should be one I will not like. Then say what an Anglo-American reasonable-accommodation approach would do differently at each step, and whether the two can be run as one procedure with a switch or are different machines. Finish with where the procedure will misfire and what it cannot decide. No reading list; I need something I can use on Monday.
