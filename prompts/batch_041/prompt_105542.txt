I am the TA for a second-year undergraduate seminar on Wittgenstein, and I have ninety minutes to make the idea of family resemblance actually land. I do not want a lecture on it, and I do not want secondary literature, readings, or citations; I want a plan for the explanation itself. The students will have read a stretch of the Philosophical Investigations, so they have already met the language game idea, but they have taken it to mean that words mean whatever a community happens to decide, and they will hear family resemblance as the flatter, duller claim that some concepts simply lack a definition. I want them to leave seeing that these are two distinct moves doing different work, and that neither one is relativism.

Give me the order in which the pieces must come, and why each depends on the one before it: what has to be established before family resemblance can be heard as a positive account rather than a concession, where the language game idea has to enter so that family resemblance does not slide into the thought that there is no rule at all, and where I should mark the difference between Wittgenstein's picture and the prototype-and-cluster reading the students half-remember from psychology. I want them to feel why a checklist of weighted criteria is not the same thing as a network of overlapping similarities held together by a practice.

For the example that carries the whole session, my instinct is the live argument over whether a hot dog counts as a sandwich: it is everyday, contested, and apparently definition-proof. Tell me whether that can bear ninety minutes or whether it degenerates into a quarrel about a word, and if it cannot carry the weight, hand me the example that should, with an account of what it must have and what it must lack.

Then lay out the misconceptions in the order they will actually surface, and name the exact point in the sequence where I should head each one off. I am already braced for family resemblance read as relativism, family resemblance read as prototype theory, and the suspicion that a concept without a definition is therefore used arbitrarily.

Close with one question I can put to them in the final ten minutes that would reveal whether they hold the positive picture rather than the negative one, and tell me what I should openly refuse to settle at this level.

Keep the whole answer under 900 words, and give it to me as a sequence I could follow at the board rather than as continuous prose.
