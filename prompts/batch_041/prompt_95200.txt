Ok so I'm putting together something on how historical analogies go wrong, and I want to use Vietnam as the test case — specifically the way the Munich analogy got drug into the 1964–65 escalation debates. The basic move was: if we back off in Vietnam it's another Munich, another round of appeasement, and we know how that turned out. I don't need the blow-by-blow of the decision-making, I know that part. What I want is the episode run through a specific apparatus.

The apparatus, which comes out of the philosophy-of-science literature on models and analogy, goes like this. Any analogy between a source case and a target case splits into three parts: the positive analogy, the respects in which the two are known to match; the negative analogy, the respects in which they're known to differ; and the neutral analogy, the respects where nobody yet knows whether they match or differ. The interesting claim is that the neutral part is where all the epistemic work happens — it's where the analogy earns its keep and also where it can mislead, because people tend to slide the neutral part over into the positive part without noticing, so untested similarities get treated as established ones.

So take Munich as the source and Vietnam as the target, and actually sort the features into those three bins. Be concrete about the mapping: who's supposed to be Hitler, who's Chamberlain, what plays the role of the Rhineland, what plays the Sudetenland, and so on. Where does the analogy hold up, where does it visibly break, and — this is the part I care about — which features were genuinely neutral and got quietly promoted to positive? Then run the test in both directions: what does this apparatus explain about the decision that a flat "they used a bad analogy" story misses, and what does the case expose as a weakness in the apparatus itself? For instance, does it need a separate account of why people promote neutral features to positive ones, and is such an account even available, or is the apparatus just redescribing the error?

Last thing, and keep it short: turn the same apparatus on the Thucydides trap as it gets applied to US–China now. Does it fare any better there, or does the same slide happen?
