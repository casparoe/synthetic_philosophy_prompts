ok so i've been going down a rabbit hole on this idea that a lot of animal displays are basically deliberate waste -- the peacock's tail, a gazelle jumping straight up in the air instead of just running when it sees a lion -- and the point is that only a genuinely fit animal can afford to throw away that much energy, so the waste itself is the message. i find it weirdly convincing and also kind of suspicious, and i'd like to see it satirised.

so: write me a leaked internal strategy memo, 600-800 words, from the leadership of a made-up mid-size company that has gone all in on this principle as actual doctrine -- every brand, hiring and spending decision justified on the grounds that it has to hurt or it doesn't mean anything. Deadpan corporate voice: headers, bullet points, a couple of action items, maybe a passing line about a rival whose signal has degraded. No narrator explaining the joke, no winking. The execs shouldn't be idiots -- the funny part is that they've got the mechanism basically right and are still building something awful with it.

the jokes have to come from the underlying logic being right, so please keep these in there somewhere: cost only separates anyone if a low-quality competitor genuinely can't match it, which means the moment the signal gets cheap it stops working (and the memo should be quietly panicking about exactly that); the arms-race part, where everyone in the industry adopting it leaves nobody separated; and the thing i keep tripping over, where "expensive to do" and "impossible to fake" get treated as the same thing when they're definately not. Also, if the theory can explain every pointless expense after the fact, have the memo say that out loud in its own voice, because that's the part that makes me wonder if the whole thing is unfalsifiable.

end with a short note, separate from the memo, where you actually commit: is the handicap principle doing real explanatory work, or is it a machine for rationalising waste?
