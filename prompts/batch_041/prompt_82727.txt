I'm in a Wittgenstein reading group that meets at my friend's flat on Sundays — we mostly argue about the tea, then about the middle bits of the Investigations — and one person in it, Priya, has spent the last couple of months handing down rulings on whether things are games. Her record so far: chess, game, obviously. Solitaire played alone with actual cards, game; she says whether anyone else is at the table has nothing to do with it. A single-player video game, game. The thing kids do where the floor is lava, game. A professional boxing match with a big purse on the line, still a game — I said boxing for money can't really be play and she got quite annoyed and said the money is neither here nor there. A street fight, not a game. A slot machine, not a game, on the grounds that there's no move you can make that's right or wrong.

Next week someone's putting a new case to her: a Go engine that plays itself overnight, nobody watching, nothing being trained, just the same program playing against a copy of itself forever. I'd like you to tell me what she'll say — alot of people in the group think it's obvious and I don't. But I'm less interested in that than in the rule she's actually running and how much her record really pins it down, because I can see at least three things fitting it: (i) the family-resemblance reading, where something's a game if it has enough of the cluster — rules, a goal, an opponent, skill, constraints that are there for no reason, doing it for its own sake; (ii) a use-and-practice reading, where it's a game only if it's caught up in a practice of playing, something people (or animals) do and treat as play; (iii) a reading where the rules have to be taken on voluntarily by someone who is making the job harder for themselves on purpose. So: predict her verdict on the engine, say which of these best fits her record and why you'd bet on it over the others, say which of the others fit her record just as well, say where each rule lands on the engine, and name the case (or cases) that would actually separate them. If her record is genuinely too thin to choose between the rules, say that rather than pretending it's tighter than it is, and if one of my three guesses is just wrong about her, say which and why. No need to point me at any literature, I just want the argument.
