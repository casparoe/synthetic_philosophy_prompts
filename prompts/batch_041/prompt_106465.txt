The question was worth 10 points and read: "What is inference to the best explanation (IBE)? Distinguish it from two other forms of ampliative inference, and then assess one important objection to the claim that IBE can support scientific realism."

The student wrote:

"IBE is the idea that we should infer the hypothesis that would, if true, best explain the evidence. Gilbert Harman introduced the term in a 1965 paper, and it has since become one of the central forms of ampliative inference in philosophy of science. It is distinct from deduction, because the conclusion is not guaranteed by the premises, and it is distinct from enumerative induction, which moves from a sample to a population by sheer repetition of instances; IBE, by contrast, is sensitive to the content of the hypotheses rather than just to their track record.

Harman's later distinction between the 'likeliest' and the 'loveliest' explanation is important here. The likeliest explanation is the one that is most probable given the evidence, while the loveliest is the one that would, if true, provide the greatest understanding — and Harman argues that IBE tells us to infer the loveliest. In practice the explanatory virtues we appeal to are things like consilience, simplicity, and falsifiability.

The main objection I'll discuss is van Fraassen's 'bad lot' argument. Van Fraassen points out that IBE only ever ranks the hypotheses we happen to have generated; the best of them may be a bad lot, and the true explanation may not be on the list at all. So even a perfectly applied IBE gives us no reason to think its conclusion is true. I think this objection is overstated. Science has used IBE for centuries and has been enormously successful, so IBE must be a reliable route to truth; and the no-miracles argument shows that realism is the best explanation of that success, which is itself an application of IBE.

One complication is that IBE and Bayesian conditionalization are usually treated as rivals. Some philosophers have tried to show that IBE can be reconstructed within a Bayesian framework, but since Bayesians assign probabilities to hypotheses and IBE does not, the two really are incompatible."
