I am designing a teaching exercise on Wittgenstein's later philosophy, and I want to reconstruct the implicit criteria a colleague—call her Dr. V—uses when she decides whether some practice counts as a language-game. Her verdicts so far are these. A chess problem worked out silently by one person: yes. A child's solitary babbling that is addressed to no one and has no role in a shared activity: no. Two builders coordinating by pointing and grunting as they pass stones: yes. A formal logical calculus whose symbols are manipulated according to explicit rules but which no one ever uses for any purpose: no. A religious ritual with fixed call-and-response: yes. A parrot that reliably produces context-appropriate requests: no. A private diary in which someone records a sensation with a sign they cannot explain to anyone else: no. A team of programmers who develop a jargon and use it to debug code together: yes. A bee's dance: no. A mathematical proof worked out by a solitary mathematician in a public notation: yes. A game of solitaire played according to public rules: yes. A child being corrected into the use of a word within a family: yes. A cryptographic protocol executed by machines, with no human understanding of the steps: no.

Next week she will judge a new case: a distributed machine-learning system whose internal states are not interpretable by any human, but which are causally coupled to human users' utterances and actions in such a way that it reliably produces coordinated behavior. The engineers have begun to describe the system's internal transitions as its language-game, and the human users adjust their own behavior in response to its outputs, though no one can state what would count as a correct or incorrect internal move; success is measured only by whether the joint task gets done.

Predict Dr. V's verdict. I am less interested in a yes or no than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her earlier verdicts just as well, and which of those rules the new case would separate. Is she operating with a family-resemblance cluster of features, or with a necessary condition such as public criteria, normative uptake, or participation in a shared form of life? Say what each candidate rule predicts here, give your best guess with your reasons, and name a further case that would best distinguish the rules. If her precedents genuinely underdetermine the new case, say what principle she would be adopting with each choice.

Finally, be explicit about which parts of your answer are settled in the interpretation of Wittgenstein's later philosophy and which are genuinely contested among people who know the material; do not present a contested reading as the consensus, and flag your own uncertainty where it exists.
