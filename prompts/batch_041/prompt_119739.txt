I do the records for a small maritime heritage trust — we own one old sailing vessel, a couple of sheds, and about four hundred feet of spare timber, and I'm the one who has to write the paperwork whenever a question comes up about whether the thing we've got is still the same boat. I never went to university, but I've been reading around the old Theseus puzzle for a few years now and I've got a feeling its not just a puzzle, it's basically my job, and I keep getting it wrong.

The decisions come up maybe six times a year and they all seem to want different answers. The insurer wants to know if a rebuilt hull counts as the same vessel so the policy carries over. The heritage listing people want to know if it's still the listed ship or a new one. The donors want to know if their grandfather's plank is still in it. If we ever sold her or broke her up, someone would ask whether we're selling the ship or just a pile of timber shaped like a ship. And there's a rival group down the coast who built a replica off the original lines and are now going round saying they've got the real continuation of her, which makes me want to hit someone.

Could you write me a procedure I can actually run: a list of questions in the order I should ask them, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure just stops and says no, and where I should stop pretending it's a factual question and take it to the trustees as a decision about what we want the ship to mean. I think the one thing I've half worked out for myself is that "the same ship" isn't one question, it's several — same for the insurance, same for the listing, same for the visitors — so the procedure has to be able to come out differently for different purposes without me looking like I'm just making it up as I go. I'd also like it to cope with the fact that a ship is a bit like a company or a club: it outlives every plank and every member and somehow carries on, and I don't know if that's a real thing or just a way of talking. Please explain any jargon in plain words, I don't know what sortal means and I looked it up once and forgot.

Then run it on three cases. Our own boat after thirty years of replacement timber, where I honestly couldn't tell you what percentage is original anymore. The rival replica, which has none of the original material at all but was built to the original drawings by people who'd worked on ours. And the old keel and six frames sitting in a museum in another country, which is the only bit that's definitely original. I've got a trustee meeting in three weeks so it needs to be something I can put in front of people who aren't going to read a book about it. Tell me where it gives an answer I won't like, and what it can't do.
