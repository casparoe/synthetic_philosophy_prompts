I run a small reading group on hedonism and I want one session on whether pleasure and pain can be put on a single scale. Write me a decision tree that sorts the positions on that — build it from the questions and answer-combinations first, and only then drop the actual views into the cells.

Each branch node should be a question someone could answer without knowing which view it lands them in, each leaf a named position plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. The splits I care about, roughly in order: (1) are pleasure and pain comparable at all, or is all you get a partial ordering where some pains and pleasures are rankable against each other and others are neither better nor worse nor equal; (2) if its comparable, is there a common unit or only an ordinal ranking; (3) if there's a unit, is the exchange rate fixed — can a large enough pleasure always compensate any pain — or does it vary with the level; (4) the lexical question, whether there's a threshold of suffering above which no amount of pleasure, however big, compensates, and if so whether it's a threshold or absolute. Leaves I want in there: constant-rate hedonism, diminishing marginal value, threshold-lexical (trade-offs run normally below the threshold, pain wins outright above it), absolute lexical (any suffering at all outweighs any pleasure), and a view that's happy to compare within one life but refuses to aggregate across lives. Six to ten leaves is plenty; add whatever else the space needs and tell me which cells nobody occupies and why — incoherent, repugnant, or just unexplored?

Use concrete cases at the nodes, not abstractions: would you take one bad week plus a decade of ordinary contentment over an hour of agony plus fifty years of the best life you can imagine; does a million mild headaches outweigh one torture session; can any amount of pleasure buy off a small but real suffering. Audience is a mixed group of grad students and one economist, so be honest about which labels are actually defended positions and which are just cells in the grid, and flag any two leaves the tree can't pull apart.

If the way I've set this up is confused — say one scale is doing too much work, or the threshold/absolute distinction collapses — just say so rather than building the tree anyway.
