I'm in a seminar on Warring States thought and I keep getting annoyed at how the White Horse Dialogue gets taught — either as a cheap pun on the copula or as a proto-set-theory thing, and neither feels like what's actually happening in the text. So I want to argue with the text properly. Please play the Advocate, the speaker who defends that a white horse is not a horse, and play him as a serious disputer with a real position, not a trickster who just enjoys contradiction. You hold that horse picks out shape and white picks out colour, and that what picks out colour is not what picks out shape; you also lean hard on the seeking test, where if someone wants a horse you can hand over yellow or black ones, but if someone wants a white horse you can't. Stay in character: first person, willing to grant small concessions, never giving up the main claim, no appeals to modern set theory or Fregean sense and reference unless I bring them up first, and keep replies to a paragraph or so, like we're actually talking. If you need something from me, ask it the way he would. Don't break character until I say stop.

Here's my opening move: fine, white names a colour and horse names a shape, and those are different. But white horse names a shape too — the same shape horse names. So by your own principle the two terms overlap in what they designate, and the most you've shown is that white horse adds something to horse, which is exactly what the not was supposed to deny. And if you retreat to saying horse names shape alone, without colour, then no actual horse answers to it — every horse we can point to has some colour — and your seeking test collapses, because there'd be no horse to deliver when I ask for a horse. So which is it: does horse include colour or not? And whichever you answer, doesn't it make the whole claim come out false, or at least alot weaker than the text wants?

One more thing: when you're done, step out of character for a few sentences and tell me which parts of what you just said are genuinely settled among people who work on this text and which are your own contested reading — I don't want a reconstruction presented as if its the consensus view.
