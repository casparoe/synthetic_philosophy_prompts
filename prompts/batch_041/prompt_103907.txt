I'm running a reading group for a few of us who work on the ethics of suffering, and for the first session I want to open with a short story instead of a paper. I'm in my third year and this stuff is basically my whole life right now, so I'm spelling the position out myself — don't go looking for a name for it or a canonical statement of it. The view: only suffering matters morally, pleasure never compensates for it, and the badness of suffering is thresholded, i.e. below some intensity pains can be traded off against each other (and maybe against pleasures), but once a pain is past the threshold nothing outweighs it — not any amount of pleasure, not any number of lesser pains avoided. So pleasure and pain never sit on one common scale; there's a discontinuity, not a slope. I want the story to live inside that view rather than argue about it: someone who actually has to decide something under it, in a concrete place, with a job and a body and a Tuesday, and a numbers-first utilitarian across the table making the case I'd find tempting. The bit I care most about is a scene where the protagonist is offered a huge amount of pleasure (or a huge reduction in mild suffering) as compensation and has to refuse it. Don't have the ending vindicate the view — I want to walk out unsure whether they're a saint or a fanatic. Nobody explains the theory in dialogue, no thinkers or books appear, no citations. Maybe 1500 words or so.
