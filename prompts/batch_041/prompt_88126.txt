Write the FAQ page for the website of a research institute that is the institutional heir to Malthus and has spent two centuries forecasting a resource-driven collapse of industrial civilization, during which every deadline it named has passed. Around 600–700 words, in the flat, unctuous register of institutional copy: the page must never acknowledge that it is a joke, and no entry may break character. The questions should be the ones an informed skeptic would actually put — wasn't the forecast refuted by two centuries of growth? when precisely is "imminent"? what would count as evidence against the model? doesn't the theory's own logic predict that a technological fix postpones the collapse rather than disproving it? and why should I keep donating? Each answer should embody a distinct, real rescue strategy: extending the deadline, adding a scope condition, relocating the collapse to the regions or sectors where it has already occurred, restating the claim as a conditional whose antecedent has not obtained, and retreating to a tendency rather than a prediction. The claim may be softened, postponed, or relocated, but never abandoned. One or two answers must be genuinely hard to dismiss, so that a reader cannot tell from the page alone where legitimate refinement of a model ends and ad hoc rescue begins; that boundary is what the piece is about, and it must never be named. The comedy should come from the argumentative structure rather than from the institute's stupidity — the modeler is competent, sincere, and increasingly boxed in — and the result should be legible to a reader who knows nothing about falsification while still being funny to one who does.
