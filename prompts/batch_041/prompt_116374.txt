I'm a second-year cognitive science student putting together a positions map for a reading group that keeps talking past itself about linguistic relativity. Everyone in the room agrees that the claim that language influences thought is either obviously true or obviously false, depending on what the person means by influence, and I want an instrument that sorts the claims rather than the slogans. Please build me a decision tree — equivalently, a taxonomy with labeled branch points — whose leaves are the distinct positions on whether and how language affects non-linguistic cognition, with every branch node phrased as a question a reader can answer about a concrete case without already knowing which position that answer leads to. The tree should be able to separate at least these commitments: whether language is constitutive of the relevant concept (you cannot have the thought without it), enabling (you can have the thought, but only with language doing part of the work), or merely facilitating (you could have it anyway, language only makes it faster or more likely); whether the claimed effect is online, arising during the processing of a task, or offline, appearing in memory, recall, or judgment after the fact; whether the mechanism is representational (language changes which distinctions are available at all) or attentional (language changes which distinctions are salient or habitually noticed); whether the effect is domain-general or confined to domains in which language is itself the task-relevant tool; and whether the effect requires long immersion in a language or can be induced in an afternoon's training. I would much rather you derive the space from the questions and then see which positions land in it than start from a list of well-known views and reverse-engineer questions to reach them, so if the derivation leaves branches that nobody occupies, I want those shown rather than quietly pruned. It should also be able to represent the possibility that part of the dispute is verbal, if you think it is.

Because the tree has to be answerable from cases rather than from labels, every branch node should turn on a concrete situation, and these situations should all find a home somewhere in the tree: a speaker whose language divides a color region into two named categories, asked to pick the matching chip from a lineup after a delay; a community whose everyday spatial vocabulary is absolute rather than body-relative, whose members are spun around and then asked to point to a hidden object; a speaker whose language has only a handful of number words, asked to carry an exact quantity across a barrier; a speaker whose language obligatorily marks how they came to know what they are reporting, asked later to recall who told them what; a language that assigns genders to inanimate objects, whose speakers are asked to judge how similar a pictured object is to a male or a female voice; an adult who learns a new spatial frame in a two-week laboratory study and is tested before and after; a bilingual who runs the same task in each of her two languages on the same day; and a speaker who has no single word for a distinction but can always produce a three-word paraphrase of it on demand. I want eight to twelve leaves, each carrying the name of a position (or unnamed if you do not think anyone holds it), one sentence on what it commits you to, one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and the case above that it handles least comfortably.

Please deliver the whole instrument as a JSON object with the following fields, followed by at most two hundred words of prose. The field branch_nodes is an array of objects with id, question, parent (null for the root), yes and no (each giving the id of the next node or leaf), and what_answer_shows (one sentence naming the case or finding that settles the answer). The field leaves is an array of objects with id, position_name, path (an array of the branch answers from root to leaf), commitment, awkward_commitment, diagnostic_case, and proponents, where proponents is a school or tradition name or unnamed, and never an individual author. The field unoccupied_paths is an array of objects with path, why_empty (one of incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored), and a one-sentence diagnosis. The field inseparable_pairs is an array of objects with leaves and why_the_tree_cannot_separate_them. The prose coda should say which parts of the dispute you take to be verbal and which single branch question you would delete if you had to cut the tree in half. Do not cite works, authors, dates, or reading lists — I want positions and the cases that discriminate between them, nothing else.
