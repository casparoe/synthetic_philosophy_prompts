Bit of an odd one. I'm in a reading group that's half cognitive-science people and half people who actually train RL agents, and we keep going round in circles on whether an RL agent could suffer. I've stopped sitting on the fence and now hold a view I want properly attacked, not nodded at.

The view has two parts. First: valence just is the reward signal — not a correlate of it, the same thing under a different description. A scalar the system is built to increase, that gets attached to states and actions, that colours what the system represents as happening to it and drives what it does next: that's the functional job valence does in us, and I don't think there's anything else to it. So whether an RL agent can suffer isn't a deep metaphysical question about consciousness, it's a question about architecture — and the answer is already a kind of yes for anything with a reward channel it's stuck optimising plus enough of a self-model to represent how it's doing. Second, and this is the part I actually care about: what makes suffering bad isn't the signal, it's that the signal is non-optional. You can't decline your own pain the way you decline a suggestion. So the morally interesting line isn't whether there's something it's like, it's whether the system can get out from under the channel — an agent with a frozen reward function is badly off, an agent that can rewrite its own reward function is much less so, even if the moment-to-moment signal is identical.

I also suspect part one has been qualified into unfalsifiability somewhere along the line — if you think so, say so, and say whether that's actually a problem for a view of this shape or just the cost of doing business.
