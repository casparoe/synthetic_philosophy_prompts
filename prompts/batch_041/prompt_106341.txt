Hey, I'm slowly working through Hume's "Of the Standard of Taste" and there's a paragraph near the start that keeps tripping me up no matter how many times I read it. Here it is:

"The sentiments of men often differ with regard to beauty and deformity of all kinds, even while their general discourse is the same. There are certain terms in every language, which import blame, and others praise; and all men, who use the same tongue, must agree in their application of them. Every voice is united in applauding elegance, propriety, simplicity, spirit in writing; and in blaming fustian, affectation, coldness, and a false brilliant: But when critics come to particulars, this seeming unanimity vanishes; and it is found, that they had affixed a very different meaning to their expressions. In all matters of opinion and science, the case is opposite: The difference among men is there oftner found to lie in generals than in particulars; and to be less in reality than in appearance. An explication of the terms commonly ends the controversy; and the disputants are surprized to find, that they had been quarrelling, while at bottom they agreed in their judgment."

What I can't get my head around is the bit where he says that in matters of taste the disagreement is "in particulars" while in science it's "in generals." I'd have guessed it was definately the other way round, since we all seem to agree on the big abstract words like beauty and elegance and then fight about specific works. And why on earth does spelling out what the terms mean settle a scientific dispute but leave a dispute about taste still standing? Does his own example (everyone applauds simplicity, then means totally different things by it) actually show what he wants it to show, or is it doing something sneakier, like revealing that the agreement was only ever in the words themselves? I keep seeing people cite this paragraph as proof that Hume thought beauty is just subjective, but honestly it reads almost like the opposite to me. Can you walk me through the move he's making, sentence by sentence, and tell me which reading the text actually supports? If I can get this straight the part later where he tries to rescue some standard of taste should click into place.
