A guy in my lab has a line he pulls out whenever anyone brings up consciousness, and I've heard versions of it from about three other people since: 'your brain edits the past — by the time you actually experience something, it's already been rewritten.' Last week he used it to shut down a question about the color phi effect, which is where you flash a blue dot at the top of a screen and a red dot at the bottom a fraction of a second later, and people report seeing a single dot move down the screen and change color halfway. The weird part is that they report the color change as happening before the red dot has even been shown. His takeaway was that your experience of the first flash gets doctored after the fact, so there's no fact of the matter about what you 'really' saw.

I dont buy that it's that simple, but every time I try to say which bit I object to I get tangled up, because I think he's smuggling three or four different things in under one sentence. Can you seperate out the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making — from the boringly true to the genuinely radical — and for each say whether it's actually defensible and whether it really gets him his conclusion? And a specific thing I keep circling: 'the brain edits it before you see it' versus 'the brain edits it after the fact and backdates it' — are those two different theories or one theory said twice, and if it's the latter, does that rescue his argument or wreck it?

Give it to me as exactly five numbered readings, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up — I want to take it to lab meeting.
