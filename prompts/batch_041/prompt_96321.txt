My roommate and I host a monthly dinner where whoever shows up argues about something, and last week it was meat. I stayed out of it, and now everyone wants me to referee, so I need a move-by-move verdict rather than a lazy "both have a point."

Dana said she's dropped chicken and pork and mostly eats beef and dairy now, because she wants to minimize the number of animals killed for her food — one cow feeds her for months, whereas a chicken is basically one meal. Ravi said that's backwards. He said what matters isn't the body count but the total suffering and death the system produces, and beef is the worst on land: way more acreage per calorie, and all that pasture and feed cropland is what drives habitat loss and kills the mice, birds and bugs caught in harvests. Count those field deaths plus the wild animals pushed off the land, he said, and beef looks terrible, while chicken — the most efficient meat — looks comparatively fine.

Dana came back with two things. First, if field deaths count, plants aren't clean either: growing wheat or soy kills field animals too, so his own logic undercuts the vegetarian conclusion he's obviously aiming at, and the honest takeaway is that everything you eat kills something, so just minimize. Second, she said his suffering-centered framing actually helps her: if what matters is how bad a life is, a broiler chicken jammed in a shed for six weeks has a much worse life than a cow on grass, so even granting more chickens per meal, each one is worse off, and she doesn't think the arithmetic obviously lands where he says. She also floated something she got from somewhere: if cows have lives worth living, then eating beef keeps more cows in existence, which is a point for beef, not against it — and nobody thinks a battery chicken's life is worth living, so there's no such offset on his side.

Ravi called that slight of hand — you can't credit the beef system with happy cow lives while ignoring that the land could hold far more wild animals, and "keeps them in existence" would justify breeding any animal whose life is barely above zero. He also said she keeps switching units when it suits her: per animal when it favors beef, per unit of suffering when it favors her again. Dana said he does the same, per calorie for beef and per animal for chicken, and that somebody has to say which denominator is actually the right one, or the whole thing is just people picking the metric that indicts the meat they already dont eat.

Sort this out move by move — who's right at each step, where each is quietly conflating things, what both are missing, and which denominator is actually right, or whether that question even has an answer. I don't need a verdict on what we should eat, just on who gets the better of each exchange.
