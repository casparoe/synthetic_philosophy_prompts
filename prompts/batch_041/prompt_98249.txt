"Everyone knows the black raven confirms 'all ravens are black'. Hempel's raven paradox is the discovery that the white shoe confirms it too. The reason is that 'all ravens are black' is logically the same as 'all non-black things are non-ravens', so any non-black non-raven is an instance of the hypothesis, just like a black raven is. Hempel concluded that science is in trouble: if a white shoe supports the raven hypothesis just as well as an actual black raven does, then no piece of evidence is better than any other, and the whole idea that we can test a generalisation by collecting instances of it collapses. Most philosophers now accept that the paradox is fatal to the hypothetico-deductive method and have switched to Bayesianism, where the problem simply doesn't arise, because Bayesians only count evidence that raises the probability."
