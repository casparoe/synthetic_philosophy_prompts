I keep trying to read Kant's universalizability tests — the contradiction-in-conception test and the contradiction-in-will test — as formal checks, and I'd like you to carry that translation through properly rather than tidily. Treat a maxim as a parameterized policy that carries a precondition and an end; treat universalizability as the requirement that the policy stay feasible when everyone runs it — that its precondition is an invariant of the multi-agent system, or that the policy class is closed under universal adoption; and map the two contradictions onto unsatisfiability and onto satisfiability-with-an-unreachable-end, respectively. Then give me the residue report: which Kantian moves survive that translation cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which drop out altogether. I am particularly interested in what becomes of the thought that a universalized false-promise maxim is not merely unsatisfiable but destroys the very practice it presupposes, and of the fact that Kant's tests issue verdicts — forbidden, permitted, required — rather than descriptions of how a system behaves. Keep the translation honest: where a Kantian notion has no counterpart in my vocabulary, say so rather than substituting the nearest thing I can express. Close by adjudicating the residue — is it a limitation of my vocabulary, or evidence that the tests do less work than they are usually taken to do, and is any of it something a formalist should try to build rather than translate away?
