The exam question was: *State Nozick's tracking conditions for knowledge, and explain how his account bears on the principle that knowledge is closed under known entailment. Then say whether the tracking account can preserve our ordinary claim to know that we have hands.* My classmate's answer, reproduced in full and unedited, is as follows:

"Nozick's tracking theory holds that a subject S knows that p just in case four conditions are met: (1) p is true; (2) S believes that p; (3) if p were false, S would not believe that p; and (4) if p were true, S would believe that p. The third condition is usually called the adherence condition and the fourth the sensitivity condition, and together they require the subject's belief to track the truth of p across nearby possible worlds. Because these conditions concern the belief's relation to the world rather than the subject's evidence or justification, Nozick's view is a form of externalism.

Nozick uses this apparatus to argue that knowledge is not closed under known entailment. Closure says that if S knows p and knows that p entails q, then S knows q. Nozick denies this. His example is that I can know I have hands, and I can know that if I have hands then I am not a brain in a vat, and yet I do not know that I am not a brain in a vat. The reason is that my belief that I have hands tracks the truth, whereas my belief that I am not a brain in a vat does not track the truth, since if I were a brain in a vat I would still believe that I was not one. The tracking conditions are not transmitted through deduction, and so the failure of the second belief does not infect the first.

This means Nozick's theory can preserve ordinary knowledge while denying that we can know we are not brains in vats, and it therefore escapes skepticism about the external world. Because closure fails, though, Nozick has to concede that we can never come to know anything new by inference; any conclusion I reach by deducing it from something I already know would fail to be knowledge, since the tracking conditions are not preserved through deduction. Nozick accepts this consequence, and it is the main price of his view."
