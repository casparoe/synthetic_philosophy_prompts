Write an essay on why promises bind. The standard accounts roughly split three ways: you're bound because of the expectations you've voluntarily raised in the person you promised, because you've invoked a social practice whose rules require it, or because of something about your own integrity or commitment. I want you to test these against a case I keep chewing on. Your closest friend is dying and asks you, as a last request, to promise to finish and publish their half-written book. You promise. They die a week later. Nobody else knows about the promise or cares about the book, the friend is gone, so there's no expectations left to disappoint, no one left to hold you to it, no reputational cost, and nothing of anyone else's hangs on you doing it. Does the promise still bind? Reach a verdict and justify it -- if it still binds, which account explains why and what does that tell you about the others; if it doesn't, say which accounts get it wrong and why. Then say whether your answer changes if keeping the promise would actually damage the friend's reputation. Don't end on the fence.
