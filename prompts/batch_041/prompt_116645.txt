I am a final-year undergraduate in philosophy, currently enrolled in a seminar on reasons and normativity, and I have been asked to prepare the question script for an interview that our department's student-run series is conducting next month. The guest is a philosopher who has spent much of her career defending an internalism about reasons in the tradition of Bernard Williams, and who has recently extended that position to epistemic reasons as well. Her distinctive move, which I would like the questions to take seriously rather than treat as a curiosity, is a proposal to split the single relation of having a reason into two: a guiding relation, which obtains when a consideration is capable of moving the agent through her existing motivational set, and a warranting relation, which obtains when a consideration genuinely counts in favour of the action. She holds that internalism is true of the guiding relation and false of the warranting relation, and that much of the dispute between Williams and Parfit is an equivocation produced by running the two together.

Act as a professional philosopher working in metaethics and the philosophy of action, with a specialist's command of this literature and a talent for the well-aimed question. Before drafting anything, spend some time working out where the pressure actually falls on a view of this shape, and only then write the questions.

I want eight to ten questions in total, ordered so that the interview builds rather than circles, and I want each question followed by a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Please include at least one question each on:

- whether splitting the relation leaves the word reason doing any single job, or whether the view is quietly a homonymy thesis;
- how a sound deliberative route can be specified without smuggling in the very external reasons she denies;
- the extension to epistemic reasons, and whether belief has anything playing the role of a motivational set;
- what the view implies for our practices of advising, blaming, and holding one another responsible, where we routinely attribute reasons to people who cannot be moved by them.

The questions should press where the literature presses. Where a standard objection has a standard reply, please state the objection in a form that anticipates the reply, then make the follow-up force her past it. The closing question should ask what would count against the two-relation picture, in a form specific enough that a serious answer is possible, and should not be answerable by restating the distinction.

Two constraints. Do not include questions about her career, influences, or what drew her to the topic; every question must turn on the content of the position. And do not supply citations, reading lists, or references to particular publications, since the script will not carry apparatus and I have no way to check such references.
