I'm preparing a long-form interview for my science-and-ideas podcast with a bioethicist whose position is that brain death is not a biological discovery but a legal and moral convention — one adopted partly to make organ procurement from ventilated patients possible — and that the so-called dead donor rule has been quietly reinterpreted to keep transplantable organs flowing. I'd like eight to ten questions that keep her from simply restating the thesis. Include at least one question each on: how she separates permanent from irreversible loss of function in donation-after-circulatory-death protocols, and why the interval before death is declared is fixed where it is; whether the whole-brain and the brainstem criteria for death identify the same biological state or only the same legal status; what she takes to be happening biologically when a ventilator keeps a brain-dead body's organs interacting for years; and how the dead donor rule can survive a practice that recovers organs from patients whose circulation has stopped but whose brains were never assessed. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the reply she is likeliest to give, and end with the one case or finding that would make her give up the thesis.
