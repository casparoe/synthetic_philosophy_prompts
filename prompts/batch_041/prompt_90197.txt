Alright, our formal epistemology reading group is making everyone write down where they stand on disagreement before we start on the Aumann stuff, and I want a sanity check on mine before I get shredded. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Full-blown Bayesian. Credences, conditonalization, Jeffrey's rule when the evidence is uncertain. That's the whole story about updating, as far as I'm concerned.

2. Aumann's agreement theorem is one of the most underrated results we have. If two agents share a prior and it's common knowledge what each of them believes, they can't rationally agree to disagree — somebody has to be making a mistake. I've used this to shut down the "let's just agree to disagree" move in seminar more than once, and I stand by it.

3. But I don't think the common prior is a constraint on rationality, it's a modelling convenience. Two people with all the same evidence can rationally have different priors, and I don't buy Harsanyi's line that any prior difference has to trace back to some difference in information. I actually think a field where everyone has converged on one prior is epistemicaly worse off than one with a spread.

4. On peer disagreement: when someone about as good as me disagrees, I should move toward them but not all the way. The disagreement is evidence I've slipped somewhere, but I'm not just handing over my credences. And some disagreements persist indefinitely with nobody being irrational.

5. The reflection principle, I'm on board: if I'm certain I'll end up at credence x about something tomorrow, and certain I'll be thinking straight when I do, then I should already be at x.

6. Though I also think you should sometimes flatly refuse to defer to future-you — if I know a long sales pitch is going to wear me down, or that I'll fold to a room full of confident people, the right move is to discount my future self rather than trust it.

So which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly? Order them by severity, and for the pairs that only look like conflicts, tell me why they're fine. Then for the real ones, what's the cheapest thing to give up and what does giving it up cost me? And if there's a single revision that would clean up the most, what is it? Please don't just tell me they're all sort of in tension — I need to know which ones are load-bearing.
