I am a high school student who runs a small interview podcast for my school's philosophy society. Next month's guest is a philosopher who defends a qualified version of national character. Her position is not that nations have unchanging essences, but that national character claims can be coherent when they are understood as claims about the shape of the distribution of behavioral dispositions within a population, rather than as claims about the average member or about every member. She also holds that a bimodal distribution can support a national character claim even when the average is uninformative, and that such claims are morally permissible only when they are used as defeasible priors that are quickly revised in light of individual evidence, never as fixed descriptions of individuals.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions for me, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The questions should press on the actual content of this position rather than invite her to restate it, and the follow-ups should make the standard replies harder to use. I would like at least one question each on: (1) how a distributional claim about a population avoids the ecological fallacy, which I understand as inferring an individual's traits from group-level statistics; (2) what distinguishes a national character claim from a mere stereotype, given that both can be statistically accurate; (3) whether the view can avoid essentialism when the relevant dispositions are socially reproduced and attributed to people by institutions; (4) how the population is individuated, especially with respect to diaspora, immigration, regional variation, and people who reject the national label; and (5) whether there are contexts in which using an accurate group-level prior is impermissible because of the way it burdens an individual. Please also include a closing question that asks what evidence would count against a particular national character claim, and a follow-up that asks how she would distinguish disconfirming evidence from the claim being unfalsifiable. Do not ask for reading recommendations, citations, or bibliographies; the interview is self-contained. Please write the questions and follow-ups in British English.
