I have been trying to find whether the following pattern has a settled name, and if it does not, I want it named and bounded properly rather than just labelled.

The pattern is this. A number of people each arrive at the same conclusion, and each of them experiences the arrival as an independent confirmation of it. The group's confidence then compounds as though it had N separate pieces of evidence, each bearing on the question from a different angle. But the independence is spurious: their verdicts all rest on a single shared upstream source — the same dataset, the same initial report, the same measurement convention, the same body of prior commentary — so one error at the source propagates into every member's verdict without being tested anywhere along the way. No one is being irrational, and no one is merely copying anyone else's action; each person consults their own evidence and finds it supportive. The trouble is that what presents itself as N witnesses is really one witness counted N times.

Two instances of the shape. First: several analysts, working separately and without conferring, all pull from the same vendor's database and all reach the same estimate; the agreement is treated downstream as convergent corroboration, though a single error in the database would have produced exactly this agreement. Second: several evaluators, again working independently, each rely on the same benchmark to certify a model, and their matching verdicts are read as many independent endorsements rather than one instrument used many times.

I am aware this sits near several things and I want the differences spelled out rather than waved at. It is not quite an information cascade, because in a cascade people set aside their own private information and copy the actions of those who went before, whereas here each person consults their own information and finds it supportive. It is not pluralistic ignorance, because there the error concerns what others privately believe rather than how the world is. And it is not simply the point that the Condorcet jury theorem needs independent voters, because I am after the specific shape of the failure, not the general observation that dependence spoils the theorem.

So: does this configuration have an established name in the literature on herding, cascades, or the aggregation of judgment? If it does, give me the term and state the precise conditions under which it is supposed to obtain. If it does not, coin a term, define it, and tell me what it excludes — in particular how it differs from an information cascade, from mere correlated error in jury aggregation, and from groupthink. I do not want a bare name; I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges fall.
