I've been defending a line in aesthetics for a few years now, mostly in a grad seminar I keep reworking, and I'd like to find out how much of it is actualy load-bearing. The line is: you can't get aesthetic knowledge by being told. If a colleague whose taste has tracked mine for twenty years tells me the new production is magnificent, I can sensibly expect to enjoy it, and I can sensibly say 'it's magnificent' at a party, but I don't think I *know* it's magnificent -- not in the way I know it after sitting through the thing myself. My reason is that an aesthetic verdict has to be grounded non-inferentially in your own experience of the object, and testimony makes the resulting belief inferential all the way down, which is the wrong kind of grounding. I've been calling the state you get instead 'borrowed taste' and treating it as confidence without the corresponding entitlement.

What I want from you is a map of what would count against this. Skip the usual grumble that we lean on critics constantly -- I want the specific things. Which facts about how taste is actually transmitted (kids, apprentices, wine, sight-reading, film school) should genuinely worry me, and which are just noise? Are there cases where a piece of aesthetic knowledge is obviously inferential, where nobody would deny that someone came to know something aesthetic from being told -- because if there are, then my non-inferentiality requirement is too strong, not just my conclusion. And I keep worrying that I'm not really defending a claim about evaluation at all: a lot of my confidence comes from the fact that nobody can tell you what a smell is like, and I can't tell whether I'm just riding that. If the view collapses into the acquaintance thesis about phenomenal knowledge, does it still say anything distinctive about aesthetics?

Also be honest about whether I've rigged it. I suspect I can redescribe any counterexample as 'that's not real aesthetic knowledge, that's just expectation,' and I want to know if that's a legitimate distinction or whether I've made the thing unfalsifiable. Sort the defeaters by how much they should actually move me, mark which ones someone who holds my view would themselves concede as real costs versus the ones only critics press, and say what I'd have to give up for each. And tell me plainly if the answer is that the view is well insulated and that this is or isn't a problem.
