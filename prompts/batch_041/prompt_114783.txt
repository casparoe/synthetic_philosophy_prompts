Hey, so I'm putting together a satirical piece and I want your help drafting it.

Setup: a fictional government agency — the National Office of Historical Apologies — whose entire remit is apologizing on behalf of the state for things it did long ago. What I want from you is the section of its internal staff handbook that tells case officers how to decide who is owed an apology and which kind. Not a news article, not an op-ed — a deadpan internal procedure document, numbered sections, civil-service register, its the kind of thing a real department would actually circulate. 600-700 words.

The funny has to come from getting the underlying philosophy right, not from silly names or gags. Here's the machinery I want built in, use all of it:

- A tiered apology menu. Tier 1: an acknowledgement that certain events took place. Tier 2: an expression of sincere regret. Tier 3: a full apology that actually contains the word sorry — and Tier 3 requires ministerial sign-off, which the handbook notes is 'rarely forthcoming'.
- Standing. Only living people can receive an apology, so a claimant has to document lineal descent from the wronged party, and the handbook treats this as a routine formality rather than a morally fraught thing.
- Uptake. An apology only counts as delivered once the recipient publicly accepts it. The handbook phrases this in a warm, considerate way; the effect is that the victim ends up responsible for whether the state ever apologises.
- Classification. Officers must sort each historical act into 'regrettable' (lawful and widely accepted at the time) or 'atrocity', and the entitlement follows from the bucket. The worry that it's unfair to judge the past by present standards should be doing work here, but nobody in the document should ever lecture about it.
- A firewall between this office and the one that handles money. Compensation is out of scope, officers are instructed not to raise it, and claimants who ask get redirected to a Remediation desk whose extension the handbook cheerfully describes as temporarily unstaffed.
- A liability shield. Nothing in an apology may be read as an admission of legal responsibility, so the templates keep the state out of the active voice — events occurred, harms were experienced, that kind of thing.
- An eligibility window that cuts off the distant past, with one worked example: two claimants, one displaced in the 1830s and one in the 1970s, the earlier one turned away as outside scope, and the officer told to communicate this 'with sensitivity'.

The target is the practice itself: the ritual state apology, the hedging, the way a symbolic gesture gets to stand in for material redress, and the way institutions apologise while insulating themselves from the cost. Someone who knows this literature should feel the document gets it; someone who doesn't should still laugh and follow whats going on. Stay deadpan the whole way — no winking, no narrator surfacing to tell us its absurd. And keep it a made-up country with made-up wrongs so it doesn't hinge on real dates and facts.
