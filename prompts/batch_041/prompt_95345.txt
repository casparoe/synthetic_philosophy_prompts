I have a position I would like you to put under pressure, and I want the result to be a map of its exposure rather than a defence of it. Please refer to arguments and positions by description rather than by citation; I am not looking for a survey of who has said what, and I will supply references myself if I need them.

The position is a metaphysical rather than a semantic form of pragmatic encroachment. I hold that a subject's epistemic position — whether she knows, and not merely whether we are prepared to describe her as knowing — is partly constituted by practical facts about what is at stake in her being right. The familiar shape of the view is that the same evidence which suffices for knowledge when little turns on the matter does not suffice when a great deal does. I want to hold the strong version of this, and I want to hold a further commitment that I suspect carries most of the weight and is rarely examined on its own: the stakes that do the work are the stakes that actually obtain, not the stakes the subject takes to obtain.

Here is a case of the kind I have in mind. A hiker consults her map on a fine morning, forms a settled and well-grounded belief about which fork to take, and sets off; the stakes are ordinary and, by her own lights, nothing much turns on the choice. Unbeknownst to her, a landslide overnight has made the other fork lethal. On my view, something happened to her epistemic standing at the moment the terrain changed — what was knowledge became something less, or her grounds ceased to be adequate — even though her evidence, her reliability, and her confidence are all exactly as they were, and she will never learn of the change. I take this to be a consequence of the view rather than an embarrassment to it, but I want to be told whether I am paying a cost I should refuse.

What I want from you:

- The defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Which observations, cases, arguments, or formal results should move me, and how much? Distinguish what would be decisive from what would be awkward.
- The pragmatic-implicature family of objections. If a general story about the pragmatics of ascribing knowledge can explain the same conversational data without locating stakes-sensitivity in the subject, is encroachment thereby refuted, or only left unmotivated? Is there a prediction my view makes that such a story cannot match — and if so, what is it?
- What the awareness-independent commitment costs me specifically. Does it make knowledge unacceptably opaque to the knower, or sever knowledge from the subject's own perspective in a way that forfeits whatever role knowledge is supposed to play in guiding assertion, inquiry, or practical reasoning? Would dropping that commitment save the view, and if so, what would be left of it?
- Whether the view collapses into a rival. If every consideration in its favour turns out to be evidence about how we talk, does it reduce to a thesis about the word rather than the world? If so, is that fatal, or merely a re-description that leaves the substance intact?
- What a committed defender would themselves concede as counting against the view, as distinct from what critics say. Which of the defeaters above would they accept, and which would they dismiss as changing the subject?
- An honest verdict on insulation. Has the position, as normally stated, been qualified until nothing could count against it? If it has, say so plainly, and say whether that is a defect in this kind of view or simply what a correct view of this kind looks like.

I would personally be most moved by a demonstration that my view and its semantic rival are not merely empirically equivalent but predictively so, since I do not want to be defending a thesis about usage while believing I am defending one about knowledge. Take that as a constraint on what would convince me.
