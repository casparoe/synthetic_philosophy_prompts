I am a doctoral student working on the metaphysics of content, and I have spent the better part of a year in a reading group on the unity of the proposition. We began with Bradley's regress expecting to dispatch it in a fortnight, and we have not moved an inch since. What I want from you is a diagnosis of the impasse rather than a verdict on the substantive issue, so let me set out the shape of the dispute as honestly as I can.

The steps of the regress nobody disputes. If a proposition is a complex entity whose constituents are bound together by a relation, then that relation appears to require a further relation to bind it to its relata, and so on without end; no stage of the series ever delivers a whole that is one thing rather than a heap. The parties divide over what this shows. One side holds the regress to be vicious, and its conclusion to be that no collection of constituents plus a tie can be the bearer of truth and falsity; the unity of the proposition is then either primitive and permanently unexplained, or the conception of a proposition as a complex must be abandoned. A second side, in the spirit of Russell's early insistence that relations do not themselves need relating, holds that the regress refutes only a particular and avoidable analysis, namely the treatment of relations as further items standing alongside their relata; on this reading the demand that some item do the unifying is itself the error, and the regress is a reductio of the question rather than of the answer. A third position — which our group can neither embrace nor dismiss — treats the tie as non-relational, so that the predicate is in some sense identical with, or exhaustive of, the property it attributes, and the unity of the proposition is accordingly not something that has to be secured from outside. The first two camps agree on every step of the argument and disagree about what it establishes; the third claims to have dissolved the problem, and the other two regard its solution as a restatement of the phenomenon in fresh vocabulary.

My own hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis rather than as a settled view, is that the disagreement is not really about the validity of the regress but about what could possibly count as an explanation of unity. For one side, 'it is primitive' is the end of inquiry and therefore an admission of failure; for the other, the request for a further item to do the unifying betrays precisely the picture of composition that the regress has already discredited, so that the request is incoherent rather than unmet. If that is right, the dispute is one about explanatory standards, or perhaps about which of two distinct questions is being asked under the single heading 'the unity of the proposition'.

I should be grateful if you would diagnose the disagreement, and in doing so tell me: whether it is verbal, substantive, or two questions wearing one name; whether a hidden formal question about the logical form of relational facts is doing the work, and whether recasting the whole matter in the idiom of grounding — one item grounding the unity of the others — changes anything or merely relabels it; and whether the comparison some of us have drawn with the Third Man regress in the Parmenides illuminates the structure of the problem or imports assumptions that distort it.

Finally, given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should no progress be expected at all, and if not, why not?

Please answer in British English.
