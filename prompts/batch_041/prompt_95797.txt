I help run a small monthly debate night — two speakers, one topic, maybe eighty people in the back room of a pub — and I write the blurbs that go out to the mailing list. We've always tagged each speaker 'left' or 'right' and it used to be easy, but the last few months it's fallen apart on me. The last event was a guy who wants tariffs, a nationalised health service and a ban on new out-of-town retail, and who also thinks marriage is between a man and a woman and that the police should be harder on the homeless. Before that, a woman who is a full libertarian about drugs, sex work and speech, and who also thinks the housing market has simply failed and the state should be building. And next month I've got a former communist who now thinks immigration is what's really holding wages down, and he's furious at being billed as a right-winger, which honestly I can see. My co-organiser says we should just bin the words, but the audience picks events by them and I think the words do track something real, I just can't say what. So what I want from you is two things, in this order. First, tell me what you take the labels to actually be picking out — a bundle of issue positions that happen to travel together, a coalition that holds for historical reasons, a temperament, a stance about hierarchy and equality, something about change versus stability — because I've noticed my answer to 'is this person left or right' changes completely depending on which of those I'm using, and I dont think I can write a procedure until I've picked one. Then turn whatever you pick into a procedure I can actually run when I sit down to write a blurb: an ordered list of questions, what counts as an answer to each one, where the procedure stops and says 'neither' or 'both', and where it hands the decision back to me because the question is about values and not about facts. Constraints: the blurbs have to be one line, I'm not a political scientist, I have an afternoon, and my co-organiser will veto anything that sounds like a seminar.

Two wrinkles you should fold in rather than wave away. The first is that the same word has to do duty for a person, a party and a policy — the mailing list tags old episodes too, and 'a left-wing policy' and 'a left-wing person' don't seem to be the same claim, or at least they don't fail in the same way, and I want to know whether the procedure has to branch for that. The second is my co-organiser's suggestion, which he's very pleased with: that a label is only honest if it predicts how the person will come down on the next ten things that happen to come up. That's appealing and it also seems to make the label hostage to whatever's in the news, so tell me whether to build that test into the procedure or throw it out, and why.

Then run the thing on my three cases — the tariff-and-traditional-marriage guy, the libertarian who wants council housing, and the ex-communist — and I want at least one where it gives an answer I don't like, whether that's a speaker I'd rather not label the way it says or an answer that says 'stop using the word for this one'. Finish by telling me what the procedure can't do: which cases it will misfire on, where I'm just deciding and dressing it up as a method, and how to write the blurb when the honest label is one the speaker will object to, because I'd rather not ambush someone on the night.
