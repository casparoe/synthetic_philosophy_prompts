I'm currently taking a philosophy of biology course, and our reading group has an end-of-term tradition where each of us has to explain one idea from the syllabus as a joke — setup, beat, punchline, delivered out loud in under a minute — and then sit there while everyone decides whether the joke actually demonstrates that we understand the idea. I've drawn free-floating rationales: the idea that there can be a reason for a trait, a signal, or a behavior that no one actually has, in the sense that the reason is there to be read off the design of the thing without any mind entertaining it. I'd like you to write that joke for me.

Some constraints, because the obvious versions of this are all bad. It can't be a philosopher explaining the idea to a baffled layperson; that's the move everyone makes, and it isn't funny. It also shouldn't lean on nature-as-engineer or evolution-as-designer imagery, since that's the trope the idea is supposed to be replacing. What I want the punchline to turn on is the part of the idea I actually find strange: a free-floating rationale gives you everything you need to say that something has gone wrong — the mechanism can misfire, the signal can mislead, the trait can fail at what it's for — while giving you no one who is wrong. There's an error with no erring subject. If the joke doesn't make the audience feel that particular oddity, it isn't the joke I need.

I've also been playing with a related case and would like it in there if you can make it work. When a creature gives a reason for what it did that isn't the rationale actually explaining the behavior — the moth's own account of why it went toward the light, versus the navigational story — I've been calling that a squatted rationale: the rationale is still free-floating, but now somebody is standing on it telling a different story. I think there's a joke in the gap between the rationale being there and somebody occupying it, but I haven't found it.

Please give me one joke rather than a menu of options. After it, add a brief note on where the joke takes liberties: which parts of the setup are doing real theoretical work, which are compressed or contested, and specifically whether I've got the free-floating rationale idea right, whether the squatted-rationale case is something the theory can actually support, and whether the punchline quietly assumes a reading of function that not everyone accepts. And if the assignment itself is confused — if what I'm asking for can't be both a joke and a fair rendering of the idea — say so plainly rather than writing something that splits the difference.
