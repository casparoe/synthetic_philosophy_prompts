I am writing the opening chapter of a thesis on self-deception and have reached the point where I need to settle what I am asking of a theory before I can adjudicate between them. The field divides fairly predictably: some hold that self-deception is to be modelled on the deception of one person by another, complete with an intention to deceive and some partition of the mind into parts; some hold that the interpersonal model is a trap and want to explain the whole thing with ordinary motivational bias and no intention at all; some deny that there is a single phenomenon to explain; and a few treat it as a kind of imaginative pretence or a strategic failure of attention. My worry is that each camp simply declines to honour the cases the others treat as central, and that the real disagreement is about the criteria rather than about the phenomena, so I want the criteria laid out before anyone is scored against them.

Please write me that list of desiderata: what any adequate theory of self-deception has to deliver. I want the test cases it must get right — including the textbook case of someone who sincerely avows a spouse's fidelity against overwhelming evidence; the case of a person whose avowal of confidence comes apart from conduct that betrays the opposite; the negative case of someone who believes themselves more worthless than the evidence warrants; a socially scaffolded case in which a group sustains a flattering falsehood that no member would hold alone; a case in which memory quietly distorts with no awareness of any deceiving; and the borderline case of someone who simply never reads the test result. I want the distinctions it cannot collapse (self-deception against weakness of will, wishful thinking, self-serving bias, denial, and lying to others) and the consistency constraints it cannot violate (with how beliefs are attributed from avowal and behaviour; with the apparent impossibility of outright contradictory beliefs at the same moment; with the ban on smuggling a little person into the head; with findings on motivated reasoning and biased assimilation). For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, say whether it is non-negotiable or merely the prejudice of one camp, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — if some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, that is the result I most want. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the popular theories, and only after that a brief scorecard of the camps against the list. The chapter is for a British audience, so please write the whole answer in British English.
