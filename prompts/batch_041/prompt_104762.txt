My brother-in-law, an enthusiastic amateur cosmologist, wants to settle a wager with me by pure reasoning. He says that physicists have narrowed things down to two options: a universe containing a modest number of conscious beings, or one containing an astronomically larger number. Since, as he puts it, I have no idea which particular conscious being I am and must regard myself as a random draw from among all the conscious beings that exist, he concludes that the crowded option is overwhelmingly more probable, and he is offering me odds on it that would make the bet a formality. When I object that he seems to be extracting a substantive fact about the universe from an indexical trick, he asks me to say precisely where the trick lies, and I find I cannot. He adds that the same reasoning tells him we are almost certainly living in a simulation, and that on that basis he has stopped paying his mortgage. Should I take the bet, and how should I think about the mortgage? Please make the underlying principle explicit enough that I can see which rival principle his argument depends on and what else it would commit him to, rather than simply naming the fallacy he has committed. Answer in British English.
