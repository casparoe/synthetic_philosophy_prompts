I've been covering philosophy of physics on and off for a few years now, and there's one fight I keep walking into at workshops that seems to never end, so I want to try to figure out what's actually going on in it. It's the argument that special relativity gives you the block universe — what people call the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. The gist, as it's always explained to me: simultaneity is relative to a frame, so if you take some event that's happening right now as far as I'm concerned, and then follow the chain of "right now" relations out through other frames, you eventually reach events that are in my own future. So my future is already real, and so is my past, and there's no privileged slice of "now" — the whole four-dimensional block just is, and "the passage of time" is something we add on top. The people who defend this think it's almost a proof. The people who don't — and there are plenty of them, including some who take relativity completely seriously — say the argument cheats somewhere, that it leans on a notion of "real" or "present" that relativity doesn't give you, or that it treats a frame-relative simultaneity as if it were a frame-independent relation between events, so you can't chain it up the way the argument needs. I've now sat through maybe six versions of this and neither side ever moves an inch. So here's what I want from you, and I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right.

My working hypothesis — and I'd like you to tell me if it's right, half-right, or just wrong — is that this isn't really a disagreement about physics at all, because both sides have the same equations and neither is disputing any prediction, and it isn't really a disagreement about the logic of the argument either, because the disputed step is spelled out plainly enough for anyone to inspect. My guess is that it's mostly a dispute about words doing double duty: one side means by "exists" or "is real" roughly "is somewhere in the spacetime manifold," the other means something more like "is present, is happening, is actual as opposed to merely possible or past or future," and once you separate those the "proof" either evaporates or becomes a trivial consequence of a definition — I can't tell which. But I suspect there's also a genuinely substantive disagreement hiding underneath, about whether accepting relativity commits you to treating every frame's "now" as equally authoritative for what's real and not just for what's true, and maybe a third thing that's neither verbal nor substantive, namely that the question is somehow ill-posed. Lay out what each side is actually committed to, tell me which strands of the disagreement are verbal, which are substantive, and which are just people talking past each other about what an explanation of time is supposed to deliver, and then tell me honestly what would count as progress here — some distinction, some formal result, some reframing of the question — or whether this is one of those disputes where the right answer is that no amount of philosophy is going to move it and we should stop expecting it to.
