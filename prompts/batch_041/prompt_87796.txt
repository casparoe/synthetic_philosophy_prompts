I've been asked to put my position on the Gödelian argument against mechanism — the Lucas–Penrose line — into writing so that colleagues can press me on whether it actually holds together. Before I circulate it, I want a consistency audit. Here is where I've landed.

(1) Anti-mechanism. Human mathematical understanding is not the operation of any Turing machine. No algorithm, however powerful, captures what a mathematician does when she grasps that the Gödel sentence of a given formal system is true.

(2) The route. What establishes (1) is Gödel's first incompleteness theorem. For any consistent formal system F strong enough for arithmetic, there is a sentence that F can neither prove nor refute but that we can recognize as true; so we are not identical with F, and since F was arbitrary, not with any such system.

(3) Fallibilism. All of our mathematical knowledge is fallible and open to revision. We can never be absolutely certain that a system strong enough for arithmetic is consistent, and I would not claim any infallible insight into the consistency of such systems.

(4) Naturalism. I am a physicalist: the mind is a physical system and there is no non-physical faculty doing the work. If (1) is right, then whatever separates us from a machine has to be a difference in physics, not a difference in kind.

(5) The positive story. I expect the eventual fundamental physics — the theory that supersedes quantum mechanics and general relativity — to be non-computable, and I take it that this non-computability is what grounds our capacity to outrun any given formal system.

(6) Computability. I accept the Church–Turing thesis without reservation: any function computable by an effective, step-by-step procedure is computable by a Turing machine.

My suspicion is that these do not all fit together, but I genuinely cannot tell where the seam is, and I would rather not be told that everything is fine. Please go through the commitments pairwise. For each pair, say whether the tension is genuine or merely apparent, and for the genuine ones rank them by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each genuine tension, state what would have to be given up, what the cheapest repair would be, and what that repair costs. For the merely apparent ones, explain why the conflict dissolves. Then tell me the single revision that would clean up the most at the lowest cost, and whether any of the commitments can be strengthened rather than weakened. Write in American English. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} that ranks the commitments from most to least implicated in genuine tension, using their numbers and nothing else inside the braces.
