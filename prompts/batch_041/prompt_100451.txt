At our town council meeting last month someone proposed capping donations to local candidates at $250, and a guy got up, said "money is speech," and sat back down like that settled it. Then in the comment section of the local paper somebody said the exact opposite, that money isn't speech, it's property, and thats why the town can regulate it. Both of them said it like it was obvious, and I've been chewing on it since.

Thing is, "money is speech" clearly cant mean just one thing, or you wouldnt get those two reactions to the same sentence. So what I want is the menu — all the different precise claims a person could actually be making with that one line, laid out from the ones so weak that nobody would deny them, up to the ones that basically say money and speech are the same kind of thing. For each one I want to know: is it true, is it doing any real work, and does it get you anywhere near "so you cant cap donations"?

Two spots where I keep getting stuck. First, when I hand a candidate a check, is the speech the check itself, or is the check just the thing that buys the speech later? And is there really no difference between me giving $250 to a candidate and me spending $250 on a sign in my own yard? The guy at the meeting seemed to think those were the same speech and I dont see why. Second, the property line: if money is property and not speech, does that mean anything I buy with it stops being speech too? That one seems to eat itself.

Also tell me which reading the guy at the meeting definately had to be using, since his one-liner only does the work he wanted on some of them and not others. Lay it out as a list please, not an essay.
