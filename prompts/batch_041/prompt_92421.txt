I am opening a graduate reading group on teleosemantic accounts of mental content and would like to begin with a joke that is genuinely about the theory, i.e. one whose punchline turns on a commitment the theory actually makes rather than on frogs, bats, or philosophers as comic types. The commitment I have in mind is the consumer-based one: what an inner state represents is fixed by what the mechanism that consumes it is supposed to do with it, rather than by what reliably causes it, so a state can mean something the creature has never encountered and may never encounter — a lab lineage that has never once met a fly can still occupy a state that means fly, provided the producer–consumer pair carries the right selection history — while conversely no state counts as a representation at all without that history, however well it happens to correlate with something. Please write the joke, and keep it short. The test I am aiming at is that someone who knows the literature laughs because the punchline exploits a real commitment of the view, and someone who does not still sees that a claim was made rather than a pun. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing, in a short phrase, the commitment the joke turns on.
