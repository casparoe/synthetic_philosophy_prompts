In a seminar on utilitarianism, a classmate keeps deploying the phrase the separateness of persons as a one-line refutation: because persons are separate, any theory that sums welfare across lives is treating them as one person, and that is enough to reject it. I want a menu of what that phrase could actually be claiming — from the trivial claim that persons are numerically distinct, through the claim that interpersonal aggregation is impermissible, through the claim that each person is a separate locus of moral value whose claims cannot be traded off against others', to the radical claim that a social welfare function or an impersonal total are category mistakes. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or false, and say which reading, if any, actually supports the refutation. Separate the metaphysical readings from the normative ones, and note which reading the slogan's users probably intend. I don't need citations or a reading list; I need the claims distinguished and judged.
