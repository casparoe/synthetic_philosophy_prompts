I'm assembling materials for a graduate seminar on explanation in the special sciences, and I want a calibrated judgment rather than a tour of the literature. The session is built around a distinction of Daniel Dennett's, from Darwin's Dangerous Idea: a crane is a sub-process that does real explanatory work by being assembled, step by step, out of parts that were already available, whereas a skyhook is an explanation that lifts itself by its own bootstraps, presupposing in some disguised form the very thing it sets out to explain. Dennett's companion notion, greedy reductionism, is the separate sin of explaining a higher-level phenomenon by leaping straight to the lowest level and skipping every intermediate level of description. The two axes are independent, and I want you to treat them as such: an explanation can be a genuine crane and still be greedy, and a resolutely non-reductionist explanation can still be a skyhook.

Below are seven explanatory strategies. Some correspond to live research programs, others are composites I have constructed for this exercise, and a few may be strawmen. Judge each on its merits and do not assume that any of them is correct or established.

1. The origin of life: an autocatalytic closure of small molecules, in which each member of a set catalyzes the formation of others in the set, so that the whole set reproduces without any template.
2. Depression: a deficit in one neurotransmitter system, with the disorder treated as exhausted by that deficit.
3. Consciousness: an irreducible property that certain physical arrangements possess once they pass some threshold of integration.
4. Language: a single innate combinatorial operation, appearing in one mutational step, that then generates the full syntactic capacity.
5. The reliability of nervous systems: many individually noisy units, each voting, with the majority vote recovered as the reliable output.
6. Moral norms: a few individuals invent norms, others copy whoever seems to be doing well, and the copying is offered as the whole of the story.
7. Cognition: the brain minimizes prediction error, and the minimization is claimed to be not a mechanism for understanding but identical with understanding.

For each of the seven, give me:
- a score from −3 to +3 on the crane-to-skyhook axis, where −3 is a pure skyhook and +3 is a pure crane;
- a score from 0 to 3 for greedy reductionism, where 0 is no premature level-skipping and 3 is the paradigm case;
- one sentence of justification, and, where you can, the single feature of the strategy that drives the score.

Then rank all seven from most trustworthy to least trustworthy on the one dimension that matters for this session: how much genuine explanatory work the strategy does without smuggling its conclusion in. Present the ranking as a tier list (S through D), and for the top tier and the bottom tier explain in two or three sentences why those members landed there rather than one tier up or down.

Finally, add a calibration note: state how confident you are, as a probability, that your top and bottom placements would survive review by a room of specialists, and single out the one judgment above that you are least sure of, saying what would have to be true for you to reverse it.
