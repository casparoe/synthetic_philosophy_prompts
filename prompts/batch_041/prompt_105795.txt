I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether a constitution can bind a later generation, and whenever the argument got hard he fell back on the same line: that a constitution is just tying yourself to the mast, and that this settles the matter. I came away convinced that neither of us knew what that image actually commits us to, and that we were using it to mean quite different things. Please treat "a constitution is tying yourself to the mast," and its cousin "that's a Ulysses contract," as a piece of language to be disambiguated rather than a thesis to be defended.

Lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise claims the image could be making. I am especially interested in the following axes, though add others if the metaphor genuinely carries them: who is bound and who does the binding; whether the binder must be the same self as the bound party, or whether the crew that lashes Odysseus to the mast is a third party exercising an authority the later self never grants at the moment of binding; whether the binding operates by making the later action impossible or merely costly, and whether that difference matters; whether the later self's contrary preference is to be treated as weakness to be overridden or as a genuine change of mind to be respected; whether the crew stand inside the contract or outside it, and who in turn binds them; whether enforcement requires a triggering condition that the enforcers can themselves identify, and who is to interpret the contract when the bound party disputes what it demands; whether the image is doing work about weakness of will, about time-inconsistency, about personal identity, or about political legitimacy; and whether the metaphor reaches the intergenerational case at all, where the bound party never had the chance to give the order in the first place.

For each reading, give a one-line verdict -- platitude, substantive and defensible, or false -- and say what it would take to make it true. Then say which reading my friend most likely meant when he insisted the image settles whether a constitution can bind a generation that never consented, and whether that reading actually delivers the conclusion he wanted.

Open with a single sentence naming the load-bearing reading. Keep the whole reply under 700 words.
