I am a high school student who has been reading about the philosophy of time, and I would like your help with a translation exercise that I think will teach me more than another summary would. Please take the role of an expert on the metaphysics of time — someone thoroughly at home with presentism, eternalism, tense, and the A-series/B-series vocabulary — who also knows professional video editing software at a working level: timelines, playheads, keyframes, source media versus the sequence, proxy files, render queues, and undo history. I know the editing side of that description far better than the philosophy side, which is the point of the exercise.

As I understand it, the disagreement runs roughly like this. Presentism says that only the present moment is real: the past is no longer and the future is not yet, and talk about past or future events is talk about something that does not, strictly speaking, exist. Eternalism says that past, present, and future events are all equally real parts of a single unchanging whole, and that the present moment is not a special place in reality but an indexical matter, like the word "here." I have also gathered that some of the disagreement turns on whether there is genuine becoming or passage, and on whether tense can be reduced to tenseless relations like earlier-than.

Before you translate anything, please brainstorm briefly and explicitly. List the features of editing software that might serve as counterparts for each position, and say which candidate mappings you considered and rejected, and why. Then do the following, in order.

First, restate each position as faithfully as you can using the vocabulary of the editing software, one position at a time. Be concrete about which element of the software plays which role. Does "the past is real" correspond to source media sitting on the drive, to the clips arranged on the timeline, or to the undo history? Is the playhead the present moment, or the perspective of a viewer, or neither? Does becoming correspond to playback, to rendering, or to nothing at all? If a mapping only works for one position and not the other, say so rather than smoothing it over.

Second, give me the residue report. Which claims survive the translation cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in the editing vocabulary at all? For the residue, tell me whether it is a defect of the editing framework — something it is simply unable to express — or a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked.

Two warnings I would ask you to take seriously. Do not smuggle the editing framework's assumptions in and call it translation: for instance, do not conclude that eternalism is obviously true merely because the software stores every frame. And notice that the software itself runs in time, so any translation that leans on playback to explain becoming risks circularity; please address that risk directly rather than ignoring it.

Think carefully and reason step by step, and be honest when a mapping fails instead of forcing it. I am not asking for reading recommendations; I want the translation and the residue itself.
