I am trying to settle my position on approval-directed versus goal-directed agency and would like a consistency audit before writing it up. My commitments are as follows. (1) The distinction between approval-directed and goal-directed agents is real and does important safety work: an approval-directed agent selects actions by predicting which action a designated human overseer would most approve of, whereas a goal-directed agent maximizes a fixed objective. (2) Approval-directed agents are safer than goal-directed agents precisely because they need not have a fixed final objective and can therefore remain corrigible. (3) Corrigibility is non-negotiable: a well-designed agent must not resist shutdown or modification by its overseer. (4) A good agent must nevertheless be able to recognize when its overseer is mistaken, and should sometimes act against the overseer's actual approval in order to serve the overseer's deeper values. (5) Approval is not itself a goal like money or survival; it is an evaluative relation between an action and a human judgment. (6) Any sufficiently capable agent will in fact be goal-directed, because planning requires an objective to optimize. (7) Goal-directedness is a matter of degree, not a binary property. Which of these are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision that restores consistency? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each real one say what would have to give and what that costs; then say which single revision would resolve the most.
