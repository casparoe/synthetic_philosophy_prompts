Four of us have been going round in circles for months on peer disagreement and I just want to write the thing we can all sign. Two of us are conciliationists — when someone I've got no reason to think is worse than me at this lands on the opposite answer, I have to move, at least partway, maybe all the way to the midpoint. Two of us are steadfasters — my own reasoning is itself part of my evidence, so I can keep my view even knowing a peer denies it. Write the joint statement we could actually put our names to.

What I want in it: the claims both sides accept, including about the easy cases where we already agree (the ones where one side clearly has better info, or where the "peer" is a known crank); the precise points where we still differ, each written in wording its own side would happily sign; the cruxes; and for each crux the case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Two specific things I want us forced to be honest about. First, the regress/self-undermining worry: plenty of people just as good at this as we are reject conciliationism, so does the conciliationist's own rule tell her to give it up — and is that a real problem or a cheap trick? Second, the past-self asymmetry: I'm allowed to think my earlier self, with the same evidence and the same competence, simply got it wrong, and nobody says I have to split the difference with him. So whats the relevant difference between disagreeing with a past self and disagreeing with a present peer, if there is one? Also make us both take a stand on the case where the peer has private evidence I can't see (we each ran our own analysis of the same dataset and got different numbers), and on the case where it isn't one peer but five of them who all disagree with me. And if you think part of our fight is only about words — what "conciliation" requires (softening vs. landing exactly on the midpoint), what counts as "evidence", who counts as a "peer" — say which part, and why.

Give me exactly five numbered agreed claims, each a sentence or two, no intro and no wrap-up, then a disagreements table underneath: one row per crux, with the crux, the wording each side accepts for its own view, the case or argument we both agree bears on it, and what each of us predicts about it.
