Write the desiderata for a theory of psychiatric classification: the specification any adequate account of what makes a psychiatric category a legitimate kind must satisfy. Give the requirements list — the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, and the distinctions it must not collapse — each with the consideration that motivates it, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of one camp. The list must accommodate at least the following: that applying a category tends to alter the people classified, so the category's extension shifts as it is used, and shifts in both directions, with categories at times becoming self-fulfilling and at times self-undermining; that the categories are polythetic, with no single feature shared by all members; that comorbidity and within-category heterogeneity are typical rather than exceptional; that what counts as disorder varies across cultures and periods; that a category's clinical usefulness and its validity as a kind can come apart; and that the account must leave room for the claim that some conditions are genuinely pathological without simply equating disorder with social disapproval. Then flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, whether the demand that a category track a stable underlying causal mechanism can survive the looping effect. End by naming the single requirement that excludes the most candidates; skip the full scorecard.
