California has a setup I cant quite wrap my head around and I want to know what it's quietly assuming. A bare majority of voters can pass an initiative that amends the state constitution — it just rewrites the top law of the state, no supermajority required. But ever since Proposition 13 in 1978, raising a state tax takes a two-thirds vote of the legislature, and local special taxes take two-thirds of the local voters. So the constitution itself is changeable by 50% plus one, while a tax increase needs 67%. The funny part: the two-thirds tax rule lives inside that same constitution, so a simple-majority initiative could just repeal it. The supermajority is only as durable as the simple majority that could undo it.

What philosophical commitments does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the theory of democratic legitimacy and of entrenchment that would make sense of the pattern, if there is one. Does it presuppose that some decisions deserve more consent than others — that a tax is a kind of taking which needs near-unanimity, while constitutional rules are just whatever the people happen to say? Or is it a precommitment picture, where the people try to bind their future selves against their own impulses, Ulysses-style? And if the tax rule is itself repealable by a simple majority, is that precommitment real or just a speed bump? Contrast it with Switzerland, where amending the constitution needs a double majority of people and cantons — there the constitution is harder to move than ordinary law, the opposite of what California seems to be doing.

Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say which rival theories of entrenchment (formal vs substantive, say) would also license it. Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined? What would a defender of supermajority rules have to add to make the whole thing coherent? And if we replaced the implicit view with a flat one-person-one-vote, majority-rules-on-everything picture — entrenchment included — what exactly would have to change?
