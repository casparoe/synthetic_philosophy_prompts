I'm applying for a small six-month grant (a postdoc thing, mostly writing plus a workshop) and need to turn a half-formed idea into a proper proposal, maybe 3-4 pages.

The idea: the whole fight over corruption — the cross-country indices, the aid conditionality stuff, the ethnographers who say gift-giving and patronage are just how those societies run and it's imperial to slap the word on them — feels stuck because everyone treats "corruption" as one thing a practice either is or isn't. I think there are two different predicates hiding under one word. One is thin and role-relative: someone is corrupt when they breach a duty that's internal to the role they occupy — they take money to do the thing the role already told them to do. The other is thick and comparative: playing favorites when a general standard of impartiality says you shouldn't. My hunch is that the cross-cultural dispute mostly dissolves once you pull these apart, because the thin one may be universal (any role with a purpose can be betrayed) while the thick one isn't, and the indices are quietly measuring the thick one while pretending it's the thin one. So the question I want the proposal built around: is "corrupt" a role-relative predicate — a practice counts as corruption only when it breaches a duty internal to some office the agent holds — and if so, does the relativist/universalist fight reduce to a factual dispute about which roles and internal duties actually exist in a given society, rather than a clash of values?

Write the proposal from this. I need the question stated so it really has a determinate answer, an account of why the relativist and universalist camps as they stand don't settle it, the method (it's conceptual, not empirical, so what do I actually do all day for six months, and why that beats just doing more ethnography), what a good result looks like versus what would count as the project failing, and a short paragraph on why a funder who cares about governance should pay for this.
