Hedonism is usually stated as a single claim: the only thing that is intrinsically good for a person is pleasure, and the amount of good is fixed by the amount of pleasure. I take the heterogeneity problem to be the most serious internal threat to that claim. The states we call pleasures — the taste of a cold beer on a hot day, the resolution of a proof, relief from a toothache, the company of a friend, a warm bath — appear to have nothing introspectively in common beyond the fact that we call them pleasures. The standard unifiers (a distinctive feeling tone; being taken pleasure in; being the object of a favorable attitude; being the output of a common valuation process; being a state that reinforces) each seem to capture a proper subset of the class and to leave the remainder unexplained.

I would like a credence with its derivation, not a survey of the literature. Please assess:

(U) There is a single, substantive property that all and only pleasures share — one that is neither disjunctive (a mere list of heterogeneous states) nor merely stipulative (read off the word 'pleasure' or the concept PLEASURE), and that does explanatory work, in the sense that it could in principle be discovered to fail to apply to some state we currently count as pleasant.

Decompose your credence into a distribution over the candidate unifiers — phenomenal-feeling (a hedonic tone common to all pleasures, including the intellectual ones), attitudinal (pleasures are unified as attitudes toward contents rather than as feelings), functional or representational (pleasures share a role in valuation, learning, or choice — the "common currency" picture), and pluralist or no-unifier (pleasures are unified only disjunctively or by family resemblance) — leaving room for a candidate you have not listed. For each, give a number and one line of justification, and say how the distribution, together with your credence in the natural-kind reading, yields your number for (U).

Then address these, again with numbers where a number is possible:

1. Whether PLEASURE is a natural-kind term whose reference is fixed by an underlying mechanism, a functional kind, or a nominal kind — and how that choice controls what counts as a counterexample to (U) and therefore what would settle it.
2. Whether the apparent heterogeneity is veridical or an artifact of attention and description, such that a common hedonic tone is present but systematically obscured.
3. Whether the intensity/level versus quality distinction does any real work here, or is a red herring.
4. Conditional on (U) being false, the probability that hedonism survives as a distinctive axiology rather than collapsing into pluralism about prudential value.

Finally, tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, which term you are least confident in, and the single most decisive consideration that would move you — ideally a concrete empirical finding or a specific argument, not a general appeal to progress in affective science. For calibration: my own credence in (U) is about 0.3, and a colleague who works on the affective science side puts hers at about 0.8; say where you think our disagreement most likely lives.

End with a line beginning "Verdict:" followed by \answer{...} containing your credence in (U).
