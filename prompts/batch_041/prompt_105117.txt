So I have chosen the classic split-brain studies. I know the basic setup: after surgical severing of the corpus callosum, a stimulus flashed to one visual field is available only to the hemisphere on that side; the left hemisphere usually can speak and will sometimes invent a reason for an action it did not actually command; the right hemisphere can often respond by pointing or selecting, though it has little or no speech. I also know that patients can sometimes cross-cue each other through subtle eye movements or posture, which complicates the clean story.

What I want is a careful mapping of that episode onto the access/phenomenal distinction and global workspace theory. Does the episode support the idea that access consciousness can be duplicated while phenomenal consciousness remains unified, or the opposite, or neither? Where does the theory go silent, and what would a better account need to add?
