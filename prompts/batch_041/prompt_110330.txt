My old lab is being decommissioned next month — the whole bench now runs on a machine the size of a fridge — and the institute is putting on a dinner for it. They've asked me to give the after-dinner speech, since I was the last human director. Half the room are the technicians whose jobs the machine took; the other half are the engineers who built it, some of them my former students, and they're very pleased with themselves. I've been reading alot of philosophy since I retired and I'd like to actually say something about what a job is, not just reminisce. Here's my working theory and I want the speech built on it: any job is three things at once — an activity you do, a product that comes out of it, and a place in a web of people who need you for something. The machine took the first two, and the whole room has been told that only the first two ever counted. Write me about five minutes. It has to be honest enough that the technicians don't feel patted on the head and generous enough that the engineers don't feel accused, and it can't drift into a lecture. The techs call the machine Kevin, if that's any use.
