Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a prioritarian and a relational egalitarian about the levelling-down objection. The two have argued past each other for years: the prioritarian treats the objection as showing that equality has no intrinsic value, while the relational egalitarian holds that it targets a view nobody should have held, because what is valuable is not an equal distribution of welfare but the absence of domination and hierarchy. Produce the document both could sign. Begin with a numbered section of claims both accept: what the objection actually asserts, what it presupposes about the structure of betterness (impersonality, welfarism, the contrast between being better in one respect and better all things considered), and what each side concedes. Then state the residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position, especially over whether the objection has any force against a relational rather than a distributive reading of equality. Then set out the cruxes, which I take to include whether non-domination has value independent of anyone's welfare, whether prioritarianism can account for the badness of domination, and whether the relevant comparison is between distributions or between relations. Treat as central a case I want both parties to address head-on: dismantling a hierarchy of unjust privilege makes the former superiors worse off and leaves no one better off. Is that levelling down, and if so, is it objectionable? For each crux, give an argument or case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
