I've been going back and forth on Quine's stuff about moral values and something keeps bugging me. The gist of his view, as I understand it: moral judgments are a different kind of animal from factual/scientific ones. Science gets checked against observation, ethics doesnt really, so it's not clear there's any sense in which a moral claim is "true" the way a claim in physics is. Fine, whatever. But Quine is also the holism guy — the whole web of belief, nothing immune from revision, everything tangled up with everything else. Those two things seem to pull in opposite directions. If moral commitments are woven into the same web as our scientific ones, then they should be revisable when experience tugs on the web, which means they're answerable to observation after all, just indirectly. But if they're a separate compartment that observation can't touch, then holism has a big exception carved out of it that Quine never really seemed to justify.

So here's what I want: an essay on whether Quine's metaethics is actually consistent with his holism. Set up the strongest version of the tension first — don't strawman it. Then consider the best ways a Quinean could try to wriggle out (maybe moral "values" aren't beliefs at all, maybe holism is about meaning or confirmation rather than about what's revisable in principle, maybe the checkpoint metaphor is doing more work than I'm giving it credit for, or something else entirely). Then actually commit. Tell me whether you think there's a genuine inconsistency here or whether the Quinean has a solid reply, and defend your verdict. Don't end with "well it depends how you read him" — pick a reading and go to bat for it.
