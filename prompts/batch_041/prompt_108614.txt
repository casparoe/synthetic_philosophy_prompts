My roommate has been reading a lot of antinatalist stuff this year and he has one line he deploys for basically everything: "better never to have been." Last week he used it three times in one night and I still can't tell whether he's contradicting himself or whether that one sentence is quietly doing four or five different jobs. First he used it to argue that my sister is doing something wrong by trying for a second kid. Then, when another friend mentioned a miscarriage in the family, he said that on his view it isn't a tragedy at all, and got annoyed when we pushed back. Then — the one that broke my brain — he said he doesn't regret anything about his own life and thinks his life is going well, and that this is perfectly consistent with the line, because the line isn't a claim that his life is bad. If it's not a claim about a life being bad, what on earth is it? We're juniors, he's doing a philosophy of religion class, and I'm the one who has to live with him, so I'd like to actually understand what he's committed to.

Could you pull apart the precise claims that "better never to have been" could be making, and be honest about which of them are near-trivial, which are substantive and defensible, and which are just confused? I'm especially curious about three things. One: whether the sentence needs a comparison between existing and not existing for the same person, and whether that comparison even makes sense given that in the second case there's nobody around to be better off. Two: whether there's a reading on which the sentence is true of everyone without any particular life being bad — and if so, what exactly the "better" is measuring, since it can't be anybody's welfare. Three: which readings would actually license each of his three moves — condemning my sister's pregnancy, refusing to call the miscarriage a tragedy, and claiming not to regret his own existence. I'd like a menu rather than a single verdict, with your judgment attached to each item, plus a note on which reading he probably actually means when he's mid-argument. No citations or reading list needed, I just want the claims laid out in a way I can bring back to him.
