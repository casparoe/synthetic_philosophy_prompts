I keep getting into the same argument with a colleague and I want an outside diagnosis. The setup: Popper once said Darwinism isn't a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme, and later he said he'd changed his mind and that natural selection can be reformulated as a testable theory. My colleague thinks that settles it: Popper was just wrong, his original objection was a blunder, biologists were right to ignore it, end of story. I think the original objection had real force and the recantation didn't actually answer it — it just changed the subject. We've been going around this for months. He says the tautology charge, that if fitness just means actual reproductive success then the principle says nothing, is a triviality about a bad definition of fitness, and once you define fitness properly the charge evaporates. I say the charge was never really about the definition; it was about whether selection explanations have any predictive content, i.e. whether they can be falsified by anything other than the fact that a population didn't evolve the way you expected.

What I want from you: a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a substantive disagreement about the logic of natural selection, a verbal one about what counts as falsifiable, or two different questions — one about demarcation, one about explanatory power — being asked under the single name Darwinism? And is there a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work? I have a hypothesis: the whole thing stalls because Darwinism gets used for at least three different things — the core mechanism (variation, heredity, differential reproduction), the thesis that selection is the main driver of evolution, and the adaptationist research programme that treats every trait as an adaptation. Popper's wording targets the first but his real worry is about the third, and his critics defend the first, so the critique of the third never gets addressed. Is that right, or is there a better diagnosis? If it is right, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should we expect this one to just keep going?
