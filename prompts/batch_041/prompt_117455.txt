Write a deadpan internal FAQ — roughly 600 words, numbered Q&A format — for the staff of a regional health authority that has replaced its cost-benefit analysis with a contractualist procedure: a policy may proceed only if no one could reasonably reject the principle that licenses it. The document should stay entirely in the voice of an earnest bureaucratic guidance memo, and the comedy should come from the framework's genuine collision with aggregation rather than from invented absurdities. The memo must address, at minimum: what an analyst is to do when a measure would save thousands of lives but imposes serious though survivable harm on one identifiable person; whether individual complaints may be added, averaged, or compared with one another at all; how the office tells a reasonable rejection from a merely self-interested one, and who is authorized to make that judgment; and whether numbers may tip a decision when the complaints on each side are of equal weight. Let the strain show through worked examples and increasingly defensive footnotes rather than through any explicit admission that something has gone wrong, and never wink at the reader. A reader who knows the literature should recognize the office as rediscovering the standard objections from the inside and find the satire fair. The target is the practice of running a complaint-based ethics as an administrative procedure, not any living person.
