A philosophy blog I follow summarized the Dennett–Caruso debate as follows: "Both Dennett and Caruso agree that determinism is probably true. Dennett, the compatibilist, argues that free will is compatible with determinism because a person can act on her own reasons even if those reasons are caused; so a wrongdoer can be morally responsible and may deserve punishment. Caruso, the hard incompatibilist, argues that because determinism is probably true, no one has the kind of free will required for basic desert moral responsibility, so punishment should be justified by forward-looking considerations like protection and deterrence rather than desert. The debate thus turns on whether the wrongdoer could have done otherwise under the same past and laws: Dennett says yes, Caruso says no. The practical upshot is that Dennett's view licenses retributive punishment, while Caruso's view supports restorative justice." I suspect this is a caricature in places, but I can't say where.
