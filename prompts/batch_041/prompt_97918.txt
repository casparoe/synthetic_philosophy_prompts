Philosophers who treat the self as a center of narrative gravity hold that a person is not a further entity lying beneath the stream of thought and behavior but a posit — something we attribute to a suitably organized system because its doings can be told as a story. I want your credence in a claim that puts pressure on that picture.

Call the claim Narrative Replacement: if a human being's entire stock of episodic memories were gradually and seamlessly replaced with confabulated but internally coherent autobiographical content, while her skills, temperament, values, and her capacity to narrate her life in the first person were left intact, she would remain the same person in the sense that matters for desert — she could still be justly praised or blamed for actions performed before the replacement, and still be bound by promises she made then.

Give me your credence that Narrative Replacement is true, as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Make the first item your overall credence. Make items two through four the three sub-questions your number most depends on, each with a rough probability and a one-line justification — for instance, whether identity-for-desert tracks narrative continuity or causal-psychological continuity; whether it is the form of a self-narrative or its content that carries the identity; and whether there is a fact of the matter here at all, as opposed to a decision about how to deploy our practices. Make the fifth item the sub-question your number is most sensitive to, together with the evidence or argument that would move it most, and name which of those sub-questions a friend who puts the claim near 0.8 and I, who put it near 0.35, most likely disagree about.
