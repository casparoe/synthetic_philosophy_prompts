Hey, I'm working on a short essay about the Judy Benjamin problem and I'd like your comments. Assume the role of a formal epistemologist who works on updating and conditionals. Before you critique, maybe brainstorm a bit about what the problem is really supposed to show.

Here's my draft/notes:

Judy Benjamin is dropped into a territory divided into Red and Blue, and also into Second Company and Headquarters. She starts with a uniform prior over the four quadrants: each gets 1/4. Then she gets a radio message: 'I can't tell whether you're in Red or Blue, but if you're in Red, then you're in Second Company.' The question is how she should update.

The obvious move is to conditionalize on the material conditional R→S. That makes P(R∧¬S)=0, and renormalizing gives P(R)=1/3. But many people think this is wrong. The message isn't 'the material conditional is true'; it's an indicative conditional, and hearing it shouldn't make her certain that the material conditional holds. Also, the message seems to say nothing about whether she's in Red or Blue—so why should her credence in Red change at all? Some update rules, like minimum relative entropy, give a different answer (I think 3/8, but I'm not sure—check me). Others leave P(R) at 1/2.

My thesis is that the Judy Benjamin problem doesn't show that Bayesian updating is impossible, but it shows that 'updating on a conditional' is underdetermined by the standard norms. The problem is that we have two competing intuitions: (a) you should become certain of the content of the message, and (b) you shouldn't change credences in propositions the message doesn't concern. These can't both be satisfied if the content is a conditional. So the real lesson is that we need a theory of what indicative conditionals contribute to credence, not just a new update rule.

I'm worried my thesis is too deflationary. Does it actually capture the problem, or am I missing something? Also, is it fair to say the message says nothing about Red? One might argue that it does give information about Red indirectly, because it tells you something about the dependence between Red and Second Company. I'd like comments on: (1) accuracy of the setup and numbers, (2) whether the thesis is interesting or just restates the problem, (3) what the strongest objection to my view is, and (4) whether there's a better way to frame the tension.
