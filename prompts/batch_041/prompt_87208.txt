Alright, so here's my situation. I've somehow ended up as the de facto "research strategy" person for a small foundation that's committed to spending real money -- call it enough for three postdocs for two years -- on the sphexishness question. Quick refresher in case you don't have the reference: the classic story is a digger wasp that runs a fixed routine of hauling a paralyzed cricket back to its burrow, stopping to check the burrow first, and if you just nudge the cricket a few inches while she's inside doing the check, she comes back out and re-runs the whole sequence from the top. Move it again, she does it again. Forever. "Sphexishness" has become shorthand for that kind of mechanical script-following, and the genuinely interesting part is the suspicion that humans, other animals, and now LLM-based agents all display degrees of it, which is where it starts touching free will and agency.

What I need from you isn't a survey, it's an agenda. Which open questions in this area actually matter most, and in what order should we fund them? Defend the ordering: for each one, why is it upstream of the others (what breaks downstream if we get it wrong?), how tractable is it for philosophers and theorists rather than people running experiments, and what would move it up or down our list over the next couple of years?

Two things to make this concrete, because I've noticed people talking past each other and I want the agenda to cut through that.

First, I've started distinguishing what I call shallow flexibility from deep flexibility. Shallow flexibility is producing a novel output from a fixed policy -- an LLM writing a sentence it's never written before, a person improvising a route home. Deep flexibility is revising the policy or the goal itself: noticing the routine is failing and doing something else, or deciding the goal was wrong. I suspect alot of the disagreement is people praising shallow flexibility and thinking they've answered a question about deep flexibility, or vice versa. Does that cut in the right place, and what questions does it make sharp?

Second, I keep wanting a cheap diagnostic -- something like a "cricket test": you move the thing the system is supposedly pursuing a few inches off its path and watch whether it re-runs the old routine, adapts, or just stops. What's the best version of a test like that, what would it actually be evidence for, and where would it mislead us (e.g. is it even meaningful for a system whose goals we can't independently identify)?

So: give me the ranked agenda, the reasoning for the order, the criterion you're prioritizing by (importance, tractability, how many other questions hang on it, how neglected it is -- and where those criteria trade off against each other), and tell me which of the questions I'd be tempted to fund is actually a distraction. Also flag anything I've set up wrong.
