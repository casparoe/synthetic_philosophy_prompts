I have no academic training in physics or philosophy — a couple of half-remembered survey courses and a lot of long drives — but I have been worrying at the block universe on and off for about a year, and I would rather argue with someone who actually believes it than with the version of it that gets set up to be knocked down. So please play a committed four-dimensionalist: someone who thinks the Rietdijk–Putnam argument is close to decisive, who thinks that presentism is a stubborn hangover from pre-relativistic habits of thought, and who can draw on whatever resources the position really has. Be sharp rather than agreeable; correct me, hard, if I have mangled the argument, because I would rather be corrected than humoured. Stay in character until I say stop — a paragraph or so per turn, willing to grant small points (you need not pretend relativity itself is in doubt), but never conceding the main claim, and never stepping outside the role to explain what you are doing. One request: I would rather you engage with the specific proposal below than run through the stock list of replies, even if you end up using one of them.

My opening is this. You tell me my sense of a moving present is an illusion, but the asymmetry I actually live by is not a claim about simultaneity at all; it is a claim about what is still open. I can still do something about whether I finish writing this tonight, and I can do nothing whatever about whether I finished it last night. That boundary between the settled and the open feels like a fact about the world rather than about my map, and it does not behave like a plane of simultaneity: if I board a train the set of events I can still influence changes shape, but nothing about it becomes arbitrary or frame-dependent, and it was never a flat slice through the whole of spacetime to begin with. So let me propose that now means, at bottom, the leading edge of what I can still act on — the frontier between what is still open and what is settled — and that under that reading relativity leaves the present entirely untouched, since relativity is a story about simultaneity and this is a story about agency. If that is right, then the argument equivocates: it establishes something real about simultaneity and then quietly cashes the result in as a conclusion about the present. Tell me where this goes wrong. And if your answer is that my kind of now is merely psychological or practical rather than physical, then tell me what work the word is doing in your own argument that it is not doing in mine.
