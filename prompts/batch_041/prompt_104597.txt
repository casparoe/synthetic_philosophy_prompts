Please compare the familiar concept of an echo chamber with a concept I will call an epistemic bunker. An epistemic bunker is a discursive environment in which members are exposed to opposing views, but only in a form pre-selected to be maximally discreditable, so that the encounter with the other side reinforces rather than weakens the group's confidence. In what ways do the two mechanisms differ in their predictions about political polarization and about partisans' beliefs concerning the good faith of their opponents? Which mechanism better explains partisans who consume a great deal of hostile media yet regard the other side as acting in bad faith, and what would each mechanism predict about the effects of exposing such partisans to a sympathetic presentation of the opposing view? Please answer in American English, and do not include citations or a reading list.
