Me and my friend Dana have been arguing about freedom for about a year now and honestly we've both gotten worse at it rather than better, which I take as a sign we're talking past each other. Neither of us has any philosophy training, we've just read a bunch of stuff online and thought about it alot. So what I want from you isn't an essay about who's right. I want the document we should have written together — the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration, the thing we could both actually put our names on.

Here's roughly where we're stuck. Dana thinks freedom is about not being interfered with: nobody blocking you, nobody stopping you doing what you want, no walls, no one saying no. I've ended up thinking it's about not being under anybody's thumb — not being in a position where someone else could do what they like to you, whether or not they actually do it. I'm pretty sure these are called negative liberty and republican freedom, the non-domination one. And I know there's a third thing, the "positive" one, where freedom means being your own master rather than a slave to your own appetites and hang-ups. I think that's a different question entirely, but I want that written down properly too, with both of us saying exactly what we take it to be and whether it's even about the same thing as our fight.

So:

- The agreed claims, numbered. What any account of freedom has to explain. Which cases we agree on the verdict for, and where we agree on the verdict but for different reasons, say so and say the reasons. And anything we should both admit we were getting wrong before this started.
- The disagreements, in a table, each one written the way that side would actually want it written. Not a strawman of my view and not a strawman of hers. If you can't state one of our positions without making it sound dumb, you haven't got it yet.
- The cruxes — the specific sub-questions where the answer would actually move one of us. My guess is one of them is "can you be unfree because of something that isn't a person, or isn't even a possible person?", and another is "is being under someone's arbitrary power just a fancy way of saying there's a decent chance they'll interfere?" But I don't know if those are the real ones. You tell me. For each crux: a case or a bit of argument we'd both accept as bearing on it, plus what each side predicts about it, written down in advance.
- If part of our fight is just about the word "freedom" — two different things that both have a claim on the name — say which part, and say what each of us should call the thing we actually care about.

Cases I want both of us forced to answer, because I think they're where we're really split: (1) someone alone in a valley where a landslide has blocked the only way out, no person involved anywhere. (2) the slave of a genuinely kind master, who will never interfere and whose slaves all report being happy. (3) two people facing exactly the same chance of losing their job, one because a boss can fire her at any moment for any reason, the other because the business is just unstable and the boss has tied his own hands. (4) someone living under a ruler who has never touched her and never will, versus her neighbour across the border living under harsh laws that are completely predictable and fall on everyone equally. (5) someone who has never been blocked, but only because she long ago stopped wanting anything outside what she's allowed — no interference, no threat, nothing she'd even describe as pressure, and she says she's content. (6) and if you can think of a case where Dana's view and mine give the same verdict for reasons that can't be the same, stick it in the agreed section, because we need some of those too.

Don't give me a reading list, and don't tell me who said what first or what the standard objection to each position is — I can't look anything up and I don't care who said it, I want the substance. And don't just tell me we're both right about different things, unless you can say exactly which claim each of us has to give up to get there. Write it so that when I show it to her, she either signs it or can point at the exact line she can't.
