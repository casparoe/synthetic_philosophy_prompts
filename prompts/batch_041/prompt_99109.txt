I'm putting together a short set of in-jokes for a holiday thing our analysis/set-theory group does, and I need one good joke about the axiom of choice. The audience is grad students plus two faculty, so they'll laugh at a choice function reference but they'll roll their eyes if the whole joke is just naming Banach-Tarski and hoping that counts as a punchline. What I want is a joke that actually turns on the content of the axiom — someone who knows the difference between ZF and ZFC should find the punchline funny *because* of the real mathematics, and someone who doesn't know any set theory should at least be able to follow the setup and feel like they're missing something specific rather than something vague.

The flavor I keep circling is the socks-vs-shoes thing: give me infinitely many pairs of shoes and I can just take all the left ones, no axiom needed, because the pairs are distinguishable; give me infinitely many pairs of identical socks and there's no rule that picks one out of each pair, so you need choice just to assert that a set containing one sock from every pair exists at all. That gap — between a thing existing and there being any way to actually say which thing it is — is what I want the punchline to hinge on. Same family of weirdness: choice hands you a well-ordering of the reals but no recipe for producing it, or lets you chop a solid ball into finitely many pieces and reassemble them into two balls the same size as the original, and all of it is existence-without-instructions. If it helps, I'm seeding you a setting: a hotel with infinitely many rooms, every room containing infinitely many pairs of identical socks, and some poor soul who has to make one sock pile out of them. Or a laundromat, or a drawer — whatever, but the socks should be identical, that's the whole point.

Write the joke. Short is fine — a two-hander between two mathematicians at the bar, or a walks-into-a-bar setup, or just a one-liner with a real punch. Don't tack an explanation onto the end; I want to be able to tell it cold. And please don't let the punchline be *because the axiom of choice* — that's the version of this joke I already have, and it isn't funny.
