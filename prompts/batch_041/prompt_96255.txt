Im in a metaphysics seminar and we spent basically the whole week on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and it kind of broke my brain. Heres the thing thats bugging me. You start with two things and a relation between them, say a loves b. But just listing the three of them, a, loves, b, doesnt yet give you a proposition, its just a heap of three items, a list, not something that says anything. So something has to hold them together, a further relation, a tie, whatever you want to call it, that makes the heap into one thing. Except now youve got four items, a, loves, b, and the tie, and by the exact same reasoning thats just a heap too, so you need a tie for the tie, and off it goes forever. What I cant tell is whether the lesson is that the regress is vicious, so building propositions out of parts plus a relation is just doomed, or whether its benign, an infinite regress that hurts nobody because the unity was never a separate thing you had to add in the first place, either because the relation is already self-unifying or because the unity of the proposition is just primitive and you should stop asking what glues it.

Anyway, for the last session I want to close with a poem about this, and I want the form itself to do the work rather than just talk about the regress. Can you write me one, maybe 16 to 20 lines, where the last line of each stanza is the first line of the next stanza, and the poems final line loops back to become the poems very first line, so the whole thing is a closed ring? Then make those repeated lines drift in meaning every time they come back around, the way a, loves and b keep turning into new relata that each demand a fresh tie. If it helps, you could write it in the voice of the tie itself, the thing thats always being asked to show up and unify the pile and never quite arriving.

And I really want the loop to stay ambiguous, because thats honestly where our seminar ended up. Read it one way and the closing line is a genuine unity, the ring holds, the regress was benign all along, the relation was never a separate item you had to bolt on. Read it another way and the closing line is just one more relatum demanding its own tie, and the ring is a treadmill. A friend whos read way more Bradley than me should be able to point at the exact lines doing each job, and someone who's never heard of any of this should still get a real poem out of it, so dont let it turn into a lecture in verse. Oh, and please dont resolve it in the final stanza. I want both readings still live when the thing ends.
