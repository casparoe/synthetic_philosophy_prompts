So I had a long argument with my mate Dan last night about Kavka's toxin puzzle — you know the setup, someone offers you a million pounds if you'll form the intention today to drink a vial of gunk tomorrow that'll have you throwing up for a day, and the catch is the money lands the moment you've genuinely made up your mind, so by tomorrow morning you've got no reason left to actually swallow the stuff. Dan reckons it's a solved relic and that everyone interesting has moved on. I think there's more in it, but we could not agree on which bits of it actually matter.

What I want from you is a prioritised agenda: what are the genuinely open questions hanging off that puzzle, and which would you put at the top if someone had a couple of years to work on them? Not a survey — I want the ordering defended. Is the whole thing really about the nature of intention (that you can't just decide to intend something you'll foresee having no reason to do), or about the gap between having a reason and being moved by it, or about self-prediction and how you forecast your own future behaviour, or about whether there's such a thing as a reason to become a certain sort of person? And which of the puzzle's supposed real-world payoffs carry actual weight — deterrence, resolutions, promises, addiction, the whole rational-irrationality cottage industry — and which are just hand-waving?

Dan also threw in a twist I can't shake. He says swap the money for something you couldn't want at will: say the prize is that a committee will henceforth regard you as the kind of person who can intend against their own interest, and you only get it if the regard is sincere. He claims that version shows the puzzle is really about a regress of higher-order wants and nothing to do with intention at all. Is he right? And does that variant change which questions sit upstream of the others?

Rank them, say what depends on what, how tractable each is with the tools philosophers actually have rather than the ones we wish we had, and what new argument or result over the next couple of years would definately move any of them up or down the list. I'm less fussed about the list itself than about the reasoning for the order. British English please, I'm fussy about that.
