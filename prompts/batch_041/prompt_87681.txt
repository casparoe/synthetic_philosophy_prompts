I sit on the allocation committee of a small animal-welfare foundation, and I have to circulate our written opinion before next week's grant vote. We have $2 million to allocate this cycle, and two proposals are live.

Proposal A, an advocacy campaign to phase out battery cages at a large egg producer, would affect an estimated 40 million laying hens over five years; our analysts put its expected gain at roughly 1.2 million hen-years of suffering averted per $100,000, and they are reasonably confident in that figure.

Proposal B, a research-and-advocacy package aimed at reforming a class of broad-spectrum insecticides, would, on our analysts' estimate, spare from death or debilitation on the order of 10^11 to 10^12 farmed and wild insects per year. Its causal chain is much longer and its success probability lower, but the numbers are enormous.

The committee is deadlocked on how to weigh B against A. Last cycle our chair drafted a working rule to structure the debate. Call it the weighted-expectation rule: score an intervention as P x N x D, where P is the committee's subjective probability that the affected taxa are sentient, N is the number of individuals affected per year, and D is a duration-and-intensity factor normalized to the hen case (so D = 1 for Proposal A); then, uneasy about multiplying three uncertain quantities, the chair proposes taking the square root of the product before comparing interventions.

Three of us have staked out positions. The chair puts P for insects at about 0.05 and believes the square-root damping is what keeps B from running away with the whole budget. One member argues P is nearer 0.001, that anything under 0.01 should simply be rounded to zero, and calls the exercise Pascal's mugging in a lab coat. A third argues that N should be counted per generation rather than per year, since insects turn over so quickly that the annual figure understates the relevant total by an order of magnitude, and that on that basis B dominates everything we will ever fund.

Please draft the opinion I will circulate. I need a concrete recommendation: how to split the $2 million, with any conditions attached; a decision on whether to keep, amend, or drop the square-root damping; and the specific values of P, N, and D you would use, with your reasons. Treat the strongest argument on the losing side seriously. If you fund B, face the charge that the same reasoning obliges us to worry about soil mites and nematodes and to treat every vertebrate intervention as rounding error; if you fund A, face the charge that you have quietly set the probability of insect sentience to zero to avoid an uncomfortable conclusion. Take a definite position rather than laying out the options for us to choose among.
