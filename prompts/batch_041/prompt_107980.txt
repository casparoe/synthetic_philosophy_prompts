My reading group has been circling the dogmatism paradox for months and I want a diagnosis rather than another attempted solution. Heres how we set it up: I know that P. Since P is true, anything that counts against P cant be a true pointer, so any counter-evidence to P is misleading evidence. That looks knowable from the armchair, so by closure I can know, of some piece of counter-evidence I havent even seen yet, that its misleading; and knowing it's misleading seems to entitle me to dismiss it unread. Nobody in the group denies the steps, we just each pick a different exit. Half of us say fine, it's a real consequence of knowledge, and the interesting question is what it tells us about the knowledge you'd actually want to act on -- maybe the usable stuff is a proper part of knowledge, or maybe knowing and being reasonable to rely on come apart. The other half says the argument only goes through on a God's-eye reading of 'misleading', and that once you relativize it to the agent's evidence the closure step either dies or the conclusion turns into something nobody was ever worried about. We also tried importing the old counterexample where a free fact is worth refusing because having it would wreck your later decisions, and it moved nobody, which I take as a bad sign.

My hunch is that the two sides are answering different questions under one word -- one is giving truth-conditions for 'knows', the other norms for inquiry. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a real substantive disagreement (or a hidden formal one, e.g. about closure) underneath? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here -- a distinction, a counterexample, a formal result, or admitting the puzzle is ill-posed?
