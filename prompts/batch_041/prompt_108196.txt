A congenitally blind woman has just received a retinal implant that gives her a crude, flickering, colorless visual signal — enough to resolve large shapes but no fine detail. After a few hours of use she can see well enough to be shown objects. She is shown a cube and a sphere that she has felt and handled many times; she may not touch them or use any other sense. What is your credence that, on the first attempt, she identifies by sight alone which is which — that Molyneux-style transfer of shape from touch to vision works in her case? Give a number and decompose it into: the probability that tactile content is already spatial in a format vision can consume directly, rather than modality-specific and merely correlated with the visual; the probability that, even granting that shared format, the implant's low resolution and instability still preserve enough shape information for identification; and whatever further terms you think belong. For each term give a number and one line of justification, then the combination, then the term your final number is most sensitive to and the single result that would move it most. Also say how much of your uncertainty is not about perception at all but about whether the proposition is well-defined. Write in American English.
