I've been lurking on the rationalist blogs for years (SSC back when, LessWrong, the whole scene) and I keep circling back to pain asymbolia. Quick version, in case you don't have it handy: people with certain brain injuries — usually something involving the insula — will tell you that a stimulus hurts. They can point to where it is, they rate the intensity about like you'd expect, but it just doesn't bother them. No distress, no flinching, some of them seem to find the whole thing mildly amusing. The standard gloss is that this proves pain and the badness of pain are two separate things, that the badness lives in the affective/emotional add-on rather than in the sensation itself, so what makes pain bad isn't the pain.

I want you to make an actual argument here, not hand me a tour of the positions. Here's the case I've been chewing on. Suppose there was a cheap, safe, fully reversible pill — the ow-off switch, basically — that made you pain-asymbolic while you're on it. Nothing about your nerves changes. You still feel the burn on your hand and the ache in your knee, same location, same intensity, you just don't mind any of it. Everyone with chronic pain goes on it. Friend one says: great, we solved pain. Friend two says: no we didn't, we just stopped people from registering a problem that's still sitting there — the knee is still being wrecked, the hand is still burning, and a creature that can't be bothered by its own damage has lost something even if it doesn't miss it.

So give me a definite verdict: in a world where everyone's on the pill, has pain been solved, or has it just been made unregistered? And you can't answer that without committing on what the badness of pain actually consists in — the felt awfulness, the fact that you're being damaged, the fact that it makes you want the thing to stop, something else — plus which of those the asymbolia cases genuinely support and which they're being made to carry. Pick the view you think is true and defend it against the obvious objections, including the one where your view implies the pill is a cure. Don't lay out options and leave the choice to me. And say where your confidence is coming from and what would move you off it — I'd rather see the joints than get a hedge. Skip the reading list, I want the argument.
