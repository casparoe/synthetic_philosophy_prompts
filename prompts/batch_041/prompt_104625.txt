last night my friend and i got into it for like two hours about Avicenna's flying man — the guy who's conjured up out of nothing, hanging in midair, eyes covered, not touching his own limbs, no senses working at all, and is still supposed to be able to affirm that he exists. my friend thinks the whole thing is a cheat, because the way it's set up the guy already has the word "I" and knows how to use it, so of course he's going to affirm himself — take the language away and theres nothing underneath. i'm not so sure, and we never landed it.

anyway we're closing out a small reading group on this next week and i'd rather end with a poem than a recap. so: write me a short poem, 10 to 14 lines, blank verse or loose free verse, in the voice of the person hanging there. here's the part i actually want you to obey — no sensory imagery anywhere. no light, no dark, no sound, no weight, no air moving on his skin, nothing pressing against him, really no body at all, since thats the entire setup. whatever is still speaking once you strip all that out is the poem. and i want the turn, near the end, to be my friend's worry: that the "I" doing the talking might be a soul that doesn't need a body, or might just be grammar with nobody home. don't resolve it — let those two sit next to each other and leave it there. someone who knows the argument should be able to hear our disagreement inside the poem, and someone who doesn't should still get a good poem out of it.

after the poem, tack on a small JSON object so i can file it in the group's notes, with exactly these fields: "no_senses_constraint" (one line on what the poem had to do without), "what_remains" (the one thing the speaker is left holding), "soul_or_grammar" (which way the ending leans, or "open" if it genuinely doesn't), and "tipoff_line" (the single line that would tell a specialist they're reading the flying man).
