In a seminar last week a colleague defended superrationality by asking us to imagine a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma played between two agents that are exact duplicates of one another — the same algorithm, the same information, the same utility function — and then inviting us to treat the situation as though it were a single agent choosing a single action that fixes both players' outcomes. On that picture, she said, defection is not merely imprudent but incoherent, and cooperation is the uniquely rational choice. I want an audit of the analogy rather than a verdict on superrationality itself, so please keep those two questions apart.

Set out the correspondence explicitly. For each element of the single-agent source — the single will, the single payoff to be maximized, the act of choosing, the agent's knowledge that its choice determines the whole outcome — say what is supposed to play that role in the two-player target, and flag any element of the source that has no counterpart at all.

Then say where the mapping breaks. Some pressure points to consider, though you should add others if you see them: the two agents are causally independent and may have divergent interests; sameness of algorithm may be undefined, or defined only relative to a level of description we have not fixed; the common knowledge that both parties reason identically is fragile in a way that one agent's access to its own reasoning is not; and the single-agent case comes with a well-defined procedure for choosing, whereas the two-player case may not. Distinguish the disanalogies that actually bear on the conclusion from those that are merely colorful.

Finally, tell me whether the cooperation verdict survives once the disanalogies are on the table — and if it survives, whether the analogy is doing genuine work or merely decorating a conclusion reached on independent grounds. If a better analogy is available for the point my colleague is trying to make, describe it and say what it buys that hers does not.

Close with a note on what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different assessment of how far the analogy holds, and where your reasoning here is most likely to fail. Please write in American English.
