Ok so I spent 38 years as a geophysicist before I retired, and about two years ago I finally decided to actually read some philosophy instead of just arguing about it at dinner parties. I've been working through Berkeley and honestly I cant tell if I'm being trolled. Anyway, my writing group wants a satire piece from each of us, and this is what I want to do.

The idea: a deadpan campus-news article, Onion style, headline plus maybe 550 words, reporting that the physics department at a mid-sized university has officially converted to Berkeley's idealism. The provost announces it, the department chair gives a perfectly straight-faced statement, the facilities crew can't fix a leaking roof because the leak turns out to be a matter of opinion, that kind of thing. The humor has to come from actually getting Berkeley right, not from a generic "lol nothing is real" — I want a philosopher to read this and think whoever wrote it knows the material. So it should run on the real moves: that to be is to be perceived, the master argument, the bit where God is what keeps the tree in the quad existing when nobody's looking, the distinction between ideas of sense and ideas of imagination, and the fact that this is NOT solipsism, which people always get wrong. The part I find funniest is that the whole thing is supposed to be empirically identical to ordinary realism — you stub your toe just the same — so I want at least one exasperated realist colleague quoted saying exactly that, and a line somewhere about how no experiment could ever tell the difference. Maybe a little sidebar where the campus health-and-safety office tries to update its forms.

Stay deadpan the whole way, no winking at the reader. Don't make it about any real living person, invent the names. And if you can work in why the master argument is supposed to work but also why people think it cheats, even better — I want it to be fair to Berkeley, not just a dunk.

I'm not a philosopher so forgive me if I've got some of this wrong, but I figure the satire lands harder the more technically accurate it is.
