I'm a backend engineer with no real philosophy background (one intro ethics class in college, and all I remember is the trolley thing), and I've fallen into an argument with my cousin that I can't climb out of. She races bikes semi-seriously and has been explaining anti-doping rules to me, and the more I hear the less I understand what the rules are actually about. Here's the practice as I understand it. There's a banned list of substances and methods, and if you test positive you're out — strict liability, I think, meaning it doesn't matter that the stuff got into you via a sketchy supplement you bought at a gym. But an athlete with a genuine medical condition can apply for a therapeutic use exemption and get permission to take something that's otherwise banned: insulin, asthma inhalers, testosterone if your testosterone really is low. Meanwhile a pile of things that are clearly not medicine and clearly make you faster are just fine: sleeping in a tent that fakes altitude, living at altitude, cortisone injections (with paperwork, I think), creatine, and I'm pretty sure caffeine got taken off the list years ago and is now only monitored. What is not fine: taking your own blood out and putting it back in later. It's your blood. There's nothing foreign in it. There's also some ongoing mess about which testosterone range a female athlete is allowed to naturally be in, which seems to assume there's a standard body you're supposed to have and anything above it is cheating. And out of curiosity I looked up whether militaries care about enhancement, and my hazy impression is that they've been happy to hand out stimulants to keep pilots awake on long missions while court-martialing people for steroids — I'm probably misremembering the details, but the double standard is what stuck. My own health insurance is also weird here: it pays for my statins even though I don't have heart disease yet and it pays for my flu shot, and both of those are things done to a healthy person to make them better off, which doesn't sound like either treatment or enhancement to me.

So here's the puzzle. The line can't be therapy versus enhancement, because the altitude tent is pure enhancement and it's legal, while testosterone for someone who is actually deficient is therapy and it's restricted or refused. It can't be natural versus artificial, because the tent is artificial and legal, and my own blood is natural and illegal. It can't just be about cheating, because nobody disqualifies you for being born with a freak mutation that does what the drugs do. And it can't be about health, because a lot of this stuff is bad for you and a lot of it isn't. So what is the practice actually committed to?

Tell me which rival views the rulebook quietly rules out, and be careful to separate what the rules strictly require from what they merely happen to fit.
