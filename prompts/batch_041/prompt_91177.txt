I am assembling a short research proposal for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship and would like help developing a half-formed idea into a fundable project. The subject is Gödel's argument, drawn from his rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations, against the objectivity of time. As I reconstruct it, the argument runs roughly as follows: if time is objective, there must be an objective lapse or becoming, and this requires a global ordering of events into successive layers of presentness, that is, a global time function that increases along every possible history; but in general relativity the existence of such an ordering is contingent on which solution of the field equations describes the actual world, and Gödel's rotating universe is a solution containing closed timelike curves—histories that return to their own past—and therefore admitting no global time function; since a feature that holds only contingently cannot belong to the nature of time, time is not objective, a conclusion Gödel took to favour an idealist, Parmenidean conception of the world.

My half-formed suspicion is that the received reconstruction of this argument leans too heavily on a feature of one particular model—the absence of a global time function in the rotating universe—when the argument's real pressure comes from what the theory leaves undetermined across its models, namely whether there is a global present at all, and that these two strands come apart in ways that matter for whether the argument survives contact with the physics. For example, it seems to matter whether the evidence were to settle the question of cosmic rotation one way or the other, and whether the argument is really about what the theory does or does not encode about becoming rather than about the features of any single solution.

Please turn this into a proposal of roughly two pages: a precise statement of the research question, framed so that it has a determinate answer; an account of why the existing positions leave that question open, described by position and argument rather than by citation, since I will supply the references myself; the approach I would take, what I would actually do at each stage, and why this approach rather than the obvious alternatives; the possible conclusions and the considerations that would decide among them; what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a realistic assessment of scope for one year, including an honest account of where the project could become too large. Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations of any kind.
