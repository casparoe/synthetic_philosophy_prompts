Quick bit of context: I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training past a couple of podcasts and one very confusing ethics elective in college, and for the last two years I've been putting together a parody newsletter that goes out to about forty friends — fake book reviews, fake corporate memos, that kind of thing. This months issue is supposed to be about a view I keep bumping into online that people sometimes call tranquilism, and I want to get it right before I make fun of it. The claim, as best I can tell, is that the only thing good in itself is the absence of craving and the absence of suffering — pleasure, love, achievement, curiosity, all of that is only worth anything insofar as it removes craving or suffering — and that satisfying a desire doesn't add anything, it just gets you back to neutral by clearing out a bad state. So the best life is one with no cravings in it at all, and the best world is one where nobody craves anything. I honestly can't tell if this is the most peaceful idea I've ever heard or a horror movie, and I think that's exactly why it'd make a good piece.

So here's what I'm after: a deadpan newspaper advice column, the kind with a headline, a columnist byline, and four or five reader letters with his replies, somewhere around 650-750 words. Invent the columnist and all the letter writers, and make him a sincere, gentle, fully committed tranquilist who never breaks character — he can only ever appeal to the absence of craving and suffering, so when a reader says she'd rather keep the ache of wanting her kid to do well in school than be serene while he flunks out, or another says he wants to stop wanting things and can't tell whether that wanting is itself a craving, or a third asks what's even the point of him writing the column if he doesn't want anything, he has to answer consistently instead of dodging. The comedy should come out of the real features of the view rather than out of making him dumb or cruel: satisfying a craving only returning you to baseline, the fact that he seems to need to crave a world without craving, whether 'no craving' is a state you can be in or even notice that your in, whether a world of no craving is distinguishable from a world of contented zombies, and whether his advice to a genuinely miserable reader quietly amounts to stop caring. If a reader throws one of those at him he should have an answer — the answers don't have to convince me, and I'd actually love at least one where I can see the problem a beat before he does without him ever seeing it. Keep it readable for someone who's never heard the word tranquilism, don't have him quote or cite any real books or scriptures (invented readers only), and no winking at the camera: if it's funny, it should be funny because the position is being taken dead seriously.
