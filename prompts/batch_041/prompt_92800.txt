I cover food and animal welfare, and there's an argument among animal advocates I keep running into that never seems to go anywhere, and I want to understand whats actually going on in it rather than referee it. Roughly: one camp says that if you care about animals, a pasture-raised beef burger can be kinder than a bowl of rice, because harvesting crops kills a huge number of field animals -- mice, birds, insects -- per calorie, while a cow on grass costs fewer of those deaths per calorie (and if you're eating meat anyway, one cow feeds way more meals than one chicken). Another camp says thats nuts: the field-death numbers are basically guesses, nobody knows how a mouse's suffering compares to a cow's, and the whole thing is a distraction from the billions of chickens we kill. A third group says the real story is wild animal suffering, which dwarfs everything and which we can't even estimate, so the whole comparison is cluelessness dressed up as arithmetic. Smart, well-meaning people have been going around this loop for years and nobody moves. So what kind of disagreement is this? An empirical fight over numbers we could in principle get, a disagreement about which animals count and how to trade their suffering off, or a disagreement about what it even means to act well when you can't know the consequences? And is there anything -- a study, a distinction, a different way of posing the question -- that would actually move it, or is it stuck for good reasons? American English, please.
