Here's the stall, as concretely as I can put it. On one side are the classicists, roughly in the Fodor tradition: they say thought has to be compositional and systematic — you can think that John loves Mary only if you can think that Mary loves John, and you can entertain indefinitely many novel thoughts — and that the only way to explain this is to posit a representational medium with a combinatorial syntax and semantics, a language-like format. On the other side are the connectionists, who say distributed representations can capture the same regularities, or that systematicity is a graded, emergent, context-sensitive matter rather than a hard constraint, or that the classicists are demanding the explanation at the wrong level of description altogether. And there's a parallel fight I keep being told is the "same" debate — the old imagery dispute between the people who say mental images are depictive, picture-like, and the people who say they're really propositional underneath.

What bugs me is that both sides seem to agree on the phenomena. Nobody disputes that we're systematic or productive. Nobody disputes that the brain is a connectionist mess at the level of neurons. They just keep sliding past each other, and alot of years in, neither has moved. Now everyone in my lab says the whole thing is obviously settled — by deep learning, or by LLMs, depending on who's talking — but I honestly can't tell whether that's the same disagreement coming back or a different one wearing its clothes.

My working hypothesis (and I want you to push on it, not just agree) is that it's a levels-of-explanation confusion: the classicists are asking a question at the computational/representational level and the connectionists keep answering at the implementational level, so they aren't even disagreeing. But maybe that's too tidy. Maybe it's verbal — maybe "language" is doing metaphorical work and once you say properly what "language-like" means, the two views collapse into one.
