Give an example of a concrete decision problem in which an embedded agent — an agent that is itself a proper part of the world it reasons about — must include a representation of its own decision procedure in the model it uses to decide, and in which the action it settles on depends on how that self-representation is drawn. Specify the environment, the available actions, and the precise step at which the self-reference becomes load-bearing. Then address whether two agents that share the same environment, evidence, and preferences but represent themselves differently are genuinely at odds about what they ought to do, or merely differ in what each can establish about its own future behavior, and defend your answer.
