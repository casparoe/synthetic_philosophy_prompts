I chair the Independent Franchise Commission that our city council convened to fix the membership of a citizens' assembly. The assembly is to make binding recommendations on one sharply contested question: whether to build a flood barrier protecting the city's low-lying eastern districts at the cost of permanently inundating farmland in the neighbouring town of Harrowfield, which lies downstream, would bear the loss, and has no vote in our municipal elections. The authorising ordinance says only that the assembly must "represent the community affected by the decision" and left us to define that phrase. The council meets in three weeks and expects a written opinion from this commission containing a concrete recommendation it can adopt.

We must settle, at minimum, five candidate groups: long-term residents who lack citizenship, including several thousand people on temporary protection permits; Harrowfield residents; our own citizens who live abroad but own property in the eastern districts; seasonal agricultural workers present for part of each year; and the not-yet-born residents of the eastern districts, whom one commissioner keeps invoking. Two rival tests have been proposed — include everyone whose interests the decision affects, or include everyone whom the decision's coercive enforcement would bind. The first seems to let the boundary of the demos expand without limit; the second seems to leave Harrowfield, which is plainly harmed, on the outside. A third commissioner wants to sidestep the dispute with a two-tier scheme, granting "deliberative membership" — a seat and a voice but no vote — to those we cannot justify enfranchising, and "decisional membership," with a vote, to those we can.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate. It should state our recommended inclusion rule, specify for each of the five groups whether it receives a voice, a vote, or neither, describe the mechanism by which the rule would take effect before the assembly convenes, and give an honest account of the strongest objection to the recommendation — namely, that any boundary we draw is merely the self-interested choice of the present majority and that we should therefore decline to fix one at all. Do not end on a non-committal note; the council needs a decision it can act on. Keep it under 800 words.
