You are a philosopher of mind and cognitive scientist with a specialization in evolutionary epistemology and the comparative study of cognition. I am building a case-study module on Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds, and I want you to analyze a real episode through it: the 2016 match in Seoul between Lee Sedol and AlphaGo, the Go-playing system developed by DeepMind. In Dennett's framework, cognitive competence comes in four grades. Darwinian creatures are shaped by natural selection over evolutionary time and do not learn within their lifetime. Skinnerian creatures can learn by operant conditioning, generating behaviors and retaining those that are reinforced. Popperian creatures can run internal simulations of possible actions and their consequences, so that hypotheses can be tested and discarded without being acted out in the world. Gregorian creatures are Popperian creatures whose cognitive reach is extended by culturally transmitted thinking tools — language, notation, instruments, and other cognitive technologies — a grade named after the psychologist Richard Gregory. The match is a useful test case because AlphaGo is not a biological organism, yet it appears to occupy several of these grades at once: it was trained on human games, it improved through self-play, it searched through possible future board positions, and it is itself a cultural artifact built with human tools. I want a disciplined mapping, not a loose analogy.

First, brainstorm the relevant features of the episode: what AlphaGo's architecture was, how it was trained, what happened during the match, and how Lee Sedol's own cognition was engaged. Then map the episode onto the four grades. Which aspects of AlphaGo's performance are Darwinian, which are Skinnerian, which are Popperian, and which are Gregorian? Be specific about the mechanisms: for instance, what plays the role of variation and selection, what plays the role of reinforcement, what plays the role of internal simulation, and what plays the role of a thinking tool. Where does the mapping strain? Is AlphaGo a Popperian creature in Dennett's sense, or does it merely mimic the outputs of one without the internal structure? Does the fact that AlphaGo is a cultural artifact make it a Gregorian creature, or is it better described as a Gregorian tool used by human creatures? Finally, turn the lens around: what does this episode show about Dennett's framework? Does it confirm the hierarchy as a general account of cognitive grades, or does it reveal a gap — for example, between simulation and mere pattern completion, or between individual and collective cognition? Say where the framework is silent rather than forcing it to speak. Write in American English, and aim for a careful, well-organized analysis of roughly 1,000 to 1,500 words.
