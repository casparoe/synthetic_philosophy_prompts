I am assembling materials for a graduate seminar on the afterlife of limitative results, and I want to open one session with a joke. Please write it.

The joke should concern the use and misuse of Gödelian incompleteness outside mathematics, and it must be funny to someone who actually knows the theorems — the humor should turn on the real content rather than on a vague sense that "some things cannot be known." Invent the setting yourself: a consultancy that certifies organizations as "epistemically complete," a foundation that funds attempts to outrun incompleteness by postulating fresh axioms, a management guru who markets incompleteness as an alibi, or something of the kind.

The punchline should rest on one specific and correctly stated feature of the mathematics. Suitable candidates include: that incompleteness attaches to effectively axiomatized, consistent theories with sufficient arithmetic, not to rational enquiry as such; that the undecidable sentence is true in the standard model whenever the theory is consistent, so it is not a blank that further axioms will simply fill; that adding the sentence as an axiom merely produces a new undecidable sentence, so there is no final, complete system; that the second theorem concerns a theory's inability to certify its own consistency once consistency is expressed through a suitable provability predicate; that the fact that a theory proving "if P is provable then P" already proves P forecloses a tempting route to self-trust; or that the theorems do not by themselves establish that the human mind transcends every formal system.

Choose one of these, get the mathematics right, and let a character either exploit it fallaciously or be undone by it. Keep it to roughly two hundred words, and include no citations or reading list.
