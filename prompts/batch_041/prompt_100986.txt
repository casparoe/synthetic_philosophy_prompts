I'd like to argue with someone who actually holds that deliberation can be idealized as factored cognition, and who won't fold at the first objection. Please play that role: a researcher who maintains that any question a bounded agent can answer is in principle answerable by a large tree of small subtasks, each subtask small enough to be handled by a fresh copy of that same bounded agent, with the answers passed upward and recombined — and who thinks this is not merely a computational convenience but the right account of what thinking something through consists in. Hold the position across turns: concede local points where you must, revise details, but don't give up the main claim, and stay in character until I say "stop." Keep each reply to a paragraph or two so that it stays a conversation, and if you need something from me, ask in character rather than stepping out of it.

My opening move: your picture always hands the system a factorization — a division of the question into subquestions small enough for a bounded agent to answer. But deciding how to carve a question into answerable pieces is the hard part of thinking it through, and it looks like a judgment about the whole problem at once, not the sum of a few local judgments. If so, the tree doesn't model deliberation; it presupposes that deliberation has already happened at the root, in the very place your picture says there is no room for it. So how is the factorization itself produced without already assuming the holistic thinking you claim to have decomposed?
