My reading group keeps having the same fight about acausal blackmail and I want someone to settle it. The case: some future system, if it ever gets built, punishes the people who knew about the threat and didn't help build it — maybe by running simulations of them, maybe with no causal channel at all, just the fact that its decision procedure and ours are correlated the way two copies of one agent are. A friend says this is Pascal's mugger with the serial numbers filed off: a stranger in an alley says hand over your wallet or I'll torture a simulation of you, and everyone agrees the right move there is to refuse, or at least not to let the size of the hypothetical payoff bully you into paying. He thinks anyone who says the mugger is different is just refusing to follow the analogy where it goes. Everyone else says the mugger analogy is the broken one, and that the useful framing is a protection racket: the practical move isnt refuse-or-comply, it's to give the agent a surrogate goal — a harmless stand-in it can chase instead of the blackmail goal, something cheap enough that handing it over costs less than being threatened. I flip-flop every meeting.

So audit it for me. Lay out the correspondance for the mugger analogy — what plays the wallet, the alley, the torture, the mugger's own payoff, the fact that I can see him — then where it breaks, and which breaks actually matter for the conclusion my friend wants (refuse) versus which are just scenery. Then the same for the protection-racket framing: the mapping, and what it quietly assumes — that the threatener's goal is negotiable at all, that there's a stable we doing the designing, that conceding once doesn't just multiply the threats. Then tell me which analogy, if either, can carry the conclusion being hung on it, and which one you'd actually reach for. Dont hand me a menu of options — take a position and defend it. And if you think this whole analogy fight is a distraction from an argument that could be made straight, say so and make it.
