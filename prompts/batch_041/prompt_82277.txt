I'm taking an epistemology seminar this term and we have to write an essay on Moore's proof of an external world, and the prof said something in lecture that I genuinely can't stop chewing on. The basic shape of the proof I get: Moore holds up his hands, says here's one and here's another, so an external world exists, and the punchline is that the premises are more certain than anything the sceptic has to throw against them, so the burden shifts and Moore wins by default. Standard objection: it begs the question. Fine, whatever, I've seen that take a hundred times.

The wrench was this. The prof asked what actually stops anyone from running the same play. He gave us the flat-earther version — the horizon looks flat to me right now, and if the earth were a ball I couldn't be seeing this, so the earth is flat — and a conspiracy version, where some guy proves the moon landing happened by citing a memory he couldn't have if it were faked. Structurally those are the same move as Moore. He also pointed out you can get two people Moore-ing at each other, each insisting their own starting point is the one that's certain, and then the burden-shifting does no work at all, it just stalemates.

So here's what I want the essay to do. Figure out whats actually doing the work in Moore's proof, and whether it's enough to make it a real proof — or at least a legitimate anti-sceptical move even if it's not a proof in the strict sense. Is the magic ingredient the special certainty of the premises, the fact that denying them costs the sceptic more than it costs Moore, the idea that some things just arent the sort of thing you prove (hinges, or whatever term you prefer), or something I havent thought of? Then the hard part: does that ingredient actually disqualify the flat-earther, or do Moore and the crank turn out to be in the same boat, with the only difference being whose premise the community happens to back? Reach a verdict — I don't want a both-sides-have-a-point ending. Tell me whether the proof works and what separates it from the abuse cases, and if nothing does, say so and tell me what that does to the whole Moorean tradition.
