You are a political philosopher with a strong background in conceptual analysis and in the empirical literature on authoritarian governance and political legitimacy. I want help pinning down a pattern I keep encountering, and a bare name will not satisfy me -- what I need is the carving.

Here is the pattern. In arguments over whether a government that has delivered rising incomes and public order is thereby justified, the government's defender does two things that seem to me in tension. Challenged on a specific cost -- a jailed journalist, a banned book, a suppressed minority -- the defender does not deny the cost. Instead they reach for some version of "compared to what?", or "you can't make an omelette without breaking eggs", and point to the prosperity and the absence of disorder. So far this looks like an ordinary consequentialist defence. But when I ask what cost, or what failure of the results, would be enough to make the government unjustified, the answer stops being empirical: I am told we cannot know what would have happened otherwise, that the alternative was chaos, that stability is fragile. The standard is result-based when the results are good and unfalsifiable when they are bad, and the counterfactual baseline -- the chaos we are supposedly being spared -- is supplied by the government's own account rather than measured.

Does this exact asymmetry have an established name and literature? Begin by brainstorming the candidate concepts and distinctions that might apply, then say which fits best and where each one falls short. I want at least three things separated that I suspect are being run together: the general claim that legitimacy can rest on outcomes rather than procedures; the move of comparing an actual state of affairs to a stipulated worse one; and the specific asymmetry by which one speaker switches between an empirical standard and an unfalsifiable one depending on which way the evidence points. If nothing names the third, coin a term, define it precisely, distinguish it from the nearest existing concepts, and give me a borderline case that tests the definition. Reason carefully and set out your reasoning before your verdict.
