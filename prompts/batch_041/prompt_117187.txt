Turing's 1950 Mind paper has that strange stretch where he takes extrasensory perception seriously — he argues the statistical evidence for telepathy is stronger than most people assume, and he worries a telepath could wreck the imitation game, so he sketches running the whole thing inside a shielded room. Now jump forward: psychology's replication crisis, preregistration, meta-analyses, the file-drawer problem, and parapsychology is filed under pseudoscience. Meanwhile the nearest modern analogue to his worry is benchmark contamination — a model that seems to know the answer without doing any reasoning, so the eval stops testing what it claims to test. If Turing were around today, what would he say about (a) whether his shielded-room proposal was methodologically sound at the time and how he'd redesign it now, (b) whether the replication crisis would actually move his credence in ESP, or whether he'd insist the crisis is a different kind of problem from the one he was pointing at, and (c) whether he'd accept the analogy between telepathy-in-the-imitation-game and contamination-in-LLM-evals, or whether the two cases come apart somewhere important. I want specifics about where he'd dig in and what would actually make him update, not just a shrug about him being a man of his time.
