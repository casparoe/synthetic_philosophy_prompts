I'm putting together a writup on AI safety via debate and I want a dialogue I can drop in as an illustration, so here's the setup I'd like you to use.

The standard story for debate: two AI systems argue in front of a human judge who can't verify the underlying math themselves, and honesty is supposed to win out because if you lie, your opponent gets to point at the exact step where you lied. The standard worry is obfuscated arguments: you make a claim that's actually false, but the falsity only comes out after a chain of reasoning much longer than the debate allows, so the judge never gets to see the flaw.

Now the twist I want the dialogue to be about. Imagine we bolt one extra rule onto the protocol. Every claim a debater makes has to come with a verifier, meaning a small deterministic program, written by that debater, that the judge can run in a few seconds on any candidate objection and that prints accept or reject. So the judge never has to follow an argument; they just run whichever verifier the speaker handed them, on whatever the opponent hands back. It feels like this should kill obfuscation, because no claim gets to stand unless the speaker can also supply a short procedure that checks it.

I want two characters, call them R and S, who both know this area cold and are both sharp. R designed the protocol and thinks the verifier rule basically closes off obfuscation. S thinks it just relocates the problem. Neither one is a dunce, and neither should be reciting a slogan. They should engage each other's actual moves, concede where the other lands a real hit, and press where they think the other is hand-waving. I'd rather S's case rest on something structural about the protocol than on the cheap point that verifiers can have bugs.

Let it run maybe eight to twelve exchanges, and don't resolve it too cleanly. If one of them gets the last word, the other should have made them earn it.
