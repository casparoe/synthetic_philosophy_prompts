Write a mock job advertisement, in the deadpan register of a municipal human-resources posting, for a newly created post at a city's Office of Rent Stabilization and Wage Standards — an agency that administers a rent ceiling over the covered housing stock and a wage floor for covered employers. The posting should run to roughly 550 words and carry the usual furniture: a title line, a short mission statement, a list of duties, required and preferred qualifications, terms and review of appointment, and a closing equal-opportunity line.

Keep the agency's stated rationale straight rather than silly. It exists because sitting tenants face displacement they cannot easily absorb, and because the local labour market is not obviously competitive; those premises are to be written as the office sincerely believes them. The comedy should come from the operational demands that follow — each individually reasonable, jointly impossible: certifying that the ceiling has not thinned the quantity or quality of covered housing; deciding what counts as a comparable unit when judging whether a rent is excessive; ruling on which capital improvements may be passed through to tenants and which are merely deferred maintenance; operating vacancy decontrol, formula-based annual increases, and landlord hardship appeals without ever conceding that the ceiling bites; drawing the boundary of the wage floor and explaining why the exclusions cluster where they do; and anticipating the substitutes that landlords and employers will find for prices they are no longer permitted to charge. The two mandates must be made to sit together in one job description as settled common sense: on the wage side, the office is correcting a market distorted by employers' buying power, and on the rent side, it is correcting a market distorted in the other direction, with no sign that it has noticed it needs a different theory of the same market for each policy.

The advertisement must never name a cost as a cost, and somewhere in its fine print it should acknowledge that the office cannot observe what the city would have looked like without the ordinance, so its statutory duty to report on the policy's effects will be discharged by other means. A specialist should recognise the mechanisms at work; a non-specialist should still get the joke.
