Suppose a scientific body were to adopt what I shall call the multiplicity rule: whenever a result is reached independently by two or more researchers, no one is named as its discoverer, and the result is referred to descriptively rather than by an eponym. Taking the calculus dispute between Newton and Leibniz and the Darwin–Wallace case as the paradigm instances of independent discovery, set out the most important implications of the multiplicity rule — for how the prospect of credit motivates research, for how priority disputes would be adjudicated, and for whether it matters at all who got there first. Be specific about which incentives the rule would strengthen or weaken, including the incentive to develop and defend a result rather than merely to announce it. Answer in no more than 300 words, and open with the single most important implication.
