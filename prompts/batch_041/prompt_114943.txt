The paper is on Sen's liberal paradox — the result where minimal liberalism and weak Pareto can't both hold. Heres my line. The paradox only bites if you assume a person's ranking of the two alternatives in their protected personal sphere is fixed independently of the social choice procedure — that my preference over (I read Ulysses tonight / I sleep in tonight) is the same whether I'm the one deciding or somebody else is. I argue real preferences over the personal sphere are procedure-sensitive: my ranking of those two can flip depending on who gets to decide, because part of what I want is just to be the one deciding. Once you index the personal sphere to the procedure, the conflict between minimal liberalism and weak Pareto dissolves — the two conditions were ranging over different preference objects and talking past each other. I have a minimal formal setup for this, like two pages, so it isn't just hand-waving.
