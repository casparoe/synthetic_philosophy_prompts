I teach a grad seminar on conditionals and we spend the middle chunk of the term grinding through Lewis's triviality results — the proofs that you can't hold onto both "the probability of a conditional is the corresponding conditional probability" and "conditionals are propositions with truth values." My students have been pestering me for weeks to open the last meeting with a joke (I promised one back in September, and a colleague keeps telling me the seminar needs more laughs and fewer proof-by-cases), so I'm finally cashing that in. Write me the joke.

Here's the seed I want it to turn on, because I am thoroughly sick of the "if pigs could fly" chestnuts. Take a conditional with an impossible antecedent — "if two plus two is five, then I'm the Queen of Sheba." Read as a truth-valued conditional it comes out vacuously true, no drama, the logician nods. Read as a conditional probability it's P(Queen of Sheba | two plus two is five), which is 0/0 — not true, not false, just undefined; the sentence doesn't get a probability at all. Same sentence, two readings, one says "true" and the other shrugs. The triviality proofs are basically the demonstration that you cant have it both ways just by squinting, and that gap is where I want the punchline to live. Make it something a room of grad students who've just spent three weeks on this would actually laugh at — the punchline turning on the actual content, not a generic "logicians are so pedantic" gag. Dialogue, one-liner, little shaggy dog, whatever works. And give me one sentence at the end telling me what the joke is doing, so I can tell whether it's the right one before I stand up there and embarrass myself.
