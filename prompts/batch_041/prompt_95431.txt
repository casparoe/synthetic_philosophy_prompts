I sit in on a machine-learning lab's alignment reading group and would like a diagnosis of their impasse rather than a survey of positions. The discussion loops because the members cannot agree on what is even being asked. One insists alignment is an engineering problem -- build the system so it does what we want, and the rest is detail; another that it is a problem in political philosophy about whose values get encoded and by what right; a third that it is a coordination problem among labs racing one another. A colleague offers a hypothesis: that "alignment" hides at least three separable questions -- what a system is supposed to do and how that is fixed (specification); whether training reliably yields a system that does it (optimization); and who is entitled to settle the first question (authority) -- and that most apparent disagreement dissolves once these are pulled apart, each party having answered a different question under one name, leaving little substantive dispute.

Diagnose this rather than adjudicate it. Is alignment one problem or several? For each question, is the disagreement it houses substantive or verbal, and if substantive, does it rest on empirical facts, on standards of evidence, on which cases count as data, or on starting intuitions that neither side can argue for? Where the decomposition is too tidy -- where the questions cannot be cleanly separated, or where a normative premise is smuggled in to make the separation look neutral -- say so and say why. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, an empirical result, a formal theorem, a change of question -- or an argument that the debate is structured so that none should be expected.
