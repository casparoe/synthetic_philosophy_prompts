so two people in my decision-theory reading group got into it last week about whether an agent who doesnt maximize expected utility is being irrational or just has different preferences, and i've been chewing on it since. calling them Dev and Mira.

Dev's opening move: take a preference pattern like A>B, B>C, C>A. if thats your ranking, i can pump you. i offer to trade you C for B if you pay me $2 — you take it, B beats C. then B for A, another $2 — you take it, A beats B. then A for C, another $2 — you take it, C beats A. you're back holding C, exactly what you started with, only now you're $6 lighter. Dev says that isnt a cute trick, it's a proof that the preferences are incoherent: an agent who can be walked around a circle like that at their own expense doesnt have a coherent thing they want. so 'maximize expected utility' isnt some extra demand imposed from outside, it's just what it means to have consistent preferences at all.

Mira: the pump only runs if the person keeps saying yes, and nobody has to keep saying yes. they spot the pattern and stop, and then the story just ends. she adds that the whole thing quietly assumes your preferences are frozen across the whole episode and that you judge each trade in isolation, and that an agent who can see the trap coming would price it in or refuse. and she says 'worse off by their own lights' is doing something shady: if your preferences really do cycle, theres no single ranking on which you've ended up lower, so Dev is sneaking in the very consistency he claims to be deriving.

Dev: if the agent 'just stops,' then the agent isnt following the ranking anymore, theyve swapped the preference ordering for a policy, which is allowed, but then dont also call it a rational agent who has intransitive preferences. and being exposed to a sure loss is precisely the mark of incoherence, so 'they could choose to stop' just changes the subject.

Mira: you can be exploitable and still be perfectly coherent about what you care about. someone who'd pay any amount to save their kid is exploitable by anyone who knows that, and it doesnt make their values incoherent, it makes them manipulable, and those arent the same thing. Dev: when the exploit is a guaranteed loss in your own currency, exploitability just is the formal face of incoherence.

Then it slid into the representation-theorem stuff. Dev: the results show that if your preferences satisfy the axioms, you can be represented as maximizing expected utility — so EU-maximization isnt an extra assumption, it's just the content of consistency. Mira: thats backwards, a representation theorem shows the axioms and the EU-representation are equivalent, it doesnt show the axioms are rationally required. 'can be represented as if maximizing' isnt 'must maximize.' Dev said thats a technicality and Mira said the technicality is the entire argument.

They turned to me to settle it and i honestly couldnt. can you go through it move by move — Dev's pump, Mira's 'they can just stop,' her frozen-preferences and 'worse off by their own lights' objection, Dev's policy reply, the exploitability-vs-incoherence exchange, and the representation-theorem bit — and say whos right on each one and why? then the big picture: if Dev is right, what does the pump actually prove, and does it get you all the way to expected utility maximization or only to some weaker consistency? if Mira is right, what would a real coherence argument have to look like instead? and is any part of this just a fight over words?
