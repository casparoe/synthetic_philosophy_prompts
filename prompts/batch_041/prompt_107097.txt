I am drafting the discounting section of an internal guidance note and want the ordering of reasons to be one I can defend rather than borrow. The question is whether a positive pure rate of time preference is justified — that is, whether a fixed increment of welfare should count for less merely because it occurs later, with the size of the increment, the number of people affected, and the probability of its occurring all held constant. Rank the following candidate justifications from most to least successful at establishing a positive pure rate on its own, and give a sentence or two of reasoning for each placement.

(U) Uncertainty: the further ahead a benefit lies, the greater the chance that no one will be there to receive it.

(G) Growth / opportunity cost: resources not spent now can be invested and yield more later.

(R) Reciprocity: our strongest claims of justice run to those who can reciprocate, and future people cannot.

(S) Spatial analogy: just as it is permissible to care less about strangers who are far away, it is permissible to care less about people who are far in the future.

(T) Time-indexed preference: a rational agent's preferences are indexed to times, and consistency requires ranking a present benefit above an equal future one.

(I) Identity: future beneficiaries are not the same people as we are, and the interests of determinate present people may be weighted above those of indeterminate future people.

Some of these arguably do not concern pure time preference at all; rank all six regardless, and say plainly which ones you think fail to support a pure rate as such. Do not hedge: give one definite ordering and defend it.

End with a final line of the form \answer{...} enclosing your ordering as a chain of the labels, most to least defensible.
