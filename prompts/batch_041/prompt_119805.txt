Write a poem on the problem of logical omniscience, in terza rima, about thirty lines, spoken by an agent who is stipulated to satisfy the standard closure conditions on knowledge. Let the form carry the argument: in terza rima each tercet takes up the rhyme the previous tercet left hanging and leaves a new one hanging in its turn, so the poem is forever paying off a debt incurred earlier and never simply adds something from outside; that is the shape of a knowledge set that already contains every consequence of what it contains. Let the speaker come to notice this about herself as she speaks — she knows the disjunctions her one fact generates, she knows the tautologies she has never had occasion to formulate, she cannot find a consequence that is not already hers — and let her notice that the chain of inferences has no natural stopping place, so the poem's ending is an arbitrary cut rather than a conclusion. Then have her reach for the usual repair, an awareness condition on inference, and let the rhyme scheme break at the moment she does: a line that does not pick up the rhyme it owes, or a closing rhyme left dangling, so the reader feels the chain stop rather than merely being told it stopped. Give one concrete case of knowledge breeding for free — something mundane entailing a disjunction no one would say she "knows" — and end without resolving the matter: the unclosed agent is incoherent, the aware agent knows far less than we wanted her to, and both should still be standing at the last line. Accurate enough that someone working on epistemic logic recognizes the problem rather than merely hearing it named; no names, dates, or citations.
