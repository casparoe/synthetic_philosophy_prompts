ok this is gonna sound dumb but ive been reading about the sphex wasp and i cant shake a worry. you know the one — it paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the burrow by the antennae, ducks inside to do its little inspection, and if you slide the cricket a couple inches away while its in there, it comes back out, drags the cricket to the opening again, and then just has to do the whole inspection over from scratch, and you can keep doing that to it. Hofstadter coined a word for that, sphexish, and Dennett uses it as the go-to example of mindless behavior, the opposite of free will. fine, sure.

but here's what i keep circling on. the whole reason the wasp counts as sphexish is that its behavior is rigid — it just runs the program no matter what you do. and rigidity is basically just determinism, right? like a rigid routine is a determined routine. so isnt sphexishness pretty much an empirical proof of determinism, and if determinism is true then we obviously have no free will? and it gets worse, because everyone loves pointing out that humans are sphexish too, we've got our own loops we cant get out of. so if the wasp proves determinism and we're the same kind of thing only fancier, doesnt that just kill free will outright and leave the compatibilists coping?

(one more thing, i saw somewhere that a guy named Keijzer thinks the wasp story is actually wrong and that it does adapt after enough repetitions. if thats true does it rescue us? like if the wasp isnt really sphexish after all, does free will come back?)
