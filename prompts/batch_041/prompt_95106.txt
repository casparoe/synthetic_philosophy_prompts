I'm a phil PhD student and I'm opening a seminar on the demandingness objection next week, and I'd rather start with a joke than a definition. The obvious gag — utilitarianism says give everything away, so I shrugged and kept my savings — is worn out and also just wrong about the objection, since it treats demandingness as a complaint about effort when the real bite is that it erases the line between the obligatory and the supererogatory. So write me a joke that only really works if you already know the objection and the standard replies: it should turn on the actual machinery — e.g. on the fact that "too demanding" is a comparison against a baseline (commonsense morality? whose?) that nobody can specify, or on the objection arriving as a first-personal complaint ("it asks too much of *me*") and getting answered by a third-personal fact ("there's a child drowning"), or on the agent-centered prerogative as the proposed patch. Don't just have a character say "that's too demanding" — the punchline has to need the machinery. A short two-hander or a one-liner both fine. It should land for someone who's read this stuff, and not be obviously clueless to someone who hasn't.
