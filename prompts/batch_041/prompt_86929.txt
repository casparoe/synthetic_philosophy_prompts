I've talked myself into a fairly specific view about Goodhart's law and I want to stress-test it before I commit to it. My claim: the interesting thing here isn't about measures at all, it's about a split between whoever picks the metric and whoever optimizes against it. The proxy has to do two jobs at once -- stand in for what the setter actually cares about, and be checkable by them -- and those two jobs pull apart, which is where the whole mess comes from. If the same agent sets the target and optimizes for it, I don't think you get Goodharting at all, you just get an agent pursuing its goal imperfectly. So the slogan is really about someone *else's* target, not targets as such.

What would count against this? In particular I'm unsure what to do with cases that look like a single person gaming their own metric with nobody watching -- someone juicing their own step counter, or p-hacking their own study that only they'll ever read. Real counterexamples, or do they smuggle in an imagined audience?
