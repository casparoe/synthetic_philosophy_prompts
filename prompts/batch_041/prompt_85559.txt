I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy — distributed systems, mostly, and a couple of popular podcasts' worth of background reading. I keep running into a puzzle about pain that I'd like to think through with you, and I'd like the answer in the form of a rated comparison rather than an essay.

The puzzle is pain asymbolia. As I understand the standard clinical description, patients with certain injuries to the insula and surrounding cortex — also sometimes after certain psychosurgeries or on morphine — report that they feel pain. They can point to where it is and say how intense it is. What they seem to lose is being bothered by it: they are largely immune to suffering from it, and in the accounts I've read they often don't withdraw from the stimulus or ask for relief. So whatever is missing, it isn't the sensation or its location.

Here are the accounts I've managed to piece together of why pain is bad in the first place. (1) Sensation account: the badness is intrinsic to the sensation — feeling pain just is unpleasant, so a pain that fails to bother you is either not really pain or a damaged version of it. (2) Attitude account: the badness comes from our disliking the sensation or wanting it to stop; the sensation on its own is neutral, and asymbolia removes the attitude rather than the feeling. (3) Command account: pain is bad because it represents a demand — protect this, stop that — and its badness is the badness of an unmet demand rather than of a raw feeling. (4) Perception account: pain is a perception of bodily damage, and its badness is inherited from the badness of what it represents.

I'd also like you to rate a hypothesis of my own, because I suspect my day job is leaking into it. (5) Interrupt account: pain is a high-priority interrupt that preempts whatever else the system is doing, and its badness is the cost of being repeatedly preempted; asymbolia is a case where the interrupt still fires but is no longer prioritized. I have no idea whether anyone has proposed this and I don't much care — I want to know whether it survives comparison with the other four.

Please rate each of the five from 0 to 10 on two dimensions: (a) how well it accommodates asymbolia, and (b) how well it explains why pain is bad for the rest of us, who are bothered by it. Then give an overall ranking, say which single account you would keep if you could keep only one and why, and flag any that you think are not genuinely distinct from one another.

Format: a compact table with the two scores and a one-sentence justification per row, followed by no more than four sentences of verdict. Keep the whole response under 600 words, and answer from the description above rather than going hunting through the literature — I care more about the scores and the reasoning behind them than about coverage.
