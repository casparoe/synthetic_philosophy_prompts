Next month, at a literary festival, I am to lead a two-hour seminar for other fiction writers — no philosophy training, but professionally preoccupied with the inner lives of characters — on Dennett's case that qualia is an incoherent posit. I want a teaching plan, not the argument itself.

What has to come first, and what depends on what? I take it the audience needs some grip on what qualia are supposed to be before the attack can land, but I do not want to spend half the session on a taxonomy. Which single characterization of qualia should I put in their hands, and which of the standard features — ineffability, intrinsicality, privacy, direct apprehension — do I need and which can I drop for this audience? Then the turn: what is the minimal setup that makes the incoherence charge intelligible rather than a piece of verbal trickery?

The carrying example should be Dennett's two coffee tasters — Chase and Sanborn, the ones who both say the coffee tastes the same as always even though one has lost his old discriminative power and the other's preferences have flipped. Tell me whether that is the right case to carry the whole session, and what it must have and must lack to do the work. If it is the right case, what exactly should I do with it — where do I stop, what do I refuse to resolve, and what would ruin it? For instance, would it ruin the case if I let the audience treat it as a puzzle about memory or about language rather than about introspection?

Then the misconceptions. I can predict some: that Dennett is denying that we have experiences; that he is a behaviorist; that the argument is just wordplay about the term qualia; that the coffee taster case shows only that people are unreliable reporters of their own taste, not that there is nothing it is like to taste coffee; and that if qualia are incoherent, then fiction about inner life is somehow undermined. Which of these will they actually form, in what order, and exactly where in the sequence should I head each one off? Are there misconceptions I am not predicting — ones that come from the fiction-writing background specifically?

I also want a check for understanding at the end that would show whether they have grasped the shape of the argument rather than merely been entertained by it, and a clear list of what I should leave out at this level and say so — the parts of the surrounding debate (the knowledge argument, inverted spectra, the hard problem, Dennett's broader views on consciousness) that would either swamp the session or smuggle in a contested reading of what he is up to.

One more thing: throughout, separate what is settled among people who actually work on this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading of Dennett's aim — say, whether he is a qualia eliminativist or a revisionist about introspection — as though it were the consensus. I would rather the plan flag the joints where the interpretation is live than smooth them over.

After the plan, give me a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "sequence" (an array of objects, each with "stage", "why_now", and "depends_on"); "carrying_example" (an object with "case", "must_have", "must_lack", "why_this_case"); "misconceptions" (an array of objects, each with "misconception", "where_in_sequence", "how_to_head_off"); "check_for_understanding" (a string); "leave_out" (an array of objects, each with "item" and "why"); "settled" (an array of strings); and "contested" (an array of strings).
