I do outbreak investigations for a state health department, and lately the way we write up "the cause" of an outbreak has been driving me a bit nuts. Three things from this year. First: in one cluster we said the cause was a church supper, and then two sentences later, answering the question of why *that* supper produced cases rather than the two dozen other church suppers that spring, we said the cause was that the kitchen had no hand-washing sink. Same event, same facts on the ground — the only thing that changed was the alternative we were quietly comparing it to, and the "cause" jumped. Second: our writeup says the outbreak was caused by the caterer coming in while sick, but the lab folks say it was caused by the specific norovirus strain; both sentences are true and it doesn't feel like the same claim at two zoom levels, it feels like two different claims. Third: we blamed the sick caterer and not, say, the fact that this town has no paid sick leave, and I've noticed we almost always name whatever was abnormal or changeable as the cause rather than the standing background conditions, even when the background conditions are doing at least as much of the work.

So what I actually want isn't a label so much as the carving. Is there one established concept for this whole thing — that "the cause" comes out relative to the question we're asking rather than being a fact sitting there in the world — or are these three seperate known things that I'm wrongly lumping together? If it's several, name each one, say exactly where the lines fall between them, and tell me which of my three examples lands where (and if any of them lands in two places at once, I want to know that). If there's genuinely no single concept covering all three, say so plainly and coin one — give me a definition, a case that's clearly in, and a case that's clearly out, so I can see the edge. Start with your one-line verdict ("yes, the name is X" or "no single name, here's why"), then the carving, becuase the naming is the part I care about least. Keep the whole thing under 500 words.
