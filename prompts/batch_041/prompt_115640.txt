Write a dialogue between two philosophers going at each other over whether knowing how to do something is really just a kind of knowing that. One is an intellectualist in the Stanley–Williamson vein: any case of knowing how is really knowing some fact, just grasped under a 'practical mode of presentation' rather than a theoretical one. The other is a Rylean: knowing how is its own kind of knowledge, and 'practical mode of presentation' is a fancy name for a disposition that does no real explanatory work.

Keep them off the worn-out examples — no bike-riding, no ski instructor who knows how but can't ski. Hand them this instead: a woman who can reliably hear when an engine is about to seize. She's never wrong, but she can't say how she does it, can't state a rule, and can't teach it except by making you stand next to her and listen. A mechanic who's memorized the whole service manual still can't hear it. The Rylean pounces: know-how with no know-that. The intellectualist pushes back: she knows the fact 'this engine is about to seize,' she just grasps it under a practical mode of presentation — which is exactly why she can't put it into words.

Have the Rylean ask whether that's a genuine explanation or just a redescription, and the intellectualist give a straight answer. Then let them fight about two more things: Ryle's regress (if applying your know-that already takes know-how, does that loop forever, and is the regress vicious or harmless?), and the way skills come in degrees — you can be a so-so driver or a great one — while knowing that p looks all-or-nothing. Neither one should be a strawman, neither should just cave, and dont let them talk past each other: every reply should answer the point actually made.
