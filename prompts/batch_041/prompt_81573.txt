I'm in a drug policy course and there's something i can't get past. In Portugal possession isn't a crime anymore -- you get sent to a commission, and if you're dependent you get treatment -- but selling is still criminal, even when the seller is basically an addict funding their own habit. Canada and a bunch of US states go further and let licensed shops sell you cannabis, while the same government will still arrest you for a bag of heroin. And the same city that arrests people for possession funds a needle exchange and a supervised consumption site down the street. What does this whole arrangement presuppose -- about drugs, harm, and responsibility? My hunch is that the implicit theory is that the wrong lives in the market rather than in the using: that's why users get health care and sellers get prison, and why "legalization" turns out to mean licensing rather than deregulation. If that's right, does it quietly rule out the Mill-ish view (you can do what you like to yourself, so why is my dealer a criminal?) and the plain paternalist view (if the state can protect me from myself, why not arrest me too)? And can the market view explain why cannabis gets a licensed market while heroin doesn't, or does that part need a separate story about the drugs themselves? Also, where does the needle exchange fit -- the state is funding the very thing it's arresting people for. Tell me which bits are real presuppositions and which are just compatible with lots of theories.
