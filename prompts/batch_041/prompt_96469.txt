Question: "Dennett draws a distinction between cranes and skyhooks, and separately draws a distinction between good reductionism and greedy reductionism. (a) Explain each distinction in your own words. (b) How, if at all, do the two distinctions relate — is one a special case of the other, are they seperate, or something else? (c) Use both distinctions to assess the following claim: 'Human grammar is emergent: once brains got complicated enough, syntax just appeared, and it cannot be explained in terms of anything lower-level.'"

Answer: "Cranes and skyhooks are the two ways an explanation can account for something that looks designed. A skyhook explains a designed thing by appealing to something even more designed and then just stopping — God making the eye is the classic case, and so are souls, vital forces, and anything else outside nature. The skyhook does all the lifting and you never have to say where the skyhook itself came from, which is why Dennett says they can't be allowed into a Darwinian account. A crane is the opposite. A crane is a sub-mechanism that is itself a product of the basic Darwinian algorithm, so it comes with a receipt, and once you have one you can use it to build higher cranes on top of it. The eye is a crane, the immune system is a crane, human language is a crane. Cranes are allowed because they're paid for; skyhooks aren't, because they're free. The reductionism distinction is the same distinction, applied to methodology. Good reductionism is explaining things one level at a time without cheating — cells in terms of molecules, molecules in terms of atoms. Greedy reductionism is trying to skip straight to the bottom and explain the whole thing in one move, like explaining culture in terms of genes. That's bad because it's really a skyhook in disguise: you collect the explanatory payoff at the top without doing the work of the intermediate levels, which is exactly what a skyhook does, just pointed in the other direction. On the grammar claim: saying syntax 'just appeared' and can't be explained in lower-level terms is a skyhook. 'Emergent' is being used as a promissory note — a label for our ignorance that stops the explanation at exactly the point where the interesting question starts. Dennett would say grammar has to be a crane, and if we can't yet say what it's built out of, that's a gap in our knowledge, not a fact about the world."
