You are an expert in moral philosophy and cultural moral psychology, with particular fluency in the contrast between honor cultures, dignity cultures, and what is sometimes called victimhood culture. I am a high school student writing an essay on whether any of these three ways of handling interpersonal wrongdoing is morally superior to the others, and I would like you to help me build a single thought experiment engineered to my specifications. Please think carefully and work step by step. Begin by briefly brainstorming three or four candidate scenarios and telling me why you discarded all but the one you keep, and only then present the finished case.

First, so that the exercise does not depend on your recalling any particular author, here is how I am using the three terms:

- An honor culture treats a person's worth as bound up with their public standing, so that a slight is a genuine loss that must be answered, often by the wronged party themselves and often in kind.
- A dignity culture treats every person's worth as inherent and independent of others' opinions, so that slights are best ignored or handled through neutral institutions rather than personal retaliation.
- A victimhood culture treats slights as evidence of an underlying asymmetry of power or status, so that the appropriate response is to publicize the wrong and appeal to a community or an authority to correct the imbalance.

Now the specifications for the case itself:

1. It must be a single realistic scenario of ordinary life, set in a school, workplace, or neighborhood, involving at least three people: someone who is wronged, someone who does the wronging, and a third party or community in a position to respond. No duels, no lawsuits, no violence, and nothing science-fictional.
2. The objective facts must be identical for everyone in the story; no one is better informed than anyone else, and the wrong must be real rather than imagined or exaggerated.
3. The wrongdoer must not be a villain: the act must be the kind of thing a reasonable person could classify in more than one way.
4. In one paragraph, write the scenario so that each of the three cultures, applied faithfully to the same facts, recommends a different course of action for the wronged person. Do not rig the case by making any one culture's recommendation obviously absurd.
5. The scenario itself must be no longer than one paragraph; the analysis that follows it may be longer.

After the scenario, please do all of the following:

(a) For each culture, state exactly what it recommends and why, showing that each verdict follows from that culture's stated logic rather than from your stipulation.
(b) Identify the single most load-bearing feature of the case, the one that makes the three verdicts diverge, and explain why that feature rather than any other is doing the work.
(c) Show what happens if that feature is removed or reversed, say whether the three verdicts converge, and state what that tells us.
(d) For each culture, give the strongest argument a defender of that culture could make that its recommended response is, in this very case, the morally best one, and then say how a critic would attack that argument.
(e) Finally, say what this case can and cannot show about whether any of the three cultures is morally superior overall.

Please reason from the structure of the case itself. Do not try to settle the question by appealing to what any named philosopher or study says, and do not include any reading list, citations, or bibliography.
