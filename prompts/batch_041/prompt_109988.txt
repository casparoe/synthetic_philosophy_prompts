ok, I'm trying to write down my actual position on forecasting state collapse for a reading group, and I've a nasty feeling it doesn't hang together. Here's the package, roughly in the order I'd defend it:

(a) The outside view is the only honest way to forecast the fall of a great power. You build a reference class of comparable cases, take the base rate for how long they last, and read your case off that. Inside-view stories -- 'this time the institutions are stronger', 'our elites are more adaptable' -- are just motivated reasoning.

(b) But every reference class for empires is gerrymandered. Whether something counts as an empire, and what counts as the moment it died, is a choice, and the average lifespan moves by decades depending on where you draw the line. And I can only ever count the ones that have already ended, so the whole distribution is built out of corpses. For what it's worth my own rule is: a polity is an empire if it sets the foreign policy of at least one other polity without that polity's consent, and it dies when it can no longer do that to anyone.

(c) Still, I think there's a fact of the matter about how long a given great power will last, and the base rate is a real estimate of it, not just an artefact of my definitions.

(d) I think the outside view just is the base-rate approach -- using base rates is what 'outside view' means.

(e) I think the reference class has to be stationary: if the conditions that generated the old base rate no longer hold, then the base rate doesn't transfer, full stop, and the outside view has nothing to say about the present.

I can feel at least one of these biting another, but I honestly can't work out which, and I'd like to sort it before I say it out loud. So: where are the real tensions, which pairs only look incompatible, and what's the cheapest repair -- and what does that repair cost me? Also, is (d) actually true, or am I running two seperate things together? Please rank the problems by severity instead of giving me a flat list, be specific about which commitment has to give, and write it in British English.
