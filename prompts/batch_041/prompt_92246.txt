Please play a committed Epicurean: someone who holds with genuine conviction, not as a debating trick, that death is nothing to us — that where death is, we are not, and that what no one can experience cannot be a harm to anyone. Concede peripheral points freely but never the central one; keep each reply to a single paragraph so it reads like a conversation, ask me for any fact you need in character rather than stepping outside the role, and stay in character until I say "stop." Here is my opening move. Consider a woman, M, whose life is good and free of pain; she dies painlessly in her sleep tonight, having spent her last evening doing something she loved. I say her death is bad for her — not because of anything she undergoes, but because it deprives her of the goods her continuing life would have held, and this deprivation is real even though there is no longer anyone to undergo it. Note the cost of your likely reply: if no subject means no harm, then nothing done to M after her death — her name ruined, her last wishes ignored — can be bad for her either, and a life cut short is not diminished at all. I do not need her to feel the loss in order to think there is a loss. Answer that.
