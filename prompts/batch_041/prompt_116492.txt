ok so I'm working on something about bet sizing and I want to get the spec down before I write anything else. The picture I'm starting from: forget maximizing expected money, maximize the growth rate of your bankroll over a run of bets instead -- bet a fraction of your money proportional to your edge, so the typical trajectory compounds best rather than the average one. Kelly, growth-optimal, geometric-mean stuff, whatever you want to call it.

What I want back is the desiderata list. Not the formula and not a defense of it: what does any adequate theory of "how much of my money goes on this" have to deliver? The cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it can't contradict -- with the case behind each requirement, plus your call on which requirements are real and which are just something one side in the argument wants. And flag any pair you think can't be satisfied at the same time.

Things I want the list to cover, though dont let this be the whole list:

- the case where expected money and the growth rate point different ways. One-shot coin flip on your entire bankroll: heads it goes up 50%, tails it goes down 40%. Expected money rises 5%, but the average of the log falls, so the growth person turns down a bet that's good in expectation. Then the same bet where you're allowed to stake any fraction you like -- I think there both rules say bet, they just disagree about how much (a quarter of the bankroll, if I've done the algebra right), so the theory needs to be able to say which of the two setups is the real disagreement.
- one-shot bets generally. The growth argument gets its force from repetition; the theory owes us something for the n=1 case, or an honest admission that it's silent.
- edges you've estimated rather than edges you know. Overbetting is punished without limit in a way underbetting isn't -- bet too small and you leave growth on the table, bet too big and eventually the growth rate goes negative, worse than not betting at all. So does the theory owe us a rule for when the edge is a guess, and does it have to be robust to the guess being wrong?
- bets that aren't independent: two positions on the same underlying, a portfolio correlated with your job, a bet that only pays off in the world where you also lose your income.
- what the bankroll even is. Liquid cash, future earnings, the house you live in. If the theory's answer turns on a quantity that isn't well-defined, that's a requirement on the theory, not a quibble.
- whether this is a theory of rationality or of prudence. If I bet half of what the growth rule says, am I irrational, or just more risk-averse? 'Maximize expected log wealth' is one utility function among alot of them, and it's not obvious that rationality picks it out. This is where I want you to plant a flag: I suspect "the theory gives a determinate answer to how much to bet" and "the theory doesn't smuggle in a controversial attitude to risk" can't both hold, and I want to know which one you'd keep and what it costs.

Don't hand me a survey of who says what. Give me the list, the case under each item, a straight answer on which requirement rules out the most candidates, and a definite verdict on whether the set can be jointly satisfied. If two of my bullets are really one requirement, merge them and say why.
