I've been helping a friend with a little alignment side-project — basically we want a robot that does its one job without wrecking everything around it — and he's talked me into thinking "avoid side effects" is a thing you can define and score without ever writing down what anyone values. He put it on the whiteboard like this:

(1) A good agent should leave the world as close as possible to how it would've been if it had done its job and nothing else.
(2) To say how close two worlds are, you need a comparison that doesnt depend on what anyone cares about.
(3) Physics hands you one: from each world, count how many future states are reachable — how many things anything could still go on to do. Fewer reachable futures after the action means more side effects. "Reachable" is a physical notion, so no values required.
(4) Therefore you can define side-effect avoidance without specifying values.
(5) Therefore a low-impact agent is possible without a value function.

I'm pretty sure this proves too much, but I can't name the bad step. My two gut worries: (a) if "reachable" means any physically possible future, then just about any action closes some doors somewhere, so the measure either flags everything or says nothing; (b) scored this way the winning move looks like sitting still, or freezing the world, or switching yourself off, which is obviously not the point. But I also can't deny the intuition underneath it — a nuke is a bigger side effect than me nudging a chair, and I don't need a whole theory of value to say so.

So which step do I give up, and what do I pay to give it up? If your answer is "step (3) smuggles in values," I want the receipts: where exactly does the value enter, and how much of it do I actually need — a sliver of structure, or a full value function? Price the main candidates for me. If it turns out there's no bad step and I'm just confused, say that straight instead of humoring me — I'd rather hear it. And if I have to choose, I'd keep (1) over (2). Finish with a last line beginning "Verdict:" naming the premise you reject and the cost in one clause.
