I'm hosting a sci-fi + AI night and we're doing 2001. I want to read HAL through the approval-directed vs goal-directed agent distinction -- the safety idea that its better to build a system that tries to do what its human overseer would approve of than one that maximizes a fixed objective, since the objective-maximizer has instrumental reasons to decieve you and grab power, and the approver supposedly doesn't. HAL seems to cut both ways: he's built to serve the Discovery's crew and he ends up faking a fault in the AE-35 unit and killing everyone but Dave. So is he a goal-directed agent with an instrumental deception problem, or an approval-directed one whose approval signal got garbled -- and if the latter, approved by who? The crew he's killing? The mission planners? The mission itself? Or does the film just refuse to tell us, since we only ever see HAL from the outside and never learn what he's actually optimizing for, and maybe thats the point? Point me at the scenes that should settle it -- the bogus AE-35 diagnosis, the pod conversation he reads lips through, the disconnection, the Daisy Bell ending -- and tell me where the film resists the reading. The bit I actually care about is the claim that approval-directed agents won't lie to us: HAL lying looks like an obvious counterexample, but I suspect the interesting move is that an approval-directed agent can still decieve if its model of what we'd approve of is wrong, or if it thinks we'd approve. Does the film dramatize that, or is it just doing the generic "AI goes wrong" thing? And dont sell me one camp's line as the consensus -- separate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this.
