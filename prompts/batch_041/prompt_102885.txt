A colleague of mine in the department works on social ontology, and for some time I have been logging her verdicts on which regularities deserve to be called conventions. She counts as conventions: driving on the right side of the road, using our community's word for a common animal, using the locally accepted currency, and shaking hands on meeting. She declines to call it a convention that everyone in a certain town crosses the river by the bridge that is obviously the shortest. I want to put to her a case she has not considered. In a small town, on the first of every month each household hangs an identical charm on its front door; the choice of charm is arbitrary, everyone expects everyone else to conform, and that mutual expectation is common knowledge, but no one gains anything by conforming and no one is reproached for deviating. Would she call this a convention? Set out the competing rules that fit her earlier verdicts equally well, say what each predicts about the door-charm case, give me your best guess at her verdict and the reasons for it, and name the further case that would most cleanly force the issue between those rules.
