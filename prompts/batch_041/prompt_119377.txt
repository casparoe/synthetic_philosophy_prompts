A close friend and I spent most of Saturday evening arguing about whether the panopticon is still the right model for how power watches us, and we ended where such arguments usually end: he said I was being pedantic about the metaphor, and I said he was letting the metaphor do the arguing. His position, which he has been developing for a while, is that Foucault's panopticon was never really about prisons; it was about a diagram of power that could be detached from any particular institution, and that what we now call dataveillance is that diagram finally realized at scale. The tower is the platform; the inmates are users; the cell is the account; the examination is the profile; the dossier is the log; and the internalization of the gaze is whatever it is that makes us edit ourselves before we post. From this he draws a practical conclusion: that the familiar nothing-to-hide reply misses the point, because the panopticon never needed everyone to be watched, only to be uncertain whether they were; and that resistance has to target the internalized gaze rather than the cameras.

I am not persuaded, but I am also not sure I can say exactly where the analogy fails. Some of the disanalogies seem to me to matter a great deal: there is no central tower, only a distributed set of collectors; the record is kept rather than the gaze held; the watchers are mostly commercial rather than disciplinary, and they want prediction rather than docility; and we often know we are being recorded and go on anyway, which is not how the panopticon was supposed to work. Other disanalogies seem cosmetic. What I want from you is an audit, not a verdict on surveillance.

Give me the correspondence table first — what actually plays the role of the tower, the inspector, the inmate, the visibility, the examination, the dossier, and the internalization — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. Separate the breaks that matter for his conclusion from the ones that do not. Tell me what the analogy quietly imports from the prison that the platform does not have, and whether his conclusion survives once those imports are removed. If a better analogy is available — control rather than discipline, the synopticon rather than the panopticon, something else — say what it is and what it costs. Throughout, say how confident you are in each claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
