I run a scrappy little interview show — two people, one decent mic, no producer — and next month's guest is a philosopher who has spent his career defending a patched-up version of the old conditional analysis of dispositions. Roughly: x is disposed to M when C just in case x would M if C occurred and x kept the causal basis it actually has. He reckons the classic counterexamples — the finks, the masks — are all absorbed once you hold the basis fixed, and he has a couple of newer papers claiming the leftover mimic cases fall to a "due to the basis" clause. He isn't famous, so don't worry about matching his real published positions; just build me the strongest, most coherent version of this guy and interrogate that.

Write me 8-10 questions I can actually ask. I want them pressed where the litrature genuinely presses, and for each question tell me the answer he's most likely to give and then the follow-up that answer begs — I don't want to get caught flat when he reaches for the standard move. No softballs, nothing like "how did you get into philosophy."

At least one question each on:
- the charge that "hold the basis fixed" is either circular (what is the basis, other than whatever makes the counterfactual come out true?) or so elastic it can't fail;
- a case I keep chewing on where the fink removes the basis precisely because C occurs, so the antecedent "C happens and the basis is retained" may be impossible — does the counterfactual then go vacuously true and hand every object every disposition?
- mimics, and why the mimic problem seems to get so much less attention than finks and masks;
- whether the "no masks" clause quietly makes dispositions extrinsic, which is awkward for anyone who wants fragility to be a property of the glass.

Then work in some cases he probably hasn't seen, so he can't coast:
- one mechanism that both blocks the disposition's own manifestation and manufactures a lookalike manifestation by another route, so the counterfactual comes out true but for entirely the wrong reason;
- a disposition that masks itself from the inside, where the neutralizer is part of the object rather than something bolted on from outside;
- a finkish acquisition, where the stimulus itself creates the basis, so the object would M if C but intuitively isn't disposed to M before C — I think that's the mirror image of the fink and I want to know whether his fix treats the two the same way;
- and maybe something gruesome: a disposition to M when C unless the object was poked before some date, else to N. Does his analysis rule gruesome dispositions out, or does it just count them as real ones?

Finish with two more. One on what this whole apparatus is supposed to do for the Rylean/behaviorist project — do the finkish cases sink the dispositional analysis of mental talk, or just force it to get more complicated? And a closer where he has to say what would actually count as a counterexample to his own analysis, i.e. what he'd have to see to give it up. That last one is definately the one I care most about getting right.
