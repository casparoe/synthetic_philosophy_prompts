I have to post a statement of my own position for a graduate seminar on truthfulness and deception, and I want it audited before I put it up for criticism. I have ended up in the rigorist camp — the line that runs from Augustine — but I have built the position out in my own way, and I suspect that the parts do not all fit together. Here is the whole package.

1. Lying is never permitted. Not to save an innocent person from a persecutor, not to spare a dying friend's feelings, not to a murderer at the door, not in war. I do not want a rule with a hidden exception; I want the prohibition to be absolute.

2. What makes an utterance a lie is the speaker's will to produce a false belief in the hearer. Falsity alone is not enough — someone who sincerely repeats a false report has not lied — and I do not think falsity is strictly necessary either, since it is the duplicity of the will that does the work.

3. It follows that fiction, jokes, hyperbole, and figurative speech are not lies, even where what they say is not the case. A parable does not deceive anyone; the hearer knows what kind of speech he is hearing.

4. The wrongness of a lie lies in what it does to the speaker, not in what it does to the hearer. Speech is the faculty by which one mind makes itself known to another; to speak against one's own mind is to set the tongue against the heart, and that injury is self-inflicted. A lie that no one believes and no one is harmed by is still a lie, and I would still refuse to tell it.

5. Lies nonetheless differ in gravity. A courteous falsehood that spares someone's feelings for an afternoon and a false statement that sends an innocent person to prison are not on the same level, and I take that ranking seriously.

6. In the murderer-at-the-door case I would not say anything false, but I do not think I owe him anything either: he has no claim on my candor. I would decline to answer, stall, and leave him with a false impression if that is what it takes, and I would not regard that as a lie.

Something in this is unstable and I cannot see what. Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how severe is each real one? For each genuine tension, tell me what has to give and what it costs to give it, and rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most at the lowest cost.
