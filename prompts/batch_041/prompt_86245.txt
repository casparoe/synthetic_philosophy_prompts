A friend and I have been going round in circles for months over why the industrial revolution happened in Britain and not in China. He thinks it's an ordinary causal question — line up the factors (coal, colonies, wages, institutions) and work out which ones did the work. I keep feeling the question is rigged, though not in a way I can state cleanly: it's not that I deny there were real differences, it's that the 'and not China' bit picks out a contrast nobody has justified, and I suspect that if you'd asked 'and not the Netherlands' or 'and not Japan' you'd end up telling a different story. I tried to seperate 'why did Britain industrialize at all' from 'why Britain rather than this particular rival,' and he says that's just bookkeeping. So I don't want a list of causes, I want a diagnosis. Is this a substantive disagreement about history, a verbal one about what a why-question is asking, or are we quietly answering two different questions under one phrase? And is the genre ill-posed — does a contrast like this have a right answer, or does picking the contrast already decide the answer? If some version of the question can be rescued, tell me what a principled contrast would look like and what would count as progress; if not, tell me why we should drop it.
