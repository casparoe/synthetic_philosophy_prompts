I'm a philosophy PhD student working on distributive justice in health, and I've been leaning hard on the fire department analogy in a chapter draft, so I want to know whether it can actually carry the weight. My brother (libertarian, reads too much) came back at me with Obion County, Tennessee, where rural residents pay a $75 a year subscription for fire service and in 2010 a family that hadn't paid watched the department stand by while their house burned, and he says that's just the market working and not obviously a catastrophe. Can you audit this for me? Give me the correspondence table -- what plays the role of the spread of the fire, the neighbor's roof, the hydrant, the 911 call, the taxes -- then tell me which of those features is actually doing the argumentative work for universal provision and which is just set dressing, where the mapping breaks, and whether my conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table.
