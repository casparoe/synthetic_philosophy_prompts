Topic is the wrong kind of reasons problem — you know, the thing where there can apparently be reasons to have an attitude (believe something, admire someone, be afraid of something) that dont bear on whether the attitude is correct or fitting, only on whether it's good or useful to have it. Two people in my group have been going at each other for months and I want to force them to write down what they actually agree on.

The two camps are: (A) the fittingness-first crowd, who think there's a real and principled right-kind / wrong-kind distinction — a reason of the right kind bears on whether the attitude meets its own standard of correctness, a wrong-kind reason bears only on the payoff of having the attitude — and that the fitting-attitude approach to value needs this distinction to work at all. (B) the skeptics, who think the distinction is ad hoc, circular, or just collapses when you look closely — that so-called wrong-kind reasons are really just reasons for some other attitude (wanting to have it, wanting to be the sort of person who has it, taking steps to acquire it), and that 'right kind' never gets a non-question-begging explanation.
