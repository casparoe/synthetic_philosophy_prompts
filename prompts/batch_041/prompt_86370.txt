I'm volunteering with a small nonprofit that's translating a health app into a language spoken by a few thousand people, mostly elders, with no real written standard. We've got two fluent speakers on the team. They went through the whole thing together and agree on every sentence we need — line by line, they'd produce the same text. But they've gotten stuck on one recurring word that shows up whenever the app talks about a particular kind of episode a person goes through, sort of a spell or an attack or a bad stretch. One of them insists the word names a thing — a discrete event, something you have, like an object — and the other insists it names a process or a condition you're in, more like weather or a mood. Both readings fit every single use we've collected so far, and neither can point to any case that settles it. My board keeps asking me which translater is right, and I honestly don't know what to tell them.

Part of what's bugging me is that one of the two says it doesn't even matter, because we only ever use the word inside full sentences, and the full sentences come out identical either way — so if there's some hidden disagreement underneath, it never surfaces in anything a user would actually read. The other one says that's a cop-out, and that whichever of us has it wrong is getting the app's whole way of thinking about illness wrong, and that it'll eventually bite us in how we design the thing.

So: is there a fact of the matter about which of them has it right, or are we just picking a convention and calling it truth? And separately, even if there is a fact of the matter, does it matter for anything we're actually going to do? I did a semester of philosophy years ago and remember roughly none of it, so spell out whatever framework you think is really doing the work here rather than just reassuring me.
