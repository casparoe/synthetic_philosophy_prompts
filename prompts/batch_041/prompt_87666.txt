Here's the setup. A small museum has this head study that was long attributed to a famous 17th century painter. Turns out it was painted in 1890 by a woman who made a career out of faking old masters. She never sold this one, left it to the museum labelled as after the guy she was copying, and in her letters said she wanted to find out whether a hand can be trained to disappear. So now the label just names her instead, and the room looks exactly the same. The museum also owns the real master's rough first sketch of the same head, and it's clearly worse.

Rosa: nothing that matters changed. Same pigment, same canvas, everything you can see and respond to is identical, and if the label changes your experience then you were responding to the story, not the painting. Nadia: the properties you respond to were never just the surface.
