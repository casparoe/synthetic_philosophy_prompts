Ok so bit of an odd one. I'm a software engineer, never taken a philosophy class in my life, and I've somehow agreed to run a single session of my flatmate's second-year seminar on rational choice and decision thoery. About 18 students, I get 75 minutes, and she wants the session on the donkey that starves between two identical bales of hay. I've read enough to know the usual move is that the donkey is a stupid model, because nothing actually forces you to starve, you'd just pick one, and picking isn't irrational. Fine. But I don't want to just say that and sit down, I want them to feel why "just pick" isn't the end of the story either. Three things I'd like them to run into, and I want the game to make them run into it rather than me announcing it:

(a) when you're genuinely indifferent, nothing inside you is doing the choosing, so the choice ends up being made by whatever junk happens to be lying around in the situation, the order things are shown in, a default, whoever shrugs first, and then people walk away treating that junk as if it was their preference all along;

(b) choosing seems to create preference rather than just report it, so if I get them to rank two near-identical options, then force a real commitment to one, then rank again, I want to see the ratings move;

(c) I'm curious whether indifference is actually exploitable, i.e. whether someone patient can just keep swapping with you and bleed you a little each time. My hunch is it mostly isn't, because indifference makes you inert and you've got no reason to move in either direction, and I'd quite like the game to settle that, because if it comes out inert that makes point (a) land harder.

So design me the activity. I need rules, materials, the timings, and what the payoffs or tokens are (no real money, they're students, and I'm not funding this out of pocket). Give me the full running order for the 75 minutes including setup and the debrief, and in the debrief I need to be able to point at specific moments and say "that thing you all just did, that's the thing". Also be honest with me about what will definately go wrong the first time I run it and what I do when it does, and tell me which part of the actual philosophical point the game can't get across and I have to just say out loud myself. And please don't send me off to read anything, I won't do it and I don't have time, just tell me what to do with the room.
