Last night my closest friend and I argued for hours about a deal we had already agreed to, and I left convinced she was being inconsistent but unable to say exactly where. We hold different moral priorities — she is convinced that animal suffering is the great neglected cause, I am convinced it is the risk from advanced AI — so we arranged a swap: for the next two years, each of us gives half of our charitable budget to the other's cause. By each of our own lights we come out ahead, since each of us buys more of what we care about with money we would otherwise have spread thinly. She has now backed out, saying the swap would compromise her integrity, because it would mean deliberately funding something she believes is wrong and treating her deepest commitments as though they were up for trade. I cannot see how both of those can be true at once. I keep returning to a principle I would defend in any other context: if a voluntary exchange leaves both parties better off by their own lights, then resistance to it is either a mistake about one's own interests or an attempt to impose one's values on someone else. And there is a consequence I cannot shake — the mere fact that we could strike a mutually advantageous swap seems to show that our disagreement was never really about what matters, only about where the marginal dollar goes, so there is no deep moral remainder here to respect in the first place. Tell me precisely where this goes wrong, and which of my claims fails.
