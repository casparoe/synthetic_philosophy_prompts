I am a high school student, and for my philosophy club I have been given an unusual assignment: not to defend a side, but to write the joint statement that two people who have argued with each other for a year could both put their names to. The two of them are, first, a classmate who has become convinced by the argument usually credited to Galen Strawson and called the Basic Argument, and second, a classmate who thinks moral responsibility is perfectly real and that the first one has been spooked by a word.

The first argues roughly as follows: you act as you do because of the way you are; so if you are responsible for what you do, you would have to be responsible for the way you are; but the way you are comes from heredity, upbringing, and experiences you never chose; and even if you set out to remake yourself, that project begins from the self you already have, so the demand only pushes further back and never lands. The conclusion is that no one is ever truly morally responsible. The second thinks this shows only that no one is the ultimate author of themselves, and that responsibility never required that: it requires that the act flow from your own values in the right way and that you be responsive to reasons.

Write the document they should have produced. I want:

1. A numbered list of claims both can sign, including what each concedes the other has right — about what the argument does and does not establish, about what work the adverb truly is doing in that conclusion, about whether the argument depends on determinism at all, and about what the target notion of responsibility is (deserved blame in its own right, as opposed to blame justified only by its effects).
2. A disagreements table: each remaining disagreement in neutral wording, followed by each side's own preferred formulation, written so that side would accept the wording as its own.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. I suspect one is whether responsibility requires that the agent have authored the self that acts, and another is whether responsibility admits of degrees. For each crux, name a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it.
4. At least one discriminating case the two of them have not already fought over. I would like it to be a case in which two people have the same present-day character and values but different histories — one whose character was deliberately remade by her own long effort, the other whose character was set by an early intervention she does not remember — so that we can see whether their judgments track the history or only the present self.
5. An honest section on whether part of the dispute is verbal, and if so which part, and what the two of them are really disagreeing about once the verbal part is set aside.

Two constraints. Please include no citations, reading lists, or attributions to particular texts; I cannot check them, and I would rather the document stand on the arguments themselves. And please do not assume I know the technical labels for positions; where a distinction is needed, state it in plain words.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the one point in the document you are least sure of.
