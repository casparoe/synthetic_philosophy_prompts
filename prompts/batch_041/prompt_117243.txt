Ok, so I'm in a reading group that's doing Kripke's rule-following argument next week — the plus/quus thing — and one of the grad students circulated this summary for us all to react to. It goes roughly like this:

Kripke's Wittgenstein starts from the fact that you've only ever performed finitely many additions, and any finite stretch of past behavior is compatable with infinitely many rules, including the 'quus' rule, which agrees with plus on everything you've ever actually done but gives 5 for 68+57 instead of 125. Since no fact about you — not your dispositions, not any mental image — settles whether you meant plus or quus, there's no fact of the matter about what you meant by '+'. This is basically Quine's indeterminacy of translation, applied to a single speaker in his own language. Kripke's solution, which he takes over from Wittgenstein, is that meaning is a matter of community agreement: '+' means plus because we all agree to go on in the same way, so meaning is a social convention, and it follows that a solitary speaker like Robinson Crusoe couldn't mean anything at all. The upshot is that our confidence that our words have determinate meanings is an illusion, and that's what really drives the private language argument.

It reads fine to me, but I have a nagging feeling it's off somewhere. A friend in the group said she thinks it makes Kripke sound like he's handing us a theory of meaning, which he says he isn't, but she couldn't say more than that. Can you go through it sentence by sentance and sort out what someone who actually works on this would accept, what's a common oversimplification, and what Kripke explicitly denies or is explicitly tentative about? In particular I want to know: does the 'skeptical solution' refute the skeptic or concede to him, and in what sense (if any) does it make meaning communal — does it really follow that a lone speaker can't mean anything? And if someone objects that the community's agreement can't do the work, because the community's responses are just more finite, uninterpreted behavior, does that objection land, or does it miss what the solution is actually claiming? Skip the who-said-what-in-which-paper stuff, I just want the shape of the argument and a verdict on each sentence.
