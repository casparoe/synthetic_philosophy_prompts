Write a joke about archaeological inference — about how we get from objects to beliefs. It should be funny to someone who has actually dug, not just someone who has heard of archaeology, so don't settle for the familiar gag about future excavators misreading our plumbing as a shrine. The comic point I want is the epistemic asymmetry that shapes the whole discipline: claims about what past people did can in principle be checked against the material record, while claims about what they believed, valued, or intended usually can't, and quite different behaviors can leave indistinguishable traces (archaeologists call this equifinality) — which is why the verdict of ritual object becomes the default whenever function can't be pinned down. It is the one hypothesis nothing can refute, and that is exactly why it keeps winning. Build the joke around that, commit to one specific artifact and one specific punchline rather than handing me a menu of options, and keep it clear of any named real site or scholar, since I don't want the joke resting on a specific fact you might be misremembering. Beneath the joke, add a short note on where it takes liberties with how the inference actually works or leans on a contested reading of it.
