I'm putting together a long interview for a small philosophy-of-psychiatry podcast -- the listeners are mostly grad students and clinicians, so nobody needs the basics explained -- and next month's guest is a philosopher who's spent years on Hacking's looping effects and has just published a book that makes a move I havent seen anyone else make. I want questions that go after the substance of it instead of letting her do the polished book-tour version.

Her position, roughly: the usual reading of Hacking draws the interactive/indifferent line between human kinds and natural kinds, and explains looping by saying people come to fit the label. She thinks both halves are wrong. What matters, on her account, is uptake -- whether the members of a kind can take the classification into their own self-understanding. Two things she's happy to accept from that: looping doesn't need humans (she thinks some non-human kinds are interactive in her sense), and lots of human kinds never loop, because the members can't take the label up at all -- infants, the dead, people with profound cognitive impairment. So on her view the interactive/indifferent line is orthogonal to the human/natural line, and it's a mistake to treat them as the same distinction.

The second part is what I'm least sure about. She says uptake isn't belief: it's practical self-ascription, taking the label as something you reason from and act on, which is why she insists looping isn't a story about suggestion or false belief. And she splits the phenomenon in two: loops that run through the self (the person changes) versus loops that run through the institution (the label changes what's done to the person -- treatment offered, stigma, insurance, what the family expects -- with no change in the person's self-understanding). Only the first counts as a looping effect for her; the second is just a label having downstream effects.

Give me 8-10 questions. I need at least one pressing the through-the-institution cases, since I think that's where the real action in psychiatry is and I want her to have to say why they don't count; one on whether practical uptake can come apart from belief, ideally with a case of someone acting on a diagnosis they don't believe they have; one on whether uptake comes in degrees and, if it does, whether the interactive/indifferent distinction survives at all; one on realism, i.e. whether a loop can generate the kind of stable causal mechanism the property-cluster realists want or whether it can only ever destabilise one; one on the declassification of homosexuality, which on her account may not even be a looping case; and one to close on what would make her give the uptake thesis up. A follow-up for each, keyed to the answer she's most likely to give -- she has a stock reply that whatever I've described is an institutional effect rather than a loop, and I'd rather the questions didn't all end up in the same place. No warm-ups about how she got into pyschiatry.
