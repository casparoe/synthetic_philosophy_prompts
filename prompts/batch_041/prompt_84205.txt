My old PhD supervisor is retiring after forty years working on the dogmatism paradox — the one where if you know something, then any evidence against it counts as misleading, so you're rational to ignore it. I'm giving the toast at her retirment dinner. Half the room are epistemologists who know the paradox cold; the other half are her family and will glaze over if I lecture. I want the paradox to actually run the speech, not just be a cute reference: once I say she was right about something, I can't then mention any evidence that she wasn't, or I'd be violating the thing she spent her life on. So the toast has to be warm, funny, and structurally trapped in its own logic. Can you write it? About five minutes, no citations or reading lists.
