I am preparing a graduate reading-group session on the triviality results for indicative conditionals, and I want to map the logical space of responses rather than rehash who said what. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations; I want the shape of the options. Please sort the following proposals into tiers — top, second, third, fourth — according to how promising each is as a way of preserving the conditional-probability thesis, roughly the idea that the probability of an indicative conditional with antecedent A and consequent B equals the conditional probability of B given A, while avoiding the triviality conclusion that this equation, together with seemingly innocent assumptions, forces conditional probabilities to collapse into unconditional probabilities. Assign each proposal a score from 0 to 10, and for each give one sentence on what it buys and one on what it costs. The proposals are: (1) reject the thesis outright, keep a truth-conditional semantics for conditionals, and explain the intuitive appeal of the thesis as a pragmatic or heuristic phenomenon; (2) keep the thesis but restrict it to simple, unembedded conditionals, denying that it extends to conditionals embedded inside other conditionals or other compounds; (3) treat conditionals as non-propositional, so that they express a suppositional or expressive state rather than a truth-evaluable proposition, and the triviality proof targets a pseudo-property; (4) adopt a dynamic or update-based semantics on which the thesis is a constraint on how credence is revised under supposition, not a claim about the probability of a proposition; (5) reject one of the background assumptions of the proof, such as closure of the language under conditionalization or the assumption that rational credence is a single probability function over a Boolean algebra containing conditionals; (6) adopt a trivalent or indeterminacy semantics on which a conditional lacks a truth value when its antecedent is false, so the relevant probability is not the probability of truth; and (7) a home-grown proposal I want you to evaluate on its own terms: take the probability of a conditional with antecedent A and consequent B to be the probability that one’s credence in B would be high after supposing A and then reasoning only from that supposition plus one’s standing background knowledge — that is, a stability condition on the suppositional credence rather than a direct equation with the conditional probability of B given A.

I am especially interested in whether proposal (7) is genuinely distinct from (4) and (3) or is just a restatement of the Ramsey test, and whether it inherits a version of the triviality result once we ask about nested conditionals. After the ranking, please say which proposal you would bet on if forced, and how the bet would change if we limited the thesis to conditionals with contingent, non-zero-probability antecedents. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this: do not present a contested interpretation of the triviality results as if it were the consensus, and flag where your own ranking is a matter of taste rather than a consequence of the proofs.
