I chair the naming and memorials committee of a mid-sized university's philosophy department, and I have to circulate a written opinion before we vote a week from Friday. The proposal on the table is to rename our annual endowed lecture in logic — currently the Frege Lecture — and, as a separate matter, to decide whether the department should attach a standing note to that lecture and to the graduate survey in which Frege is taught. I need you to draft that opinion for me.

The relevant facts are not in dispute, though I'll state them because the memo has to be usable by people who know less about Frege than I do. Late in his life Frege kept a private diary, and in it he wrote about Jews, about democracy, and about the German nation in terms that are contemptible, including what amounts to a crude political program. The diary surfaced among his papers and was published decades after his death, long after his technical work had become canonical. So the man who insisted that the laws of logic are laws of truth, and that thoughts belong to a realm independent of any thinker, also held a politics of ethnic resentment and national decline.

The committee keeps collapsing three questions into one, and I want the memo to hold them apart: first, whether the diary is evidence about the content of the philosophy — whether his anti-psychologism, his objectivism about logic, or his account of the laws of truth are implicated, tainted, or left untouched; second, whether it is evidence about his epistemic character, and what, if anything, follows for how we teach his arguments; third, whether honoring a person is a distinct question from reading him, and if so whether a name on an endowment can be separated from a name on a syllabus. I have read enough rationalist blogs to distrust two reflexive moves — that a bad person's arguments are thereby defeated, and that a thinker's private life is simply irrelevant to his work — so I want the memo to say precisely what would follow if the diary did bear on the philosophy and what would follow if it did not.

Please draft the opinion I'll circulate. It needs a concrete recommendation on the renaming, a separate recommendation on the note and its content, and a time frame we can actually execute given that the endowment terms may require donor consent and the next lecture is in the spring. Give the reasoning in a form the committee can argue with, state the strongest case for the position I end up rejecting as fairly as I would want it stated for me, and include criteria general enough to govern the next such case rather than this one only.
