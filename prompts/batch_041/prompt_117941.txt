Draft the joint statement that a committed Austrian in the tradition of Mises and Hayek and a committed market socialist in the tradition of Lange and Lerner could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims both accept about what their dispute is and is not about, including which of each other's objections they now grant; the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would sign off on; and the cruxes, each paired with a case or observation both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I expect one crux to be whether the planner's obstacle is acquiring dispersed knowledge or motivating its honest use, and another to be whether a price system's job is to clear given demands or to discover unimagined ones -- but do not treat those as exhaustive. To keep the exercise concrete, require both parties to assess a mechanism neither has treated: an allocation authority that each quarter solves a linear programme over final-goods demands, posts the resulting shadow prices as the only prices for intermediate goods, collects firms' reported input coefficients, and permits firms to trade deviations from their assigned inputs at those posted prices, while consumer goods, occupational choice, and wages remain free and the investment rate is fixed by the authority. Have each side say what this would and would not deliver, whether their disagreement over whether it counts as a market is merely verbal, and what evidence would move it. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
