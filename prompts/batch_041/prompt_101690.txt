My opening move: a labmate of mine has an electrode array in her visual cortex. With the current on she reports a red cube sitting on the desk in front of her, edges and shading and all, in the same words and with the same confidence she uses when the current is off and she's reporting the actual mug thats really there. There is no red thing on the desk. Now flip it. One afternoon the current is on and there happens to be a red cube on the desk, but her eyes are shut and the implant is doing all the work — so she's hallucinating a red cube that is, by luck, exactly where a real one is. Identical report, identical confidence, identical everything, and in one case she perceives and in the other she doesn't, and from the inside she has no way to tell which is which. So if these two episodes are different in kind, the difference is invisible from where she's standing, and then I want to know what work 'different in kind' is doing. It can't be the evidence, because the evidence is the same. It can't be the experience, because if the experiences differed she could in principle notice, and she can't. And please don't tell me the difference is that in the good case there's a cube and a causal link — everyone grants that, the question is what any of that has to do with her.
