I've just moved into a job where basically my whole role is getting people to do stuff they wouldn't otherwise do — I write the fundraising emails, the landing pages, the subject lines, the whole thing. And then last month my sister told me I was being manipulative during an argument about our dad's care, which stung, partly because I don't think I was, but mostly because I couldn't say why not.

So here's the view I've landed on, and I'd like you to try to break it. Manipulation isn't fundamentally about lying, or about emotion, or about bad intentions. It's about answerability. If I could sit the person down afterwards and walk them through exactly what I did and why — the framing I picked, the urgency I manufactured, the thing I left out — and the influence would still stand up, then it was persuasion. If telling them would kill it, or make them feel used, then it was manipulation. Manipulation is influence that can't survive its own disclosure. A good teacher survives being asked why we're doing it this way; a cult recruiter doesn't.

What I want is what would count against this. Not the obvious "but all communication is framing" objection — I've heard that, I want the sharper stuff. Specifically: what does this view get wrong that a plainer deception-plus-exploiting-bias account gets right; is there such a thing as honest manipulation, where I announce that I'm manipulating you and you go along anyway, and if so has my account just quietly reclassified it as persuasion; does the disclosure test make manipulation a fact about the target's feelings rather than about the act, so that something can be manipulation even though the person would never find out and wouldn't have minded; and what does my view predict that its rivals don't — or has it just been built so nothing can touch it?

Rank whatever you give me from "should make me drop this" down to "mildly annoying," tell me which of them someone who actually holds this view would have to concede, and give me a straight verdict on whether I'm just dressing up don't be a hypocrite in fancier clothes. And if theres a case I might realistically run into at work that should change my mind, I'd rather have that than the abstract stuff.
