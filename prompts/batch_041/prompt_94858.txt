ok so I'm taking this phil of science/religion course and we just did fine-tuning, and I've realized my gut about when a coincidence is evidence of some unseen agent/mechanism and when it isn't is a mess. Here's what I actually think, going case by case:

A hundred expert marksmen all miss me at once -- strong evidence someone ordered them to miss, not luck. A puddle waking up in a hole and concluding the hole was made for it -- no evidence of a hole-maker, it'd fit whatever hole it was in. A shuffled deck coming out in perfect suit order -- evidence of a trick, even though every order is equally likely. Me existing, given the millions of sperm -- not evidence my parents intended *me* specifically. A random number generator printing my home address -- evidence someone messed with it. The constants being life-permitting -- some evidence for a designer or a multiverse, weak but real.

The pair I can't square is the marksmen vs. the sperm. In both cases I only get to notice the lucky outcome if the lucky outcome happens, so by that logic neither should count, but the marksmen case clearly does.

My tentative principle is something like: a coincidence is evidence of a hidden mechanism when the outcome was specified independently of the coincidence itself, and there's no selection process that would make the coincidence inevitable. Does that get all six? And what does it say about a case I keep going back and forth on -- suppose there's a universe-generator that spits out universes at every possible setting of the constants, but only the life-permitting ones last long enough to contain anyone who can look around. Does that kill the fine-tuning inference entirely, or just move it up a level? Which of my verdicts does the principle force me to give up?
