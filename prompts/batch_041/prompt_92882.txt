I've been reading that late Wittgenstein material on certainty (On Certainty) and trying to sort out what I actually think about it, so I figured I'd just lay out my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and see if you can find the principle behind them, or tell me I'm inconsistent.

So here's the deal. If someone asks me how I know I have two hands, the question feels like it's gone wrong somehow. I want to say "look, here they are," but I also feel like holding them up isn't really giving a reason — it's more like showing you the thing you're already pretending to doubt. Same vibe with "the world didn't just pop into existence five minutes ago with all our memories baked in": I can't give you evidence for that, and I don't think I'm supposed to, and asking for evidence feels like a category mistake rather than a hard question.

But now "I've never been to Brazil." I'm as sure of this as I am of anything, I'd bet my life on it. And yet if you showed me a passport stamp and a photo and my mom said yeah we went when you were three, I'd believe you over my own memory. So it's not bedrock the same way the hands thing is, even though right now I can't really doubt it.

"Water boils at 212F at sea level" — confident, but it's the kind of thing that could turn out false and that would be totally fine, the world goes on, I just update.

"2 + 2 = 4" — feels more like the hands case than the water case, but I can also imagine being told I'd misunderstood something and that I was wrong about some piece of math, and that doesn't feel world-ending either. So I don't know where to put this one.

"My memory is mostly reliable" — if I doubted this, I couldn't check anything at all, because every check I run goes through memory. Doubting it seems to eat the very tool I'd use to doubt it.

And here's the pair that's really bugging me. "I have a brain in my skull" and "other people have minds." Both feel completely undeniable, I'd never seriously doubt either. But the brain one I could in principle get evidence for — a doctor could show me a scan, and if there was nothing there I'd have to revise something. The other-minds one I could never get evidence for either way, not really; any evidence would already presuppose it. So one of these feels like it's inside the game of evidence and the other feels like it's underneath the game, even though my confidence in both is basically 100%.

So here's the principle I keep reaching for: a proposition is bedrock for me when doubting it wouldn't just be hard, it would undercut the whole practice of giving and asking for reasons — so it's not that I know it with extra certainty, it's that it sits outside the justification game altogether, and the certainty is more like a way I act than a belief I hold.

Does that actually get all my verdicts? It seems to handle the hands case and the memory case, but the Brazil case is a problem, because I can't doubt it right now and yet it clearly sits inside the game — it's the kind of thing evidence could overturn. So either the principle is wrong or I have to give up my Brazil verdict, and I want to know which.

Two more things. First, if the principle is right, does it mean I don't really "know" I have hands? Because that feels flat-out wrong, I'd say I definitely know that. Second, tell me what the principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: someone who's utterly certain their spouse still loves them, not as a guess but as a thing they'd stake everything on, even though they've seen no sign one way or the other in years. Is that bedrock or just a very confident belief? And if the principle can't tell the difference there, say so and tell me what extra clause it needs.
