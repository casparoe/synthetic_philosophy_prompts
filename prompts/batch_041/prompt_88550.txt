Ok so I need help with something that's been bugging me for like two years, and I think it's a philosophy question but I'm a backend engineer with no formal background in any of this, so bear with me if I say something dumb or use the wrong words.

There's this argument I keep running into — on r/AskHistorians, in a Discord with a couple of archaeology grad students, and once in person at a museum talk — and it's always the same fight with the names swapped out. Did the Exodus happen. Did the Trojan War happen. Was there a real King Arthur. The two sides are always roughly this:

One side says: nobody invents something like that out of nothing, the stories have weirdly specific details that no one would make up, ancient writers didn't really do fiction the way we do, so there's got to be something under there. The other side says: you can't squeeze an event out of a story, the ground where it supposedly happened doesn't show it (there's a mound at Troy with destruction layers in it, sure, but nobody agrees which layer is *the* war, and for the other two there isn't even a site everyone agrees on), and anyway the burden of proof is on the person claiming it happened.

And then nobody moves. That's the part I can't get over. New digs come out and both sides just sort of absorb them into their existing story. Nobody's position has shifted in decades as far as I can tell, and these aren't stupid people.

Here's my hunch, and I want you to tell me if it's wrong: I don't think they're actually disagreeing about the evidence. I think they disagree about what a yes would even look like. Specifically, the pro side seems to assume that if it happened, it happened once — one exodus, one war, one king — so the job is to find *the* event behind the story. But that assumption might be doing all the work. The story could be an accretion of a bunch of small unrelated things that got welded together later, in which case did-it-happen has no clean yes/no answer even if every piece traces back to something real. And there's a second thing: once the story gets written down, the written version becomes the only thing anyone can argue about, so both sides end up fighting over a late snapshot of a moving target and calling it the original.

So what I want from you:

1. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal — are the two sides using the word happened in two different senses, one meaning an event occurred and the other meaning the story is a basically faithful record of an event, so they can both be right? Is it a hidden empirical question that a shovel could in principle settle? Is it about standards of evidence and burden of proof, where each side is applying a different threshold and neither can argue for their threshold from anything more basic? Or is it bedrock intuition all the way down, where one side just can't take seriously the idea that a whole people would build an identity on something that didn't occur?

2. Is this even one debate, or three debates wearing one hat? If Troy, Arthur and the Exodus have different structures — different kinds of source, different kinds of stake, different things counting as evidence — then maybe the thing that keeps stalling is that people are porting a diagnosis that works for one case onto the others.

3. Given whatever diagnosis you land on, what would actually count as progress here? A distinction, a change in the question, a kind of find, or nothing — maybe some of these are just permanently undecidable and the honest answer is to say so and stop pretending the next excavation will do it.

Please don't give me a reading list or a pile of book titles, I can't check any of them and it wouldn't help. I want the diagnosis with reasons, engaging with what the two sides actually say, not a survey of who wrote what. American English please. And don't dumb it down too much, I can handle it, I just don't have the vocabulary for it yet.
