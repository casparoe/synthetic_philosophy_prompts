ok so I'm supposed to write up my "position" on consciousness-and-ethics for a seminar and honestly I suspect half of it is incoherent, I just can't see where the seams are. Here's the whole package, warts and all:

(1) I'm a physicalist. No facts over and above the physical facts.
(2) I think panpsychism is probably right — roughly: physics only ever tells us how fundamental things relate to and dispose toward each other, never what they are in themselves, and the most plausible filler for that intrinsic nature is some very basic form of experience.
(3) I think what makes something morally considerable is just that it can suffer. Doesn't need to be smart, doesn't need a self-concept, doesn't need language. Suffering is the thing.
(4) I'm a conseqentialist and I think the point is to reduce the total amount of suffering in the universe.
(5) I think the experiences of fundamental particles, if there are any, are so faint and so simple that they just don't add up to anything morally worth worrying about.
(6) I think our current physics is roughly right about what the fundamental entities are — electrons, quarks, fields, whatever they turn out to be.

Please don't just tell me panpsychism is woo, assume it for the sake of the argument. What I actually want is: which of these pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, how bad each real conflict is, and what the cheapest revision is that restores consistency — plus what I'd be giving up to make it. And if one of these is doing sneaky work I haven't noticed, tell me which.
