For a term paper in my philosophy of mind course I am working on the colour phi phenomenon: when two differently coloured dots are flashed in rapid succession, subjects report a single dot travelling across the gap and changing colour abruptly about halfway along its path, a change that seems to be seen before the second dot is even presented. Dennett's treatment of this puzzle distinguishes two families of explanation — an Orwellian one, on which the subject first has an experience whose record is then revised in light of later input, and a Stalinesque one, on which the revision happens before the content ever becomes conscious. His further claim is that these are not genuinely competing hypotheses but two descriptions of the same underlying processing, and that asking which is correct — asking when the experience really happens — presupposes a Cartesian theatre in which contents are presented in order to a central observer.

Write an essay that assesses this controversy and defends a verdict. Is Dennett right that there is no fact of the matter about when the colour change is experienced, or can the timing question be posed and answered without smuggling in a Cartesian theatre? Engage the strongest case on each side — for instance, whether the two explanations make different predictions, and whether what seems to happen and what happens can be pulled apart — and end with a defended position rather than a survey.
