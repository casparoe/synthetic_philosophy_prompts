Okay so I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training at all, and I've gone down a rabbit hole I cant get out of. It started at work: our alerting is done by the same service that's suposed to tell us the service is healthy. So the thing that's supposed to catch a problem is itself one of the things that could be broken. Someone on my team called it "the thermometer problem" and now it's all I think about.

Here's the shape of it. I read a thermometer and it says 20C. Fine. But then I take a bunch of those readings and conclude "this thermometer is reliable." That second move feels like I'm using the thermometer to vouch for itself. And yet it also feels like thats just... what calibrating an instrument is? At some point you either trust the thing or you dont. Same with my memory: I use my memory to check whether my memory is any good. Same with the test suite at work that's supposed to validate the test suite.

I want a decision tree / flowchart that sorts people (including me) into the actual positions on whether this kind of easy knowledge is legit. But I dont want a list of famous philosophers with labels attached. I want the tree built out of questions a normal person can answer by looking at a concrete case, so you end up at a position because of what you say about the cases, not because of which name you like.

Some stuff I want the tree to actually get at:

- Whether the circular move is a *problem* at all, or just how justification works.
- Whether it matters that the person has no independent reason to trust the instrument, vs. the instrument just actually being reliable.
- Whether the bootstrapped belief is "the thermometer is reliable" or "it's 20 degrees outside" — does the problem bite the same on both?
- Whether it matters that the agent *couldnt* have gotten the belief some other way (like, if the only thermometer in the world is this one).
- Whether two identical uncalibrated instruments agreeing counts as evidence, or is just two things agreeing.
- Whether "knows" changes meaning between the everyday case and the "but what if the thermometer is lying" case.

The cases I want it to route through, at minimum: the single thermometer you've never calibrated; two identical thermometers that agree with each other; a scale you use to weigh a 1kg reference weight, then use that weight to check the scale; using your memory to audit your memory; and the CI pipeline that validates itself.

Positions I need to come out as leaves, at minimum: (1) the readings justify themselves, no prior reason needed, so this is all fine; (2) you need an independent reason to trust the instrument first, so the easy move is blocked; (3) if the process is in fact reliable then the belief is justified, full stop, and the agent doesnt need to see why; (4) it's fine because the belief just has to fit with everything else you believe, even if the fit is circular; (5) easy knowledge really is impossible and that's a bullet you just have to bite; (6) the whole thing is a verbal dispute about how strict "knows" is, and it dissolves once you fix the context.

If two leaves end up giving the same answer to every node, say so instead of pretending the tree separates them. Keep it to about 7 questions deep max and 7-ish leaves.
