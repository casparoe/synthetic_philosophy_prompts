I'm in this seminar on moral theory and we spent like two weeks on the view that the right thing to do is whatever would be agreed to by people who are fully rational, fully informed, and cared about everyone equally — like a hypothetical council of ideal deliberators that we obviously can't actually convene, but whose verdict is supposed to settle what's moral. I get the appeal of it, but here's my problem. My roommate keeps saying that in real life you're not supposed to actually try to do that — you're just supposed to follow ordinary rules like keep your promises and don't lie, and treat the whole ideal-council thing as a standard that you mostly don't directly use. That sounds to me like a cop-out, or maybe just circular, because if the ideal council's verdict is what makes something right, then the most right thing I could do is try to figure out their verdict and then do it, right? Anything else would be knowingly not doing the best thing. So the idea that I should just follow rules and not aim at the council's answer seems incoherent to me.

Concretely, I promised my friend I'd help her move this weekend, but I also have a chance to do something that seems like it has way more impartial value — I could spend those same hours volunteering at a thing that (I'm pretty sure) helps a much bigger number of people. My instinct is to try to mentally run the ideal council and see what they'd pick, but I honestly can't get a stable answer out of it — sometimes it feels like they'd obviously say keep the promise, because promise-keeping is the kind of thing that makes coordinated life possible at all, and other times it feels like they'd obviously say go volunteer, because the numbers are just bigger and they care about everyone equally. Its frustrating and I keep going back and forth.

So my question is basically two things. First, does the fact that I can't compute a determinate verdict here show that the whole idealized-agreement idea is empty, or at least that morality isn't really objective after all? And second, if it's not empty, what am I actually supposed to do with it on Sunday, since I have a real decision to make and can't just defer it forever?
