I'm a TA for a big intro course on the birth of toleration, and we just spent two weeks on Montaigne's essay about the cannibals. Every year the same discussion happens: someone says Montaigne was basically the first multiculturalist, so obviously he'd be against laïcité and the French laws that keep conspicuous religious signs out of public schools, and against the ban on face-covering veils in public. And every year I feel like thats too easy — the whole point of the cannibals essay is that he uses a foreign culture to make his own country look bad, which isn't the same thing as saying every custom is as good as every other, and he was also a magistrate and a Catholic who cared a lot about civil peace during the wars of religion. So I want a proper reconstruction, not the bumper sticker.

Here's the concrete thing I want you to work on. Imagine the French education ministry puts out a new circular (this part is my invention, don't go looking for it): public schools must stay strictly neutral in matters of religion, no conspicuous religious signs on students or staff, but at the same time the same circular requires every school to run a cultures of the world unit teaching pupils that all cultural and religious traditions are equally worthy of respect and that no culture may be judged by another culture's standards. So one document tells teachers to keep religion out of the school and to celebrate all religions equally, and plenty of people think those two instructions can't both be followed. Suppose Montaigne is somehow given a seat on the committee that has to decide whether to sign off on this circular, and he has to put his opinion in writing.
