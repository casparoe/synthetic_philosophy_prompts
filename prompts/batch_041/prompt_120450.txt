I haven't read any of the philosophy here, I only know my own end of it. My gut says I've seen this film: in factor analysis the loadings are only identified up to a rotation, so whatever factors you end up reporting are partly a product of the constraint you imposed to make the solution unique, and everyone shrugs and moves on. There's also the older indeterminacy result where even with the loadings fixed the factor scores aren't uniquely pinned down. Nobody concludes from that that factors are fake. They conclude you have to be explicit about which quantity you actually care about.

So: restate the main positions in the interpretability debate -- the realist, the instrumentalist, and the deflationist "you're all arguing about a choice of basis" one -- as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary.
