Please set out the most important implications of Sen's entitlement approach to famine for how a government ought to respond to a drought that has left the national food supply largely intact. Suppose the affected region contains smallholders who sell grain, landless labourers and pastoralists who must buy it, and suppose the government is weighing price controls on staples against a ban on grain moving between districts; explain what the approach implies about whose command over food collapses under each policy, and why aggregate food-availability statistics can be actively misleading. Finally, consider whether the approach entitles us to conclude that famines are political crimes rather than natural disasters, or whether that inference needs qualification.
