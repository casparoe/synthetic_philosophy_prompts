Two people in my department have been arguing for about a year about whether things persist by being wholly present at each moment or by having temporal parts (endurantism vs perdurantism, though they both hate the labels), and I've spent every Thursday seminar stuck in the middle of it. I want to force them to write the document they should have written a year ago: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration that both of them could actually put their names to. The agreed-claims part has to be substantive — not "we both agree things persist" — I want what they can both sign about what the question even is, about what a theory of persistence owes us, and about which of the standard moves are not by themselves decisive: the temporary-intrinsics argument and the Leibniz's-law reasoning about change; the vagueness argument for unrestricted composition; the fact that the relativity of simultaneity rules out a privileged present but doesn't obviously touch endurance as such; and the suspicion that "momentary stages with temporal counterparts" is just perdurantism in different notation rather than a genuine third way. And the disagreement statements have to be worded so that each side would accept the wording of its own position — no strawmen, no "so you think nothing really changes."

Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. I'm fairly sure one is whether "wholly present" can be defined without begging the question against temporal parts, which may make part of this whole fight verbal, and I'd guess another is whether the vagueness arguement goes through. But tell me if I've missed one — something about instantaneous total replacement, or about what either side can say about de re modality for a thing that persists. For each crux, give me the case or argument both of them would agree bears on it, each side's prediction written down in advance, and what would actually change each side's mind about its own view. They've already agreed to talk about three cases: the department mug that gets chipped, glued, and (hypothetically) has every one of its parts swapped in a single instant; an amoeba that splits in two and whose halves later fuse back into one; and a thing that exists at t1 and at t3 but not at t2. If they'd agree that some case is irrelevant to a crux, say so and why. Don't bother with a reading list — I know the literature exists, I want the content.

Format: a numbered agreed-claims section, then a table with these columns: Crux | Sub-question stated neutrally | Endurantist's statement of the disagreement, in wording they'd accept | Perdurantist's statement, in wording they'd accept | Shared case or argument bearing on it | Endurantist's prediction | Perdurantist's prediction | What would move each side | Genuine or verbal. In the "stated neutrally" column, don't use the words "part" or "whole" at all — I want to see whether the sub-question can even be put that way. Then, after the table, a short section on any residue you think is purely verbal, and a short one listing the places where you had to make a judgement call about what one side's view actually is, so they can push back on it.
