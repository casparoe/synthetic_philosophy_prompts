I'm trying to sort out my own views on non-human causes in history and I keep contradicting myself, so I want the principle behind my verdicts, if there is one. Also I dont want to end up in the climate-explains-everything camp, but I also don't want to say only humans are real causes.

The 1815 Tambora eruption causing the 1816 crop failures in Europe: yes, obviously the volcano did that. Tambora causing New England farm families to pack up and move to Ohio: no, that feels like the volcano getting credit for a decision people were already making. Smallpox and the other introduced diseases causing the demographic catastrophe in the Americas: yes, and I think they did more work than the conquistadors did. But the way people put it, that germs conquered the Americas, bothers me -- not because the causal claim is wrong but because it makes the whole thing sound automatic when the timing and severity were wildly different from place to place. Yellow fever destroying Napoleon's army in Haiti: yes, that's what happened. Yellow fever winning Haitian independence: no. The Black Death ending serfdom in western Europe: yes, labor got scarce and peasants suddenly had leverage. The same plague making serfdom stronger in eastern Europe: I know that happened, and I still want to keep the previous verdict. Bad weather in the late 1780s causing the French Revolution: no -- my impression is the weather was lousy all over Europe and the revolution happened in one country. Drought emptying a particular Maya city: probably yes. Drought causing the Maya collapse: no.

The pattern I half-see is that a non-human thing can cause what happens to a population -- dying, starving, a city emptying out -- but not what a population does -- revolting, migrating, inventing an institution. Is there a version of that which gets all of the above, or close, and what does each bit of it rest on? Does the plague verdict survive it, or is that the one I have to give up? And tell me what to do with the actor talk: is calling a volcano or a virus a historical actor just shorthand for a causal claim, or does it say something the causal claim doesn't? Then apply whatever you end up with to a case I haven't mentioned, say the 1918 flu and the mess at Versailles.
