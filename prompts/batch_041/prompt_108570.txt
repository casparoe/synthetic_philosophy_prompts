I'm in a small philosophy of mind reading group — five of us, grad students, we meet in someone's kitchen — and we spent this term on panpsychism. I'm mostly persuaded that experience goes all the way down, but the ethics is where the conversation keeps dying and I can't get past it. If the microphysical has experience, either that experience has valence or it doesn't, and I genuinely can't tell which option is worse: if it does, then a particle collider is the largest atrocity there has ever been and the heat death of the universe is a mercy; if it doesn't, then nothing at all follows from panpsychism and the view is ethically inert. Ana thinks there's a third possibility — experience that no one is having, pain with nobody in pain — which she says is either incoherent or the worst idea she's ever heard. Ravi says the real trouble is that on this view there's no fact about where one subject stops and the next begins, so moral status comes in vague degrees and never lands anywhere. My mother died in March and I've been trying to write something for her, and the only honest thing I can think to say at a funeral on this view is that she didn't stop, she just stopped being one.

What I want out of this is a sestina, and I want the form to do the arguing rather than the sentences. Give me six end-words that each have to carry a microphysical sense and an experiential one at the same time; I've been fiddling with field, grain, count, fold, one, again, but swap out any that don't earn there keep and tell me why the final six do. The envoi has to gather all six into its three lines, and I want that forced gathering to be the moment the poem tries to reassemble her out of the parts and fails — the form demanding a combination the philosophy can't deliver. Don't let it console. The speaker is a person at a kitchen table, not a lecturer: no naming of problems, no philosophers' names, no citations, no reading list, nothing I couldn't say out loud to my friends. Someone who has read the literature should be able to tell exactly which problem each turn is tracking without being told. Before you write, spend a while deciding on the six words and on where the turn of the sestina should fall; write as a poet who happens to know this literature cold, not as a philosopher who has decided to try poetry.

After the poem, give me a short table with these columns: the line or stanza or device; the idea it's tracking, in your own words; why that device does the work rather than just decorating; and where you think the mapping is a stretch or a cheat. Then a JSON object with these fields: end_words (an array of objects, each with word, its microphysical sense, its experiential sense), envoi_move (one or two sentences on what the envoi is doing), and cant_reproduce (the one thing about the ethics of pan-sentience that the sestina can't get at, and what you'd have to say in prose instead).
