An essay I read summarizes Daniel Dennett's heterophenomenology roughly as follows: the method tells the scientist to collect a subject's verbal reports about her experience, treat those reports as just another stream of behavior, and then explain the behavior that produces them without ever having to settle whether there is anything it is like to be the subject. Because the reports are recorded but treated as fictions, they carry no evidential weight; the view is a sophisticated behaviorism that dissolves the hard problem instead of answering it. And since nothing a subject could say or do would count against the theory, heterophenomenology is unfalsifiable — which is why the essay concludes it is not really an empirical proposal at all.
