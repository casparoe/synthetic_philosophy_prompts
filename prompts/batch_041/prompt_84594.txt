I design typefaces, and I work mostly in the variable-font idiom. In my head a letter is a set of compatible outlines laid out in a designspace: a handful of masters at the corners, axes running between them — weight, width, optical size, slant, and now and then a custom axis of my own devising — so that any point inside the space interpolates an instance and any point outside it extrapolates. My working furniture is masters, axes, instances, deltas, contour and point compatibility, the default instance, and the recurring judgement call about which axes are worth exposing to a user at all.

I would like you to take the philosophical question of what makes every 'a' an 'a' — the question about letterform categories, letter identity, and the place of style — and restate the main positions on it in my vocabulary, one at a time, as faithfully as the translation permits. Take at least these four: that there is an essence or abstract schema of the 'a' which each concrete 'a' instantiates; that 'a' is nothing but a convention or a usage, fixed by history rather than by any property of the shape; that the category has no single necessary feature but is held together by family resemblance, with central and peripheral members; and that a letter is defined by its role in a system of contrasts, so that 'a' is what it is only against 'b', 'o', 'd', and the rest. Where a position has a natural home in my framework, say precisely where it lands — I suspect the essence view becomes something like a designspace invariant, but tell me if that is a fudge rather than a translation.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually want. Divide it three ways: what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Use concrete cases as the test bench and say what my framework is forced to say about each: the default instance; extrapolation past the ends of an axis, where the machinery manufactures shapes no designer ever drew; a hypothetical axis whose two extremes are a double-storey 'a' and a single-storey 'a'; and the possibility of interpolating one glyph into another letter altogether. I especially want to know whether my framework can distinguish an extreme stylistic choice from a mistake, and whether it can say of some axis that it is illegitimate as a dimension of style rather than merely unused.

Close by telling me whether the residue is a defect of my vocabulary — something a font engineer ought to be able to say and cannot — or a sign that the original question was doing less work than it appeared to, or was about practice and normativity all along. I do not need citations or a reading list; I want the translation and the accounting, not the literature.
