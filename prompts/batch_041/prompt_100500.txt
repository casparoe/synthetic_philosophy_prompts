I work in health economics — cost-effectivness analysis, QALYs, ICERs, cost per life-year gained, that whole apparatus. I keep bumping into the procreation asymmetry (roughly: it's bad to bring into existence someone whose life goes badly, but not good to bring into existence someone whose life goes well, and there's no duty to create happy people) and I want to know how much of it I can actually say in the vocabulary I use every day. So: restate the asymmetry as faithfully as you can in my terms — health states, the comparator or baseline, QALY gains and losses, and the fact that every model I build has a fixed population of people already in it — and then give me the residue report. What survives the translation cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no slot at all? For instance, can "bringing a person into existence" even be represented as a health state or a QALY gain, or does the additive way QALYs get summed across people quietly presuppose that the set of people is given? And does the person-affecting flavour of the asymmetry — that the good of a good life only counts for someone who exists to receive it — have any counterpart in a framework that just totals health across whoever happens to be in the model? I'd like to know whether the leftover is a blind spot in cost-effectiveness analysis or a sign that the asymmetry is answering a question my framework never asks.
