Some friends and I run a little decision-making discussion night and we've been stuck on the same fight for months, and at this point I want to know what kind of fight it actually is.

Background: we started with the dollar auction — the one where a dollar bill goes to the highest bidder but the second-highest bidder also has to pay whatever they bid. So everyone bids past a dollar, someone ends up paying $1.60 for a buck, the runner-up pays $1.50 and gets nothing, and neither of them was obviously stupid at any single step, because once you're in for $1.40, bidding $1.50 is the move that loses you the least. We then tried to generalize to real escalations — the war that keeps going because of the dead already lost, the software project everyone privately knows is doomed but nobody wants to be the one who killed it, the lawsuit that settles for more than the claim is worth because of the fees already sunk into it, the two companies undercutting each other until neither makes money.

And this is where we split, and nobody has moved since.

One side says this is just the sunk cost fallacy with extra steps — a bias, a failure of the agent's reasoning, and the dollar auction is a vivid demonstration of how the bias gets you trapped. The other side says no, look at the structure: inside the auction, continuing is *rational*, each bid is the least-bad option given the bids already on the table, so calling it a bias is a category mistake — the trap is in the game, not in the head. They also like to point out that an agent who fully *sees* the trap still can't get out once they're in it, so where is the irrationality even supposed to live?

My own hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than just nod along to: the two camps aren't disagreeing about the facts or even about the psychology, they're answering different questions under one word. One side is asking "is this agent reasoning well?", the other is asking "is this a situation where reasoning well leads you off a cliff?" — and both can be true at once, so the whole thing is two questions wearing one coat, maybe even a verbal dispute.

What I want from you: give me a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this verbal, is it substantive, is it a disagreement about the unit of analysis (the single decision vs the whole trajectory vs the game that frames them), or is one side quietly smuggling in a normative standard the other rejects? If my hypothesis is wrong, say exactly how. And be honest about whether anything could actually settle it — a formal result, a sharper distinction, an experiment — or whether it just stays open forever because the two sides want different things out of the word "rational." Don't survey the escalation literature at me, I've read enough of it; I want the shape of the disagreement and what would count as progress.
