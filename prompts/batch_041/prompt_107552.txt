I work in AI safety. Here's something I keep watching happen and I've never found the word for it, and I don't think it's just the alignment tax.

Someone proposes a precaution — don't ship until the red team signs off, don't build the plant without the scrubber. A critic says: fine, but whoever skips that moves faster and the careful ones lose, so the precaution is a tax and it won't get paid. I know that move. The part I can't name is what comes after.

The tax gets treated like a number nature handed down. But it's not a number, it's a gap — how far ahead the fastest skipper gets — and that gap depends on how many people are skipping, which depends on how many people think the tax is big. So if enough people believe the precaution is unaffordable, it becomes unaffordable: a price made partly out of other people's estimates of the price. And it never bottoms out, because even if everyone adopts the precaution, the precaution is now just the baseline and the skipper is still ahead. What's being bought is position, not outcome.

I know the neighborhood — race to the bottom, tragedy of the commons, prisoner's dilemma, arms races — but those all have everyone ending up worse off, plus a cooperative fix they'd all prefer. Here nobody's choosing a bad outcome over a good one; they're just refusing to be the one who gives up the lead, and any treaty seems to just move the compeition somewhere else. Is there a name for this, or does it need one? Either way I want the carving more than the label: when is it a coordination problem that a norm can actually solve, and when is it a pure selection effect with nothing to coordinate, because the thing people want — being ahead — isn't divisible?
