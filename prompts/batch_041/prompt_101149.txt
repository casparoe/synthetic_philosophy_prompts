Last night a friend and I argued for like three hours about embedded agency and I came away genuinely unsure whether we disagree at all. Here's the shape of it. I say the standard setup -- an agent with a policy or utility function, plus an environment it's cleanly separated from, joined at a well-defined interface -- is a bookkeeping choice, and the boundary is wherever you the modeler find it convenient to draw; as long as the thing predicts, who cares. He says no, that's the whole mistake: real agents are embedded, the boundary isn't handed to them from outside but maintained by the agent itself, and treating it as exogenous quietly bakes in assumptions that fail exactly where we care -- self-modifying agents, agents that reshape their own environment, agents whose environment includes their own body and memory. When I ask what observation would show my setup is wrong, he says it's not about prediction, it's about what an agent is; when he asks why I'd want to derive the boundary from scratch, I say because otherwise it's a free parameter and free parameters explain nothing. The people who try to get the boundary out of something like a Markov blanket, or out of the agent's own self-maintenance, look like they're on his side, but I honestly can't tell if that's a real alternative or just relabling. So: is this a verbal fight about the word model, a disguised formal question (is there actually a class of embedded agents that exogenous-boundary MDPs provably can't capture?), or do we just have different senses of what an explanation of agency even has to do? My own guess is we're answering two questions under one name, but I want a real diagnosis grounded in what each side actually claims, and a straight answer about what would count as progress here -- a theorem, a distinction, or nothing.
