Been messing around with interpretabillity on small open models, and there's a pattern I keep hitting that I dont have a word for. I train a linear probe on a middle layer for some property -- whether the current token sits inside a string literal, whether the sentence so far contains an odd number of negations, something like that -- and it comes out at 95%+. But then I ablate that direction, or steer along it, and the model's behaviour barely moves. The information is plainly legible to the probe, but the rest of the network doesn't seem to be using it. Then the mirror image happens too: I patch a single activation or swap a direction and the whole output flips, yet no probe I can train at that layer predicts the property at all. So "the network has it" and "the network uses it" come apart in both directions, and I'm no longer sure those are even the same axis. Is there an established name for this, or a name for each half of it, and if the field only has a word for one of the two, what would you call the other? I don't really care about the label, I care about where the line falls. When is a probe reading a representation the model actually has, versus decoding a correlation that only exists because the probe gets to look at the whole layer at once -- and how would I ever tell those apart from the outside? And in the unused case, is there any sense in which the model represents the property at all, or is that just a fact about my probe?
