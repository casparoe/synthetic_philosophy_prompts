I'm about 60k words into a heist novel and I've hit a scene that two of my writing friends have been arguing about for a week, and I've been refereeing without being able to tell who's actually got the better of it. I need you to settle it.

Here's the setup I wrote. Vic and Rene are two safecrackers. Every night for a week they crack a different vault, and every morning they split the take. But at every stop each of them can bolt with the whole haul. If one bolts and the other doesn't, the bolter walks with everything and the other gets nothing. If both bolt, they each grab a little. If neither bolts, they split it evenly. So for each of them, being the only one to bolt is best, splitting is second, both bolting is third, and getting left behind is worst. Classic prisoner's dilemma, except it runs seven nights and they both know it's exactly seven.

Friend A, who plays alot of poker, says the scene is broken. A rational Vic knows there's no tomorrow on night seven, so both bolt that night, so night seven is already decided, so night six is just like night seven, and so on all the way back — by backward induction they bolt on night one and my whole "these two learn to trust each other" arc is a fantasy. He says if I want the trust to be real I have to make them irrational or change the game.

Friend B, who writes literary fiction, says that's a story about two calculators, not two people. She says the backward induction only goes through if it's common knowledge that both of them are perfectly rational, both know the payoffs cold, both know that they know, and so on forever — and that's never actually true of people. She says a real Vic has no idea whether Rene is a hard case or a soft one, and once you drop even a little doubt in there, bolting on night one stops being obviously best, because the early nights are worth something as information and as trust you're building for the later ones.

Friend A fires back: fine, but the second you open the door to doubting the other guy's rationality you get a regress — Vic doubts Rene, Rene doubts Vic's doubt, and you never get a determinate answer at all, so "they might cooperate" is just you smuggling in the ending you wanted.

Friend B says the regress is the interesting part, and anyway the prediction isn't "they might cooperate," it's that what they do depends on what each thinks the other thinks, all the way up — which is exactly what she wants a scene to be about.

Then A said something I didn't follow about how if I just make the last night uncertain — neither of them knows it's seven nights, or the job could get called off any night — then cooperation suddenly becomes sustainable, and he acted like that proved his original point rather than hers. She said it proved the opposite.

Can you go through this move by move and tell me who's right about what? What I actually need to know is whether my scene can stand as written or whether I have to change the setup, and if I do, which change is the one actually doing the work. And be straight with me about how confident you are in what you're telling me — flag the one point in all this that you're least sure about, because I'd rather see the soft spot than get a smooth answer that falls apart when someone smarter reads it.
