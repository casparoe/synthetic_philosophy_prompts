I have been trying to state a dilemma that a colleague calls the crane dilemma, and I cannot decide whether it is a genuine proof that non-reductive physicalism is incoherent or an unusually polished piece of greedy reductionism. The argument is this. (1) Every real difference is a physical difference: if two worlds agree in their fundamental physical facts, no higher-level fact can differ between them. (2) Therefore any higher-level property—being a belief, being an adaptation, being a recession—supervenes on the fundamental physical facts. (3) My colleague calls this the inheritance principle: if a higher-level property supervenes on the physical facts, then whatever causal work it appears to do is inherited from the physical facts, and it is not an additional causal difference-maker. (4) If it is not an additional causal difference-maker, it does no causal work of its own. (5) If it does no causal work of its own, then citing it in a causal explanation is either a convenient shorthand for the physical facts or a mistake. (6) But some higher-level explanations are not shorthand: evolutionary explanations cite selection, heritable variation, and differential reproduction rather than particle trajectories, and no physical translation we could actually give would preserve their generality, their counterfactual robustness, and their predictive power. (7) So either those explanations are mistakes and the properties they cite are unreal, or those properties are additional causal powers after all. (8) Non-reductive physicalism was supposed to be the third option: higher-level properties are real and explanatory without being additional fundamental causes. (9) Therefore non-reductive physicalism is incoherent. My colleague concludes that the only consistent positions are eliminativism and some form of emergentism or dualism, and that anyone who talks about cranes rather than skyhooks is merely refusing to choose. I would rather keep (6) and (8) than (7), but I do not know what that costs. Which step should I reject, why did it look plausible, and what must I give up if I reject it?
