I run a small decision-theory reading group, and next month's guest argues two connected claims: that so-called acausal threats — threats meant to influence a predictor's earlier choice through correlation rather than causation — can be both effective and rationally permissible; and that a rational agent may deliberately adopt a surrogate goal she does not intrinsically care about, precisely because holding it makes such threats credible. Draft six to eight interview questions that get past having her restate the view. Include at least one that presses the sincerity problem: if merely appearing to have adopted the surrogate goal suffices for the predictor to condition on it, the goal looks idle, whereas if genuine adoption is required, the strategy seems to demand a self-manipulation a rational agent might refuse. Include one about what happens when two predictors each offer to condition on your choice, so that the resulting threats conflict, and one about how an agent could know she belongs to the class of agents whose choices the predictor's prediction actually tracks. Include one pressing the line between an acausal threat and a bare prediction of retaliation. Give every question a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and close with what would have to be true for her to give up the view. If my framing misdescribes the position — say, if the term "surrogate goal" already entails the agent's insincerity, which would make the sincerity problem a non-starter — tell me plainly rather than writing questions around it.
