I've come round to moral particularism of the holist sort: a feature that is a reason for an action in one situation can be no reason at all, or a reason against, in another, so there are no true exceptionless moral principles. Tell me what would count against it. I don't mean the familiar charge that this makes moral judgement arbitrary — I mean the observations, arguments, or formal results that should actually move someone who holds the view. Could a feature ever be shown to carry a fixed valence across every context, and if so, would that refute holism or would the particularist simply redraw the context and absorb the counterexample? Is there anything the position predicts that a generalism armed with ceteris paribus hedges does not? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, note which a committed particularist would themselves concede as counting against them, and say honestly whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability — and whether that is a defect for a view of this kind.
