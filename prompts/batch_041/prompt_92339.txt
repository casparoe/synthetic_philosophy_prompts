I hold a three-year postdoctoral fellowship and one collaborator, a proof theorist, and I intend to spend both on paraconsistent logic and dialetheism. My aim is not to add another entry to the zoo of systems but to produce work that a skeptical classical logician would have to count as progress. I would like you to tell me which open problems in this area most deserve that time, in what order, and why.

Here is my own shortlist, stated loosely so that you can correct me if I have misdescribed any item:

1. The Curry-style paradox, which appears to derive an arbitrary conclusion from a sentence asserting its own conditional and which, unlike the liar, does not turn on negation — so it cannot be defused by weakening negation, and each repair seems to breed a new paradox aimed at the repair itself.
2. The recapture problem: if classical logic is weakened to tolerate contradiction, one still needs all the classical reasoning back in consistent domains such as ordinary mathematics, and it is unclear how to get it back without surrendering the tolerance.
3. The problem of exclusion negation: a negation that tolerates a sentence being both true and false cannot straightforwardly express the exclusive not, the sense in which negating a claim rules the claim out rather than merely failing to affirm it.
4. Non-triviality and its metatheory: proving that the theory does not entail everything, and whether such a proof can be given without a metatheory that already assumes the classical principles under dispute.
5. The spread of contradictions: even where explosion is blocked, a contradiction may still infect every sentence built from the same vocabulary, so the damage is less local than one wants.
6. The tension between dialetheism as a thesis about reality — that some contradictions are true — and the pluralism about logical systems that the proliferation of paraconsistent logics invites; if choice of logic is pragmatic, it is unclear what remains of the metaphysical claim.
7. The doxastic and normative question: what it is to believe, assert, or act on a contradiction, and whether such acceptance is rationally permissible.
8. Applied reasoning: whether conflicting legal norms, inconsistent databases, and inconsistent mathematical theories genuinely require paraconsistent consequence or are better served by classical logic plus a bookkeeping layer.

Please rank these for someone in my position, and defend the ranking rather than presenting the options. For each item, say how it stands to the others — which would be settled, or made substantially easier, if another were solved first — how tractable it is for a philosopher who can lean on a proof theorist, and what sort of result over the next two years would move it up or down the list. State the criteria you are using and how you trade them off against one another. Tell me which of my candidates you would drop, and name the one or two problems I have left off that belong near the top, with your reasons. I do not need a reading list; I need an agenda. Take a definite position: give me one ordering and argue for it, and say where your confidence in it is lowest and what evidence would change it. If you think I have misdescribed the purpose I have fixed, say so and rerank under the purpose you think I should have.
