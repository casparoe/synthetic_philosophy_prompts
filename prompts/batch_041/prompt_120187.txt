Question:

(a) The Szilard engine is one particle in a box; a demon measures which half it's in, slides in a partition, and lets the particle push a piston, extracting kT ln 2 of work. Explain why this looks like it pulls work out of a single heat bath, and explain how the information-theoretic accounting restores the second law.

(b) A student proposes a variant she calls the "packrat demon": it never erases its measurement record. Each cycle it writes the new result onto a fresh cell of a tape that is never overwritten. She argues that since nothing is ever erased, Landauer's bound never applies, so the packrat demon really does pull net work out of a single heat bath, cycle after cycle. Is she right? Do the entropy bookkeeping for the whole gas + bath + memory system.

Student's answer:

"(a) The Szilard engine gets kT ln 2 of work per cycle out of a single reservoir, which would violate the second law. The fix is due to Bennett: the demon has to record which side the particle was on, and writing down that bit is an irreversible act that costs kT ln 2. So the measurement burns exactly the work the engine produced and the books balance. This is Landauer's principle — any logically irreversible operation, like measurement, dissipates at least kT ln 2 per bit, so no information-processing trick can beat the second law.

(b) The packrat demon is a real loophole. Landauer's bound only kicks in when you erase information, and a tape that is never overwritten never erases anything, so the bound never bites. It can therefore extract kT ln 2 per cycle from a single bath forever. What this really shows is that the second law isn't a law of physics at all, just a statement about how much memory you're willing to spend."
