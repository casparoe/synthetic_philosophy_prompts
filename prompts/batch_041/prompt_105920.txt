My thesis, roughly: the Fermi paradox is a framing mistake. Everyone asks "where is everybody?" — the spatial question. I think the real question is temporal: "when is everybody?" Which means the silence is exactly what my model predicts, and the right conclusion is that we're probably alone in *time*, not alone in space. I've got a little toy model behind it: treat detectability as a Poisson process with a per-civilization loud lifetime, and show the overlap probability comes out tiny over the galaxy's history.
