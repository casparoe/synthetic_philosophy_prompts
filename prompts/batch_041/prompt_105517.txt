The episode, in case you need it: around 1900–1904 a retired Berlin schoolteacher, Wilhelm von Osten, exhibited a horse, Hans, who appeared to add, subtract, multiply, tell the time, spell and so on, tapping out answers with a hoof. Big public sensation. A commission of thirteen worthies convened by Stumpf looked into it and reported that no trickery was involved. Then Pfungst ran systematic trials: isolating horse and questioner from spectators, using questioners other than von Osten, using blinders to vary whether Hans could see the questioner, and varying whether the questioner knew the answer in advance. Hans was about 89% correct when he could see a questioner who knew the answer, and about 6% (2 of 35) when he couldn't see the questioner. The cue was the questioner's own body — mounting tension in posture and face as the taps approached the right number, released on the correct tap. Pfungst got the same tell out of human subjects he had stand in for the horse, catching a sudden slight upward jerk of the head. Von Osten never accepted any of it. And there's a wrinkle I like: critics have pointed out Pfungst never clinched the body-language story by deliberately giving the cues himself to drive Hans to wrong answers.

Now the theory: realism about interpretability — whether the features, probes and circuits we find are the network's actual internal representations or artifacts of the tool and the researcher. I'd like the anti-realist's worry kept in separate flavors rather than blended: (a) the probe or autoencoder is itself a fitted model, so we're reading the probe, not the network, and the structure we "find" is what our search over hypotheses was built to find (the standard remedies being control tasks, selectivity, capacity matching); (b) the causal version — a property can be decodable from an activation while the network doesn't use it, which is why people do amnesic/ablation interventions instead of correlations; (c) the visualization version — feature viz is optimized to excite a unit, so of course it looks like something, and the question is whether the something belongs to the unit or to the optimizer; (d) the maximalist version, the adversarial-examples-are-features-not-bugs line, which is a realism claim if anything is.
