My sister's been dating this guy for maybe eight months and she's definately sure he's serious, mostly because of what he's given up for her: he quit a job he'd had for a couple of years, moved across the country to her city, sold the motorbike he was always tinkering with to cover her rent for two months while she was between jobs, and told basically everyone he knows that he was moving for her. Her argument is: nobody does all that for someone they're not all in on, and the fact that it hurt him is exactly what makes it mean something — if it had been easy, it wouldn't prove anything. My worry runs the other way. None of it seems to have cost *him* that much: he'd been complaining about the job for a year, he doesn't really have a career or a plan, and as far as I can tell he didn't have anywhere else he was choosing between. So I keep thinking the sacrifices show he had nothing to lose, not that he loves her — and that the burned bridges are actually the reason he'll stay, not any feeling he has about her. She says I'm being a cynic. Is there a principled way to tell whether a sacrifice like that is genuine evidence of the thing it's supposed to be evidence of, versus just the cheapest move available to someone with no other options? And does it matter whether the cost is the kind a faker could just as easily pay, or whether the cost is one that gets *lighter* the more you actually mean it? Also does the fact that he announced it to everyone change anything, or is that just noise? I'd rather not get "every relationship is different" — I want to know what would actually settle it.
