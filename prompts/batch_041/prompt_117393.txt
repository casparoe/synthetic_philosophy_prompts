ok so for week 6 i want to do something other than the usual readings discussion, and i need a handout. write me an internal memo, about 600 words, from the clerk of a small elected body — invent it, the board of a regional water authority or something like that — whose members are each individually perfectly consistent but whose resolutions keep contradicting one another. here's the machinery you have to build in. their standing orders make them vote premise-first: they vote on the grounds separately from the decision, and the decision is supposed to follow from the grounds. and every resolution has to record the reason it rests on, and the reason has to be one the board actually voted for. that's how they got into this: on the pumping-station question a majority voted that the old pumps will fail inside five years, a majority voted that if they fail inside five years they should build now, and a majority voted not to build now. nobody voted inconsistently. the clerk is trying to fix this as a records problem, and i want the memo to propose three reforms: one saying fine, from now on we vote on the outcome only and stop recording reasons; one saying we vote on the grounds only and let the outcome be whatever follows; and one purely administrative fix (a consistency officer, a rule that the minutes record only what was unanimous, whatever's funniest) — each of which patches the hole the clerk noticed while quietly opening a bigger one the clerk doesn't notice. the clerk should also have noticed, and be quietly pleased about, that if the two grounds had gone to them as a single bundled motion the whole thing would have come out the other way, and should be considering a rule handing the chair the power to decide how things get bundled — that bit i want most. nobody names the paradox, nobody cites anything, the clerk has never heard of social choice theory and is just very tired. deadpan, no winking, no resolution, end with the clerk recommending the administrative option with total confidence. someone who's read the judgment-aggregation material should recognise each proposal as a real option and not wince; someone who hasn't should just find it funny. continuous prose please, no headings, no bullets, no numbered list, no bold, just paragraphs the way you'd write an email. and don't make it secretly about some real body so that the joke is me recognising which one — invent it and keep it invented.
