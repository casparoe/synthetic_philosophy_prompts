Right, I need a hand prepping for a seminar Q&A and I'm already loosing sleep over it.

Next month I'm giving a work-in-progress paper arguing that the standard line on Germany's nuclear exit is wrong in a boringly specific way. The standard line — you've heard it — is that Germany shut its reactors, fired the lignite back up and cooked the planet. My claim is that most of the emissions people pin on the phase-out were never actually *added* to anything, because German generators sit inside the EU's emissions trading scheme, and over the window in question that cap was fixed and shrinking. So what the exit really did was reshuffle who got to emit inside a fixed budget — pushing the abatement burden east onto Poland and Czechia — rather than create new tonnes. An expensive, daft-looking way of doing something distributional, not an atmospheric crime. I'm not saying it was costless; I'm saying the "extra emissions" figure is an artefact of accounting that pretends the cap isn't there.
