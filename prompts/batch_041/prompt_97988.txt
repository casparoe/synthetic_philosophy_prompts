Consider the prion controversy as a case study in mechanistic anomaly detection. The background mechanistic model in twentieth-century infectious disease research treated transmissible agents as replicating entities whose information is carried by nucleic acid; on that model, an infectious agent's pathogenicity is explained by a mechanism that includes nucleic-acid replication, protein synthesis, and immune recognition. The scrapie agent, a transmissible neurodegenerative disease of sheep, and later the agent held responsible for Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease and bovine spongiform encephalopathy, presented a cluster of anomalies: it survived doses of ionizing radiation and ultraviolet light that inactivate nucleic acids, provoked no detectable immune response, and produced a slow, non-inflammatory degeneration of brain tissue. Some researchers treated these as anomalies within a slow-virus framework; others proposed that the infectious agent was a protein that propagated by inducing a conformational change in a normal host protein. Analyze this episode through the following framework for mechanistic anomaly detection. A mechanistic model specifies a mechanism type: a causal structure involving entities and activities. A token process is an instance. An anomaly is a token process that deviates from the model's expected behavior. Detecting an anomaly requires deciding whether the token is the same mechanism type, with a hidden parameter or boundary condition; a variant of the same type; or a different mechanism type. Sameness can be assessed along at least three axes: compositional sameness, meaning the same kinds of parts; causal-structural sameness, meaning the same organization of activities; and etiological sameness, meaning the same origin or lineage. Using these distinctions, analyze the prion episode. What exactly was anomalous relative to the background model? Which of the three axes of sameness were in play in the dispute, and how did proponents of the slow-virus view and the protein-only view weigh them differently? Did the prion hypothesis count as a new mechanism type or as a re-description of the same mechanism at a different level of organization? Then say what the episode shows about the framework itself: does it vindicate a pluralist account of mechanism sameness, or does it reveal that sameness judgments are interest-relative and cannot be settled by the mechanistic evidence alone? Be specific about where the framework runs out, rather than forcing the history into it.
