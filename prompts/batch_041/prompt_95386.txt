title: once you know it, you can ignore the evidence against it

1. if i know something then its true and i cant be wrong about it. thats just what knowing is — you cant know something false.

2. now say someone shows me evidence against something i know. i know its true, so that evidence has to be leading me somewhere false. so the evidence is misleading.

3. so i know the evidence is misleading.

4. so i can ignore it. actually its more than that: if i know the evidence is misleading, then letting it change my mind would be a mistake, because id be trading something i know for something i know is wrong. so the confident move is to not budge at all. ive been calling this the nothing-can-touch-me rule in my head, which i realize is not a real name for anything.

example A: i know my friend jaden didnt take the money. i was standing right there the whole time and the money was still in the drawer when we walked out. next day somebody tells me they saw jaden pocket money. by my argument, ive already got 'jaden didnt take it', so whatever they saw, it wasnt that. i should just say nope you saw wrong and go back to my lunch.

example B: i know the earth is round. someone shows me a video about it being flat. same move, i close the tab and feel great about it.

where im stuck: step 3 feels like the trick but i cant say why. 'i know the evidence is misleading' doesnt feel like the same kind of knowing as 'i know jaden didnt take the money.' like in example A, if the person keeps going and then actually shows me a video of jaden, at some point id think hmm, maybe i didnt see what i thought i saw. but if i really know what i saw, shouldnt i be sure? and if i would give it up that easily, did i know it in the first place?

fix attempt 1: knowing has to include a rule for when youd stop knowing. so 'i know P' means P is true, im sure, and id give it up if the evidence got strong enough. but then the whole problem comes back, because if id give it up later then right now im not really sure the evidence is misleading, so step 3 breaks... except now knowing means 'knowing unless the evidence gets strong', which seems to mean i never actually know anything right at this moment. and that cant be right, because then nobody knows anything and thats obviously silly.

fix attempt 2: maybe the mistake is that 'the evidence is misleading' isnt something i know, its something im allowed to just assume until its disproven, like a default setting on my beliefs. or maybe the argument is fine and being stubborn about what you know is correct, and it only feels gross because we're bad at telling whether we really know something or just think we do.
