ok so i'm a junior and my creative writing elective has this final assignment where the story has to "carry an idea" and we turn in a short note saying what the idea was. i want to do the one my sister told me about from her philosophy class, but i only half get it, so i'm gonna write out what she said and then you write the story.

here's the idea. nothing in the world actually makes anything else happen. fire doesn't make things burn, the sun doesn't make the plant grow, my hand doesn't make the pencil fall. it all just happens in the same order every time, and we get fooled by the repetition into thinking the fire has some power inside it. every single moment is produced directly, one at a time, from outside the whole chain. so a miracle and a boring tuesday aren't different in kind, only in how often we've seen them. which means a "law of nature" is more like a schedule or a habit than a force, and when the schedule changes, nothing in the world has to break. she said it also doesn't make anything random, the order is perfectly reliable, and she said you can't tell the difference by watching, no experiment shows you the power, you only ever see one thing and then another thing. i think the guy was al-Ghazali? some medieval dude, the spelling is wierd.

now the story. some things i want:

- set it somewhere ordinary. present day, small town, no fantasy kingdom, no wizards.
- the main character should have a job or a responsibility where this actually matters, and the story should be about them deciding and acting, not about somebody sitting around having thoughts about thoughts.
- include one scene where the pattern changes. i want it to land like the schedule shifted, not like special effects. nobody's mind should be blown by physics breaking. it should be almost boring to look at and horrible to live through.
- include one scene where the main character tries to test it, like actually sets up a little experiment, and the test turns out unable to tell the two views apart. that scene should be the emotional center of the story, not a gotcha.
- no jargon, nobody lectures, nobody says "occasionalism." the idea has to be visible in what happens and what people do.
- don't settle whether there's a someone or something doing the producing. keep whoever or whatever it is offstage. no ending where the camera pulls back and there's a guy.
- one character should be totally sure everything has a cause, and should not be dumb for thinking that.
- make it so i can't tell if the narrator believes any of it.
- under about 1500 words, one narrator, and please don't end on it was all a dream or a simulation.
- i'd rather it be sad than clever.

last thing: i want the final line to make me feel the difference between "it always happens that way" and "it has to happen that way" without actually saying either of those sentences. definately dont put them in there.
