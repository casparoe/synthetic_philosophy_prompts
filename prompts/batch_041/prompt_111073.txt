For a graduate seminar on the philosophy of deliberation, I would like you to write a short story that engages with the idealisation of deliberation known as HCH, or factored cognition. In this idealisation, a single reasoner is replaced by a committee of reasoners who answer subquestions; each member of that committee can in turn be replaced by a further committee, and so on recursively, so that a complex deliberative conclusion is reached through many small, independently answerable steps. Your story should imagine a world in which this idealisation has been implemented as a technology, and should dramatise a philosophical problem that arises when a conclusion is reached in this way—for example, what becomes of responsibility, understanding, or the unity of the self when no single agent grasps the whole chain of reasoning. Please write in British English.
