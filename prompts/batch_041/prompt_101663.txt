I'm a program officer at a small foundation and I've got enough money to fund about six theory people for two or three years on inner alignment / goal misgeneralization stuff -- conceptual work, philosophy and theory, not training runs. I have to write a prioritization memo and I keep goign in circles, so I want your take.

Here's the catch though: I don't want the standard list. Everyone says "deceptive alignment," "mesa-optimization," "the alignment problem for learned optimizers," and I've read all of that. What I want is which open questions are actually upstream of the rest, and I want the ordering defended, not just asserted.

To make it concrete, here are four candidate framings people have handed me. I want you to place each of them:

- The "it's just underspecification" line: goal misgeneralization isn't a new phenomenon, it's the ordinary fact that many hypotheses fit the training data and you end up with whichever one you end up with. On this view the real work is in specification and inductive bias, and "alignment" is mostly a relabeling.
- The retention question: suppose we somehow got a model that, at the end of training, really did have the objective we wanted. Does anything about further capability growth or further optimization pressure threaten to change it later? And is "keeping" a goal even a coherent thing to ask of a system like this?
- The operationalization problem: we don't have a principled account of what it would mean for a model to "have" a goal as opposed to merely behaving as if it did, so a lot of claims about misgeneralized goals may not even be well-posed.
- The training-signal / proxy question: when the thing we train on differs from the thing we care about, when does that gap produce something that generalizes in a systematically wrong direction versus just a noisy approximation of the right thing?

For each one, tell me: is it upstream or downstream of the others, how tractable is it for a philosopher/theorist with no ML lab, and how neglected is it relative to the others? Then give me your ordering, the criteria you used and where those criteria pull against each other, and what over the next couple of years would move something up or down the list. Also tell me definately if I'm missing a question that should be sitting in the top slot.

Keep it concrete, no bibliography needed, and at the very end put a line that says \answer{...} with just the name of the single question you'd fund first.
