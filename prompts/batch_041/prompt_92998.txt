Ok so this is a weird one, but stick with me. My flatmate Sam has zero interest in politics as a subject — she's a radiographer, she's never read a book about any of this, she thinks I'm a massive nerd for caring — but she has incredibly firm views about who should be allowed to vote, and we've been going back and forth about it for about two years. Her record so far, as best I can keep track:

- Compulsory voting in general elections: yes. Her thing is that if your on the list you should have to turn up, otherwise the people who care the most just decide everything for everyone else.
- Votes at 16: yes. She says a 16 year old has to live with these governments way longer than she does.
- Non citizens who are permanent residents — here ten years, working, paying tax: yes.
- Our neighbour, been on the street fifteen years with no papers, works cash in hand, three kids in the local school: yes, and she got genuinely annoyed when I pushed back. Apparently he's got more riding on what the council does than half the people already on the roll.
- Her cousin who moved to Australia twelve years ago and still keeps a UK address so he can vote here: no. He doesn't live here, he doesn't deal with the schools or the bins, that whole speech.
- Her mate who's inside for another two years: yes. He's from here, his mum's here, he's coming back out.

Now the council's got a consultation open and I honestly cannot work out what she'll say about it. Two things in the proposal: (a) people who own property or run a business in the city but live somewhere else entirely would get a vote in local elections, and (b) anyone who's moved here from abroad would have to wait five years before they can vote locally, even though they're living here and paying council tax the whole time.

What does she say to those two? And the thing I actually want: whats the rule she's running? When I ask her she just says its obvious and wont say more than that. I want to know what other rules would fit everything she's said just as well, which one she's most likely actually on, and which one extra case would settle it — because right now we just go round in circles and I'd like to win at least once.
