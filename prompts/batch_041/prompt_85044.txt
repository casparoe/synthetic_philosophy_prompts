After forty years in experimental science, most of it in laboratories in three different countries, I have stopped believing that the contrast I grew up with — that some cultures celebrate success while others resent it — describes what I actually saw. I have never found a term for what I did see.

What I kept running into was a single compound move, not two opposed attitudes: warm and, so far as I could tell, entirely sincere congratulations, followed in the same breath by a reassignment of the achievement away from the person — to luck, to timing, to the instrument, to the students who did the work. The reassignment never sounded sour; those making it took themselves to be stating a plain fact, and they applied it to their own achievements as readily as to anyone else's. The same people would celebrate an identical achievement without qualification once it was attributed to a group, an institute, or a country; and successes that could not be attached to a person at all — a better instrument, a cleaner dataset — drew none of this. The whole performance looked less like envy than like the defence of a norm: that achievements are not the property of individuals.

The familiar labels do not fit. Tall poppies and the law of Jante both name the cutting down of the successful, and this was not cutting down; damning with faint praise is not it either, since the praise was not faint.

Is there a name for this? If so, tell me what it is and where it fails to fit. If not, coin one, and give it edges — what the move is, what it is not, and how it stands to the concepts I have just ruled out.
