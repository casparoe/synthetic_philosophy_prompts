I'm putting together a small glossary of concepts for a side project, and I keep running into a pattern I can't name. Can you tell me if there's an established term for it, and if there isn't one, coin a term and tell me exactly where its edges are? I'm not after a bare label — the whole point is knowing whats in and whats out, so a name with no boundary cases is useless to me.

Here's the pattern. Something looks like it's responding to what's going on, but when you actually look at how it "responds," it's running down a short fixed list of moves and taking whichever one matches. My sister's thermostat does this. It's got a "learning" mode and it turns the heat down when the house is empty. But it only "knows" the house is empty by checking a fixed set of things — motion sensor, whether my phone is on the wifi, the schedule — and if none of them fire it runs the whole nobody-home routine anyway, including a bit where it "waits to see if anyone objects," which is really just a twenty-minute timer. So the adapting is a lookup. Same deal at my old call-center job: we were trained to "read the caller's mood and adjust," and adjusting meant angry goes to page 3, confused goes to page 9. When I pointed this out my manager said no, we can also escalate to a human — and escalating was page 11.

That version I can sort of name, I think. Here's the version I actually can't. What if the thing that looks like a lookup really isn't one — it genuinely adapts — but the way it decides when to *stop* adapting is itself fixed? Suppose I decide to stop being so rigid. So I make a rule: any time I catch myself doing something just out of habit, stop and actually consider the alternatives. Fine. But now the catching is itself a habit, so I make a rule about when to catch the catching, and that rule has the exact same problem, and there's no floor to it. You can always be mechanical one level up. So "flexibility" never gets cashed out, it just gets pushed up a floor. And the nasty part is you can't tell from the outside either way, because all you ever see is what the thing does — any long enough stretch of behavior fits "fixed routine" and "really adapting" equally well.

I've heard there's some old story about a wasp that just keeps repeating the same little routine whenever you move the cricket it's dragging, and that people trot that out as the poster child for this whole area. Is that story about my first case or my second one? Does the name cover the version where the flexibility is real but nested, or only the version where the "flexibility" is a lookup? Is there a separate name for the nesting, and for the fact that you can't tell from behavior alone which of the two you're looking at? It's not idle for me — I got onto this reading about free will, and if every level of choosing turns out to be either a lookup or a rule about when to stop looking up, I don't know what's left over that's doing the choosing.

Since it's for a glossary, give it to me as a JSON object with exactly these fields, written in American English:

- closest_established_term: the name of the nearest established concept, or null if there really isn't one
- what_that_term_covers: a couple sentences on what it does and doesn't include
- divergences: an array of short strings, each one a specific way my case is not that concept
- coined_term: a name for what I described, if the established term doesn't fit
- coined_definition: one or two sentences
- boundary_cases: an array of objects, each with "case" (a short description), "inside" (true or false), and "why" (one sentence)
- ruled_out: an array of objects, each with "concept" and "why_not" (one sentence each), for the near misses you considered and rejected
- glossary_line: the single sentence you'd actually put in the glossary
