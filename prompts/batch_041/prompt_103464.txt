I have just been elected to the board of a small housing cooperative, and we are about to vote on a change that would modestly benefit every household — it would trim a little off each member's monthly charge and improve a shared amenity — while imposing a serious and ongoing burden on exactly one household, whose rooms sit beside the only spot where the change could go. Our chair argues that the arithmetic settles it: many small gains plainly outweigh one large loss, so the change is justified and the dissenting member is simply being asked to bear her fair share of the common good. The affected member, who has been entirely reasonable throughout, says she is being made to accept a life she did not choose so that a hundred others can each save a trivial amount, and that nobody could agree in advance to a rule that singled them out this way. I find both of them compelling, and I cannot tell whether my unease with the chair's arithmetic is a real moral insight or mere squeamishness. One further wrinkle: a colleague proposes to soften the proposal by offering the affected member a compensation payment funded by the rest of us. Please help me decide how to vote — specifically, whether the sum of many small benefits can ever justify imposing a severe burden on one person, and whether paying her off would change the answer.
