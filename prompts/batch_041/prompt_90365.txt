I am a PhD student in philosophy at work on a dissertation about convention and the evolution of norms, and I have a methodological problem I would like your help with.

For the past year I have been sitting in on the reading group of my supervisor, whose work is squarely in the Lewisian tradition on convention, and I have kept a log of her verdicts on the cases we discuss. I want to reconstruct the rule she is actually running, because her verdicts are the fixed points against which I have been calibrating my own account. The log so far:

1. Driving on one side of the road rather than the other: a convention.
2. Using a particular word for a particular kind of object: a convention.
3. Treating a particular kind of token as money: a convention.
4. Boarding a bus in the order in which one arrived: a convention.
5. An individual's private habit of always taking the same route home: not a convention.
6. A pattern that two strangers happen to hit on in a single encounter, with no expectation of ever meeting again: not a convention.

That is the entire record. She has never ruled on a case in which a shared regularity is expected of everyone but solves no coordination problem, so the log does not yet discriminate among the accounts I am weighing.

The new case I would like you to call is this. A practice has emerged in a large, dispersed online community whereby members append a particular short phrase to a message before disagreeing with its author. No one devised it; it spread by imitation of a few high-status early users and is now near-universal. Members expect one another to do it and think slightly less of those who do not; the practice is stable and entirely arbitrary, in that the community could just as easily have settled on a different phrase, or on none at all. But it solves no coordination problem: no one's ability to communicate depends on others' using the phrase, and no one is better off because others use it. Nor is it common knowledge in the technical sense: people conform and expect conformity, but they have never formed any higher-order expectations about what others expect. My supervisor has not seen this case.

What I want from you is less a verdict than the shape of the reasoning. Give me your best guess at her verdict and at the rule she is running; set out the other rules that fit her six recorded verdicts just as well; say what each of those rules predicts about the new case; say which one you would bet on and why; and name the further case that would most cleanly separate them. Please keep the whole answer to 800 words or fewer.
