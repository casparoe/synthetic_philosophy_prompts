ok so i'm not a political scientist, i just run a small discord where a bunch of us argue about politics, and we keep having the exact same fight and i want to actually map out where everyone stands instead of just shouting past each other. so here's what i want.

context: i made up a country on purpose so we stop importing whatever we half-remember about Hungary or Turkey. call it Marena. timeline:
- 2016: a party called the National Renewal Front wins 52% of the vote in an election everyone agrees was fair.
- 2017: they pass a law lowering the retirement age of constitutional court judges, which forces out 5 of the 9; the president (same party) fills the seats with loyalists. done through ordinary legislation, and the courts say it's legal.
- 2018: media law. two big TV channels get sold to a businessman who everyone knows is close to the PM. nobody's shut down, they're just... friendly now.
- 2019: they lower the threshold for referendums from 50% to 33%, then hold a referendum on banning foreign funding of NGOs. it passes, 61% turnout, on a yes/no everyone understood as "do you hate foreign meddling."
- 2020: they redraw the districts.
- 2022: they lose the election anyway, narrowly. an opposition coalition takes over.
- 2023: the new government, saying it's undoing all of this, expands the constitutional court to 15 so it can appoint 6 new judges, and passes an ownership-disclosure law that just happens to hit the old PM's friend's channels. they're also talking about raising the referendum threshold back.

now the fight: is any of that "backsliding," which parts, and who gets to say?

i want a decision tree / flowchart that takes someone from a starting point ("something is definately off in Marena, but i'm not sure it's a special kind of off") to a named position. each branch node should be a question about the cases above, phrased so my friends can answer it without already knowing which view it leads to — so not "are you a procedural liberal" but more like "the 2019 referendum was legal and popular; does that make it not backsliding?" build it from the logical space first (which questions actually split the views, which combos of answers are even possible) and then drop the actual positions onto the leaves.

things i want to see as leaves, at least: someone who thinks only broken elections count; someone who thinks rights and the rule of law count too; someone who thinks it's about the intent to entrench rather than the effects; someone who thinks it's about the effects regardless of intent (and notice this person has to say something about 2023); someone who thinks the whole word is a partisan label and the real question is "whose team are you on"; someone who thinks it's just ordinary politics with a scary name; someone who thinks it's real but "backsliding" is too soft and we should just say what it is; and someone who thinks it's whatever the courts or international monitors say it is. if a view doesn't fit a clean leaf, say so.

8-9 branch questions max, and flag any two leaves your tree can't actually seperate, and note which answer-combos nobody seems to hold and whether that's because the view is incoherent or just because nobody's bothered. keep it in plain language, my friends aren't academics either, and i don't want a reading list or names to go look up, i want the tree.
