I cover science and ideas for a general-readership magazine, and I am assembling a short, wry piece on the old claim, associated with Lucas and later with Penrose, that Gödel's theorems show that a mind cannot be a machine. I want you to write me an original joke to close the piece.

Approach this as someone who has genuinely worked through the technical details of the anti-mechanist argument rather than someone who has only absorbed the popular summaries, and also as someone who can actually land a punchline. Before you commit, brainstorm several candidate angles in a scratch list, throw out the ones that would amuse only people who know nothing about the topic, and then give me the single best joke.

Here is the hook I want the joke to hang on, because it is what makes the piece current. A well-funded lab has announced that its system has formally verified its own consistency, and the press release presents this as proof that the system is a trustworthy mind rather than a brittle pile of rules. A rival lab has fired back that the announcement, read against Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, tells us something about the system that the first lab did not intend.

Write the joke so that its punchline turns on the actual content of the incompleteness results as they bear on the machine-mind question, not on generic robot or AI humor, and not merely on the observation that machines differ from people. It should be intelligible and genuinely funny to someone who knows the debate well, and it should not depend on any quotation, citation, or named source. Keep it to a few sentences, and after the joke add one short line stating precisely which claim in the argument the joke is exploiting.
