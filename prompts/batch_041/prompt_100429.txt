I am assembling the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in decision theory and practical rationality, and I want a single essay question on the difference between two ways of representing risk aversion in a theory of rational choice. The students have worked through the standard expected-utility material — the von Neumann–Morgenstern framework, the independence or sure-thing axiom, and the idea that a concave utility function is what represents risk aversion — and they have also encountered the rank-dependent family of models, in which it is probabilities rather than utilities that get transformed. Within that family I want the question to focus on Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility, because it is the version that makes the sharpest conceptual claim. What I want the question to test is whether students can articulate the contrast rather than recite either formalism. In particular, a strong answer should explain how risk-weighted expected utility prises apart two things that expected utility fuses: the value of outcomes and the agent's attitude toward chance itself. It should state the risk-weighted representation, say what the risk function takes as its argument and why it is applied to cumulative, rank-ordered probabilities rather than to individual probabilities, and explain why that rank-dependent structure is demanded by a normative constraint rather than adopted for mathematical convenience. It should also say what the boundary conditions on the risk function must be and why, and then take a position on whether the resulting theory's abandonment of the full independence/sure-thing axiom is a cost or something a correct theory of rationality should welcome — giving Buchak's own answer to that question and saying whether the student finds it persuasive. Finally, I want them to distinguish the descriptive use of this same formalism, as in Quiggin's rank-dependent expected utility and in cumulative prospect theory, from Buchak's normative use of it, since a very common confusion is to read her as offering a psychological model of how people happen to behave.

Please write the essay question itself — a few sentences of setup followed by the actual instruction to the student — then a model answer of roughly 650 to 750 words pitched at the level of a strong undergraduate or beginning graduate student, and then a marking scheme out of 20 points that names the specific moves a top answer must contain and the specific errors that should cost marks. Among the errors I would like the scheme to catch are conflating the risk function with the utility function, treating the rank-dependent structure as a mere technical fix for the Allais pattern, and claiming that risk-weighted expected utility is simply expected utility with a differently shaped utility function. The scheme should let me separate a genuine A answer from a B answer that has the vocabulary right but the concepts wrong.
