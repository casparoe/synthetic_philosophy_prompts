Two friends from my cohort got into it at the pub on Friday and I couldn't referee it, so I'm outsourcing. A works on metaethics, B is more of a decision-theory person. Roughly how it went (I've cleaned it up a bit, they were alot drunker than this sounds):

1. A: If someone genuinely doesn't care about anything that would move him to take his meds, then he has no reason to take them. Reasons have to be able to move you; otherwise "reason" is just a word for what you'd like other people to want.

2. B: So your uncle, who's serenely indifferent to his own health and doesn't care about his family either — he has no reason at all to take them? He has one, he's just not moved by it. That's what makes him irrational rather than off the hook.

3. A: That's smuggling in a different sense of "reason." You mean there'd be a reason for him if he cared, or that it would be good for him to take them. Neither of those is a reason *for him*.

4. B: Then your view can't criticize anyone. Anyone fully coherent in his indifference gets a pass. That's relativism with extra steps.

5. A: Not relativism — "S has a reason to φ" is a conditional: if S's motivations were properly deliberated on, S would be moved to φ. The conditional is objectively true or false, same as any other. It's just that the antecedent is about S's psychology, not about the world's normative furniture.

6. B: Then which S is in the consequent? Deliberation changes what you care about, that's the whole point of it. So the well-deliberated S is a different person with different cares, and you've got no principled way to say which transformations count as deliberating well and which are just replacing him with someone else. And if you fix the cares to stop that, you've defined away every interesting case.

7. A: Deliberation clarifies, it doesn't add new content. The fully coherent you is still you, just less confused.

8. B: That's the stipulation doing all the work again.

Can you go through this move by move and say who's right about what, not just who wins overall?
