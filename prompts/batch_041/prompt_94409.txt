I am a high school student preparing a handout for my AP Biology discussion section on whether species is a real category or a useful label. Please write a decision tree that takes someone from the concrete cases we keep arguing about, including ring species, bacteria that swap genes, asexual rotifers, fossil lineages, hybrids, and cryptic species, to a named position. Each branch node should be a question about what has to be true for a grouping to count as a species, phrased so a classmate can answer it without already knowing the labels: whether interbreeding is necessary, whether common ancestry is sufficient, whether ecological role or morphology can do the work, whether species taxa are individuals or classes, and whether one definition must cover all life. The leaves should include the biological, phylogenetic, morphological, and ecological species concepts, species pluralism, species eliminativism, and the view that the species category is real but heterogeneous. Flag any leaves the tree cannot separate and any combinations of answers that seem unoccupied, with a sentence on why. Keep it to about eight to ten questions, and make sure the questions turn on cases where the definitions conflict rather than just asking for textbook definitions.
