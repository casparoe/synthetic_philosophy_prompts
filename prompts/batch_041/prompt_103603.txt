I have no academic background in philosophy — I'm a self-taught game developer who spends most of their time on a small voxel engine — but I've been chewing on the brain-in-a-vat problem on my own for a while, and I keep running into the suspicion that my day job has already handed me a vocabulary for it. In my engine almost nothing exists until it is needed: terrain is generated on demand from a seed when the player gets close, distant geometry is a cheap stand-in that gets refined as you approach, anything outside the view frustum is simply never computed, and I've been sketching a save system that records only the deltas the player actually observed, so the unvisited parts of the world have no stored state at all. Much of that sounds, at least to me, like the skeptic's picture of a world that is only ever manufactured at the moment of observation. I'd like you to test that suspicion rather than flatter it.

Please take the skeptical position — the one on which every experience you could ever have is equally consistent with your being a normal embodied person and with your being a brain in a vat being fed a simulated world, so that no possible observation could settle the matter — and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: computed versus persisted state, seeds and procedural generation, level of detail, the render and update loops, save files, the difference between an entity that is simulated and one that is merely described, and the fact that the developer can always step outside the running world and look down at it from above. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the skeptical argument translate cleanly into those terms, which parts I can only get by quietly altering what they mean, and which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all — and for that last category, tell me whether the gap is a defect in how I think about engines or a sign that the skeptics are leaning on something I have never once had to implement. The point I most want pressure on is whether the lazy-evaluation picture can even state the skeptic's doubt, since it seems to me that not yet computed and not real are completely different claims, and I suspect my vocabulary may contain only the first.
