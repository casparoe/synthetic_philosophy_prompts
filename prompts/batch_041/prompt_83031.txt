I keep running into a shape of thing and I dont have a name for it. Wondering if there's one, or if I should just make one up.

The shape: somebody makes a run of choices, and if you look at any single one of them on its own it's fine. Not just defensible -- it's what they'd pick again if you put them back there with the same options in front of them. At no point do they contradict themselves, at no point do they pass over the option they rank higher, there's nothing you can point at and call a mistake. And yet the whole run leaves them worse off than if none of it had ever been offered, they'll tell you so afterwards, and they'd have refused the whole package in advance if you'd handed it to them as a package.

Best example I've got is a mate of mine and cigarettes. Buys a pack, buys the gum, chucks the pack, buys another pack, buys more gum. Every single purchase is what he most wants at that moment given how he feels at that moment. He never once sits down, ranks the whole years worth of options and deliberately picks the worst one. He never says two things that cant both be true. And the year is a write-off.

Second flavour, where his tastes don't budge at all and it's the situation that moves instead. An auction where the runner up has to pay what they bid. Two people, and every raise is the sensible move from where the bidder is standing, because dropping out now means paying for nothing. Nobody has made an error at any point. They both walk away having paid well over the odds, and both of them know it, and both would have laughed if you'd proposed the whole sequence up front.

I keep thinking of this as a money pump but I don't think that's quite it, because with a money pump as I understand it the person's preferences go round in a circle -- prefers A to B, B to C, C back to A -- and then someone charges them a toll each lap. Here you never catch anybody with a circle in their preferences at any one time. Stop them halfway, make them rank the things actually in front of them, you get a tidy order, no loops. The loop only appears if you line up choices made at different moments and treat them as coming from one person standing in one spot. And in the auction case there's no loop at all, plus the other bidder is doing half the work, which I don't think is part of the money pump story.

So, does this have a name? I'm not after a label, I want to know how to carve it. Is the taste-moving case and the situation-moving case one phenomenon or two? What exactly does the money pump need that these don't have -- or is the money pump just the special case where the circle happens to sit inside a single moment? And is there a general name for a loss that can't be pinned on any one choice, and if there isn't, what would you call it and where would you draw the edges -- same animal as a bloke pouring more money into something that's already gone bad, or different, given that there the other party's behaviour is doing the work?
