I've been going down a rabbit hole about the digger wasp — the one that stings a cricket, drags it back to her burrow, leaves it at the door, goes inside to check the burrow is empty, comes back out, and only then hauls the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's inside checking, she comes out, finds it moved, drags it back to the door, and goes in to check again — and she'll apparently do this forever if you keep nudging. There's a word I've seen for this, 'sphexish', named after the wasp, and my reading group has been arguing about whether it applies to us. Here are my gut verdicts on a pile of cases. The wasp: sphexish, obviously — she's clearly tracking the cricket's position, so it isn't a blind reflex, but she can't seem to register that the burrow has already been checked. My mate who has to check the stove exactly seven times before he can leave the house: sphexish, even though he's a person, because he literally cannot make himself stop. Me driving to the shop but ending up in the office car park on autopilot: NOT sphexish — I just wasn't paying attention, and the second I notice I turn around. A chess engine with a bug that makes it replay a losing move every game: sphexish. A grandmaster who riffs off an opening book for fifteen moves and then drops it the moment the position demands: not sphexish at all. A chatbot that gets stuck apologizing in a loop no matter what you type: sphexish. And my uncle, who will keep pressing the same point in an argument for an hour after everyone has already conceded: honestly sphexish — he's definately a broken record. But my other friend, who has voted the same way for forty years and never once reconsidered: I want to say NOT sphexish, because he could reconsider, he just doesn't care to.

The principle I keep reaching for is roughly this: a bit of behavior is sphexish when the agent has no capacity to revise the routine in light of the point it serves — when it would run to completion even if running to completion had become pointless. That seems to get the wasp, the stove-checker, the buggy engine and the chatbot right, and it gets the autopilot-driver and the grandmaster right too. But it tells me my uncle the broken record is NOT sphexish (he could stop), and my gut keeps insisting he is; and it tells me my forty-year voter is not sphexish, which I'm happy with, except I can't say why the uncle and the voter come out differently when they look so similar — both could stop and neither does.

So what I want from you: give me the principle, or a small set of competing principles, stated precisely enough that I could apply them myself, with each clause tagged to the case that forces it. Tell me what the one you'd defend says about cases I haven't listed — say, a sleepwalker making a cup of tea, someone following a recipe they've made a thousand times who never deviates but would the moment the oven ran cold, and a person whose 'spontaneity' is itself something they do on a schedule. Then be honest about which of my verdicts your principle cannot save, and whether I should give that verdict up or buy it back with an extra clause. Take a definite side — I don't want a menu of options, I want to know what you actually think sphexishness is, and whether the account leaves any room for the kind of flexibility free will would need. No reading lists or citations, please; I just want your take.
