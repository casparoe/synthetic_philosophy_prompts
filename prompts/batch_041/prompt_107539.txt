On our forum we've been going in circles for like three months about a proposal we came up with ourselves, and everyone is just restating their priors at this point, so what I actually want is the adversarial-collaboration document. Here's the proposal, call it the Civic Jury: in a city of about 200,000, the 9-seat council stays elected, but a 150-person body chosen by lot from the voter rolls sets the annual budget priorities and can veto up to two council ordinances a year by majority vote. Jurors get paid the local median wage for roughly six weeks of work, childcare covered, one term ever, and you can only decline for documented hardship, so the people who actually end up serving are self-selected out of the pool. Nobody who's held office or lobbied in the last five years is eligible, and the experts who brief the jury are picked by the jury itself, not by staff.

The two camps: the lottocrats, who want sortition to replace competitive elections wherever possible, and the electoralists, who think the real work is done by the fact that you can get thrown out, and that no lottery buys you that.

Write the joint statement the two of them could both sign. I want: (1) numbered agreed claims, covering what democracy is even for, what this proposal would mechanically do, what's genuinely unknown, and what each side concedes is the other's strongest point; (2) the live disagreements, each written in wording that side would accept for itself; (3) the cruxes, and for each crux the case, statistic, or thought experiment both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; (4) a flag on which parts of this are just a fight over the word democracy or over what counts as a better decision, and what's left of the dispute once you set those aside. I especially want both sides forced to say whether the self-selection in the pool guts the descriptive-representation argument, since that's the one neither of them will touch.
