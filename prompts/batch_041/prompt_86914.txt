Case one: indifference that's actually forced. Give me a physical randomising device with a symmetry — a real symmetry of the hardware, not of a formula — such that exactly one probability distribution is consistent with it and that distribution has total mass 1. Then run a refinement test on it: cut the outcome space a different way, ideally a way a reasonable person might have chosen instead, and show that the probability of a fixed event doesn't move. Case two: indifference that provably has no verdict. Give me a device that is exactly self-similar in the following sense: there's an operation on the gadget (a zoom, a stretch, a scale-down) that carries the whole setup onto a proper part of itself, and it's a symmetry of the physical process, not just of your coordinates. Constraints on both: no infinite coin flips, no picking a uniformly random real as a primitive, no measure-theory deus ex machina. One paragraph per gadget, then the analysis.
