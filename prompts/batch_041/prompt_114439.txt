I'm writing a long piece about periodization — whether the Middle Ages, the Renaissance and the Scientific Revolution are real seams in history or labels we stitched on afterwards — and I want to open it with a poem. The hook I keep circling is that nobody in the so-called Middle Ages knew they were in the middle of anything, and the medievalists I've talked to say the Renaissance was basically a branding exercise by later humanists who wanted something to have come after. So the name comes after the thing, and the thing only exists once its named. Can you write me a poem where the form does that work? Something like: each stanza ends on one of the textbook boundary dates (476, 1453, 1543, 1687, you know the ones), but the sentences refuse to stop there and run on across the break into the next stanza, so the poem keeps drawing a border that its own grammar wont honor — and maybe once, near the end, a sentence actually does stop on the date, just to make you feel the difference. No rhyme needed, don't let it resolve either way (I don't want "periods are fake" and I don't want "obviously they're real"), and it has to be a real poem, not a lecture with line breaks — someone who's never thought about historiography should still get something out of it. Max 24 lines.
