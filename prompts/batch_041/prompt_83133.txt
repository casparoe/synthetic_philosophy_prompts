I've been going back and forth with my brother in law about this for months and I cant find anything that quite matches it, so I'm hoping you can tell me whether it has a name.

Here's what I keep noticing. Two people can agree completely about how a rule has been applied so far, agree on every single case that's already been settled, and still both insist the other one is doing it wrong the moment a new case comes up. It's not that they disagree about the facts of the new case. They disagree about what the old cases meant. Both of them think their reading is the one that was already in there, the one the rule had all along, and that the other person is quietly bending the rule to suit themselves. That's the part that gets me -- the fight is always about the past. Nobody says fine, from now on we'll do it this way. They say that is not what we have been doing.

And the flip side, which I only noticed later. You can have two people who give completely different accounts of the rule. Sit them down, get them to write it out, the two descriptions wont match, one will say the other one's version is flat wrong. And yet they never once diverge in what they actually do. A friend of mine plays in a band, and him and the drummer could not tell you what the timing is, they'd argue if you asked them, but they are never off. Meanwhile I've watched people recite a rule back word for word, identical words, and then do different things with it.

So the rule as said and the rule as done seem to come apart in both directions, and I dont think either one is just a sloppy version of the other. When I try to fix the first problem by writing the rule out more carefully, or adding a note about how to read the rule, the argument just moves up a level -- now we're arguing about the note. And at some point somebody always says oh come on, you know what I mean, and everyone goes back to work as if it were settled, which it isn't.

There's a third case that might be the same thing or might not: reading an old note I wrote myself, and not being able to tell whether I meant something specific by it or whether I was just scribbling.

I looked this up and the nearest thing I found was some maths example with a made up word for adding that isn't adding, plus a lot of talk about how any rule can be read in endless ways. That's close but it isn't it, because in those examples the dispute is about the future, what to do with a case that hasn't come up, and mine is about what already happened -- and those examples don't have the second half at all, the agreeing in practice while disagreeing in words half.

Is there a name for this? And if there isn't one, or if there's one that only covers half of it, I want you to actually pin the thing down -- what it is, what it isn't, where the edges are. Is the band case the same phenomenon as the argument about the rule, or a different one that just looks similar? Is there a real difference between a rule nobody can put into words and a rule everybody puts into words differently? And if you end up coining a name, give me the borderline cases, the ones that are half in and half out, because that's the part I actually care about. Don't just hand me a word, I've got words.
