Okay, I need help writing something, so I'd rather you just dive in. I sit on the grants committee of a mid-sized family foundation — we move about six million a year, mostly global health, a little animal welfare. Here's my problem.

Marcus's baby is a campaign to get a big food company to go cage-free on eggs; Priya thinks that campaign is a waste of money and, off the record, that it does more harm than good by pushing up food prices. Under our normal process neither project clears the bar — we have a mission filter and both are borderline, and honestly we'd be pulling the money from a bed-net program that everyone on the committee agrees is a better buy. So the deal is: Priya votes to fund Marcus's campaign and Marcus votes to fund Priya's deworming. Both get funded. Marcus has even offered to record a short video calling the deworming program one of our top priorities, which he privately doesn't believe.

The chair is squirming and so am I, but when I try to say why it's wrong I end up sounding like I'm just against them getting what they want. One trustee said the money isn't theirs to trade anyway, that we hold it in trust and you cant trade what isn't yours. And a couple of other trustees have started pairing off on their own pet projects now that they've seen this work, and the treasurer floated treating the whole thing like an offset system — you're allowed to do a little bad as long as you buy a matching good. That gives me the creeps but I can't tell if that's a real objection or just aesthetics.

I have to circulate a memo to the governance committee before Thursday. Draft it for me. I want a real recommendation — ban the trades outright, allow them with conditions, or allow them — plus the actual rule I could move to adopt and when it should kick in.
