Ok so my brother-in-law Ray and I agree on basically nothing. He's a church every Sunday guy, I'm one of those people who gives 10% of my income away and thinks factory farming is the moral emergency of our time. We get along great, we just argue every Thanksgiving.

Last year he made this half joking offer: he'll go vegetarian for a full year if I write a $500 check to his church's prison ministry. I laughed, and then we both went a bit quiet, because... actually is that a real deal? Here's my problem though. I don't have extra money for this. I have a set amount I give each year and that $500 was going to an animal charity, so I'm not adding anything, I'm just moving it. And honestly I think his prison ministry is kind of a waste of money — not evil, just, they play guitar at people and hand out tracts, and I suspect alot of it is a waste. But Ray really believes in it, and he really loves ribs, so a year without meat is a genuine sacrifice for him, and a year of Ray not eating meat is worth something to me.

The part that weirds me out is that the whole thing only works because we disagree. If he already cared about animals there'd be nothing to trade, we'd just both donate to our own stuff and feel smug. My friend says the whole idea is gross, that I'd be funding something I think is pointless just to get my brother in law to eat beans, and that you can't buy someone's moral behavior like that. But isn't that what every trade is?

And I keep feeling like I'm getting away with something clever here, which makes me suspicious that I'm not. Am I loosing sight of something? Should I do it, and if so is there a better version?
