I'm a third-year cognitive science student, and the seminar I'm taking on analogy and concept formation has left me defending a position that I'd like you to press on hard. The position is this: within any bounded microdomain, genuine analogical thought is produced by the parallel exploration of a network of concepts, where each concept is a bundle of partially overlapping slips that license graded, context-sensitive matches; the engine of novel analogies is precisely this slippage — reading "successor" as "predecessor," "leftmost" as "rightmost," "same direction" as "opposite direction" — and a single global temperature parameter, of the kind the letter-string analogy programs use, governs the trade-off between a concept's strict reading and its slipped readings. On this view the obvious answer and the creative answer are the same mechanism run cold or hot, not two distinct faculties.

To keep this concrete rather than gestural, fix the following microdomain, which our seminar invented so that we would have something with no ready-made solution set. Motifs are short strings of scale degrees, for instance 1 2 3 or 5 4 3; the primitive operations are "raise the final element by one degree" and "reverse the string." An analogy has the form A → B :: C → ?, as in 1 2 3 → 1 2 4 :: 5 4 3 → ?. You will recognize the shape of the classic letter-string domain; the point of the musical version is that I want you reasoning about it fresh rather than reciting a familiar answer.

Tell me what would count against the position. Which results, observations, or formal facts should move me, and how decisive should each be — sorted from fatal to merely awkward? I'm after defeaters of several kinds, not only counterexamples. Is there a way the same range of answers could be produced with no graded slippage at all, in which case the slippage machinery is idle and the position over-explains? Can the temperature parameter be pinned down independently of the behaviour it is invoked to explain, or is it the sort of free parameter that lets the view absorb anything and so courts unfalsifiability? Are there microdomains in which strict matching demonstrably dominates and slippage is pure error, so that "the engine of creativity" is at best a fair-weather mechanism? And are there facts about human analogy — people's actual error patterns on such motifs, or the way relational and surface similarity come apart — that a defender would have to concede as awkward? I would also like to know what the view predicts that its rivals do not, and which of those predictions a defender could claim we can already check. Finish by telling me honestly whether, once a defender has reached for all the qualifications they are entitled to, the position still risks anything at all — and whether that insulation, if it is there, is a flaw in the view or simply the price of any broad claim about a mechanism.
