I run a monthly workshop for tabletop game designers — sharp people with no philosophy or computer science background — and next month I'm teaching tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level: the idea that a hierarchy of levels can loop back on itself so that the top and the bottom turn out to be the same thing, and that such a loop is only stable because something is held off the board altogether. It comes up for them practically, since they keep designing games in which cards or moves rewrite the rules, and those games tend to collapse into either anarchy or an unreachable judge. Don't explain the concepts to me; plan the session. I want the order the ideas have to come in and why each depends on the one before it, the single concrete case that should carry the whole thing and what it must have and must lack (something they can run in the room, not a picture they have already seen — if Escher's Drawing Hands is still the right vehicle, argue for that rather than assume it), the misconceptions they will arrive with and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off (my guesses: they will hear "tangled hierarchy" as either a vicious regress or merely a feedback loop, and they will take the inviolate level to be simply the top level, or the referee), the check that would show the idea has landed rather than merely been admired, and what I should openly refuse to cover at this level.
