so i run a tiny podcast where i interview philosophers of science, and next week's guest has spent the last decade defending a version of structural realism i cant find anywhere else in quite this shape. her line is roughly: dont be a realist about objects, and dont be a realist about the equations either — be a realist about the *invariance structure*, i.e. the set of features of a theory that are preserved under every symmetry transformation the theory admits and that stay put when the theory gets replaced by its successor. everything else, the entities, the ontology, even the particular formalism, is scaffolding for getting at that invariant core, and the realist's entire commitment should be to the core and nothing besides. she also has a claim i find suspicious: that this dissolves the pessimistic meta-induction, because the historical cases where theories got thrown out were cases where the *ontology* changed but the invariant structure was carried over, so there was never a real discontinuity in what we were committed to.

write me 8-10 interview questions for her. i want them to actually bite, so press where the objections actually are, not 'so what is structural realism'. specifically i need at least one question on the Newman objection — the one where a Ramsey-sentence style 'structure' turns out to be satisfied by any old set of the right size, so realism about structure collapses into realism about cardinality — and whether her invariance criterion does anything to block that or just relabels it. one on the epistemic-vs-ontic divide: is she saying structure is all we can *know*, or all there *is*, and can she hold the ontic version without losing the relata that relations supposedly need. and one on whether the criterion is too permissive in the other direction as well — whether two rival theories that are empirically equivalent but structurally different, or structurally identical with different ontologies, come out the same or different on her account, and what that does to her meta-induction argument.
