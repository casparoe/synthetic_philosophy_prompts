I recently took a job as the in-house ethics person at a small company that sells an LLM-based case-prioritization tool to child welfare agencies, and Foucault has suddenly become personally relevant in a way I did not expect. I keep having to write memos about the tool, and every time I try to say something smart about power I end up recycling the panopticon, which feels too easy. So here is what I want from you: put yourself in Foucault's head and predict what he would have thought about this whole setup, even though he died before any of it existed. Not a summary of his actual views, I mean extrapolate. Specifically: would he treat the model as a new kind of archive, or as a new confessional technology, or as something else? Does the fact that the model's knowledge is stored as weights rather than as a discourse matter for his picture of power/knowledge, or is that a distinction without a difference? What would he make of RLHF -- the thing where humans rank model outputs and the model learns to please them -- and of the annotators doing the ranking? Is that pastoral power, disciplinary training, or just labor? Would he see the demand for explainability as a new ritual of truth-telling, and if so, who is confessing to whom? And what would he say about the caseworker who has to click accept on a recommendation she does not understand -- is she a subject of power, a relay of it, or both? I am less interested in whether he would be for or against the tool than in which of his concepts still do work here and which would need to be rebuilt. Also, if you think he would refuse the question or say the tool is just capitalism with a new interface, say that and explain why. And I definately don't want a bibliography or reading list; I want the reconstruction.
