ok so I'm running a small reading group on panpsychism next term and I want to open the first session with something that isn't a lecture. can you write me a round — an actually singable round, four voices, entries marked, so we can stand in a circle and do it — where the form itself is doing the combination problem.

heres the thought. in a round every voice sings the same line, so all the voices are tokens of one type, which is the panpsychist's micro-subjects; and the harmony only exists while they overlap. but nobody singing ever hears the harmony as a whole — only someone standing outside the circle does, and what they hear isn't in any one voice. thats the worry I want the poem to make audible: the unity is real only from the outside, which is either the solution or the begging of the question depending on your mood. so no 'merge', no 'together', no 'one' in the lyrics — the combining has to be done by the entries and the overlaps, not by the diction. each voice's line should also stand up on its own as a small complete experience, since thats the whole premise.

and I want the standard escape hatch in there somewhere: the move where someone says fine, theres just a primitive relation that ties two experiences into one and thats that. give that line to whichever voice enters last, when everything's already going, and make it sound like it's just restating the thing it was supposed to explain — the relation has to hold *between* them, and 'between' already sounds like the whole is sitting there waiting. 12-20 lines total. if you need performance notes at the bottom about where each voice comes in, put them in, we'll definately need them.
