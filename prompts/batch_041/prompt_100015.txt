So I'm a novelist — literary fiction, three books in, the kind of writer who spends two years on a draft and then realizes chapter 4 has to go and the whole back half collapses with it. Which is maybe why I can't stop thinking about the grandfather paradox. Every time I read about it I think: isn't this just the revision problem? You kill the grandfather in an early chapter, and now the guy doing the killing can't have been born to do it. I do that to myself constantly, except I just delete the scene and move on.

Here's what I actually want. Take the grandfather paradox and the main moves people make about it — the idea that the universe simply won't let you pull the trigger, the idea that you just split off a new timeline and the old one's fine, the idea that the whole thing is a closed loop with no first cause, and the whole presentism-versus-block-universe thing about whether the past is still sitting there somewhere — and restate all of it, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use. Drafts and revisions, continuity, retcons, backstory, canon, foreshadowing, setup and payoff, characters as products of their history, unreliable narrators, the difference between what the story says happened and what the reader believes happened. That's my native tongue. The physics I'm a tourist in at best — I know the words, I don't trust myself with them.

Then the part I really care about: the residue report. What comes across cleanly, what I have to bend or fudge to make it fit, and what doesn't exist on my side of the fence at all. My hunch, and I'd like you to test it rather than flatter it, is that the paradox is basicaly a continuity error that a decent writer would never make — that it only looks deep because physicists are stuck with one manuscript and can't just revise. Tell me if that survives the translation or if it's an artifact of me thinking in stories. And my editor keeps telling me the past is a promise you make to the reader, which I've never understood — if that maps onto any of this, all the better.
