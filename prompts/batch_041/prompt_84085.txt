I'm making a small taxonomy of thought experiments and I need a worked specimen for one entry. Definitions, so we're on the same page: the payload is the general principle the case is meant to support; the lever is the one feature of the case that actually does the persuading; and the case is decorative when the lever has nothing to do with the payload — the pump works by smuggling in something the principle itself treats as irrelevant.

Build me a decorative pump that genuinely works on ordinary thoughtful people. Specs: the scenario itself is one paragraph, realistic (an office, a family, a waiting room — no trolleys, no brains in vats, no experience machines, no violins, and nothing with a famous name attached to it), and the payload should be a claim those same people would also accept if you just argued for it directly — keep it in the neighbourhood of "a vivid single case shouldn't drive policy" or "you're responsible for what you foresee, not only for what you intend".

Then do the workup, in this order: (1) name the payload, the lever, and the verdict the case actually gets; (2) swap the lever for something neutral, hold everything else fixed, and say what happens to the intuition; (3) keep the lever but point it at the opposite payload — if the same lever now sells the opposite conclusion, that's your evidence it's doing no arguementative work; (4) say which step is load-bearing for your claim that the case is decorative, and where someone defending the payload would say you've cheated. Last thing: is there a version that keeps the payload but makes the lever legit, i.e. the feature doing the persuading is one the principle actually cares about?
